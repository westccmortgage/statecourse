# Four trust-account exercises with complete source facts

Original StateCourse formative coursework, November 5, 2026. Use with Record Keeping module 02 and the retained Commission outline. These are fictional accounting records, not operating trust accounts, completed Commission contracts or secure final-examination items. No observed duration or student completion is asserted.

The existing lesson explains journals, beneficiary ledgers, bank reconciliation and the four required exercise types. This workbook supplies the numeric files and a restore task. Actual current contract/form completion, independent review, production record controls and the written final remain separate open requirements. All dates below are supplied bookkeeping facts; they do not replace the current legal deposit deadlines.

## A. Broker-closed sale: connect settlement to journal and ledger

Use problem 2 of the Closings additional workbook as the fictional settlement instructions. Price is $480,000; earnest money $12,000; new-loan proceeds $384,000; buyer's remaining cash $88,480. Cash disbursements are seller $242,640; existing payoff $210,000; negotiated compensation $24,000; title $1,800; release recording $60; loan origination $3,840; prepaid interest $1,920; buyer recording $220. The already-paid $650 appraisal does not pass through the settlement account.

**Supplied receipt file.** May 4, receive the buyer's $12,000 check EM-02 for the property identified in the exercise; deposit May 5. On June 1 receive the $384,000 lender wire LN-02 and $88,480 buyer wire BC-02. Disburse the listed settlement amounts June 1, with check or electronic references P-01 through P-08. Use the settlement instructions and hypothetical invoices SI-02/T-02/L-02 as supporting records, each labeled by its exercise role. They are exercise instructions, not a representation that real signed documents are held.

**Student work.** Prepare the six-column settlement worksheet, then a chronological journal and property/beneficiary ledger. Include receipt and deposit dates, tendering party, beneficiary/property, purpose, amount and running balance; for payments, include date, payee, payment reference, support reference and running balance. Explain the relationship between the seller's $242,640 proceeds and the brokerage's total $484,480 cash receipts. Do not post the $480,000 price as an extra cash receipt.

**Worked control totals.** After earnest money the balance is $12,000. After lender funds it is $396,000. After buyer funds it is **$484,480**. The listed payments total **$484,480**, leaving journal and ledger zero. The $240,000-looking owner-carry figure from another exercise is irrelevant; do not combine cases.

**Sample bank statement at June 1.** Ending statement balance is **$452,640**. The $210,000 payoff check and $242,640 seller check are outstanding; all other listed items cleared. Reconciled bank balance is $452,640 − $452,640 = **$0**, matching journal and beneficiary ledger. Show both outstanding checks individually. A zero book balance does not mean the bank statement must already be zero, and outstanding checks are not new expenses to deduct from the ledger a second time.

**Changed-fact application.** The bank next reports that the $220 recording payment cleared for $250. Identify the $30 difference, inspect the payment authorization and record the actual supported correction. Do not change a bank statement to force reconciliation or borrow another beneficiary's funds to hide a shortage. Explain what evidence is needed before deciding whether this is a bank error, bookkeeping error or unauthorized payment.

## B. Eight-property management: two accounts and an independent liability check

The fictional office keeps rental operating trust money and tenant security-deposit trust money in separate accounts. Each row identifies one property and its beneficiary. All amounts are dollars. Each management agreement authorizes the supplied month's rent, documented repair payment, stated management fee and owner distribution. The given fee amounts are negotiated exercise facts, not legal or customary rates.

**Supplied rental journal and bank facts.** Post all rents before the authorized payments. Rent receipts are July 3 except H, received and deposited July 31. Repairs and fees are July 15. Owner distributions are July 30. Identify each event by property and a unique receipt/payment reference. The July 31 bank statement ends at **$5,670**. H's $1,550 deposit is in transit. Owner checks E $1,820, F $1,405 and H $1,195 are outstanding. There are no other bank reconciling items in the supplied file.

**Student work.** Create the rental journal and all eight ledgers, preserving the beneficiary at each posting. Reconcile the statement without changing the journal for already-recorded outstanding items. Check every running ledger for negative balances. Then prepare each owner's statement from the same underlying entries. An aggregate nonnegative account does not excuse a negative individual ledger.

**Worked rental totals.** Opening balance **$2,800** + rents **$14,450** − repairs **$1,500** − fees **$1,445** − owner distributions **$11,505** = **$2,800**. The eight ending ledgers total $2,800. Bank reconciliation: $5,670 + $1,550 − ($1,820 + $1,405 + $1,195) = **$2,800**. Verify each property independently; the aggregate identity alone cannot detect a charge posted to the wrong owner.

**Security-deposit account.** At the start of the sample period the eight current tenant deposits above plus $450 for former property I total $11,250. A properly authorized $450 refund check to former tenant I was posted July 20; it has not cleared. The July 31 bank statement shows **$11,250**. The journal and eight current ledgers show **$10,800**. No other bank reconciling item exists. The eight lease deposit obligations match the table **except G's signed lease states $1,500**.

**Student work.** Produce a separate deposit-account reconciliation and a lease-to-ledger comparison. Identify the apparent three-way agreement and the independent liability discrepancy. State which file is short, by how much, what evidence must be requested and why another tenant's balance cannot be used to repair it. Preserve the discrepancy rather than certifying the file as reconciled and complete.

**Worked deposit findings.** Reconciled bank: $11,250 − $450 outstanding refund = **$10,800**, equal to journal and recorded ledgers. The leases require **$10,850**, creating a **$50 G liability discrepancy**. Three equal accounting totals do not prove all obligations were recorded. Inspect the transfer receipt, lease, amendments and tenant/owner records; determine what actually happened and correct through properly supported funds and entries. Do not invent a cash receipt for $50 that was never received, charge another tenant's ledger, or sign an unqualified reconciliation certification while the shortage is unresolved.

## C. Title-company closing: record only money the brokerage holds

Repeat the same sale and amounts as exercise A, but the title company receives the lender's $384,000 and buyer's $88,480 directly. The brokerage holds only the $12,000 earnest deposit and transfers it June 1 to the title company under the closing instructions. The title company supplies a dated and signed receipt TC-02. All settlement disbursements, including compensation, are made by that company. The brokerage's earnest-money bank statement shows both its original $12,000 deposit and the cleared $12,000 transfer, ending at zero.

**Student work.** Prepare the brokerage's journal and beneficiary ledger, the transfer record and reconciliation. List documents needed to connect the transfer to the final closing statement. Separately show where an earned compensation payment belongs under the actual account arrangement; do not create a fictional trust receipt for all transaction funds just because the closing statement contains them.

**Worked control totals.** Brokerage trust receipts **$12,000**; transfer **$12,000**; journal, ledger and reconciled bank **$0**. The title company's cash-source proof is still $484,480, but it is a different entity's receipt/disbursement record. Retain the dated signed transfer receipt and the closing evidence. Delegating settlement does not erase the broker's separate closing-document and consumer obligations. The exercise does not deem a fictional receipt an actually executed one.

**Changed-fact application.** The firm ledger shows the transfer but no signed receipt is in the file. Arithmetic is correct. Identify the documentary defect and the follow-up required under the course's retained source rules; do not award completion solely because the bank agrees.

## D. Backup, restore and production: make the evidence retrievable

Use your completed A–C records. Export the journal, all ledgers, both management bank reconciliations, the lease-obligation comparison, settlement worksheet, support-reference list and correction log into a dated exercise folder. A filename list without the underlying records does not satisfy this task.

Create an inventory recording file name, version, date range, beneficiary/property scope, row count and a cryptographic digest. Keep the original export unchanged. Restore the copy into a separate empty location or a fresh application profile. Reopen every file, recompute the A/C zero balances and the management $2,800/$10,800 balances, and recover the unresolved G $50 discrepancy. Recompute file digests and investigate any mismatch; do not update the inventory merely to conceal a changed file.

Produce a readable audit packet from the restored copy with a transaction-to-support index. Confirm that amounts, dates, beneficiary identities and payment references remain legible. Capture what software/version was used, the actual restore date, the records checked, the observed results and the reviewer. A statement that “cloud backup is on” is not a restoration test. A spreadsheet whose formatting survived but whose amounts changed is not a successful restoration either.

**Acceptance.** Require demonstrated restoration and retrieval, not the student's assertion that the folder exists. Return missing or inconsistent records for correction. Do not declare a live system, legal retention control or disaster-recovery service accepted from this fictional exercise. Instructor assignment, actual learner performance and independent course review remain unverified.
