## Learning objectives

1. State the internal control requirement and who it must reach.
2. State why the outline puts the broker personally into the reconciliation.
3. State what a broker does with an earnest money dispute and what a broker must not do.
4. State the property management rules that create separate accounts and forbid negative ledgers.
5. State when commissions become available to the firm.
6. State the rules that apply when a broker holds or manages their own property.

## A. Separation of duties — outline Topic III.A

### A.1 Employee theft and internal controls — III.A.1

> **Rule 5.1**: *"Any Brokerage Firm or Broker who receives Money Belonging to Others **must establish
> WRITTEN accounting control policies and procedures, which must include adequate CHECKS AND BALANCES over
> the financial activities of the Broker, Brokerage Firm, AND UNLICENSED PERSONS, as well as MANAGE THE RISK
> OF FRAUD OR ILLEGAL ACTS.**"*

**Read the three things the rule requires and the one it names expressly.**

| Requirement | Note |
|---|---|
| **Written** policies and procedures | Not an understood office practice |
| **Adequate checks and balances** over financial activities | The separation of duties this topic is named for |
| Reaching **the broker, the firm, AND UNLICENSED PERSONS** | The bookkeeper, the assistant, the office manager |
| **Manage the risk of fraud or illegal acts** | The rule states the purpose, so the test is whether the controls actually do it |

**The classic separation, and the one an auditor looks for:** the person who **receives** money is not the
person who **records** it is not the person who **reconciles** the account is not the person who **signs
the checks.** In a small firm one person may do more than one of those — which is precisely why the rule
requires the broker's own involvement in Part A.2.

**Rule 5.3.C is the sentence that closes the loophole.** The employing or independent broker **may authorize
other licensed or unlicensed cosigners** — but *"**such authorization will not relieve the Employing Broker
or Independent Broker of any responsibility under the Commission statutes and these Rules.**"*

**And Rule 6.3.A.2**: employing brokers **must supervise all unlicensed employees**, *"including, but not
limited to, Unlicensed On-Site Managers, secretaries, bookkeepers, and personal assistants of Associate
Brokers."*

### A.2 The broker performs or checks the reconciliation — III.A.2

The outline's own words: *"**Broker's need to personally perform bank reconciliations or periodically
compare reconciliations against source documents (transaction files).**"*

**Two alternatives, and the Commission names both:**

1. **Perform the reconciliation personally**; or
2. **Periodically compare the reconciliations against SOURCE DOCUMENTS** — the transaction files.

**Alternative 2 is the one that catches what alternative 1 alone would miss.** A bookkeeper who is diverting
funds can produce a reconciliation that balances every month — the journal, the ledgers and the bank all
agree. **What they cannot do is make the transaction files agree**, which is why the Commission's identity
at Topic II.B.3 ends with **"= Transaction File Liabilities"** and why this sub-topic exists.

**Rule 5.14.C** requires the **three-way reconciliation monthly, with a report completed and maintained**;
**Rule 5.22** makes the **employing or independent broker jointly responsible with the firm** for all of
Chapter 5.

### A.3 Practitioner aids — III.A.3

The outline names *"**Use of practitioner aids at auditing Website**"* — material the Division publishes for
brokers.

> **No Division practitioner aid, audit guide, checklist or template was obtained by this project**, and
> **no module of this build states the content of one.** **This is a submission gate.**

## B. Sales — outline Topic III.B

### B.1 Promissory notes: safekeeping and collection — III.B.1

> **Rule 5.13**: *"If a **promissory note is received as earnest money** pursuant to an executed contract,
> **the seller must be INFORMED OF THE DATE such promissory note becomes due.** If payment is not made by
> the due date, the Broker **must PROMPTLY NOTIFY THE SELLER and DELIVER THE ORIGINAL promissory note.**"*
>
> **Rule 5.18**: *"**Any instrument, equity, or Thing of Value taken in lieu of cash MUST BE HELD BY THE
> BROKERAGE FIRM, except as otherwise agreed.**"*

**Three obligations in Rule 5.13, in sequence:** tell the seller **the due date**; watch it; and if it
passes unpaid, **notify promptly and hand over the original.** A note is not earnest money in the account —
it is a promise, and **Rule 5.18** says the firm holds it.

### B.2 Void and NSF checks — III.B.2

**A check that does not clear was never funds.** The journal recorded a receipt; the bank did not.

**What has to happen:** the journal and the affected ledger are **corrected**; the **parties are told**;
and — the point of the sub-topic — **nothing is disbursed against it.**

> **Rule 6.19.E** — the Good Funds rule — states it directly for closing: a broker or firm *"**must not
> disburse or authorize disbursement of funds UNTIL THOSE FUNDS HAVE BEEN RECEIVED and are ... available
> for immediate withdrawal AS A MATTER OF RIGHT** from the financial institution ... **or available for
> immediate withdrawal as a consequence of an agreement with a financial institution** ... **If the
> agreement contains contingencies or reservations, NO DISBURSEMENTS CAN BE MADE until these are
> satisfied.**"*

**And the ledger consequence:** a disbursement made against a check that then bounces leaves that
beneficiary's ledger **negative**, which **Rule 5.14.B** forbids absolutely — *"**No ledger may ever be
allowed to have a negative cash balance.**"*

### B.3 Earnest money disputes and interpleaders — III.B.3

**Three things are true about disputed earnest money whatever the contract says**, and none of them depends
on a statute this build did not read:

> **1. The broker holds.** The broker does **not** decide who is entitled to it.
> **2. The parties or a court decide.** An **interpleader** is the proceeding by which a stakeholder pays
> disputed funds into court and lets the claimants litigate between themselves.
> **3. Nothing is disbursed on the broker's own judgement.**

**Why a broker must not simply release it.** **Rule 5.9**: money belonging to one beneficiary **must not be
used for the benefit of another.** Releasing disputed funds to the wrong claimant is exactly that — and
**Rule 5.14.B**'s no-negative-ledger rule bites the moment it is posted.

**What the broker does:** stop, tell the designated broker, and get **legal advice before the money moves.**
**Rule 6.2.B.3** names the route expressly — obtain the assistance of *"the Employing Broker, Supervisory
Broker, a Broker who meets the requirements ... **or LEGAL COUNSEL that is competent in the matter.**"*

> **No Colorado interpleader statute or rule of civil procedure was read by this project**, and **no
> procedure, deadline, filing requirement, fee or cost rule is stated anywhere in this build.** The
> **Contracts and Regulations** outline names *"**remedies including damages and specific performance**"*
> among the **Current Legal Issues** representative areas, and the Commission supplies that course's
> mandatory material to authorized providers.

### B.4 Good funds — III.B.4

The outline names six arrangements a broker meets, and **Rule 6.19.E** is the rule behind all of them:

| The outline's item | What it is |
|---|---|
| **a. Cashier's or teller's check** | An instrument drawn by the bank itself |
| **b. Broker check in advance of closing** | The brokerage's own check issued before the closing funds arrive |
| **c. Wire transfer** | Funds moved bank to bank |
| **d. Tri-party agreements** (broker–title company–broker's bank) | The **agreement** contemplated by Rule 6.19.E.2 — and *"**if the agreement contains contingencies or reservations, no disbursements can be made until these are satisfied**"* |
| **e. Netting earned commission against earnest money deposit** (security deposits also) | Offsetting rather than moving money — permitted only within **Rule 5.10.C**, which makes fees available **only after all contracted services are performed and there is no remaining right of recall** |
| **f. Requirements of the Great Colorado Payback Program for unclaimed funds** | The state's unclaimed property programme |

> **§ 38-35-125, which Rule 6.19.E cites, was NOT read by this project**, and **no definition of good funds,
> clearing period or list of acceptable instruments from the statute is stated anywhere in this build.**
>
> **The Great Colorado Payback Program and Colorado's unclaimed property statute were NOT read**, and **no
> dormancy period, reporting deadline, remittance requirement or form is stated anywhere in this build.**
> **This is a submission gate**, and a real one: a brokerage that holds money it cannot return has an
> obligation somewhere, and this course does not state what it is.

## C. Property management — outline Topic III.C

Five sub-items, and every one of them is a rule read in full.

### C.1 Separate trust account — III.C.1

> **Rule 5.5**: a firm engaging in property management *"**must deposit rental receipts and security deposits
> and disburse money collected for such purposes in SEPARATE Trust or Escrow Accounts, a MINIMUM OF ONE for
> rental receipts and a MINIMUM OF ONE for security deposits.**"*

**Two accounts minimum, by purpose** — and **Rule 5.2** then requires each to carry **the word "trust" or
"escrow" plus a label identifying the purpose** in the deposit agreement.

### C.2 Commingling and negative ledgers — III.C.2

> **Rule 5.10**: *"A Broker's or Brokerage Firm's **personal or business operating funds must not be
> commingled** with Money Belonging to Others."*
>
> **Rule 5.9**: money belonging to **one beneficiary must not be used for the benefit of another**, and
> **never** for the firm or the broker.
>
> **Rule 5.14.B**: *"**No ledger may ever be allowed to have a negative cash balance.**"*

**On a managed portfolio the negative ledger is how commingling shows up.** One owner's account overdrawn
means another owner's money is standing behind it, however briefly, and the rule admits no *however
briefly.*

**The one permitted exception is narrow and stated:** **Rule 5.14.D** lets a firm deposit **personal funds
as may be required to pay bank charges** on the account **without violating Rule 5.10** — with an entry in
**both the journal and the ledger.**

### C.3 Security and advance deposits — separate accounts — III.C.3

**Rule 5.5** again, and **Rule 5.8**:

> **5.8.A — owner-held.** A firm receipting for security deposits *"**will not deliver such security deposits
> to an owner without the tenant's WRITTEN AUTHORIZATION IN A LEASE or unless WRITTEN NOTICE has been given
> to the tenant**"* — notice given so the tenant knows **who is holding the deposit** and **the specific
> requirements for the procedure in which the tenant may request return.** If the deposit goes to the
> owner, the management agreement **should** place financial responsibility for its return on the owner and
> **must** authorize disclosure of the owner's **true name and current mailing address** in a dispute.
>
> **5.8.B — a change of management company.** **Written disclosure to owner and current tenant within 30
> DAYS** of execution or assignment of a management agreement, of the status of any deposit held by the
> previous firm; the **previous firm transfers within 60 DAYS** and confirms the amount; the **new firm
> verifies each deposit matches the amount in the current lease and discloses any discrepancy**; and the
> tenant is **informed in writing if the owner is holding the deposit.**

**Rule 5.11** adds a case brokers do not expect: **money accepted for activities NOT involving real estate
brokerage services** — **guest deposits for short-term rentals**, **security deposits for the broker's own
rental properties**, **deposits from a buyer where the broker is acting as a builder**, and any other
non-brokerage purpose — **must still go into a trust or escrow account**, and if into the broker's own
rather than the firm's, must comply with **Rules 5.6, 5.9, 5.10, a journal with monthly two-way
reconciliation, and 5.21.**

### C.4 Owner reserves — III.C.4

**Reserves are the owner's money held for future expenses.** They are Money Belonging to Others, they sit
on that owner's ledger, and they are subject to every rule in Chapter 5.

**What the management agreement must settle:** **how much** is held, **what it may be used for**, and **when
it is replenished.** Module 05 of the **Real Estate Law and Practice** course carries the management
agreement checklist.

### C.5 Commissions earned versus taken — III.C.5

> **Rule 5.10.C**: *"In the absence of a specific written agreement to the contrary, **commissions, fees, and
> other charges collected by a Brokerage Firm for performing any service on behalf of another are considered
> 'EARNED' and available for use by the Brokerage Firm ONLY AFTER ALL CONTRACTED SERVICES HAVE BEEN PERFORMED
> and there is NO REMAINING RIGHT OF RECALL by others for such money.**"*
>
> **Rule 5.10.A**: money in the account that becomes **due and payable to the firm must be withdrawn
> MONTHLY.**

**Two different failures, and the outline names the distinction: "earned vs. taken."**

- **Taken before earned** — the firm has used money that is still somebody else's. That is **Rule 5.9**.
- **Earned and not taken** — the firm's own money is sitting in the trust account past month end. That is
  **Rule 5.10.A**, and it is commingling in the other direction.

**Rule 5.10.C's recording requirement:** the firm **must identify and record all commissions, fees or other
charges withdrawn** on the **account journal and the individual ledgers of those against whom they are
charged** — and where a single disbursement covers more than one transaction, rental period or beneficiary,
must on request produce **a schedule detailing the individual components and identifying the affected
beneficiary or property ledgers.**

## D. The broker ledger — outline Topic III.D

**A ledger for the brokerage firm itself, within the trust or escrow account.** It is where **Rule 5.14.D's
bank-charge deposits** are recorded, where **money that has become due and payable to the firm** sits until
it is withdrawn under **Rule 5.10.A**, and where **advances made under Rule 5.10.B** are identified.

> **Rule 5.10.B**: money **advanced by the firm for the benefit of another** may be placed in the account
> and **identified as an advance**, but may be **withdrawn by the firm only on behalf of that person** — and
> **must be identified and recorded in the journal and the ledger and disclosed in the accounting to the
> beneficiary** as **Rule 5.15** requires.

**The broker ledger is not an exception to Rule 5.10's prohibition on commingling.** It is the mechanism by
which the three narrow permitted cases — bank charges, advances, and earned-but-not-yet-withdrawn fees —
are made **visible** rather than invisible.

## E. Broker acting as principal and licensee — outline Topic III.E

**Partnerships, joint ventures and syndications**, as the outline names them. **Rules 5.10.D and 5.10.E**
address exactly this, and the trigger is the **20 percent** ownership threshold in **§ 12-10-201(6)(b)(VII)**.

> **Rule 5.10.D** — rental proceeds for a broker's own property managed **through a firm the broker is NOT
> licensed with** must be deposited in a **trust or escrow account maintained for Money Belonging to
> Others**, subject to **Rules 5.2 and 5.14.**
>
> **Rule 5.10.E** — rental proceeds for property **owned by a broker who IS licensed with the firm** must be
> deposited into **one or more trust or escrow accounts SEPARATE from any other accounts containing Money
> Belonging to Others** — though those accounts **may hold the proceeds of other brokers licensed with the
> same firm** whose own properties it manages. Subject to **Rules 5.2 and 5.14.**
>
> **A "broker's property" for both rules includes properties held in PARTNERSHIP with others, JOINT
> VENTURES, or SYNDICATIONS**, provided the broker's ownership in the entity or property is **more than the
> 20% threshold** in § 12-10-201(6)(b)(VII).

**And the disclosure that goes with it, from Chapter 6:**

> **Rule 6.17.B**: *"If a Broker **sells, buys, or leases real property on the Broker's own account**, such
> Broker **must disclose in the contracting instrument, or in a separate concurrent writing, that they are a
> licensed Broker.**"*
>
> **Rule 6.17.A**: a **continuing duty** to disclose, in writing, **any known Conflict of Interest** arising
> in the course of any real estate transaction.

**Twenty percent is the line, and a broker whose interest is in an entity rather than in the property
directly still has to compute it.**

## F. Duplicate signatures — outline Topic III.F

**The outline names it and states nothing further**, and this module states what the rules read support and
no more.

**What the sub-topic is about:** who may sign on a trust or escrow account, and the control risk that
arises when signature authority is duplicated or delegated.

> **Rule 5.3.C** is the provision: the **Employing Broker or Independent Broker** are *"**responsible for,
> must maintain and be able to withdraw money from each separate account**"*, and **may authorize other
> licensed or unlicensed cosigners** — *"**However, such authorization will not relieve the Employing Broker
> or Independent Broker of any responsibility under the Commission statutes and these Rules.**"*

**Two requirements and one warning.** The broker **must be able to withdraw** — authority cannot be
delegated away entirely. Cosigners **may** be authorised, licensed or not. And **the responsibility does not
move with the authority.**

**Read against Rule 5.1's internal controls, the point of the sub-topic emerges:** every additional
signature is a control weakened, so a firm that adds one adds a compensating control — which is the
separation of duties in Part A, and the broker's own reconciliation or source-document comparison in A.2.

> **No Commission rule on duplicate or facsimile signatures beyond Rule 5.3.C was found in the reading**,
> and **no requirement about signature cards, dual signature thresholds, electronic signature authority or
> signature stamps is stated anywhere in this build.**
