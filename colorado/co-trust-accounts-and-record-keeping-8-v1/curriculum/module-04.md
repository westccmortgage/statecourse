## Learning objectives

1. State when a brokerage relationship ends and the one-year backstop.
2. State what survives termination for a single agent and what survives for a transaction-broker.
3. State what happens to trust funds and documents on termination.
4. State what happens to security deposits when management passes to another firm, and by when.
5. State the retention obligation that outlives the relationship.

## A. When the relationship ends — § 12-10-409

> **(1)(a)** The relationship **commences when the broker is engaged** and continues **until performance or
> completion** of the agreement by which the broker was engaged.
>
> **(1)(b)** If the agreement is **not performed or completed for any reason**, the relationship ends at the
> **EARLIER** of:
> **(I)** any **date of expiration agreed** by the parties;
> **(II)** any **termination or relinquishment** of the relationship by the parties; or
> **(III)** **ONE YEAR after the date of the engagement.**

**The one-year backstop runs whether or not anyone remembers it.** A file left open ends by statute at a
date neither party chose.

### A.1 What survives, and it differs by relationship

| | **Single agent** — § 12-10-409(2)(b) | **Transaction-broker** — § 12-10-409(3) |
|---|---|---|
| **Accounting** | **Survives** — *"accounting for all money and property related to and received during the engagement"* | **Survives** — the same words |
| **Confidentiality** | **Survives** — *"keeping confidential all information received during the course of the engagement **that was made confidential by request or instructions from the engaging party**"* | **Not stated in (3)** |
| **Everything else** | *"**owes no further duty or obligation**"* — (2)(a) | *"**owes no further obligation or responsibility**"* |

**The accounting obligation is the one this course is about, and it survives for BOTH.**

**And the surviving confidentiality is not unlimited.** **§ 12-10-409(2)(b)(II)** lifts it where **(A)** the
engaging party **grants written consent to disclose**, **(B)** disclosure is **required by law**, or **(C)**
the information **is made public or becomes public by the words or conduct of the engaging party or from a
source other than the broker.**

## B. Forwarding trust funds and documents — outline Topic IV.A

### B.1 The money

**Money Belonging to Others does not become the firm's money because the relationship ended.** It is the
beneficiary's, and **every Chapter 5 rule continues to apply to it until it is properly delivered.**

| Rule | What it still requires |
|---|---|
| **5.9** | It **must not be used** for another beneficiary, or for the firm or broker |
| **5.10** | It **must not be commingled** with the firm's own funds |
| **5.14.B** | The ledger **must not go negative**, and the ledgers must still sum to the journal |
| **5.14.C** | **Three-way reconciliation monthly** while the account holds Money Belonging to Others |
| **5.15** | The **accounting** to the beneficiary is still owed |
| **5.21** | The records **must still be produced** to the Commission on request |
| **5.22** | The **employing or independent broker remains jointly responsible** with the firm |

**Rule 5.14.C's own relief is the only thing that changes:** the broker is *"**not required to maintain
records or reconcile any Trust or Escrow Account when such account does not contain Money Belonging to
Others.**"* — which is to say, **when the money has actually gone.**

**And where entitlement is disputed, nothing changes about that either.** The broker **holds**; the parties
or a court decide; and **Rule 6.2.B.3** offers the route — obtain the assistance of the employing broker,
a supervisory broker, a competent broker, **or legal counsel competent in the matter.**

### B.2 Security deposits when management passes to another firm — Rule 5.8.B

This is the case the outline's sub-topic is really about, and the rule fixes **two deadlines.**

> **Rule 5.8.B**: A firm which **begins management** of a property most recently managed by another firm
> *"**must disclose to the owner and the current tenant, IN WRITING, and WITHIN THIRTY (30) DAYS after
> execution or assignment of a Management Agreement, the STATUS of any security deposit held by the previous
> Brokerage Firm.**"*
>
> *"The **PREVIOUS Brokerage Firm must TRANSFER the security deposit WITHIN SIXTY (60) DAYS after the
> execution or assignment** of a management agreement to the new Brokerage Firm **and CONFIRM to the owner
> and the tenant the amount of the funds transferred.**"*
>
> *"The **NEW Brokerage Firm must VERIFY that each security deposit transferred to them MATCHES THE AMOUNT
> LISTED IN THE CURRENT LEASE and DISCLOSE ANY DISCREPANCY to the owner and current tenant.** The Brokerage
> Firm **must inform the tenant, in writing, IF THE OWNER IS HOLDING the security deposit.**"*

| Who | What | By when |
|---|---|---|
| **New firm** | **Written disclosure of the deposit's status** to **owner and current tenant** | **Within 30 days** of execution or assignment |
| **Previous firm** | **Transfer the deposit**, and **confirm the amount** to owner and tenant | **Within 60 days** of execution or assignment |
| **New firm** | **Verify each deposit against the current lease** and **disclose any discrepancy** to owner and tenant | On receipt |
| **New firm** | **Inform the tenant in writing if the owner is holding it** | — |

**The verification step is the fourth term of the reconciliation identity in the field.** *"= Transaction
File Liabilities."* A transfer that balances against the previous firm's ledgers can still be wrong against
**the leases**, and the rule puts the check on the incoming firm.

**And Rule 5.8.A governs delivery to an owner at any time**: no delivery **without the tenant's written
authorization in a lease or written notice** telling the tenant **who holds the deposit** and **how to
request its return.**

### B.3 The documents

**Rule 6.19.A** requires a broker licensed with a firm to *"**deliver closing statements to the Brokerage
Firm along with any other closing documents, IMMEDIATELY FOLLOWING CLOSING**"* — so on a transaction that
closed, the documents are already with the firm.

**Rule 6.20** then requires **both the broker and the firm** to retain the transaction file for **four
years**, from **consummation** or from **the expiration of a listing contract that does not consummate.**

**Rule 5.19** applies where a **branch office** maintained its own trust or escrow account and record
keeping system: **the employing broker is responsible for it**, which does not end when the branch does.

## C. Closing accounting records and paperwork reduction — outline Topic IV.B

### C.1 Closing the records

**An account is closed when it holds nothing.** Until then **Rule 5.14.C** requires the **monthly three-way
reconciliation**, and **Rule 5.2**'s deposit agreement and its retained copy remain the firm's.

**The final reconciliation is the one that matters most.** It should show **zero** across all four terms —
journal balance, sum of ledger balances, reconciled bank balance, and transaction file liabilities. **A
final reconciliation that does not reach zero means money is still owed to somebody**, and the firm needs to
know to whom before the account is closed.

**Money the firm cannot return to its owner does not become the firm's.** The outline names the answer's
location at its Topic III.B.4.f — *"**Requirements of Great Colorado Payback Program for unclaimed
funds**"*.

> **The Great Colorado Payback Program and Colorado's unclaimed property statute were NOT read by this
> project**, and **no dormancy period, reporting deadline, remittance requirement or form is stated anywhere
> in this build.** **This is a submission gate**, and it is exactly the situation this module describes: a
> firm winding up an account with funds it cannot deliver has an obligation this course cannot state.

### C.2 Paperwork reduction — what may be reduced and what may not

**Rule 5.14.E** permits the **three-way reconciliation reports, ledgers, journals and bank account
statements** to be **kept electronically**, and **Rule 6.24** governs **electronic records and production of
records.**

**What may NOT be reduced is the ability to produce.**

> **Rule 5.21**: *"A Broker and Brokerage Firm **must produce for inspection by the Commission any document
> or record as may be reasonably necessary for investigation or audit** ... **Failure to submit such
> documents or records within the time set by the Commission in its notification will be GROUNDS FOR
> DISCIPLINARY ACTION** unless the Commission has granted an extension of time for such production."*

**A firm that converts to a new system, closes an office, or ends a relationship still has to produce
records for four years.** Module 02's exercise (d) — *"proper backup and printouts for software accounting
systems"* — exists for this moment, and the test of whether it was done properly is whether the records can
be produced **after** the system that made them is gone.

### C.3 What a broker should have when a relationship ends

| Item | Source |
|---|---|
| **A final reconciliation showing zero, or showing exactly what is still held and for whom** | Rule 5.14.C |
| **A dated and signed receipt for every delivery of funds** | Rule 5.12.A and 5.12.B |
| **Written confirmation of every security deposit transferred, to owner and tenant** | Rule 5.8.B |
| **The accounting owed to each beneficiary** | Rule 5.15 |
| **The transaction file, retained four years** | Rule 6.20 |
| **The records, producible on demand for that period** | Rules 5.21, 5.14.E, 6.24 |
| **Confidences kept** — for a single agent, subject to the three exceptions | § 12-10-409(2)(b)(II) |
