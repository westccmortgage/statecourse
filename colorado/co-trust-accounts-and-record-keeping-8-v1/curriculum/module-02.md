## Learning objectives

1. State what a journal records and every field each entry must contain.
2. State what a ledger records and the two rules a ledger can never break.
3. Perform a three-way reconciliation and state what it proves.
4. State the outline's reconciliation identity and explain each term.
5. Complete the four exercises the Commission describes.
6. State the method of accounting required absent written agreement.
7. State the reporting deadline to beneficiaries absent a contrary provision.

## A. Transaction file documents — outline Topic II.A

**Rule 6.20** requires both a broker and a firm to **retain transaction files for four years** — from
**consummation**, or from **the expiration of a listing contract that does not consummate.**

**Rule 5.14** then requires the accounting records for each trust or escrow account, and **Rule 5.15.B**
requires supporting records:

> *"The Brokerage Firm must maintain supporting records, which accurately detail all cash received and
> disbursed under the terms of any Management Agreement.*
> **1.** *"**All deposits** of funds into a Trust or Escrow Account **must identify each person tendering
> funds, the amount of funds tendered, types of funds received from each person, and the property address
> affected.**
> **2.** *"**All disbursements** of funds from a Trust or Escrow Account **must be supported by documents
> such as bids, invoices, contracts, etc.** Ledger and journal records must **identify the payees, property
> addresses affected and amount of funds transferred for each property.**"*

**Money in must be traceable to a person and a property. Money out must be supported by a document.** That
is the whole of the file discipline, and it is what makes the reconciliation in Part D provable rather than
merely arithmetical.

**The Commission's Transaction File Checklist, which Rule 6.20 names as designating the required documents,
was NOT obtained by this project**, and **no module of this build states what it lists.** That is a
submission gate.

## B. The journal — outline Topic II.B.1, and Rule 5.14.A

> **Rule 5.14.A**: *"A **'journal' or an equivalent accounting system which records, IN CHRONOLOGICAL ORDER,
> ALL Money Belonging to Others which is received or disbursed** by the Brokerage Firm."*

**Every field the rule requires, and there is no discretion about any of them:**

| For funds RECEIVED — Rule 5.14.A.1 | For funds DISBURSED — Rule 5.14.A.2 |
|---|---|
| **a.** The **date of receipt AND deposit** | **a.** The **date of payment** |
| **b.** The **name of the person giving the money** | **b.** The **check number or electronic transfer record** |
| **c.** The **name of the person AND property** for which received | **c.** The **name of the payee** |
| **d.** The **purpose** of the receipt | **d.** A **reference to vendor documentation or other physical records verifying purpose for payment** |
| **e.** The **amount** | **e.** The **amount paid** |
| **f.** **A resulting CASH BALANCE for the account** | **f.** **A resulting cash balance for the account** |

**Two fields new brokers routinely omit.**

**Receipt field (a) is TWO dates** — the date received and the date deposited. The gap between them is
exactly what **Rule 5.7** measures, and a journal recording only one date cannot show compliance with the
three- or five-business-day deadline.

**Disbursement field (d) is a reference to a document.** Not a description. **Rule 5.15.B.2** says which
kinds: *"bids, invoices, contracts, etc."* A disbursement with no supporting document is a disbursement
nobody can justify to an auditor.

**And field (f) on both sides:** the journal carries a **running cash balance** after every entry. That
balance is the first term of the reconciliation identity.

## C. The ledger — outline Topic II.B.2, and Rule 5.14.B

> **Rule 5.14.B**: *"A **'ledger' or an equivalent component of an accounting system which records, in
> chronological order, all money which is received or disbursed by the Broker ON BEHALF OF EACH PARTICULAR
> BENEFICIARY** of a Trust or Escrow Account. The ledger record must show the monetary transactions
> **affecting each individual beneficiary** and must **SEGREGATE such transactions from those pertaining to
> other beneficiaries.** The ledger record for each beneficiary **must contain the same transactional
> information** as set forth in subsection A. **NO LEDGER MAY EVER BE ALLOWED TO HAVE A NEGATIVE CASH
> BALANCE. The sum of all ledger balances must agree AT ALL TIMES with the corresponding journal after each
> transaction has been posted.**"*

**The journal is the account. The ledgers are the people.** One journal per trust or escrow account; one
ledger per beneficiary within it.

**Two rules a ledger can never break, and both are absolute on their face:**

> **1. NO NEGATIVE BALANCE — EVER.** A negative ledger means **one beneficiary's money has been used for
> another's**, which is exactly what **Rule 5.9** prohibits: *"Money Belonging to Others belonging to one
> beneficiary **must not be used for the benefit of another beneficiary.**"* On a managed portfolio it
> happens when a disbursement for one property exceeds that property's balance — and it is a violation the
> moment it is posted, not at month end.
>
> **2. THE SUM OF LEDGER BALANCES EQUALS THE JOURNAL — AT ALL TIMES, after each transaction is posted.**
> Not monthly. Not at reconciliation. **After each posting.**

**Rule 5.19** adds: if a **branch office** maintains a trust or escrow account separate from the main
office's, **a separate record keeping system must be maintained in the branch**, and the **employing
broker** is responsible for it.

## D. Reconciliation — outline Topic II.B.3, and Rule 5.14.C

### D.1 The rule

> **Rule 5.14.C**: *"**THREE-WAY RECONCILIATION must be performed MONTHLY to show that ON THE SAME DATE the
> cash balance shown in the JOURNAL, the sum of the cash balances for all LEDGERS, and the RECONCILED BANK
> BALANCE are the same. A three-way reconciliation report must be COMPLETED AND MAINTAINED MONTHLY** to show
> such three-way reconciliation. The Broker is **not required to maintain records or reconcile any Trust or
> Escrow Account when such account does not contain Money Belonging to Others.**"*
>
> **Rule 5.14.D**: a firm **may deposit personal funds as may be required to pay bank charges** incurred in
> connection with maintaining the account **without violating Rule 5.10** — with **an entry in the journal
> and on the ledger.**
>
> **Rule 5.14.E**: the reports, ledgers, journals and bank statements **may be kept electronically.**

### D.2 The Commission's identity, term by term

> ***"Journal Balance + Sum of Ledger Balances = Bank Balance = Transaction File Liabilities"***

**Read carefully, this states three separate equalities and the Commission wrote it as a memory device
rather than as arithmetic** — the journal balance and the sum of ledger balances are the **same number**,
not two numbers to be added. What the outline is naming is the **four things that must agree**:

| Term | What it is | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Journal balance** | The running cash balance of the account | **Rule 5.14.A.1.f and A.2.f** |
| **Sum of ledger balances** | Every beneficiary's balance, added | **Rule 5.14.B** |
| **Reconciled bank balance** | The bank statement balance adjusted for deposits in transit and outstanding items | The bank statement plus the reconciliation |
| **Transaction file liabilities** | What the firm actually owes to the beneficiaries, from the files | **Rule 5.15.B** and the transaction files |

**The fourth term is the one that makes this an audit rather than a bookkeeping check.** The first three can
agree with each other and still be wrong if the records do not match **what the firm actually owes the
people whose money it holds.**

### D.3 Reconciling the bank balance

> **Bank statement balance**
> **plus** deposits in transit (received and journalled, not yet on the statement)
> **minus** outstanding checks and transfers (disbursed and journalled, not yet cleared)
> **plus or minus** bank errors
> **= RECONCILED BANK BALANCE**

**Bank charges and interest posted by the bank but not yet journalled are corrected in the JOURNAL, not in
the reconciliation** — and **Rule 5.14.D** permits the firm to deposit its own money to cover charges, with
entries in the journal and the ledger.

### D.4 A three-way reconciliation worked

**Every figure supplied as a given.**

> **Sales escrow account, 31 May.** Bank statement balance **$92,400.00**. Deposits in transit
> **$18,000.00**. Outstanding checks **$7,250.00** and **$2,150.00**. The journal shows a cash balance of
> **$101,000.00**. Ledgers: Alvarez **$15,000.00**, Boone **$22,000.00**, Chen **$8,000.00**, Delgado
> **$31,000.00**, Everly **$25,000.00**.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Bank statement balance | | 92,400.00 |
| plus deposits in transit | +18,000.00 | 110,400.00 |
| less outstanding checks | −7,250.00 −2,150.00 | **101,000.00** |
| **Reconciled bank balance** | | **$101,000.00** |
| **Journal balance** | given | **$101,000.00** |
| **Sum of ledger balances** | 15,000 + 22,000 + 8,000 + 31,000 + 25,000 | **$101,000.00** |

**Three agree. The fourth term — transaction file liabilities — is then checked against the files**: does
the firm in fact owe Alvarez $15,000, Boone $22,000, and so on? If a file shows a $16,000 deposit for
Alvarez, **the reconciliation balanced and the records are still wrong.**

> **A worked failure, because the failures are what a broker must recognise.**
>
> Same account, and the ledgers are Alvarez **$15,000**, Boone **$22,000**, Chen **−$1,200**, Delgado
> **$31,000**, Everly **$25,000**. The sum is **$91,800**, the journal shows **$91,800**, and the bank
> reconciles to **$91,800**. **All three agree.**
>
> **And it is a violation.** **Rule 5.14.B**: *"**No ledger may ever be allowed to have a negative cash
> balance.**"* Chen's negative balance means **$1,200 of somebody else's money is standing behind Chen's
> disbursement** — which is **Rule 5.9**'s diversion prohibition. **A reconciliation that balances proves
> the arithmetic, not compliance.**

## E. The four practical exercises the Commission describes

> The outline requires *"**Preparation of Minimum of 2 Practical Exercises**"* and then describes four.
> **This course delivers all four and records each separately.**

### E.1 Exercise (a) — a broker-closed sale

> *"Working from a **sample contract and 6-column settlement worksheet**, student shall **create journal and
> ledger entries leading to the closing of a real estate sale by a broker.** **Reconcile the ledger totals
> to the journal balance and a sample bank statement.**"*

**What the student produces:**

1. A **six-column worksheet** for the transaction — the instrument taught in the **Closings** course,
   Module 02.
2. **Journal entries** in chronological order for every receipt and disbursement, with **all six fields**
   Rule 5.14.A requires on each.
3. A **ledger** for the beneficiary, segregated, with the same transactional information.
4. A **reconciliation** of the ledger total to the journal balance and to a sample bank statement.

**The connection this exercise exists to make.** The **closing statement** says what the parties owe each
other. The **journal and ledger** say what the brokerage received and disbursed. **They are two views of
the same money**, and a student who can produce both from one contract understands trust accounting.

**The deposit deadline is part of the exercise.** **Rule 5.7.B** — earnest money into the firm's account
**no later than three business days** following receipt **or mutual execution of contract, whichever is
later** — and the journal's **two dates** (received and deposited) are what show it.

### E.2 Exercise (b) — a property manager's reconciliation

> *"Working from **sample files, journals and ledgers**, student shall **reconcile the bank accounts for a
> property manager managing and holding security deposits for a MINIMUM OF 8 PROPERTIES.** (Lease
> information may be provided as necessary to show amount of security deposits and other needed lease
> data.)"*

**What makes this harder than exercise (a):** **eight ledgers**, not one; **two accounts**, not one, because
**Rule 5.5** requires **a minimum of one for rental receipts and a minimum of one for security deposits**;
and every ledger subject to **Rule 5.14.B's no-negative-balance rule** at every posting.

**A worked fragment, figures given.**

> **Security deposit escrow, eight properties.** Deposits held: **$1,400, $1,150, $1,600, $975, $1,825,
> $1,300, $1,450, $1,100.**
>
> **Sum = $10,800.00.** That is what the **ledger total**, the **journal balance** and the **reconciled bank
> balance** must all equal — and what the **eight leases** must show the firm owing.
>
> **The fourth term is the point of the exercise.** If a lease shows a $1,500 deposit for a property whose
> ledger shows $1,450, the reconciliation balances and **the firm owes $50 it has not recorded.** **Rule
> 5.8.B** requires a firm taking over management to **verify each deposit transferred matches the amount in
> the current lease and disclose any discrepancy** to owner and tenant — the same check, done at the point
> of transfer.

**And the Chapter 5 rules that shape the exercise:**

| Rule | What it requires here |
|---|---|
| **5.5** | Separate accounts — minimum one for rental receipts, minimum one for security deposits |
| **5.7.A** | Property management money deposited **no later than five business days** after receipt or mutual execution of a lease, **whichever is later** |
| **5.8.A** | No delivery of deposits to an owner without the **tenant's written authorization in a lease or written notice** naming who holds them and how to request return |
| **5.10.A** | Money **due and payable to the firm withdrawn MONTHLY** |
| **5.10.C** | Fees are *"earned"* and available **only after all contracted services are performed and there is no remaining right of recall** |
| **5.15.A** | Reports to beneficiaries per the management agreement; absent a contrary provision, **within 30 days after the end of the month** in which funds were received or disbursed |

### E.3 Exercise (c) — the same sale, closed by a title company

> *"**Repeat exercise (a) above where the broker holds an earnest money deposit but the CLOSING IS PERFORMED
> BY A TITLE COMPANY.**"*

**What changes, and it is the point of the exercise:** the brokerage's records now show **a receipt of
earnest money and a single disbursement of it to the closing entity** — not the whole closing.

**Rule 5.12.B**: the firm **may transfer the earnest money from its trust or escrow account to a lawyer or
a closing entity closing the transaction**, and **must obtain a dated and signed receipt** from the person
or entity providing settlement services.

**And Rule 6.19.A does not change.** The broker with the brokerage relationship is *"**responsible for the
proper closing of the transaction**"* **whoever conducts it**, must ensure the consumer receives a closing
statement that is **accurate, complete and detailed and signed by the broker**, and must **deliver closing
statements and other closing documents to the brokerage firm immediately following closing.**

**A student who thinks a title company closing means the brokerage has no records has misunderstood the
exercise.** The trust account entries are fewer; **the responsibility is the same.**

### E.4 Exercise (d) — software, backup and imaging

> *"**Proper backup and printouts for software accounting systems, CD-ROM imaging of records.**"*

**The outline was written in 1996 and updated in 1999, and its media references show it.** What the
requirement is about has not changed: **records kept in a system must be producible, and must survive the
system.**

**Rule 5.14.E** permits the reconciliation reports, ledgers, journals and bank statements to be **kept
electronically.** **Rule 6.24** governs **electronic records and production of records.** And **Rule 5.21**
supplies the test that matters:

> *"A Broker and Brokerage Firm **must PRODUCE FOR INSPECTION by the Commission any document or record as
> may be reasonably necessary for investigation or audit** ... **Failure to submit such documents or records
> within the time set by the Commission in its notification will be GROUNDS FOR DISCIPLINARY ACTION** unless
> the Commission has granted an extension of time."*

**What the student produces:** a demonstration that the system's **journal, ledgers and reconciliation
reports can be produced on demand**, that **backups exist and have been tested**, and that the records
remain available for the **four years Rule 6.20 requires** — including after a change of software.

**"We can't get at those records any more" is not an answer to a Commission notification**, and **Rule 5.21
makes the failure itself the ground.**

## F. Method of accounting and reporting

> **Rule 5.16 — Method of Accounting.** *"In the absence of a written agreement to the contrary, the **'CASH
> BASIS' of accounting MUST BE USED** for maintaining all required Trust or Escrow Accounts and
> corresponding records. A Brokerage Firm **may use another method of accounting IF IT IS AGREED UPON IN
> WRITING** by the Brokerage Firm and the beneficiary. The Brokerage Firm **must maintain SEPARATE Trust and
> Escrow Accounts and corresponding records for each beneficiary using a different accounting method.**"*
>
> **Rule 5.15.A — Reports to beneficiaries.** Prepared and delivered **according to the terms of the
> Management Agreement**; *"**in the absence of a provision in the Management Agreement to the contrary,
> Brokerage Firms must deliver these reports WITHIN THIRTY (30) DAYS after the end of the month in which
> funds were either received or disbursed.**"*

**Cash basis is the default and a different method needs the beneficiary's written agreement — and its own
separate accounts and records.** A firm that lets one owner use accrual has just created a second account
and a second set of records.
