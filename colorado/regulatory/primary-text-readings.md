> ### The finding that makes Colorado different from every other state in this project
>
> **The regulator publishes its own course outlines, and this build is written to them.**
>
> For Arizona, this project recorded as its first submission gate that **the Department's prescribed
> prelicensure course content was not obtained.** For Washington, the same gate — WAC 308-124H-810 requires
> an approved school to use the Department's prescribed curriculum, and the Department's curriculum was not
> obtained. **Colorado publishes all six.** Each names its **course objective**, its **major topics**, and
> **the hours allocated to each topic.** They were downloaded and read in full.
>
> That changes what this build is. Where the Arizona modules are written from statute and rule and must
> later be conformed to content nobody outside the Department has seen, **the Colorado modules are written
> to the Commission's own outline, topic by topic and hour by hour**, and a reviewer can check them against
> a public document.

## 1. The education requirement — 168 hours, six courses

### C.R.S. § 12-10-203(4)(a) — the statute

> *"An applicant for a broker's license shall be **at least eighteen years of age**. The applicant must
> furnish proof satisfactory to the commission that the applicant has **either received a degree from an
> accredited degree-granting college or university with a major course of study in real estate** or has
> **successfully completed courses of study, approved by the commission**, at any accredited college or
> university or any private occupational school ... as follows:*
>
> **(I)** *"**Forty-eight hours** of classroom instruction or equivalent correspondent hours in **real
> estate law and real estate practice**; and*
>
> **(II)** *"**Forty-eight hours** of classroom instruction or equivalent correspondent hours in
> **understanding and preparation of Colorado real estate contracts**; and*
>
> **(III)** *"**A total of seventy-two hours** of instruction or equivalent correspondence hours from the
> following areas of study:*
> **(A)** *"Trust accounts and record keeping;*
> **(B)** *"Real estate closings;*
> **(C)** *"Current legal issues; and*
> **(D)** *"Practical applications."*

**48 + 48 + 72 = 168.** Note what the statute does and does not do: it fixes **48 for law and practice**,
**48 for contracts**, and **72 for the other four together** — and it **allocates no hours among those
four.** The statute names the four subjects and leaves their division to the Commission.

### 4 CCR 725-1, Rule 2.1.A.2 — the Commission's allocation

> *"Proof of completion of **one hundred sixty-eight (168) hours** of classroom instruction or equivalent
> Distance Learning hours from any accredited college or university, or any Real Estate School for the
> following courses:*
> **a.** *"Real Estate Law and Real Estate Practice: **48 hours**;*
> **b.** *"Colorado Real Estate Contracts: **48 hours**;*
> **c.** *"Real Estate Closings: **not less than 24 hours**;*
> **d.** *"Trust Accounts and Record Keeping: **not less than 8 hours**;*
> **e.** *"Current Legal Issues: **not less than 8 hours**; and*
> **f.** *"Practical Application: **not less than 32 hours**."*

**Read the asymmetry, because it is deliberate and this build records it.** The first two are stated as
exact figures — *"48 hours."* The other four are stated as **floors** — *"not less than."* 24 + 8 + 8 + 32
is exactly the 72 the statute requires, so a course built to the floors delivers exactly 168; a course that
exceeds any floor delivers more than 168 and still satisfies the rule.

**The rule's course names differ slightly from the outlines' titles**, and this build records both:

| Rule 2.1.A.2 name | Outline title | Hours |
|---|---|---|
| Real Estate Law and Real Estate Practice | **Real Estate Law and Practice** | 48 |
| Colorado Real Estate Contracts | **Contracts and Regulations** | 48 |
| Real Estate Closings | **Closings** | not less than 24 |
| Trust Accounts and Record Keeping | **Record Keeping and Trust Accounts** | not less than 8 |
| Current Legal Issues | **Current Legal Issues** | not less than 8 |
| Practical Application | **Practical Applications** | not less than 32 |

### § 12-10-203(4)(b) and (c) — what an out-of-state licensee still owes

> **(b)** *"An applicant for a broker's license who has been **licensed as a real estate broker in another
> jurisdiction** shall be required to complete **only** the course of study comprising the subject matter
> areas described in **subsections (4)(a)(II) and (4)(a)(III)(B)**."*
>
> **(c)** *"An applicant for a broker's license who has been **licensed as a real estate salesperson in
> another jurisdiction** shall be required to complete **only** the course of study required in
> **subsections (4)(a)(II) and (4)(a)(III)**."*

**Translated into course names:**

| Applicant | Must still complete |
|---|---|
| **Licensed BROKER elsewhere** | **Contracts (48)** and **Closings (24)** — 72 hours |
| **Licensed SALESPERSON elsewhere** | **Contracts (48)** and **all 72 hours** of the four-subject group — 120 hours |
| **No out-of-state licence** | **All 168** |

**Colorado's unwaivable core is the Contracts course.** Every applicant completes it, whatever they hold
elsewhere. Compare Arizona, whose unwaivable block is a 27-hour Arizona-specific course; **Colorado's is
the 48-hour contracts course plus, for a broker, closings.**

### Rule 2.2 — the examination

> **A.** The Real Estate Licensing Examination is **administered and developed by a third party testing
> service** and consists of **two parts**: *"a **national part**; and a **Colorado part**."*
>
> **C.** *"Educational requirements as set forth in Rule 2.1.A. **must be completed and proof filed** in a
> manner as prescribed by the Commission **prior to taking** the Real Estate Licensing Examination and
> applying for an Associate Broker level license."*
>
> **E.** An applicant who fails one or both parts **may retake the failed part(s)**.
>
> **F.** *"A passing score for either part ... is **valid for one (1) year**. Failure to submit a complete
> application within one (1) year will result in the examination grade being invalid."*
>
> **G.** The Commission **will not certify examination results** to any person, state or agency for anyone
> who is not or has not been licensed as a Broker.

**Education comes first, and proof of it is filed before the examination.** That is the reverse of several
states in this project, and it means a Colorado school's completion record is a document the applicant
needs before they can sit.

**§ 12-10-203(5)(a)** adds what the examination covers, and one item worth naming: *"The examination for a
broker's license shall **also include the preparation of a real estate closing statement**."* The
Commission *"shall have the authority to **set the minimum passing score**"* — and **this build states no
passing score for the state examination, because the rules read state none.**

### Rule 2.1.D — course audits

> *"The Commission **may audit courses** set forth in subsection A.2. and B. of this Rule **at any time and
> at no cost.** The Commission may request **all instructional materials and student attendance records** ...
> The purpose of the audit is to ensure **adherence to the approved course of study** ... **Failure to
> comply with statutes and these Rules may result in the removal of the course provider, instructor, and/or
> the course from the approved provider list.**"*

## 3. Brokerage relationships — C.R.S. Part 4

This is where Colorado differs most sharply from the other states in this project, and the differences are
in the statute's own sentences.

### 3.1 The default is TRANSACTION-BROKER — § 12-10-403(1) and (2)

> **(1)** *"When engaged in any of the activities enumerated in section 12-10-201(6), a broker may act in
> any transaction as a **single agent or transaction-broker**."*
>
> **(2)** *"**A broker shall be considered a transaction-broker unless a single agency relationship is
> established through a written agreement between the broker and the party or parties to be represented by
> the broker.**"*

**Agency in Colorado is opt-in, in writing.** A broker who signs nothing is a transaction-broker. Compare
**Washington**, where RCW 18.86.020(1) makes **buyer agency the default** arising from performing services;
**Colorado's default is the non-agency relationship**, and a student who arrives with another state's
assumption has it backwards.

### 3.2 DUAL AGENCY IS PROHIBITED — § 12-10-406, in a single sentence

> **§ 12-10-406. Dual agent.** *"**A broker shall not establish dual agency with any seller, landlord,
> buyer, or tenant.**"*

That is the whole section. **No consent cures it, because the statute offers no consent mechanism.**

| State in this project | Dual agency |
|---|---|
| **Colorado** | **Prohibited outright** — § 12-10-406 |
| **Washington** | **Limited dual agency permitted** with written consent separately initialed — RCW 18.86.020(2)(b)(iv) |
| **Arizona** | Permitted with the **prior written consent of all parties** — A.A.C. R4-28-1101(F) |

**Rule 6.7** states how Colorado handles a broker on both sides without dual agency:

> *"**Neither Brokers nor Teams may enter into a Brokerage Relationship with one Consumer as a Single Agent
> and the other Consumer as a Single Agent or Transaction-Broker in the same transaction.**"* If properly
> disclosed in writing, the broker or team working with both consumers may do so as:
> **A.** *"A **Transaction-Broker for both** Consumers to the transaction;"*
> **B.** *"A **Transaction-Broker for one** Consumer ... and **treating the other Consumer as a
> Customer**;"* or
> **C.** *"A **Single Agent for one** Consumer and **treating the other Consumer as a Customer**."*

### 3.3 DESIGNATED BROKERAGE — the relationship does not extend to the firm

> **§ 12-10-403(6)(a)**: If a brokerage firm has more than one licensed natural person, the employing
> broker or an individual broker **shall be designated** to work with the seller, landlord, buyer or tenant
> as a **designated broker**.
>
> **(6)(b)**: *"The brokerage relationship established between the seller, landlord, buyer, or tenant and a
> designated broker, including the duties, obligations, and responsibilities of that relationship, **shall
> not extend to the employing broker nor to any other broker employed or engaged by that employing broker
> who has not been so designated and shall not extend to the firm**, partnership, limited liability
> company, association, corporation, or other entity that employs the broker."*
>
> **(6)(c)**: A firm may have **designated brokers working as single agents for both sides of the same
> transaction without creating dual agency** for the employing broker or any other broker.
>
> **§ 12-10-408(2)(e)(II)**: *"Nothing in this subsection (2)(e) shall be construed to **limit the
> employing broker's or firm's responsibility to supervise** licensees employed by the broker or firm nor
> to **shield the broker or firm from vicarious liability**."*

**Compare Arizona directly.** A.A.C. R4-28-1103(D) makes *"an employing broker responsible for the acts of
all licensees and other employees."* **Colorado's statute says the brokerage RELATIONSHIP does not extend
to the firm — and then says in the next breath that this does not limit supervision or shield the firm from
vicarious liability.** Two different things, and a module that merges them misstates both.

### 3.4 The single agent duty lists — §§ 12-10-404 and 12-10-405

**Both begin the same way:** *"A broker engaged by a [seller or landlord / buyer or tenant] ... **is a
LIMITED AGENT** with the following duties and obligations."*

> **(1)(a)** To **perform the terms of the written agreement**;
> **(1)(b)** To **exercise reasonable skill and care**;
> **(1)(c)** To **promote the interests of the [party] with the UTMOST GOOD FAITH, LOYALTY, AND FIDELITY**,
> including:
> **(I)** Seeking a price and terms acceptable to the party — *"except that the broker **shall not be
> obligated to seek additional offers** to purchase the property while the property is subject to a contract
> for sale"* (seller side) / *"shall not be obligated to seek other properties while the buyer is a party to
> a contract to purchase"* (buyer side);
> **(II)** **Presenting all offers to and from the party in a timely manner REGARDLESS of whether the
> property is subject to a contract** for sale or lease;
> **(III)** Disclosing to the party **adverse material facts actually known** by the broker;
> **(IV)** **Counseling** the party as to material benefits or risks actually known;
> **(V)** **Advising the party to obtain expert advice** as to material matters beyond the broker's
> expertise;
> **(VI)** **Accounting in a timely manner** for all money and property received; and
> **(VII)** **Informing the party that they shall NOT be vicariously liable** for the agent's acts not
> approved, directed or ratified by them;
> **(1)(d)** To comply with article 10 and its rules; and
> **(1)(e)** To comply with applicable federal, state or local law **including fair housing and civil
> rights statutes**.

**Note the tension between (c)(I) and (c)(II), because it is the point students miss.** The broker **need
not seek additional offers** once under contract — but **must present all offers regardless.** Not seeking
and not presenting are different acts, and the statute permits the first and forbids the second.

**§ 12-10-404(2) and § 12-10-405(2) — the five items not disclosed without informed consent:**

| Seller's/landlord's agent — (2) | Buyer's/tenant's agent — (2) |
|---|---|
| That the seller or landlord **will accept less** than the asking price or lease rate | That the buyer or tenant **will pay more** than the price or lease rate offered |
| The party's **motivating factors** | The party's **motivating factors** |
| That the party **will agree to financing terms other than those offered** | That the party **will agree to financing terms other than those offered** |
| **Any material information about the party** unless disclosure is required by law or failure to disclose would constitute **fraud or dishonest dealing** | The same |
| Any facts or suspicions regarding circumstances that may **psychologically impact or stigmatize** the property **pursuant to § 38-35.5-101** | The same |

**§ 12-10-404(3)(a) — what a seller's agent still owes the other side:**

> *"A broker acting as a seller's or landlord's agent **owes no duty or obligation to the buyer or
> tenant**; except that a broker shall, subject to the limitations of section 38-35.5-101, ... **disclose to
> any prospective buyer or tenant ALL ADVERSE MATERIAL FACTS ACTUALLY KNOWN by the broker** ... including
> but not limited to adverse material facts pertaining to **the title and the physical condition of the
> property, any material defects in the property, and any environmental hazards** affecting the property
> that are required by law to be disclosed."*

**§ 12-10-405(3)(a) — the mirror, and it is not symmetrical:**

> A buyer's or tenant's agent owes no duty to the seller or landlord *"except that the broker shall
> **disclose to any prospective seller or landlord all adverse material facts actually known** by the broker
> **including but not limited to adverse material facts concerning the buyer's or tenant's financial ability
> to perform the terms of the transaction and whether the buyer intends to occupy the property to be
> purchased as a principal residence.**"*

**"Actually known" is the standard on both sides**, and **§§ 12-10-404(3)(b) and 12-10-405(5) both provide
that the agent owes NO DUTY to conduct an independent inspection** or to independently verify statements
made by a party or an independent inspector.

**§ 38-35.5-101 was NOT read by this project**, and **no module states what it provides.** It is named here
only because the duty sections cite it.

### 3.5 The transaction-broker — § 12-10-407

> **(1)** *"**A broker engaged as a transaction-broker is NOT AN AGENT for either party.**"*

**(2) The obligations and responsibilities**, which are owed to **both** parties:

> **(a)** Perform the terms of any **written or oral** agreement made with any party;
> **(b)** Exercise **reasonable skill and care as a transaction-broker**, including:
> **(I)** **Presenting all offers and counteroffers in a timely manner regardless** of whether the property
> is subject to a contract for sale or lease or letter of intent;
> **(II)** **Advising the parties** regarding the transaction and **suggesting that the parties obtain
> expert advice** as to material matters known to the broker but beyond the broker's expertise;
> **(III)** **Accounting in a timely manner** for all money and property received;
> **(IV)** **Keeping the parties fully informed** regarding the transaction;
> **(V)** **Assisting the parties in complying** with the terms and conditions of any contract including
> closing the transaction;
> **(VI)** **Disclosing to all prospective buyers or tenants any adverse material facts actually known** by
> the broker, including facts pertaining to title, physical condition, defects, and environmental hazards
> required by law to be disclosed;
> **(VII)** **Disclosing to any prospective seller or landlord all adverse material facts actually known**,
> including facts pertaining to **the buyer's or tenant's financial ability to perform** and **the buyer's
> intent to occupy the property as a principal residence**; and
> **(VIII)** **Informing the parties that they shall not be vicariously liable** for the transaction-broker's
> acts;
> **(c)** Comply with article 10 and its rules; and
> **(d)** Comply with applicable federal, state and local law **including fair housing and civil rights**.

**(3) The six items a transaction-broker shall not disclose without the INFORMED CONSENT OF ALL PARTIES** —
the union of the two single-agent lists, plus a sixth:

> **(a)** That a buyer or tenant **will pay more**;
> **(b)** That a seller or landlord **will accept less**;
> **(c)** The **motivating factors** for any party;
> **(d)** That any party **will agree to financing terms other than those offered**;
> **(e)** Facts or suspicions regarding circumstances that may **psychologically impact or stigmatize** the
> property pursuant to § 38-35.5-101; or
> **(f)** **Any material information about the other party** unless disclosure is required by law or failure
> to disclose would constitute **fraud or dishonest dealing**.

**(4) through (8) — what a transaction-broker may do and what does not happen:**

> **(4)** No duty to conduct an independent **inspection of the property**, and no duty to verify statements
> made by the seller, landlord or independent inspectors.
> **(5)** No duty to conduct an independent **investigation of the buyer's or tenant's financial
> condition**.
> **(6)** May, **without breaching any obligation**: show alternative properties; list competing properties;
> show properties to other prospective buyers; and serve as single agent or transaction-broker for the same
> or different parties in **other** transactions.
> **(7)** *"**There shall be NO IMPUTATION OF KNOWLEDGE OR INFORMATION between any party and the
> transaction-broker or among persons within an entity engaged as a transaction-broker.**"*
> **(8)** May cooperate with other brokers but *"**shall not engage or create any subagents.**"*

**Subagency is prohibited across all three relationships.** § 12-10-404(5), § 12-10-405(6) and
§ 12-10-407(8) each say so in the same words.

### 3.6 Disclosures — § 12-10-408 and Rule 6.5

> **(1)(a)** Every broker *"**shall adopt a WRITTEN OFFICE POLICY that identifies and describes the
> relationships offered to the public by the broker.**"*
> **(1)(b)** *"A broker **shall not be required to offer or engage in any one or in all** of the brokerage
> relationships enumerated in section 12-10-404, 12-10-405, or 12-10-407."*
> **(1)(c)** Written disclosures and agreements *"**shall contain a statement ... that different brokerage
> relationships are available that include buyer agency, seller agency, or status as a transaction-broker.**"*
> If a consumer asks about a relationship the broker does not offer, *"the broker shall provide to the party
> a **written definition of that brokerage relationship that has been promulgated by the real estate
> commission**."*
>
> **(2)(a)(I)** *"**Prior to engaging in any of the activities** enumerated in section 12-10-201(6), a
> transaction-broker **shall disclose in writing** to the party to be assisted **that the broker is not
> acting as agent** for the party and that the broker is acting as a transaction-broker."*
> **(2)(a)(II)** The disclosure **shall contain a signature block** for acknowledgment of receipt. *"The
> disclosure and acknowledgment, **by itself, shall not constitute a contract** with the broker. **If the
> [party] chooses not to sign the acknowledgment, the broker shall note that fact on a copy of the
> disclosure and shall retain the copy.**"*
> **(2)(a)(III)** Obligations **in addition to or different from** § 12-10-407 must be **disclosed in a
> writing signed by the involved parties**.
> **(2)(b)** A broker intending to establish **single agency** shall, **before engaging in any activity**,
> **enter into a WRITTEN AGENCY AGREEMENT** disclosing the § 12-10-404 or § 12-10-405 duties. Notice of the
> relationship **shall be furnished to any prospective party in a timely manner**.
> **(2)(c)(I)** A broker intending to **work with a buyer or tenant as the seller's agent** shall provide a
> written disclosure containing: **(A)** a statement that the broker **is the seller's agent and not the
> buyer's**; **(B)** **a list of the tasks the agent intends to perform** for the seller with the buyer; and
> **(C)** a statement that the buyer **shall not be vicariously liable** for the agent's acts unless they
> approve, direct or ratify them.
> **(2)(d)** A broker who has already established a relationship with one party *"**shall advise at the
> earliest reasonable opportunity** any other potential parties or their agents of the established
> relationship."*
> **(2)(e)(I)** The written agreement **shall advise** that the relationship **exists only with the
> designated broker**, does not extend to the employing broker or other non-designated brokers, and **does
> not extend to the brokerage company**.

**Rule 6.5.A** fixes the moment:

> *"Written disclosures pursuant to section 12-10-408, C.R.S. must be made to a Consumer **prior to
> eliciting or discussing confidential information** from a Consumer for Real Estate Brokerage Services."*
>
> **6.5.B**: *"Such activities **do not include preliminary conversations or 'small talk'** concerning price
> range, location and property styles, or responding to general factual questions from a potential Consumer
> concerning properties which have been Advertised for sale or lease."*

**Rule 6.8 — ministerial tasks**, which a broker may perform for a **customer** after proper disclosure:

> *"showing a property, **preparing as a scrivener**, and conveying written offers and counteroffers, making
> known the different types of financing alternatives, and providing information related to professional,
> governmental, and community services ... Performing ministerial tasks **will not of themselves violate the
> terms of any relationship** ... and **will not create an agency or Transaction-Broker relationship with
> the Customer being assisted.**"*

**Rule 6.9 — Change of Status**: a broker or team changing from single agent for one consumer to
transaction-broker for both must provide the **Commission-approved "Change of Status" form** to the consumer
whose relationship changed, *"**at the time the Broker begins to assist both Consumers** as a
Transaction-Broker, **but not later than at the time the Consumer signs the contract.**"*

### 3.7 Duration — § 12-10-409

> **(1)(a)** The relationship **commences when the broker is engaged** and continues until **performance or
> completion** of the agreement.
> **(1)(b)** If not performed or completed for any reason, it ends at the **earlier** of: **(I)** any agreed
> expiration date; **(II)** any termination or relinquishment by the parties; or **(III)** **one year after
> the date of the engagement.**
> **(2)(b)** After termination, expiration or completion, a **single agent** remains responsible for:
> **(I)** **accounting** for all money and property related to and received during the engagement; and
> **(II)** **keeping confidential all information received during the engagement that was made confidential
> by request or instructions from the engaging party** — unless the party consents in writing, disclosure is
> required by law, or the information becomes public by the party's own words or conduct or from a source
> other than the broker.
> **(3)** A **transaction-broker** owes no further obligation after termination *"except that the broker
> shall account for all money and property related to and received during the engagement."*

**The one-year backstop in (1)(b)(III) is Colorado's own**, and it runs whether or not anyone remembers to
end the engagement.

**Note the asymmetry in (2)(b) and (3):** a single agent's **confidentiality survives**; the
transaction-broker section carries **only the accounting obligation.**

### 3.8 Compensation — § 12-10-410

> **(1)** Compensation may be paid by **the seller, the buyer, the landlord, the tenant, a third party, or
> by sharing or splitting** between brokers.
> **(2)** *"**Payment of compensation shall not be construed to establish an agency relationship** between
> the broker and the party who paid the compensation."*
> **(5)** *"A buyer's or tenant's agent shall obtain the **WRITTEN APPROVAL** of the buyer or tenant
> **before** the agent may propose to the seller's or landlord's agent that the buyer's or tenant's agent be
> compensated by sharing compensation paid by the seller or landlord."*
> **(6)** *"**Prior to entering into a brokerage or listing agreement or a contract to buy, sell, or lease,
> the identity of those parties, persons, or entities paying compensation or commissions to any broker shall
> be disclosed to the parties to the transaction.**"*
> **(7)** A broker may be **compensated by more than one party** if those parties **consented in writing
> prior to entering into a contract** to buy, sell or lease.

**§ 12-10-411 — Violations:** a violation of Part 4 *"constitutes an act pursuant to section
12-10-217(1)(m) for which the real estate commission may investigate and take administrative action."*
**§§ 12-10-217 and 12-10-219 were NOT read**, and **no penalty, sanction or procedure is stated anywhere in
this build.**

## 4. Trust accounts and record keeping — 4 CCR 725-1 Chapter 5

The Commission's 8-hour outline names Rules **5.2, 5.8.A, 5.21, 6.3.B and 6.25.A** by number. All of
Chapter 5 was read; the rules that carry a figure or a prohibition are transcribed here.

### 4.1 Separation and identification

> **5.1** Any brokerage firm or broker receiving **Money Belonging to Others** must establish **written
> accounting control policies and procedures**, including adequate checks and balances over the financial
> activities of the broker, firm **and unlicensed persons**, and manage the risk of fraud or illegal acts.
>
> **5.2** *"**All Money Belonging to Others** accepted by a Broker or Brokerage Firm **must be kept separate
> from other money** belonging to the Broker or Brokerage Firm and **must be deposited into one or more of
> the Broker's or Brokerage Firm's Trust or Escrow Accounts.**"* The fiduciary nature of each account must
> be identified in the deposit agreement with a Recognized Depository **by use of the word "trust" or
> "escrow"** plus a label identifying its purpose — *"sales escrow", "rental escrow", "security deposit
> escrow"* — and a copy of each executed deposit agreement retained for inspection.
>
> **5.4** The number of accounts *"may vary from Zero to Unlimited"* — *"**If the Brokerage Firm is not in
> possession of Money Belonging to Others, there is no obligation to maintain a separate Trust or Escrow
> Account.**"*
>
> **5.5** A firm engaging in property management **must deposit rental receipts and security deposits in
> SEPARATE accounts — a minimum of one for rental receipts and a minimum of one for security deposits.**
>
> **5.6** Unless otherwise agreed in writing, the money **must not be invested in any account, security or
> certificate of deposit with a fixed maturity or any fee or penalty for early withdrawal.**

### 4.2 The deposit deadlines — Rule 5.7

| Money | Deadline | Measured from |
|---|---|---|
| **Property management** money | **No later than 5 BUSINESS DAYS** | receipt of funds **or mutual execution of a lease, whichever is later** |
| **All other** Money Belonging to Others | **No later than 3 BUSINESS DAYS** | receipt of funds **or mutual execution of contract, whichever is later** |

**Business days, not calendar days**, and the clock starts at **the later** of the two events.

### 4.3 Security deposits — Rule 5.8

> **A. Owner-held.** A firm receipting for security deposits **will not deliver them to an owner without the
> tenant's written authorization in a lease or unless written notice has been given to the tenant** — notice
> given so the tenant knows **who holds the deposit** and **the procedure for requesting its return.**
>
> **B. New property management company.** A firm beginning management of a property most recently managed by
> another firm **must disclose to the owner and current tenant, in writing, WITHIN 30 DAYS** after execution
> or assignment of a management agreement, the status of any security deposit held by the previous firm. The
> **previous firm must transfer the deposit WITHIN 60 DAYS** and confirm the amount to owner and tenant. The
> new firm **must verify each deposit matches the amount in the current lease and disclose any discrepancy**
> to owner and tenant, and **must inform the tenant in writing if the owner is holding the deposit.**

### 4.4 The two prohibitions — Rules 5.9 and 5.10

> **5.9 Diversion and Conversion Prohibited.** *"Money Belonging to Others belonging to **one beneficiary
> must not be used for the benefit of another beneficiary.** Money Belonging to Others **must not be used
> for the benefit of the Brokerage Firm or Broker.**"*
>
> **5.10 Commingling Prohibited.** *"A Broker's or Brokerage Firm's **personal or business operating funds
> must not be commingled** with Money Belonging to Others."* Subject to:
> **A.** Money that becomes **due and payable to the firm must be withdrawn MONTHLY.**
> **B.** Money **advanced by the firm for the benefit of another** may be placed in the account, identified
> as an advance, and withdrawn **only on behalf of that person** — recorded in the journal and the ledger and
> disclosed in the accounting.
> **C.** Absent a specific written agreement to the contrary, commissions, fees and other charges are
> *"**'earned' and available for use by the Brokerage Firm only after ALL contracted services have been
> performed and there is NO REMAINING RIGHT OF RECALL by others** for such money."*
> **D.** *"A Brokerage Firm **may deposit personal funds as may be required to pay any bank charges**
> incurred in connection with maintaining a Trust or Escrow Account **without violating Rule 5.10**"* — with
> an entry in the journal and the ledger. *(stated at Rule 5.14.D)*

### 4.5 Earnest money — Rules 5.12 and 5.13

> **5.12.A** Any broker receiving earnest money **must deliver it to the earnest money holder to be
> deposited in accordance with the contract**, and **must obtain a dated and signed receipt** from the
> person or entity instructed to receive it.
> **5.12.B** If the firm holds it, it is deposited under **Rule 5.7.B** (3 business days). The firm may
> transfer it to a lawyer or closing entity, and **must obtain a dated and signed receipt.**
> **5.13** If a **promissory note** is received as earnest money, **the seller must be informed of the date
> it becomes due**, and if payment is not made by that date the broker **must promptly notify the seller and
> deliver the original promissory note.**

### 4.6 The records themselves — Rule 5.14

> **A. The journal** — records **in chronological order** all Money Belonging to Others received or
> disbursed. For **funds received**: date of receipt and deposit; name of the person giving the money; name
> of the person and property for which received; purpose; amount; **and a resulting cash balance.** For
> **funds disbursed**: date of payment; check number or electronic transfer record; name of payee; a
> reference to vendor documentation verifying purpose; amount; **and a resulting cash balance.**
>
> **B. The ledger** — records in chronological order all money received or disbursed **on behalf of each
> particular beneficiary**, segregated from other beneficiaries, with the same transactional information.
> *"**No ledger may ever be allowed to have a negative cash balance.** The **sum of all ledger balances must
> agree AT ALL TIMES with the corresponding journal after each transaction has been posted.**"*
>
> **C. Three-way reconciliation** *"**must be performed MONTHLY to show that on the same date the cash
> balance shown in the journal, the sum of the cash balances for all ledgers, and the reconciled bank
> balance are the same.** A three-way reconciliation report must be completed and maintained monthly."* The
> broker **need not reconcile an account holding no Money Belonging to Others.**
>
> **E.** The reports, ledgers, journals and bank statements **may be kept electronically.**

**The Commission's own course outline states the same identity in its own notation:** *"Journal Balance +
Sum of Ledger Balances = Bank Balance = Transaction File Liabilities."*

### 4.7 Reporting, method and mark-ups — Rules 5.15 to 5.20

> **5.15.A** Reports to beneficiaries are prepared and delivered **according to the management agreement**;
> in the absence of a provision to the contrary, **within 30 DAYS after the end of the month** in which
> funds were received or disbursed.
> **5.16** *"In the absence of a written agreement to the contrary, the **'CASH BASIS' of accounting must be
> used**"*; another method may be used **if agreed in writing**, with separate accounts and records for each
> beneficiary using a different method.
> **5.17 Mark-Ups.** The broker and firm **must obtain PRIOR WRITTEN CONSENT from the Consumer they
> represent** to assess and receive mark-ups or other compensation for services performed, **regardless of
> whether it benefits the broker or a third party**, and **must disclose all mark-ups paid for by any
> Customer** — retaining records verifying disclosure and consent and fully accounting for amounts.
> **5.18** Any instrument, equity or Thing of Value **taken in lieu of cash must be held by the brokerage
> firm, except as otherwise agreed.**
> **5.20.A** Money collected **before services are performed** must be deposited in a trust or escrow
> account, and **no money withdrawn except for authorized expenses** of performing them.
> **5.20.B** A **non-refundable retainer** need not be deposited **provided it is specifically agreed to in
> writing.**
> **5.21** A broker and firm **must produce for inspection by the Commission any document or record**
> reasonably necessary for investigation or audit; failure within the time set **is grounds for disciplinary
> action** absent an extension.
> **5.22** *"The **Employing Broker or Independent Broker are held JOINTLY RESPONSIBLE with the Brokerage
> Firm** in complying with Chapter 5."*

## 5. Practice standards — 4 CCR 725-1 Chapter 6

### 5.1 Competency — Rule 6.2

> **A.** To conduct real estate brokerage services a broker **must possess the necessary experience,
> training, and knowledge** and maintain compliance with applicable law.
> **B.** If the broker does not have them, the broker **must**:
> **1.** *"**Decline** to provide Real Estate Brokerage Services;"*
> **2.** *"**Obtain** the necessary experience, training, and knowledge;"*
> **3.** *"**Obtain the assistance** of their Employing Broker, Supervisory Broker, a Broker who meets the
> requirements ... or **legal counsel that is competent in the matter**;"* or
> **4.** *"**Co-list with another Broker** who meets the requirements."*

**Four routes, and the first is to decline.** Compare Arizona's R4-28-1101(H), which offers two routes:
engage a competent person, or disclose the lack of expertise in writing first and be employed after.

### 5.2 Supervision — Rule 6.3

> **A.1** Employing brokers *"**must exercise authority, direction, and supervision** over any Associate
> Brokers shown in the records of the Commission as supervised by the Employing Broker to ensure conformance
> to the Commission statutes and these Rules"* — and *"**whenever a complaint is filed with the Commission
> against an Associate Broker, the Commission MAY INVESTIGATE whether there have been violations of section
> 12-10-217(1)(r) by the EMPLOYING BROKER.**"*
> **A.2** Employing brokers **must also supervise all unlicensed employees**, including unlicensed on-site
> managers, secretaries, bookkeepers and personal assistants of associate brokers.
> **B.** Employing brokers must: **1.** maintain all trust and escrow accounts and records under Chapter 5;
> **2.** maintain all transaction records under Rule 6.20; **3.** develop the firm's written policies under
> Rule 6.4; and **4.** provide a **"Reasonable-Level of Supervision"** for all associate brokers.

### 5.3 Square footage — Rule 6.11

> When a broker **advertises the square footage** of a residential property, including for submission to a
> multiple listing service, the broker **must disclose the SOURCE of the square footage of the floor space
> of the living area** to consumers **on the Commission-Approved Form**.
>
> **A. Broker measurement.** *"**A Broker is not required to measure** the square footage of a property. If
> the Broker takes an actual measurement, **it does not have to be exact**; however, the Broker's objective
> **must be to measure accurately and calculate competently in a manner that is not misleading**"* — and the
> broker must **1.** disclose the **standard, methodology or manner** used; **2.** advise that it is **for
> marketing and not for loan, valuation or any other purpose**; and **3.** advise that **if exact square
> footage is a concern the property should be independently measured.**
>
> **B. Other sources.** Any other source — actual measurement, building plans, prior appraisals, assessor's
> office — **must include the date of issuance, if any**, and be **disclosed in writing in a timely manner
> on the Commission-Approved Form**. *"A Broker **may not provide information to a person from a source
> known to be unreliable** and **is responsible for indicating obvious mismeasurement by others.**"*

### 5.4 CMA and BPO — Rule 6.12

> When a broker prepares a CMA or BPO **for any reason other than the anticipated sale or purchase of the
> property**, the broker **must include a notice stating**:
>
> ***"This evaluation was prepared by a licensed real estate broker and is not an appraisal. This evaluation
> cannot be used for the purposes of obtaining financing."***
>
> Pursuant to § 12-10-602(9)(b)(II), *"**Brokers are prohibited from completing CMAs or BPOs that are used
> for the purpose of obtaining financing.**"* Preparation for reasons other than anticipated sale, purchase
> or lease **is not Real Estate Brokerage Services**, so compensation for it **is not required to be paid to
> the brokerage firm** unless the firm's office policy manual says otherwise.

### 5.5 Presenting offers — Rule 6.13

> *"A Broker must **present all offers to the other Consumer's Broker** if such other Consumer has an
> unexpired Listing Contract. If the Broker has made **reasonable, but unsuccessful, attempts** to present an
> offer to the other Consumer's Broker, the Broker must present the offer **to the other Consumer's Broker's
> Employing Broker**. If **no Employing Broker exists**, or if reasonable attempts to present the offer to
> the Employing Broker have failed, **the Broker may present the offer directly to the other Consumer.**"*

**A three-step ladder, and no clock.** Compare **Arizona's R4-28-1102(A)**, which prohibits contacting
another licensee's principal unless the designated broker, a delegated broker representative and the
licensee have **all been unavailable for 24 hours.** **Colorado's rule states "reasonable, but unsuccessful,
attempts" and names no period**, and **no module of this build supplies one.**

### 5.6 Conflicts of interest — Rule 6.17

> **A.** Firms and brokers have a **continuing duty to disclose, IN WRITING, any known Conflict of Interest**
> that may arise in the course of any real estate transaction.
> **B.** A broker who **sells, buys or leases on the broker's own account must disclose in the contracting
> instrument, or in a separate concurrent writing, that they are a licensed Broker.**
> **C.** A firm or broker engaged in **property management** must disclose in writing **any known conflict of
> interest in the selection or use of a business or vendor** providing services applicable to lease
> transactions including property maintenance — **any ownership, financial, or familial interest.**

### 5.7 Transaction files — Rule 6.20

> *"Both a Broker and a Brokerage Firm **must retain transaction files for all transactions for a period of
> FOUR (4) YEARS** beginning from **the consummation date of the transaction or the expiration date of any
> Listing Contracts that do not consummate.** Required documents in a transaction file are designated in the
> **Commission's Transaction File Checklist** and may be found on the Division's website."*
>
> A broker is **not** required to obtain and retain copies of **existing public records, title commitments,
> loan applications, lender required disclosures, or related affirmations from independent third party
> closing entities after the closing date.**

**The Transaction File Checklist itself was NOT obtained**, and no module of this build states its contents.

### 5.8 Referral fees — Rule 6.21

> **A.** Brokers and firms *"**will not pay or receive a referral fee except in accordance with RESPA** and
> unless a **reasonable cause for payment** of the referral fee exists pursuant to section 12-10-304(1)."*

**§ 12-10-304 was NOT read**, and RESPA's own text was not read for this build. **No module states a RESPA
requirement, exemption or penalty.**

## 6. Standard forms — 4 CCR 725-1 Chapter 7 and § 12-10-403(4)

> **§ 12-10-403(4)(a)** A broker, *"**whether acting as a single agent or transaction-broker, may complete
> standard forms** for use in a real estate transaction"* when performing licensed activities.
>
> **§ 12-10-403(4)(c)** *"**A broker shall use a COMMISSION-APPROVED FORM when such a form exists and is
> appropriate for the transaction.**"* ... *"In any transaction described in this subsection (4), the broker
> **shall advise the parties that the forms have important legal consequences and that the parties should
> consult legal counsel before signing** the forms."*

**Rule 7.1 defines the eight kinds of Standard Form.** The two that matter most to a student:

> **A. Commission-Approved Form** — promulgated by the Commission for current use. *"A Broker **must use a
> Commission-Approved Form when such form exists and is appropriate** for the transaction. The Broker **may
> advise the parties as to the effects thereof.**"*
>
> **B. Attorney Form** — drafted by a licensed Colorado attorney representing the broker, employing broker or
> firm. *"A Broker **may only use an Attorney Form if a Commission-Approved Form does not exist or is not
> appropriate** for the transaction. The form **must contain the language that says: 'This form has not been
> approved by the Colorado Real Estate Commission'.**"* It must name the attorney or firm that prepared it
> and the broker or firm for whom it was prepared, and *"**may not be altered by the Broker other than by
> completing any blank spaces.**"*
>
> **C. Client Form** — provided by a party the broker represents or works with; the broker must **retain
> written confirmation that the party provided it**, and use is **limited to inserting transaction-specific
> information.**
>
> **D. Government/Lender Form** — prescribed and mandated by an agency or regulated lender; use is again
> **limited to inserting transaction-specific information.**

**§ 12-10-403(4)(b)(V) to (VIII)** add: a form **approved in writing by the Colorado Bar Association** and
designated for broker use; a **disclosure-only form** that neither waives nor creates legal rights; a form
**prescribed by a title company providing closing services**; and a **letter of intent** created by the
broker **that states on its face that it is nonbinding and creates no legal rights or obligations.**

> ### The forms themselves were NOT obtained
>
> **No Commission-approved form was downloaded, read, or reproduced by this project.** The Contracts course
> outline requires students to complete **one of each of six listing and buyer brokerage families**, **one
> of each of four sales contract families**, and **one of each of the related forms** — and those are the
> Commission's own promulgated forms.
>
> **This is the largest submission gate in the Colorado build**, and it is different in kind from Arizona's.
> In Arizona no form is prescribed and the student drafts from scratch. **In Colorado the Commission
> promulgates the forms, the rule requires their use, and the outline requires the student to complete
> them** — so the Contracts and Closings courses cannot be delivered as the Commission specifies until the
> current forms are obtained from the Division's website and built into the course.

## Addendum — audit pass, 2026-08-21

Two items were checked against the Division's own pages after the build, because both are named in the
public state record and neither had been traced to a primary source.

### REC-33 — CONFIRMED from the Division's own page

The Division's **Real Estate Broker Application Instruction Guide** at
`dre.colorado.gov/division-programs/real-estate-broker/licensing/real-estate-broker-application-instruction-guide`
states, under **First Time Applicants**:

> *"In order to be eligible to apply for the broker credential from the Colorado Division of Real Estate you
> must: **complete 168 course work and obtain proof of completion, (REC-33 certificate(s)) from your course
> provider** or hold a degree with a major course of study in real estate, and take and pass both national
> and state portions of the Colorado Real Estate Broker's Exam. **Exam results are good for one year from
> the date of each portion's passing score.** PSI is the current exam vendor for the Division of Real
> Estate."*

**This confirms three things from the regulator directly:** the **168** figure the six courses total; that
**proof of completion is the REC-33 certificate**, issued **by the course provider**, not by the Division;
and that **exam results are good for one year from each portion's passing score.** It also names **PSI** as
the current exam vendor. **No passing score, item count, time limit or fee is stated on that page and none
is imported here.**

### The Online Course Approval Checklist — STILL NOT OBTAINED

The **Practical Applications (32 Hours) Qualifying Education Outline** requires that *"Courses taught online
must meet the requirements set forth in the Online Course Approval Checklist created by the Division of Real
Estate."* That qualifying-education checklist was searched for again and **was not found on the Division's
Broker Education Providers & Course Approval page.**

What that page does publish is a **different, continuing-education-scoped form** — the **REAL ESTATE BROKER
LIVE ONLINE VIRTUAL CLASSROOM CHECKLIST, Rev. Date 2025-05**, which by its own terms is submitted *"in
conjunction with a **Continuing Education Course Application** form"* and defines a live-online course as
*"a real-time, interactive program delivered by electronic devices that permit mutual webcam viewing of
faces, participation and communication between the presenters and students."* It requires **Zoom, WebEx or
an equivalent platform**, an **administrator monitoring the live active video feed of every student** (one
administrator for **fifty students or less**, **two** for more than fifty), an **active video feed
throughout the course to receive credit**, **registration in the licensed name**, and submission **at least
thirty days prior to the initial proposed course date**. It states that **classes are audited** and that
**failure to comply may result in suspension or revocation of course approval.**

**That form is CE-scoped and is NOT the qualifying-education checklist the Practical Applications outline
names.** None of its figures are imported into any qualifying-education module or into the public state
record. **The qualifying-education Online Course Approval Checklist remains an unclosed submission gate for
every one of the six courses delivered online**, and the public record now says so rather than asserting an
approved asynchronous delivery method.
