## A. Promissory notes

**The note is the promise to repay. It creates the DEBT.** The security instrument pledges the property and
creates the **lien**. Two documents, two functions — and a release of one does not by itself release the
other.

**What a note states:** the **principal**; the **interest rate**; the **payment amount and schedule**; the
**maturity date**; and the provisions the parties negotiated — **prepayment**, **late charges**,
**acceleration**, and what constitutes **default**.

**A note may be fully amortising, partially amortising with a balloon, or interest-only.** Which it is
determines what the borrower owes at maturity, and a buyer under an owner carry arrangement who has not
understood that a balloon is coming has not understood the transaction.

### A.1 The two Colorado rules about notes that WERE read

> **Rule 5.13 — a promissory note taken as earnest money.** *"If a promissory note is received as earnest
> money pursuant to an executed contract, **the seller must be informed of the date such promissory note
> becomes due.** If payment is not made by the due date of the promissory note, the Broker **must promptly
> notify the seller and deliver the original promissory note.**"*
>
> **Rule 5.18 — items in lieu of cash.** *"**Any instrument, equity, or Thing of Value taken in lieu of cash
> must be held by the Brokerage Firm, except as otherwise agreed.**"*

**The Record Keeping and Trust Accounts outline names this at its Topic III.B.1 — "Promissory Notes
(Safekeeping and Collection)"** — and that course carries it in full.

### A.2 Who prepares a note

**Not the broker, unless a Commission-approved form exists and is appropriate.** **§ 12-10-403(4)(b)**
lists the eight kinds of standard form a broker may complete; a note prepared for an owner carry
arrangement ordinarily comes from **counsel**, and **§ 12-10-403(4)(c)** requires the broker in any event to
**advise the parties that the forms have important legal consequences and that they should consult legal
counsel before signing.**

## B. Liens and encumbrances

**Every lien is an encumbrance; not every encumbrance is a lien.** A lien is a **claim securing payment of a
debt**. An encumbrance is **any claim, right or interest** held by someone other than the owner that
affects title or use — so easements, restrictive covenants and encroachments are encumbrances securing no
debt.

| Distinction | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Voluntary / involuntary** | Created by the owner's act, as a deed of trust is / imposed by law, as a judgment or tax lien is |
| **General / specific** | Attaching to all of a debtor's property / to identified property |

**The general priority principle is first in time, first in right, established by recording** — with
statutory exceptions that ordinarily include **property tax liens** and, in many states, **mechanics' liens
relating back to an earlier date.**

> **Colorado's recording statutes and its mechanics' lien statute were NOT read by this project.** **No
> Colorado priority rule, relation-back date, filing deadline, notice requirement or lien amount is stated
> anywhere in this build.** A priority question in a live transaction goes to the **title company and
> counsel.**

### B.1 Deeds of trust — V.B.1

**Three parties**: the **borrower**, the **lender (beneficiary)**, and a **trustee** who holds title or a
power of sale for the lender's benefit until the debt is satisfied. That third party is what distinguishes
it from a mortgage, where the borrower holds title and the lender holds a lien.

### B.2 Foreclosure — V.B.2

**The enforcement of a security interest against the property.**

> **This is the largest gap in this module, and it is stated plainly.**
>
> **Colorado's foreclosure statutes and its PUBLIC TRUSTEE system were NOT read by this project.**
> Colorado's public trustee arrangement is distinctive among the states, and **this course does not describe
> it, does not state any notice, period, cure right, redemption right, bidding procedure or deficiency
> rule, and does not state who does what.**
>
> **Where the answer lives.** The **Current Legal Issues** outline names *"**financing, liens,
> foreclosures**"* among its representative areas at Topic 3.3, and the Commission staff supplies that
> course's mandatory content to authorized providers. **This build is not on those records and has not
> received that material** — a submission gate recorded in the readings file.
>
> A broker whose transaction involves a property in foreclosure, a short sale, or a deed of trust in
> default takes it to **counsel**, and **Rule 6.2.B.1** offers the route a broker should take seriously:
> **decline.**

### B.3 General property taxes — V.B.3

An **ad valorem** lien on the parcel, ordinarily taking priority over other liens, and one of the items
**prorated** at closing.

**No Colorado property tax statute, assessment ratio, mill levy, rate, exemption, due date, delinquency
consequence or tax sale procedure was read by this project, and none is stated anywhere in this build.**
The **Closings** course prorates taxes on **the convention the contract states**, using figures the
transaction supplies.

### B.4 Special districts — V.B.4

**A special district is a local government entity formed to finance and provide infrastructure or services
for a defined area** — water, sanitation, roads, parks, fire protection, metropolitan services — funded by
a **levy on the property within it** and often by **debt the district issued.**

**Why it matters to a buyer, stated generally.** A property inside a special district may carry a **tax
burden materially above** that of an otherwise similar property outside one, and that burden runs with the
land. It is a fact about the **cost of owning** that a buyer discovers from the district's own disclosures,
the tax records and the title work.

> **Colorado's special district statutes were NOT read by this project.** **No formation requirement,
> disclosure requirement, notice, form, levy limit, debt limit or deadline is stated anywhere in this
> build.**
>
> **What IS stated, because it was read, is the broker's own duty**, which does not depend on the district
> statute: **§ 12-10-404(3)(a)** requires a seller's agent to disclose to any prospective buyer **all adverse
> material facts ACTUALLY KNOWN**, and **§ 12-10-407(2)(b)(VI)** imposes the same on a transaction-broker.
> **§§ 12-10-404(3)(b) and 12-10-407(4)** equally provide there is **no duty to conduct an independent
> investigation.** A broker who **knows** of a district's burden discloses it; a broker who does not know is
> not required by these sections to go and find out — **and a competent broker in a district-heavy market
> will know, because Rule 6.2.A requires the experience, training and knowledge to serve that market.**

### B.5 Other liens — V.B.5

Judgment liens; mechanics' liens; homeowners association assessment liens; federal and state tax liens;
child support liens; and any other statutory lien. **Each arises under its own statute, and none of those
statutes was read.**

## C. Errors and omissions insurance

The outline names it at Topic V.C.

**What it is.** Professional liability insurance covering claims arising from a licensee's professional
services — errors, omissions, and negligent acts in the course of brokerage.

**Its source in Colorado.** **Chapter 3 of 4 CCR 725-1 is titled "Licensure, Renewal, License Status, and
Insurance"**, and the Division publishes a **Broker Insurance Requirements** page.

> **The insurance rules within Chapter 3 were NOT read in detail for this build**, and **no Colorado
> coverage amount, deductible limit, carrier requirement, policy form, effective date or exemption is stated
> anywhere in this build.** **This is a submission gate**: a course claiming to teach the errors and
> omissions requirement must state the current rule, and this one does not state it.
>
> **What this module states is the practical point that does not depend on the figure**: an errors and
> omissions policy is a **condition of doing business** in Colorado, it is a **cost of entering the field**
> — the **Practical Applications** outline names *"**E/O insurance, license fees, car insurance**"* at its
> Topic I.B.4 among the costs to get started — and **a broker checks the current requirement with the
> Division and their employing broker rather than assuming last year's answer still holds.**

## D. Land descriptions: surveys and Improvement Location Certificates

The outline names both at Topic V.D, and the distinction is one Colorado brokers use constantly.

| | **Survey** | **Improvement Location Certificate (ILC)** |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | A measurement and depiction of the parcel's boundaries by a licensed surveyor | A depiction showing the **location of improvements** relative to the boundaries as the preparer understood them |
| **What it establishes** | The **boundaries** | Whether improvements **appear** to lie within the boundaries and easements |
| **What it is used for** | Boundary questions, encroachments, subdivision, construction | Commonly required by **lenders and title companies** as a condition of closing |

**An ILC is not a boundary survey and does not establish boundaries.** A buyer who needs to know where the
line is needs a survey. A lender who needs comfort that the house is not sitting in the easement is
ordinarily satisfied by an ILC.

**Why this belongs in a contracting course.** The contract decides **whether one is obtained, who pays for
it, by when, and what happens if it shows a problem** — which is the **three-part contingency test** from
Module 04 applied to one more condition: **what event, by when, what happens if it does not occur.**

**And it is where an encroachment surfaces.** A records search will not reveal one; a survey or ILC will.
Module 01 of the **Real Estate Law and Practice** course develops encroachments as an encumbrance outside
the record.

**No Colorado surveying statute, licensing requirement, standard, monument requirement or ILC form was read
by this project**, and **none is stated here.**

## E. Water rights

The outline names water rights at **Topic V.E of this course** and again at **Topic I.A.1.a of the Real
Estate Law and Practice course.** The Commission puts it in two of the six outlines, which is a signal about
its weight in Colorado practice.

**What this module states, and it is the general point only:**

**Water rights in the western states may be owned, conveyed, leased and encumbered SEPARATELY FROM THE
LAND.** A right to divert and use water is a distinct property interest. **It does not necessarily pass
with a conveyance of the parcel**, and a contract that says nothing about it may have transferred nothing.

**What that means for a broker completing a contract.** A listing or a contract on land with a well, a
ditch, a share in a ditch company, an augmentation plan, or any recorded water right is a transaction in
which **the water is a separate subject requiring its own treatment in the document** — and getting it
wrong may transfer a farm without the means of irrigating it.

> **NO Colorado water law statute, case, decree, administrative rule or form was read by this project.**
>
> **This course states NOTHING about**: appropriation; priority dates; adjudication; augmentation plans;
> well permits; exempt wells; ditch company shares; change of use proceedings; the division engineer's role;
> or how any of these is conveyed.
>
> **This is a submission gate, and it is one a Colorado course cannot responsibly leave open** in a market
> where farm, ranch and vacant land brokerage is ordinary work.
>
> **What a broker does in the meantime is what Rule 6.2 requires:** possess the necessary experience,
> training and knowledge — or **decline**, **obtain** them, **obtain the assistance** of a competent broker
> or **legal counsel competent in the matter**, or **co-list** with a broker who has them. **Rule 6.2.B.3
> names legal counsel expressly**, and a water question is the clearest case for it.

## F. Financing

Four items at Topic V.F, and the Commission groups them because each is a way a transaction can be
structured wrongly or dishonestly.

### F.1 Equity skimming — V.F.1

**A scheme in which a person acquires property subject to existing loans, collects rents or payments, makes
no payments on the underlying debt, and allows the property to go to foreclosure.** The owner or tenants
bear the loss; the operator keeps the cash.

**What a broker should recognise:** a buyer who wants title without assuming or paying the loan, an
arrangement in which the seller stays liable while someone else collects, and a transaction whose economics
only work if the debt is not serviced.

**No Colorado or federal equity skimming statute was read by this project**, and **no element, threshold or
penalty is stated here.** The description above is of the practice, not of an offence.

### F.2 U.C.C.C. — V.F.2

The **Uniform Consumer Credit Code**, which in states that adopted it governs **consumer credit
transactions** — disclosure, rates, charges, and remedies.

> **Colorado's U.C.C.C. was NOT read by this project.** **No rate ceiling, charge limit, disclosure
> requirement, threshold, exemption or remedy from it is stated anywhere in this build.**
>
> **Why the outline puts it here:** an **owner carry** transaction may be a consumer credit transaction, and
> a seller who becomes a lender may be taking on obligations under a statute they have never read.
> **That is a question for counsel before the seller agrees to carry**, not after.

### F.3 Standards of mortgage servicing — V.F.3

**The obligations of the entity that collects payments, administers escrow, applies payments, and handles
default on a mortgage loan.**

**Why it matters to a broker.** A borrower's loan may be **sold and its servicing transferred** — that is
ordinary and does not change the terms of the note — and a seller's payoff figure, a buyer's assumption
approval, and a short sale all come through the servicer rather than the original lender.

**No federal or Colorado mortgage servicing statute or regulation was read by this project**, and **no
standard, timeline, requirement or penalty is stated here.**

### F.4 Loan fraud — V.F.4

**Misrepresentation to a lender to obtain a loan or better terms** — misstated income, employment or
assets; undisclosed side agreements; a price inflated to disguise a payment outside closing; occupancy
misstated as owner-occupied; or a straw buyer.

**Where a broker meets it.** In the **undisclosed side agreement**: a payment, a credit, a repair
allowance, or a rebate agreed between buyer and seller and **kept off the closing statement.**

**Two things a broker does, and both come from provisions read for this build.**

> **§ 12-10-410(6)** requires that, **before entering into a brokerage or listing agreement or a contract to
> buy, sell or lease**, *"**the identity of those parties, persons, or entities paying compensation or
> commissions to any broker shall be disclosed to the parties to the transaction.**"*
>
> **Rule 5.17** requires **prior written consent** from the consumer the broker represents before assessing
> or receiving **mark-ups or other compensation** for services performed, **regardless of whether it
> benefits the broker or a third party**, plus disclosure of all mark-ups paid by any customer, plus
> **records verifying disclosure and consent.**
>
> And the underlying discipline: **everything the parties agreed goes into the contract or an amendment, and
> what goes into the contract appears on the closing statement.** The **Closings** outline puts it in six
> words at its Topic III.A.1 — *"**Close to contract or amend contract in writing.**"*

**No fraud statute, element, threshold or penalty is stated in this module.** **§§ 12-10-217 and 12-10-219
were not read**, and no disciplinary consequence is stated anywhere in this build.

## G. Landlord Tenant Act — C.R.S. § 38-12-101 et seq.

The outline names the statute by citation at Topic V.G.

> **§ 38-12-101 et seq. was NOT read by this project.**
>
> **This course states NO Colorado rule on**: security deposit limits; deposit return periods; the
> consequences of a wrongful withholding; notice to enter; habitability or the warranty of habitability;
> late fees; repair and deduct; retaliation; lease termination notice periods; eviction or unlawful detainer
> procedure; or any tenant remedy.
>
> **This is a submission gate**, and it is a large one for anyone whose practice includes leasing or
> property management.

**What this build DOES state about leasing comes from rules that were read**, and a student meets them in
two places:

| Source | What it requires |
|---|---|
| **Rule 5.5** | A firm engaging in property management keeps **separate trust or escrow accounts — a minimum of one for rental receipts and a minimum of one for security deposits** |
| **Rule 5.7.A** | Property management money is deposited **no later than five business days** following receipt or mutual execution of a lease, **whichever is later** — where Rule 5.7.B gives all other money **three** |
| **Rule 5.8.A** | A firm **will not deliver security deposits to an owner** without the tenant's **written authorization in a lease** or **unless written notice has been given** telling the tenant who holds the deposit and how to request its return |
| **Rule 5.8.B** | On a change of management company: **written disclosure to owner and tenant within 30 days**; **transfer by the previous firm within 60 days**; the new firm **verifies each deposit against the current lease and discloses any discrepancy** |
| **Rule 6.17.C** | A property manager must **disclose in writing any known conflict of interest in the selection or use of a business or vendor**, including **any ownership, financial, or familial interest** |
| **§§ 12-10-404 and 12-10-405** | Speak of *"seller **or landlord**"* and *"buyer **or tenant**"* throughout — the duty lists run to leasing in their own terms |

**Module 05 of the Real Estate Law and Practice course** delivers the leasehold estates, the lease drafting
checklist, and the management agreement, and states the same gap.
