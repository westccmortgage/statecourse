## A. Definitions — § 12-10-402

The Part opens with its definitions. **The ones that carry the Part are used throughout the sections
below**, and the two a student must hold onto are these: the Part distinguishes a **single agent** — a
broker engaged by one party — from a **transaction-broker**, who **§ 12-10-407(1)** says *"**is not an agent
for either party.**"*

**Rule 1** of 4 CCR 725-1 supplies further definitions the Commission uses in its own rules, including
**Advertise or Advertising** (Rule 1.2), **Anniversary Year Cycle** (Rule 1.4), and the terms **Money
Belonging to Others**, **Trust or Escrow Account**, **Consumer** and **Customer** used throughout Chapters 5
and 6.

**Note the vocabulary Colorado's rules use and other states do not.** Colorado's rules speak of a
**Consumer** — the person with whom a brokerage relationship exists — and a **Customer** — a person the
broker assists without a relationship. **Rule 6.8** turns on that distinction, and Part G below reaches it.

## B. Relationships between brokers and the public — § 12-10-403

### B.1 The two relationships, and the default

> **(1)** *"When engaged in any of the activities enumerated in section 12-10-201(6), a broker **may act in
> any transaction as a single agent or transaction-broker.** The broker's general duties and obligations
> arising from that relationship **shall be disclosed to the seller and the buyer or to the landlord and the
> tenant pursuant to section 12-10-408.**"*
>
> **(2)** *"**A broker shall be considered a TRANSACTION-BROKER unless a single agency relationship is
> established through a WRITTEN AGREEMENT between the broker and the party or parties to be represented by
> the broker.**"*

**Agency in Colorado is opt-in, and the opt-in is a writing.** A broker who signs nothing is a
transaction-broker — and a transaction-broker **is not an agent for either party.**

**Compare the other states this project has built for**, because a licensee arriving from one of them has
the assumption backwards:

| State | The default |
|---|---|
| **Colorado** | **Transaction-broker** — the NON-agency relationship — unless single agency is established in **writing** |
| **Washington** | **Buyer agency arises by DEFAULT** from performing brokerage services for a buyer — RCW 18.86.020(1) |
| **Arizona** | A licensee owes a **fiduciary duty to the client**; the relationship is established as the parties agree — A.A.C. R4-28-1101(A) |

### B.2 A broker may hold different relationships with the same party — (3)

> *"A broker **may work with a single party in separate transactions pursuant to different relationships**
> including, but not limited to, **selling one property as a seller's agent and working with that seller in
> buying another property as a transaction-broker or buyer's agent**, but **only if the broker complies with
> this part 4 in establishing the relationships for each transaction.**"*

**Each transaction gets its own paperwork.** The relationship does not travel from one deal to the next.

### B.3 Authority to complete forms — (4)

Delivered in full in **Module 01 Part B.3**: the eight kinds of standard form, the requirement to use a
**Commission-approved form when one exists and is appropriate**, the split between forms the broker may
only **insert information into** and forms the broker may also **advise on the effects of**, and the
closing requirement to advise the parties that **the forms have important legal consequences and that they
should consult legal counsel before signing.**

### B.4 The public may contract for more — (5)

> *"**Nothing contained in this section shall prohibit the public from entering into written contracts with
> any broker that contain duties, obligations, or responsibilities that are IN ADDITION TO those specified
> in this part 4.**"*

**The statutory duties are a floor, not a ceiling.** A party may bargain for more, in writing. Note that
**§ 12-10-408(2)(a)(III)** requires a **transaction-broker** who undertakes obligations *"in addition to or
different from those set forth in section 12-10-407"* to disclose them *"**in a writing that shall be signed
by the involved parties.**"*

### B.5 Designated brokerage — (6)

This is the structural feature that most distinguishes Colorado, and the statute states it in four
paragraphs.

> **(6)(a)** If a firm has **more than one licensed natural person**, the employing broker **or an individual
> broker** employed by them *"**shall be designated to work with the seller, landlord, buyer, or tenant as a
> DESIGNATED BROKER.**"* The employing broker **may designate more than one.**
>
> **(6)(b)** *"The brokerage relationship established between the [party] and a designated broker, including
> the duties, obligations, and responsibilities of that relationship, **SHALL NOT EXTEND to the employing
> broker nor to any other broker employed or engaged by that employing broker who has not been so designated
> and SHALL NOT EXTEND to the firm**, partnership, limited liability company, association, corporation, or
> other entity that employs the broker."*
>
> **(6)(c)** A firm *"**may have designated brokers working as single agents for a seller or landlord and a
> buyer or tenant in the SAME real estate transaction WITHOUT CREATING DUAL AGENCY** for the employing real
> estate broker, or any broker employed or engaged by that employing real estate broker."*
>
> **(6)(d)** An individual broker **may be designated to work for both** a seller or landlord **and** a buyer
> or tenant in the same transaction **as a transaction-broker for both.**

**Paragraph (c) is why the in-house transaction works in Colorado.** Two designated brokers in one firm may
be single agents for the two sides of the same deal, **and the firm is not thereby a dual agent** — which
matters because **§ 12-10-406 prohibits dual agency outright.**

**And the savings clause, stated immediately in § 12-10-408(2)(e)(II):**

> *"Nothing in this subsection (2)(e) shall be construed to **limit the employing broker's or firm's
> responsibility to SUPERVISE licensees** employed by the broker or firm **nor to SHIELD the broker or firm
> from VICARIOUS LIABILITY.**"*

**Two different things, and a student who merges them misstates both.** The **relationship** does not extend
to the firm. **Supervision** and **vicarious liability** are untouched — and **Rule 6.3.A.1** confirms it
from the rule side: *"whenever a complaint is filed with the Commission against an Associate Broker, **the
Commission may investigate whether there have been violations ... by the Employing Broker.**"*

**Rule 6.6** adds the mechanics: an associate broker may enter a listing contract **as the designated
broker** (6.6.A); **all** members of a team working with a consumer must be designated brokers and **the
names of all team members must be disclosed in the listing contract** (6.6.B); a written disclosure that a
transaction-broker is the designated broker for that consumer (6.6.C); and the employing broker **may
substitute or add** designated brokers, **which must be disclosed to the consumer** (6.6.D).

## C. Single agent engaged by seller or landlord — § 12-10-404

> **(1)** *"A broker engaged by a seller or landlord to act as a seller's agent or a landlord's agent **is a
> LIMITED AGENT** with the following duties and obligations."*

**"Limited agent" is the statute's own term**, and it signals that the duties are the ones the statute
lists rather than the whole of the common law.

### C.1 The duties — (1)(a) to (e)

| | Duty |
|---|---|
| **(a)** | **Perform the terms of the written agreement** made with the seller or landlord |
| **(b)** | **Exercise reasonable skill and care** for the seller or landlord |
| **(c)** | **Promote the interests of the seller or landlord with the UTMOST GOOD FAITH, LOYALTY, AND FIDELITY**, including (I) to (VII) below |
| **(d)** | **Comply with article 10 and its rules** |
| **(e)** | **Comply with applicable federal, state or local law including FAIR HOUSING and civil rights statutes** |

**The seven items inside (c):**

> **(I)** **Seeking a price and terms acceptable** to the seller or landlord — *"except that the broker
> **shall not be obligated to seek additional offers** to purchase the property **while the property is
> subject to a contract for sale**"*, or to seek additional lease offers while subject to a lease or letter
> of intent;
> **(II)** **Presenting all offers to and from the seller or landlord in a timely manner REGARDLESS of
> whether the property is subject to a contract for sale** or a lease or letter of intent to lease;
> **(III)** **Disclosing to the seller or landlord adverse material facts ACTUALLY KNOWN** by the broker;
> **(IV)** **Counseling** the seller or landlord as to material benefits or risks **actually known**;
> **(V)** **Advising the seller or landlord to obtain expert advice** as to material matters known to the
> broker but whose specifics are **beyond the broker's expertise**;
> **(VI)** **Accounting in a timely manner** for all money and property received; and
> **(VII)** **Informing the seller or landlord that they shall NOT be vicariously liable** for the agent's
> acts **not approved, directed, or ratified** by them.

> ### The tension between (I) and (II), which is the examinable point
>
> Once the property is under contract, the broker **need not SEEK additional offers** — (I) says so
> expressly.
>
> But the broker **must PRESENT all offers, "regardless of whether the property is subject to a contract for
> sale"** — (II) says so expressly.
>
> **Not seeking and not presenting are different acts.** The statute permits the first and forbids the
> second, and a broker who stops presenting because the property is under contract has breached (II).
>
> **Rule 6.13** then governs how an offer reaches the other side: present it **to the other consumer's
> broker**; if reasonable but unsuccessful attempts fail, **to that broker's employing broker**; and if none
> exists or those attempts fail, **directly to the other consumer.** **The rule states no time period**, and
> **no module of this build supplies one.**

### C.2 What may not be disclosed — (2)

Five items, **none disclosed without the informed consent of the seller or landlord**:

> **(a)** That the seller or landlord **will accept less than the asking price** or lease rate;
> **(b)** The party's **motivating factors**;
> **(c)** That the party **will agree to financing terms other than those offered**;
> **(d)** **Any material information about the seller or landlord** unless disclosure is **required by law**
> or failure to disclose **would constitute fraud or dishonest dealing**; or
> **(e)** Facts or suspicions regarding circumstances that may **psychologically impact or stigmatize** the
> property **pursuant to § 38-35.5-101.**

**§ 38-35.5-101 was NOT read by this project**, and **no module states what it provides.** It is named here
because the statute names it.

### C.3 What is owed to the other side — (3)

> **(3)(a)** *"A broker acting as a seller's or landlord's agent **owes no duty or obligation to the buyer or
> tenant**; **except that** a broker shall, subject to the limitations of section 38-35.5-101 ... **disclose
> to any prospective buyer or tenant ALL ADVERSE MATERIAL FACTS ACTUALLY KNOWN** by the broker ... including
> ... **the title and the physical condition of the property, any material defects in the property, and any
> environmental hazards** affecting the property that are required by law to be disclosed."*
>
> **(3)(b)** The agent *"**owes no duty to conduct an independent inspection** of the property for the
> benefit of the buyer or tenant and **owes no duty to independently verify the accuracy or completeness of
> any statement** made by the seller or landlord or any independent inspector."*

**"Actually known" is the standard, and there is no duty to go looking.** Both halves are the statute's.

### C.4 What does not breach a duty, and sub-agency — (4) and (5)

> **(4)** The agent **may show alternative properties** not owned by the seller and **may list competing
> properties** without breaching any duty.
>
> **(5)** A designated broker acting as a seller's or landlord's agent *"**may cooperate with other brokers
> but MAY NOT ENGAGE OR CREATE ANY SUBAGENTS.**"*

**Subagency is prohibited in all three relationships.** § 12-10-404(5), § 12-10-405(6) and § 12-10-407(8)
each say it. **The Commission's outline names sub-agency in the title of its sub-topic C**, and this is what
the statute does with it: **it forbids it.**

## D. Single agent engaged by buyer or tenant — § 12-10-405

**The structure is the mirror of § 12-10-404**, and a student should read them side by side. The duties in
(1)(a) through (e) and the seven items in (1)(c) are the same, with **buyer or tenant** in place of seller
or landlord, and with (c)(I)'s exception reading *"**shall not be obligated to seek other properties while
the buyer is a party to a contract to purchase**"*.

**(2) — the five items, mirrored:** that the buyer or tenant **will pay more**; their **motivating
factors**; that they **will agree to other financing terms**; **any material information about them**
subject to the same fraud-or-dishonest-dealing proviso; and **§ 38-35.5-101** facts.

**(3)(a) — and here the mirror is NOT symmetrical, which is the point:**

> The broker owes no duty to the seller or landlord *"**except that the broker shall disclose to any
> prospective seller or landlord all adverse material facts actually known** by the broker **including but
> not limited to adverse material facts concerning the BUYER'S OR TENANT'S FINANCIAL ABILITY TO PERFORM the
> terms of the transaction and WHETHER THE BUYER INTENDS TO OCCUPY the property to be purchased as a
> PRINCIPAL RESIDENCE.**"*

**A seller's agent discloses facts about the PROPERTY to the buyer. A buyer's agent discloses facts about
the BUYER to the seller** — specifically the buyer's **financial ability to perform** and **whether they
intend to occupy as a principal residence.** Two different subject matters, and the second surprises people.

**(3)(b), (4) and (5)** supply the counterparts: no duty to investigate the buyer's financial condition for
the seller's benefit; the agent **may show the same property to competing buyers and assist competing
buyers in attempting to purchase the same property** without breach; and **no duty to inspect the property
or verify statements**, *"except that nothing in this subsection (5) shall be construed to limit the
broker's duties and obligations imposed pursuant to subsection (1)."*

## E. Dual agency — § 12-10-406

**The section, in its entirety:**

> ***"A broker shall not establish dual agency with any seller, landlord, buyer, or tenant."***

**One sentence. No consent mechanism. No exception.**

| State | Dual agency |
|---|---|
| **Colorado** | **Prohibited outright** — § 12-10-406 |
| **Washington** | **Limited dual agency permitted** with consent in the services agreement, **separately initialed** — RCW 18.86.020(2)(b)(iv) |
| **Arizona** | Permitted with the **prior written consent of all parties** — A.A.C. R4-28-1101(F) |

**Rule 6.7 states the three lawful configurations** when a broker or team works with both consumers in one
transaction:

> *"**Neither Brokers nor Teams may enter into a Brokerage Relationship with one Consumer as a Single Agent
> and the other Consumer as a Single Agent or Transaction-Broker in the same transaction.**"* If properly
> disclosed in writing, the broker or team may act as:
> **A.** a **transaction-broker for both** consumers;
> **B.** a **transaction-broker for one** and treating the other as a **customer**; or
> **C.** a **single agent for one** and treating the other as a **customer**.

**Rule 6.9 supplies the form and the deadline for getting there:** a broker or team changing **from single
agent for one consumer to transaction-broker for both** must give the consumer whose relationship changed
the **Commission-approved "Change of Status" form** — *"**at the time the Broker begins to assist both
Consumers as a Transaction-Broker, but NOT LATER THAN at the time the Consumer signs the contract.**"*

**Rule 6.8 defines what may be done for a customer without creating a relationship** — the **ministerial
tasks**: *"showing a property, **preparing as a scrivener**, and conveying written offers and counteroffers,
making known the different types of financing alternatives, and providing information related to
professional, governmental, and community services."* Performing them *"**will not create an agency or
Transaction-Broker relationship with the Customer being assisted.**"*

## F. Transaction-broker — § 12-10-407

> **(1)** *"**A broker engaged as a transaction-broker is NOT AN AGENT for either party.**"*

### F.1 The obligations — (2), owed to BOTH parties

> **(a)** Perform the terms of any **written OR ORAL** agreement made with any party;
> **(b)** Exercise **reasonable skill and care as a transaction-broker**, including:
> **(I)** **Presenting all offers and counteroffers in a timely manner regardless** of an existing contract
> or letter of intent;
> **(II)** **Advising the parties regarding the transaction and suggesting they obtain expert advice** as to
> material matters the broker knows of but whose specifics are beyond the broker's expertise;
> **(III)** **Accounting in a timely manner** for all money and property received;
> **(IV)** **Keeping the parties FULLY INFORMED** regarding the transaction;
> **(V)** **Assisting the parties in complying with the terms and conditions of any contract including
> closing the transaction**;
> **(VI)** **Disclosing to all prospective buyers or tenants any adverse material facts ACTUALLY KNOWN** —
> title, physical condition, defects, environmental hazards required by law to be disclosed;
> **(VII)** **Disclosing to any prospective seller or landlord all adverse material facts actually known** —
> including the **buyer's financial ability to perform** and the **buyer's intent to occupy as a principal
> residence**; and
> **(VIII)** **Informing the parties that they shall not be vicariously liable** for the transaction-broker's
> acts;
> **(c)** Comply with article 10 and its rules; and
> **(d)** Comply with applicable law **including fair housing and civil rights**.

**Note (a): "written OR ORAL."** The transaction-broker relationship does not require a written agreement to
exist — **§ 12-10-403(2) makes it the default** — and (a) binds the broker to whatever was agreed, however
agreed.

**Note (VI) and (VII) together.** The transaction-broker carries **both** single-agent disclosure duties at
once: property facts to the buyer, buyer facts to the seller. **Not being an agent does not mean owing less
in disclosure.**

### F.2 What may not be disclosed — (3), and it is SIX

> **(a)** That a buyer or tenant **will pay more**;
> **(b)** That a seller or landlord **will accept less**;
> **(c)** The **motivating factors** for any party;
> **(d)** That any party **will agree to financing terms other than those offered**;
> **(e)** **§ 38-35.5-101** facts or suspicions; or
> **(f)** **Any material information about the OTHER PARTY** unless disclosure is required by law or failure
> to disclose would constitute **fraud or dishonest dealing**.

**Six, not five** — the union of the two single-agent lists, because the transaction-broker holds
confidences from both sides. **All six require the informed consent of ALL PARTIES**, not of one.

### F.3 No duty, no breach, no imputation, no subagents — (4) to (8)

> **(4)** No duty to conduct an independent **inspection of the property**, and none to verify statements by
> the seller, landlord or independent inspectors.
> **(5)** No duty to conduct an independent **investigation of the buyer's or tenant's financial
> condition**.
> **(6)** May, **without breaching any obligation**: **show alternative properties**; **list competing
> properties**; **show properties to other prospective buyers**; and **serve as single agent or
> transaction-broker for the same or different parties in OTHER transactions.**
> **(7)** *"**There shall be NO IMPUTATION OF KNOWLEDGE OR INFORMATION between any party and the
> transaction-broker or among persons within an entity engaged as a transaction-broker.**"*
> **(8)** May cooperate with other brokers but *"**shall not engage or create any subagents.**"*

**(7) is a rule other states reach differently.** At common law an agent's knowledge is imputed to the
principal. **Colorado's statute says it is not imputed here — in either direction, and not among people
inside the entity.**

## G. Required broker disclosures — § 12-10-408

### G.1 The written office policy — (1)

> **(a)** Every broker *"**shall adopt a WRITTEN OFFICE POLICY that identifies and describes the
> relationships offered to the public** by the broker."*
> **(b)** *"A broker **shall not be required to offer or engage in any one or in all** of the brokerage
> relationships enumerated in section 12-10-404, 12-10-405, or 12-10-407."*
> **(c)** Written disclosures and agreements *"**shall contain a statement ... that DIFFERENT BROKERAGE
> RELATIONSHIPS ARE AVAILABLE that include buyer agency, seller agency, or status as a transaction-broker.**"*
> If a consumer asks about a relationship the broker does **not** offer, *"the broker shall provide to the
> party a **written definition of that brokerage relationship that has been promulgated by the real estate
> commission.**"*
> **(d)** *"Disclosures made in accordance with this part 4 **shall be sufficient** to disclose brokerage
> relationships to the public."*

**A firm may decline to offer agency, or to offer transaction-brokerage — and must still tell a consumer
that the other relationships exist**, supplying the Commission's own written definition on request.

### G.2 The disclosures themselves — (2)

| Situation | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Transaction-broker** — (2)(a)(I) | **Prior to engaging in any licensed activity**, disclose **in writing** that the broker **is not acting as agent** and **is acting as a transaction-broker** |
| — (2)(a)(II) | The disclosure **shall contain a signature block** for acknowledgment. *"The disclosure and acknowledgment, **by itself, shall not constitute a contract**"* — and **if the party does not sign, the broker shall NOTE THAT FACT on a copy and RETAIN the copy** |
| — (2)(a)(III) | Obligations **in addition to or different from** § 12-10-407 must be **in a writing signed by the involved parties** |
| **Single agency** — (2)(b) | **Prior to engaging in any licensed activity**, **enter into a WRITTEN AGENCY AGREEMENT** disclosing the § 12-10-404 or § 12-10-405 duties; notice of the relationship **furnished to any prospective party in a timely manner** |
| **Working with the other side as the seller's agent** — (2)(c)(I) | A written disclosure containing **(A)** that the broker is the **seller's agent and not the buyer's**; **(B)** **a list of the tasks the agent intends to perform** for the seller with the buyer; and **(C)** that the buyer **is not vicariously liable** unless they approve, direct or ratify |
| — (2)(c)(II) | Same **signature block**, same **note-and-retain** rule if unsigned |
| **Already engaged** — (2)(d) | *"**shall advise at the earliest reasonable opportunity** any other potential parties or their agents of the established relationship"* |
| **Designated brokerage** — (2)(e)(I) | The written agreement **shall advise** that the relationship **exists only with the designated broker**, **not** the employing broker or other non-designated brokers, and **not the brokerage company** |

**The note-and-retain rule in (2)(a)(II) and (2)(c)(II) is the one brokers forget.** A consumer who declines
to sign has not defeated the requirement — **the broker notes the refusal on a copy and keeps it**, and that
copy is the record.

### G.3 When — Rule 6.5

> **A.** *"Written disclosures pursuant to section 12-10-408, C.R.S. **must be made to a Consumer PRIOR TO
> ELICITING OR DISCUSSING CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION** from a Consumer for Real Estate Brokerage Services."*
>
> **B.** *"Such activities **do not include preliminary conversations or 'small talk'** concerning **price
> range, location and property styles**, or **responding to general factual questions** from a potential
> Consumer concerning properties which have been Advertised for sale or lease."*

**The trigger is confidential information, not first contact.** A broker may answer a caller's question
about an advertised listing and discuss price range and location without triggering the disclosure — and
the moment the conversation turns to what the consumer can afford, why they are moving, or what they would
really accept, the disclosure is already due.

## H. Duration of relationship — § 12-10-409

> **(1)(a)** The relationship **commences when the broker is engaged** and continues **until performance or
> completion** of the agreement.
> **(1)(b)** If not performed or completed **for any reason**, it ends at the **EARLIER** of:
> **(I)** any **date of expiration agreed** by the parties;
> **(II)** any **termination or relinquishment** by the parties; or
> **(III)** **ONE YEAR after the date of the engagement.**

**The one-year backstop runs whether or not anyone remembers it**, and it is the statute's own limit on an
engagement that simply drifts.

**(2) and (3) — what survives, and it differs by relationship:**

| | Survives termination |
|---|---|
| **Single agent** — (2)(b) | **(I) Accounting** for all money and property received during the engagement; **and (II) KEEPING CONFIDENTIAL** all information received during the engagement **that was made confidential by request or instructions from the engaging party** |
| **Transaction-broker** — (3) | **Accounting only** — *"except that the broker shall account for all money and property related to and received during the engagement"* |

**The single agent's confidentiality survives; the transaction-broker section carries only accounting.**

**And the confidentiality that survives is not unlimited** — (2)(b)(II) lifts it where **(A)** the engaging
party **grants written consent**, **(B)** disclosure is **required by law**, or **(C)** the information
**is made public or becomes public by the words or conduct of the engaging party or from a source other than
the broker.**

## I. Compensation — § 12-10-410

> **(1)** Compensation **may be paid by the seller, the buyer, the landlord, the tenant, a third party, or
> by sharing or splitting between brokers.**
> **(2)** *"**Payment of compensation shall NOT be construed to establish an agency relationship between the
> broker and the party who paid the compensation.**"*
> **(3)** A seller or landlord **may agree** that a transaction-broker or single agent **may share** the
> compensation with another broker.
> **(4)** A buyer or tenant **may agree** to the same.
> **(5)** *"A buyer's or tenant's agent shall obtain the **WRITTEN APPROVAL of the buyer or tenant BEFORE**
> the agent may **propose to the seller's or landlord's agent** that the buyer's or tenant's agent be
> compensated by sharing compensation paid by the seller or landlord."*
> **(6)** *"**PRIOR TO entering into a brokerage or listing agreement or a contract to buy, sell, or lease,
> the IDENTITY of those parties, persons, or entities PAYING COMPENSATION or commissions to any broker shall
> be disclosed to the parties to the transaction.**"*
> **(7)** A broker **may be compensated by more than one party** if those parties **consented in writing
> PRIOR to entering into a contract** to buy, sell or lease.

**(2) is the sentence to memorise.** Who pays does not decide who is represented. A seller paying a buyer's
agent's compensation does not thereby become that agent's client, and a buyer whose agent is compensated
from the listing side is not thereby the listing broker's client.

**(5) is a duty that runs to the buyer's OWN client and is easy to miss.** Before a buyer's agent even
**proposes** the sharing arrangement to the other side, they need **the buyer's written approval.**

**Two rules read for this build sit alongside § 12-10-410:** **Rule 5.17** requires **prior written consent**
from the consumer the broker represents before assessing or receiving **mark-ups or other compensation**,
and disclosure of all mark-ups paid by any customer; **Rule 6.21.A** provides that brokers *"**will not pay
