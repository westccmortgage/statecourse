## A. What a brokerage relationship agreement is, in Colorado terms

A listing contract or buyer brokerage contract is a contract of **employment**. It **conveys nothing** and
it is **not an offer to sell.**

**In Colorado it does one further thing that matters more than anything else in this module.**
**§ 12-10-403(2)**: *"**A broker shall be considered a transaction-broker UNLESS a single agency
relationship is established through a WRITTEN AGREEMENT** between the broker and the party or parties to be
represented."*

**The agreement is what creates agency.** Without it there is a relationship — the transaction-broker
relationship — but not an agency one. That is why **§ 12-10-408(2)(b)** requires a broker intending single
agency to **enter into a written agency agreement before engaging in any licensed activity**, and why the
agreement must **disclose the § 12-10-404 or § 12-10-405 duties.**

**And § 12-10-408(2)(e)(I)** requires every such written agreement to advise the party that the relationship
**exists only with the designated broker**, does **not** extend to the employing broker or other
non-designated brokers, and **does not extend to the brokerage company.**

## B. The six families

The Commission's outline lists them at Topic III.A under the heading *"Listing and Buyer Brokerage
Contracts. (Agency and Transaction-Broker)"* — the parenthesis mattering because each family may be used to
establish **either** relationship.

### B.1 The three seller- and landlord-side families

| # | Family | What the owner gives up | Compensation earned |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Exclusive Right-to-Sell Listing Contract** | **Full exclusivity** | However the sale comes about during the term — including a sale the owner finds themselves |
| **2** | **Exclusive Agency Listing Contract** | Exclusivity **against other brokers** | If any broker produces the sale; **not** if the owner sells it themselves |
| **3** | **Open Listing Contract** | **Nothing exclusive** | Only to the broker who **produces** the buyer; the owner may engage others and may sell themselves |

**The exclusive right-to-sell is the one most used and the one whose consequence an owner most often
misunderstands.** An owner who signs it and then sells to their own cousin still owes compensation, because
the compensation is earned by the **sale during the term**, not by the broker's procurement of the buyer.
**Explaining that before signature is the substance of the broker's job at this stage** — and the
**Practical Applications** outline names it at its Topic VI.A.2.a, *"**Effects of an exclusive
relationship**"*, and at VI.A.2.b, *"**Explaining the holdover period.**"*

### B.2 The three buyer- and tenant-side families

| # | Family | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **4** | **Exclusive Right-to-Buy Contract** | Engages the broker to work with a **buyer**, exclusively, for a defined property type and area |
| **5** | **Exclusive Right-to-Lease Listing Contract** | The **landlord** side of a lease transaction — the letting equivalent of the right-to-sell |
| **6** | **Exclusive Tenant Contract** | The **tenant** side — engages the broker to find premises for a tenant |

**Four and five are opposite sides of the same coin, and students confuse them.** *Right-to-Lease* is
signed by the **owner** who wants the property let. *Exclusive Tenant* is signed by the **tenant** who wants
premises found.

### B.3 What any of the six must settle

**This is a checklist of what the instrument settles, not a statement of any Commission form's contents.**

1. **Parties** — the consumer as they hold or will hold title, and **the brokerage firm**, with the
   **designated broker** identified.
2. **Property** (seller side) or **the search criteria** — property type, price range and area (buyer side).
3. **The relationship** — **single agent or transaction-broker**, established as § 12-10-403(2) requires.
4. **Term** — a **definite beginning and a definite end.**
5. **Compensation** — the amount or rate, **what earns it**, and when it is payable.
6. **The holdover period**, if any — how long after the term a sale to a protected buyer still earns
   compensation, and to whom the protection attaches.
7. **Cooperation and compensation with other brokers**, and — buyer side — the **§ 12-10-410(5) written
   approval** before proposing to be compensated from the listing side.
8. **The broker's authority**, and any **express limitation** on it.
9. **The consumer's own undertakings.**
10. **The designated brokerage advisory** required by **§ 12-10-408(2)(e)(I)**.
11. **Signatures**, with the firm correctly named.

> **Item 4 is checked strictly, and here is why.** **§ 12-10-409(1)(b)** ends a relationship at the
> **earlier** of an agreed expiration date, a termination by the parties, or **one year after the date of
> the engagement.** A student who leaves the term blank has not created an open-ended engagement; they have
> created one that ends by statute at a date neither party chose.
>
> **Item 5 is checked against § 12-10-410(6)**, which requires that **before** entering into a brokerage or
> listing agreement, *"**the identity of those parties, persons, or entities paying compensation or
> commissions to any broker shall be disclosed to the parties to the transaction.**"*

### B.4 The comparison the outline requires

> *"...and include **by comparison the differences between residential, commercial and farm and ranch/vacant
> land** form."*

**The Commission requires the student to see the same family across three property types.** What ordinarily
differs between them is the description of the property and its appurtenances (water rights, mineral
rights, growing crops, personal property and equipment on a farm or ranch); the due diligence a buyer will
need and the time it takes; the financing available; and the disclosures the transaction attracts.

**Water rights recur here**, as they did in Module 01 of the Real Estate Law and Practice course and again
in Module 05 of this course under the outline's Topic V.E. **No Colorado water law statute was read by this
project**, and **this course states no rule on whether or how a water right passes with a parcel.** For a
farm and ranch or vacant land listing, that question goes to **counsel and a water engineer before the
listing is taken**, and **Rule 6.2** makes it a competency requirement rather than a courtesy: a broker
**must** possess the necessary experience, training and knowledge, or **decline**, obtain them, obtain
assistance, or **co-list**.

## C. Related forms

The outline lists these at Topic III.B, and requires **one of each type** completed.

### C.1 The in-company transaction — III.B.1

**The problem.** A firm has a listing. A broker in the same firm is working with a buyer for it. Colorado
prohibits dual agency outright — **§ 12-10-406** — so something has to change before the transaction can
proceed.

**What the statute permits.** **§ 12-10-403(6)(c)**: a firm **may have designated brokers working as single
agents for both sides of the same transaction without creating dual agency** for the employing broker or
any other broker. **§ 12-10-403(6)(d)**: an **individual** broker may be designated for both sides **as a
transaction-broker for both.**

**What Rule 6.7 forbids and permits.** *"**Neither Brokers nor Teams may enter into a Brokerage Relationship
with one Consumer as a Single Agent and the other Consumer as a Single Agent or Transaction-Broker in the
same transaction.**"* The three lawful configurations for one broker or team with both consumers are:
**transaction-broker for both**; **transaction-broker for one and the other as a customer**; or **single
agent for one and the other as a customer.**

**a. Change of Status.** **Rule 6.9** states exactly when it is due:

> A broker or team who changes their relationship *"**from a Single Agent for one Consumer to assisting both
> Consumers in the same real estate transaction as a Transaction-Broker must provide the written
> Commission-Approved 'Change of Status' Form to the Consumer that has the changed relationship with the
> Broker, AT THE TIME the Broker begins to assist both Consumers as a Transaction-Broker, BUT NOT LATER THAN
> at the time the Consumer signs the contract.**"*

**Two deadlines in one sentence, and the second is the backstop.** The form goes to **the consumer whose
relationship changed** — the one who had a single agent and now has a transaction-broker.

**b. Transaction-Broker Addendum.** Named by the outline. It is the instrument by which an existing
agreement is adapted to the transaction-broker relationship.

### C.2 Seller's Property Disclosure — III.B.2

The instrument on which a seller states what they know about the property.

**It is the seller's statement, not the broker's.** The broker's own obligation runs separately and
independently under **§ 12-10-404(3)(a)** — to disclose to any prospective buyer or tenant **all adverse
material facts ACTUALLY KNOWN** by the broker, including facts as to title, physical condition, material
defects and environmental hazards required by law to be disclosed. **A completed seller's disclosure does
not discharge that duty**, and a fact the broker knows that the seller did not state is still the broker's
to disclose.

**And § 12-10-404(3)(b)** supplies the other half: the broker **owes no duty to conduct an independent
inspection** and **no duty to independently verify** the seller's statements.

**a. Energy Rated Homes of Colorado.** The outline names it under the seller's property disclosure with
three sub-items: **background**, **defining ERHC**, and **benefits of the system.**

> **No Energy Rated Homes of Colorado material, statute or programme document was obtained or read by this
> project**, and **this module states nothing about the programme's content, requirements, ratings or
> operation.** **This is a submission gate.** The outline names it and this build cannot deliver it from
> primary text.

### C.3 Agreement to Amend/Extend Contract with Broker — III.B.3

The instrument that changes the terms of a **brokerage relationship agreement** already signed — most often
the **term** or the **price**.

**Note it is a different form from the Agreement to Amend/Extend Contract** at Topic IV.B.2, which amends
the **contract to buy and sell** between the parties. **One amends the employment; the other amends the
sale.** Confusing them is a common student error and produces a document that changes nothing.

### C.4 The brokerage relationship disclosure documents — III.C

The outline lists five items, and they are the **§ 12-10-408(2)** disclosures in their form-name shapes.

**1. Definitions of Working Relationships.** **§ 12-10-408(1)(c)** requires that written disclosures and
agreements contain a statement that **different brokerage relationships are available — buyer agency,
seller agency, or status as a transaction-broker** — and that where a consumer asks about a relationship
**the broker does not offer**, the broker provide *"**a written definition of that brokerage relationship
that has been promulgated by the real estate commission.**"*

**2. Brokerage Disclosure to Buyer.** The disclosure a broker gives a **buyer** they are not representing —
required by **§ 12-10-408(2)(c)(I)** where the broker works with the buyer **as the seller's agent**, and
containing **(A)** that the broker is the seller's agent and **not** the buyer's; **(B)** **a list of the
tasks the agent intends to perform** for the seller with the buyer; and **(C)** that the buyer is **not
vicariously liable** for the agent's acts unless they approve, direct or ratify them. It **must contain a
signature block**, and **if the buyer does not sign, the broker notes that fact on a copy and retains it.**

**3. Brokerage Disclosure to Seller (FSBO).** The counterpart given to an **unrepresented seller** — the
for-sale-by-owner across the table from a broker working with a buyer.

**4. Change of Status.** As at C.1(a) above, under **Rule 6.9**.

**5. Brokerage Disclosure to Seller (REO and Non-CREC approved listings).** The disclosure used where the
seller is an institutional owner of real estate owned after foreclosure, or where the listing is on a form
the Commission has not approved.

> **Rule 6.5 fixes the moment for all of them.** *"Written disclosures pursuant to section 12-10-408 **must
> be made to a Consumer PRIOR TO ELICITING OR DISCUSSING CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION** from a Consumer."* And
> **Rule 6.5.B** says what does **not** trigger it: *"**preliminary conversations or 'small talk' concerning
> price range, location and property styles**, or **responding to general factual questions** ... concerning
> properties which have been Advertised."*

## D. What a completed form must show

The Commission requires the student to **complete** one of each of the six families and one of each of the
related forms. **This course records each as a separate completed artefact**, and a form is accepted as
complete when it shows all of the following.

| Check | What is looked for |
|---|---|
| **Parties correct** | The **consumer** as they hold title; the **brokerage firm**, with the **designated broker** identified |
| **Relationship stated** | **Single agent** or **transaction-broker**, unambiguously — § 12-10-403(2) |
| **Term stated** | A **definite beginning and end** — and never left blank |
| **Compensation stated** | Amount or rate, **what earns it**, and when payable |
| **Holdover addressed** | Present or expressly absent, not left ambiguous |
| **Designated brokerage advisory present** | § 12-10-408(2)(e)(I) |
| **Compensation identity disclosed** | § 12-10-410(6) — **before** the agreement is entered into |
| **Buyer-side sharing approval** | § 12-10-410(5) — the buyer's **written approval before** the proposal is made |
| **Signature blocks used correctly** | Including, on a disclosure, the **note-and-retain** step if the consumer declines to sign |
| **Nothing invented** | No term, figure or provision supplied from another state or from memory |

**A form completed with a blank the Commission's form requires filled is not complete**, and the reason is
the one the outline gives for the whole course: *"to protect the public by having students **prepare and
understand** Colorado Real Estate Commission approved contracts."*

**The last check is the one this build enforces on itself as well.** No module of this course states a
figure, period or provision from a source it did not read, and a student's completed form should meet the
same standard — **every term in it comes from the Commission's form, the transaction, or the parties'
instructions.**
