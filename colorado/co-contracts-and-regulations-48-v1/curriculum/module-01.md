# Colorado Contracts and Regulations — Module 01

## Regulations: the licence law, Colorado contract law, and the statutes the Commission names

**Course:** Contracts and Regulations (48 classroom hours) · **Instructional hours: 8.0** of 48.0
**Commission outline topic:** **I — Regulations**, allocated **8 hours**
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No Colorado qualifying education hours are available.

> ### This is the Colorado course
>
> **C.R.S. § 12-10-203(4)(a)(II)** requires *"**forty-eight hours** of classroom instruction ... in
> **understanding and preparation of Colorado real estate contracts**."* **Rule 2.1.A.2.b** names it
> *"Colorado Real Estate Contracts: **48 hours**."*
>
> **This is the course no applicant escapes.** **§ 12-10-203(4)(b)** relieves a broker licensed in another
> jurisdiction of everything **except** this course and **Closings**; **§ 12-10-203(4)(c)** relieves a
> salesperson licensed elsewhere of everything **except** this course and the whole 72-hour group. **Every
> applicant completes these 48 hours**, whatever they hold elsewhere.

> *"To protect the public by having students **prepare and understand Colorado Real Estate Commission

## Learning objectives

1. Name the sources of Colorado real estate regulation the Commission's outline lists.
2. State what § 12-10-403(4) authorises a broker to complete and what Rule 7.1 requires.
3. State what the Commission's outline names in Colorado contract law, and say which of those this build
   read and which it did not.
4. State the Statute of Frauds requirement in general terms and identify Colorado's own as unread.
5. Name the subject-matter statutes the outline lists and say what each governs.
6. State the four-year transaction file retention rule and what it does not require.

## A. Colorado real estate license law

The Commission's outline names three sources at Topic I.A, and this build read all three categories of
document — though not every section within them.

### A.1 Statutes — "Title 12 Article 10, Parts 1-8 CRS"

The outline names the whole of Article 10. **What this project read is §§ 12-10-203 and 12-10-402 through
12-10-411**, and the readings file says so.

| Part | Subject | Read for this build? |
|---|---|---|
| **Part 1** | Real estate brokers — definitions, licensing, the Commission, discipline | **§ 12-10-203 only** |
| **Part 2** | (as organised in Article 10) | § 12-10-203 |
| **Part 4** | **Brokerage relationships — §§ 12-10-402 to 12-10-411** | **In full** |
| **Part 5** | Subdivisions | **No** |
| **Part 6** | Appraisers | **No** |
| **Parts 3, 7, 8** | Other regulated activities | **No** |

**Every disciplinary ground, penalty and procedure lives in §§ 12-10-217, 12-10-219 and 12-10-222, and
NONE of them was read.** **No sanction, fine, suspension, revocation ground or procedure is stated anywhere
in this build.** Where a rule read for this build cites one of those sections — as **Rule 5.17** cites
§§ 12-10-217(1)(d) and (t), **Rule 5.20.A** cites § 12-10-217(1)(i), **Rule 6.3.A.1** cites
§ 12-10-217(1)(r), and **§ 12-10-411** cites § 12-10-217(1)(m) — the citation is reproduced and **its
content is not.**

### A.2 Commission rules — 4 CCR 725-1

**Read in full**: 89 pages, rule version 12355, from the Secretary of State's CCR. Nine chapters:

| Chapter | Subject |
|---|---|
| **1** | Definitions |
| **2** | Licensure requirements — including **Rule 2.1**, the 168 hours |
| **3** | Licensure, renewal, license status, and insurance |
| **4** | Continuing education requirement |
| **5** | **Separate accounts and accounting** — the Trust Accounts course |
| **6** | **Practice standards** |
| **7** | **Use of standard forms** |
| **8** | Declaratory orders |
| **9** | Commission review of initial decisions and exceptions |

**Rule 2.1.A.2 is where the 168 hours are divided**, and the division is worth reading closely because the
rule does two different things in one list:

> *"a. Real Estate Law and Real Estate Practice: **48 hours**;*
> *b. Colorado Real Estate Contracts: **48 hours**;*
> *c. Real Estate Closings: **not less than 24 hours**;*
> *d. Trust Accounts and Record Keeping: **not less than 8 hours**;*
> *e. Current Legal Issues: **not less than 8 hours**; and*
> *f. Practical Application: **not less than 32 hours**."*

**The first two are exact. The other four are FLOORS.** That is not an accident of drafting: **§ 12-10-203
(4)(a)(III)** fixes *"a total of **seventy-two hours**"* for the four subjects **together** and allocates
none among them, so the Commission's rule sets minimums that add to exactly 72.

**Rule 2.1.D** gives the Commission an audit power over this course and every other: it *"**may audit
courses ... at any time and at no cost**"*, may request **all instructional materials and student
attendance records**, and *"**failure to comply with statutes and these Rules may result in the removal of
the course provider, instructor, and/or the course from the approved provider list.**"*

### A.3 Commission interpretations and positions on the license law

**The Commission issues position statements.** The **Practical Applications** outline names one by number —
**CP 41**, on gaining competency — and the **Record Keeping and Trust Accounts** outline names *"Position
Statements"* among the Commission rules and regulations a student must meet.

> **NO Commission position statement was obtained or read by this project**, and **no module of this build
> states the content of one.** This is a submission gate. A course claiming to teach *"Commission
> interpretations and positions on the license law"* — the outline's own words — cannot do so from
> statute and rule alone.

## B. Review of Colorado contract law

The Commission's outline names six items at Topic I.B. **This module states honestly which of them this
build can teach from primary text and which it cannot.**

### B.1 The Conway-Bogue decision — NOT READ

**Named by the outline at I.B.1.** It is the Colorado decision that settles what a real estate broker may
do with contract documents without engaging in the unauthorized practice of law.

> **The decision was NOT read by this project**, and **no module of this build states its holding, its
> reasoning, or its limits.**
>
> **What this build DOES state comes from the statute and rule that were read**, and they are the operative
> law a broker follows daily:
>
> **§ 12-10-403(4)(a)**: a broker *"**whether acting as a single agent or transaction-broker, MAY COMPLETE
> STANDARD FORMS** for use in a real estate transaction, **including standard forms intended to convey
> personal property as part of the real estate transaction**, when a broker is performing the activities
> enumerated or referred to in section 12-10-201(6) in the transaction."*
>
> **§ 12-10-403(4)(c)**, closing sentence: *"**In any transaction described in this subsection (4), the
> broker shall advise the parties that the forms have important legal consequences and that the parties
> should consult legal counsel before signing the forms.**"*
>
> **This is a submission gate**, and the **Current Legal Issues** outline names *"**Unauthorized Practice of
> Law concerns**"* among its representative areas — which is where a course built to that outline's
> Commission-supplied materials would carry it.

### B.2 Colorado Bar Association v. The Title Guaranty Company — NOT READ

**Named at I.B.2**, and the same is true of it: **not read, holding not stated.**

### B.3 Authority to complete forms — § 12-10-403(4), READ IN FULL

**This one this build can deliver, because the section was read.** It is the statutory basis for everything
Modules 03 and 04 do.

**§ 12-10-403(4)(b) defines "standard form" in eight paragraphs:**

| # | Kind of form |
|---|---|
| **(I)** | A form **promulgated by the real estate commission** for current use by brokers — a **"commission-approved form"** |
| **(II)** | A form **drafted by a licensed Colorado attorney** representing the broker, employing broker or firm — with **the attorney's or law firm's name and the broker's or firm's name on the form itself** |
| **(III)** | A form **provided by a party** to the transaction where the broker is that party's transaction-broker or single agent — **the broker retaining written confirmation that the party provided it** |
| **(IV)** | A form **prescribed by a governmental or quasi-governmental agency, or by a lender regulated by state or federal law**, where **use of the form is mandated** |
| **(V)** | A form **issued with the written approval of the Colorado Bar Association** and specifically designated for use by brokers in Colorado, used within any guidelines or conditions the CBA specified |
| **(VI)** | A form **used for disclosure purposes only**, if it **does not purport to waive or create any legal rights or obligations** and provides only information about **the real estate specifically** or **the geographic area generally** |
| **(VII)** | A form **prescribed by a title company** providing closing services where the broker is transaction-broker or single agent for a party |
| **(VIII)** | A **letter of intent** created by the broker, employing broker or firm, **so long as it states on its face that it is nonbinding and creates no legal rights or obligations** |

**§ 12-10-403(4)(c) then states the hierarchy and the limits, and this is the operative sentence:**

> *"**A broker shall use a COMMISSION-APPROVED FORM when such a form exists and is appropriate for the
> transaction.** A broker's use of any standard form described in subsection (4)(b)(III) or (4)(b)(IV) ...
> **must be limited to inserting transaction-specific information within the form.** In using standard forms
> described in subsection (4)(b)(II), (V), (VI), (VII), or (VIII) ... **the broker may also advise the
> parties as to effects thereof** ..."*

**Read the split.** For a **client form** or a **government/lender form**, the broker may do **one thing
only — insert transaction-specific information.** For an **attorney form**, a **CBA form**, a
**disclosure-only form**, a **title company form** or a **letter of intent**, the broker **may also advise
the parties as to the effects.** And **Rule 7.1.A** says the same of a **Commission-approved form**: *"The
Broker **may advise the parties as to the effects thereof.**"*

**Rule 7.1.B adds two requirements to an attorney form that the statute does not:**

> An attorney form may be used **only if a Commission-approved form does not exist or is not appropriate**;
> it *"**must contain the language that says: 'This form has not been approved by the Colorado Real Estate
> Commission'**"*; and it *"**may not be altered by the Broker other than by completing any blank spaces in
> the form.**"*

> ### The gate that runs through this entire course
>
> **NO Commission-approved form was obtained, read, or reproduced by this project.** The outline requires
> students to **complete** them — one of each of **six** listing and buyer brokerage families at Topic III,
> one of each of **four** sales contract families at Topic IV, and one of each of the related forms in
> both.
>
> **Modules 03 and 04 teach what each form must settle and what the statute and rules require of its use.
> They do not reproduce any form, state any form's contents, or name any form version.** Conforming this
> course to the Commission's current forms — downloaded from the Division's website, as Rule 7.1.A directs
> — **is the largest submission gate in the Colorado build.**

### B.4 Statute of Frauds — Colorado's own NOT READ

**Named at I.B.4.** In general terms, a contract for the sale of an interest in land must be **in writing**
and **signed by the party to be charged**, identifying the **parties**, the **property**, and the **price or
a means of determining it**.

**Colorado's own statute of frauds was NOT read by this project**, and **no section number, wording,
exception or part-performance doctrine is stated anywhere in this build.**

### B.5 Uniform Power of Attorney Act — NOT READ

**Named at I.B.5.** A power of attorney authorises an agent to act for a principal, and a conveyance signed
under one raises questions about the instrument's scope, its currency, and its recording.

**Colorado's Uniform Power of Attorney Act was NOT read.** **No requirement, form, scope rule, durability
rule or recording requirement from it is stated anywhere in this build.** A transaction in which a party
signs under a power of attorney is one for **counsel and the title company**, and **Rule 6.2.B** gives the
broker four routes: decline, obtain the knowledge, obtain assistance, or co-list.

### B.6 Electronic duplication — and the rules that WERE read

**Named at I.B.6.** Two Colorado rules read for this build bear on records in electronic form:

> **Rule 5.14.E**: *"The three-way reconciliation reports, ledgers, journals, and bank account statements
> **may be kept electronically.**"*
>
> **Rule 6.24** governs **electronic records and production of records**, and **Rule 5.21** requires a broker
> and firm to *"**produce for inspection by the Commission any document or record** as may be reasonably
> necessary for investigation or audit"*, with **failure within the time set being grounds for disciplinary
> action** absent an extension.

**No Colorado electronic transactions or electronic signature statute was read by this project**, and **no
rule about the validity or execution of an electronic signature is stated anywhere in this build.**

## C. The subject-matter statutes the outline names

Topic I names four more bodies of law at C through F. **This module states what each governs and states
plainly that none of them was read.**

### C.1 Subdividing, conversions and time sharing — Topic I.C

**C.R.S. Title 12, Article 10, Part 5** governs subdivisions, and **§ 12-10-501(3)** defines *"subdivision"*
in terms this build encountered while reading Part 4 but **did not read as a whole**.

**No subdivision registration requirement, public report requirement, developer obligation, exemption,
threshold, conversion rule or time share provision is stated anywhere in this build.**

### C.2 Colorado Fair Housing Act — Topic I.D

**NOT read.** Module 03 of the **Real Estate Law and Practice** course delivers the **seven federal
protected classes** — race, colour, religion, national origin, sex, disability, familial status — and
**states no additional Colorado protected class, exemption, remedy or procedure.**

**What this build does state, because it was read**, is that **§§ 12-10-404(1)(e), 12-10-405(1)(e) and
12-10-407(2)(d)** each require a broker to comply with applicable law *"**including fair housing and civil
rights statutes or regulations**"* — so a fair housing violation is **also** a violation of the brokerage
relationship statute, and **§ 12-10-411** makes any Part 4 violation an act the Commission may investigate.

### C.3 Appraiser Licensing Act — Topic I.E

**NOT read.** What **was** read is **Rule 6.12**, delivered in Module 02 of the Real Estate Law and Practice
course: a broker preparing a **CMA or BPO for any reason other than an anticipated sale or purchase** must
include the notice the rule prescribes verbatim, and **brokers are prohibited from completing CMAs or BPOs
used for the purpose of obtaining financing**, the rule citing **§ 12-10-602(9)(b)(II)** for that.

**§ 12-10-602 itself was not read**, and **no appraiser licensing level, requirement, exemption or penalty
is stated anywhere in this build.**

### C.4 Colorado Common Interest Ownership Act — Topic I.F

**NOT read.** **CCIOA is article 33.3 of title 38**, and **§ 12-10-501(3)(b)(I)(A)** — encountered in the
Part 5 definitions — refers to it in defining a subdivision to include *"the conversion of an existing
structure into a common interest community ... of twenty or more residential units."*

**No CCIOA requirement, disclosure, document delivery obligation, association power, budget requirement,
resale certificate requirement or deadline is stated anywhere in this build.** The Commission promulgates a
**Common Interest Community Addendum to the Contract to Buy and Sell**, named at the outline's Topic
IV.A.2.c — **and no Commission form was obtained.**

## D. Two rules from Chapter 6 that belong with the regulations

### D.1 Transaction files — Rule 6.20

> *"Both a Broker and a Brokerage Firm **must retain transaction files for all transactions for a period of
> FOUR (4) YEARS** beginning from **the consummation date of the transaction or the expiration date of any
> Listing Contracts that do not consummate.** Required documents in a transaction file are designated in the
> **Commission's Transaction File Checklist** and may be found on the Division's website."*

**Two start dates, not one.** A transaction that closes runs from **consummation**; a listing that never
produces one runs from **the listing's expiration.**

**And what the rule expressly does NOT require:** a broker is not required to obtain and retain copies of
**existing public records, title commitments, loan applications, lender required disclosures, or related
affirmations from independent third party closing entities after the closing date.**

**The Commission's Transaction File Checklist was NOT obtained**, and **no module of this build states what
documents it designates.** That is a submission gate.

### D.2 Continuing education — noted, not taught

**Chapter 4** of the rules carries the continuing education requirement, and **Rule 4.2.A.3** is the basis
of the **Broker Reactivation Course (24 hours)** the Division publishes as an alternative method of
satisfying CE.

**This is a qualifying education course, not a continuing education course**, and **no CE requirement,
hour figure, cycle or deadline is stated in this build** beyond noting that **Rule 3** ties licensing to a
**three-year Anniversary Year Cycle** as **Rule 1.4** defines it.
