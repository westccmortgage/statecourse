## Learning objectives

1. Name the four families of sales contracts the Commission's outline lists.
2. State what a contract to buy and sell must settle, in the order a broker completes it.
3. State the three-part test a complete contingency must satisfy.
4. Distinguish new financing, loan assumption and owner carry, and state what each requires of the contract.
5. State what each related form does and the error each is meant to prevent.
6. State the difference between a counterproposal and an amend/extend agreement.
7. State the Colorado earnest money rules a completed contract must be consistent with.
8. State what a completed form must show for the drafting requirement to be satisfied.

## A. The four families

The outline lists them at Topic IV.A.

| # | Family | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Contract to Buy and Sell Real Estate (Residential)** | The residential sale — the contract a new broker uses most |
| **2** | **Contract to Buy and Sell Real Estate (Commercial)** | Commercial property |
| **3** | **Contract to Buy and Sell Real Estate (Vacant Land/Farm & Ranch)** | Land, farm and ranch |
| **4** | **Contract to Exchange Real Estate (Simultaneous Exchange)** | An exchange rather than a sale |

The outline places two further instruments inside the family list rather than among the related forms:
the **Common Interest Community Addendum to the Contract to Buy and Sell** (at IV.A.2.c) and the
**Addendum to Contract to Buy and Sell** (at IV.A.3.b, attached to the exchange contract).

**Why there are separate forms rather than one contract with options.** The three property types attract
different due diligence, different financing, different disclosures, and different appurtenances. A vacant
land or farm and ranch transaction reaches **water rights, mineral rights, growing crops, access, and
equipment**; a commercial transaction reaches **leases in place, tenant estoppels, environmental review and
the buyer's intended use**; a residential transaction reaches **the seller's property disclosure,
inspection, and the buyer's financing**.

> **A recurring boundary.** **No Colorado water law statute, mineral rights statute, or environmental
> statute was read by this project**, and **this course states no rule on whether or how any of those pass,
> what must be disclosed about them, or what due diligence they require.** For a farm and ranch, vacant land
> or commercial listing, those are questions for **counsel and the relevant professional**, and **Rule
> 6.2.A** makes it a requirement rather than advice: a broker **must** possess the necessary experience,
> training and knowledge to provide the service, and **Rule 6.2.B** gives four routes if they do not —
> **decline**, obtain them, obtain assistance, or **co-list**.
>
> **The Colorado Common Interest Ownership Act was NOT read**, and **nothing about what the Common Interest
> Community Addendum requires, discloses or delivers is stated anywhere in this build.**

## B. What a contract to buy and sell must settle

**A checklist of what the instrument settles, in roughly the order a broker completes it.** It is **not** a
statement of any Commission form's contents.

### B.1 The parties and the property

1. **Buyer and seller**, identified **exactly as they hold or will hold title.** Entities, trusts and
   estates raise **authority** questions — who may sign — that go to **counsel and the title company.**
2. **The property**, by the description that identifies it **unambiguously.** A street address is an
   identifier, not a legal description.
3. **Inclusions and exclusions** — what personal property goes and what stays. **§ 12-10-403(4)(a)**
   expressly authorises a broker to complete standard forms *"**including standard forms intended to convey
   personal property as part of the real estate transaction.**"*

### B.2 The money

4. **Purchase price**, and **how it is to be paid** — the sum of the parts must equal the price.
5. **Earnest money** — **amount**, **who holds it**, and **what happens to it in each outcome.**
6. **The financing structure** — Part C below.

> **Three Colorado rules a completed contract must be consistent with, all read in full.**
>
> **Rule 5.12.A**: *"**Any Broker receiving earnest money must deliver such earnest money to the earnest
> money holder to be deposited in accordance with the contract.** The Broker **must obtain a dated and
> signed receipt** from the person or entity to whom the Broker has been instructed to deliver the
> deposit."*
>
> **Rule 5.12.B**: if the **brokerage firm** holds it, it is deposited under **Rule 5.7.B** — *"**no later
> than THREE (3) BUSINESS DAYS following receipt of funds or mutual execution of contract, whichever is
> later**"*; and a transfer to a lawyer or closing entity likewise requires **a dated and signed receipt.**
>
> **Rule 5.13**: if a **promissory note** is taken as earnest money, **the seller must be informed of the
> date it becomes due**, and if payment is not made by that date the broker **must promptly notify the
> seller and deliver the original promissory note.**
>
> **A contract naming an earnest money holder the broker then fails to deliver to is not a paperwork
> problem; it is a Chapter 5 problem**, and **Rule 5.22** holds the **employing broker jointly responsible
> with the firm** for Chapter 5 compliance.

### B.3 The dates and the conditions

7. **Every date the contract runs on**, consistent with each other. The **Practical Applications** outline
   names this at its Topic VI.D.3 — *"**Consistency of dates**"* — and it is the single most common defect
   in a new broker's completed contract: a loan objection deadline after the closing date, an inspection
   deadline before the contract is signed, a title objection deadline that leaves no time to cure.
8. **Each contingency**, complete.

> **The three-part test for a contingency.** A contingency clause is not finished until it states:
>
> 1. **WHAT EVENT** must occur;
> 2. **BY WHEN** it must occur; and
> 3. **WHAT HAPPENS** if it does not.
>
> An event with no deadline, or a deadline with no consequence, has settled nothing. **This is the check
> applied to every completed contract in this module.**

**The ordinary contingencies**: financing; inspection; appraisal; title review; survey; sale of the buyer's
existing property; and, in a common interest community, review of association documents.

### B.4 Title, closing and possession

9. **Title and conveyance** — what is to be delivered, and what the buyer may object to.
10. **Closing date and place**, and who conducts it.
11. **Possession** — **when, and by whom.** The **Practical Applications** outline names **early
    occupancy** at its Topic VI.L, and it is a question that ordinarily needs counsel and an insurance
    conversation before anyone agrees to it.
12. **Prorations** — what is prorated and **as of when**, on the convention the contract states.
13. **Signature and acceptance provisions**, including **how and by when acceptance must be communicated.**

**An acceptance signed and not communicated has not been communicated.** Fixing the method and the deadline
in the document removes the argument about whether and when a contract was formed.

### B.5 The advice the statute requires with every one of them

**§ 12-10-403(4)(c)**, closing sentence: *"In any transaction described in this subsection (4), the broker
**shall advise the parties that the forms have important legal consequences and that the parties should
consult legal counsel before signing the forms.**"* **Rule 7.1** repeats it.

**It is not a formality and it is not discharged by the form's own printed language.** It is something the
broker says.

## C. The three financing structures the outline requires

> *"**Include examples of new financing, loan assumption, and owner carry financing.**"*

### C.1 New financing

The buyer obtains a **new loan** from a lender.

**What the contract must settle:** the **loan type and terms** the buyer will seek; the **deadlines** for
application, for the loan objection, and for the appraisal; **what happens if the loan is not obtained**;
and **who pays which loan costs.**

**The appraisal problem is the one that surfaces late.** A lender lends against the **lesser** of price or
appraised value, so an appraisal below the contract price leaves a gap the buyer must close with cash, a
renegotiated price, or a termination on the appraisal deadline. **Whether the contract permits that
termination depends on what the appraisal contingency says** — event, deadline, consequence.

### C.2 Loan assumption

The buyer **takes over the seller's existing loan** on its terms.

**What the contract must settle:** which loan; **whether the lender's approval is required and by when**;
what happens if approval is refused; **the balance as of closing** and how the difference between it and
the price is paid; and — the item that matters most — **whether the seller is RELEASED.**

**Without a written release the seller may remain liable on the note after closing.** The alienation
(due-on-sale) clause is why the lender is involved at all, and the release is a separate document the
lender must issue. **A broker ensures the seller understands the question and takes it to counsel**;
**§§ 12-10-404(1)(c)(V), 12-10-405(1)(c)(V) and 12-10-407(2)(b)(II)** each require advising the party to
obtain expert advice on material matters beyond the broker's expertise.

### C.3 Owner carry financing

**The seller finances the purchase**, taking back a **note** secured by the property — or selling on a
**contract for deed**, where the seller **retains legal title** and the buyer takes **equitable title** and
possession.

**"Owner carry" is the Colorado term of art** and a broker will meet it in exactly that form.

**What the contract must settle:** the **amount carried**; the **rate, payment and term**; **whether it
amortises or balloons, and when**; **what secures it**, and **in what lien position**; **who prepares the
note and security instrument**; **what happens on default**; **insurance and taxes** during the term; and
**whether the buyer may prepay or transfer.**

> **The largest boundary in this module.** **Colorado's foreclosure statutes, its public trustee system, and
> its law governing contracts for deed were NOT read by this project.** **No Colorado foreclosure procedure,
> notice, period, cure right, redemption right, forfeiture rule or deficiency rule is stated anywhere in
> this build.**
>
> An owner carry transaction is one in which **the seller becomes a lender** and the consequences of default
> are governed entirely by law this course has not read. **The note and the security instrument are prepared
> by counsel** — they are not among the eight kinds of standard form **§ 12-10-403(4)(b)** authorises a
> broker to complete unless the Commission has promulgated one and it is appropriate for the transaction.
>
> **Module 05 of this course** carries the outline's Topic V.A, **Promissory Notes**, and Topic V.B, **liens
> and encumbrances including deeds of trust and foreclosure**, and states the same boundary there.

## D. The related forms

The outline lists four at Topic IV.B, and requires **one of each** completed.

### D.1 Counterproposal — IV.B.1

**What it is.** The instrument by which a party responds to an offer **on different terms.**

**What it does at law.** A counter-offer **rejects the original offer.** The original cannot then be
accepted unless it is renewed, and the counterproposal operates as a **new offer** the original offeror may
accept or reject.

**The error it prevents.** A student who changes a term on the face of the contract and initials it has
produced a document the parties will disagree about. **The counterproposal states which terms it changes
and which of the original terms survive**, so there is one answer to what the parties agreed.

**The Practical Applications outline names counter offers and multiple offers together** at its Topic
VI.E.1 and VI.E.2 — and **§§ 12-10-404(1)(c)(II), 12-10-405(1)(c)(II) and 12-10-407(2)(b)(I)** all require
presenting **all** offers **regardless** of an existing contract, so a counterproposal outstanding does not
stop the next offer from being presented.

### D.2 Agreement to Amend/Extend Contract — IV.B.2

**What it is.** The instrument that **changes or extends the contract to buy and sell** after it is formed —
most often a deadline, a date, or a price.

**Not to be confused with the Agreement to Amend/Extend Contract with Broker** at Topic III.B.3, which
amends the **brokerage relationship agreement.** **One amends the sale; the other amends the employment.**
Using the wrong one produces a document that changes nothing.

**The Closings outline names this too**, at its Topic III.A.1: *"**Close to contract or amend contract in
writing.**"* The instruction is the whole discipline in six words — **the closing follows the contract, and
if the parties want something different, the contract changes first, in writing.**

### D.3 Licensee's Buyout Addendum — IV.B.3

**What it is.** The instrument used where a **licensee** is buying the property — the licensee taking the
role of principal.

**Two Colorado rules attach immediately, and both were read.**

> **Rule 6.17.B**: *"If a Broker **sells, buys, or leases real property on the Broker's own account**, such
> Broker must **disclose in the contracting instrument, or in a separate concurrent writing, that they are a
> licensed Broker.**"*
>
> **Rule 6.17.A**: brokers and firms have a **continuing duty to disclose, in writing, any known Conflict of
> Interest** that may arise in the course of any real estate transaction.

**A licensee buying is not doing anything prohibited.** They are required to say, in writing, in the
contracting instrument or a separate concurrent writing, that they hold a licence.

### D.4 Inspection Notice — IV.B.4

**What it is.** The instrument by which a buyer states the result of the inspection — objecting, resolving,
or terminating — within the inspection deadline the contract set.

**The error it prevents.** An objection made by email, by telephone, or after the deadline is not the notice
the contract required, and a buyer who misses the deadline may have accepted the property's condition.
**This is the clearest illustration of the three-part contingency test**: the event (an inspection), the
deadline (a date in the contract), and the consequence (what happens if the buyer objects, and what happens
if they say nothing).

**And it is where the broker's own disclosure duty runs alongside.** Whatever the inspection reports, the
broker's obligation under **§ 12-10-404(3)(a)**, **§ 12-10-405(3)(a)** or **§ 12-10-407(2)(b)(VI) and
(VII)** is to disclose **adverse material facts ACTUALLY KNOWN** — and **§§ 12-10-404(3)(b), 12-10-405(5)
and 12-10-407(4)** each provide there is **no duty to inspect** and **no duty to verify** what an
independent inspector said.

## E. What a completed contract must show

**This course records each completed contract and related form separately**, and one is accepted as
complete when it shows all of the following.

| Check | What is looked for |
|---|---|
| **Parties correct** | Exactly as they hold or will hold title |
| **Property identified unambiguously** | Not by street address alone |
| **Price and payment reconcile** | The parts sum to the price |
| **Earnest money complete** | Amount, holder, and disposition in each outcome — consistent with **Rules 5.7.B, 5.12 and 5.13** |
| **Financing structure stated** | New financing, assumption, or owner carry, with what each requires |
| **Every contingency complete** | **Event, deadline, consequence** — all three, every time |
| **Dates consistent** | No deadline out of sequence with another |
| **Title, closing and possession settled** | Including who conducts the closing |
| **Prorations stated** | What, and as of when, on the convention the contract states |
| **Acceptance provisions stated** | How and by when acceptance must be communicated |
| **Conflict disclosed** | **Rule 6.17.B** where the broker is a principal; **Rule 6.17.A** for any known conflict |
| **Nothing invented** | No term, figure, period or provision supplied from another state or from memory |

**And across the set, the outline's own requirement:** the student's completed work must include **new
financing, loan assumption, and owner carry** examples, and **one of each of the four families** and **one
of each of the related forms.**
