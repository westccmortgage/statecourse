# Colorado Real Estate Law and Practice — Module 03

## Contracts, agency relationships with buyers and sellers, and federal requirements

**Course:** Real Estate Law and Practice (48 classroom hours) · **Instructional hours: 12.0** of 48.0
**Commission outline topic:** **III — Contracts, Agency Relationships with Buyer & Sellers, and Federal
Requirements**, allocated **12 hours**
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No Colorado qualifying education hours are available.

> ### What this module is, and what it deliberately is not
>
> The Commission's outline for this course states its objective as *"...a common body of knowledge ... as
> well as **related federal laws practiced in a majority of states on a national level**."* **This is the
> national agency and contract module.**
>
> **Colorado's own brokerage relationship law is a different course.** The **Contracts and Regulations**
> course carries **Topic II, Colorado Statutory Relationships**, allocated four hours, and it delivers
> **§§ 12-10-402 through 12-10-411** section by section. **This module supplies the general framework that
> Colorado's statute then displaces in specific and important ways**, and it says so at each point rather
> than letting a student carry a general rule into a Colorado transaction.
>
> **Three places where Colorado departs from the general account, flagged here and delivered there:**
>
> | General rule taught here | Colorado's own rule |
> |---|---|
> | Agency arises from the relationship, often by default | **§ 12-10-403(2): a broker is a TRANSACTION-BROKER unless single agency is established through a WRITTEN agreement** |
> | Dual agency is permitted with informed consent | **§ 12-10-406: "A broker shall not establish dual agency with any seller, landlord, buyer, or tenant."** |
> | An agent's acts bind the principal and the firm | **§ 12-10-403(6)(b): the relationship does NOT extend to the employing broker or to the firm** — though § 12-10-408(2)(e)(II) preserves supervision and vicarious liability |
>
> The outline's sub-topics **A** through **F** are delivered in the Commission's own order.

## Learning objectives

1. State the elements of an enforceable contract and the terminology the outline names.
2. Distinguish listing agreements and buyer agency agreements as employment contracts.
3. State what a purchase contract must settle and how a contingency clause is completed.
4. State the general fiduciary duties, and state where Colorado's statute replaces them.
5. State a broker's obligation on property conditions and disclosures.
6. State the protected classes under the federal Fair Housing Act and identify steering and blockbusting.
7. Name the other federal requirements the Commission's outline lists and say what each governs.
8. State the firm/licensee relationship concepts the outline names.

## A. Contract elements, types and terminology

### A.1 The elements

| Element | In a real estate transaction |
|---|---|
| **Offer** | A definite proposal, communicated, showing intent to be bound on stated terms |
| **Acceptance** | Unqualified assent to those exact terms, communicated to the offeror |
| **Consideration** | Something of legal value each way |
| **Capacity** | Legal ability of each party to contract |
| **Legality of object** | A lawful purpose |
| **Mutual assent** | A genuine meeting of the minds |
| **Writing** | Required for a contract for the sale of an interest in land |

**A counter-offer rejects the original offer.** The original cannot then be accepted unless renewed, and
the counter operates as a new offer. A document drafted to look like an acceptance while changing a term
leaves the parties disagreeing about which paper controls — the most common drafting failure a new broker
produces.

**Acceptance must be communicated.** One signed and left in a drawer has not been. Say in the document
**how and by when** acceptance must be communicated.

### A.2 Terminology the outline expects

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Bilateral / unilateral** | Promise for a promise / promise for an act |
| **Express / implied** | Stated in words / inferred from conduct |
| **Executed / executory** | Fully performed / still to be performed |
| **Valid / void / voidable / unenforceable** | Fully effective / no effect from the outset / effective until avoided by the party with that power / valid but not enforceable in court |
| **Novation** | Substitution of a new party or contract, **releasing** the original |
| **Assignment** | Transfer of rights, which does **not** by itself release the assignor |
| **Contingency** | An obligation made dependent on a stated event |
| **Condition precedent** | An event that must occur **before** a duty arises |
| **Covenant** | A promise; failure to perform is a **breach** |
| **Liquidated damages** | An agreed sum stated in the contract |
| **Specific performance** | A remedy compelling performance rather than paying damages |

**Assignment and novation are different, and the difference is whether the original party is still
liable.** That is a legal question and it goes to counsel.

### A.3 The statute of frauds, in general terms

A contract for the sale of an interest in land must ordinarily be **in writing** and **signed by the party
to be charged**, identifying the **parties**, the **property**, and the **price or a means of determining
it**.

**Colorado's own statute of frauds was NOT read by this project**, and **no Colorado section number,
wording or exception is stated in this course.** The **Contracts and Regulations** outline names the
Statute of Frauds under its Topic I.B.4, and that is where a Colorado answer belongs.

### A.4 Completing a contingency

A contingency clause is not finished until it states **three** things:

1. **What event**;
2. **By when**; and
3. **What happens if the event does not occur.**

An event with no deadline, or a deadline with no consequence, has settled nothing. This is the drafting
discipline the **Contracts and Regulations** course then applies to **Commission-approved forms**, whose
Topic IV requires students to complete one of each of four families of sales contracts and one of each of
the related forms.

## B. Agency and employment contracts: listing and buyer agency agreements

### B.1 What they are

A **listing agreement** is a contract of **employment**: an owner employs a broker. It **conveys nothing**
and it is **not an offer to sell.** A **buyer agency agreement** is the same instrument facing the other
way.

**What either must settle:** the parties, identified as they hold or will hold title; the property or the
search criteria; price and terms; the broker's authority; a **definite term with a beginning and an end**;
compensation and what earns it; cooperation and compensation with other brokers; the party's own
undertakings; any express limitation on authority; and signatures.

### B.2 The general families

| Listing type | What the owner gives up |
|---|---|
| **Open listing** | Nothing exclusive — the owner may sell themselves or engage others, and pays only the broker who produces |
| **Exclusive agency** | Exclusivity against other brokers, but the owner may still sell themselves without compensation |
| **Exclusive right to sell** | Full exclusivity — compensation is earned however the sale comes about during the term |

> **In Colorado these are Commission-promulgated forms, and the Contracts course requires the student to
> complete them.** The Contracts and Regulations outline's Topic III.A names **six families**: Exclusive
> Right-to-Sell, Exclusive Agency Listing, Open Listing, Exclusive Right-to-Buy, Exclusive Right-to-Lease
> Listing, and Exclusive Tenant Contracts — and requires *"a minimum of one of each of the 6 families."*
>
> **No Commission-approved form was obtained by this project**, and **no module of this build reproduces
> one or states its contents.** That is recorded as the build's largest submission gate.

### B.3 The Colorado duration rule, flagged here

The general law lets the parties fix the term. **Colorado adds a backstop that runs whether or not anyone
remembers it:**

> **§ 12-10-409(1)(b)**: if the agreement is not performed or completed for any reason, the relationship
> ends at the **earlier** of any agreed expiration date, any termination or relinquishment by the parties,
> or **one year after the date of the engagement.**

Delivered in full by the **Contracts and Regulations** course, Topic II.H.

## C. Purchase and sales contracts and contingencies

**What a purchase contract must settle:** parties; property; price and how paid; earnest money and its
disposition in each outcome; closing date; possession; each contingency with **event, deadline and
consequence**; title and conveyance; prorations; and signature and acceptance provisions.

**The common contingencies:** financing; inspection; appraisal; title review; sale of the buyer's existing
property; survey; and, in common interest communities, review of association documents.

> **Two Colorado rules govern this in practice, and both were read.**
>
> **§ 12-10-403(4)(c)** and **Rule 7.1.A**: *"**A Broker must use a Commission-Approved Form when such form
> exists and is appropriate for the transaction.**"* A broker in Colorado is not drafting a purchase
> contract from scratch; they are completing the Commission's.
>
> **§ 12-10-403(4)(c)**, closing sentence: the broker *"**shall advise the parties that the forms have
> important legal consequences and that the parties should consult legal counsel before signing the
> forms.**"*
>
> Both are delivered by the **Contracts and Regulations** course, whose Topic IV allocates sixteen hours to
> the sales contracts and related forms.

**Earnest money in Colorado is governed by rules this project read**, and a student meets them in the
**Record Keeping and Trust Accounts** course: **Rule 5.12** requires a broker receiving earnest money to
deliver it to the earnest money holder **and obtain a dated and signed receipt**; **Rule 5.7.B** fixes
deposit **no later than three business days** following receipt or mutual execution, whichever is later.

## D. General agency relationships and fiduciary responsibilities

### D.1 The general account

At common law an agent owes a principal:

| Duty | What it requires |
|---|---|
| **Loyalty** | To put the principal's interest ahead of the agent's own |
| **Obedience** | To follow **lawful** instructions |
| **Disclosure** | To tell the principal what is material |
| **Confidentiality** | To keep the principal's confidences |
| **Reasonable care and skill** | To perform competently |
| **Accounting** | To account for money and property |

**Agency may arise** by express agreement, by implication from conduct, by ratification, or by estoppel.
**Actual authority** is what the principal conferred; **apparent authority** is what a third party
reasonably believes because of **the principal's own conduct.**

**A client is the party to whom agency duties are owed; a customer is a party the broker deals with but
does not represent.**

### D.2 Where Colorado departs, stated plainly

This is the point of putting the general account in a national course, and a student who leaves this module
carrying only the general account into a Colorado transaction will be wrong three times over.

**First — the default.** **§ 12-10-403(2)**: *"**A broker shall be considered a transaction-broker unless a
single agency relationship is established through a written agreement** between the broker and the party or
parties to be represented by the broker."* Agency in Colorado is **opt-in and in writing.** A broker who
signs nothing is a **transaction-broker**, and **§ 12-10-407(1)** says a transaction-broker *"**is not an
agent for either party.**"*

**Second — dual agency.** **§ 12-10-406** in its entirety: *"**A broker shall not establish dual agency
with any seller, landlord, buyer, or tenant.**"* No consent cures it, because the statute offers no consent
mechanism. **Rule 6.7** then states the three lawful ways a broker or team may work with both consumers:
transaction-broker for both; transaction-broker for one and the other as a **customer**; or single agent
for one and the other as a **customer.**

**Third — who the relationship binds.** **§ 12-10-403(6)(b)**: the relationship with a **designated broker**
*"**shall not extend to the employing broker nor to any other broker** ... **and shall not extend to the
firm.**"* And immediately, **§ 12-10-408(2)(e)(II)**: *"**Nothing ... shall be construed to limit the
employing broker's or firm's responsibility to supervise licensees** ... **nor to shield the broker or firm
from vicarious liability.**"* **Two different things**, and a module that merges them misstates both.

**Fourth, and it is not a departure but an addition — Colorado's single agent is a LIMITED agent.**
**§§ 12-10-404(1) and 12-10-405(1)** each open: *"A broker engaged by a [party] ... **is a limited agent**
with the following duties and obligations"* — and then list them, closing with the requirement to promote
the party's interests *"**with the utmost good faith, loyalty, and fidelity.**"* **The Contracts and
Regulations course delivers both lists in full.**

## E. Property conditions and disclosures

**The general standard a broker works to:** disclose what you know that is material; do not misrepresent;
do not conceal; and route every question of condition to a professional qualified to answer it.

**Colorado's own standard was read and it uses a specific phrase — "actually known".**

> **§ 12-10-404(3)(a)** — a seller's or landlord's agent owes no duty to the buyer or tenant *"except that
> a broker shall ... **disclose to any prospective buyer or tenant all ADVERSE MATERIAL FACTS ACTUALLY
> KNOWN** by the broker ... including ... adverse material facts pertaining to **the title and the physical
> condition of the property, any material defects in the property, and any environmental hazards** affecting
> the property that are required by law to be disclosed."*
>
> **§ 12-10-407(2)(b)(VI) and (VII)** impose the same on a transaction-broker, in both directions.
>
> And in the other direction — **§ 12-10-405(3)(a)** — a buyer's agent must disclose to a prospective seller
> all adverse material facts actually known *"**including ... adverse material facts concerning the buyer's
> or tenant's financial ability to perform the terms of the transaction and whether the buyer intends to
> occupy the property to be purchased as a principal residence.**"*

**And the counterpart:** **§§ 12-10-404(3)(b), 12-10-405(5) and 12-10-407(4) and (5)** each provide that
the broker **owes no duty to conduct an independent inspection** of the property, and **no duty to
independently verify** statements made by a party or an independent inspector.

**Environmental conditions a broker may encounter:** soil and groundwater contamination; **asbestos and
lead** in older construction; **radon**; **mould and moisture intrusion**; underground storage tanks; and,
in some areas, **methamphetamine contamination**. In every case the broker's role is the same: **recognise
that a question exists, say so, and route it to someone qualified.** **Rule 6.2** gives the four routes.

**The federal lead-based paint requirements for pre-1978 housing** are named by the **Current Legal Issues**
outline at its Appendix E. **The federal rule text was NOT read by this project**, and **no form, deadline,
period or penalty from it is stated anywhere in this build.**

**§ 38-35.5-101, psychologically impacted property**, is cited by **four** provisions read for this build —
§§ 12-10-404(2)(e), 12-10-405(2)(e), 12-10-407(3)(e) and 12-10-404(3)(a). **The statute itself was NOT
read**, and **this course states nothing about what it provides.** What it does state is what the duty
sections say: facts or suspicions regarding circumstances that may psychologically impact or stigmatize
real property **pursuant to § 38-35.5-101** are **not disclosed without informed consent.**

## F. Federal procedures and laws governing real estate

The Commission's outline names these by number. Each is here, with what it governs.

### F.1 The Federal Fair Housing Act

**Protected classes:** **race, colour, religion, national origin, sex, disability, and familial status.**

| Prohibited practice | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Steering** | Directing prospects toward or away from areas or properties on the basis of a protected characteristic, **whatever reason is offered** |
| **Blockbusting** | Inducing or attempting to induce owners to sell or rent by representations about the entry of persons of a protected characteristic — prohibited **whether or not the representation is true and whether or not any property changes hands** |
| **Redlining** | Denying or varying the terms of loans or insurance by the area's composition |
| **Discriminatory advertising** | Advertising indicating a preference, limitation or discrimination |
| **Refusal to make a reasonable accommodation** | Refusing a change in rules, policies, practices or services needed for a person with a disability to use a dwelling |
| **Refusal to permit a reasonable modification** | Refusing to permit a physical change to the premises |

**When asked directly about the composition of a neighbourhood:** decline to characterise it, say why, and
point the enquirer to publicly available data. **The honesty of the answer is not the issue; the effect of
it is.**

**State and local law may reach further.** **The Colorado Fair Housing Act was NOT read by this project**,
and **no additional Colorado protected class, exemption or remedy is stated in this course.** It is named
by the **Contracts and Regulations** outline at Topic I.D, and that is where it belongs.

**Colorado's statute puts fair housing inside the duty sections.** **§§ 12-10-404(1)(e), 12-10-405(1)(e)
and 12-10-407(2)(d)** each require the broker to *"comply with any applicable federal, state, or local
laws, rules, regulations, or ordinances **including fair housing and civil rights statutes or
regulations**"* — so a fair housing violation is also a violation of the brokerage relationship statute, and
**§ 12-10-411** makes a Part 4 violation an act the Commission may investigate.

### F.2 The Americans with Disabilities Act

Governs **accessibility in places of public accommodation and commercial facilities**, and employment. It
is not the same statute as the Fair Housing Act and it does not have the same reach: **ADA questions in
commercial property are questions for counsel and for an accessibility professional.** **The ADA text was
NOT read by this project** and **no requirement, standard, dimension or deadline from it is stated here.**

### F.3 Antitrust

**Price fixing, market allocation, group boycotts and tying arrangements** are the classic categories.

**What that means for a broker in practice:** commission rates are set **between the brokerage and its
client**, never by agreement among competing firms, and never described to a client as standard, customary,
or set by anyone. A conversation with a competitor about what to charge is the conversation to leave.

**No antitrust statute was read by this project**, and **no penalty, threshold or exemption is stated
here.** The principle is stated because the outline names the subject and because the practice consequence
does not depend on the statutory detail.

### F.4 Marketing controls

The outline names four, and each is here:

| **a. UCC** | The Uniform Commercial Code, which governs transactions in **goods** and secured interests in personal property — reached in a real estate transaction where personal property is conveyed alongside the realty. **§ 12-10-403(4)(a)** authorises a broker to complete standard forms *"including standard forms intended to convey personal property as part of the real estate transaction."* |
|---|---|
| **b. Interstate Land Sales Full Disclosure** | Federal disclosure and registration requirements applying to certain sales of subdivided land across state lines |
| **c. Telephone Consumer Protection** | Federal restrictions on telephone solicitation, automated calls and faxes — the statute behind do-not-call compliance in prospecting |
| **d. Securities laws** | Certain real estate interests, particularly fractional and passive investment interests, may be **securities**; note that **§ 12-10-201(6)** exempts licensed broker-dealers from parts of the licensing provisions, and that offering an interest that is a security is a matter for securities counsel |

**None of these four statutes was read by this project.** **No registration threshold, exemption, deadline,
call-time restriction, disclosure requirement or penalty from any of them is stated anywhere in this
build**, and a broker whose practice touches one takes it to counsel. **Rule 6.2** again: decline, obtain
the knowledge, obtain assistance, or co-list.

### F.5 Firm and licensee relationships

The outline's last sub-item, and it is the bridge to Colorado's own supervision rules.

**The general concepts:** an employing broker and the licensees under them; the difference between an
**employee** and an **independent contractor** for tax purposes; the firm's **written office policy**; and
the firm's responsibility for what its people do.

**Colorado's own requirements were read, and three are worth stating here because a student meets them
immediately:**

> **§ 12-10-408(1)(a)**: every broker *"**shall adopt a WRITTEN OFFICE POLICY that identifies and describes
> the relationships offered to the public** by the broker."*
>
> **§ 12-10-408(1)(b)**: *"A broker **shall not be required to offer or engage in any one or in all** of the
> brokerage relationships"* — a firm may decline to offer agency, or to offer transaction-brokerage.
>
> **Rule 6.3.A.1**: employing brokers *"**must exercise authority, direction, and supervision** over any
> Associate Brokers"*, and *"**whenever a complaint is filed with the Commission against an Associate
> Broker, the Commission may investigate whether there have been violations** ... **by the Employing
> Broker.**"* **Rule 6.3.A.2** extends supervision to **all unlicensed employees**, including on-site
> managers, secretaries, bookkeepers and personal assistants.

The **Practical Applications** course develops this from the new broker's side under its Topic I, *Entering
the Field*, and the **Record Keeping and Trust Accounts** course develops the employing broker's accounting
responsibilities under **Rule 5.22**, which holds the employing broker **jointly responsible with the firm**
for Chapter 5.
