## A. Leasehold estates

A lease does **two** things at once: it **conveys a leasehold estate** — possession for a term, with the
landlord keeping the **reversion** — and it **contains covenants** between the parties. A drafter who
settles one half has finished half the document.

| Estate | How it runs | How it ends |
|---|---|---|
| **Estate for years** | A **fixed term** with a stated beginning and end | **Expires by its own terms** — ordinarily no notice needed |
| **Periodic tenancy** | Period to period — month to month, year to year — **renewing automatically** | On **notice** by either party |
| **Tenancy at will** | At the will of **either** party, with no fixed term | On the will of either party, or by operation of law |
| **Tenancy at sufferance** | A **holdover** after a lawful tenancy has ended | When the holdover ends or is ended |

**The estate for years is not necessarily years long.** A three-month lease with stated dates is an estate
for years. The name describes the certainty of the term, not its length.

**A tenancy at sufferance is not a tenancy the landlord agreed to.** It is what remains when a tenant stays
past a lawful right to possession, and what the landlord may then do is a matter of statute.

> **The notice periods that end a periodic tenancy, and the procedure for ending a holdover, are Colorado
> statutory questions.** **§ 38-12-101 et seq. and Colorado's eviction statutes were NOT read by this
> project**, and **no notice period, no procedure and no remedy is stated anywhere in this build.**
> **Rule 6.2.B** gives the broker four routes, and for a property manager the practical one is to have the
> firm's forms and counsel settled **before** the question arises rather than after.

## B. What a lease settles, and the principal clauses

### B.1 The drafting checklist

**This is a checklist of what a lease must SETTLE. It is not a statement of Colorado legal requirements**,
and the exercise instruction in this course says so on its face.

1. **Parties** — landlord and tenant, identified exactly.
2. **Premises** — what is let, and anything included or excluded.
3. **Term** — beginning, end, and what happens at the end.
4. **Rent** — amount, when due, where and how paid, and what happens if it is late.
5. **Deposits** — amount, purpose, and the conditions of return.
6. **Use** — what the premises may be used for.
7. **Maintenance and repair** — allocated between the parties, item by item.
8. **Utilities and services** — who pays and who arranges.
9. **Assignment and subletting** — permitted, prohibited, or conditional.
10. **Default and remedies** — what constitutes default and what follows.
11. **Access by the landlord** — when, and on what notice.
12. **Insurance** — who carries what.
13. **Signatures.**

**Any figure or period a student writes into items 3, 4, 5, 10 or 11 must come from the brokerage's forms
and from counsel, not from this course.**

### B.2 Assignment and subletting are different

| | What transfers | Who remains liable |
|---|---|---|
| **Assignment** | The tenant's **entire remaining interest** | The original tenant, unless released |
| **Sublease** | **Part** of the term or of the premises, the sublessor keeping a reversion | The original tenant, to the landlord |

**In neither case is the original tenant released without the landlord's agreement.** That agreement is a
**novation**, and it is a legal document.

### B.3 How rent is calculated

| Lease | Who pays operating expenses |
|---|---|
| **Gross** | The **landlord**, out of the rent |
| **Net** | The **tenant** pays some or all of them in addition to base rent |
| **Percentage** | Rent varies with the **tenant's sales**, ordinarily a base rent plus a percentage over a breakpoint |
| **Ground** | The tenant leases land and ordinarily improves it, for a long term |
| **Graduated / escalated** | Rent changes on stated dates or by a stated index |

**"Net" says nothing by itself.** Which expenses a net lease actually shifts is settled item by item in the
document, and a lease that says "net" without saying which expenses has settled nothing.

**Percentage rent worked, with every figure given:** base rent **$4,000** a month plus **5 percent** of
gross sales over a **$80,000** monthly breakpoint. In a month with **$140,000** of sales, the overage is
**$60,000**, the percentage rent is **$3,000**, and total rent is **$7,000.**

## C. Rights, responsibilities and recourse

### C.1 The general framework

| | Landlord | Tenant |
|---|---|---|
| **Principal right** | To receive rent, and to have the premises returned in the agreed condition | To **quiet enjoyment** — possession without wrongful interference by the landlord |
| **Principal duty** | To deliver and maintain the premises as the lease and the law require | To pay rent and to use the premises as the lease permits |
| **On the other's default** | The remedies the lease and the statute provide | The remedies the lease and the statute provide |

**Constructive eviction** describes a landlord's conduct that so interferes with the tenant's use that the
tenant is treated as evicted. **Actual eviction** is dispossession. **What either requires, and what a
tenant must do to claim it, is a matter of Colorado law this project did not read.**

> **This is the largest gap in this module and it is stated on its face.** Colorado's residential and
> commercial landlord-tenant statutes were **NOT** read. **This course states no rule on**: security deposit
> limits or return periods; notice to enter; habitability or the warranty of habitability; late fees;
> repair and deduct; retaliation; lease termination; eviction procedure or timelines; or any tenant remedy.
>
> **A property manager needs those answers before taking an assignment**, and they come from the statute,
> from the brokerage's forms, and from counsel. **Rule 6.2.A** puts it as a requirement: a broker **must
> possess the necessary experience, training, and knowledge** to provide the service and to **maintain
> compliance with the applicable federal, state and local laws.** If not, **Rule 6.2.B.1** is available:
> **decline.**

### C.2 Fair housing runs through leasing

Every prohibition in Module 03 Part F.1 applies to leasing as fully as to sales — **and the applications
that most often go wrong are in leasing**: screening criteria applied unevenly; occupancy standards used as
a proxy for familial status; advertising that indicates a preference; refusal of a **reasonable
accommodation** in rules, policies, practices or services; and refusal to permit a **reasonable
modification** of the premises.

**The safe practice is one written set of criteria applied to every applicant in the same order**, with the
result recorded.

## D. Management contracts and the obligations of the parties

### D.1 What a management agreement settles

1. **Parties**, and the property managed.
2. **Term**, and how it ends.
3. **The manager's authority** — what the manager may do without asking, and what needs the owner's
   approval, **including a dollar threshold for repairs.**
4. **Compensation** — how calculated, when taken, and out of what.
5. **Trust accounting** — which accounts, and how reported.
6. **Owner reserves** — how much is held and what it may be used for.
7. **Reporting** — what reports, and when.
8. **Leasing authority** — terms the manager may accept.
9. **Maintenance and vendors** — who selects, who pays, and what disclosures.
10. **Insurance and indemnity.**
11. **Termination**, including **what happens to funds and documents.**

**Item 11 is the one most often left thin**, and Colorado's own course outline for **Record Keeping and
Trust Accounts** devotes a topic to it: **Topic IV, Termination of Brokerage Relationship**, half an hour,
covering *"Forwarding Trust Funds and Documents"* and *"Closing Accounting Records."*

### D.2 The functions of a property manager

Marketing and leasing space; screening applicants against consistent criteria; collecting rent;
maintaining the property; controlling operating costs; keeping records and reporting to the owner; and
complying with the law applicable to the tenancy.

**In Colorado the relationship is a brokerage relationship like any other.** **§ 12-10-403(2)** applies: a
broker is a **transaction-broker** unless single agency is established in **writing**. **§§ 12-10-404 and
12-10-405** speak of *"seller **or landlord**"* and *"buyer **or tenant**"* throughout, so the duty lists
run to management relationships in their own terms.

### D.3 The Colorado money rules that apply specifically to property management

**All four were read in full and each is transcribed in `colorado/regulatory/primary-text-readings.md` § 4.**

> **Rule 5.5 — separate accounts.** A brokerage firm engaging in property management *"**must deposit rental
> receipts and security deposits and disburse money collected for such purposes in SEPARATE Trust or Escrow
> Accounts, a minimum of one for rental receipts and a minimum of one for security deposits.**"*
>
> **Rule 5.7.A — the five-day deadline.** *"All Money Belonging to Others received by a Brokerage Firm **for
> Property Management** must be deposited ... **no later than FIVE (5) BUSINESS DAYS following receipt of
> funds or mutual execution of a lease, whichever is later.**"*
>
> Compare **Rule 5.7.B**, which gives **all other** Money Belonging to Others **three business days**.
> **Property management money gets five; everything else gets three**, and both are **business** days
> measured from **the later** of the two events.
>
> **Rule 5.8.A — owner-held deposits.** A firm receipting for security deposits *"**will not deliver such
> security deposits to an owner without the tenant's written authorization in a lease or unless written
> notice has been given to the tenant**"* — notice given so the tenant knows **who is holding the deposit**
> and **the procedure for requesting its return.** If the deposit goes to the owner, the management
> agreement **should** place financial responsibility for its return on the owner and **must** authorise
> disclosure of the owner's true name and current mailing address in a dispute.
>
> **Rule 5.8.B — changing management companies.** A firm taking over management **must disclose to the owner
> and current tenant, in writing, within 30 DAYS** of execution or assignment of the management agreement,
> the status of any security deposit held by the previous firm. **The previous firm must transfer the
> deposit within 60 DAYS** and confirm the amount. **The new firm must verify each deposit matches the
> amount in the current lease and disclose any discrepancy**, and **must inform the tenant in writing if the
> owner is holding the deposit.**

**Three more Chapter 5 rules a property manager lives inside:**

- **Rule 5.9** — money belonging to **one** beneficiary must not be used for the benefit of another, and
  never for the firm or broker.
- **Rule 5.14.B** — *"**No ledger may ever be allowed to have a negative cash balance.**"* On a managed
  portfolio that means one owner's funds cannot cover another owner's overdraft, even for a day.
- **Rule 5.15.A** — reports to beneficiaries go out as the management agreement provides and, absent a
  provision to the contrary, **within 30 days after the end of the month** in which funds were received or
  disbursed.

**The Record Keeping and Trust Accounts course delivers all of Chapter 5**, and its outline requires an
exercise in which the student *"shall **reconcile the bank accounts for a property manager managing and
holding security deposits for a minimum of 8 properties.**"*

### D.4 The vendor conflict rule — Rule 6.17.C

This one exists because of property management specifically, and it was read in full.

> **Rule 6.17.C**: *"A Brokerage Firm or Broker engaged in **Property Management Services** has a duty to
> **disclose, in writing, any known Conflict of Interest** that may arise **in the selection or use of a
> business or vendor** that provides services applicable to lease transactions, **including property
> maintenance.** The Brokerage Firm or Broker is required to disclose **any ownership, financial, or
> familial interest** associated with the selection or use of a particular business or vendor."*

**Three interests named: ownership, financial, familial.** A manager who sends maintenance work to a
company they own a piece of, profit from, or are related to must **disclose it in writing** — and
**Rule 5.17** separately requires **prior written consent** before assessing or receiving a **mark-up** on
that work, plus disclosure of all mark-ups paid by any customer, plus records that verify both.

**Rule 6.17.A** sits above it: a **continuing** duty to disclose, in writing, **any known conflict of
interest** in the course of any real estate transaction.

### D.5 Managing property the broker owns

**Rules 5.10.D and 5.10.E** address it directly, and the trigger is the **20 percent** ownership threshold
in **§ 12-10-201(6)(b)(VII)**:

- **5.10.D** — rental proceeds for a broker's own property managed **through a firm the broker is not
  licensed with** must go into a trust or escrow account for Money Belonging to Others.
- **5.10.E** — rental proceeds for property owned by a broker **licensed with that firm** must go into one
  or more trust or escrow accounts **separate from any other** accounts containing Money Belonging to
  Others, though they may hold the proceeds of **other** brokers licensed with the same firm whose own
  properties it manages.

A **broker's property** for both rules includes properties held in **partnership, joint venture or
syndication** where the broker's interest exceeds **20 percent**.

**And Rule 6.17.B** requires a broker who **sells, buys or leases on their own account** to disclose **in
the contracting instrument or a separate concurrent writing** that they are a **licensed Broker.**

## E. The arithmetic of a managed property

Every figure below is supplied as a given.

> **A 16-unit building.** Each unit rents at **$1,450** a month. Vacancy and collection loss runs at
> **6 percent**. Annual operating expenses are **$94,000**.
>
> | Line | Calculation | Amount |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Potential gross income** | 16 × $1,450 × 12 | **$278,400** |
> | less vacancy and collection loss | 6% of $278,400 | ($16,704) |
> | **Effective gross income** | | **$261,696** |
> | less operating expenses | | ($94,000) |
> | **Net operating income** | | **$167,696** |
>
> At a capitalisation rate of **7 percent**, the indicated value is **$167,696 ÷ 0.07 = $2,395,657**
> rounded to the nearest dollar.

**Debt service is not an operating expense**, which is what keeps net operating income a measure of the
**property** rather than of a particular owner's financing. Module 02 Part C.2 builds the same lines.

**A management fee of 7 percent of collected rent** on the effective gross income above is
**$261,696 × 0.07 = $18,318.72** — and **Rule 5.10.A** requires money in a trust or escrow account that
becomes **due and payable to the firm** to be **withdrawn monthly**, while **Rule 5.10.C** provides that
fees are *"earned"* and available **only after all contracted services have been performed and there is no
remaining right of recall.**
