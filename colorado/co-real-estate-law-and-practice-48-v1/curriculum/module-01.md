## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish real from personal property, and state why the distinction decides what a deed conveys.
2. State the methods of legal description and why a street address is not one.
3. Name the estates in real property and distinguish freehold from non-freehold.
4. Distinguish the forms of co-ownership and severalty, and state what legal capacity means.
5. Distinguish public land use control from private, and state the priority principle for liens.
6. Distinguish easements, encroachments, and covenants, conditions and restrictions.
7. State the voluntary and involuntary means of transferring title the Commission's outline names.
8. Distinguish the types of deed by what the grantor warrants, and name the assurances of title.

## A. Definitions, description and ways to hold title

### A.1 Elements of real and personal property

**Real property** is land, everything permanently attached to it, and the rights that go with it.
**Personal property** is movable. The line decides which instrument conveys what: a **deed** conveys real
property, a **bill of sale** conveys personal property, and a purchase contract that names the disputed
items settles the argument before anyone has it.

**Fixtures** sit on the line. The tests ordinarily applied are the **method of attachment**, the
**adaptation** of the item to the property, the **intention** of the party who installed it, and **any
agreement between the parties.** The last of the four is the only one a broker controls, and it is the one
to use.

### A.2 Water rights — named by the Commission's outline

The Commission's outline names **water rights** under this topic, and again under **Topic V of the
Contracts and Regulations course.** That is deliberate: water is a distinct estate in the western states
and it does not necessarily travel with the land.

> **A boundary this module holds.** **No Colorado water law statute was read by this project**, and **this
> course states no Colorado rule on the appropriation, adjudication, transfer, or conveyance of water
> rights.** What is stated is the fact the outline requires a student to know: **water rights may be owned,
> conveyed and encumbered separately from the land**, so a broker who assumes a well or a ditch right
> passes with the parcel has assumed something that may not be true. **That question goes to counsel and to
> a water engineer**, and Rule 6.2's four routes — decline, obtain the knowledge, obtain assistance, or
> co-list — are the broker's own options.

### A.3 Methods of legal description

Three systems:

| System | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Metes and bounds** | A perimeter traced by courses and distances from a point of beginning, returning to it |
| **Rectangular (government) survey** | Townships six miles square, each divided into **36 sections** of **640 acres**, numbered 1 to 36 beginning in the northeast corner in a serpentine pattern |
| **Lot and block** | A lot within a block within a **recorded plat**, referenced to the plat's recording data |

**An acre is 43,560 square feet.** A section is 640 acres; a quarter section is 160; a quarter-quarter is
40. Quarter descriptions are read **right to left**: *the northeast quarter of the southwest quarter of
Section 14* is 40 acres.

**A street address and a tax parcel number are identifiers, not legal descriptions.** They exist for
administration and mail. A conveyance needs the description that identifies the parcel unambiguously, and a
broker completing a Commission-approved form uses the description the record supplies rather than one they
compose.

**The Contracts and Regulations course carries this forward.** Its Topic V names **land descriptions,
surveys and Improvement Location Certificates** — the Colorado-specific practice — and this module supplies
the general framework beneath it.

### A.4 Estates in real property

| Estate | Duration | Family |
|---|---|---|
| **Fee simple absolute** | Potentially infinite, no conditions | Freehold |
| **Defeasible fee** | Potentially infinite, but may end on a stated condition or use ceasing | Freehold |
| **Life estate** | Measured by a life, then to a remainderman or reverting to the grantor | Freehold |
| **Estate for years** | A fixed term with a stated end | Leasehold (non-freehold) |
| **Periodic tenancy** | Period to period, renewing until notice | Leasehold |
| **Tenancy at will** | At the will of either party | Leasehold |
| **Tenancy at sufferance** | A holdover after a lawful tenancy ends | Leasehold |

**Freehold means ownership of indefinite duration; non-freehold means possession for a term.** A life
tenant can convey no more than they hold, which is why a purchaser dealing with a life tenant alone
receives an interest that ends when the measuring life does.

**Module 05 of this course develops the leasehold estates** under the Commission's Topic V.

### A.5 Forms, rights, interests and obligations of ownership

The Commission's outline names five sub-items, and each is here.

**a. Joint ownership.** Two or more owners holding concurrently, each with an **undivided** interest — a
right to possession of the whole rather than to a physical portion. The principal forms are:

| Form | Survivorship | Interests |
|---|---|---|
| **Tenancy in common** | **No** — the decedent's share passes to their estate | May be unequal |
| **Joint tenancy** | **Yes** — the decedent's interest passes to the survivors outside probate | Ordinarily equal |

**How co-owners describe themselves to each other creates nothing.** The **recorded instrument** determines
the form, and whether survivorship exists is a question of what the deed says and of the law that governs
it.

> **A boundary this module holds.** **No Colorado statute on joint tenancy, survivorship, community
> property, intestacy or probate was read by this project**, and **this course states no Colorado rule on
> how a joint tenancy is created, severed, or terminated, and none on what happens to a decedent's
> interest.** A client's question about survivorship is a legal question. **§ 12-10-403(4) and Rule 7.1
> require a broker to advise the parties that standard forms have important legal consequences and that
> they should consult legal counsel before signing**, and a survivorship question is squarely inside that.

**b. Severalty ownership.** Ownership by **one** person or entity alone. The word does not mean "several"
in the ordinary sense — it means severed from anyone else's interest.

**c. Legal capacity.** The legal ability of a person or entity to contract and to convey. Minors, persons
adjudicated incompetent, and entities acting outside their authority raise capacity questions.

**d. Individuals.** An individual conveys in their own name, as they hold title.

**e. Corporations, limited liability companies, partnerships.** **Who may sign for an entity is a question
of the entity's own governing documents and of the statute under which it exists.** A broker identifies
the owner **exactly as they hold title** and routes every authority question — corporate resolution,
operating agreement, partnership authority, trustee's power — **to counsel.** **No Colorado entity statute
was read by this project and none is stated here.**

**Note where Colorado's own rules touch this.** **Rule 5.10.D and 5.10.E** turn on whether a broker's
ownership in an entity or property held in partnership, joint venture or syndication exceeds the **20
percent threshold in § 12-10-201(6)(b)(VII)** — a figure this build states because Chapter 5 was read.
Module 02 of the Trust Accounts and Record Keeping course covers it.

## B. Land use controls and restrictions

### B.1 Public controls

> The **police power** is the government's power to regulate for public health, safety, morals and general
> welfare — **without compensating the owner.** **Eminent domain** is the power to take private property
> for public use **on payment of just compensation.** The absence or presence of compensation is what
> distinguishes them.

**a. Zoning.** Districts, permitted uses, dimensional standards, and the mechanisms that bend them —
**variance** (relief from a dimensional standard on hardship), **special or conditional use permit** (a use
allowed subject to conditions), **rezoning** (a change to the map), and **nonconforming use** (a use lawful
when established that no longer conforms, ordinarily permitted to continue subject to limits on expansion
and on resumption after abandonment).

**The limits on a nonconforming use are what matter to a buyer.** A use that cannot be expanded, rebuilt
after destruction, or resumed after a period of abandonment is worth less than a buyer may assume, and
**every one of those limits is in the local ordinance.** **No Colorado zoning or land use statute and no
local ordinance was read by this project.** The answer for any parcel comes from the jurisdiction that
zoned it.

**b. Taxation.** The Commission's outline names **capital gains, income tax and property tax** under this
head. General property taxes are an **ad valorem** lien on the parcel, ordinarily taking priority over
other liens. Capital gains and income tax consequences of a sale belong to the client's **accountant** —
**§§ 12-10-404(1)(c)(V), 12-10-405(1)(c)(V) and 12-10-407(2)(b)(II) each require the broker to advise the
party to obtain expert advice as to material matters beyond the broker's expertise**, and tax is the
clearest example.

**No Colorado property tax statute, rate, exemption, assessment ratio or deadline was read by this
project, and none is stated in this course.** The Contracts and Regulations course's Topic V names
**general property taxes and special districts**; Module 05 of that course routes them to the Colorado
sources.

**c. Police power.** Named above, and the source of zoning, building codes, subdivision regulation and
environmental controls alike.

### B.2 Private controls

**a. Covenants, conditions and restrictions (CC&Rs).** Private restrictions recorded against the land,
ordinarily enforced by other owners bound by the same scheme or by an association — **not** by a government
body. Where a private restriction and public zoning conflict, **the more restrictive governs the owner's
practical options**, because the owner must satisfy both.

**Colorado has a statute on common interest communities — the Colorado Common Interest Ownership Act,
article 33.3 of title 38 — which the Contracts and Regulations outline names under its Topic I.** **CCIOA
was NOT read by this project, and no module states any of its requirements.**

**b. Types and priority of liens.**

| Distinction | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Voluntary** vs **involuntary** | Created by the owner's act (a deed of trust) vs imposed by law (a judgment, a tax lien, a mechanics' lien) |
| **General** vs **specific** | Attaching to all of a debtor's property vs to identified property |

**Priority.** The general principle is **first in time, first in right** as established by **recording** —
with statutory exceptions that ordinarily include **property tax liens** and, in many states, **mechanics'
liens relating back to an earlier date.** **Colorado's mechanics' lien statute was NOT read by this
project**, and **this course states no Colorado priority rule, relation-back date, filing deadline or
notice requirement.** The Contracts and Regulations outline names **deeds of trust, foreclosure, general
property taxes, special districts and other liens** under its Topic V, and that is where a Colorado answer
belongs.

**Every lien is an encumbrance; not every encumbrance is a lien.** An easement, a restrictive covenant and
an encroachment are encumbrances securing no debt.

**c. Easements.**

| Kind | Benefits | Passes with the land? |
|---|---|---|
| **Easement appurtenant** | A **dominant parcel**, burdening a **servient parcel** | **Yes** — with the dominant estate, whether or not the deed mentions it |
| **Easement in gross** | A **person or entity**, as a utility easement does | Not with a parcel |

Easements may arise by **express grant or reservation**, by **implication**, by **necessity**, or by
**prescription**. **Colorado's own law on prescription and on easement creation was NOT read**, and no
period, standard or element is stated here.

**d. Encroachments.** An unauthorised physical intrusion of an improvement onto adjoining land. **A records
search will not surface one; a survey will.** It is the classic defect that lives outside the recorded
record, which is why a fence, shed or driveway in an unexpected place is a question a broker raises rather
than assumes away. The Contracts and Regulations outline names the **Improvement Location Certificate**
under its Topic V.

**e. Recording and priorities.** Recording gives **constructive notice to the world** and establishes
priority against later claimants. **It does not pass title between grantor and grantee** — delivery and
acceptance do. An unrecorded deed is good between the parties and dangerous against everyone else.

**Constructive notice** is notice the law imputes because a fact is recorded or would be revealed by
inspection, whether or not the person knows it. **Actual notice** is what the person in fact knows.
**Possession is the third source**: a person in visible possession puts a purchaser on **inquiry**, which
is why an unrecorded interest held by an occupant is not the safe assumption a buyer may think.

**Colorado's recording statutes were NOT read by this project.** Which class of recording act Colorado
follows, and what its priority rules provide, are **not stated in this course.**

## C. Transfer and alienation of title

### C.1 Voluntary transfer

By **deed** during life; by **will** at death; by **dedication** of land to public use; by **gift**.

### C.2 Involuntary transfer — the Commission's own list

The outline names seven, and each is here:

| Means | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Dedication** | Transfer of private land to public use, which may be statutory or by common law |
| **Adverse possession** | Title acquired by possession that is, in general terms, actual, open and notorious, hostile, exclusive and continuous for a statutory period |
| **Sheriff's sale** | A judicial sale of property to satisfy a judgment |
| **Foreclosure** | Enforcement of a security interest against the property |
| **Condemnation** | The proceeding by which the power of eminent domain is exercised |
| **Escheat** | Property passing to the state where an owner dies with no will and no heirs |
| **Eminent domain** | The power itself — a taking for public use on payment of just compensation |

> **A boundary this module holds, and it is a large one.** **Colorado's adverse possession statute, its
> foreclosure statutes and public trustee process, its condemnation procedure, its escheat provisions and
> its intestacy statutes were NONE of them read by this project.** **No period, no element, no notice
> requirement, no redemption right and no procedure is stated anywhere in this course.** Every one of these
> is a court proceeding or a statutory process, and a client facing one needs counsel, not a broker.
>
> Colorado's **public trustee** system is distinctive among the states, and this course does not describe it
> because the statute establishing it was not read.

### C.3 Deeds, warranties and defects in title

**a. Types**, distinguished by **what the grantor warrants** rather than by the size of the estate
conveyed:

| Deed | What the grantor warrants |
|---|---|
| **General warranty** | Against defects arising **at any time**, including before the grantor owned |
| **Special warranty** | Against defects arising **during the grantor's own ownership** only |
| **Bargain and sale** | Ordinarily implies the grantor holds title, with limited or no express warranty |
| **Quitclaim** | **Nothing.** It conveys whatever interest the grantor has, if any |

**A quitclaim and a general warranty deed can convey an identical estate.** What differs is the promise
about it, and that promise is the bargain. A buyer paying market value and receiving no warranty has taken
a risk they may not have understood.

**b. Characteristics and elements.** A competent grantor and an identifiable grantee; **words of
conveyance**; an adequate **description**; the **grantor's signature**; and **delivery to and acceptance by
the grantee.** **Delivery and acceptance are the elements students forget**, and they decide when title
actually passed. A signed deed in a drawer has conveyed nothing.

**c. Warranties.** The covenants traditionally associated with a general warranty deed — seisin, right to
convey, against encumbrances, quiet enjoyment, warranty, and further assurance.

**A broker does not select the form of deed.** The choice determines what the grantor warrants and what
the grantee receives, which is a legal question. **Rule 6.2.B** gives the broker four routes and the first
is to **decline**; **§ 12-10-403(4)** and **Rule 7.1** require the broker to advise the parties that the
forms have important legal consequences and that they should consult counsel before signing.

### C.4 Assurances of title

| Assurance | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Abstract and opinion** | A summary of the record with an attorney's opinion of title based on it |
| **Title insurance** | A policy indemnifying against covered defects **existing as of the policy date**, subject to the policy's **exceptions and exclusions** |
| **Torrens registration** | A registration system used in some jurisdictions |

**The exceptions schedule is where a title policy's substance lives.** A policy does not insure against
what it excepts, does not insure against events after the policy date, and does not insure the physical
condition of the property. Reading the commitment is a task for the buyer, the title company and counsel —
and **Rule 6.2** tells a broker what to do when the reading is beyond them.

**Note one Colorado rule that touches title work and WAS read.** **Rule 6.20** requires a broker and
brokerage firm to **retain transaction files for four years** from the consummation date or from the
expiration of a listing contract that does not consummate — but expressly does **not** require a broker to
obtain and retain **existing public records, title commitments, loan applications, lender required
disclosures, or related affirmations from independent third-party closing entities after the closing
date.**
