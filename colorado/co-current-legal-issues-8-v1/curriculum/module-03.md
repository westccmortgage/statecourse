# Colorado Current Legal Issues — Module 03

## The representative areas, and the outline's appendices

**Course:** Current Legal Issues (8.0 classroom hours) · **Instructional hours: 2.0** of 8.0
**Commission outline topic:** **3 — "Other"**, and the outline's five appendices (the third of the outline's
three major topic areas)
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No Colorado qualifying education hours are available.

> ### What the Commission's outline says about this topic area
>
> After naming its three major topic areas, the outline adds:
>
> > *"\* **In addition to the above prescribed areas which will be specified from time to time by the Real
> > Estate Commission, the following representative areas, among others, MAY, AS CLASSROOM TIME PERMITS, be
> > included:**"*
>
> and then lists **sixteen.** It closes with **five appendices.**
>
> **Read the qualifiers: "representative", "among others", "may", "as classroom time permits."** These are
> **not** mandatory content — the mandatory content is what Commission staff specifies, and **this project
> has not received it.** The sixteen are the Commission's illustration of the range this course can cover.
>
> **This module takes each of the sixteen, states what it concerns in one or two lines, and says where a
> Colorado answer comes from** — which for most of them is a statute **this build did not read.** That is
> the useful thing a student can be given: **a map of where the questions live**, rather than sixteen
> shallow accounts of law nobody here read.
>
> **The 2.0 hours is a StateCourse decision.** The outline states no hours for its topic areas.

## Learning objectives

1. State what each of the sixteen representative areas concerns.
2. Identify, for each, whether this build read the governing Colorado law.
3. State the five appendices the outline names.
4. State what a broker does with a question in an area this course did not cover.

## A. The sixteen representative areas

**Each row states what the area concerns and where a Colorado answer comes from. "NOT READ" means this
project did not read the governing law, and therefore states none of it anywhere in this build.**

### 1. Brokerage relationships

**Delivered in full elsewhere in this build.** **§§ 12-10-402 to 12-10-411** were **read in full** and are
the subject of **Contracts and Regulations Module 02**. The transaction-broker default (§ 12-10-403(2)), the
dual agency prohibition (§ 12-10-406), designated brokerage (§ 12-10-403(6)), the three duty lists, the
disclosures, duration and compensation — all stated from primary text.

**Rules 6.5 through 6.9** supplement them and were read.

### 2. Licensing concerns — the broker and the real estate company

**Partly delivered.** **§ 12-10-203** and **Chapter 2 of 4 CCR 725-1** were **read**: the **168 hours**, the
**two-part examination**, the **one-year validity of a passing score**, the **fingerprint requirement**, and
the education waivers in **§ 12-10-203(4)(b) and (c)**.

**NOT READ:** **§§ 12-10-217, 12-10-219 and 12-10-222** — every **disciplinary ground, sanction, penalty and
procedure**; and the **insurance rules in Chapter 3** in detail, including every **errors and omissions**
requirement.

### 3. Real property law

The outline's own list under this head: *"annexation, condemnation, easements, landlord and tenant, lease,
financing, liens, foreclosures, unclaimed property, real estate contracts, remedies including damages and
specific performance, zoning, common interest ownership, various forms of ownership and entities, title
insurance and other evidence of ownership."*

**The general content is delivered** in **Real Estate Law and Practice** Modules 01, 03, 04 and 05.

**NOT READ, and stated nowhere:** Colorado's **condemnation** procedure; its **easement** and
**prescription** law; **§ 38-12-101 et seq.**, the **Landlord Tenant Act**; its **foreclosure statutes and
public trustee** system; its **unclaimed property** statute and the **Great Colorado Payback Program**; its
**statute of frauds**; its **mechanics' lien**, **recording** and **adverse possession** statutes; its
**zoning and land use** statutes and every local ordinance; **CCIOA (article 33.3 of title 38)**; its
**entity, trust and probate** statutes; and every **remedies** provision.

### 4. Local government legal developments

**Zoning, subdivision regulation, building codes, licensing, and local disclosure or transfer requirements
vary by jurisdiction.**

**NOT READ:** **no Colorado local ordinance of any jurisdiction was read**, and **no local requirement,
threshold, fee or procedure is stated anywhere in this build.**

### 5. Commercial law affecting real estate transactions (including sale and purchase of a business)

**The UCC**, secured transactions in personal property, bulk transfers, and the interaction between a
business sale and the real property it occupies.

**NOT READ:** the **UCC**; the **U.C.C.C.**, named separately by the **Contracts and Regulations** outline
at its Topic V.F.2.

**What WAS read and bears on it:** **§ 12-10-403(4)(a)** authorises a broker to complete standard forms
*"**including standard forms intended to convey personal property as part of the real estate
transaction**"*; **§ 12-10-201(6)** contains an exemption touching **licensed broker-dealers**.

### 6. Environmental law

Contamination, hazardous materials, asbestos, lead, radon, mould, underground storage tanks, and
methamphetamine contamination.

**NOT READ:** every **federal and Colorado environmental statute**, and the **federal lead-based paint
regulations** — which the outline names at its **Appendix E**.

**What WAS read:** the disclosure duties themselves. **§ 12-10-404(3)(a)**, **§ 12-10-405(3)(a)** and
**§ 12-10-407(2)(b)(VI)** each require disclosure of **adverse material facts ACTUALLY KNOWN**, expressly
including *"**any environmental hazards affecting the property that are required by law to be
disclosed**"* — and **§§ 12-10-404(3)(b), 12-10-405(5) and 12-10-407(4)** each provide there is **no duty to
inspect or verify.**

### 7. Business organizations and real estate

Corporations, limited liability companies, partnerships, trusts — **who may sign, and what authority they
hold.**

**NOT READ:** every Colorado **entity, trust and probate** statute, and the **Uniform Power of Attorney
Act**, named by the Contracts outline at Topic I.B.5.

**What WAS read:** **§ 12-10-203(6)** — a partnership, LLC or corporation applying for a licence must
**designate a qualified active broker** responsible for management and supervision, and **no licence issues
until that broker passes the examination on the entity's behalf**; **Rule 5.3.B** — a firm licensed as an
entity maintains trust accounts **in the entity's name**; and **Rules 5.10.D and 5.10.E** with the **20
percent** threshold in **§ 12-10-201(6)(b)(VII)**.

### 8. Bankruptcy and real estate

The effect of a bankruptcy filing on a pending transaction, a listing, a lease, or a lien.

**NOT READ:** the **federal Bankruptcy Code** and every provision of it. **No stay, deadline, procedure,
exemption or effect is stated anywhere in this build.**

**What a broker does:** a party in bankruptcy is a transaction that needs **counsel before anything else
happens**, and **Rule 6.2.B.1 — decline — is a legitimate answer** for a broker without the experience.

### 9. Intellectual property law and real estate transactions and business

Photographs, floor plans, listing copy, virtual tours, trademarks and trade names in advertising, and
database rights in listing data.

**NOT READ:** every **copyright, trademark and trade secret** statute.

**What WAS read and constrains advertising regardless:** **Rule 6.10 — Advertising** — and **Rule 1.2**,
which defines *"Advertise or Advertising"* to include *"business cards, brochures, websites, signage,
property flyers, mailings (paper or electronic), **social media**, letterhead, email signatures, and
contract documents."*

### 10. Taxation

**Real and personal property taxation; federal income and related taxes; sales, use and transfer taxes.**

**NOT READ:** Colorado's **property tax, assessment and levy** statutes; **every federal tax provision**;
and every **sales, use or transfer tax**.

**What this build states:** taxes are **prorated at closing on the convention the contract states**, using
figures the transaction supplies — **Closings** Modules 02, 03 and 05. **No rate, ratio, cycle or due date
is stated anywhere.** Tax questions go to the client's **accountant**, and **§§ 12-10-404(1)(c)(V),
12-10-405(1)(c)(V) and 12-10-407(2)(b)(II)** make advising the party to obtain expert advice a duty.

### 11. Unauthorized practice of law concerns

**Delivered as far as this build can.** **§ 12-10-403(4)** and **Rule 7.1** were **read in full** and are
stated in **Contracts and Regulations Module 01 Part B.3** and **Current Legal Issues Module 02 Part C** —
the eight kinds of standard form, the requirement to use a Commission-approved form, the **advise-versus-
insert split**, and the duty to tell the parties to consult counsel.

**NOT READ:** the **Conway-Bogue decision** and **Colorado Bar Association v. The Title Guaranty Company**,
both named by the Contracts outline. **No holding from either is stated anywhere in this build.**

### 12. Legal issues involved with property management and appraisals

**Property management:** delivered from the rules that **were** read — **Rules 5.5, 5.7.A, 5.8, 5.10.D and
E, 5.11, 5.15, 6.17.C** — in **Real Estate Law and Practice Module 05** and the **Record Keeping and Trust
Accounts** course.

**NOT READ:** the **Landlord Tenant Act** and every deposit, notice, habitability and eviction provision.

**Appraisals:** **Rule 6.12** was **read** and is delivered verbatim in **Real Estate Law and Practice
Module 02** — the required CMA/BPO notice, and the **prohibition** on CMAs or BPOs used to obtain
financing.

**NOT READ:** **§ 12-10-602**, the **Appraiser Licensing Act** named by the Contracts outline at Topic I.E,
and **USPAP**.

### 13. Local legal issues

**NOT READ:** as with area 4, **no Colorado local ordinance or local practice requirement was read**, and
none is stated anywhere in this build.

### 14. Legal issues involving Colorado Real Estate Commission approved contracts and other forms

**The largest gate in the whole Colorado build.**

**NO Commission-approved form was obtained, read or reproduced by this project.** **§ 12-10-403(4)(c)** and
**Rule 7.1.A** require a broker to **use a Commission-approved form when one exists and is appropriate**, and
the **Contracts and Regulations** outline requires students to **complete** one of each of **six** listing
and buyer brokerage families, one of each of **four** sales contract families, and one of each of the
**related forms.**

**This build states no form's contents, version, date, paragraph numbering or provisions**, and obtaining
the current forms from the Division's website is a submission gate recorded in the course engine.

### 15. Legal issues and closings

**Delivered from the rules that were read.** **Rule 6.19** in full — closing responsibility, the **signed**
closing statement, attendance or reasonable availability, joint responsibility of a substitute, and **Good
Funds**; **Rule 6.20** — **four-year** retention; **Rules 5.12 and 5.7.B** — earnest money.

**NOT READ:** **RESPA**; the **non-resident seller withholding** statute and **FIRPTA**, both named by the
Closings outline at its Topic I.D; **C.R.S. § 38-35-125**, cited by Rule 6.19.E; and the **Commission's
Transaction File Checklist**, named by Rule 6.20.

### 16. Others

The outline's own examples: *"insurance and real estate; family and probate affecting real estate; mining,
oil and gas, water laws and public lands law; legal concerns related to technological developments and
practices such as facsimile transmissions, email, privacy and consent issues, wireless and cordless
telephonic communications, Internet, copyright issues, etc."*

**NOT READ:** every statute in that list — including Colorado's **water law**, named by **two** of the six
Commission outlines and identified in **Contracts and Regulations Module 05 Part E** as a gate this build
cannot responsibly leave open in a state where farm, ranch and vacant land brokerage is ordinary work.

**What WAS read and bears on the technology items:** **Rule 1.2's** definition of advertising, which
expressly reaches **websites, electronic mailings and social media**; **Rule 5.14.E**, permitting electronic
records; and **Rule 6.24**, on electronic records and production.

## C. What a broker does with a question in an area this course did not cover

**The same three things, every time, and all three are in rules this build read in full.**

> **1. Recognise it.** Module 02 Part B lists the signals: ownership, authority to sign, default, a period
> or procedure, a dispute over money the broker holds, or anything that would change what someone signs.
>
> **2. Route it.** **Rule 6.2.B** — **decline**; **obtain** the experience, training and knowledge; **obtain
> the assistance** of the employing broker, a supervisory broker, a competent broker **or legal counsel
> competent in the matter**; or **co-list.**
>
> **3. Tell the party to get advice.** **§§ 12-10-404(1)(c)(V) and 12-10-405(1)(c)(V)** — **advise the party
> to obtain expert advice** as to material matters beyond the broker's expertise. **§ 12-10-407(2)(b)(II)**
> — for a transaction-broker, **advise the parties and suggest they obtain expert advice.**
> **§ 12-10-403(4)(c)** — on any standard form, **advise the parties that the forms have important legal
> consequences and that they should consult legal counsel before signing.**

**And the continuing obligation underneath all of it:** **Rule 6.2.A** — a broker **must possess the
necessary experience, training and knowledge** to provide the service and to **maintain compliance with the
applicable federal, state and local laws, rules, regulations and ordinances.**
