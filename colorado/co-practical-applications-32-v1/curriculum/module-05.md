## A. Business opportunities — outline Topic V.A

### A.1 What it is

**The sale or purchase of a business**, which in a real estate broker's hands ordinarily involves **real
property** — owned or leased — **plus** everything else the business consists of.

| Component | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Real property** | Owned, or a **leasehold** the buyer must succeed to |
| **Tangible personal property** | Equipment, fixtures, inventory |
| **Intangibles** | Goodwill, trade name, customer lists, contracts |
| **Intellectual property** | Marks, copyrights, licences |
| **Licences and permits** | Often **not transferable**, and often the thing that stops the deal |
| **Contracts and leases** | Assignable, or not |
| **Employees** | Retained, or not |
| **Liabilities** | Assumed, or excluded |

**The structural question that governs everything else: is this an ASSET sale or an EQUITY sale?** An asset
sale transfers named assets; an equity sale transfers the entity and everything in it, including
liabilities the buyer may not know about. **That is a legal and tax question and it is not the broker's.**

### A.2 What Colorado law says, and what this build read

**§ 12-10-201(6)** — the definition of the activities requiring a licence — reaches transactions involving
**a business opportunity that owns an interest in real estate or in a leasehold**, with an exemption for
**broker-dealers licensed under article 51 of title 11** actually engaged generally in the securities
business.

**And § 12-10-403(4)(a)** authorises a broker to complete standard forms *"**including standard forms
intended to convey personal property as part of the real estate transaction.**"*

> **NOT READ, and stated nowhere in this build:** the **UCC** — including secured transactions and bulk
> transfers; the **U.C.C.C.**, named by the Contracts and Regulations outline at Topic V.F.2; **federal and
> Colorado securities law** beyond the § 12-10-201(6) exemption's existence; Colorado's **entity, trust and
> probate** statutes; **every tax provision**; and **every licensing and permitting statute** governing any
> particular trade.
>
> **No requirement, threshold, exemption, filing, notice, tax treatment or penalty from any of them is
> stated anywhere in this build.**

### A.3 What a broker does

**Recognise that an equity sale, a securities question, a liquor or professional licence, or an assumed
liability is not a real estate question** — and apply **Rule 6.2.B**: **decline**; **obtain** the
experience, training and knowledge; **obtain the assistance** of the employing broker, a supervisory broker,
a competent broker **or legal counsel competent in the matter**; or **co-list.**

**And the duty that runs alongside:** **§§ 12-10-404(1)(c)(V), 12-10-405(1)(c)(V) and 12-10-407(2)(b)(II)**
each require **advising the party to obtain expert advice** as to material matters known to the broker but
whose specifics are beyond the broker's expertise. **A business sale generates several of those in the first
meeting.**

## B. Oil and gas leasing — outline Topic V.B

### B.1 Why the Commission names it

**In Colorado, mineral interests are frequently SEVERED from the surface**, and the person who owns the
house may not own what is beneath it.

**An oil and gas lease** grants a lessee the right to explore for and produce oil and gas from a tract, in
exchange for a bonus, delay rentals and a **royalty**. The terms a party will encounter — the **primary
term**, the **habendum** or "so long as produced" clause, **pooling and unitisation**, **surface use**, and
the **royalty fraction** — are the substance of the instrument.

### B.2 Why it reaches an ordinary residential broker

**Because a residential buyer may be buying a surface estate whose minerals belong to someone else, and
because that someone else may have rights to use the surface.**

That is a fact about the property, and Colorado's duty rules speak to facts about the property:

> **§ 12-10-404(3)(a)** — a seller's agent must **disclose to any prospective buyer all ADVERSE MATERIAL
> FACTS ACTUALLY KNOWN** by the broker, *"including but not limited to adverse material facts pertaining to
> **the title** and the physical condition of the property."*
>
> **§ 12-10-407(2)(b)(VI)** — the same for a transaction-broker.
>
> **§§ 12-10-404(3)(b) and 12-10-407(4)** — and **no duty to conduct an independent inspection** or to
> **verify** statements made by the seller or an independent inspector.

**"Actually known" and "no duty to investigate" together mean a broker who knows discloses, and a broker who
does not know is not required by these sections to go and find out — but Rule 6.2.A requires the experience,
training and knowledge to serve the market they are in**, and in parts of Colorado severed minerals are
ordinary.

**Where the answer comes from:** the **title commitment**, whose **Schedule B-2 exceptions** are where a
mineral reservation appears — read in the **Closings** course, Module 03 Part B — and **counsel.**

> **NOT READ, and stated nowhere in this build:** Colorado's **oil and gas statutes**; its **severed mineral
> interest**, **surface use** and **dormant mineral** provisions; every **Colorado Oil and Gas Conservation
> Commission** rule; every **local ordinance** governing operations; and every **disclosure requirement**
> attaching to mineral estates.
>
> **No royalty fraction, term, pooling rule, setback, notice, disclosure requirement or procedure is stated
> anywhere in this build.**

## C. Farm and ranch — outline Topic V.C

### C.1 Why it is a specialty, and it is not mainly about acreage

**The Commission names water rights in TWO of its six outlines** — here by implication, and expressly at
**Contracts and Regulations Topic V.E** and **Real Estate Law and Practice Topic I.A.1.a.** That repetition
is the signal.

> **Water rights in the western states may be owned, conveyed, leased and encumbered SEPARATELY FROM THE
> LAND.** A right to divert and use water is a distinct property interest. **It does not necessarily pass
> with a conveyance of the parcel**, and a contract that says nothing about it may have transferred nothing.
>
> **Getting it wrong can transfer a farm without the means of irrigating it** — which is the clearest
> example in this whole build of the Commission's phrase *"so no harm is done to a member of the public."*

### C.2 What else distinguishes the work

| Element | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Water** | As above — and the single largest value driver on irrigated land |
| **Mineral interests** | Frequently severed, as in Part B |
| **Access** | Legal access, easements, and roads that may not be public |
| **Growing crops, and the crop year** | Whether they pass, and how they are apportioned |
| **Livestock and equipment** | Personal property conveyed alongside — **§ 12-10-403(4)(a)** |
| **Grazing leases and federal or state land permits** | Often not transferable as of right |
| **Conservation easements** | A recorded restriction that changes what the land may be used for permanently |
| **Government programme participation** | Enrolments that may or may not follow the land |
| **Fencing, wells, ditches and headgates** | Physical infrastructure attached to legal rights |

**And the Commission's own form structure recognises the difference.** The **Contracts and Regulations**
outline's Topic IV.A names a distinct family — **Contract to Buy and Sell Real Estate (Vacant Land/Farm &
Ranch)** — and its Topic III requires the student to *"include **by comparison the differences between
residential, commercial and farm and ranch/vacant land** form."*

### C.3 What this build states, and what it does not

> **NOT READ, and stated nowhere in this build:** Colorado's **water law** in its entirety — appropriation,
> priority dates, adjudication, augmentation plans, well permits, exempt wells, ditch company shares, change
> of use proceedings, and the division engineer's role; its **conservation easement** statutes; its **fence
> law**; its **agricultural** statutes; and every **federal or state grazing and land programme.**
>
> **No period, priority, permit requirement, decree, procedure, exemption or programme rule is stated
> anywhere in this build.**
>
> **This is a submission gate**, and **Contracts and Regulations Module 05 Part E** records it as one this
> build **cannot responsibly leave open** in a state where farm, ranch and vacant land brokerage is ordinary
> work.

### C.4 What a broker does before taking the listing

**Rule 6.2.A is a requirement, not advice:** a broker *"**must possess the necessary experience, training,
and knowledge** to provide Real Estate Brokerage Services **and maintain compliance with the applicable
federal, state and local laws.**"*

**Rule 6.2.B, applied to a farm and ranch listing offered to a residential broker:**

| Route | What it looks like here |
|---|---|
| **1. Decline** | The honest answer for most residential brokers, and the rule lists it **first** |
| **2. Obtain** the experience, training and knowledge | Coursework and mentoring in the specialty **before** taking the work |
| **3. Obtain the assistance** — including **legal counsel competent in the matter** | For water in particular: **counsel and a water engineer** |
| **4. Co-list** with a broker who meets subsection A | The ordinary answer when the opportunity is real and the competency is not |

## D. The pattern across all three

**The three specialties in this topic have the same shape, and a student should leave able to state it.**

| | Business opportunities | Oil and gas | Farm and ranch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What is really being conveyed** | More than the real property | An interest **beneath** the surface, often severed | Land **plus** water, minerals, access and programme rights |
| **The governing law** | UCC, securities, tax, entity, licensing | Oil and gas statutes and COGCC rules | Water law, conservation easements, agricultural statutes |
| **Read by this project?** | **No** | **No** | **No** |
| **What this build states** | The § 12-10-201(6) reach and the § 12-10-403(4)(a) personal property authority | The **actually known** disclosure duty and where a reservation appears | The **actually known** disclosure duty and that water may not pass |
| **The broker's move** | **Rule 6.2.B** | **Rule 6.2.B** | **Rule 6.2.B** |

**In every one of the three the transaction contains something a residential broker has no training to
value, describe, or convey — and in every one the rule gives four routes, the first of which is to
decline.**
