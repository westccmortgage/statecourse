## Learning objectives

1. Sequence the disclosures and agreements against the events that trigger them.
2. Calculate seller's net proceeds and buyer's cash to close from given figures.
3. State what a broker may and may not say about an exclusive relationship and a holdover period.
4. State the rules governing showings, access and property information.
5. Prepare and present an offer with complete contingencies and consistent dates.
6. State the multiple-offer and counter-offer duties.
7. Build a suspense date schedule and say what happens when one is missed.
8. State what a broker does with a failed transaction and disputed earnest money.

## A. Working with buyers and sellers — outline Topic VI.A

### A.1 Pricing and motivation — VI.A.1

**a. CMAs.** Delivered in **Real Estate Law and Practice Module 02** and **Module 02 Part D of this
course** — including **Rule 6.12**'s prescribed notice for a CMA or BPO prepared for any reason **other
than an anticipated sale or purchase**, and the **prohibition** on CMAs or BPOs **used to obtain
financing.**

**b. Neighborhood analysis; fair housing concerns.** The outline names them **in the same breath**, and so
does this course. A broker may discuss **the property and the physical, economic, governmental and legal
characteristics of an area** using sources a client can check. A broker may **not** characterise an area by
the **protected characteristics** of the people in it — that is **steering**, whatever reason is offered.

**§§ 12-10-404(1)(e), 12-10-405(1)(e) and 12-10-407(2)(d)** each make compliance with **fair housing and
civil rights statutes** part of the brokerage relationship duties, and **§ 12-10-411** makes a Part 4
violation an act the Commission may investigate.

**c. Calculating seller's net proceeds and buyer's cash to close.** The Commission names this expressly, and
it is the calculation clients ask for most.

> **Seller's net, figures given.** Price **$620,000**. Compensation **5.5 percent**. Payoff **$341,800**.
> Title insurance **$2,240**. Recording **$40**. Estimated prorated taxes charged to the seller
> **$2,150**.
>
> | Line | Amount |
> |---|---|
> | Price | 620,000.00 |
> | less compensation (5.5%) | (34,100.00) |
> | less payoff | (341,800.00) |
> | less title insurance | (2,240.00) |
> | less recording | (40.00) |
> | less tax proration | (2,150.00) |
> | **Estimated net to seller** | **$239,670.00** |

> **The reverse — a seller who wants a stated net.** A seller wants **$250,000** after a **5.5 percent**
> compensation and **$4,500** of other costs.
>
> $250,000 + $4,500 = **$254,500**, which is **94.5 percent** of the price, so the price is
> **$254,500 ÷ 0.945 = $269,312.17.**
>
> **The compensation is a percentage of the PRICE, not of the net** — which is why the net plus costs is
> divided by the complement of the rate.

> **Buyer's cash to close, figures given.** Price **$620,000**. Down payment **10 percent**. Loan costs
> **$7,400**. Prepaid interest **$1,120**. Prepaid taxes and insurance escrow **$3,900**. Recording
> **$180**. Earnest money already paid **$15,000**.
>
> | Line | Amount |
> |---|---|
> | Down payment (10%) | 62,000.00 |
> | Loan costs | 7,400.00 |
> | Prepaid interest | 1,120.00 |
> | Escrow deposits | 3,900.00 |
> | Recording | 180.00 |
> | **Subtotal** | **74,600.00** |
> | less earnest money | (15,000.00) |
> | **Estimated cash to close** | **$59,600.00** |

**Every figure is an estimate until the closing entity produces the statement**, and a broker says so.
**Rule 6.19.A** puts the accuracy obligation at closing on the broker with the brokerage relationship, and
**Rule 6.19.B** on the broker who prepares the documents.

### A.2 Explaining the right to sell / buy agreements — VI.A.2

**a. Effects of an exclusive relationship.** Under an **exclusive right-to-sell**, compensation is earned by
**a sale during the term, however it comes about** — including one the owner finds themselves. Under an
**exclusive agency**, the owner may still sell it themselves without compensation. Under an **open
listing**, only the broker who produces is paid.

**Explaining this BEFORE signature is the substance of the broker's job at that stage.**

**b. Explaining the holdover period.** A holdover clause continues the compensation obligation for a stated
period after the term where a sale is made to a **protected** buyer. **What the period is, who is protected
and how they become protected are terms of the agreement** — and **no Commission form was obtained by this
project**, so **no holdover period, protection mechanism or form provision is stated anywhere in this
build.**

**And § 12-10-409(1)(b)(III) stands behind every agreement:** where it is not performed or completed, the
relationship ends at the **earlier** of an agreed expiration, a termination by the parties, or **one year
after the engagement.**

### A.3 Changing brokerage relationships — VI.A.3

**Rule 6.9** and the **Change of Status** form, delivered in **Module 02 Part A.1**: on changing **from
single agent for one consumer to transaction-broker for both**, the Commission-approved form goes to the
consumer whose relationship changed *"**at the time the Broker begins to assist both Consumers ... but not
later than at the time the Consumer signs the contract.**"*

**And Rule 6.7's three lawful configurations**, because **§ 12-10-406 prohibits dual agency outright.**

## B. Preparing the house to be listed — outline Topic VI.B

**What a broker does:** advise on condition, presentation and access; gather what the listing needs —
**legal description**, **square footage and its SOURCE**, inclusions and exclusions, association
information, and any known condition matters; and get the **seller's property disclosure** completed by the
seller.

**Two Colorado rules bite here.**

> **Rule 6.11** — advertising square footage, **including for submission to a multiple listing service**,
> requires **disclosing the SOURCE on the Commission-approved form**, with the **date of issuance** where
> the source has one. *"A Broker **may not provide information to a person from a source known to be
> unreliable** and **is responsible for indicating obvious mismeasurement by others.**"*
>
> **Rule 6.10.C** — the broker is **responsible for the accuracy of advertising including MLS entries**, but
> *"**does not have a duty to investigate the information provided ... by the seller or obtained from a third
> party source (e.g. assessor records, building plans).**"*

**Read together: no duty to investigate is not permission to pass on what you can see is wrong.**

**And the seller's disclosure does not discharge the broker's own duty.** **§ 12-10-404(3)(a)** requires
disclosure to any prospective buyer of **all adverse material facts ACTUALLY KNOWN by the broker** —
including title, physical condition, material defects and environmental hazards required by law to be
disclosed. **A fact the broker knows that the seller did not state is still the broker's to disclose.**

## C. Showing a property — outline Topic VI.C

**Access is the owner's to give.** **Rule 6.10.F** governs advertising availability without authority;
**Rule 6.16** governs **access information for a property**; and the listing agreement governs how showings
are arranged.

**Fair housing governs who is shown what.** Every prospect gets the same information and the same sources,
and **the decision about which properties to see is the prospect's.**

**Confidentiality governs what is said.** **§ 12-10-404(2)** forbids a seller's agent disclosing, without
informed consent, that the **seller will accept less**, their **motivating factors**, or that they **will
agree to other financing terms.** **§ 12-10-407(3)** binds a transaction-broker to a **six-item** list
requiring the **informed consent of all parties.**

**And the disclosure timing from Rule 6.5 applies at the door.** A showing conversation that moves to what
the prospect can afford or why they are moving has reached **confidential information**, and the written
§ 12-10-408 disclosure was due **before** it.

## D. Preparing and presenting an offer — outline Topic VI.D

The outline names four sub-items, and each is a check.

### D.1 Forms and computer generated contracts — VI.D.1

> **§ 12-10-403(4)(c)** and **Rule 7.1.A**: *"**A broker shall use a Commission-approved form when such a
> form exists and is appropriate for the transaction**"* — a form *"promulgated by the Commission **for
> CURRENT USE**"*, obtained from **the Division's website.**
>
> And the advice that goes with every one: the broker *"**shall advise the parties that the forms have
> important legal consequences and that the parties should consult legal counsel before signing.**"*

**"Computer generated" changes nothing about which form must be used**, and a generated document that is not
the current Commission form is not a Commission-approved form.

### D.2 Proper contingencies — VI.D.2

**The three-part test, applied to every one:**

> **1. WHAT EVENT** must occur; **2. BY WHEN**; and **3. WHAT HAPPENS** if it does not.

**An event with no deadline, or a deadline with no consequence, has settled nothing.**

### D.3 Consistency of dates — VI.D.3

**The Commission names this expressly, and it is the most common defect in a new broker's completed
contract.**

**The check:** lay every date in the contract on one line in order and ask whether each is **possible**. A
loan objection deadline **after** closing; an inspection deadline **before** the contract is signed; a title
objection deadline leaving **no time to cure**; a possession date **before** closing without an early
occupancy agreement — each of these appears in real contracts, and each is caught by reading the dates in
sequence.

### D.4 Inclusions and exclusions — VI.D.4

**What stays and what goes**, named in the contract. **§ 12-10-403(4)(a)** authorises a broker to complete
standard forms *"**including standard forms intended to convey personal property as part of the real estate
transaction.**"*

**Anything anyone might argue about goes in by name.**

## E. Presenting the offer to the seller — outline Topic VI.E

### E.1 The presentation duty

> **§ 12-10-404(1)(c)(II)**: **present all offers to and from the seller in a timely manner REGARDLESS of
> whether the property is subject to a contract for sale.**
>
> **§ 12-10-404(1)(c)(I)**: the broker **is not obligated to SEEK additional offers** while the property is
> under contract.
>
> **Rule 6.13**: present **to the other consumer's broker**; on **reasonable but unsuccessful attempts**, to
> that broker's **employing broker**; and if none exists or those attempts fail, **directly to the other
> consumer.** **The rule states no time period, and this build supplies none.**

### E.2 Counter offers — VI.E.1

**A counter-offer REJECTS the original offer.** The original cannot then be accepted unless renewed, and the
counter operates as a new offer. The **Counterproposal** is the instrument, named at the **Contracts and
Regulations** outline's Topic IV.B.1.

**A term changed on the face of the contract and initialled produces a document the parties will disagree
about.** The counterproposal states **which terms change and which survive.**

### E.3 Multiple offers — VI.E.2

**What the rules require:** every offer is **presented** — **§ 12-10-404(1)(c)(II)**. **What may be
disclosed about competing offers is the seller's decision**, and **§ 12-10-404(2)(d)** forbids disclosing
**material information about the seller** without informed consent.

**And every offeror is treated the same way.** Fair housing runs through multiple-offer handling, and so
does **§ 12-10-404(1)(e)**'s requirement to comply with fair housing and civil rights statutes.

**A broker does not tell a buyer what to offer to beat another offer**, and does not disclose the terms of
one offer to another offeror without the seller's informed consent.

### E.4 Acceptance — VI.E.3

**Acceptance must be unqualified and COMMUNICATED.** One signed and left unsent has not been communicated.
The contract should state **how and by when** acceptance must be communicated, and the broker should know
which document and which moment formed the contract, because **every suspense date in Part H runs from
it.**

## F. Under contract — outline Topic VI.F

### F.1 The listing broker's role — VI.F.1

Deliver the executed contract; deliver **earnest money per the contract and obtain a dated and signed
receipt** (**Rule 5.12.A**); order what the seller must produce; track the seller's deadlines; keep the
seller informed; and **continue presenting offers** unless released as **Rule 6.13** and § 12-10-404
contemplate.

### F.2 The selling broker's role — VI.F.2

Deliver the contract to the buyer; ensure the **loan application** goes in; order the **inspection**;
deliver the **title commitment** and diarise the objection deadline; track the buyer's deadlines; and keep
the buyer informed.

**§ 12-10-407(2)(b)(IV) and (V)** state it for a transaction-broker in the statute's own words: **keep the
parties fully informed**, and **assist the parties in complying with the terms and conditions of any
contract including closing the transaction.**

## G. Working with third parties — outline Topic VI.G

**Lenders, title companies, home inspectors and appraisers** — the four the outline names, developed in

## H. Tracking contingencies and suspense dates — outline Topic VI.H

**This is the operational core of the module, and it is what most often fails.**

**A suspense date schedule lists every date in the contract, what must happen by it, who must do it, and
what happens if it passes.**

| Date | What must happen | Who | If missed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Earnest money delivery | Delivered to the holder per the contract, **dated signed receipt obtained** | Broker | **Rule 5.12.A** breach |
| Earnest money deposit | If the firm holds it, deposited **within three business days** of receipt or mutual execution, **whichever is later** | Firm | **Rule 5.7.B** breach |
| Loan application | Application submitted | Buyer | Contract's consequence |
| Inspection | Inspection performed | Buyer | Objection right may lapse |
| Inspection objection | **Inspection Notice** delivered | Buyer | Condition may be accepted |
| Title documents delivered | Commitment and documents to buyer | Seller / title | Contract's consequence |
| Title objection | Objection delivered | Buyer | Exceptions may be accepted |
| Association documents | Delivered and reviewed | Seller / association | Contract's consequence |
| Appraisal | Completed | Lender | Contract's consequence |
| Loan objection | Buyer's decision on financing | Buyer | Financing contingency may lapse |
| Survey or ILC | Obtained | Per contract | Contract's consequence |
| Closing | Closing occurs | All | Default provisions engage |
| Possession | Possession delivered | Seller | Default provisions engage |

**Three disciplines that make the schedule work:**

> **1. Every date is diarised the day the contract is executed**, not the week it falls due.
> **2. Every date has an OWNER.** A date nobody owns is the date that passes.
> **3. Nothing changes without a writing.** *"**Close to contract or amend contract in writing**"* — the
> Closings outline's Topic III.A.1 — and the instrument is the **Agreement to Amend/Extend Contract.**

**And § 12-10-407(2)(b)(V)** makes assisting the parties in complying with the contract's terms an express
obligation of a transaction-broker.

## I. Final walk-through — outline Topic VI.I

**What it is:** the buyer's opportunity, before closing, to confirm the property is in the condition the
contract requires and that agreed repairs and inclusions are as agreed.

**What it is not:** an inspection, a new objection right, or a renegotiation. **What the buyer may do if
something is wrong depends on the contract**, and if the parties resolve it differently, **the contract is
amended in writing.**

**Access is still the owner's to give**, and **Rule 6.16** governs access information.

## J. Setting the closing and notifying the parties — outline Topic VI.J

**Confirm the closing entity, date, time and place; confirm what each party must bring; confirm how funds
will move; and notify everyone who must be there.**

**Two Colorado requirements bear on it.**

> **Rule 6.19.C** — a broker with a brokerage relationship *"**must review closing documents AND attend
> closing OR BE REASONABLY AVAILABLE.**"* If not available, **another broker designated by the firm may
> review and attend on their behalf** and **assumes JOINT RESPONSIBILITY** for accuracy, completeness and
> delivery of the signed closing statement.
>
> **Rule 6.19.E — Good Funds.** A broker or firm *"**must not disburse or authorize disbursement of funds
> until those funds have been RECEIVED and are ... available for immediate withdrawal AS A MATTER OF RIGHT
> ... or available for immediate withdrawal as a consequence of an agreement with a financial institution
> ... If the agreement contains contingencies or reservations, NO DISBURSEMENTS CAN BE MADE until these are
> satisfied.**"*

**How the buyer's funds will arrive is a question asked days before closing, not on the morning.**

## K. Verifying figures and documents prior to closing — outline Topic VI.K

**The check, and it is the Closings course's Module 03 Part D.3 applied here:**

1. **Does every charge trace to the contract or a written amendment?**
2. **Does each proration's two halves sum to the whole?**
3. **Does each party's column settle to one figure?**
4. **Does money in equal money out?**
5. **Do the names, legal description, price and loan figures match across the contract, the commitment, the
   worksheet and the statement?**
6. **Is the statement accurate, complete and detailed?**

> **Rule 6.19.A**: the broker with the brokerage relationship **must ensure the consumer receives a closing
> statement that is ACCURATE, COMPLETE AND DETAILED and SIGNED BY THE BROKER**, and must **deliver closing
> statements and other closing documents to the brokerage firm IMMEDIATELY FOLLOWING CLOSING.**
>
> **Rule 6.19.B**: a broker who **prepares** the documents is responsible for their **accuracy and
> completeness.**

**The broker signs the statement.** That is why this sub-topic exists.

## L. Early occupancy — outline Topic VI.L

**A buyer in possession before closing, or a seller in possession after it.**

**What it needs:** a **written agreement** stating who occupies, from when, on what terms, who insures, who
maintains, what rent or consideration is paid, what happens if the closing does not occur, and how
possession is recovered.

> **A boundary this module holds firmly.** **Colorado's landlord-tenant statutes were NOT read by this
> project**, and **whether an early or holdover occupancy creates a tenancy — and what would then be
> required to end it — is a question this course does not answer.**
>
> **Insurance is the other half**, and it is not the broker's field either: whose policy covers an occupant
> who does not own, and an owner who does not occupy, is a question for **the carriers.**
>
> **§§ 12-10-404(1)(c)(V), 12-10-405(1)(c)(V) and 12-10-407(2)(b)(II)** each require **advising the party to
> obtain expert advice** as to material matters beyond the broker's expertise. **Early occupancy is the
> clearest case in an ordinary residential file**, and the advice needed is **legal and insurance advice
> before anyone moves in.**

## M. Failed transactions — outline Topic VI.M

### M.1 Handling earnest money deposits — VI.M.1

**Three things are true whatever the contract says:**

> **1. The broker HOLDS.** The broker does not decide entitlement.
> **2. The parties or a court decide.**
> **3. Nothing is disbursed on the broker's own judgement.**

**Why:** **Rule 5.9** — money belonging to one beneficiary **must not be used for the benefit of another**;
and **Rule 5.14.B** — *"**no ledger may ever be allowed to have a negative cash balance**"*, which is what a
wrong release produces.

**Rule 6.2.B.3** supplies the route: obtain the assistance of the employing broker, a supervisory broker, a
competent broker **or legal counsel competent in the matter** — **before the money moves.**

**The Record Keeping and Trust Accounts outline names the mechanism** at its Topic III.B.3 — *"**Earnest
Money Disputes and Interpleaders**"* — and **no Colorado interpleader procedure, deadline or cost rule is
stated anywhere in this build**, because none was read.

### M.2 Alternative dispute resolution — VI.M.2

**Mediation and arbitration**, where the contract provides for them. **What the contract requires, and what
it does to a party's other remedies, is a legal question**, and **no Colorado statute or rule on mediation
or arbitration was read by this project.**

### M.3 Evictions — VI.M.3

**Named by the outline under failed transactions**, which places it where a failed early occupancy or a
holdover seller would land it.

> **Colorado's eviction and unlawful detainer statutes were NOT read by this project.** **No ground, notice
> period, procedure, timeline or remedy is stated anywhere in this build.** **This is a submission gate**,
> and it is one a Practical Applications course cannot leave open — the outline names evictions expressly.

## N. Closing the transaction — outline Topic VI.N

### N.1 Responsibilities of the listing and selling brokers — VI.N.1

**Rule 6.19.A** — the broker with the brokerage relationship is **responsible for the proper closing of the
transaction**, whoever conducts it; must ensure the consumer receives a **closing statement that is
accurate, complete and detailed and signed by the broker**; and must **deliver closing statements and other
closing documents to the firm immediately following closing.**

### N.2 Responsibilities of associate brokers — VI.N.2

**Rule 6.19.C's substitute provision**, and behind it **Rule 6.3.A.1** — employing brokers **exercise
authority, direction and supervision**, and a complaint against an associate **may become an investigation
of the employing broker.**

### N.3 Changes to contract terms, amend/extend agreements — VI.N.3

***"Close to contract or amend contract in writing."*** A change made at the closing table is **amended in
writing and signed by everyone**, or it does not appear on the statement. **A side agreement kept off the
statement is the loan fraud problem** the Contracts and Regulations outline names at its Topic V.F.4.

### N.4 Follow up on recordings, issuance of title policy, etc. — VI.N.4

**The transaction is not finished at signing.** The deed and security instrument are **recorded**; the
**title policy** issues; the **releases** of paid-off liens are recorded; and the file is completed.

> **Rule 6.20**: **four years** of retention, from **consummation** or from **the expiration of a listing
> contract that does not consummate** — and expressly **not** requiring the broker to obtain and retain
> **existing public records, title commitments, loan applications, lender required disclosures, or related
> affirmations from independent third party closing entities after the closing date.**
>
> The **Commission's Transaction File Checklist**, which Rule 6.20 says designates the required documents,
> **was NOT obtained by this project** — a submission gate.

**And § 12-10-409(2)(b)** — for a **single agent**, **accounting** and **confidentiality** survive
termination; for a **transaction-broker**, **§ 12-10-409(3)** carries **accounting** only.
