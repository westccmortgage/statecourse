## Learning objectives

1. State what brokerage relationship arises in a commercial transaction and how it is established.
2. Name the property types and the elements that distinguish commercial from residential work.
3. Compute the financial measures a commercial party will expect, from given figures.
4. State the advertising rules that apply to commercial marketing.
5. State what a commercial lease settles that a residential lease does not.
6. Identify the pitfalls and state which of them this build can answer from primary text.

## A. Broker relationships and overview of practice — outline Topic IV.A

**Colorado's brokerage relationship statute makes NO commercial exception in any provision read for this
build.**

That is worth stating plainly because **other states do**. This project's Washington build records that
**RCW 18.86.020(3)** removes the services-agreement requirement where a broker acts as a buyer's agent
*"solely for commercial real estate"*, substituting a written compensation disclosure. **Colorado's
§§ 12-10-403 through 12-10-411 contain no analogous carve-out in the text read**, and **no module of this
build states one.**

**So the same rules apply, in the same order:**

| Provision | In a commercial transaction |
|---|---|
| **§ 12-10-403(2)** | **Transaction-broker by default** unless single agency is established **in writing** |
| **§ 12-10-406** | **Dual agency prohibited** — and **Rule 6.7**'s three lawful configurations |
| **§§ 12-10-404 / 405 / 407** | The duty lists, including **presenting all offers regardless** of an existing contract **or LETTER OF INTENT** |
| **§ 12-10-408** and **Rule 6.5** | The disclosures, **before eliciting or discussing confidential information** |
| **§ 12-10-409** | Duration, with the **one-year** backstop |
| **§ 12-10-410** | Compensation — **payment does not establish agency**; the **buyer-side written approval** in (5); the **identity disclosure before** the agreement in (6) |
| **Rule 6.2** | Competency — **decline**, **obtain**, **obtain assistance**, or **co-list** |

**Note the letters of intent.** **§§ 12-10-404(1)(c)(I) and (II), 12-10-405(1)(c)(I) and (II) and
12-10-407(2)(b)(I)** each mention **a letter of intent to lease** alongside a contract for sale — the
statute contemplates the commercial practice expressly.

**And § 12-10-403(4)(b)(VIII)** authorises a broker to complete **a letter of intent** as a standard form
*"**so long as the letter of intent states on its face that it is nonbinding and creates no legal rights or
obligations.**"* **That sentence is a requirement, not a description.**

## B. Elements of commercial real estate — outline Topic IV.B

| Type | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Office** | Space let by the square foot, ordinarily on multi-year leases with expense structures |
| **Retail** | Space where the tenant's **sales** matter to the landlord — percentage rent, co-tenancy, exclusivity |
| **Industrial** | Warehouse, distribution, manufacturing — clear height, loading, power, zoning |
| **Multi-family** | Income residential — **and note it sits in both worlds**, since the tenancies are residential |
| **Land** | Held for development, with entitlement, access and utilities as the value drivers |
| **Special purpose** | Built for one use, with few comparable sales — where the **cost approach** does the work |

**What differs from a residential transaction, item by item:**

| Element | Commercial |
|---|---|
| **The buyer's decision basis** | **Income and return**, not preference |
| **Due diligence** | Longer, and its own contingency — surveys, environmental review, leases and estoppels, financials, zoning and entitlement |
| **Leases in place** | The buyer is buying **the leases**, and inherits every term in them |
| **Environmental** | A phase of the diligence rather than an incident of it |
| **Financing** | Underwritten against the **property's income**, with different terms and often recourse questions |
| **Documents** | Letters of intent; estoppel certificates; subordination, non-disturbance and attornment agreements; assignment of leases |
| **Timeframe** | Longer, at every stage |

> **A gate stated plainly.** **No Colorado environmental statute, zoning or land use statute, or local
> ordinance was read by this project**, and **none of the federal environmental statutes was read.** **No
> requirement, standard, threshold, liability rule, procedure or deadline from any of them is stated
> anywhere in this build.**
>
> What **was** read is the disclosure duty: **§§ 12-10-404(3)(a), 12-10-405(3)(a) and 12-10-407(2)(b)(VI)**
> each require disclosure of **adverse material facts ACTUALLY KNOWN**, expressly including *"**any
> environmental hazards affecting the property that are required by law to be disclosed**"* — and
> **§§ 12-10-404(3)(b), 12-10-405(5) and 12-10-407(4)** each provide there is **no duty to inspect or
> verify.**

## C. Financial composition — outline Topic IV.C

**A commercial party will expect these figures and will expect them right.** Every figure below is supplied
as a given.

### C.1 The income statement

| Line | |
|---|---|
| **Potential gross income** | Full occupancy, full collection |
| less **vacancy and collection loss** | |
| plus **other income** | Parking, storage, fees |
| = **Effective gross income** | |
| less **operating expenses** | Fixed, variable, and reserves — **not** debt service, depreciation or capital improvements |
| = **Net operating income** | The property measure |
| less **debt service** | |
| = **Cash flow before tax** | The equity measure |

### C.2 Worked

> **A 22,000 square foot office building.** Rent **$26.00 per square foot per year**. Vacancy and collection
> loss **8 percent**. Other income **$14,000**. Operating expenses **$196,000**. A loan with annual debt
> service of **$183,000**. Purchase price **$4,150,000**, with **$1,245,000** of equity.
>
> | Line | Calculation | Amount |
> |---|---|---|
> | Potential gross income | 22,000 × $26.00 | **$572,000** |
> | less vacancy and collection loss | 8% of 572,000 | (45,760) |
> | plus other income | | 14,000 |
> | **Effective gross income** | | **$540,240** |
> | less operating expenses | | (196,000) |
> | **Net operating income** | | **$344,240** |
> | less debt service | | (183,000) |
> | **Cash flow before tax** | | **$161,240** |

**The three measures a commercial party asks for, from those figures:**

> **Capitalisation rate** = NOI ÷ price = **$344,240 ÷ $4,150,000 = 8.29 percent** (to two decimals).
>
> **Cash-on-cash return** = cash flow before tax ÷ equity = **$161,240 ÷ $1,245,000 = 12.95 percent.**
>
> **Debt coverage ratio** = NOI ÷ debt service = **$344,240 ÷ $183,000 = 1.88.**

**The capitalisation rate measures the PROPERTY; the cash-on-cash return measures THIS BUYER'S POSITION.** A
client who conflates them will misjudge leverage in both directions. **The debt coverage ratio is the
lender's figure**, and it is why a lender may decline a purchase the buyer's own arithmetic supports.

**Expense ratio** = operating expenses ÷ effective gross income = **$196,000 ÷ $540,240 = 36.28 percent** —
the figure a buyer compares across candidate properties.

**A broker can do all of this with figures the parties and the lender supply.** What a broker does **not**
do is tell the client whether the investment is right for them: **§§ 12-10-404(1)(c)(V), 12-10-405(1)(c)(V)
and 12-10-407(2)(b)(II)** each require **advising the party to obtain expert advice** on material matters
beyond the broker's expertise, and **tax treatment, entity structure and financing suitability are all
outside it.**

## D. Marketing — outline Topic IV.D

**Rule 6.10 applies in full to commercial marketing**, and three of its subsections bite hardest here.

> **6.10.F — authority.** *"**Brokers may not Advertise the availability or price of a property whether for
> sale or lease WITHOUT AUTHORITY FROM THE OWNER OR THE OWNER'S BROKER.**"* An owner's broker **may not
> withhold** it unless doing so is **contradictory to the owner's instructions as memorialized in the
> listing contract or other writing.** Disseminating **another broker's** advertising requires that broker's
> **written permission** and **conspicuous disclosure of the owner's firm**; disseminating an
> **unrepresented owner's** advertising requires the **owner's written permission**, **conspicuous
> disclosure that the owner is not represented**, and **must not include submitting the information into a
> property exchange or multiple listing service.**
>
> **6.10.G — price.** *"**The price quoted in any Advertising will not be anything other than the price
> agreed upon between the Broker and the owner.**"*
>
> **6.10.C — accuracy.** Firms and brokers are **responsible for ensuring all advertising is accurate**,
> including **information entered into multiple listing services** — with **no duty to investigate**
> information from the seller or a third-party source.
>
> **6.10.E — past sales data.** Sales recaps for a subdivision or area **must cite the source** and **carry
> the disclaimer**, if accurate, that the sales **were not necessarily listed or sold by the broker** and
> **are intended only to show trends** or **will separately identify the broker's own activity.** Commercial
> brokers publish market reports; **this is the rule that governs them.**

**And confidentiality constrains what may be said about a live deal.** **§ 12-10-404(2)** and
**§ 12-10-405(2)** each forbid disclosing, without informed consent, that a party **will accept less or pay
more**, their **motivating factors**, or that they **will agree to other financing terms** — and
**§ 12-10-407(3)** binds a transaction-broker to a **six-item** list requiring the **informed consent of all
parties.**

## E. Leasing — outline Topic IV.E

**What a commercial lease settles that a residential lease does not:**

| Term | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Rentable vs. usable area, and the load factor** | Decides what the tenant pays for versus occupies |
| **The expense structure** | **Gross**, **net**, or a **base year with escalations** — and *"net"* alone settles nothing until the document says **which** expenses |
| **CAM reconciliation** | How common area costs are estimated, billed and trued up |
| **Percentage rent** | A base rent plus a percentage of sales over a **breakpoint** |
| **Tenant improvement allowance** | Who builds, who pays, and who owns the improvements at the end |
| **Use and exclusivity** | What the tenant may do, and what the landlord may not let a neighbour do |
| **Co-tenancy** | Relief if anchor tenants leave |
| **Assignment and subletting** | Ordinarily conditional on the landlord's consent |
| **Options** | To renew, expand, terminate, or purchase |
| **Estoppel and SNDA** | What the tenant certifies to a buyer or lender, and how the lease survives a foreclosure |
| **Guaranty** | Who stands behind the tenant entity |

**Percentage rent worked, figures given:** base rent **$7,500** a month plus **6 percent** of gross sales
over a **$125,000** monthly breakpoint. In a month with **$210,000** of sales the overage is **$85,000**, the
percentage rent is **$5,100**, and total rent is **$12,600.**

**A load factor worked, figures given:** a suite of **8,400 usable** square feet in a building with a
**15 percent** load factor is **8,400 × 1.15 = 9,660 rentable** square feet. At **$28.00** per rentable
square foot the annual base rent is **$270,480** — **$32.20 per USABLE foot**, which is the number the
tenant actually feels.

> **A gate.** **No Colorado commercial landlord-tenant statute was read by this project**, and **no rule on
> commercial deposits, notice, default, remedies or eviction is stated anywhere in this build.** Commercial
> leases are heavily negotiated documents, and **the lease itself is prepared by counsel** — it is not among
> the eight kinds of standard form **§ 12-10-403(4)(b)** authorises a broker to complete unless a
> Commission-approved form exists and is appropriate, or another category applies.

## F. Pitfalls — outline Topic IV.F

| Pitfall | The rule or the reason |
|---|---|
| **Treating a letter of intent as harmless** | **§ 12-10-403(4)(b)(VIII)** — a broker-prepared LOI **must state on its face that it is nonbinding and creates no legal rights or obligations** |
| **Assuming commercial work is outside the relationship rules** | **§ 12-10-403(2)** applies; **no commercial carve-out appears in any provision read for this build** |
| **Advertising a listing or a price without authority** | **Rule 6.10.F and G** |
| **A market report with no source and no disclaimer** | **Rule 6.10.E** |
| **Disclosing a party's position to move a deal along** | **§§ 12-10-404(2), 12-10-405(2), 12-10-407(3)** |
| **Quoting a capitalisation rate as though it were a fact about the property** | It is derived from **price and NOI**, both of which are inputs the parties supply |
| **Advising on tax, entity structure or suitability** | **§§ 12-10-404(1)(c)(V), 12-10-405(1)(c)(V), 12-10-407(2)(b)(II)** — advise the party to **obtain expert advice** |
| **Taking a commercial engagement without commercial competency** | **Rule 6.2.B** — **decline**, obtain, obtain assistance, or **co-list** |
| **Missing that the buyer is buying the leases** | Estoppels and lease review are the diligence; a lease term the buyer did not read is a term they now own |
| **Treating environmental review as optional** | The **duty to disclose actually known** adverse material facts including environmental hazards — and **no duty to investigate** is not a reason for a buyer to skip diligence |

**The last row is the competency point of the whole module.** A residential broker's instinct — that
inspection is a contingency the buyer either exercises or waives — **does not transfer.** In commercial work
the diligence **is** the transaction, and a broker who does not know that should be reaching for **Rule
6.2.B.4 — co-list** — before the letter of intent goes out.
