## Learning objectives

1. State what a new broker interviews a brokerage firm about, and why each item matters.
2. State the Colorado supervision rules that stand behind an employment relationship.
3. Identify the costs of entering the field the Commission's outline names.
4. State how a new broker may lawfully find buyers and sellers, and the advertising rules that constrain it.
5. State the competency requirement in Rule 6.2 and its four routes.
6. State what the outline means by "gaining competency" and identify CP 41 as unread.

## A. Interviewing brokerage firms — outline Topic I.A

The outline names four sub-items, and Colorado's rules give each of them a legal edge.

### A.1 The employment agreement — I.A.1

**A new broker is licensed to a firm, and the firm's employing broker supervises them.** What the agreement
settles: **compensation** — how calculated, when paid, on what; **the split and any fees**; **expenses** —
who bears what; **term and termination**, including what happens to pending transactions and to the
broker's files; **whether the relationship is employment or independent contractor** for tax purposes; and
**what the broker may and may not do** — which is the office policy manual in Part A.2.

**One Colorado rule bears directly on compensation and new brokers miss it.**

> **Rule 6.12**, on CMAs and BPOs prepared for a reason **other than an anticipated sale, purchase or
> lease**: such preparation *"**is not considered Real Estate Brokerage Services. As such, any compensation
> received for such preparation IS NOT REQUIRED TO BE PAID TO THE BROKER'S BROKERAGE FIRM UNLESS STATED
> OTHERWISE IN BROKERAGE FIRM'S OFFICE POLICY MANUAL.**"*

**Check the manual before assuming.**

### A.2 Office policies — I.A.2

**Two Colorado requirements make the manual a legal document rather than a handbook.**

> **§ 12-10-408(1)(a)**: every broker *"**shall adopt a WRITTEN OFFICE POLICY that identifies and describes
> the relationships offered to the public** by the broker."*
>
> **§ 12-10-408(1)(b)**: *"A broker **shall not be required to offer or engage in any one or in all** of the
> brokerage relationships enumerated in section 12-10-404, 12-10-405, or 12-10-407."*
>
> **Rule 6.3.B.3**: employing brokers must **develop the Brokerage Firm's written policies as set forth in
> Rule 6.4.**
>
> **Rule 5.1**: a firm receiving Money Belonging to Others must establish **written accounting control
> policies and procedures** including **checks and balances over the financial activities of the broker, the
> firm and UNLICENSED PERSONS**, and **manage the risk of fraud or illegal acts.**

**The first question a new broker asks about the manual is which relationships the firm offers.** A firm may
decline to offer **single agency**, or decline to offer **transaction-brokerage**. That decision shapes
every transaction the new broker will do — and **§ 12-10-408(1)(c)** requires that where a consumer asks
about a relationship the firm does **not** offer, the broker **provide the Commission's own written
definition of it.**

### A.3 Support — I.A.3

Forms, systems, transaction coordination, marketing, technology, errors and omissions coverage, and access
to the employing broker.

**Support is not a courtesy; some of it is required.** **Rule 6.3.B.4** requires the employing broker to
provide *"a **'Reasonable-Level of Supervision'** for all Associate Brokers"*, and **Rule 6.2.B.3** names
*"the Employing Broker, Supervisory Broker, a Broker who meets the requirements ... or legal counsel that is
competent in the matter"* as one of the four routes out of an incompetence problem — which presupposes the
new broker can reach one of them.

### A.4 Training and supervision — I.A.4

> **Rule 6.3.A.1**: employing brokers *"**must exercise authority, direction, and supervision over any
> Associate Brokers** shown in the records of the Commission as supervised by the Employing Broker **to
> ensure conformance to the Commission statutes and these Rules**"* — and *"**whenever a complaint is filed
> with the Commission against an Associate Broker, the Commission MAY INVESTIGATE whether there have been
> violations ... by the EMPLOYING BROKER.**"*
>
> **Rule 6.3.A.2**: employing brokers **must also supervise all unlicensed employees**, *"including, but not
> limited to, Unlicensed On-Site Managers, secretaries, bookkeepers, and personal assistants of Associate
> Brokers."*
>
> **Rule 6.3.B**: employing brokers must **maintain all trust and escrow accounts and records** (Chapter 5),
> **maintain all transaction records** (Rule 6.20), **develop the firm's written policies** (Rule 6.4), and
> **provide a reasonable level of supervision.**

**A new broker should read Rule 6.3.A.1's second sentence carefully**, because it explains why a
well-supervised firm is stricter than a new broker expects: **a complaint against the associate can become
an investigation of the employing broker.**

**And a statutory limit on independence a new broker meets immediately.** **§ 12-10-203(5)(c)(I)**: a
broker licensed after 31 December 1996 *"**shall [not] engage in an independent brokerage practice without
first having served actively as a real estate broker for at least TWO YEARS**"*, and the Commission *"shall
adopt rules requiring an employing broker to ensure that **a HIGH LEVEL OF SUPERVISION** is exercised over
such a broker during the two-year period."*

**§ 12-10-203(5)(c)(II)**: no such broker may **employ another broker** without first completing **24 clock
hours in brokerage administration**; and **(5)(c)(III)**: from **1 January 2019**, a broker may not act as
an **employing broker** without first demonstrating, under Commission rules, **experience and knowledge
sufficient to employ and adequately supervise other brokers.**

**Rule 2.1.B** carries the education half of that: an applicant for an **Employing Broker level licence**
must complete **24 hours of classroom instruction or equivalent distance learning in Brokerage
Administration.**

## B. Cost to get started — outline Topic I.B

The outline names four sub-items, and this module states what each is without inventing a figure.

| Sub-item | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **1. Signs, business cards, brochures, etc.** | Marketing materials — **all of which are Advertising under Rule 1.2** |
| **2. Home office vs. desk fee** | Where the broker works and what the firm charges for it |
| **3. Memberships** | Realtor boards, **multiple listing services**, showing services |
| **4. E/O insurance, license fees, car insurance** | The costs of being licensed and insured |

> **No figure is stated for any of them.** Fees, dues, premiums and splits are set by the Division, by
> private organisations, and by firms, and **none was read from a primary source by this project.**
>
> **Errors and omissions insurance in particular.** **Chapter 3 of 4 CCR 725-1 is titled "Licensure,
> Renewal, License Status, and Insurance"**, and the Division publishes a **Broker Insurance Requirements**
> page. **The insurance rules in Chapter 3 were not read in detail for this build**, and **no coverage
> amount, deductible, carrier requirement, policy form or effective date is stated anywhere in this
> build.** **This is a submission gate**, and the **Contracts and Regulations** outline names errors and
> omissions insurance at its Topic V.C for the same reason.

**Rule 1.2 is worth reading at the point a new broker orders business cards**, because it settles what
counts as advertising:

> *"**Advertise or Advertising**: The promotion, solicitation, or representation of Real Estate Brokerage
> Services requiring a License. Advertising may include, but is not limited to, **business cards, brochures,
> websites, signage, property flyers, mailings (paper or electronic), SOCIAL MEDIA, letterhead, EMAIL
> SIGNATURES, and contract documents.** A uniform resource locator (URL) and an email address are **not**
> considered Advertising for purposes of Rule 6.10 so long as they are **not directly used to promote or
> solicit** Real Estate Brokerage Services."*

**Email signatures and social media are advertising.** **Rule 6.10** then governs what advertising must
contain and must not do, and Module 02 of this course delivers it.

## C. How to find buyers and sellers — outline Topic I.C

The outline names three: **networking**, **family and friends**, and **marketing**.

**All three are lawful, and all three are constrained by the same three provisions.**

> **1. Disclosure comes before confidential information.** **Rule 6.5.A**: written disclosures under
> § 12-10-408 *"**must be made to a Consumer PRIOR TO ELICITING OR DISCUSSING CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION**"*.
> **Rule 6.5.B**: this does **not** include *"**preliminary conversations or 'small talk' concerning price
> range, location and property styles**, or **responding to general factual questions** ... concerning
> properties which have been Advertised."*
>
> **A conversation at a family gathering that moves from "what are prices doing" to "what could we afford"
> has crossed the line**, and the disclosure is already due.
>
> **2. Everything published is advertising.** **Rule 1.2** and **Rule 6.10.**
>
> **3. Conflicts are disclosed in writing, continuously.** **Rule 6.17.A**: brokers and firms have a
> *"**continuing duty to disclose, in writing, any known Conflict of Interest that may arise in the course
> of any real estate transaction.**"* **Rule 6.17.B**: a broker who **sells, buys or leases on their own
> account must disclose in the contracting instrument or a separate concurrent writing that they are a
> licensed Broker.**

**Working with family and friends is where new brokers most often meet Rule 6.17.** A relationship that
would affect how the broker acts is a conflict, and the duty to disclose it is **continuing** and **in
writing.**

**And fair housing runs through prospecting as fully as through showing.** **§§ 12-10-404(1)(e),
12-10-405(1)(e) and 12-10-407(2)(d)** each require compliance with applicable law *"**including fair housing
and civil rights statutes or regulations**"*, so a targeting decision that excludes on a protected basis is
**also** a brokerage relationship violation, and **§ 12-10-411** makes any Part 4 violation an act the
Commission may investigate.

## D. Gaining competency — outline Topic I.D

The outline names three sub-items — **acquiring knowledge**, **experience**, and **types of training** — and
cites **CP 41** for the topic.

> **CP 41 was NOT obtained or read by this project.** **No position statement content is stated anywhere in
> this build.** **This is a submission gate**, and it is the second course in this build to record it — the
> **Record Keeping and Trust Accounts** outline names *"Position Statements"* at its Topic I.D.2 for the
> same reason.

**What IS stated comes from Rule 6.2, which was read in full and is the rule the 2012 outline revision was
written around.**

> **Rule 6.2 — Competency**
>
> **A.** *"In order to conduct Real Estate Brokerage Services, a Broker **must possess the necessary
> experience, training, and knowledge** to provide Real Estate Brokerage Services and **maintain compliance
> with the applicable federal, state and local laws, rules, regulations and ordinances.**"*
>
> **B.** *"**If a Broker does not have the necessary experience, training, and knowledge, the Broker
> must:**"*
> **1.** *"**DECLINE** to provide Real Estate Brokerage Services;"*
> **2.** *"**OBTAIN** the necessary experience, training, and knowledge;"*
> **3.** *"**OBTAIN THE ASSISTANCE** of their Employing Broker, Supervisory Broker, a Broker who meets the
> requirements as set forth in subsection A., **or LEGAL COUNSEL that is competent in the matter**;"* or
> **4.** *"**CO-LIST** with another Broker who meets the requirements as set forth in subsection A."*

**Four routes, and the FIRST is to decline.** That ordering is deliberate and a new broker should notice
it: **the rule does not treat declining as a failure.**

**And the outline's own footnote, attached to Topics II through V, states the purpose:**

> *"**Educators should make sure students understand HOW COMPETENCY IS GAINED in each specialty e.g. through
> working with an experienced mentor or partner and/or taking educational courses to become competent in the
> field or area SO NO HARM IS DONE TO A MEMBER OF THE PUBLIC.**"*

**"So no harm is done to a member of the public" is the Commission's own statement of why competency is a
rule and not an aspiration.**

**Modules 02 through 05 of this course are the four specialties the Commission then names** — residential,
property management, commercial, and other — **and each is a competency module**, not a survey of the
specialty.
