## Learning objectives

1. State how each Schedule B-1 requirement becomes a document at closing.
2. Name the documents ordinarily signed and recorded at a closing and say what each does.
3. State what a broker reviews a document for, and what a broker does not do with it.
4. State the four-year retention rule, its two start dates, and what it excludes.
5. State what escrow or closing instructions do.

## A. The title commitment as it relates to legal documents

Module 03 read the commitment. **This part turns Schedule B-1 into paper.**

**Every requirement on Schedule B-1 becomes a document someone must produce.**

| A typical B-1 requirement | The document that satisfies it | Who produces it |
|---|---|---|
| Pay and release the existing deed of trust | **Payoff statement**, then the **release** | Lender / public trustee, through the closing entity |
| Satisfy a judgment lien | **Satisfaction or release of judgment** | Judgment creditor's counsel |
| Evidence of authority for an entity seller | **Corporate resolution**, **statement of authority**, or operating agreement excerpt | The entity, through counsel |
| Evidence of authority for a trust or estate | **Trustee's certificate**, **letters testamentary**, **death certificate** | Counsel or the probate court |
| Deed from the vested owner | **The deed** | Prepared by counsel or on a form appropriate to the transaction |
| Survey or ILC where required | **Survey** or **Improvement Location Certificate** | Licensed surveyor / preparer |
| Payment of taxes and assessments | **Tax certificate**, receipts | Treasurer, through the closing entity |

**The broker's job with Schedule B-1 is assignment and tracking, not production.** Each item needs **an
owner and a date**, and an item nobody owns is the item that stops the closing.

> **A boundary this module holds.** **Who may sign for an entity, a trust or an estate is a question of
> Colorado law this project did not read.** **No Colorado entity, trust, probate or statement-of-authority
> statute was read**, and **no requirement, form or authority rule from any of them is stated anywhere in
> this build.** Those requirements come from **the title company and counsel.**

## B. Review of prepared legal and financial documents

### B.1 The documents

| Document | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Deed** | Conveys the property — and **which deed** determines what the grantor warrants |
| **Promissory note** | Creates the **debt** |
| **Deed of trust** | Creates the **lien**, with a **trustee** holding title or a power of sale for the lender |
| **Release of the existing deed of trust** | Discharges the seller's lien |
| **Closing statement** | The accounting between the parties — and **Rule 6.19.A requires it to be signed by the broker** |
| **Bill of sale** | Conveys personal property included in the transaction |
| **Affidavits and certifications** | As the closing entity, the lender or the title company requires |
| **Escrow or closing instructions** | Direct the closing entity — Part D |

**A broker does not select the form of deed.** The choice determines what the grantor warrants and what the
grantee receives, which is a legal question — and **§ 12-10-403(4)(b)** lists the eight kinds of standard
form a broker may complete, of which a deed is one only if a **Commission-approved form** exists and is
appropriate, or one of the other seven categories applies.

**§ 12-10-403(4)(c)**, closing sentence: the broker *"**shall advise the parties that the forms have
important legal consequences and that the parties should consult legal counsel before signing the
forms.**"*

### B.2 What a broker reviews for, and what a broker does not do

> **Reviews for:** does the **name** match how title is vested and how the contract reads; does the **legal
> description** match the commitment; does the **price** match the contract; do the **loan figures** match
> the lender's; do the **prorations** match the worksheet; are the **dates** consistent; is anything on the
> statement that is not in the contract or a written amendment.
>
> **Does not do:** opine on the **legal effect** of a document; advise which **deed form** to use; advise
> whether a **title exception** is acceptable; or advise on **tax consequences.**

**Rule 6.19.C** states the attendance requirement that makes this review possible: a broker with a
brokerage relationship *"**must review closing documents AND attend closing OR BE REASONABLY AVAILABLE**"*,
and where they will not be, **another broker designated by the firm may review and attend on their behalf**
and **assumes joint responsibility** for accuracy, completeness and delivery of the signed closing
statement.

**Rule 6.2** governs what happens when the review runs past the broker's competence: **decline**, **obtain**
the experience, training and knowledge, **obtain the assistance** of the employing broker, a supervisory
broker, a competent broker **or legal counsel competent in the matter**, or **co-list.**

## C. Transaction files — retention of file documents

> **Rule 6.20**: *"Both a Broker and a Brokerage Firm **must retain transaction files for all transactions
> for a period of FOUR (4) YEARS** beginning from **the consummation date of the transaction OR the
> expiration date of any Listing Contracts that do not consummate.** Required documents in a transaction
> file are designated in the **Commission's Transaction File Checklist** and may be found on the Division's
> website. A Broker is **not required to obtain and retain copies of existing public records, title
> commitments, loan applications, lender required disclosures, or related affirmations from independent
> third party closing entities after the closing date.**"*

**Three things to hold onto:**

1. **Four years**, and **both** the broker and the firm must retain.
2. **Two start dates.** A transaction that **closes** runs from **consummation**. A **listing that never
   produces a closing** runs from **the listing contract's expiration** — so a file with no transaction in
   it still has a retention clock.
3. **Five categories are expressly excluded**, and every one of them is a document generated by somebody
   else: existing public records, **title commitments**, loan applications, lender required disclosures,
   and related affirmations from independent third party closing entities **after the closing date.**

**And Rule 6.19.A adds the delivery step at the front of it:** if the broker is licensed with a brokerage
firm, they *"**must deliver closing statements to the Brokerage Firm along with any other closing documents,
IMMEDIATELY FOLLOWING CLOSING.**"*

> **The Commission's Transaction File Checklist was NOT obtained by this project**, and **no module of this
> build states what documents it designates.** **This is a submission gate**, and it is one a Closings
> course cannot leave open — the rule points to the checklist by name for exactly this content.

**Two more retention-adjacent rules read for this build:**

- **Rule 5.14.E** — the three-way reconciliation reports, ledgers, journals and bank statements **may be
  kept electronically.**
- **Rule 5.21** — a broker and firm **must produce for inspection by the Commission any document or record**
  reasonably necessary for investigation or audit, and **failure within the time set is grounds for
  disciplinary action** absent an extension. **Rule 6.24** governs electronic records and production.

## D. Other closing documents — escrow instructions

**Escrow or closing instructions tell the closing entity what to do**: what documents to obtain, what
conditions must be satisfied, what to disburse and to whom, and what to record and in what order.

**Their function is that neither party has to perform first on trust.** The neutral third party holds
documents and funds and releases them only when the stated conditions are met — which is why a closing
entity that is asked to disburse into a dispute should not, and why **a broker who directs one to do so has
asked for something the entity should refuse.**

**Colorado's Good Funds rule applies to the broker and the firm directly.** **Rule 6.19.E**: a broker or
firm *"**must not disburse or authorize disbursement of funds until those funds have been received and are
either ... available for immediate withdrawal as a matter of right ... or available for immediate withdrawal
as a consequence of an agreement with a financial institution ... If the agreement contains contingencies or
reservations, NO DISBURSEMENTS CAN BE MADE until these are satisfied.**"*

**§ 38-35-125, which Rule 6.19.E cites, was NOT read by this project**, and **no definition of good funds,
clearing period or list of acceptable instruments from the statute is stated anywhere in this build.**

**Other documents that travel with a closing**, none of whose contents this build states: the **seller's
property disclosure**; the **inspection notice**; **lead-based paint** disclosure and acknowledgment for
pre-1978 housing; **HOA status letters and association documents**; **withholding certificates** for a
non-resident or foreign seller; and the **lender's own disclosures.**
