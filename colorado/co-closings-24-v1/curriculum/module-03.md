## Learning objectives

1. State the rule in Topic III.A.1 and apply it to a charge nobody agreed to.
2. Identify a non-contractual fee and say what a broker does about one.
3. Read a title commitment's four schedules and say what each does.
4. Identify what on a commitment must be cleared before closing.
5. Calculate a proration on a stated convention, in either direction.
6. Calculate prepaid interest, and distinguish it from a proration.
7. Compute cash due from the buyer and cash to the seller and prove the total.

## A. Interpreting the prepared contract

### A.1 "Close to contract or amend contract in writing"

**The contract is the instruction sheet, and the closing follows it.** If the parties want something
different from what the contract says, **the contract changes first, in writing** — and the instrument is
the **Agreement to Amend/Extend Contract**, named at the **Contracts and Regulations** outline's Topic
IV.B.2.

**Where this bites, in order of how often it happens:**

| Situation | The rule's answer |
|---|---|
| A repair the seller said they would make, not in the contract | **Amend, or it is not a closing charge** |
| A credit the parties agreed on the phone | **Amend** |
| A cost allocated differently from the contract | **Amend** |
| A date moved | **Amend** |
| A party wants something added at the closing table | **Amend, and everyone signs** |

**Why it matters beyond tidiness.** A closing statement carrying a charge no contract supports is not
**accurate**, and **Rule 6.19.A** requires the consumer to receive a closing statement that is *"**accurate,
complete and detailed ... SIGNED BY THE BROKER.**"* **Rule 6.19.B** makes a broker who prepares the
documents responsible for their **accuracy and completeness.** The broker signs it.

**And a side agreement kept off the statement is the loan fraud problem** the **Contracts and Regulations**
outline names at its Topic V.F.4: a payment, credit or allowance agreed between the parties and not
disclosed to the lender. **Everything the parties agreed goes into the contract or an amendment, and what
is in the contract appears on the statement.**

### A.2 Non-contractual fees and costs — Topic III.A.2

**A non-contractual fee is a charge that arises at closing but is not allocated by the contract** — a
lender's late-appearing fee, a closing entity's administrative charge, a payoff statement fee, a wire fee,
a courier charge, an HOA transfer or status letter fee.

**Three questions, in order:**

1. **Is it real?** Someone is charging for something. What?
2. **Does the contract allocate it?** If it does, it is contractual and it goes where the contract says.
3. **If not, who is going to pay it, and have they agreed?**

**A charge nobody agreed to does not become agreed by appearing on a statement.** The broker raises it
**before** the closing, and if the parties resolve it differently from the contract, the answer is A.1 —
**amend in writing.**

> **Two Colorado rules bear directly on charges the broker themselves assesses or receives, and both were
> read.**
>
> **Rule 5.17 — Mark-Ups.** The broker and firm *"**must obtain PRIOR WRITTEN CONSENT from the Consumer they
> represent to assess and receive mark-ups and/or other compensation for services performed, regardless if
> for the benefit of the Broker or another third party.**"* They must also **disclose all mark-ups paid for
> by any Customer**, and **retain accurate on-going records** verifying disclosure and consent and fully
> accounting for the amounts.
>
> **§ 12-10-410(6).** *"**Prior to entering into a brokerage or listing agreement or a contract to buy,
> sell, or lease, the identity of those parties, persons, or entities paying compensation or commissions to
> any broker shall be disclosed to the parties to the transaction.**"*
>
> And **Rule 6.21.A**: **no referral fee except in accordance with RESPA** and unless a **reasonable cause**
> exists under **§ 12-10-304(1)**. **Neither RESPA nor § 12-10-304 was read**, and neither is stated here.

## B. Reading a title commitment

The outline requires the student to read and understand a **sample title commitment** at Topic III.B.

**A commitment is the title company's undertaking to issue a policy on stated terms.** It is arranged in
schedules, and each does a different job.

| Schedule | What it contains | What the broker looks for |
|---|---|---|
| **Schedule A** | The **commitment date**; the **policy or policies** to be issued and the **amounts**; the **estate** covered; **who currently holds title**; and the **legal description** | Does the vested owner match the seller on the contract? Does the description match the property? Is the policy amount right? |
| **Schedule B-1** | **Requirements** — what must be **done** before the policy issues | Every item here is a task with an owner and a deadline |
| **Schedule B-2** | **Exceptions** — what the policy will **NOT** cover | Every item here is a risk the buyer takes unless it is removed |
| **Schedule C / notes** | Further matters, as the company arranges its commitment | Read them |

**The two schedules that matter to a broker are B-1 and B-2, and they matter for opposite reasons.**

**Schedule B-1 is a to-do list.** Payoff and release of an existing deed of trust; satisfaction of a
judgment; a corporate resolution or trustee's certificate; a death certificate; a survey or ILC; an
executed deed. **Each item needs someone assigned to it and a date**, and an item nobody owns is the item
that stops the closing.

**Schedule B-2 is what the buyer is taking subject to.** Easements, restrictive covenants, mineral
reservations, special district obligations, the standard printed exceptions. **A buyer who has not read
Schedule B-2 does not know what they are buying.**

**What the broker does and does not do with it.**

> **Does:** deliver it promptly; make sure the **buyer knows the objection deadline the contract set**;
> make sure each **B-1 requirement is assigned**; and raise anything that does not match the contract.
>
> **Does not:** opine on the legal effect of an exception, advise whether a title defect is material, or
> tell a party whether to object. **§§ 12-10-404(1)(c)(V), 12-10-405(1)(c)(V) and 12-10-407(2)(b)(II)**
> each require **advising the party to obtain expert advice** as to material matters beyond the broker's
> expertise, and **Rule 6.2** requires the broker to possess the necessary experience, training and
> knowledge — or **decline**, **obtain** them, **obtain the assistance** of a competent broker or **legal
> counsel competent in the matter**, or **co-list.**

**And the title objection deadline is a contingency**, so the three-part test from the Contracts course
applies: **what event, by when, what happens if it does not occur.**

**Rule 6.20** expressly does **not** require a broker to obtain and retain copies of **title commitments**
after the closing date, along with existing public records, loan applications, lender required disclosures,
and related affirmations from independent third party closing entities.

## C. Calculating: prorations

### C.1 The method, again

1. **Find the daily or monthly rate** — the annual amount ÷ the number of periods the **contract's**
   convention uses.
2. **Count the periods on each side of closing**, using the contract's convention for the **day of
   closing**.
3. **Multiply**, and place the result with the right sign for each party.

**Prepaid or in arrears decides the direction:**

| | Seller | Buyer |
|---|---|---|
| **Paid in ADVANCE by the seller** | **Credit** — reimbursed for the unused part | **Debit** |
| **Payable in ARREARS** | **Debit** — owes their share | **Credit** — will pay the bill |

### C.2 Worked both ways, on given figures

> **Annual amount $5,400. Closing 1 October. 360-day year, 30-day months. Seller charged through the day
> before closing.**
>
> Daily rate: **$5,400 ÷ 360 = $15.00**. Seller's days: **270** (January–September). Buyer's days: **90**.
>
> | If the amount is | Seller | Buyer |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Prepaid by the seller** | **Credit $1,350** (90 × $15) | **Debit $1,350** |
> | **Payable in arrears** | **Debit $4,050** (270 × $15) | **Credit $4,050** |

**Same transaction, same date, same convention — two entirely different entries.** The question that
decides it is whether the money has already been paid.

### C.3 On a 365-day year with actual days

> **Annual amount $5,475. Closing 15 April. 365-day year, actual days. Buyer charged for the day of
> closing.**
>
> Daily rate: **$5,475 ÷ 365 = $15.00**. Seller's days: 31 + 28 + 31 + 14 = **104** (through 14 April).
> **Seller's share = 104 × $15.00 = $1,560.** Buyer's days: 365 − 104 = **261**, **$3,915.**
>
> **$1,560 + $3,915 = $5,475.** The two shares always sum to the whole — which is the check to run before
> entering either.

### C.4 Rents and deposits on an income property

> **A four-unit building at $1,600 per unit per month. Closing 20 June, 30-day month, buyer charged for the
> day of closing. Rents for June were collected in full by the seller. Security deposits of $1,600 per unit
> are held.**
>
> June rent collected: **4 × $1,600 = $6,400**. Daily: **$6,400 ÷ 30 = $213.3333**.
> Buyer's days: **11** (20th through 30th). **Buyer's share = 11 × $213.3333 = $2,346.67.**
>
> | Entry | Buyer | Seller |
> |---|---|---|
> | Rent proration | **Credit $2,346.67** | **Debit $2,346.67** |
> | Security deposits (4 × $1,600) | **Credit $6,400.00** | **Debit $6,400.00** |
>
> **The deposits transfer in full, not prorated** — they are the tenants' money, and the buyer will have to
> return them.
>
> **And in Colorado a brokerage holding them is inside Chapter 5:** **Rule 5.5** requires **separate trust
> or escrow accounts — a minimum of one for rental receipts and a minimum of one for security deposits**;
> **Rule 5.8.A** forbids delivering deposits to an owner without the **tenant's written authorization in a
> lease or written notice** telling the tenant who holds them and how to request return; and **Rule 5.8.B**
> governs a transfer between firms — **written disclosure within 30 days**, **transfer within 60 days**, and
> the new firm **verifying each deposit against the current lease.**

## D. Calculating: prepaid interest, and cash to close

### D.1 Prepaid interest is not a proration

**Mortgage interest is paid in arrears** — the payment made on the first of a month covers the interest that
accrued the **previous** month. So a loan funding mid-month produces no payment until the month after next,
and the lender collects the intervening interest **at closing**. That is **prepaid interest**, and it is a
**buyer debit only** — there is no seller side to it.

> **A new loan of $384,000 at 6.5 percent funds on 12 September. A 365-day year is used. Interest is
> collected from funding through the end of the month.**
>
> Daily interest: **$384,000 × 0.065 ÷ 365 = $68.3836** (rounded at the end).
> Days 12 through 30 inclusive: **19 days.**
> **Prepaid interest = 19 × $68.3836 = $1,299.29**, **debited to the buyer.**

### D.2 A full cash-to-close calculation

**Every figure supplied as a given.**

> Price **$525,000**. Earnest money **$15,000** already paid. New loan **$420,000**. Buyer's loan costs
> **$5,600**, including the prepaid interest. Buyer's recording **$180**. Buyer's share of prepaid HOA dues
> **$310**. Buyer's title endorsement **$150**.
>
> | Buyer debit | | Buyer credit | |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Purchase price | 525,000.00 | Earnest money | 15,000.00 |
> | Loan costs | 5,600.00 | New loan proceeds | 420,000.00 |
> | Recording | 180.00 | | |
> | HOA proration | 310.00 | | |
> | Title endorsement | 150.00 | | |
> | **TOTAL** | **531,240.00** | **TOTAL** | **435,000.00** |
>
> **Cash due from the buyer = 531,240.00 − 435,000.00 = $96,240.00.**

> And the seller's side of the same transaction: existing loan payoff **$268,900**; compensation at
> **5.5 percent** of the price; title insurance premium **$1,940**; recording of the release **$40**; HOA
> proration **$310** credited.
>
> | Seller debit | | Seller credit | |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Existing loan payoff | 268,900.00 | Purchase price | 525,000.00 |
> | Compensation (5.5%) | 28,875.00 | HOA proration | 310.00 |
> | Title insurance | 1,940.00 | | |
> | Recording | 40.00 | | |
> | **TOTAL** | **299,755.00** | **TOTAL** | **525,310.00** |
>
> **Cash to the seller = 525,310.00 − 299,755.00 = $225,555.00.**

**The proof:**

| In | | Out | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buyer's cash | 96,240.00 | Payoff | 268,900.00 |
| Earnest money | 15,000.00 | Compensation | 28,875.00 |
| Loan proceeds | 420,000.00 | Title insurance | 1,940.00 |
| | | Recording (180 + 40) | 220.00 |
| | | Buyer's loan costs | 5,600.00 |
| | | Buyer's title endorsement | 150.00 |
| | | Seller's proceeds | 225,555.00 |
| **TOTAL** | **531,240.00** | **TOTAL** | **531,240.00** |

**In equals out.** Note that the **HOA proration nets to zero across the two sides** — a debit to the buyer
and a credit to the seller of the same $310 — which is what a proration between the parties should do.

### D.3 The check to run before signing anything

1. **Does every charge trace to the contract or a written amendment?** (Topic III.A.1)
2. **Does each proration's two halves sum to the whole?**
3. **Does each party's column settle to a single figure?**
4. **Does in equal out?**
5. **Is the statement accurate, complete and detailed?** **Rule 6.19.A** — and the broker signs it.
