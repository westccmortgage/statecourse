# Colorado Closings — Module 02

## Introduction to a six-column worksheet

**Course:** Closings (24 classroom hours) · **Instructional hours: 2.0** of 24.0
**Commission outline topic:** **II — Introduction to a 6-Column Worksheet**, allocated **2.0 hours**
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No Colorado qualifying education hours are available.

> ### Why the Commission gives this its own topic
>
> The **six-column worksheet** is the instrument on which every closing problem in Module 05 is worked, and
> the Commission names it twice across its six outlines:
>
> - **Closings outline, Topic II** — *"Introduction to a 6-column worksheet"*, **2.0 hours**;
> - **Record Keeping and Trust Accounts outline, Topic II.B.4.a** — *"Working from a sample contract and
>   **6-column settlement worksheet**, student shall create journal and ledger entries leading to the
>   closing of a real estate sale by a broker."*
>
> **The same worksheet, used twice, for two different purposes.** Here it produces the closing figures.
> There it feeds the brokerage's own trust accounting. **A student who does not have the worksheet cold
> cannot do either.**
>
> **No Commission form, template or worksheet was obtained by this project.** This module teaches **the
> structure and the arithmetic**, which are the same whatever form the columns are printed on, and states
> **no form's layout, column headings, line numbering or version.**

## Learning objectives

1. Name the six columns and say what each accumulates.
2. State the meaning of debit and credit for each of the buyer and the seller.
3. Place any ordinary closing item in the right column with the right sign.
4. State the balancing identity the worksheet must satisfy.
5. Distinguish an item that is prorated from one that is a single-party charge.
6. Compute cash due from the buyer and cash due to the seller from a completed worksheet.

---

## A. The structure

The worksheet arranges every item of a transaction across **three pairs of columns** — a **debit** and a
**credit** column for each of three accounts.

| Pair | Columns | What it accumulates |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Buyer debit** / **Buyer credit** | Everything the buyer owes and everything the buyer is credited with |
| **2** | **Seller debit** / **Seller credit** | Everything charged to and credited to the seller |
| **3** | **Broker (or settlement) debit** / **Broker credit** | The money actually passing through the broker's or closing entity's hands |

**Six columns.** The third pair is what makes this a **broker's** worksheet rather than a settlement
statement: it tracks **the funds the broker receives and disburses**, which is exactly what **Rule 5.14**
requires the **journal** and **ledger** to record.

---

## B. Debit and credit, said in one sentence each

> **A DEBIT is what a party PAYS or is CHARGED. A CREDIT is what a party RECEIVES or is credited toward what
> they owe.**

Applied to the two parties:

| | Buyer | Seller |
|---|---|---|
| **Debit means** | The buyer must bring this money | This is charged against the seller's proceeds |
| **Credit means** | This reduces what the buyer must bring | This increases the seller's proceeds |

**The purchase price is the anchor, and it is the one item every student places correctly:**

| Item | Buyer | Seller |
|---|---|---|
| **Purchase price** | **Debit** — the buyer owes it | **Credit** — the seller is owed it |

Everything else follows from asking two questions in order: **who bears this cost**, and **has it already
been paid?**

---

## C. Placing the ordinary items

| Item | Buyer | Seller | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Purchase price** | Debit | Credit | The anchor |
| **Earnest money already paid** | **Credit** | — | The buyer has already put it up; it reduces what they bring |
| **New loan proceeds** | **Credit** | — | The lender is funding part of what the buyer owes |
| **Loan assumed by the buyer** | **Credit** | **Debit** | The buyer takes on the debt; the seller is relieved of it |
| **Seller carry note taken back** | **Credit** | **Debit** | The same logic — the seller is financing part of the price |
| **Existing loan to be paid off** | — | **Debit** | Paid from the seller's proceeds |
| **Broker's compensation** | Per contract | Usually **Debit** | Whoever the contract says pays it |
| **Buyer's loan costs** | **Debit** | — | Origination, points, appraisal, credit |
| **Prepaid interest on the new loan** | **Debit** | — | Funding date to the end of the month |
| **Taxes paid in advance by the seller** | **Debit** | **Credit** | The buyer reimburses the unused part |
| **Taxes payable in arrears** | **Credit** | **Debit** | The seller owes their share; the buyer will pay the bill |
| **Rents collected by the seller for the current period** | **Credit** | **Debit** | The buyer owns the days after closing |
| **Security deposits held** | **Credit** | **Debit** | They belong to tenants and must travel to the buyer |
| **HOA assessments paid in advance** | **Debit** | **Credit** | Same logic as prepaid taxes |
| **Title insurance premium** | Per contract | Per contract | Whoever the contract says |
| **Recording fees** | Usually **Debit** | Sometimes **Debit** | Deed vs. release |
| **Repairs the seller agreed to pay** | — | **Debit** | Only if the contract or an amendment says so |

**The last row is the discipline of the whole course**, and the Commission's outline states it at Topic
III.A.1 in six words: ***"Close to contract or amend contract in writing."*** A charge that is not in the
contract or in a written amendment does not belong on the worksheet.

**Two items students place backwards more than any others:**

**Security deposits.** They are **not** the seller's money and **not** the buyer's — they belong to the
tenants. They are **debited to the seller and credited to the buyer** because the buyer will have to return
them. And in Colorado, if a brokerage is holding them, **Rule 5.5** requires them in a **separate trust or
escrow account** from rental receipts, and **Rule 5.8** governs their transfer.

**Rents.** The seller has collected the whole month; the buyer owns the days from closing forward. So the
**buyer is credited** and the **seller debited** with the buyer's share.

## D. The balancing identity

**A completed worksheet must balance in two ways at once.**

> **1. Each party's own columns settle to a single figure.**
>
> **Buyer:** total debits − total credits = **cash the buyer must bring** (or, if credits exceed debits,
> cash back to the buyer).
>
> **Seller:** total credits − total debits = **cash the seller receives** (or, if debits exceed credits,
> cash the seller must bring).

> **2. The money in equals the money out.**
>
> Everything received at closing — the buyer's cash, the earnest money, the loan proceeds — must equal
> everything disbursed: the payoff, the compensation, the fees, and the seller's proceeds.
>
> **This is the third column pair**, and it is the one that ties the closing to **Rule 5.14**: *"**The sum
> of all ledger balances must agree AT ALL TIMES with the corresponding journal after each transaction has
> been posted**"*, with **three-way reconciliation performed monthly** to show that the journal balance, the
> sum of the ledger balances, and the reconciled bank balance are the same.
>
> **The Record Keeping and Trust Accounts outline states the identity in its own notation** at Topic II.B.3:
> ***"Journal Balance + Sum of Ledger Balances = Bank Balance = Transaction File Liabilities."***

**A worksheet that does not balance has an error, not a rounding difference.** Find it before the closing,
because **Rule 6.19.A** requires the consumer to receive a closing statement that is **accurate, complete
and detailed — and signed by the broker**, and **Rule 6.19.B** makes a broker who prepares the documents
responsible for their **accuracy and completeness.**

## E. A worked worksheet

**Every figure below is supplied as a given.**

> **The transaction.** Price **$480,000**. Earnest money of **$10,000** already paid and held by the
> brokerage. The buyer obtains a **new loan of $384,000**. The seller's existing loan payoff is
> **$212,400**. Compensation of **6 percent of the price**, charged to the seller. Buyer's loan costs
> **$4,200**. Title insurance premium **$1,850**, charged to the seller. Recording fees **$180** to the
> buyer and **$40** to the seller. Annual property taxes of **$4,320** are payable **in arrears**; closing
> is **1 September** on a **360-day year with 30-day months**, and the **seller** is charged through the day
> before closing.

**Step 1 — the tax proration.** Daily rate **$4,320 ÷ 360 = $12.00**. The seller held **240 days**
(January through August, 8 × 30). **Seller's share = 240 × $12.00 = $2,880**, and because the taxes are
**in arrears** the buyer will pay the bill — so it is a **credit to the buyer** and a **debit to the
seller.**

**Step 2 — the worksheet.**

| Item | Buyer debit | Buyer credit | Seller debit | Seller credit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Purchase price | **480,000.00** | | | **480,000.00** |
| Earnest money | | **10,000.00** | | |
| New loan proceeds | | **384,000.00** | | |
| Existing loan payoff | | | **212,400.00** | |
| Compensation (6% of 480,000) | | | **28,800.00** | |
| Buyer's loan costs | **4,200.00** | | | |
| Title insurance premium | | | **1,850.00** | |
| Recording fees | **180.00** | | **40.00** | |
| Tax proration (240 days, in arrears) | | **2,880.00** | **2,880.00** | |
| **TOTALS** | **484,380.00** | **396,880.00** | **245,970.00** | **480,000.00** |

**Step 3 — the two settlement figures.**

> **Cash the buyer must bring = 484,380.00 − 396,880.00 = $87,500.00**
>
> **Cash the seller receives = 480,000.00 − 245,970.00 = $234,030.00**

**Step 4 — prove the money.**

| Received at closing | | Disbursed at closing | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buyer's cash | 87,500.00 | Existing loan payoff | 212,400.00 |
| Earnest money | 10,000.00 | Compensation | 28,800.00 |
| New loan proceeds | 384,000.00 | Title insurance premium | 1,850.00 |
| | | Recording fees (180 + 40) | 220.00 |
| | | Buyer's loan costs | 4,200.00 |
| | | Seller's proceeds | 234,030.00 |
| **TOTAL IN** | **481,500.00** | **TOTAL OUT** | **481,500.00** |

**In equals out.** That third pair of columns is the proof, and it is the figure the brokerage's **journal**
must show.

## F. What the worksheet does NOT decide

**It does not decide who pays what.** The **contract** does. The worksheet records the contract's
allocation; it does not create one.

**It does not decide the proration convention.** The contract states it, and **this course prescribes
none** — a 360-day year with 30-day months and a 365-day year with actual days are both in use, and
**whether the day of closing is charged to the buyer or the seller is likewise the contract's.**

**It does not decide Colorado property tax timing.** **No Colorado property tax statute, assessment ratio,
mill levy, due date or billing cycle was read by this project**, and **none is stated anywhere in this
build.** Whether taxes in a given transaction are prepaid or in arrears, and for what period, comes from
**the tax records and the closing entity**, not from this course.

**And it does not disburse anything.** **Rule 6.19.E** — the Good Funds rule from Module 01 — prohibits a
broker or firm from **disbursing or authorizing disbursement until the funds have been received and are
available for immediate withdrawal.** A balanced worksheet is not money in the account.
