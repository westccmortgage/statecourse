# Establish the Colorado brokerage relationship before licensed work

Original StateCourse lesson and formative workshop, November 5, 2026. Development material; no approval or credit is claimed. Use with Contracts and Regulations module 02 and the corresponding relationship discussions in Law and Practice, Current Legal Issues and Practical Applications. Designed minutes are not observed course-hour evidence.

## The controlling change

HB26-1426 section 87 amended C.R.S. 12-10-403(2), effective August 12, 2026. Both a transaction-broker relationship and a single-agency relationship must be established through a written agreement with the party or parties to be represented. The agreement must specify and conspicuously disclose the amount or rate of compensation. Complete it before performing the activities specified in section 12-10-201(6)(a).

This replaces the former transaction-broker default. A broker cannot use the absence of a signed agency agreement as authority to proceed as a transaction-broker. A transaction-broker remains a nonagent under section 12-10-407(1); requiring a writing does not convert that role into agency, authorize dual agency, or erase the role's duties and confidentiality limits.

The August 12 DRE advisory specifically distinguishes a listing contract establishing the relationship from the Buyer's Broker's Compensation Agreement at the end of the Brokerage Disclosure to Buyer. The compensation document states compensation but does not establish the relationship or describe brokerage activities and duties. The advisory says it is insufficient for the new requirement and directs brokers to the applicable listing contract. Do not treat a consumer's acknowledgment of disclosure as a substitute for the contract that establishes the relationship.

## Read three documents in sequence

First read the amended section 12-10-403(2), not the superseded quotation in a January manual or older case annotation. Next identify the proposed activity in section 12-10-201(6)(a). Finally read the DRE advisory and the current applicable Commission agreement. The signed Act and advisory are retained under `colorado/regulatory/captures/2026-11-05/` with source URLs and hashes. The current forms themselves remain a separate acquisition and exercise-completion dependency; this workshop does not reproduce a proprietary contract or pretend a term sheet is an executed Commission form.

These documents answer different questions. The statute establishes the obligation and timing. The activity definition identifies what the broker proposes to do. The advisory explains the Division's view of adequate documents. The completed agreement records the parties' actual choices. A fee field alone answers only one of those questions.

## Guided agreement review

Create a review sheet with six columns: required fact; source; proposed term; location in the current agreement; unresolved issue; and disposition. Identify the correct party and designated broker; the relationship selected; the scope of the brokerage work; the term; the amount or rate of compensation shown conspicuously; and signatures and completion timing. Preserve the distinct designated-broker advisory and applicable statutory disclosures. Do not infer that a firm-wide agency relationship follows from designating one broker.

Compensation is negotiated. The statute's conspicuous-disclosure requirement is not a state commission schedule. Record the agreed amount or rate and the applicable payment conditions; do not replace them with an unsupported “standard” fee. A consumer's proposed limitation or uncertainty must be resolved through the appropriate agreement and professional review rather than hidden in an informal side conversation.

Before marking the agreement complete, compare its relationship selection and duties with every accompanying disclosure. A document calling the broker a single agent cannot coherently accompany another document describing the same work as transaction brokerage without resolving the discrepancy. Keep the agreed scope and transaction identity clear when the same consumer sells one property and buys another. Different relationships can apply to separate transactions only if each is properly established.

## Case 1: the unsigned “default”

A buyer and broker discuss a home search. The broker plans to negotiate an offer for the buyer and says, “You have not signed agency, so I can handle this automatically as a transaction-broker.” No relationship agreement has been completed.

**Student submission.** Identify the obsolete proposition, the proposed activity, the necessary writing, the compensation term and the correct sequence. Explain why the transaction-broker's nonagent status does not excuse the writing.

**Feedback after the attempt.** The old automatic-default rule no longer supplies authority. Establish the selected relationship by an appropriate written agreement and conspicuously disclose compensation before the specified licensed activity. Do not characterize the missing writing as an automatic transaction-broker election. A nonagent is still a broker subject to the amended obligation and the applicable duties.

## Case 2: a signed fee page

A prospective buyer signs the compensation agreement attached to a brokerage disclosure. The broker points to the fee and signature and says those two facts satisfy the new law. The page does not establish a relationship or describe brokerage duties.

**Student submission.** Compare this document with the DRE advisory. List what the fee page supplies and what it omits. Identify the current applicable relationship agreement needed before proceeding, and preserve the disclosure as its own document.

**Feedback after the attempt.** The advisory expressly rejects the compensation-only document as sufficient. Compensation disclosure and relationship establishment are connected requirements, but they are not the same act. Locate the applicable Commission listing contract and complete the relationship, scope, duties, compensation and other required terms. Do not destroy a previously delivered disclosure or falsely backdate a replacement agreement.

## Case 3: two properties and two roles

An owner engages a broker to sell a rental property and later asks the same broker to help buy a replacement property. The seller-side agreement states single agency. The broker proposes transaction brokerage for the separate purchase and says the sale agreement covers everything because the consumer is the same person.

**Student submission.** Identify the two transactions. Read section 12-10-403(3). For each transaction, record the party, role, scope, compensation and required agreement. Explain why a different role is not created merely by saying its name in a call.

**Feedback after the attempt.** The statute permits different relationships with the same party in separate transactions when established in compliance with Part 4. It does not turn one property's sale agreement into an unlimited engagement for another transaction. Establish the purchase relationship and its terms in the appropriate writing before the specified work. Keep duties and documents intelligible to the consumer in both files.

## Case 4: pressure to postpone

A commercial prospect says there is no need for paperwork until after a letter of intent is negotiated. The broker also proposes drafting a special legal clause to solve the prospect's unusual ownership dispute. The current course's older explanation says transaction brokerage is the default in commercial work.

**Student submission.** Identify the old-law statement, check the proposed activity against the statute, distinguish relationship paperwork from transaction documents, and identify the competency and legal-advice issues. Do not assume a commercial label creates an exception to the amended section.

**Feedback after the attempt.** The amended writing requirement is not limited by the former default explanation. Complete the required relationship agreement before the specified activity. A relationship agreement does not authorize the broker to draft legal solutions beyond permitted form use or competence. Address the dispute through competent legal assistance. Record unresolved issues and the broker's actual role; do not fill a legal gap with a promise of later paperwork.

## Case 5: a principal requires its own purchase contract

A home builder requires a represented buyer to sign the builder's purchase contract. The broker says the buyer need not obtain legal advice because the broker has already read the price and closing date. In another file, the brokerage proposes a contract with a unilateral cancellation provision in the broker's favor.

**Student submission.** Read the newly added section 12-10-408.5 in HB26-1426 section 89, signed Act page 59. Identify the required advice, its timing and the particular disclosure obligation for the broker-favoring cancellation provision. Distinguish advising the consumer to seek licensed legal advice from the broker giving that advice.

**Feedback after the attempt.** When the stated principal-created purchase-contract conditions apply, the broker must advise the represented consumer to seek legal advice from a licensed attorney before entering the purchase contract. The statute also requires clear and conspicuous disclosure of a unilateral cancellation clause favoring the broker in a broker contract. Reading a few economic terms is not a substitute for the required advice. Keep a factual record of the advice and disclosure; this exercise does not manufacture an attorney review or decide whether the clause is otherwise enforceable.

## Acceptance and retained evidence

For each case retain the initial response, exact statutory/advisory pinpoint, identified document, timing decision, correction and reviewer disposition. Critical errors require correction: restoring the automatic default, treating the fee page as sufficient, omitting conspicuous compensation, performing the specified licensed work before the required writing, creating prohibited dual agency, or inventing a current form.
