# Pennsylvania disclosure workshop

Version 1.1, November 5, 2026. Original StateCourse formative instruction. Not a licensing examination or an approved credit offering. This workshop supplements Practice modules 03 and 07; it is not a substitute for the official notices or independent course review. No observed duration is claimed.

## Materials and learning task

Use the retained official sections 35.284, 35.335a and 35.336–35.339 under `pennsylvania/regulatory/captures/2026-11-05/`. The original HTML retains the Commission's text and layout. Read the entire applicable notice, including its acknowledgment, rather than copying a relationship label from this workshop. The accompanying `regulatory/disclosure-source-appendix.md` places the prescribed notice text together for study. The official publication controls if the transcription or layout differs.

For each file below, produce: the consumer and property classification; the applicable authority; the required notice and its delivery event; the confidential-information risk; a completed fictional acknowledgment or documented refusal; and a short record of the next action. Distinguish notice delivery from an agreement creating an agency relationship. A signature acknowledging receipt does not by itself hire a broker.

The instructor reviews factual application, correct document choice, sequence, completeness and source pinpoints. All critical errors must be corrected before the exercise is accepted: promising confidentiality before establishing the relationship, using the owner-side lease notice for a tenant representative, inventing a seller's knowledge, concealing a known defect, or applying a future amendment as though it already operates.

## File 1: a telephone buyer interview

At 10 a.m. Monday, Morgan calls a licensee about buying a residence and begins to explain the maximum price the household can afford. The licensee and Morgan plan to meet Thursday. A colleague may show Morgan a house Wednesday. No business relationship has been established.

**Student work.** Stop the conversation at the correct point. Locate and read the complete section 35.339 oral disclosure. Identify the earlier of the two written-notice events in section 35.284(a)(2). Prepare the written Consumer Notice, leaving personal names and signatures fictional. Explain what changes if Wednesday's showing is cancelled but Thursday's meeting occurs.

**Feedback.** The initial interview requires the prescribed oral disclosure then, not at the later meeting. The written section 35.336 disclosure is due no later than the earlier of the first in-person meeting after the interview or the first showing by the licensee or someone working with the licensee. On the stated schedule the Wednesday showing is earlier; if it is cancelled and no earlier showing occurs, Thursday's meeting becomes the relevant event. A promise to email later does not replace the oral disclosure. The maximum-price information illustrates why the warning must precede sensitive discussion.

## File 2: two rental inquiries

For Apartment A, a licensee is acting under the landlord's property-management agreement. For Apartment B, a different licensee represents the prospective tenant in locating premises. Both prospects are individuals. Neither transaction has an applicable exception on the supplied facts.

**Student work.** Select the correct prescribed disclosure for each encounter. Complete the capacity portion for Apartment A using the actual authority described; do not check owner or direct employee merely because those boxes are available. For Apartment B, explain why the general Consumer Notice applies even though no purchase is involved.

**Feedback.** Section 35.284(b)(2) directs the owner-side licensee to section 35.337. Section 35.284(b)(1) directs the tenant-side licensee to section 35.336 under the timing provisions of subsection (a). A property-management agreement makes the first licensee an agent of the owner on these facts, not the owner. Selecting the document by transaction type alone misses the role distinction. Review the actual notice format and acknowledgment before treating a completed example as a deliverable.

## File 3: refusal and the transaction file

An individual seller receives the applicable Consumer Notice at an in-person initial interview but declines to sign the receipt. The licensee says a refusal means the office need not retain anything. The home later sells through that office.

**Student work.** Correct the licensee's record. Cite section 35.284(d), section 35.286(a), and section 35.336(b). Explain why the six-month acknowledgment language should not cause deletion of a consummated transaction file before the general three-year period has run.

**Feedback.** Note the refusal on the acknowledgment. Preserve evidence of delivery and give the consumer the required copy. Section 35.336(b) states six months for the signed/refused acknowledgment; section 35.286 separately requires transaction records for at least three years following consummation, with specified six-month categories. Retaining the acknowledgment with the completed sale's file for at least three years avoids treating a shorter acknowledgment rule as permission to destroy a required transaction record. This is the provider's conservative file-handling instruction, not an invented amendment to either rule. A legal hold or other applicable obligation may require longer preservation.

## File 4: a commercial entity

A corporation, rather than an individual, seeks to purchase commercial property. A staff member proposes omitting all duties because section 35.284(e) provides a consumer-disclosure exception.

**Student work.** Identify the two elements of the exception: the commercial-property classification under the Act and the consumer being other than an individual. Explain why the exception does not erase all licensee duties, contract requirements or required conflict disclosures. Rework the analysis if the same corporation buys a residential property, or an individual buys the commercial property.

**Feedback.** Section 35.284(e) is limited to the disclosures required by subsections (a) and (b) for the described commercial transactions with consumers who are not individuals. It is not a general exemption from RELRA. The alternate facts remove one element of the stated exception. Do not expand the exception by treating every investment as commercial or every person buying for a business purpose as a corporation.

## File 5: known water entry and an aging roof

The seller reports a 28-year-old roof with no known leak. A separate basement wall admits water during heavy rain. A contractor installed a sump pump last year, but water returned this spring. The seller proposes answering “no” to water problems because a contractor was paid.

**Student work.** Identify the roof, basement/crawl-space and structural questions in section 35.335a. Draft a factual seller narrative from the supplied knowledge, preserving dates and the unsuccessful repair. Separate the old roof's age from an actual known condition. Identify whether any fact about flood-zone status, insurance or the cause of the water entry remains unknown.

**Feedback.** Age alone is not the defined material defect. Known recurrent water entry is not erased by paying for a repair that failed. A useful response states the location, occurrences, repair and recurrence without inventing a warranty or diagnosis. The seller, not the licensee, supplies and signs the seller's knowledge. The licensee must not transmit a response the licensee actually knows conceals a material defect. Basement water entry does not, without more facts, prove every element of the newly enacted definition of a natural flood event.

## File 6: information changes before settlement

After a seller delivers a signed statement, a previously sound pipe bursts and damages a wall before final settlement. The seller says the statement was accurate when signed and therefore needs no action. The buyer has not been told of the event.

**Student work.** Read sections 7307 and 7312. Distinguish a pre-agreement written amendment from the obligation to notify the buyer when later events render information inaccurate before settlement. Prepare a dated factual update and a delivery record; do not silently overwrite the original statement.

**Feedback.** The original date does not excuse leaving subsequently inaccurate information uncorrected. Section 7307 requires notification of the inaccuracy before final settlement when the stated conditions apply. Section 7312 separately allows written amendment before the agreement of transfer is signed. Retain the sequence of original disclosure, new event, update and delivery. The exercise does not decide contractual remedies, which depend on the agreement and law beyond the supplied facts.

## File 7: applicability before form completion

An executor sells estate property. In a second file, a developer sells a never-occupied new home with a written warranty, an inspection and a certificate of occupancy. A salesperson says both are automatically exempt without examining the supporting facts. A third file is a condominium resale with both a unit leak and a common-roof concern.

**Student work.** Apply section 7302 to each file. For the new construction, list all three statutory conditions rather than choosing only the warranty. For the condominium, separate the seller's unit disclosure from common-element resale-certificate obligations identified by section 7302(b). Explain why a Chapter 73 exception does not authorize a licensee to conceal something the licensee must disclose under independent duties.

**Feedback.** The statutory exceptions have defined facts. An executor's fiduciary capacity must fit the provision. The new-construction exception requires all stated conditions, including no prior occupancy; a warranty alone is insufficient. The condominium provision limits the Chapter 73 obligation to the seller's unit and points to separate common-element law. Do not use “exempt” as a substitute for identifying the particular duty and authority.

## File 8: enacted law and its dates

A manager reads the consolidated statute on November 5, 2026 and sees the new flood provisions from Act 74 of 2026. The manager tells students that every seller already had to use the amended state form beginning October 22. Another manager says no flood-related fact matters until a new form appears.

**Student work.** Open Act 74 itself, including sections 4 and 5. Record enactment, the 60-day effective-date clause, the Commission's 18-month form-revision direction, and the posting condition in new section 7305.2. Separate these events. Identify existing section 35.335a questions about flood zones, drainage and flooding that already exist independently of the amendment.

**Feedback.** Both blanket statements fail. Enactment is not the same as the Act's effective date; a form-revision deadline is not proof that a revised form has been promulgated. The future insurance notice has its own website-posting condition. As of this workshop's November 5 review, the 60-day period has not elapsed. Existing known-defect and existing form questions remain relevant. Recheck the operative law, Commission form and Insurance Department posting before freezing a course for a later filing or teaching date. Do not infer a July 2028 universal compliance date from a news summary when the Act supplies the controlling mechanics.
