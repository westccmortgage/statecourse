# Pennsylvania Real Estate Practice — Module PA-P-04

## Written agreements and listing agreements — §§ 35.281, 35.331, 35.332

**Course:** Real Estate Practice · **Instructional hours: 5.0** of 45.0
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No Pennsylvania prelicensing credit is available.

## Learning objectives

1. State what § 35.281 requires to be in writing, and note that it is broader than the Statute of Frauds.
2. Recite the eight contents § 35.331(a) requires in every such agreement.
3. State the Real Estate Recovery Fund notification and what the Fund actually covers.
4. Distinguish the two forms of exclusive listing agreement.
5. Recite the six provisions § 35.332(c) prohibits in an exclusive listing agreement.
6. State the bold-face requirement for an exclusive right-to-sell or right-to-lease.
7. Explain why compensation is a negotiated term and what the rule requires be said about it.

## A. § 35.281 — what must be in writing

> **(a)** *"**All contracts, commitments and agreements** between a broker, or a licensee employed by the
> broker, and a **principal or a consumer who is required to pay a fee, commission or other valuable
> consideration shall be in writing** and contain the information specified in § 35.331."*

Three features of that sentence matter.

**"All contracts, commitments and agreements."** Not just listings. Not just formal agreements. A
**commitment** counts.

**The trigger is compensation.** The rule reaches any principal **or consumer** *"who is **required to pay** a
fee, commission or other valuable consideration."* If money may be owed to the broker, the arrangement is in
writing.

**It is broader than the Statute of Frauds.** A listing agreement or a buyer agency agreement is an
**employment agreement**, not a contract to convey an interest in land — so the Statute of Frauds may or may
not reach it. **§ 35.281 reaches it regardless, as a condition of the licence.** A licensee who asks
"does the Statute of Frauds require this in writing?" has asked a question that does not matter here.

*(§ 35.281(b)(3) separately requires a **written disclosure statement establishing a subagent or transaction
licensee relationship**, which § 35.286(a)(4) then requires be retained for **6 months**.)*

## B. § 35.331(a) — the eight required contents

Every written agreement caught by § 35.281 must contain all eight.

### B.1 (1) The Real Estate Recovery Fund notification

> *"Notification that a **Real Estate Recovery Fund** exists to reimburse a person who has **obtained a final
> civil judgment against a Commonwealth real estate licensee owing to fraud, misrepresentation or deceit in a
> real estate transaction** and who has been **unable to collect the judgment after exhausting legal and
> equitable remedies**. Details about the Fund may be obtained by calling the Commission at **(717)
> 783-3658**."*

**Every condition is a hurdle**, and a licensee should be able to explain them:

1. **A final civil judgment** — not a complaint, not a Commission disciplinary finding, not a settlement.
2. **Against a Pennsylvania real estate licensee.**
3. **Owing to fraud, misrepresentation or deceit** — not negligence, not a bad outcome.
4. **In a real estate transaction.**
5. **Unable to collect after exhausting legal and equitable remedies.**

**The Fund is not insurance and it is not a complaint remedy.** It is a payer of last resort standing behind
an uncollectable judgment. **This course states no payment limit, per-licensee cap or aggregate cap**,
because the Real Estate Licensing and Registration Act was not read for this project.

### B.2 (2) The escrow notification

> *"Notification that **payments of money received by the broker on account of a sale** — regardless of the
> form of payment and the person designated as payee — **shall be held by the broker in an escrow account
> pending consummation of the sale or a prior termination thereof**."*

Note *"regardless of the form of payment and the person designated as payee."* A cheque made out to someone
else does not escape the escrow duty. Module PA-P-06 develops this.

### B.3 (3) The negotiation notification — read it twice

> *"Notification that **the broker's commission AND the duration of the agreement have been determined as a
> result of negotiations** between the broker, or a licensee employed by the broker, and the seller/landlord
> or buyer/tenant."*

**Two things are declared negotiated, not one.** The **commission** and the **duration**.

Compensation in Pennsylvania is **not** set by the Commission, by any trade association, by any multiple
listing service, or by any course — and the rule requires the agreement to **say so on its face.** **No
commission rate, split, or minimum appears anywhere in this build**, and every practice problem supplies its
rate as a given inside the problem.

The duration half is equally deliberate, and it connects to § 35.332(c)(1) and (2) below: a listing period is
negotiated, capped at one year, and cannot renew itself.

### B.4 (4) through (6)

> **(4)** *"A **description of the services to be provided and the fees to be charged**."*
> **(5)** *"Notification about the **possibility that the broker or any licensee employed by the broker may
> provide services to more than one party in a single transaction**, and an explanation of **the duties owed
> to the other party** and **the fees which the broker may receive** for those services."*
> **(6)** *"Notification of the licensee's **continuing duty to disclose in a reasonably practicable period of
> time any conflict of interest**."*

**(5) is the dual agency warning, given at the front end.** Before anyone is in a transaction, the consumer is
told the firm may end up serving both sides, what the other party would be owed, and what the broker would be
paid. It is the same information § 35.314 and § 35.315 govern later, disclosed before the situation arises.

**(6) says the conflict duty is continuing.** It does not discharge at signing. § 35.283 governs disclosure of
interest, and § 35.292(a)(7) makes it one of the twelve unwaivable duties.

### B.5 (7) and (8) — the mirrored disclosures

> **(7)** *In an agreement between a **broker and a seller/landlord**: a statement regarding **cooperation with
> subagents and buyer agents**; a disclosure that **a buyer agent, even if compensated by the listing broker or
> seller/landlord, will represent the interests of the buyer/tenant**; and a disclosure of **any potential for
> the broker to act as a dual agent.***
>
> **(8)** *In an agreement between a **broker and a buyer/tenant**: an explanation that **the broker may be
> compensated based upon a percentage of the purchase price**; **the broker's policies regarding cooperation
> with listing brokers willing to pay buyer's brokers**; a disclosure that **the broker, even if compensated by
> the listing broker or seller/landlord, will represent the interests of the buyer/tenant**; and a disclosure of
> **any potential for the broker to act as a dual agent.***

**The same sentence appears on both sides**: a buyer agent represents the buyer **even if paid by the seller.**
The seller is told it in (7); the buyer is told it in (8). That is § 35.311(c) and § 35.313(c) written into
the paperwork so that neither party can later claim surprise.

**(8) also requires the buyer to be told something about the broker's own incentives** — that the fee may be a
**percentage of the purchase price**. A buyer's agent paid on percentage is paid more when the buyer pays
more, and Pennsylvania requires the buyer be told.

### B.6 § 35.331(b) — incorporation by reference

> *"To the extent that any of the information required in subsection (a) is set forth in the **disclosure
> summaries in §§ 35.336—35.338**, those provisions **need not be repeated, but may be incorporated by
> reference**."*

A drafting convenience, and it has a practical consequence: the consumer notice and the written agreement are
**designed to work together**, which is another reason the notice must actually be delivered.

## C. § 35.332 — Exclusive listing agreements

### C.1 The two forms

> **(a)** *"An exclusive listing agreement may comprise one of the following:*
> **(1)** *"**The exclusive agency of the broker.**"*
> **(2)** *"**The exclusive right-to-sell or exclusive right-to-lease.**"*

The difference is **whether the owner can sell it themselves and owe nothing.** Under an **exclusive agency**,
the broker is the only broker — but an owner who finds their own buyer may owe no commission. Under an
**exclusive right-to-sell**, the broker earns on a sale during the listing period **by whomever made**.

### C.2 What must be in it — § 35.332(b)

**In addition to everything § 35.331 requires**:

> **(1)** *"The **sale or lease price**."*
> **(2)** *"The **commission, fees or other compensation** expected on the sale or lease price."*
> **(3)** *"The **duration of the agreement**."*
> **(4)** *In an **exclusive right-to-sell** agreement, ***"a statement in bold face type that the broker earns
> a commission on the sale of the property during the listing period by whomever made, including the
> owner."****
> **(5)** *In an **exclusive right-to-lease** agreement, **"a statement in bold print"** to the same effect for
> a lease **"by whomever made, including the lessor."***

**The bold-face requirement exists because of what it says.** The single most common listing dispute is an
owner who sold to their own cousin and is astonished to owe a commission. Pennsylvania requires that
consequence be printed **in bold face**, in the agreement the owner signs, in the rule's own words: **"by
whomever made, including the owner."**

### C.3 What it may NOT contain — § 35.332(c), all six

> **(1)** ***"A listing period exceeding 1 year."***
> **(2)** ***"An automatic renewal clause."***
> **(3)** *"A **cancellation notice** to terminate the agreement at the end of the listing period set forth in
> the agreement."*
> **(4)** *"**Authority of the broker to execute a signed agreement of sale or lease for the owner** or
> lessor."*
> **(5)** *"**An option by the broker to purchase** the listed property."*
> **(6)** *"**Authority of the broker to confess judgment** against the owner or lessor for the Commission in
> the event of a sale or lease."*

Take them in pairs, because they group naturally.

**(1) and (2) — the listing must end, and it must end by itself.** Maximum **one year**, and **no automatic
renewal**. A listing that rolls over until cancelled is prohibited twice over.

**(3) removes the trap that would defeat (1) and (2).** An agreement cannot require the owner to give notice
in order to stop the listing **at its own stated end.** Without (3), a listing could technically expire while
practically continuing for anyone who forgot to send a letter.

**(4) and (6) — the broker does not get to act for the owner or against them.** No **authority to sign an
agreement of sale for the owner**; no **confession of judgment** for the commission. A confession of judgment
lets a creditor take judgment without the debtor's day in court, and Pennsylvania forecloses it in a listing
agreement outright.

**(5) — no option to purchase.** A broker holding an option to buy the property they are engaged to sell has
an interest directly opposed to the client's, and no amount of disclosure cures it in a listing agreement.

## D. Two practical sequences

### D.1 Taking a listing

1. **Consumer notice first** — the correct summary under § 35.284, signed, refusal noted if refused, copy
   given, acknowledgement filed.
2. **Confirm who is on title**, because the agreement must be signed by the owners — Module PA-F-04.
3. **Written agreement** under § 35.281, containing **all eight § 35.331 contents** plus the **three
   § 35.332(b) additions**, with the **bold-face by-whomever-made statement** if it is an exclusive
   right-to-sell.
4. **Check the six prohibitions in § 35.332(c)** before it is signed, not after.
5. **Duration and commission** stated as negotiated — § 35.331(a)(3).

### D.2 Taking on a buyer

Everything above, plus the § 35.331(a)(8) disclosures: **percentage compensation explained**, **cooperation
policy stated**, **representation notwithstanding who pays**, and **dual agency potential disclosed.**
