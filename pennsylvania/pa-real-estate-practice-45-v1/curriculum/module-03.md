2. State when the summary must be provided in an in-person initial interview.
3. State what must happen when the initial interview is not in person.
4. State what the licensee must do with the signed acknowledgement — and what to do when a consumer refuses

5. State the retention period for each acknowledgement, and why one of them is different.

## A. Which summary, and to whom — § 35.284

The rule allocates four summaries across four situations. Read the allocation carefully, because the lease
case splits and students routinely miss the split.

| The consumer is | The summary is | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Seeking to **purchase or sell** real estate | **§ 35.336** | § 35.284(a)(1) |
| Seeking to **lease**, and the licensee is working **on behalf of the tenant** | **§ 35.336** | § 35.284(b)(1) |
| Seeking to **lease**, and the licensee is working **on behalf of the owner** | **§ 35.337** | § 35.284(b)(2) |
| Seeking to purchase a **time-share estate** | **§ 35.338** | § 35.284(c) |

**The two lease rows are the point.** The same transaction type produces **two different summaries**
depending on **whose side the licensee is on.** A licensee who keeps one lease form in the drawer and uses
it for every rental will be handing the wrong document to half of them.

Note also the title of § 35.336 as the rule prints it — *"disclosure summary for the purchase or sale of
residential or commercial real estate **or for the lease of residential or commercial real estate when the
licensee is working on behalf of the tenant**."* The tenant-side lease case is folded into the sale
summary; the owner-side lease case gets its own.

## B. When — the initial interview

### B.1 In person

> **§ 35.284(a)(1):** *"a licensee shall provide the disclosure summary in § 35.336 to consumers seeking to
> purchase or sell real estate **at the initial interview if the interview occurs in person**."*

**At the initial interview.** Not before showing property, not when an offer is written, not when the
consumer asks. At the **first** substantive contact.

The reason is the whole purpose of the disclosure: a consumer is about to start telling a licensee things —
what they can afford, what they will take, why they are moving — and the consumer is entitled to know, before
they say any of it, **whose side the licensee is on.** A disclosure delivered after the consumer has already
talked has protected nobody.

### B.2 Not in person

> **§ 35.284(a)(2):** *"If the initial interview **does not occur in person**, the licensee shall provide the
> **oral disclosure in § 35.339 at the initial interview** and the **written disclosure statement in § 35.336
> no later than the earlier of**"* the points the rule then states.

**Two obligations, not one.** A telephone or online first contact does **not** postpone the disclosure — it
**converts** it into an oral disclosure **now** plus the written statement **shortly after**. The licensee who
says "I'll email you the form" has done half the rule.

**The text of the § 35.339 oral disclosure was not obtained by this project and appears nowhere in this
build.** Nor are the two alternative outer limits in § 35.284(a)(2) stated here as specific events, because
reproducing them accurately requires the section's own words, which this project did not transcribe.
**Delivering the oral disclosure in the Commission's words, and meeting the correct outer limit, is part of
this module's submission gate.**

## C. The acknowledgement — § 35.284(d)

> *"A licensee shall **provide a copy of the signed disclosure** to the consumers referenced in subsections
> (a)—(c) and **retain the signed acknowledgment** under § 35.286. **If a consumer refuses to sign the
> acknowledgment, the refusal shall be noted on the acknowledgment.**"*

Three obligations in one subsection, and the third is the one that saves licensees.

**Give the consumer a copy of the signed disclosure.**

**Retain the signed acknowledgement** under the retention rule below.

**If the consumer refuses to sign, note the refusal on the acknowledgement.** The rule does not treat a
refusal as a failure — it treats it as an **event to be recorded.** A consumer is entitled to decline to
sign; a licensee is not entitled to have no record of what happened. **The noted refusal is the licensee's
evidence that the disclosure was made**, and it is the difference between a documented compliance and a
disputed memory two years later.

---

## D. Retention — § 35.286, and the one that is only six months

> **§ 35.286(a):** *"A broker or cemetery broker shall **retain records pertaining to a real estate
> transaction for at least 3 years following consummation** — **except for the following which shall be
> retained for 6 months**:*
>
> **(1)** *"The **acknowledgement portion of the consumer notice** applicable to the sale or purchase of
> real estate **in § 35.336 ... when there is not a real estate transaction**."*
> **(2)** *"The acknowledgement portion of the consumer notice applicable to **time shares in § 35.338**."*
> **(3)** *"The acknowledgement portion of the consumer notice applicable to **leases in § 35.336 and
> § 35.337**."*
> **(4)** *"The **written disclosure statement establishing a subagent or transaction licensee relationship**
> required by § 35.281(b)(3)."*

### D.1 Read the qualifier in (1)

> *"... **when there is not a real estate transaction**."*

**That is the whole distinction.** If the consumer you disclosed to went on to buy or sell, the
acknowledgement is a **record pertaining to a real estate transaction** and is kept for **3 years following
consummation** under the general rule. If nothing came of it — the consumer walked into an open house,
signed the acknowledgement and was never heard from again — it is kept for **6 months.**

The rule is proportionate: records of transactions live with the transaction; records of contacts that never
became transactions do not need to be kept for years.

### D.2 The other three six-month categories

**Time-share** acknowledgements and **lease** acknowledgements are six months **without** the
"no-transaction" qualifier, and so is the **written disclosure statement establishing a subagent or
transaction licensee relationship** under § 35.281(b)(3).

**Everything else pertaining to a real estate transaction: three years following consummation.**

### D.3 Production — § 35.286(b)

The broker **shall produce** these records for examination by the Commission or its authorized
representatives **upon written request** or **pursuant to an office inspection under § 35.246** — and an
entity holding a broker's licence must produce its **corporate, partnership or association records** the
same way.

## E. Where this obligation comes from — § 35.292(a)(6)

Duty (6) of the twelve owed to **all consumers** and **not waivable**:

> *"**Provide consumers with the information in section 608 of the act at the initial interview.**"*

So the disclosure is not merely a form to hand over. It is one of the **twelve unwaivable duties**, and a
failure to make it is a failure of § 35.292 as much as of § 35.284.

**Section 608 of the Real Estate Licensing and Registration Act was not read for this project**, and this
module states none of its content. The citation is § 35.292's own, reproduced so a reader knows where to
go.

## F. What the disclosure does not do

Three things worth stating plainly, because consumers and new licensees both assume otherwise.

**It does not create a relationship.** Handing a consumer a disclosure summary makes nobody anybody's
client. **§ 35.281(a)** requires the agreement itself — *"all contracts, commitments and agreements ...
shall be in writing"* — and Module PA-P-04 covers what that agreement must contain.

**It does not fix the relationship for the transaction.** Relationships can change: designation may take
place **at any time** under § 35.315(b), and a licensee who moves from one relationship to another has
obligations under the rule governing the new one.

**It does not substitute for the § 35.292 duties.** Nothing on any form reduces the twelve. A consumer who
receives, reads and signs every disclosure Pennsylvania requires is still owed honesty, good faith,
presentation of all offers, and the duty to be told when a question is beyond the licensee's expertise.

---

## G. The practical sequence, in order

1. **First substantive contact.** Stop before the consumer starts telling you things.
2. **In person?** Provide the correct summary — § 35.336, § 35.337 or § 35.338 — and go through it.
3. **Not in person?** Make the § 35.339 **oral disclosure now**, and get the **written summary out** within
   the rule's outer limit.
4. **Get it signed.** If the consumer declines, **note the refusal on the acknowledgement.**
5. **Give the consumer a copy** of the signed disclosure.
6. **File the acknowledgement** — 3 years after consummation if a transaction followed, 6 months if not, and
   6 months for time-share and lease acknowledgements.
7. **Only then**, if a relationship is to be formed, put the **written agreement** in place under § 35.281 and
   § 35.331.
