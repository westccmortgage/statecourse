# Pennsylvania Real Estate Practice — Module PA-P-01

## Duties owed to every consumer — § 35.292

**Course:** Real Estate Practice — the second of the two courses **49 Pa. Code § 35.272(b)(2)** names by
title. · **Instructional hours: 5.0** of 45.0
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No Pennsylvania prelicensing credit is available.

> **§ 35.292 is the spine of Pennsylvania practice.** Every relationship section that follows —
> §§ 35.312 to 35.316 — opens with the same phrase: *"**In addition to the duties required in § 35.292**
> ..."* The relationship duties are **additions**. The twelve duties below are owed to **everyone**,
> **whatever the relationship**, and **cannot be waived.** A student who learns nothing else from this
> course should learn this section.

## Learning objectives

1. Recite the twelve duties § 35.292(a) owes to all consumers and state that they may not be waived.
2. Apply the presentation duty in § 35.292(a)(3) and identify its single narrow exception.
3. Apply § 35.292(a)(8) — the duty to route.
4. Apply the boundary between (a)(11) and (a)(12).
5. State the three limits in § 35.292(b), (c) and (d), and what they do not excuse.
6. Distinguish a duty owed to all consumers from a duty owed only to a principal.

## A. The twelve, transcribed

> **§ 35.292(a)** *"The following duties are **owed to all consumers of real estate services and may not
> be waived**:*
>
> **(1)** *"Exercise **reasonable professional skill and care**."*
> **(2)** *"**Deal honestly and in good faith.**"*
> **(3)** *"**Present, in a reasonably practicable period of time, all offers, counteroffers, notices and
> communications to and from the parties in writing**, unless the property is subject to an existing
> contract and the seller/landlord has agreed in a written waiver."*
> **(4)** *"Comply with **68 Pa.C.S. Chapter 73** (relating to Real Estate Seller Disclosure Law)."*
> **(5)** *"**Account for escrow and deposit funds** as required by section 604(a)(5) of the act and
> §§ 35.321—35.328."*
> **(6)** *"**Provide consumers with the information in section 608 of the act at the initial
> interview.**"*
> **(7)** *"**Disclose, in a reasonably practicable period of time, all conflicts of interest and
> financial interests** as required in § 35.283."*
> **(8)** *"**Advise the consumer to seek expert advice on matters about the transaction that are beyond
> the licensee's expertise.**"*
> **(9)** *"Ensure that all services are provided in a **reasonable, professional and competent
> manner**."*
> **(10)** *"**Keep the consumer informed about the transaction and the tasks to be completed.**"*
> **(11)** *"**Provide assistance with document preparation.**"*
> **(12)** *"**Advise the consumer about compliance with laws pertaining to real estate transactions
> without rendering legal advice.**"*

### A.1 Read the opening words first

**"Owed to all consumers of real estate services."** Not to clients. Not to principals. To **consumers** —
which reaches the buyer your seller-client is negotiating against, the tenant your landlord-client is
screening, and the person who walks into an open house and never signs anything.

**"And may not be waived."** No agreement, no signature, no disclosure form and no instruction from a
client removes any of the twelve. A licensee who is asked to set one aside has been asked to do something
the rule forecloses, and the answer does not depend on how the request is phrased.

---

## B. Duty (3) — the presentation duty, and its exception

> *"Present, in a **reasonably practicable period of time**, **all** offers, counteroffers, notices and
> communications to and from the parties **in writing** ..."*

Four things it reaches: **offers, counteroffers, notices and communications.** In **both directions** —
*"to and from the parties."* **In writing.** And **all** of them.

**"All" is the word that does the work.** A licensee does not filter offers by their own judgement of
merit. An offer the licensee thinks is insulting still goes to the seller. An offer from a buyer the
licensee doubts still goes. The seller decides what to do with it; the licensee decides nothing.

**The exception has two conditions and they operate together:**

1. **the property is subject to an existing contract**, **and**
2. **the seller/landlord has agreed in a written waiver.**

Either alone is not enough. A property under contract whose seller signed no waiver still gets every
offer presented.

**"Reasonably practicable" is not "when convenient."** It is measured against what the situation allows —
an offer with a short expiry demands faster handling than one open for a week.

---

## C. Duty (8) — the duty to route

> *"**Advise the consumer to seek expert advice on matters about the transaction that are beyond the
> licensee's expertise.**"*

This is a **duty**, not a courtesy. It is owed to everyone and cannot be waived. And it is the reason the
routing language throughout both of these courses is not evasion — it is the rule.

Three consequences worth stating.

**It is affirmative.** The licensee must **advise** the consumer to seek advice. Staying silent while a
consumer walks into a question outside your competence is not neutrality; it is a failure of (8).

**It defines competence honestly.** Nobody expects a licensee to know Pennsylvania's mine subsidence
programme, or whether a defeasible fee has been triggered, or what a title exception means. What the rule
expects is that you **know you do not know**, and say so.

**It pairs with (9).** *"Ensure that all services are provided in a reasonable, professional and competent
manner."* Duty (9) requires competence in what you do; duty (8) requires you to hand off what you cannot.
Together they say: **be good at your job, and know where your job ends.**

---

## D. Duties (11) and (12) — the narrowest line in the chapter

> **(11)** *"Provide assistance with document preparation."*
> **(12)** *"Advise the consumer about compliance with laws pertaining to real estate transactions
> **without rendering legal advice**."*

Most states draw the practice-of-law line by prohibition alone. **Pennsylvania draws it by stating the
affirmative duty first.** You are **required** to assist with document preparation. You are **required** to
advise about compliance with laws. And in the same sentence, you may not render legal advice.

**Inside the line — assistance and compliance advice:**

- Completing the blanks on a form.
- Telling a seller a property disclosure statement is required under the Seller Disclosure Law.
- Telling a buyer that an agreement of sale must contain the zoning classification, and that its omission
  makes the agreement voidable at the buyer's option.
- Telling either party that a written agreement must contain the § 35.331 notifications.
- Assembling the addenda a transaction needs.

**Outside the line — legal advice:**

- Composing a clause.
- Construing what a provision will mean if litigated.
- Advising whether a party has a claim, a defence, or a right to terminate.
- Telling a co-owner what their interest is worth in a partition.

**The test is not length.** It is whether you are **completing a form** or **inventing a term**, and
whether you are **stating what the law requires** or **predicting what a court would do.**

## E. The three limits — § 35.292(b), (c) and (d)

> **(b)** *"A licensee is **not required to conduct an independent inspection of the property**."*
> **(c)** *"A licensee is **not required to independently verify the accuracy or completeness of any
> representation made by the consumer** to a transaction **which the licensee reasonably believes to be
> accurate and reliable**."*
> **(d)** *"A licensee is **not liable for the acts of a consumer** unless the consumer is acting at the
> **express direction of the licensee** or as a result of a **representation by a licensee reasonably
> relied on by the consumer**."*

These are real protections and they are narrower than they first read.

**(b) does not excuse ignoring what you see.** You need not inspect. You may not conceal. A licensee who
notices a stain spreading across a ceiling has not conducted an inspection — they have acquired
information, and duty (2) governs what happens next.

**(c) turns on the words "reasonably believes."** The shelter protects a licensee who relied on a
consumer's statement they had reason to trust. It does **not** protect reliance on a statement that
contradicted what the licensee could see, or that the licensee had reason to doubt. A seller who says the
roof is five years old on a house with visible curling shingles has made a statement the licensee no
longer reasonably believes.

**(d)'s two exceptions are the interesting part.** A licensee **is** answerable for a consumer acting at
the licensee's **express direction**, and for a consumer acting on a **licensee's representation the
consumer reasonably relied on.** Telling a seller "don't mention the basement, nobody asks" is express
direction. Telling a buyer "the survey is fine, you don't need one" is a representation.

## F. Duties owed to all consumers versus duties owed to a principal

The structure of Chapter 35 is worth seeing whole, because the rest of this course is built on it.

| | Owed to whom |
|---|---|
| **The twelve § 35.292(a) duties** | **All consumers.** Not waivable. |
| **Loyalty** (§§ 35.312(a)(1), 35.313(a)(1), 35.315(e)(1)) | Only the **principal**. |
| **Confidentiality** (§§ 35.312(a)(2), 35.313(a)(2), 35.314(b)(3), 35.311(d)) | Only the **principal** — and with a stated **material defect** exception for a seller's agent and a dual agent. |
| **Continuous and good faith effort** to find a buyer or a property | Only the **principal**. |
| **Limited confidentiality** — three listed items (§ 35.316(2)) | The consumer a **transaction licensee** works with. |

**Loyalty is the dividing line.** A licensee owes honesty and good faith to everyone; a licensee owes
**loyalty** to the principal alone. That is what an agency relationship adds — and it is why the next
module, on the five permitted relationships, is the heaviest in this course.
