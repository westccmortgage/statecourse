# Pennsylvania Real Estate Practice — Module PA-P-02

## Permitted relationships — §§ 35.311 to 35.316

**Course:** Real Estate Practice · **Instructional hours: 5.0** of 45.0
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No Pennsylvania prelicensing credit is available.

> **Pennsylvania recognises FIVE named relationships, and one of them is not an agency at all.** They are
> creatures of rule and statute, not of common law, and each is defined by the duties it **adds** to
> § 35.292. Get the five straight and most of Pennsylvania practice follows.

## Learning objectives

1. Name the five permitted relationships and state what each adds to § 35.292.
2. State the two things a broker may not do with an agency relationship without written consent.
3. State the five exceptions to the confidentiality duty in § 35.311(d).
4. Distinguish the seller's agent's confidentiality duty from the buyer's agent's.
5. Explain why compensation does not determine representation.
6. Apply designated agency, including what it makes the employing broker.
7. State exactly what a transaction licensee may not disclose — and what that list does not include.

---

## A. § 35.311 — Generally

> **(a)** *"A licensee and a consumer may enter into the relationship specified in **sections 606.2—606.4
> and 606.6 of the act**."*
> **(b)** *"**A broker may not extend or delegate the broker's agency relationship without the written
> consent of the principal.**"*
> **(c)** *"**Compensation paid by a broker to another broker** who assists in the marketing and sale/lease
> of a consumer's property **does not create an agency relationship** between the consumer and that other
> broker."*
> **(d)** *A licensee in an agency relationship **may not knowingly, during or following the termination of
> an agency relationship, reveal or use confidential information of the principal**, except where one or
> more of the following apply:*
> **(1)** *"The principal **consented** to the disclosure."*
> **(2)** *"The information is disclosed to **another licensee or third party acting solely on behalf of
> the principal**."*
> **(3)** *"The information is **required to be disclosed under subpoena or court order**."*
> **(4)** *"The disclosure is **necessary to prevent the principal from committing a crime**."*
> **(5)** *"The information is used by the licensee to **defend in a legal proceeding against an accusation
> of wrongdoing**."*

### A.1 Three things to take from § 35.311

**Subsection (b) is why subagency needs paper.** A broker cannot hand the agency relationship to someone
else — cannot extend it, cannot delegate it — without the **principal's written consent.** § 35.312(c)
says the same thing from the other side for subagents specifically.

**Subsection (c) severs compensation from representation, and it is the most misunderstood sentence in the
chapter.** Paying a cooperating broker does not make that broker the payer's agent, or the payer's client's
agent. Money moves one way; representation is decided another way — by **agreement**, in writing. § 35.313(c)
says the converse plainly: *"**A buyer's agent represents the interests of the buyer/tenant even if paid by
the seller/landlord.**"*

**Subsection (d) makes confidentiality outlive the relationship.** *"During **or following the termination
of** an agency relationship."* A licensee who learned a seller's bottom line does not become free to mention
it when the listing expires.

**And its five exceptions are worth reading as a group**, because they show what the duty is for and what it
is not. Confidentiality protects a principal's bargaining position and private information. It does **not**
shield a **crime the principal is about to commit** — (4) — and it does not require a licensee to stay silent
while being **accused of wrongdoing** — (5). Neither exception is an invitation; both are narrow.

## B. § 35.312 — Seller's agent

> **(a)** *"In addition to the duties required in § 35.292, a seller's agent owes the additional duties
> of:*
> **(1)** *"**Loyalty** to the seller/landlord **by acting in the seller's/landlord's best interest**."*
> **(2)** *"**Confidentiality, except that a licensee has a duty to reveal known material defects about
> the property.**"*
> **(3)** *"Making a **continuous and good faith effort to find a buyer/tenant** for the property except
> when the property is subject to an existing agreement of sale/lease."*
> **(4)** *"**Disclosure to other parties in the transaction that the licensee has been engaged as a
> seller's agent.**"*

And four operating rules:

> **(b)** *"A licensee **does not breach a duty** to a seller/landlord by **showing alternative properties**
> to a prospective buyer/tenant **or listing competing properties**."*
> **(c)** *"A seller's agent **may compensate other brokers as subagents if the seller/landlord agrees in
> writing**. **Subagents have the same duties and obligations to the seller/landlord as the seller's
> agent.**"*
> **(d)** *"A seller's agent may also compensate **a buyer's agent and a transaction licensee who do not
> have the same duties and obligations** to the seller/landlord as the seller's agent."*
> **(e)** *"Upon entering into a written agreement with the seller/landlord, **each licensee employed by the
> broker will act as a seller's agent** unless a licensee has been named ... a designated agent."*

### B.1 The material defect exception — the most important seven words in the section

> *"**Confidentiality, except that a licensee has a duty to reveal known material defects about the
> property.**"*

**Loyalty to the seller does not buy the seller silence about a known material defect.** The exception is
written into the confidentiality duty itself, so there is no tension to resolve: confidentiality never
covered it.

A seller who instructs a licensee to keep quiet about a known defect has asked for something § 35.312(a)(2)
forecloses, and duty (2) of § 35.292 — deal honestly and in good faith — is owed to the buyer anyway.

### B.2 Subsection (c) and (d) side by side

Read together they say something precise about the Pennsylvania market:

- **A subagent has the same duties to the seller as the seller's agent** — which is why creating one takes
  the **seller's written agreement.**
- **A buyer's agent and a transaction licensee do not** — and a seller's agent may compensate them anyway.

**So a cheque from the listing broker tells you nothing about whose side anyone is on.** That is § 35.311(c)
made concrete.

### B.3 Subsection (e) — the firm-wide default

Sign a listing and **every licensee in the firm is a seller's agent** for that property, unless designated
agency has been used. A buyer who walks into that firm and starts talking is talking to the seller's agents.

## C. § 35.313 — Buyer's agent

> **(a)** *Additional duties: **loyalty** to the buyer/tenant; **confidentiality**; **a continuous and good
> faith effort to find a property**, except where the buyer is subject to an existing contract; and
> **disclosure to other parties that the licensee has been engaged as a buyer's agent.***
> **(b)** *"A licensee does not breach a duty to a buyer/tenant by **showing a property the buyer/tenant is
> interested in to other buyer/tenants**."*
> **(c)** ***"A buyer's agent represents the interests of the buyer/tenant even if paid by the
> seller/landlord."***
> **(d)** *Upon a written agreement with the buyer/tenant, **each licensee employed by the broker acts as a
> buyer's agent** unless designated.*

### C.1 A difference students miss

Compare § 35.312(a)(2) with § 35.313(a)(2):

| | Confidentiality duty as stated |
|---|---|
| **Seller's agent** | *"Confidentiality, **except that a licensee has a duty to reveal known material defects about the property**."* |
| **Buyer's agent** | *"**Confidentiality.**"* |
| **Dual agent** | *"Confidentiality, **except that a licensee is required to disclose known material defects about the property**."* |

**The material defect exception appears in the seller's agent and dual agent duties. It is not written into
the buyer's agent duty** — and the structural reason is plain: the defects in question are defects **about
the property**, and it is the seller's side that ordinarily knows them.

This is a difference in the **text of the rules**, stated here as such. A licensee facing a live question
about what they must reveal reads the rule that applies to their own relationship, and where anything turns
on it, § 35.292(a)(8) says what to do.

## D. § 35.314 — Dual agent

> **(a)** *"A licensee **may act as a dual agent if both parties consent in writing**."*
>
> **(b)** *Additional duties:*
> **(1)** *"**Taking no action that is adverse or detrimental to either party's interest in the
> transaction.**"*
> **(2)** *"Unless otherwise agreed in writing, making a **continuous and good faith effort** to find a
> buyer/tenant for the property **and** a property for the buyer/tenant, except where either is subject to
> an existing contract."*
> **(3)** *"**Confidentiality, except that a licensee is required to disclose known material defects about
> the property.**"*
>
> **(c)** and **(d)** — a dual agent does not breach a duty by **showing properties not owned by the
> seller** to the buyer, by **listing competing properties**, or by **showing the buyer's property of
> interest to other buyers.**

### D.1 What is missing from the dual agent's list, and why

**Loyalty does not appear.** § 35.312 and § 35.313 both name loyalty first; § 35.314 does not name it at
all. In its place stands **(b)(1)** — *"taking no action that is adverse or detrimental to either party's
interest."*

That substitution is the whole concept. **A dual agent cannot advocate for one side against the other**, so
the rule does not ask for loyalty it could not deliver. What it asks for is **neutrality with teeth**: not
merely refraining from favouring one side, but taking **no action adverse or detrimental to either.**

**Written consent from both parties is the entry condition**, and it is a condition, not a formality. A
dual agency without both written consents is not a permitted relationship.

## E. § 35.315 — Designated agent

> **(a)** *"A broker, **with the written consent of the principal**, may **designate one or more licensees to
> act exclusively as the agent of the seller/landlord**, and designate one or more licensees to act
> **exclusively** as the agent of the buyer/tenant **in the same transaction**."*
>
> **(b)** *"**Designation may take place at any time.**"* If it happens after an initial designation or after
> a written agreement, the broker shall **obtain the principal's written consent to the newly designated
> licensee** and, where applicable, **the principal's agreement to renounce any previous agency relationship
> with the other licensees employed by the broker.**
>
> **(c)** *"Regardless of when the designation takes place, the broker and the designated agents shall use
> **reasonable care to ensure that confidential information is not disclosed or used**."*
>
> **(d)** ***"The licensees employed by the broker who are not designated have no agency relationship with
> either party in the transaction."***
>
> **(e)** *Each designated agent owes, in addition to § 35.292:* **loyalty to their own principal**; a
> **continuous and good faith effort**; ***"Disclose to the principal prior to writing or presenting an offer
> to purchase that the other party to the transaction is represented by a designated agent also employed by
> the broker"***; and ***"Confirm that the broker is a dual agent in the transaction."***
>
> **(f)** *The employing broker, **as a dual agent**, additionally owes:* ***"Taking reasonable care to
> protect any confidential information that has been disclosed to the designated licensees"*** and
> ***"Taking responsibility to direct and supervise the business activities of the designated licensees
> while taking no action that is adverse or detrimental to either party's interest."***

### E.1 The structural fact that makes designated agency work

**When designation happens, the broker becomes a dual agent.** The rule says so twice — in (e)(4), where
each designated agent must *"confirm that the broker is a dual agent in the transaction,"* and in (f), which
imposes dual agency duties on the employing broker by name.

So designated agency is not an escape from dual agency. It is **dual agency at the broker level with
exclusive representation at the licensee level.** The broker takes on the neutrality duty; the two
designated licensees each keep loyalty to their own principal.

**And (d) is the clean-up rule that makes it coherent.** Everyone else in the firm has **no agency
relationship with either party.** Compare § 35.312(e) and § 35.313(d), where the whole firm takes the
relationship — designation switches that default off for the undesignated.

### E.2 The disclosure in (e)(3) has a deadline attached to an event

> *"Disclose to the principal **prior to writing or presenting an offer to purchase** that the other party
> to the transaction is represented by a designated agent also employed by the broker."*

**Before writing or presenting the offer** — not at settlement, not when asked. The principal is entitled to
know the shape of the transaction before they commit to a number.

## F. § 35.316 — Transaction licensee — **not an agent**

> *"In addition to the duties required in § 35.292, a transaction licensee shall **advise the consumer** that
> the licensee:*
>
> **(1)** ***"Is not acting as an agent or advocate for the consumer and should not be provided with
> confidential information."***
>
> **(2)** *"Owes the additional duty of **limited confidentiality** in that the following information may not
> be disclosed:*
> **(i)** *"The **seller/landlord will accept a price less than the asking/listing price**."*
> **(ii)** *"The **buyer/tenant will pay a price greater than the price submitted in a written offer**."*
> **(iii)** *"The **seller/landlord or buyer/tenant will agree to financing terms other than those
> offered**."*

### F.1 The fifth relationship, and the one students get wrong

A **transaction licensee provides services without being anyone's agent.** § 35.292's twelve duties still
apply in full — honesty, good faith, presentation of all offers, routing, competence, all of it. What is
absent is **loyalty**, **advocacy**, and general **confidentiality**.

**Note the affirmative duty in (1).** The transaction licensee must **advise the consumer** of the
arrangement — including, in the rule's own words, that the consumer *"should not be provided with
confidential information."* That is a warning the rule requires the licensee to give.

### F.2 "Limited confidentiality" is a list, not a standard

**Three items. That is the whole of it.** Price flexibility on the seller's side, price flexibility on the
buyer's side, and financing flexibility on either side.

**What is not on the list is not protected.** A consumer's motivation for moving, their timeline, their
personal circumstances, their other properties — none of those appear in § 35.316(2), which is precisely
why (1) tells the licensee to warn the consumer not to share confidential information in the first place.

## G. The five, in one table

| Relationship | Loyalty? | Confidentiality | Key condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Seller's agent** | **Yes**, to seller/landlord | Yes — **except known material defects about the property** | Written agreement; firm-wide default under (e) |
| **Buyer's agent** | **Yes**, to buyer/tenant | Yes | Written agreement; **represents the buyer even if paid by the seller** |
| **Dual agent** | **Not named** — replaced by *no action adverse or detrimental to either* | Yes — **except known material defects about the property** | **Both parties consent in writing** |
| **Designated agent** | **Yes**, each to their own principal | **Reasonable care that confidential information is not disclosed or used** | **Written consent of the principal**; the **broker becomes a dual agent**; undesignated licensees have **no** relationship |
| **Transaction licensee** | **No — not an agent or advocate** | **Limited** — three listed items only | Must **advise the consumer** of the arrangement |

**All five sit on top of § 35.292.** None of them subtracts anything from it.
