# Pennsylvania Real Estate Practice — Module PA-P-06

## Escrow — §§ 35.321 to 35.328

**Course:** Real Estate Practice · **Instructional hours: 5.0** of 45.0
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No Pennsylvania prelicensing credit is available.

> **Escrow is where licences are lost.** Every other rule in this course can be broken by carelessness;
> these can be broken by carelessness **and** they involve other people's money. Eight sections, and a
> licensee should be able to state each of them from memory.

## Learning objectives

1. State the escrow duty, who it falls on, and the one category of money it does not reach.
2. State that the duty cannot be waived, and by whom it cannot be waived.
3. Allocate the escrow duty in a cobrokerage transaction under the three § 35.323 cases.
4. State the deposit deadline and the single exception with both of its conditions.
5. State the four characteristics an escrow account must have.
6. State the commingling prohibition and the two permitted deposits of the broker's own money.
7. Apply § 35.327 when entitlement is disputed.
8. Recite the six escrow records § 35.328 requires.

---

## A. § 35.321 — the duty to deposit

> **(a)** *"a broker shall **deposit money that the broker receives belonging to another into an escrow
> account in a Federally or State-insured bank or depository** to be **held pending consummation of the
> transaction or a prior termination thereof that does not involve a dispute** between the parties, at which
> time the broker shall **pay over the full amount to the party entitled to receive it**. **If a broker is a
> partnership, association or corporation, its broker of record shall be responsible for ensuring that the
> escrow duty is performed.**"*

Four things in one subsection.

**The duty is the broker's**, and in an entity it is the **broker of record** who must ensure it happens. A
salesperson who receives a deposit is handling the broker's obligation.

**"Money ... belonging to another."** That is the test. Not "earnest money," not "deposits" — **money
belonging to somebody else.**

**Held pending consummation or a prior termination "that does not involve a dispute."** Where there **is** a
dispute, § 35.327 takes over and the money stays put.

**Pay over the FULL amount to the party entitled.** Not net of anything.

### A.1 The one carve-out — rents

> **(b)** *"A broker is **not required to hold in escrow rents** that he receives as a property manager for a
> lessor. A broker shall **deposit rents received into a rental management account that is separate from the
> broker's escrow and general business accounts**."*

**Rents are not escrow — but they are not the broker's money either.** They go into a **third** account, a
**rental management account**, separate from **both** the escrow account and the general business account.

**Three accounts, three purposes.** A broker who runs rents through the general business account has
commingled, whatever they call it.

### A.2 Security deposits — when the duty to pay over arises

> **(e)** *"If a broker receives a **security deposit** belonging to another under a lease agreement, the
> broker's **duty to pay over the deposit ... shall arise when the tenancy ends**."*

And on a mid-tenancy change of ownership or management, the broker **may transfer the security deposit** to
the escrow account of the lessor or the lessor's broker — but only **upon notification in writing to each
tenant** from whom a deposit was received, stating **the name and address of the banking institution** where
the deposits will be held and **the amount of the deposits.**

**A security deposit is not a rent.** It is money belonging to another, held in escrow, and it moves only with
written notice to the tenant.

### A.3 Two special consummation rules

> **(c)** Under an **installment land purchase agreement**, the transaction is consummated for escrow purposes
> *"when the buyer has been afforded the opportunity, by means of the **seller's written acknowledgement on or
> affixed to the agreement, to record the agreement**, unless the agreement specifies otherwise."*
>
> **(d)** Under an agreement of sale involving **cemetery property**, consummated *"when the **buyer receives a
> copy of the agreement of sale**."*

## B. § 35.322 — the duty cannot be waived

> *"A broker's escrow duty **may not be waived or altered by an agreement** between **the parties to the
> transaction**, between **the broker and the parties**, or between **the broker and other brokers** who may
> be involved in the transaction."*

**Three groupings, all of them closed.** Buyer and seller cannot agree it away between themselves. The broker
cannot get the parties to sign it away. Brokers cannot arrange it among themselves.

There is exactly **one** place in these sections where the parties' written permission changes anything, and
it is § 35.324(b)'s narrow timing exception — which delays a deposit, not the duty.

---

## C. § 35.323 — cobrokerage: who holds it

Three cases, and the difference between the second and third is **prior notice.**

| Case | Deposit tendered to | Who holds it |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)** | the **listing broker** | **The listing broker.** |
| **(b)** | the **selling broker**, buyer having **prior notice** it goes to the listing broker | **The listing broker.** |
| **(c)** | the **selling broker**, **without** that prior notice | **The selling broker.** |

### C.1 What "prior notice" requires — and it is not casual

Under **(b)** the selling broker *"shall require the buyer to **acknowledge in writing, prior to his signing
the agreement of sale**, that the prior notice contained the following information":*

> **(1)** *"The **name of the listing broker**."*
> **(2)** *"That the **selling broker's acceptance of the buyer's deposit is on behalf of the listing broker as
> subagent for the listing broker**."*
> **(3)** *"That the **listing broker is a licensed real estate broker who is required to hold the deposit in
> escrow**."*
> **(4)** *"That the **listing broker be designated as payee**, if the buyer's deposit is in the form of a
> check."*

**Four items, acknowledged in writing, before the buyer signs the agreement of sale.** Miss any of it and
subsection (c) applies: **the selling broker holds the money**, and holds the duty with it.

**Note item (4).** The cheque should be made payable to the **listing broker**. And note § 35.331(a)(2) and
§ 35.333(a)(8), which both say the escrow duty applies *"regardless of the form of payment and the person
designated as payee"* — so a cheque made out to the wrong party does not defeat the duty; it just makes a
mess.

## D. § 35.324 — the deadline

> **(a)** *"a broker shall deposit money belonging to another into an escrow account **by the end of the next
> business day following its receipt in the real estate office where the escrow records are maintained**."*

**Next business day.** And note the clock's starting point: **receipt in the office where the escrow records
are maintained** — not receipt by the salesperson in a car park.

### D.1 The single exception, with both conditions

> **(b)** *"If the money ... has been tendered to the broker **in the form of a check under an offer to
> purchase or lease** real estate, the broker **may, with the written permission of both the buyer and the
> seller or the lessee and the lessor**, refrain from depositing the money ... by the deadline in subsection
> (a) **pending the seller's or lessor's acceptance of the offer**. The broker shall **deposit the check into
> an escrow account within 1 business day of the seller's or lessor's acceptance of the offer**."*

**Three conditions together:**

1. The money is **a check** — not cash, not a wire.
2. Tendered **under an offer** to purchase or lease.
3. **Written permission of BOTH sides.**

**And the exception buys a delay, not a discharge.** Once the offer is accepted, the check goes in **within 1
business day.**

**This is the only place the parties' agreement affects the escrow duty at all** — which is exactly consistent
with § 35.322, because it alters **timing**, not the duty.

## E. § 35.325 — what the account must be

> **(a)** *"An escrow account shall:*
> **(1)** *"Be maintained in a **Federally- or State-insured bank or recognized depository**."*
> **(2)** *"**Designate the broker as trustee.**"*
> **(3)** *"**Provide for the withdrawal of funds without prior notice.**"*
> **(4)** *"**Be used exclusively for escrow purposes.**"*

**(3) is easily overlooked and it has a reason.** A certificate of deposit or a notice account, however
sensible as an investment, would delay a payout the broker may be obliged to make at once.

### E.1 Authority to move money — § 35.325(b)

A broker who is a **sole proprietor or broker of record** may give **an employee** written authority to
**deposit** into the account, and may give a **licensed** employee written authority to **withdraw** for
payments properly chargeable to the account.

**Read the asymmetry.** Any employee may be authorised to **put money in**. Only a **licensed** employee may
be authorised to **take money out** — and only in writing, and only for proper charges.

### E.2 Interest — § 35.325(c)

> *"If money is expected to be held in escrow for **more than 6 months**, the broker is **encouraged** to
> deposit the money into an **interest-bearing** escrow account. **Interest earned on an escrow account shall
> be held and disbursed, pro rata, in the same manner as the principal amount**, unless the parties direct
> otherwise by agreement. **A broker may not claim the interest earned on an escrow account**, unless the
> broker is a lessor as provided in section 511.2 of the Landlord and Tenant Act."*

**"Encouraged," not required** — the six-month interest-bearing provision is a recommendation. **But the
interest rule is mandatory:** interest follows the principal, **pro rata**, and **the broker may not claim
it.**

**Section 511.2 of the Landlord and Tenant Act was not read for this project**, and no rule about a lessor's
entitlement to escrow interest is stated in this build.

### E.3 § 35.325(d) — the authorisation letter

On written request, or under an office inspection under § 35.246, the broker shall provide the Commission
**a letter addressed to the bank authorising the release of records** pertaining to the account. The broker
cannot decline to let the Commission see the account.

## F. § 35.326 — commingling and misappropriation

> **(a)** *"a broker **may not commingle money that is required to be held in escrow — or interest earned on an
> escrow account — with business, personal or other funds**."*
> **(b)** *"A broker **may** deposit business or personal funds into an escrow account **to cover service
> charges assessed to the account by the bank** or **to maintain a minimum balance in the account as required
> by the regulations of the bank**."*
> **(c)** *"A broker **may not misappropriate** money that is required to be held in escrow — or interest
> earned — **for business, personal or other purposes**."*

**Commingling and misappropriation are two different violations.** **Commingling** is **mixing**;
**misappropriation** is **using**. A broker who parks escrow money in the business account for a week and
touches none of it has commingled without misappropriating — and has still violated (a).

**The two permitted deposits in (b) are exhaustive and tiny.** **Bank service charges** and a **required
minimum balance.** Nothing else the broker's own money may do inside that account.

**And note that (a) and (c) both reach the interest.** Interest earned on escrow is escrow money.

## G. § 35.327 — when entitlement is disputed

> *"If a dispute arises between the parties to a real estate transaction over entitlement to money that is
> being held in escrow by a broker, **the broker shall retain the money in escrow until the dispute is
> resolved**. If resolution of the dispute appears remote without legal action, the broker **may, following 30
> days' notice to the parties, petition the county court having jurisdiction in the matter to interplead the
> rival claimants**."*

### G.1 The rule in three sentences a licensee can actually use

**The broker does not decide who wins.** Not the seller because the seller is the client. Not the buyer
because the buyer is sympathetic. Not whoever complains loudest.

**The broker holds.** Until the dispute is resolved — by the parties' agreement, or by a court.

**If it will not resolve, the broker may go to court**, after **30 days' notice to the parties**, and ask the
court to make the claimants fight each other. **"May," not "shall"** — interpleader is an option, not an
obligation.

### G.2 The one place a deposit comes back without any of this

**§ 35.333(a)(6)**, from Module PA-P-05: where an agreement of sale omitted the required zoning classification
and the buyer voids it, *"deposits tendered by the buyer **shall be returned to the buyer without a
requirement of court action**."* That is a direction in the rule itself, not a broker's judgement call.

## H. § 35.328 — the six records

> *"A broker shall **keep records of monies received by him that are required to be held in escrow** and shall
> **produce the records for examination by the Commission** ... upon written request or pursuant to an office
> inspection under § 35.246. The records shall contain:*
>
> **(1)** *"The **name of the party from whom the broker received the money**."*
> **(2)** *"The **name of the party to whom the money belongs**."*
> **(3)** *"The **name of the party for whose account the money is deposited**."*
> **(4)** *"The **date the broker received the money**."*
> **(5)** *"The **date the broker deposited the money into the escrow account**."*
> **(6)** *"The **date the broker withdrew the money from the escrow account**."*

**Three names and three dates**, and they are chosen so that a reader can audit compliance from the record
alone.

**(1), (2) and (3) can all be different people** — the buyer hands it over, it belongs to the buyer until
consummation, and it is deposited for the account of the transaction. The rule wants all three because
assuming they are the same is how money ends up with the wrong person.

**(4) and (5) together prove the § 35.324 deadline was met.** Received Tuesday, deposited Wednesday: compliant
on the face of the record. Received Tuesday, deposited the following Monday: the record convicts.

**(6) proves the money left when it should have, and to nobody's account before then.**

**And this is separate from § 35.286's general retention rule** — three years following consummation for
records pertaining to a transaction.

## I. The eight sections in one sequence

1. **Receive** money belonging to another. *(§ 35.321)*
2. **Determine who holds it** if two brokers are involved — and whether the four-item written prior notice was
   given before the buyer signed. *(§ 35.323)*
3. **Deposit by the end of the next business day** — unless it is a check under an offer and **both** parties
   gave **written permission**, in which case within **1 business day of acceptance.** *(§ 35.324)*
4. **Into an account** that is insured, names the broker **as trustee**, allows withdrawal **without prior
   notice**, and is used **exclusively** for escrow. *(§ 35.325)*
5. **Do not mix and do not use** — except bank charges and a required minimum balance. *(§ 35.326)*
6. **Record** three names and three dates. *(§ 35.328)*
7. On consummation or an undisputed termination, **pay over the full amount to the party entitled.**
   *(§ 35.321)*
8. On a **dispute**, **hold** — and, if it will not resolve, **30 days' notice** and **interplead.**
   *(§ 35.327)*

**And none of it can be waived by anyone.** *(§ 35.322)*
