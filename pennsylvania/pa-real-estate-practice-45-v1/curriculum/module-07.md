## Learning objectives

1. State the two things § 35.284a requires a licensee to do.
2. State the seller's disclosure obligation in the words § 35.335a uses.
3. Apply the definition of a material defect — including the sentence about useful life.
4. Explain what the disclosure statement is not.
5. Connect the seller's obligation to the licensee's own duties under § 35.292 and § 35.312(a)(2).
6. Identify what a licensee does when a seller declines to disclose something the licensee knows.

## A. § 35.284a — what the licensee must do

> *"Disclosures required by the Real Estate Seller Disclosure Law"* — the licensee shall, among the
> obligations the section states:
>
> **(2)** *"**Provide sellers with a property disclosure statement that meets or exceeds the disclosures set
> forth in § 35.335a** (relating to seller property disclosure statement)."*

**"Meets or exceeds."** § 35.335a is a **floor**, not a ceiling. A form that asks more is compliant; a form
that asks less is not.

**And note whose obligation is whose.** The **seller** discloses. The **licensee** provides the seller with a
conforming statement. Those are two different acts by two different people, and confusing them is the source
of the most serious error in this area — a licensee filling in the seller's form.

**§ 35.292(a)(4)** puts the same obligation among the twelve duties owed to **all consumers** and **not
waivable**:

> *"**Comply with 68 Pa.C.S. Chapter 73** (relating to Real Estate Seller Disclosure Law)."*

So a failure here is a failure of § 35.292 as well as of § 35.284a.

## B. § 35.335a — the seller's obligation, in the rule's own words

The rule opens the disclosure statement with these paragraphs, transcribed:

> ***"A seller must disclose to a buyer all known material defects about property being sold that are not
> readily observable.*** *This disclosure statement is designed to **assist the seller in complying with
> disclosure requirements** and to **assist the buyer in evaluating the property being considered**."*
>
> *"This statement **discloses the seller's knowledge of the condition of the property as of the date signed
> by the seller** and is **not a substitute for any inspections or warranties that the buyer may wish to
> obtain**. This statement is **not a warranty of any kind by the seller** or **a warranty or representation
> by any listing real estate broker, any selling real estate broker or their agents**. **The buyer is
> encouraged to address concerns about the condition of the property that may not be included in this
> statement.** **This statement does not relieve the seller of the obligation to disclose a material defect
> that may not be addressed on this form.**"*

### B.1 The seller's obligation has three limits built into one sentence

> *"A seller must disclose to a buyer **all known material defects** about property being sold **that are not
> readily observable**."*

**Known.** The seller discloses what the seller knows. The Law does not, on this text, require the seller to
investigate.

**Material defects.** Not every imperfection — the defined term in B.2 below.

**Not readily observable.** A condition a buyer can plainly see is not the target of the rule. The purpose is
to surface what a buyer **cannot** see.

### B.2 The definition — and the last sentence, which is the examinable one

> ***"A material defect is a problem with the property or any portion of it that would have a significant
> adverse impact on the value of the residential real property or that involves an unreasonable risk to people
> on the land. The fact that a structural element, system or subsystem is near, at or beyond the end of its
> normal useful life is not by itself a material defect."***

**Two alternative branches, and either one suffices:**

1. A problem with **significant adverse impact on value**, **or**
2. A problem involving an **unreasonable risk to people on the land.**

A condition that endangers people qualifies **even if it costs little to fix.** A condition that guts the
value qualifies **even if nobody is at risk.**

**And then the sentence that decides most real questions:**

> *"**The fact that a structural element, system or subsystem is near, at or beyond the end of its normal
> useful life is not by itself a material defect.**"*

**A twenty-eight-year-old roof is old. Old is not, by itself, a defect.** A twenty-eight-year-old roof that
**leaks** is a defect. The Law distinguishes **age** from **condition**, and the words *"by itself"* are doing
the work — age is not disqualifying evidence either, it is simply not sufficient on its own.

This is the single most useful thing a licensee can explain to an anxious seller, and it takes one sentence:
**you are not being asked to certify that everything is new; you are being asked to disclose what you know is
wrong.**

## C. What the statement is not — four denials in one paragraph

The rule's own second paragraph disclaims four things, and a licensee should be able to state all four:

1. **Not a substitute for inspections or warranties the buyer may wish to obtain.**
2. **Not a warranty of any kind by the seller.**
3. **Not a warranty or representation by any listing broker, selling broker, or their agents.**
4. **Not a release** — *"does not relieve the seller of the obligation to disclose a material defect that may
   not be addressed on this form."*

### C.1 Denial 3 protects the licensee — and denial 4 removes the seller's escape

**Denial 3** is why a licensee must never adopt the seller's answers as their own. The statement is the
**seller's** knowledge as of the **date the seller signed it.** A licensee who says "the disclosure says the
basement is dry" has repeated the seller's statement; a licensee who says "the basement is dry" has made a
representation the rule expressly says the form is not.

**Denial 4 is the answer to the most common seller question:** *"It's not on the form — do I have to say
anything?"* **Yes.** The form is a **prompt**, not the boundary of the duty. A material defect the form does
not ask about is still a material defect the seller must disclose.

## D. Where the licensee's own duties come in

The seller's obligation and the licensee's are **separate**, and a licensee who conflates them will get the
answer wrong in the direction that produces liability.

| | The seller | The licensee |
|---|---|---|
| Source | **68 Pa.C.S. Chapter 73**, via **§ 35.335a** | **§ 35.292(a)** and the relationship rules |
| Scope | **Known material defects not readily observable** | **The twelve unwaivable duties**, plus relationship duties |
| Honesty | Must not misstate what they know | **§ 35.292(a)(2)** — *"deal honestly and in good faith"* — to **all consumers** |
| Confidentiality | — | **§ 35.312(a)(2)**: a seller's agent owes confidentiality ***"except that a licensee has a duty to reveal known material defects about the property"*** |

### D.1 The seller who instructs you to stay quiet

**§ 35.312(a)(2)** answers it before the question arises. The seller's agent's confidentiality duty is written
with the exception **inside it**:

> *"**Confidentiality, except that a licensee has a duty to reveal known material defects about the
> property.**"*

There is no conflict to resolve, because **confidentiality never covered it.** § 35.314(b)(3) states the same
exception for a **dual agent.**

And even if it did not, **§ 35.292(a)(2)** — deal honestly and in good faith — is owed to **all consumers**,
including the buyer on the other side, and **may not be waived.**

So the instruction is refused. Not negotiated, not documented and quietly ignored — **refused**, and the
reason given.

### D.2 What a licensee is not required to do

**§ 35.292(b):** *"A licensee is **not required to conduct an independent inspection of the property**."*

**§ 35.292(c):** *"A licensee is **not required to independently verify the accuracy or completeness of any
representation made by the consumer** ... **which the licensee reasonably believes to be accurate and
reliable**."*

**Both shelters have a limit and it is the same limit: what you actually know or reasonably believe.** (b)
excuses you from inspecting; it does not excuse you from what you saw. (c) excuses you from verifying; it does
not excuse reliance on a statement you had reason to doubt. A seller who writes "no water infiltration" on a
form, in a basement with a visible tide line and a running dehumidifier, has made a statement the licensee no
longer reasonably believes.

## E. What the licensee actually does, in order

1. **Provide the seller with a conforming statement** — one that **meets or exceeds** § 35.335a. § 35.284a(2).
2. **Explain the obligation**, in the rule's own terms: **known**, **material**, **not readily observable** —
   and that **age alone is not a defect.** That is compliance advice, which § 35.292(a)(12) requires; it is not
   legal advice.
3. **Do not complete it for the seller.** It is the seller's statement of the seller's knowledge, signed on a
   date.
4. **Do not adopt the seller's answers as your own.** Attribute them.
5. **Disclose what you know**, whatever the seller wrote — § 35.312(a)(2) and § 35.292(a)(2).
6. **Route the rest.** Where a condition needs a professional, § 35.292(a)(8) makes the referral a **duty**.
7. **Retain the records** — § 35.286: **3 years following consummation** for records pertaining to a
   transaction.

## F. Conditions a licensee routes rather than opines on

Module PA-F-03 introduced these; here is the practice consequence. **On every one of them, a licensee's job is
to identify the question and name who answers it:**

**Lead-based paint** in pre-1978 target housing — the federal disclosure obligation, whose regulation this
project did not read. **Radon** — a current test, not an old result, and not a licensee's reassurance.
**Asbestos, mould, underground storage tanks, wetlands, on-lot septic suitability, well water quality.**
**Mine subsidence**, in a Commonwealth with Pennsylvania's mining history — a question for the Commonwealth's
own programme and an insurer, and **this project read no Pennsylvania mining or subsidence statute or
programme document.**

**A licensee who opines on any of these has stepped outside § 35.292(a)(8) and (12) simultaneously** — giving
advice beyond their expertise, and giving it as though it settled a legal or technical question.

## F. Complete disclosure work from the actual source

Read every item in the retained section 35.335a, not only its introduction. Its subjects are seller expertise and occupancy; roof; basement/crawl spaces; pests and rot; structural condition; additions and remodeling; water and sewage; plumbing; heating/air conditioning; electrical equipment; included appliances; land and drainage; hazardous substances; association interests; storm-water facilities; and miscellaneous legal, title and other defect questions. The statutory list in section 7304 and the regulatory form organize some of these differently. A matching topic label is not a completed response: follow each actual question, explain required affirmative answers, preserve an honest unknown and never invent the seller's knowledge.

The form asks about repairs as well as current conditions. A repaired condition may still require explaining known history where the question asks it. Water and sewage questions distinguish public, community and private systems and ask about shared systems and known problems. The storm-water section separately asks location/condition and maintenance responsibility. Association interests can require additional resale documents; a checked box on this statement does not replace them.

Chapter 73 supplies the legal workflow. Under sections 7303 and 7305, deliver the signed and dated statement by a permitted method before the seller and buyer sign the agreement of transfer. Check applicability and exceptions under section 7302 before assuming every residential transfer follows the same path. For unavailable information, section 7306 permits the best information available; section 7308 does not require a specific investigation but prohibits knowingly or reason-to-know false, deceptive or misleading representations and omission of a known material defect. These are not permission to guess.

Section 7307 requires notifying the buyer when subsequent events before final settlement render disclosed information inaccurate. Section 7312 separately allows written amendment before the agreement is signed. Keep the original, the factual change, the written update and proof of delivery. Section 7310 does not immunize an agent with actual knowledge of an undisclosed material defect or a defect-related misrepresentation. Section 7313 preserves other disclosure obligations and requires a seller's agent to advise the seller and supply the form. A Chapter 73 exception does not erase separate licensee duties.

Complete workshop files 5–8 in [the disclosure workshop](../activities/disclosure-workshop.md). Submit a topic-by-topic source checklist, a fictional seller narrative that does not invent facts, an applicability analysis, an update record and an effective-date analysis. The model feedback follows each student task for review after an attempt. These are formative activities, not secure examination items.

## G. Enacted flood amendment: separate the events

Act 74 was approved October 22, 2026. Section 5 states a 60-day effective-date period. Section 4 directs the Commission to revise the property disclosure statement within 18 months of that section's effective date. The new section 7305.2 insurance notice is tied to the Insurance Department posting specified information under section 7305.1. Enactment, effective date, website posting and promulgation of the revised form are different events. None proves the others occurred.

As of November 5, 2026, teach the existing form and known-defect duties as current. Explain Act 74 as an upcoming amendment and recheck all implementation events before a later content freeze or filing. The amendment adds flood-risk indicators to the land item and defines them in section 7102; it does not authorize treating every basement leak as a natural flood event without applying the definition. Existing form questions already address flood-zone knowledge, drainage and flooding. The new-law change does not justify concealing those existing facts while waiting for a revised form.
