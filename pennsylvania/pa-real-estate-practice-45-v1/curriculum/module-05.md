# Pennsylvania Real Estate Practice — Module PA-P-05

## Agreements of sale and statements of estimated cost — §§ 35.333, 35.334

**Course:** Real Estate Practice · **Instructional hours: 5.0** of 45.0
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No Pennsylvania prelicensing credit is available.

## Learning objectives

1. Recite the eleven contents § 35.333(a) requires in an agreement of sale.
2. State the zoning-classification requirement, its exception, and the consequence of omitting it.
3. State the six additional contents required where the agreement is mortgage-contingent.
4. State the two provisions that must be printed in bold face if used.
5. State the time-share and campground cancellation statement and its formatting requirement.
6. Apply § 35.334 — when the estimate is due, to whom, and to what standard of accuracy.

## A. § 35.333(a) — the eleven required contents

An agreement of sale, other than for a cemetery lot, mausoleum or cremation space, **shall contain**:

> **(1)** *"The **date of the agreement**."*
> **(2)** *"The **names of the buyer and seller**."*
> **(3)** *"A **description of the property and the interest to be conveyed**."*
> **(4)** *"The **sale price**."*
> **(5)** *"The **dates for payment and conveyance**."*
> **(6)** The **zoning classification** — see Part B.
> **(7)** *"A statement **identifying the capacity in which the broker, or a licensee employed by the broker
> is involved in the transaction and whether services have been provided to another party in the
> transaction**."*
> **(8)** The **escrow provision** — *"payments of money received by the broker on account of the sale —
> regardless of the form of payment and the person designated as payee — shall be held by the broker in an
> escrow account pending consummation of the sale or a prior termination thereof."*
> **(9)** The **Real Estate Recovery Fund statement**, in the words the rule prints — see Part C.
> **(10)** *"A statement that **access to a public road may require issuance of a highway occupancy permit
> from the Department of Transportation**."*
> **(11)** For a **time share or campground membership**, the **cancellation statement** — see Part E.

**A cemetery agreement** must contain **(1) through (5) and (9)** only — § 35.333(d).

### A.1 Item (7) is the agency disclosure, restated in the contract

> *"A statement identifying **the capacity in which the broker ... is involved** ... and **whether services
> have been provided to another party** in the transaction."*

Whatever relationship was disclosed at the initial interview under § 35.284, and whatever agreement was
signed under § 35.281, the **agreement of sale itself must say what the licensee is.** Consumer notice,
written agreement, and contract all carry it. A licensee who is a **transaction licensee**, a **dual agent**,
or a **designated agent** says so here.

## B. § 35.333(a)(6) — the zoning classification, and the consequence Pennsylvania attaches to omitting it

> *"The **zoning classification of the property**, except if the property (or **each parcel thereof, if
> subdividable**) is **zoned solely or primarily to permit single-family dwellings**, together with a
> statement that **the failure of the agreement of sale to contain the zoning classification of the property
> shall render the agreement voidable at the option of the buyer** and, **if voided, deposits tendered by the
> buyer shall be returned to the buyer without a requirement of court action**."*

Four points, and every one is examinable.

**The exception is narrow.** It applies where the property is zoned **solely or primarily** for
**single-family dwellings** — and where a tract is **subdividable**, the test applies to **each parcel
thereof.** A duplex, a mixed-use building, a commercial parcel, farmland and a subdividable tract all need
the classification stated.

**The classification alone is not enough.** The rule requires the classification **"together with a
statement"** of the consequence. Two things go in, not one.

**The consequence runs one way.** The agreement is **voidable at the option of the buyer.** A seller cannot
use the omission to escape a deal they regret; only the buyer may void.

**The deposit comes back without litigation.** *"**Without a requirement of court action**."* This is a
genuine exception to the ordinary escrow-dispute machinery in § 35.327, where a broker holds until the
dispute resolves and may need to interplead. Where an agreement is voided under (a)(6), the rule itself
directs the return.

**This course states no zoning classification, district name, setback or use category**, because
Pennsylvania's Municipalities Planning Code and every local ordinance were **not read for this project.** The
classification comes from the **municipality**, and a licensee obtains it rather than guessing at it.

## C. § 35.333(a)(9) — the Recovery Fund statement, in the rule's own words

> *"A Real Estate Recovery Fund exists to reimburse any person who has obtained a final civil judgment
> against a Pennsylvania real estate licensee owing to fraud, misrepresentation, or deceit in a real estate
> transaction and who has been unable to collect the judgment after exhausting all legal and equitable
> remedies. For complete details about the Fund, call (717) 783-3658."*

**The rule prints this statement.** It also appears, in slightly different words, in **§ 35.331(a)(1)** for
written agreements between a broker and a consumer. A licensee meets it twice in every listed transaction.

**What it covers, and does not:** a **final civil judgment**, against a **licensee**, for **fraud,
misrepresentation or deceit**, in a **real estate transaction**, that the claimant **could not collect after
exhausting legal and equitable remedies.** Not negligence. Not a bad outcome. Not a Commission disciplinary
finding. **No payment limit is stated in this build**, because RELRA was not read.

## D. § 35.333(b) — the mortgage-contingent agreement

> *"An agreement of sale that is **conditioned upon the ability of the buyer to obtain a mortgage** shall
> contain:*
> **(1)** *"The **type of mortgage**."*
> **(2)** *"The **mortgage principal**."*
> **(3)** *"The **maximum interest rate** of the mortgage."*
> **(4)** *"The **minimum term** of the mortgage."*
> **(5)** *"The **deadline for the buyer to obtain the mortgage**."*
> **(6)** *"The **nature and extent of assistance that the broker will render to the buyer in obtaining the
> mortgage**."*

Six items, and notice which way three of them point.

**"Maximum interest rate" and "minimum term" define the financing the buyer must accept.** If a lender offers
a loan **within** those limits, the buyer has obtained the financing the contract described — the contingency
is satisfied and the buyer is bound. If the only available loan exceeds the maximum rate or falls short of
the minimum term, it is not the loan the contract described. **Leaving these blank, or filling them in
carelessly, is how a buyer ends up committed to financing they never intended to accept.**

**(5) is a deadline, and deadlines in a contract are the licensee's business to track.**

**(6) is unusual and worth noting:** the agreement must state **what the broker will actually do** to help the
buyer get the mortgage. Pennsylvania requires the broker's own role to be written down.

**This course states no interest rate, term, qualifying ratio or lender requirement.** Those come from the
lender, and Module PA-F-06 records that the federal lending regulations were not read for this project.

## E. § 35.333(a)(11) — the time-share and campground cancellation statement

> *"a statement regarding the purchaser's right of cancellation that is set forth **conspicuously in bold
> face type of at least 10 point size immediately above the signature line for the purchaser** and that is in
> substantially the following form:*
>
> *"**You, the purchaser, may cancel this purchase at any time prior to midnight of the fifth day following
> the date of this transaction.** If you desire to cancel, you are required to **notify the seller, in
> writing**, at (insert address). Such notice shall be given by **certified return receipt mail or by any
> other bona fide means of delivery which provides you with a receipt.** Such notice shall be effective **upon
> being postmarked** by the United States Postal Service or **upon deposit of the notice with any bona fide
> means of delivery which provides you with a receipt.**"*

Five requirements packed into one paragraph:

1. **Bold face type of at least 10 point size.**
2. **Immediately above the purchaser's signature line** — not elsewhere in the document.
3. **Midnight of the fifth day** following the date of the transaction.
4. **Written notice**, by **certified return receipt mail** or another **bona fide means of delivery providing
   a receipt.**
5. **Effective on postmark or deposit** — not on the seller's receipt of it.

Point 5 is the one that protects the purchaser: a cancellation posted on the fifth day is effective, whatever
happens to it afterwards.

## F. § 35.333(c) — the two provisions that must be bold if used

> *"The following terms **shall be printed in bold face if made part of an agreement of sale**:*
> **(1)** *"A provision **relieving the seller from responsibility for defects** involving the sale property,
> or a provision **requiring the buyer to execute a release to that effect at the time of settlement**, or a
> provision of **similar import**."*
> **(2)** *"A provision **reserving to the builder-seller the right to change, or depart from, the building
> specifications** for the sale property."*

Both are provisions a buyer would very much want to notice, and both are the kind a buyer would very easily
miss. Pennsylvania's answer is not to prohibit them but to require they be **conspicuous.**

**Note "or a provision of similar import" in (1).** The rule reaches the substance, not the label. A clause
that achieves the same effect by different words is caught.

**And note that (1) reaches a release signed later.** A provision requiring the buyer to sign a release **at
settlement** must be bold **in the agreement of sale** — so that the buyer knows at signing what they will be
asked to sign at the table.

## G. § 35.334 — Statements of estimated cost and return

> **(a)** *"**Before an agreement of sale is executed**, the brokers involved in the transaction shall provide
> **each party** with a **written estimate of reasonably foreseeable expenses** associated with the sale that
> the party may be expected to pay, including, **but not limited to**:*
> **(1)** *"The **broker's commission**."*
> **(2)** *"The **mortgage payments and financing costs**."*
> **(3)** *"**Taxes and assessments**."*
> **(4)** *"**Settlement expenses**."*
>
> **(b)** *"The estimates of costs required under subsection (a) shall be **as accurate as may be reasonably
> expected of a person having knowledge of, and experience in, real estate sales**."*

### G.1 Four features of this rule

**The timing is "before an agreement of sale is executed."** Not before settlement, when the numbers are
known and it is too late to reconsider. Before signing.

**"The brokers involved" — plural — and "each party."** Both sides' brokers, both parties. A buyer's agent
does not rely on the listing broker having done it.

**"Including, but not limited to."** The four items are a floor. A transaction with an assessment pending, a
transfer tax split, a homeowner association capital contribution or a fuel adjustment includes those too.

**The standard in (b) is competence, not effort.** *"As accurate as may be reasonably expected of a person
having **knowledge of, and experience in, real estate sales**."* Not best efforts, not good faith — the
accuracy a professional in this business ought to achieve.

### G.2 The rule prints an exemplary statement

**§ 35.334(c)** sets out a **Statement of Estimated Costs to Buyer at Settlement** as exemplary of the
requirement. Its line items are the map of a Pennsylvania closing:

**Purchase price · payment on account · balance at settlement.** Then estimated closing expenses — **title
search and insurance; conveyancing or preparation of papers; recording fees for the deed and the mortgage;
the mortgagee's charges — appraisal fee, credit report, origination and placement fees; mortgage service
charge; fire insurance.** Then miscellaneous charges — **local realty transfer tax** and **Pennsylvania realty
transfer tax.**

**Two transfer taxes, local and state.** A licensee estimating costs on a Pennsylvania sale computes both, or
computes neither correctly.

**This course states no realty transfer tax rate, state or local**, because Pennsylvania's transfer tax
statutes and every local ordinance were **not read for this project.** Every practice problem supplies both
rates as givens inside the problem.

### G.3 A worked estimate, all rates given

*Purchase price $315,000; deposit $10,000; state realty transfer tax 1 percent and local realty transfer tax
1 percent, split evenly between buyer and seller by the agreement; title search and insurance $2,150;
conveyancing $350; recording fees $195; mortgagee's charges $1,780; first-year fire insurance $1,020.*

Transfer tax total = 315,000 × 0.02 = **$6,300**; the buyer's half = **$3,150.**
Balance at settlement before expenses = 315,000 − 10,000 = **$305,000.**
Estimated closing expenses = 2,150 + 350 + 195 + 1,780 + 1,020 + 3,150 = **$8,645.**
**Estimated cash the buyer brings, before any mortgage proceeds, = $313,645.**

*(Rates are given inside the problem. Substitute the current statutory and local rates before delivery.)*
