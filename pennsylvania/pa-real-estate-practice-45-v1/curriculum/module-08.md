# Pennsylvania Real Estate Practice — Module PA-P-08

## Advertising, solicitation and prohibited practices — §§ 35.282, 35.283, 35.288, 35.301 to 35.308

**Course:** Real Estate Practice · **Instructional hours: 5.0** of 45.0
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No Pennsylvania prelicensing credit is available.

## Learning objectives

1. State the authority requirement before advertising any property.
2. Apply the harassment and panic-selling rules.
3. State the two disclosure obligations a licensee has when selling or leasing their own real estate.
4. State what must appear in an advertisement by a salesperson or associate broker, and in what size.
5. State the five items required in a prize, contest or gift advertisement.
6. State the three restrictions on claims about sales volume, market position and number of offices.
7. Apply § 35.283 and § 35.282 to a conflict of interest and to a misleading assurance.

---

## A. § 35.301 — you may not advertise property you were not authorised to advertise

> **(a)** *"A licensee **may not advertise the sale or lease of real estate, or otherwise solicit prospective
> buyers or tenants** for the real estate, **without the authority of the seller or lessor or of the agent of
> the seller or lessor**."*

**Authority first.** Not a phone call afterwards, not an assumption that the owner will be pleased. A licensee
who posts an expired listing, or a property they have heard is coming to market, has advertised without
authority.

**(b)** applies the same principle to a rental listing referral agent: no publishing a rental property where
*"the lessor or property manager **expressly states that the property is not to be included** in lists prepared
by rental listing referral agents."*

---

## B. § 35.302 — Harassment

> *"A licensee, **whether acting on behalf of a prospective buyer or not**, may not solicit — **by personal
> contact, telephone, mail or advertising** — the sale or other disposition of real estate **with such
> frequency as to amount to clear harassment of the owner** or other person who controls the sale or
> disposition."*

**Note "whether acting on behalf of a prospective buyer or not."** Having a real buyer with a real need is no
defence. The rule is about **frequency**, and it names **four channels**: personal contact, telephone, mail and
advertising.

Prospecting is lawful and normal. Prospecting the same owner until it amounts to **clear harassment** is not,
and a licensee who has been told to stop and has not stopped is on the wrong side of the line.

---

## C. § 35.303 — Panic selling

> **(a)** *"The Commission will regard an **attempt by a licensee to bring about panic selling in order to
> profit from it** as **bad faith** under section 604(a)(20) of the act. For purposes of this section, **'panic
> selling' is frequent efforts to sell residential real estate in a particular neighborhood because of fear of
> declining real estate values when the fear is not based on facts relating to the intrinsic value of the real
> estate itself**."*
> **(b)** *"**Proof of systematic solicitation of sales listings may be considered sufficient, but not
> conclusive, evidence** of an attempt to bring about panic selling."*

### C.1 Read the definition's last clause

> *"... when the fear is **not based on facts relating to the intrinsic value of the real estate itself**."*

That clause is what separates panic selling from ordinary market commentary. A licensee may tell an owner that
a nearby plant is closing and that comparable sales have softened — those are **facts relating to the intrinsic
value**. What the rule prohibits is generating fear from something else, and profiting from the listings that
fear produces.

**In practice the "something else" is usually who is moving into the neighbourhood**, which is why this rule
sits beside federal and state fair housing law. **This course states no provision of the federal Fair Housing
Act or of any Pennsylvania anti-discrimination statute**, because neither was read for this project; the rule
transcribed above is the Commission's own, and it is enough to answer the practice question.

**And subsection (b) tells a licensee what evidence looks like.** **Systematic solicitation of listings** in a
neighbourhood **may be sufficient** — not conclusive, but sufficient — proof of the attempt. A licensee whose
entire prospecting strategy is one street, at one moment, in one direction, has created the record.

## D. § 35.288 and § 35.304 — selling your own property

**Two separate obligations, at two different moments.**

> **§ 35.288(b):** *"A broker or salesperson who is selling or leasing his own real estate shall **disclose his
> licensed status to a prospective buyer or lessee before the buyer or lessee enters into an agreement of sale
> or lease**."*
>
> **§ 35.304:** *"A licensee who sells or leases his own real estate **shall disclose that he is a real estate
> licensee in advertisements for the property**. **This requirement does not apply if the property is listed
> with a real estate company.**"*

**§ 35.288(b) is disclosure to the person**, before they sign. **§ 35.304 is disclosure in the advertisement**,
to everyone who sees it.

**Only § 35.304 has the listed-with-a-company exception**, and the reason is structural: an advertisement placed
by a brokerage already carries the brokerage's name under § 35.305, so the reader knows a licensee is involved.
An advertisement placed by the owner privately carries nothing.

**§ 35.288(a)** states the general principle these sit under: *"A broker or salesperson who sells or leases his
own real estate **shall comply with the requirements of the act and this chapter**."* Selling your own house
does not put you outside the rules.

## E. § 35.305 — the business name and the equal-size rule

> **(a)** *"Brokerage companies ... shall advertise or otherwise hold themselves out to the public **only under
> the business name designated on their license**."*
> **(b)** *"Licensees who wish to use and advertise a **nickname** for their first names shall **include the
> nickname on their licensure applications or biennial renewal applications**."*
> **(c)** *"An advertisement by an **associate broker, salesperson**, cemetery associate broker or cemetery
> salesperson **shall contain the business name and telephone number of the employing broker**. **The names and
> telephone numbers shall be of equal size.**"*

### E.1 The equal-size rule is not decorative

**"The names and telephone numbers shall be of equal size."** A salesperson's name in 48 point over the
employing broker's in 8 point violates the rule even though both appear.

**Pennsylvania courts have enforced this closely.** The regulation's own annotations record cases where
advertisements carried the employing broker's name **and a telephone number the broker owned**, but where that
number **connected directly to the salesperson** and **directory assistance gave a different number for the
broker** — held not to satisfy the rule. The purpose the courts identified is worth carrying: the requirement
**advances employee supervision** and **prevents the public from mistakenly believing the salesperson is
self-employed.**

**So the test is functional, not cosmetic.** A number that reaches the salesperson is not the employing broker's
telephone number, however it was purchased.

**And (b) means a nickname is not a free choice.** "Bill" for William must be on the licensure or renewal
application before it goes in an advertisement.

## F. § 35.306 — prizes, contests, gifts and free lots

An advertisement employing **lotteries or contests**, or offering **prizes, certificates, gifts or free lots**,
shall contain **five** things:

> **(1)** *"A **description** of each prize, certificate, gift or lot offered."*
> **(2)** *"The **prerequisites for receiving** each ... offered."*
> **(3)** *"**Limitation on the number** of prizes ... offered."*
> **(4)** *"The **fair market value** of each ... **If the advertisement is in a print medium, the statement of
> fair market value shall be in the same size type as the description**."*
> **(5)** *"The **odds of winning or receiving** each ... **If the advertisement is in a print medium, the
> statement of odds shall be the same size type as the description, and shall appear immediately adjacent to
> the description**."*

**The rule defines fair market value itself:** *"the price or value that a prospective buyer would expect to pay,
or be charged for, if he were to acquire a **similar item of like quality and quantity in a retail outlet that
offers the item for sale to the general public**."*

**(b)** requires records: the **number and description** of each item distributed; **the name and address of each
person who received one**; and **the name and address of each person who responded but did not receive one.**

**(c)** names four things the Commission **will regard as deceptive conduct** under section 604(a)(18) of the
act: **failure to comply with (a) or (b)**; **failure to disclose the possibility that a particular item may not
be distributed**; **advertising the availability of an item when it is not available**; and **giving a misleading
description.**

**The two type-size requirements in (4) and (5) are the operative ones**, because the whole abuse the rule
addresses is a headline prize in large type with the value and the odds in small type below.

## G. § 35.307 — claims about volume, market and offices

> **(a)** *"An advertisement by a broker about **'sales volume' or 'production' shall refer only to closed
> transactions**. ... a **'closed transaction' is either a listing sold or a sale made after a fully executed
> deed is delivered**."*
> **(b)** *"An advertisement by a broker about his production or position in the **'market' shall identify the
> municipality that the market comprises**."*
> **(c)** *"An advertisement by a broker about the **number of offices** that he operates **shall refer only to
> those offices that have been issued branch office licenses by the Commission**."*

Three claims, three limits, and each closes a specific gap.

**(a) closes the pipeline gap.** Pending sales are not volume. The rule defines a closed transaction precisely:
**a listing sold, or a sale made after a fully executed deed is delivered.**

**(b) closes the moving-boundary gap.** "Number one in the region" means nothing until the region is named, and
the rule requires a **municipality** — a definite unit, not a marketing area the advertiser drew.

**(c) closes the office-count gap.** Only offices that hold **branch office licences from the Commission** may
be counted. A desk in a shared workspace is not an office for this purpose.

## H. Two general rules that catch what the specific ones miss

### H.1 § 35.282 — Misleading advice, assurances and representations

The section's **title** is the substance. A licensee's assurances are regulated, not merely their formal
advertisements. *"It'll appraise, don't worry."* *"That contingency protects you."* *"The township always
approves those."* Each is an **assurance**, and each is the kind of statement this section exists for.

Module PA-F-05 gave the test: **puffing** is opinion nobody would reasonably rely on; **misrepresentation** is a
false statement of fact; and the line moves toward misrepresentation as the statement becomes **more specific and
more verifiable.**

### H.2 § 35.283 — Disclosure of interest

Read with **§ 35.292(a)(7)**, one of the twelve unwaivable duties:

> *"**Disclose, in a reasonably practicable period of time, all conflicts of interest and financial interests**
> as required in § 35.283."*

And with **§ 35.331(a)(6)**, which requires every written agreement to carry *"notification of the licensee's
**continuing duty** to disclose in a reasonably practicable period of time **any conflict of interest**."*

**Continuing.** The duty does not discharge at signing. An interest that arises in week six is disclosed in week
six.

### H.3 § 35.308 — relationships with education providers

A real estate company, franchise or network **may** promote or advertise its association with a real estate
education provider or an accredited college — but *"**an association, affiliation or connection which includes an
ownership interest shall be disclosed in all promotions, endorsements or advertisements**."*

The rule then defines ownership interest broadly: *"proprietary or beneficial interests through which the real
estate company ... **earns or has the potential to earn income, or which produces a direct or indirect economic
benefit**."*

**"Potential to earn"** and **"indirect economic benefit"** are wide words, and they are chosen so that a
recommendation that looks disinterested cannot quietly be a revenue stream.

## I. The advertising checklist

Before anything goes out:

1. **Do I have authority** to advertise this property? *(§ 35.301)*
2. **Does it carry the employing broker's business name and telephone number, in equal size?** *(§ 35.305(c))*
3. **Is the company name the one on the licence?** Is any nickname on file? *(§ 35.305(a), (b))*
4. **If I own it** — does the advertisement say I am a licensee? *(§ 35.304)*
5. **If it makes a volume, market or office claim** — closed transactions only, municipality named, licensed
   branch offices only? *(§ 35.307)*
6. **If it offers a prize or gift** — description, prerequisites, limits, **fair market value in the same size
   type**, and **odds in the same size type immediately adjacent**? *(§ 35.306)*
7. **Does it contain any assurance I could not stand behind?** *(§ 35.282)*
8. **Does it disclose any interest I hold?** *(§ 35.283, § 35.292(a)(7))*
9. **If it advertises credit terms** — Module PA-F-06's note applies: **Regulation Z was not read for this
   project**, and the safest practice is to publish **no numbers other than the price.** For anything more, ask
   the lender.
