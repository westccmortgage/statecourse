# Pennsylvania Real Estate Fundamentals — Module PA-F-01

## Pennsylvania licensure and the regulatory framework

**Course:** Real Estate Fundamentals — one of the two courses **49 Pa. Code § 35.272(b)(2)** names by
title for the salesperson's education requirement.
**Instructional hours:** **5.0** of the course's 30.0
**Credits:** this module sits within the course's **2 credits**, at the rate § 35.358(b)(1) fixes —
*"one credit for every 15 clock hours of instruction."*
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No Pennsylvania prelicensing credit is available.

> ### Three things about this course, stated before anything else
>
> **1. The regulation's minimum is 60 hours, not 75.** **§ 35.272(a)(2)** requires an examination
> candidate to have *"successfully completed **four credits, or 60 hours of instruction**, in basic real
> estate courses."* **§ 35.201** defines a credit: *"**A period of 15 hours of instruction.**"* Four
> credits is sixty hours. That is the floor the Commission set.
>
> **2. This course and its companion together run 75 hours, which exceeds that floor.** *Real Estate
> Fundamentals* here is **30 hours / 2 credits**; *Real Estate Practice* is **45 hours / 3 credits**;
> together **5 credits / 75 hours.** **The regulation names both courses by title and assigns hours to
> neither.** The 30/45 split is a **StateCourse decision requiring Commission confirmation**, not a
> regulatory requirement, and it is recorded as such in
> `pennsylvania/regulatory/primary-text-readings.md`.
>
> **3. This course has not yet been conformed to the Commission's own outline.** **§ 35.358(a)(2)**
> provides: *"**Instruction in a Commission required course shall conform to the content or the outline
> developed by the Commission for the course.**"* **This project has NOT obtained the Commission's
> content outline for either named course.** Everything here is written from **49 Pa. Code Chapter 35
> itself**, read on 2026-08-20. **Conforming it to the Commission's outline is a submission gate.**

## Learning objectives

1. State the education, age and timing requirements for the salesperson's examination.
2. Explain what a *credit* is and how a course's hours convert to credits.
3. State the standards a real estate education provider must observe in delivering this course.
4. Identify the licence categories 49 Pa. Code Chapter 35 recognises.
5. State the reporting obligations a licensee carries to the Commission.
6. State what the Real Estate Recovery Fund is, and what it is not.
7. Distinguish a **standard** licence from a **reciprocal** licence.

## A. What the Commission requires before you may sit the examination

### A.1 § 35.272(a) — the three conditions

> **(1)** *"Be **18 years of age or older**."*
> **(2)** *"Have successfully completed **four credits, or 60 hours of instruction, in basic real estate
> courses** as determined by the Commission under subsection (b)."*
> **(3)** *"Submit a completed examination application to the Commission or its designee with the
> examination fee."*

**This course states no examination fee**, because the fee schedule in § 35.203 was read only as a list
of headings and fees change; the current fee comes from the Commission.

### A.2 § 35.272(b) — how the education requirement is satisfied

**Three degrees deem it satisfied outright**, with no showing of coursework in specific areas:

- **a bachelor's degree with a major in real estate** from an accredited college as § 35.201 defines it;
- **a bachelor's degree** from such a college **having completed coursework equivalent to a major in real
  estate**;
- **a juris doctor degree from an accredited law school.**

Otherwise, **credits will be allowed for *Real Estate Fundamentals* and *Real Estate Practice* and all
acceptable basic real estate courses** when offered by:

- **an accredited college** as defined in § 35.201;
- **a real estate education provider who has met the approval requirements in § 35.341**; or
- **a real estate education provider outside Pennsylvania approved by the licensing authority of its own
  jurisdiction** — and in that case *"**the course transcript or certificate of completion must state
  that the course is approved by the licensing authority of the jurisdiction where the real estate
  education provider is located**."*

### A.3 The ten-year clock — § 35.272(b)(3)

> ***"Courses shall have been completed within 10 years prior to the date of successful completion of
> the licensing examination."***

Read the anchor carefully, because it is unusual. The ten years does **not** run to the application, and
does **not** run to the day the licence issues. **It runs to the date the candidate successfully
completes the licensing examination.** A candidate who took this course eleven years ago and passes the
examination today has education the rule does not count — even if everything else is in order.

### A.4 Reciprocal conversion — § 35.272(c)

> *"A licensee who is **converting a reciprocal license to a standard salesperson's license is exempt
> from the requirements in subsections (a) and (b)** and is **only required to pass the state portion of
> the examination**."*

**Standard** and **reciprocal** are the two licence types § 35.201 distinguishes: a **standard licence**
is *"a license, other than a reciprocal license, issued to an individual or entity who has fulfilled the
education/experience and examination requirements of the act."*

**This course states no Pennsylvania licensing examination passing score, item count or time limit**,
because **no Commission examination content outline or candidate handbook was obtained by this
project.** Those figures come from the Commission and its examination vendor.

## B. What the provider delivering this course must do

You are entitled to know the standards your provider is held to, because several of them determine
whether your hours count.

### B.1 The clock hour — § 35.358(a)(1)

> *"Instructor-led learning **may not exceed 7 1/2 clock hours of instruction per day**. For purposes of
> this section, a **clock hour is defined as a 60-minute period comprising 50 minutes of instruction and
> a 10-minute break**. **A student may not be required to attend class for more than 90 consecutive
> minutes without a break.**"*

Three separate limits, and they are limits on the **provider**, not on the student. A Pennsylvania clock
hour is **fifty minutes of instruction inside a sixty-minute period** — so this 30-hour course is
**1,500 minutes of instruction** within 1,800 minutes of clock time.

### B.2 Content — § 35.358(a)(2)

> *"The substantive content of the course, as evidenced by the **course outline, text and other
> instructional materials**, shall adequately reflect the **stated purpose of the course**, as evidenced
> by the **course title and course description**. **Instruction in a Commission required course shall
> conform to the content or the outline developed by the Commission for the course.**"*

This is the sentence behind the submission gate at the head of this module. The Commission develops
outlines; instruction **shall conform** to them; **this project does not have the outline.**

### B.3 Attendance — § 35.358(a)(3)

> *"**Unless the course is taught by means of distance education**, a student shall be **physically
> present during at least 80% of the classroom instruction for a prelicensure course** ... **to receive
> credit**. **The real estate education provider shall be responsible for verifying student
> attendance.**"*

Note the carve-out for distance education, and note what the regulation does **not** then say: the text
read for this project **states no equivalent attendance standard for a distance course.** This build
therefore **asserts none**, and treats the attendance condition as a value the provider sets under
whatever standard the Commission approves.

### B.4 Distance delivery — § 35.358(a)(4)

> *"**Courses delivered by distance education**, in addition to meeting the content requirements in
> § 35.384, **must have the delivery method approved by the Association of Real Estate License Law
> Officials or another certifying body with similar approval standards approved by the Commission.**"*

**No such approval is held for this build.** That is the second submission gate, and it is not a
formality — it is a precondition to the course counting at all.

### B.5 Grading — § 35.358(b)(2)

> ***"A prelicensure course must be graded by proctored examination"*** — except where a student's
> handicap or disability would make grading by examination impractical.

**Proctored.** The in-module checks you will meet throughout this course are **not** that examination.
They exist to keep you honest with yourself as you go; the graded event is separate and is watched.

### B.6 Your transcript — §§ 35.359 and 35.360

> § 35.359(a): *"**Within 30 days after a course has been taught**, a real estate education provider
> shall provide each student in the course with an **official course transcript** ... **signed by the
> director**."*

The transcript must carry — among other things — the provider's **Commission approval number**, the
**date you completed the course**, the **number of hours of the course**, your **final grade**, and *"the
fact that the course **will be accepted by the Commission** towards fulfillment of the education
requirement."*

**Keep it.** § 35.272(b)(3)'s ten-year clock is measured against a date that appears on that document.

> § 35.360(e): *"Continuing education records must be maintained for **4 years**. **All other records
> must be retained for 10 years.**"*

Ten years for prelicensure records, which matches the ten-year education window exactly. That is not a
coincidence.

### B.7 Your enrolment agreement — § 35.357

Every student must enter into one, and it must **itemise the tuition and other fees**, state the
provider's **refund policy** on withdrawal, dismissal or the provider's termination of operations, and
**contain the Bureau's toll-free telephone number, (800) 822-2113**, which you may call *"to obtain
information about **filing a complaint against the real estate education provider**."*

That number is in the rule so that a student who is being mistreated by a school has somewhere to go
that is not the school.

## C. The licence categories Chapter 35 recognises

The chapter's licensure subchapter addresses, among others: **broker**; **associate broker**;
**salesperson**; **cemetery broker**; **cemetery salesperson**; **builder-owner salesperson**; **rental
listing referral agent**; **campground membership salesperson**; and **time-share salesperson.**

Three of these carry education requirements the regulation states in its own numbers, and they are worth
knowing because they show what a *credit* buys:

- **Campground Membership Sales** — *"the **one-credit (15 hours)**, Commission-developed course."*
- **Time Share Sales** — *"the **two-credit (30 hours)**, Commission-developed course."*
- **Salesperson, cemetery broker and rental listing referral agent** — **four credits, or 60 hours**, in
  basic real estate courses.

Note the phrase that recurs: **Commission-developed course.** The Commission writes courses itself. That
is the same fact § 35.358(a)(2) states from the provider's side.

### C.1 Where a salesperson must practise — § 35.243 area

> *"An **associate broker, salesperson**, cemetery salesperson, campground membership salesperson or
> time-share salesperson **shall practise in affiliation with a broker** holding either a reciprocal or
> standard license issued by the Commission."*

A salesperson's licence does not stand on its own. It operates **through** an employing broker, and that
relationship shapes almost everything in the companion course — escrow, advertising, agency, and
supervision all run through the broker.

## D. What you must tell the Commission — § 35.290

> **(a)** *"A licensee shall notify the Commission of being **convicted of, or pleading guilty or nolo
> contendere to, a felony or misdemeanor, within 30 days of the verdict or plea**."*
> **(b)** *"A licensee shall notify the Commission of **disciplinary action taken against him by the real
> estate licensing authority of another jurisdiction within 30 days of receiving notice** of the
> disciplinary action."*

Two obligations, both **30 days**, both self-reporting. Note the trigger in (a): **the verdict or the
plea**, not the sentence and not the exhaustion of appeals. And note that a **nolo contendere** plea
counts — a licensee who pleads no contest precisely to avoid admitting anything has still triggered the
reporting duty.

### D.1 § 35.291 — Posting of suspension notice

A **broker** whose licence is suspended **shall return the licence to the Commission** and **post a
notice of the Commission's action**, provided by the Commission, **prominently on or near the public
entrance to each office**, main and branch. *"Failure to post the notice constitutes grounds for further
disciplinary action."*

## E. The Real Estate Recovery Fund — what it is, and what it is not

The Fund is described in the words **§ 35.331(a)(1)** requires to appear in every written agreement
between a broker and a consumer who may owe compensation:

> *"Notification that a **Real Estate Recovery Fund** exists to **reimburse a person who has obtained a
> final civil judgment against a Commonwealth real estate licensee owing to fraud, misrepresentation or
> deceit in a real estate transaction and who has been unable to collect the judgment after exhausting
> legal and equitable remedies**. Details about the Fund may be obtained by calling the Commission at
> **(717) 783-3658**."*

Read the conditions in order, because every one of them is a hurdle:

1. **A final civil judgment** — not a complaint, not a Commission disciplinary finding, not a settlement.
2. **Against a Pennsylvania real estate licensee.**
3. **Owing to fraud, misrepresentation or deceit** — not to negligence, not to a bad outcome.
4. **In a real estate transaction.**
5. **Unable to collect after exhausting legal and equitable remedies.**

**The Fund is not insurance and it is not a complaint remedy.** It is a payer of last resort behind an
uncollectable judgment.

**This course states no Recovery Fund payment limit, per-licensee cap or aggregate cap**, because those
are in the **Real Estate Licensing and Registration Act, which this project has not read.**

**§ 35.333(a)(9)** requires substantially the same statement, **in the words the rule prints**, in every
agreement of sale. You will meet it again in the companion course.

## F. How to read a rule — a working habit for this course

Chapter 35 is written in a particular way, and noticing the pattern will make the rest of both courses
easier.

**Rules state who the duty falls on.** § 35.321(a) makes the *broker* deposit escrow money and makes the
*broker of record* responsible for ensuring it happens. § 35.358(a)(3) makes the *provider* responsible
for verifying attendance. When you read a rule, find the actor first.

**Exceptions are stated in their own subsections, and they are narrow.** § 35.324(a) gives a deadline;
§ 35.324(b) gives the only exception, and it requires the **written permission of both parties**.
§ 35.326(a) prohibits commingling; § 35.326(b) permits exactly two deposits of the broker's own money,
for bank service charges and minimum balances. When you find a prohibition, check the next subsection.

**Where the Commission wants exact words, it prints them.** § 35.340 prints the CMA disclaimer and says
*"printed conspicuously and **without change**."* § 35.333(a)(9) prints the Recovery Fund statement.
§ 35.333(a)(11) prints the time-share cancellation notice and specifies **bold face type of at least 10
point size immediately above the signature line.** Where the rule prints words, they are not a model —
they are the requirement.

**Where the Commission does not state a figure, this course does not invent one.** You will see routing
language throughout both courses — *"this project did not read that source; the answer comes from
there."* That is deliberate. A course that guesses a deadline teaches a licensee to guess deadlines.
