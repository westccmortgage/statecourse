## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish real property from personal property, and apply the fixture tests.
2. Identify the bundle of rights and what each right permits.
3. Distinguish freehold estates from leasehold estates.
4. Distinguish the forms of concurrent ownership.
5. Identify encumbrances and distinguish liens from non-lien encumbrances.
6. Explain why a salesperson's judgement on any of these routes to counsel — and connect that to
   § 35.292(a)(8) and (12).

## A. Real property and personal property

**Land** is the surface, what lies beneath it, and the airspace above it. **Real property** is the land
plus everything permanently attached to it plus the rights that run with it. **Personal property** —
sometimes called *chattels* or *personalty* — is everything else, and it moves.

The distinction decides what conveys. A deed carries the real property. It does not carry the
refrigerator unless the agreement says so.

### A.1 Fixtures

A **fixture** is personal property that has become real property by being attached. The tests, in the
order they are usually applied:

1. **Method of annexation** — how firmly is it attached, and what damage would removal cause?
2. **Adaptation** — was it made or fitted specifically for this property? Custom shutters cut to these
   windows are adapted; a standing lamp is not.
3. **Relationship of the parties** — a tenant's attachment is read differently from an owner's.
4. **Intention** — usually controlling, and read from the objective circumstances rather than from what
   somebody now says they meant.

**The practical answer is not the doctrine.** The reliable practice is to **name in writing what stays
and what goes**, item by item, before anyone signs. Every fixture dispute a licensee ever sees could
have been prevented by a sentence in the agreement.

### A.2 Trade fixtures

Articles a **tenant** attaches for the conduct of a **business** — shelving, display counters, a walk-in
cooler. They remain the **tenant's**, and are removable **before the term ends**, after which they
generally pass to the landlord by **accession**. The lease can change that default and often does.

### A.3 Emblements

Annual crops produced by cultivation. A tenant farmer generally retains the right to harvest the crop
planted before the tenancy ended.

## B. The bundle of rights

Ownership is best understood as a **bundle** rather than a single thing, because the rights can be
separated and sold apart:

- **Possession** — to occupy and hold.
- **Use (enjoyment)** — to use it in lawful ways.
- **Exclusion** — to keep others out.
- **Disposition** — to sell, give, lease, mortgage or devise.
- **Encumbrance** — to pledge it as security or grant lesser interests in it.

A landowner who grants a utility easement has sold a stick from the bundle. A landowner who leases has
transferred possession while keeping the rest. A landowner who grants a mortgage has pledged, not
conveyed.

### B.1 Appurtenant rights

Rights that **run with the land**: **air rights**, **subsurface and mineral rights**, **water rights**,
and **appurtenant easements**. They pass with the parcel unless severed.

**Severance** is the key idea. Mineral rights and surface rights can be owned by different people, and a
buyer who assumes otherwise has assumed something no deed said. **This module states no Pennsylvania
rule on subsurface support, coal, oil or gas rights**, because the Pennsylvania statutes governing them
were **not read for this project**. In a Commonwealth with Pennsylvania's mining and drilling history, a
licensee who guesses here will be wrong expensively — the question goes to counsel and to a title
examination.

## C. Estates in land

### C.1 Freehold estates — of indefinite duration

**Fee simple absolute.** The largest estate: perpetual, inheritable, freely transferable, subject only to
government powers and to whatever encumbrances burden it.

**Fee simple defeasible.** A fee that can end on a stated condition. Two branches:

- **Fee simple determinable** — ends **automatically** on the event, with a possibility of reverter in
  the grantor. Language of duration: *so long as*, *while*, *during*.
- **Fee simple subject to a condition subsequent** — the grantor holds a **right of re-entry** and must
  act. Language of condition: *but if*, *provided that*, *on condition that*.

The difference is whether title snaps back by itself or must be taken back, and it turns on words in a
deed drafted by a lawyer. A licensee's job is to **notice** the language and **route it**, not to
construe it.

**Life estate.** Measured by a life. The **life tenant** may possess and use, and must avoid **waste** —
permanent injury to the property that damages the remainderman's future interest. When the measuring
life ends the estate passes to the **remainderman**, or **reverts** to the grantor.

A **life estate pur autre vie** is measured by the life of someone other than the holder.

### C.2 Leasehold estates — of limited duration

| Estate | How it ends |
|---|---|
| **Estate for years** | A definite beginning and a definite end. **Terminates on its own terms; no notice required.** |
| **Periodic tenancy** | Renews automatically period to period **until proper notice** is given. |
| **Tenancy at will** | Continues at the pleasure of both parties; either may end it. |
| **Tenancy at sufferance** | A holdover the landlord never agreed to — possession without right. |

**Notice periods for a Pennsylvania periodic tenancy are set by the Pennsylvania Landlord and Tenant
Act, which this project did not read. No notice period is stated in this course.**

### C.3 A lease is two things at once

A lease is **a conveyance** of a possessory interest for a term **and a contract** of covenants between
the parties. That dual nature is why a lease dispute can be framed either as a breach of contract or as
an interference with possession, and why the **covenant of quiet enjoyment** matters as much as any
written clause.

**Assignment** transfers the tenant's **entire** remaining interest. A **sublease** transfers **less than
all** of it, leaving the original tenant as a landlord to the subtenant. **In both cases the original
tenant generally remains liable unless expressly released** — and tenants routinely assume otherwise.
Whether either is permitted at all is a question for the lease's own terms.

## D. Concurrent ownership

**Tenancy in common.** Two or more owners, **undivided** interests that may be **unequal**, **no right of
survivorship** — an interest passes by will or intestacy — and each co-tenant may generally transfer or
encumber their own interest and may seek **partition**.

**Joint tenancy.** Requires the four unities — **possession, interest, time, title** — and carries a
**right of survivorship**. Severing a unity converts the severed share to a tenancy in common.

**Tenancy by the entirety.** Available only to **legally married couples**, with survivorship. Its
availability, its incidents and how it is created and severed are matters of **Pennsylvania law this
project did not read**, and **this module states no Pennsylvania rule on it.**

**Ownership in trust, by entity, and in common-interest communities** — condominium and planned
community forms — are additional structures. **Pennsylvania's Uniform Condominium Act and Uniform Planned
Community Act were not obtained by this project**, and no provision of either is stated here.

### D.1 Why this matters at a listing appointment

The names on a deed determine **who must sign**. A licensee who takes a listing signed by one of two
owners has a listing that may not bind the property. Read the deed, ask who is on title, and where the
answer is not obvious, **route it** — which is exactly what § 35.292(a)(8) requires: *"**Advise the
consumer to seek expert advice on matters about the transaction that are beyond the licensee's
expertise.**"*

## E. Encumbrances

An **encumbrance** is any claim, right or interest held by someone other than the owner that burdens the
title or limits the use.

### E.1 Liens — money claims

- **Mortgage lien** — voluntary, given as security for a loan.
- **Property tax lien** — involuntary, statutory, and generally superior in priority.
- **Mechanic's lien** — a contractor's or supplier's claim for work or materials. **Pennsylvania's
  Mechanics' Lien Law was not read for this project; no filing deadline, threshold or priority rule is
  stated here.**
- **Judgment lien** — attaching by operation of law to property of a judgment debtor.
- **Special assessment** — for a public improvement benefiting the property.

**General liens** attach to all of a debtor's property; **specific liens** attach to identified property.
**Voluntary liens** are granted; **involuntary liens** arise by law.

### E.2 Non-lien encumbrances — use claims

- **Easement** — a right to use another's land. **Appurtenant** easements benefit a dominant parcel and
  run with the land; easements **in gross** benefit a person or entity.
- **Licence** — permission to use, **revocable**, personal, and not an interest in land.
- **Encroachment** — a physical intrusion across a boundary. Found by a **survey**, not by a fence.
- **Deed restriction / restrictive covenant** — a private limitation running with the land.

**A restrictive covenant is private and is enforced privately; zoning is public and is enforced by the
municipality. Where the two differ, the more restrictive controls the owner's actual freedom of
action** — and a licensee who checks only the zoning has checked half of it.

## F. Where this module stops, and why

Everything above is vocabulary. **None of it authorises a licensee to give an opinion.** § 35.292(a)(12)
draws the line precisely, and it is worth reading twice:

> *"**Advise the consumer about compliance with laws pertaining to real estate transactions without
> rendering legal advice.**"*

Both halves are in one sentence. You are expected to **advise about compliance** — to tell a seller a
disclosure statement is required, to tell a buyer an agreement must contain the zoning classification, to
tell either that a question needs a lawyer. You may not **render legal advice** — construe a defeasible
fee, opine on whether an easement was extinguished, or tell a co-tenant what their interest is worth in
a partition.

The vocabulary in this module is what lets you tell the two apart.
