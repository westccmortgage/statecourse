## A. Finance vocabulary

**Principal and interest. Amortization. Debt service. PITI. Equity. Loan-to-value ratio. Points. Yield.**

**PITI** is the four-part housing payment — principal, interest, taxes, insurance — and it is the figure
qualifying ratios are computed against, not principal and interest alone.

**Equity** is value less what is owed. **Loan-to-value** is the loan divided by the value the lender
recognises — and lenders customarily recognise **the lower of the contract price and the appraised
value.** That single convention is why an appraisal below contract price is a problem for the buyer: the
lender lends against the lower figure and the difference must come from somewhere.

**A discount point is one percent of the loan amount**, paid at closing, and it buys the lender yield. A
useful rule of thumb in national content is that a point moves yield by about **one-eighth of one
percent** — an approximation, not a formula.

**Worked example.** A $280,000 loan with 2 points: 280,000 × 0.02 = **$5,600** paid at closing. If the
same borrower's monthly payment covers interest of 280,000 × 0.065 ÷ 12 = **$1,516.67** in the first
month, and the payment is $1,770, then **$253.33** reduces principal that month and the new balance is
**$279,746.67.** Interest is always computed on the **previous** balance; only the remainder touches
principal.

## B. The note and the security instrument

They are two documents doing two jobs.

**The promissory note** is the borrower's **promise to repay**. It is a contract, and everything in Module
PA-F-05 applies to it. Its special provisions include the **acceleration clause** (on default the whole
balance falls due at once), the **prepayment penalty clause**, and the **due-on-sale (alienation) clause**
(the balance falls due if the property is sold).

**The mortgage** is the instrument that **pledges the property** as security. **Hypothecation** is the
concept that ties them: the borrower pledges the property **while keeping possession of it.** Nothing
about a mortgage loan moves the borrower out.

**Pennsylvania is a mortgage state**, using a two-party mortgage — **mortgagor** (borrower) and
**mortgagee** (lender) — rather than the three-party deed of trust used elsewhere. **Pennsylvania's
mortgage and foreclosure statutes were not read for this project**, and **this course states no
Pennsylvania foreclosure procedure, notice period, redemption right or deficiency rule.**

**The due-on-sale clause is why assumption is rare.** Where the lender may call the loan on transfer, a
buyer taking over a seller's existing financing is the exception rather than the routine.

## C. Loan categories and types

### C.1 By programme

- **Conventional** — not government insured or guaranteed. **Conforming** loans meet secondary-market
  purchase standards; **nonconforming** loans do not, and are priced accordingly. **Private mortgage
  insurance (PMI)** typically appears where the loan-to-value is high.
- **FHA-insured** — FHA does not build housing and does not lend; it **insures approved lenders against
  loss**. Borrowers pay a **mortgage insurance premium (MIP)**.
- **VA-guaranteed** — the VA **guarantees** approved lenders against loss for eligible veterans; a
  **funding fee** applies; the defining feature is the possibility of **no down payment**.
- **USDA Rural Development** — direct and guaranteed loans in eligible areas.

**This course states no loan limit, down payment percentage, MIP or funding fee rate, or eligibility
threshold**, because no agency handbook or programme guide was read for this project. Those figures change
and they come from the lender.

### C.2 By repayment

**Fixed-rate level payment (fully amortized)** — the most common residential plan. **Adjustable rate
(ARM)** — with a **periodic cap**, a **lifetime cap**, and an **index**; note that a cap on the
**payment** is not a cap on the **interest**, and a payment cap can produce **negative amortization** as
the shortfall is added to principal. **Interest-only / term.** **Balloon (partially amortized).**
**Buydown**, where a lump sum paid at closing reduces the rate temporarily or permanently and may be paid
by buyer, seller or a third party. **Construction**, **blanket**, **package**, **open-end** and **bridge**
loans serve particular purposes.

### C.3 Primary and secondary markets

The **primary market** is where a loan is **made** — banks, savings institutions, credit unions, mortgage
banking companies and mortgage brokers. The **secondary market** is where a loan already made is
**bought** — **Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac and Ginnie Mae** — through **loan pooling** and **mortgage-backed
securities.**

The link between them is conforming standards: a lender writes to those standards **so the loan can be
sold**, and selling it returns capital to lend again.

### C.4 Federal consumer protection, in outline

**TILA / Regulation Z** governs disclosure of credit terms and advertising of consumer credit; **RESPA /
Regulation X** governs settlement services and prohibits kickbacks and referral fees among settlement
service providers; the **TRID** rule consolidated the two sets of disclosures into the **Loan Estimate**
and the **Closing Disclosure**.

**None of these regulations was read for this project.** **This course states no disclosure deadline, no
tolerance category, no trigger term list and no penalty.** What a licensee should carry away is the shape:
**advertising credit terms pulls in mandatory disclosures**, and **the safest advertising practice is to
publish no numbers other than the price.** For any live deadline, the lender is the source.

## D. Valuation

### D.1 Five different figures

**Value** is worth. **Cost** is what was spent to create it. **Price** is what someone actually paid.
**Market value** is what a property should bring under defined market conditions. **Assessed value** is
what a taxing authority places on it, and it need not equal market value.

A house can cost $400,000 to build, be priced at $360,000, have a market value of $375,000 and be assessed
at $290,000, all at once, with no contradiction.

### D.2 The four elements of value

**Demand, utility, scarcity, transferability.** All four must be present. A thing everyone wants, that is
useful and rare, has no market value if it cannot be transferred.

### D.3 Principles

**Substitution** — a buyer will pay no more than the cost of an equally desirable substitute; it is the
basis of the sales comparison approach. **Contribution** — an improvement adds what it **returns**, not
what it **cost**. **Highest and best use** — one at a time, and it changes. **Conformity.**
**Anticipation.** **Supply and demand.** **Plottage**, the increment of value from merging adjacent
parcels, produced by the process of **assemblage**.

### D.4 The three approaches

**Sales comparison.** Compare closed sales of similar properties. Two rules govern it: **adjust the
comparables, never the subject**, and a **currently listed or pending property is not a comparable** — a
listing is what a seller hopes for and a pending has not yet survived appraisal or financing. **Superior
comparables adjust down; inferior comparables adjust up.** Reconcile by **weighted consideration**, giving
most weight to the comparable that needed least adjustment — not by simple averaging, which treats a
$2,000 adjustment and a $40,000 adjustment as equally good evidence.

**Cost.** Estimate the **land** separately by sales comparison (**land is never depreciated**), estimate
**replacement or reproduction cost** of the improvements, subtract **accrued depreciation**, and add the
two. Depreciation is **physical deterioration**, **functional obsolescence**, or **external (economic)
obsolescence** — and **external obsolescence is incurable**, because its cause lies outside the boundary
line and the owner does not own it.

**Income.** **Direct capitalization: Value = NOI ÷ capitalization rate.** **Net operating income excludes
debt service**, because NOI describes the **property's** earning power and debt service belongs to the
buyer. The cap rate measures risk, and it is inversely related to value: at the same income, a higher cap
rate gives a lower value.

**Worked example.** Potential gross income $190,000, vacancy and collection loss 6 percent, operating
expenses $64,000, annual debt service $71,000, market cap rate 7.5 percent.
Effective gross income = 190,000 × 0.94 = **$178,600.** NOI = 178,600 − 64,000 = **$114,600.**
Value = 114,600 ÷ 0.075 = **$1,528,000.** *(114,600 − 71,000 = $43,600 is before-tax cash flow — a
different figure answering a different question.)*

## E. The Pennsylvania rule that governs every CMA you will ever produce

**§ 35.340 — Comparative market analysis:**

> *"A comparative market analysis shall contain the following statement **printed conspicuously and
> without change on the first page**:*
>
> ***"This analysis has not been performed in accordance with the Uniform Standards of Professional
> Appraisal Practice which requires valuers to act as unbiased, disinterested third parties with
> impartiality, objectivity and independence and without accommodation of personal interest. It is not to
> be construed as an appraisal and may not be used as such for any purpose."***

Four requirements in one short rule: **the statement**, **conspicuously**, **without change**, and **on the
first page**. Not paraphrased, not in a footer, not on page three.

**A licensee is not an appraiser.** A CMA is a licensee's opinion of probable sales price, produced for a
consumer making a pricing or offering decision. An appraisal is an appraiser's opinion produced under
professional standards. § 35.340 exists so that nobody downstream can mistake one for the other.

**And a broker price opinion is a third thing.** New **Subchapter J (§§ 35.501–35.503)**, added December 26,
2025, effective December 27, 2025, requires the **same USPAP disclaimer** on a BPO, restricts BPOs to four
permitted uses, prohibits them in a list of situations including **mortgage loan origination**, requires a
salesperson to have held an **active licence for the 3 years immediately preceding** the BPO's effective
date, and requires **Commission-approved education**. The companion course covers it in full. It appears
here so that a student learning valuation learns, at the same moment, that **Pennsylvania regulates what
you may call your opinion and what it may be used for.**
