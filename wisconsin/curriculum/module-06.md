## Hour 28 — Contract law and the elements of a valid contract

### 28.1 What a contract is — REEB 25.033(8)(a) and (b)

A **contract** is a legally enforceable agreement. Four elements are required, and a fifth practical
requirement attaches to contracts concerning land.

**Mutual assent** — an offer and an acceptance producing agreement on the essential terms. Assent must
be genuine, and it is undermined by **fraud**, **misrepresentation**, **mutual mistake**, **duress**
and **undue influence**.

**Competent parties** — legal age and legal capacity. A **minor's** contract is generally **voidable
by the minor**. A person adjudicated incompetent cannot contract; a person lacking capacity at the
moment of contracting produces a voidable contract. Entities act through authorised signatories,
trusts through the trustee, estates through the personal representative.

**Lawful objective** — a contract to do something illegal is **void** and no court will enforce it.

**Consideration** — something of legal value exchanged by each side. Courts examine whether it exists,
not whether it was adequate. In an offer to purchase the mutual promises are the consideration; the
**earnest money is evidence of good faith and ability to perform, not the consideration itself.**

**The statute of frauds** — a contract for the sale of an interest in real property must be **in
writing and signed by the party to be charged**. Wisconsin's conveyancing requirements sit in
**ch. 706**, and REEB 25.033(9)(a) requires instruction on the **requirements of a valid conveyance
under ss. 240.10, 706.02 and 706.03**. Module WI-07 takes them.

### 28.2 Genuine assent, and where a licensee supplies the defect

**Fraud** is a knowing misstatement of material fact relied on to the other party's detriment.
**Innocent misrepresentation** is the same statement made without knowledge of its falsity, and it can
still support rescission.

This is where the duties of Module WI-02 meet contract law. A firm that fails to disclose a material
adverse fact under **§ 452.133(1)(c)**, or repeats a seller's statement without qualification, has
supplied the element that makes the contract voidable — and **§ 452.139(2)(a)** does not protect the
client where "the firm or licensee is repeating a misrepresentation made by the client."

**REEB 25.033(14)(h)** lists **civil liability for misrepresentation** as a required topic in its own
right, and Module WI-10 takes it.

### 28.3 Capacity in a Wisconsin transaction

Three capacity questions recur and are established at listing rather than at closing:

**Marital property and homestead.** Wisconsin is a **marital property state** under ch. 766, and a
spouse may hold an interest and may need to sign to convey or encumber the homestead **even where not
on the deed**. Module WI-04 Hour 21.2. The question goes to the title company and counsel.

**Entities and trusts.** Authority is evidenced by organisational documents, resolutions, and trust
instruments, and is verified by the closing agent.

**Powers of attorney**, which must be in the correct form and recorded where the transaction requires
it. A power of attorney discovered at the closing table is a delayed closing.

## Hour 29 — Classifications, and the vocabulary examinations use

### 29.1 Four states of a contract

**Valid** — all requirements met; binding on both parties.
**Void** — no legal effect at all, and never had any. An unlawful objective.
**Voidable** — valid and operative **unless** the party with the power to avoid it disaffirms. A
minor's contract; a contract induced by fraud.
**Unenforceable** — valid on its face but not enforceable in court. An oral land contract; a claim
barred by limitation. The parties may perform it voluntarily; no court will make them.

### 29.2 Bilateral or unilateral

**Bilateral** — a promise for a promise. An offer to purchase is bilateral.
**Unilateral** — a promise for an act; the offeree accepts by performing. An **open listing** and a
reward offer are the classic examples, and an **option** is unilateral until exercised.

### 29.3 Executory or executed

**Executory** — something remains to be done. A binding offer to purchase before closing is executory.
**Executed** — **fully performed**.

The trap: the industry says "executed" loosely to mean signed. **The legal meaning is performed**, and
examinations use the legal meaning.

### 29.4 Express or implied

**Express** — stated in words, written or spoken. **Implied** — inferred from conduct.

Note the Wisconsin overlay: an **agency** relationship arises from an **agency agreement**, and
**§ 452.135(2)(a)** requires the Disclosure to Clients not later than the time the firm enters into
it. The parties' conduct does not substitute for the agreement.

### 29.5 Assignment and novation

**Assignment** transfers a party's rights under a contract to an assignee. Whether it is permitted
depends on the contract's own terms, and Wisconsin's approved offer addresses it.

**Novation** substitutes a new contract or a new party with the consent of all, and — unlike
assignment — **releases** the original party. A party who "assigned the contract" is not released
unless everyone agreed to a novation in writing.

## Hour 30 — Offer, acceptance, and binding acceptance

### 30.1 The offer

An **offer** shows present intent to be bound, is communicated to the offeree, and states the
essential terms definitely enough that a court could enforce them.

An advertisement is generally **not** an offer; it is an invitation to make one. A listing is not an
offer to sell to whoever appears.

### 30.2 How an offer ends

**Acceptance** — a contract forms.

**Rejection** — the offer is dead and cannot afterwards be accepted.

**Counter-offer** — in law a **rejection plus a new offer**. The moment a seller counters, **the
buyer's original offer no longer exists**, and if the buyer walks away the seller cannot then accept
it. This is the mechanic most often misunderstood and the one with the greatest practical consequence
in a multiple-offer week.

**Revocation by the offeror**, effective when communicated, at any time before acceptance — unless the
offeree has paid for an **option**, which is a separate contract holding the offer open.

**Lapse of time**, on the offer's own expiry or after a reasonable time if none is stated. A Wisconsin
offer states a **binding acceptance deadline**, and it is real.

**Death or incapacity** of either party before acceptance.

**Destruction of the property** before acceptance.

### 30.3 Binding acceptance — the Wisconsin concept every deadline runs from

Wisconsin's approved offer to purchase uses **binding acceptance** as its reference point, and
essentially every deadline in the offer is expressed as a number of days from it.

**Binding acceptance occurs when a party accepts the offer or the counter-offer and delivers that
acceptance in the manner the offer requires.** Both halves matter: acceptance must be unqualified —
the mirror image rule — and it must be **delivered** as the form defines delivery.

Three consequences a salesperson must be able to state:

**An oral acceptance is not a binding acceptance.** "They said yes" starts no clock.

**The delivery method matters.** The approved offer defines what delivery means and how it may be
accomplished, and a licensee must read that provision rather than assume the common-law **mailbox
rule** that treats a mailed acceptance as effective on dispatch.

**The date of binding acceptance is the single most important date in the file**, because every
inspection, financing, appraisal, title and closing deadline is computed from it. Getting it wrong
mis-computes the entire deadline map.

### 30.4 Multiple offers

A seller may accept one offer, counter one, counter several, or reject all.

**Countering several simultaneously creates a real risk of two contracts on one property**, which is
exactly the problem Wisconsin's **multiple counter-proposal** form addresses — Hour 34.

The licensee's own duties in a multiple-offer situation come from Module WI-02 and do not bend:
**§ 452.133(1)(g)** requires proposals to be presented **objectively and without bias, with their
advantages and disadvantages disclosed**; **REEB 24.12 and 24.13** — listed at REEB 25.033(13)(b)3 —
require **fair presentation of offers**, **prompt delivery to buyer**, and **prompt notification**,
which Module WI-10 takes; and **§ 452.133(1)(d)** protects each party's confidences separately, so a
buyer's stated maximum is not the seller's to hear.

## Hour 31 — Performance, discharge, breach, and remedies

### 31.1 Performance and discharge — REEB 25.033(8)(c) and (d)

A contract is discharged by **performance**; by **mutual agreement to rescind**; by **novation**; by
**operation of law** — including bankruptcy, destruction of the subject matter, or the running of a
limitation period; or by **breach**.

**Substantial performance** is performance of everything essential with only trivial deviation, and it
generally entitles the performing party to enforce, subject to an allowance for the deficiency.

### 31.2 Default and breach — REEB 25.033(8)(e)

A **material breach** defeats the purpose of the contract. A trivial failure is not one, and calling a
trivial failure a breach is itself a breach.

**Time is of the essence** clauses make deadlines strict; where the clause applies, a day late is a
default.

**Remedies**: **damages**; **liquidated damages**, commonly the earnest money; **rescission**; and
**specific performance**, which is available on a land contract because every parcel is unique and
money is treated as an inadequate substitute. Contracts frequently require **mitigation**.

### 31.3 The earnest money, and the two questions about it

**Where does it go?** Into the firm's **trust account** under **§ 452.133(1)(f)** and the rules under
**s. 452.13(5)** — Module WI-13 — or to another holder the offer names, in the time the offer and the
rules require.

**Who gets it when the deal fails?** That is a **legal** question. Wisconsin's approved offer contains
provisions governing disbursement of earnest money, including what happens when the parties disagree,
and a firm holding disputed funds follows those provisions and the trust-account rules.

**A licensee never picks a side.** The matter goes to the **supervising broker** and, where necessary,
to counsel. And a licensee who tells a client "you're entitled to the earnest money" has given legal
advice outside **§ 452.133(2)(am)**'s scope.

### 31.4 The sentence that governs the whole hour

**Whether a party is in default, and what remedy follows, are legal conclusions.** A licensee's job
when a deal is failing is to **know the deadline, put the facts in writing, and get the question in
front of the right person in time**. Under **§ 452.133(2)(am)** the duty to advise is limited to
matters within the scope of the knowledge, skills and training required under ch. 452, and default
analysis is outside it.

## Hour 32 — Ch. REEB 16 and the approved-form regime

### 32.1 Why Wisconsin has approved forms

**§ 452.05(1)(b)** empowers the Board to approve forms, and **ch. REEB 16** governs the forms used in
real estate transactions. REEB 25.033(8)(f) requires instruction on **"forms used in real estate under
ch. REEB 16"** and then lists fourteen of them.

This is a structural difference from most states, and it exists for a specific reason: **a licensee
completing a Board-approved form is not practising law.** The Board has approved the language; the
licensee supplies the facts the parties have agreed. Take the approved forms away and every completed
contract becomes a drafting question.

### 32.2 What a licensee may do

**Use the current approved form** for the transaction.
**Complete the blanks** with what the parties have agreed — names, property, price, dates, amounts,
selections among the form's own options.
**Select and attach the addenda** the transaction requires.
**Explain, in ordinary language, what a provision of the form provides.**

That is licensed activity, and refusing to help a client complete an approved form would itself be a
failure of the duties in § 452.133(2).

### 32.3 What a licensee may not do

**Alter the form's printed provisions.**
**Draft bespoke legal language** to solve a legal problem — however sensible the sentence would be.
**Advise on the legal effect** of a provision, or on whether a party is in default, or on what a
remedy would be.
**Prepare a deed**, or advise which deed to use.
**Use a form the Board has not approved** where an approved form exists for the transaction.

**§ 452.133(2)(am)** supplies the line: the duty to advise runs to matters **within the scope of the
knowledge, skills and training required under ch. 452**. **REEB 24.03** — competence in area of
service — carries the same idea as an ethics rule. And **§ 452.135**'s own printed disclosure says it
to the consumer directly: *if you need legal advice, tax advice, or a professional home inspection,
contact an attorney, tax advisor, or home inspector.*

### 32.4 The practical discipline

**Use the current version.** Approved forms are revised, and using a superseded version is an error a
reviewer will find.

**Complete every blank the transaction requires**, and do not leave a provision ambiguous because the
parties have not decided. An undecided term is a term to be decided, not a blank to be left.

**Attach every addendum the form references**, and reference every addendum that is attached.

**Initial and date every change**, by every party.

**Control the version.** Where several signed counterparts circulate by email, the question of which
document is the contract must never be genuinely open.

**This course does not reproduce any approved form, and states no provision of one**, because ch. REEB
16 and the forms themselves have not been read from primary text for this project. A student is taught
the **structure and the discipline**; the forms in use are the Board's current versions, obtained from
the Board.

## Hour 33 — The offer to purchase

### 33.1 Structure — REEB 25.033(8)(f)2

A Wisconsin offer to purchase, in outline, settles:

**Parties**, in their full legal capacity, and — where marital property or an entity is involved —
everyone whose signature is required.

**Property**, by address and legal description, with **included and excluded items**, which is where
the fixture work of Module WI-01 lands and where **leased** water treatment equipment and fuel tanks
must be identified.

**Purchase price and earnest money** — the amount, who holds it, when it is delivered, and its
treatment in every way the transaction can end.

**Binding acceptance** — the deadline for acceptance and delivery, from which every other deadline
runs.

**Financing** — whether the purchase is cash or financed, the type and amount, and the **financing
contingency** period.

**Closing and occupancy** — the closing date, and when occupancy transfers. **They are separate**, and
any gap needs its own written provision.

**Title evidence** — what the seller must furnish, the buyer's right to object, the objection period,
and the seller's cure right and obligation.

**Property condition** — the condition the property must be in at closing, and the **inspection**
contingency.

**Real estate condition report** under ch. 709, and its delivery and any right attached to it.

**Risk of loss** between binding acceptance and closing.

**Default** and the remedies each party has.

**Notices and delivery** — how notice is given and when effective. This provision decides deadline
disputes.

**Addenda**, each referenced and attached.

### 33.2 Contingencies, and the failure mode

A **contingency** makes a duty conditional on a stated event. Three things can happen to it:

**Satisfaction** — the event occurs and the duty becomes absolute.
**Waiver** — the party it protects gives it up, in writing.
**Failure with a stated consequence** — the event does not occur in the period and the form says what
follows, usually a right to terminate on written notice within a stated time.

And the dangerous fourth: **the period expires and nobody acts.** Under many provisions a contingency
not exercised in time is deemed satisfied or waived, and the protection is simply gone.

**This is why deadline management is a professional skill rather than an administrative chore**, and
why Hour 39 builds the map.

### 33.3 The contingencies a Wisconsin offer commonly carries

**Financing.** The buyer must obtain a written commitment for a loan of a stated type and amount within
a stated period. A commitment is not funding, and a commitment can be withdrawn if the buyer's
circumstances change.

**Appraisal**, sometimes inside the financing contingency and sometimes separate. If value comes in
below the price, the lender lends against the lower figure and the gap must be closed by the buyer, by
a price reduction, by reconsideration, or by termination.

**Inspection.** The buyer investigates the condition. A Wisconsin inspection routinely extends to
**radon**, **private well water quality**, **septic**, **wood-destroying insects**, and on pre-1978
housing a **lead** assessment. **Laboratory turnaround alone can consume a short inspection period**,
which is a planning fact rather than a legal one.

**Title evidence and objection.**

**Sale of the buyer's current property**, the most fragile contingency, often paired with a bump
clause allowing the seller to continue marketing.

**Condominium or association documents**, where applicable.

**Well and septic**, which on rural Wisconsin property is frequently a separate contingency with its
own testing requirements.

## Hour 34 — Counter-offers, multiple counter-proposals, amendments, and notices

### 34.1 Counter-offers — REEB 25.033(8)(f)5

A **counter-offer** rejects the offer and makes a new one, incorporating the original by reference and
changing stated terms. It has its own **binding acceptance** deadline, and — this is the point students
miss — **the original offer is dead the moment the counter is delivered.**

A counter-offer may itself be countered, and a chain of counters is common. Each one kills the last.

### 34.2 Multiple counter-proposals — REEB 25.033(8)(f)6

**This is a Wisconsin instrument that most states do not have**, and it exists to solve the problem
Hour 30.4 identified.

A seller facing several offers who counters all of them simultaneously risks having two accepted
counters and therefore two contracts on one property. A **multiple counter-proposal** is structured so
that the seller may respond to more than one buyer at once **without becoming bound to more than one**
— typically by making any resulting agreement subject to the seller's further written acceptance or
selection.

Three consequences for practice:

**A buyer who receives one must understand that acceptance does not by itself create a contract**, and
the licensee working with that buyer must say so plainly.

**The seller's selection must be documented**, and the buyers who were not selected must be notified
promptly — **REEB 24.13**'s prompt notification duty, listed at REEB 25.033(13)(b)3.e.

**REEB 25.033(8)(f)6 was amended by CR 23-075**, effective 9-1-24, and the version taught is the
amended one. The form itself is the Board's current version.

### 34.3 Amendments — REEB 25.033(8)(f)7

An **amendment** changes an existing contract and **requires the assent of all parties**. It is not
created by the other agent saying "that's fine", and it is not created by conduct.

Amendments are the instrument for: extending a deadline; changing the closing date; adjusting the
price after an inspection or appraisal; agreeing repairs; and changing any other agreed term.

**REEB 25.033(8)(f)7 was amended by CR 23-075.**

### 34.4 Notices — REEB 25.033(8)(f)8

A **notice** is a one-sided communication the contract authorises a party to give — typically to
exercise, satisfy or waive a contingency, or to trigger a right.

The distinction from an amendment is worth stating precisely: **an amendment changes the deal and
needs everyone's agreement; a notice exercises a right the deal already gave and needs only the
party's delivery.**

Two things decide whether a notice worked: **the form the contract requires** and **delivery as the
contract defines it**. A notice given by a method the contract does not authorise, or delivered after
the period, has not been given — and the right it was meant to exercise may be gone.

**REEB 25.033(8)(f)8 was amended by CR 23-075.**

### 34.5 The practical rule for all four

**Everything is in writing, on the approved form, delivered as the contract requires, and kept.** A
licensee's file should be able to show, for every one of them, **what was sent, by whom, to whom, by
what method, and when.**

## Hour 35 — Addenda, buyer agency agreements, and listing contracts

### 35.1 Addenda — REEB 25.033(8)(f)3

An **addendum** is attached at the time of contracting and forms part of the agreement. Wisconsin's
approved forms include addenda for the situations that recur — condominium transactions, land
contracts, financing arrangements, occupancy before or after closing, and the sale of a buyer's
property.

The discipline: **reference every addendum in the offer, attach every addendum referenced, and have
every party sign each one.** An addendum referenced but missing is a defect a reviewer finds and a
court has to construe.

### 35.2 Buyer agency agreements — REEB 25.033(8)(f)4

A **buyer agency agreement** is the agency agreement that makes a buyer a **client** rather than a
party. Without it, the buyer is a customer owed the seven duties in **§ 452.133(1)** — which is a
substantial list, but not representation.

Three things follow:

**§ 452.135(2)(a)** requires the **Disclosure to Clients** to reach the buyer **not later than the time
the firm enters into the agreement**.

**§ 452.135(1)(a)** requires the **Disclosure to Customers** before the firm negotiates on behalf of a
party who is **not** its client — so a firm working with an unrepresented buyer must give that
disclosure instead.

**The agreement's terms** — its scope, its term, its compensation and how it is sourced, and whether
it is exclusive — are the negotiating points, and they are completed on the approved form.

### 35.3 Listing contracts — REEB 25.033(8)(f)1

Module WI-03 took listings substantively. Two form points belong here.

**REEB 25.033(8)(f)1 lists "listing agreements under s. 240.10"**, and **s. 240.10** is Wisconsin's
statute of frauds provision for real estate broker contracts — which is why a Wisconsin listing must be
in writing and signed.

**The listing contract is an approved form**, completed rather than drafted, on the same terms as the
offer.

## Hour 36 — The disclosure forms

### 36.1 The seller condition report — REEB 25.033(8)(f)10.a

The **Real Estate Condition Report** under **ch. 709** is the seller's statement about the property. It
is a Board-recognised disclosure form and it is completed **by the seller**.

The licensee's part, stated as rules:

**Give it to the seller and let the seller complete it.** A licensee who fills it in has substituted
their knowledge for the seller's.

**Do not coach toward "not aware".** A seller who genuinely does not know may say so; a seller
encouraged to say so about something they do know has been helped into a misrepresentation, and the
licensee's own knowledge now engages § 452.133(1)(c).

**Read the completed report as an investigation prompt list.** Blanks and "not aware" answers are
information about what to ask next, not answers.

**Deliver it as the transaction requires**, and **update it when things change**.

**And know that it does not discharge the firm's own duty.** § 452.133(1)(c) requires the **firm** to
disclose in writing material adverse facts **the firm** knows and the party cannot reasonably discover.
A seller's "not aware" is irrelevant to what the firm knows.

**This course states no ch. 709 requirement, exemption, deadline or report item**, because the chapter
has not been read from primary text for this project.

### 36.2 Agency disclosure — REEB 25.033(8)(f)10.b

The **Disclosure to Customers** under § 452.135(1)(a) and the **Disclosure to Clients** under
§ 452.135(2)(a), whose timing Module WI-02 Hour 10 set out. The statute prints the customer disclosure
text; Hour 10.1 quotes its substance.

### 36.3 Buyer and seller disclosure — REEB 25.033(8)(f)10.c

The rule lists "buyer and seller disclosure" as a category of its own. In practice a Wisconsin
transaction carries disclosures running in both directions: the seller's condition report and any
statutory disclosures attached to the property; and the buyer's disclosures where the transaction
requires them, including any disclosure of the buyer's relationship to a licensee.

Note the two provisions of **§ 452.133(3)** that create disclosure obligations in the contract itself:
**(3)(b)** requires the written consent of all parties to a licensee's self-dealing to be obtained
**in the offer to purchase, option, lease or other transaction contract**; and **(3)(c)** requires
written disclosure of a referral interest.

## Hour 37 — Cancellation, options, bills of sale, and exchanges

### 37.1 Cancellation agreement and mutual release — REEB 25.033(8)(f)11

The instrument by which the parties end a contract by agreement and release each other. It is the
clean way out, and it is where the **disposition of the earnest money** is agreed.

Its importance is practical: a transaction that simply stops, with nobody signing anything, leaves the
contract technically alive, the earnest money undisbursed, and the property difficult to re-market.
**REEB 25.033(8)(f)11 was amended by CR 23-075.**

### 37.2 Options — REEB 25.033(8)(f)12

An **option** is a contract in which the optionor holds an offer open for a stated period in exchange
for **consideration**. It is **unilateral** until exercised: the optionee may buy but is not obliged
to.

Three points: **consideration must actually pass**; the option should be **recorded** to bind
successors; and the terms on which the purchase will proceed must be settled in the option itself, or
there is nothing to exercise.

A **right of first refusal** is a different instrument frequently confused with an option: it gives the
holder the right to **match a bona fide offer** the owner receives, and it is **triggered** by that
offer rather than exercisable at will. Rights of first refusal appear in Wisconsin condominium
documents, family land arrangements and lake association covenants, and a listing licensee who
discovers one routes it to counsel immediately.

**REEB 25.033(8)(f)12 was amended by CR 23-075.**

### 37.3 Bills of sale — REEB 25.033(8)(f)13

The instrument transferring **personal property**. Module WI-01 Hour 1.2 set out why the line matters,
and **§ 452.133(3)(d)** permits a licensee to use a Board-approved form under s. 452.05(1)(b) for the
conveyance of the seller's interest in personal property.

Where a Wisconsin transaction conveys a boat, a lift, farm equipment, furnishings, or a manufactured
home that has not become real property, the bill of sale is how it moves — and what it covers must
match the offer's included items exactly.

**REEB 25.033(8)(f)13 was amended by CR 23-075.**

### 37.4 Exchanges — REEB 25.033(8)(f)14

An **exchange** is a transaction in which property is traded rather than sold for cash, and the term is
usually met in the context of a **section 1031 like-kind exchange**, which permits deferral of gain on
an exchange of real property held for productive use or investment, subject to identification and
completion periods and the use of a **qualified intermediary**.

The licensee's role is narrow and important: **recognise that a party intends an exchange, and get
them to their tax adviser and the intermediary before the offer is written**, because the structure
must be in place beforehand. A licensee who advises on whether an exchange will qualify has given tax
advice outside § 452.133(2)(am).

**REEB 25.033(8)(f)14 was amended by CR 23-075.**

## Hour 38 — Understanding closing statements

### 38.1 Why this is in the contracts topic — REEB 25.033(8)(f)9

The rule places **"understanding closing statements"** among the forms used in real estate, and the
reason is that the closing statement is where the contract's terms become numbers. A licensee who
cannot read one cannot check that the transaction closed on the terms the parties agreed.

### 38.2 Debit and credit

A **debit** is a charge against a party; a **credit** is an amount in their favour. Each side's
statement balances, and any **prorated amount appears on both statements, in opposite directions, to
the penny.**

**Buyer's principal debits:** purchase price; loan charges; title and closing charges; recording
charges allocated to the buyer; prepaid interest, insurance and escrow reserves; and prorated items the
seller has paid in advance.
**Buyer's principal credits:** the loan amount; earnest money; seller and lender credits; and prorated
items the seller owes.

**Seller's principal debits:** mortgage payoffs and accrued interest; the brokerage fee; the **real
estate transfer fee** under ss. 77.22 and 77.27, conventionally the seller's; other seller-allocated
charges; and prorated items the seller owes.
**Seller's principal credit:** the purchase price.

### 38.3 The Wisconsin proration trap

**Property taxes.** Wisconsin's tax bill issues in **December for the year just assessed**, and the
instalment schedule is the municipality's. So at a closing in, say, June, the current year's tax has
been assessed but not billed, and the parties are prorating an **estimated** amount for a year that is
not yet complete.

**How the estimate is made is a contract term**, not a habit: the approved offer addresses the basis on
which taxes are prorated, and the licensee's job is to read that provision and confirm what the parties
actually agreed. Using the prior year's bill, using the current assessment with the prior year's mill
rate, and using the actual bill when it issues are different answers, and the difference on a
reassessed property can be substantial.

**Credits matter too.** The **lottery and gaming credit** applies only where claimed by an owner using
the property as a primary residence, so a buyer of a non-owner-occupied property may not have it in the
first year.

### 38.4 Reading a closing statement against the file

A licensee is not verifying the closing agent's arithmetic. A licensee **is** the person who should
notice when the documents and the contract do not describe the same transaction:

The **purchase price** against the offer and every amendment. Any **seller credit** actually appearing,
in the agreed amount. **Prorations** on the basis the offer specified. The **earnest money** credited.
**Agreed repairs** reflected where the parties agreed they would be. The **loan type and amount**
matching the financing contingency. The **payoff** and its good-through date on the seller's side. And
the **firm's fee** stated correctly.

Where they do not match, raise it **in writing, immediately**, with the closing agent and the lender —
and do not attempt to resolve it.

## Hour 39 — Arbitration, the deadline map, and file quality control

### 39.1 Arbitration — REEB 25.033(8)(g)

The rule requires instruction on the **agreement to arbitrate real estate transaction disputes under
s. 788.015**.

**s. 788.015** is the provision of Wisconsin's arbitration chapter addressing arbitration agreements in
real estate transactions, and an approved form may carry one. What a licensee must understand is the
**effect** of agreeing to arbitrate — that disputes go to an arbitrator rather than to a court, with
different procedures and limited review — and that **whether to agree is the party's decision on
advice**, not the licensee's recommendation.

**This course states no requirement or limit from s. 788.015**, which has not been read from primary
text for this project.

### 39.2 The deadline map

Built from **binding acceptance** — the date Hour 30.3 made the most important in the file — after
reading the offer's day-counting rule and its notice and delivery provisions.

| # | Deadline | Who acts | Document required | If it passes |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Earnest money delivery | Buyer | Cleared funds to the named holder | Seller may have a remedy |
| 2 | Loan application | Buyer | Application; Loan Estimate follows | Financing contingency at risk |
| 3 | Real estate condition report delivery | Seller | Completed ch. 709 report | Buyer right may arise |
| 4 | Inspection period ends | Buyer | Report; **written notice** if acting | Contingency may be deemed satisfied or waived |
| 5 | Radon, well water, septic results | Laboratory and inspectors | Reports | May outrun a short inspection period |
| 6 | Lead assessment period | Buyer | Federal ten-day opportunity | Waived if unused |
| 7 | Appraisal | Lender | Appraisal report | Reconsideration window closes |
| 8 | Title evidence and objection | Buyer | Written objection | Objection right may be lost |
| 9 | Seller cure period | Seller | Curative instruments | Buyer's remedy arises |
| 10 | Financing commitment | Lender and buyer | Written commitment | Financing contingency terminates |
| 11 | Closing Disclosure delivery | Lender | Received by borrower | Closing date moves |
| 12 | Walk-through | Buyer | Completed checklist | Condition claims weaken |
| 13 | Closing and occupancy | All | Signed, funded, recorded | Default analysis begins |

Internal reminders sit several days ahead of each, and the map is reviewed weekly rather than consulted
when something goes wrong.

### 39.3 File quality control

Run before the file is closed:

Every page present and every referenced addendum attached. Every change initialled and dated by every
party. The version in the file matching the version everyone signed. **Binding acceptance date
recorded and every deadline computed from it.** Every notice given, with the method and the date. The
earnest money receipted and its delivery to the named holder evidenced. **§ 452.135** disclosures
delivered — customers before negotiating on their behalf, clients not later than the agency agreement.
The **ch. 709 condition report** delivered. The **federal lead-based paint package** for pre-1978
housing, with the signed acknowledgment. **Every § 452.133(1)(c) written disclosure of a material
adverse fact**, with the date. Any **§ 452.133(3)(b) or (3)(c)** consent or disclosure, in the
transaction contract where the statute requires it. And the correspondence — because in a dispute the
correspondence is the file.

### Module WI-06 engagement check

**Question.** Your seller client receives three offers. She wants to counter all three at $10,000 above
her list price and take whichever comes back first. The buyer on the second offer told you privately he
would pay $15,000 more than he offered. One of the three buyers is your sister. Work through what you
do.

**Expected answer.**

**Countering all three with ordinary counter-offers would risk three contracts on one property.** The
Wisconsin instrument for this is the **multiple counter-proposal** under **REEB 25.033(8)(f)6**, which
is structured so the seller can respond to more than one buyer without becoming bound to more than one
— typically by making any resulting agreement subject to the seller's further written selection. I
explain the mechanism, use the Board's current approved form, and make sure each buyer's side
understands that acceptance alone does not create a contract.

**The second buyer's $15,000 is confidential and I may not tell my seller.** **§ 452.133(1)(d)**
protects information the firm obtains that it knows a reasonable person would want kept confidential,
and it is owed to **every party**, not only to clients — and it **continues after the transaction is
complete**. My loyalty duty to the seller under § 452.133(2)(a) does not override it: that duty is
expressly about not disclosing to non-clients information contrary to my client's interests, not about
disclosing non-clients' confidences to my client.

**My sister is a prohibited-conduct problem.** **§ 452.133(3)(b)** bars a licensee from acting in a
transaction on behalf of the licensee's **immediate family where the firm is an individual** — and even
where it does not apply on its face, the interest must be disclosed. Written consent of all parties is
required, and the statute prescribes **where**: "a licensee shall obtain the written consent **in the
offer to purchase**, option, lease, or other transaction contract." I raise this with my **supervising
broker before anything else**, because how the firm handles it decides whether the file is defensible.

**And when I present all three**, **§ 452.133(1)(g)** requires me to present each proposal
**objectively and without bias, disclosing the advantages and disadvantages** — including the
disadvantages of my sister's offer and the advantages of the others. **REEB 24.12 and 24.13** require
fair presentation and prompt notification, and the buyers not selected are notified promptly.
