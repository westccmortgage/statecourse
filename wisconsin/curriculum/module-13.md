## Learning objectives

By the end of this module the student will be able to:

- Name each REEB chapter REEB 25.033(19) requires and say what it governs.
- Define a trust account and trust funds, and say when a trust account is required.
- Distinguish interest-bearing from non-interest bearing trust accounts.
- State the practice consequences of Wisconsin's marital property regime for a conveyance.
- Identify a mortgage banking question and route it.
- Distinguish the escrows that require a separate escrow agreement from those that do not.

## Hour 70 — The REEB chapters

### 70.1 What this topic is for

**REEB 25.033(19)** gathers the parts of Wisconsin's regulatory scheme a salesperson meets that do not
sit inside another topic. It is the module that tells a licensee **where things are**, and its value is
navigational.

**This course names each chapter and states what it governs. It does not state the text of any of them**,
because **chs. REEB 15, 16, 17, 18, 23 and 24 have not been read from primary text for this project.** A
student must read them, and an instructor supplementing this module must obtain and date the reading.

### 70.2 Ch. REEB 15 — REEB 25.033(19)(a)

**Ch. REEB 15** governs **licensure** — the requirements, procedures and conditions attaching to a real
estate licence in Wisconsin.

What a salesperson needs from it: how the licence is obtained and renewed; what continuing education is
required and when; what must be reported to the department and within what period; what happens to a
licence on lapse; and the mechanics of association with a firm and of leaving one.

**This course states no education hour count, fee, renewal date or reporting deadline.** They change,
and a licensee's own obligations must be read from ch. REEB 15 and the department's current publication
rather than from a course.

### 70.3 Ch. REEB 16 — REEB 25.033(19)(b)

**Ch. REEB 16** governs the **forms** used in real estate transactions, under the Board's power at
**§ 452.05(1)(b)**. Module WI-06 took the regime: a licensee **completes** an approved form rather than
drafting one, may not alter its printed provisions, and may not use a non-approved form where an approved
one exists.

### 70.4 Ch. REEB 17 — REEB 25.033(19)(c)

**Ch. REEB 17** governs **business practices** — the operational requirements a firm and its licensees
must meet.

The subjects a salesperson should expect to find there and must read: the firm's **office and
supervision** requirements; **records** — what must be kept, in what form, and **for how long**; the
handling of documents and copies to the parties; and the operational obligations that sit alongside the
conduct rules in ch. REEB 24.

**This course states no record retention period**, because ch. REEB 17 has not been read. It is exactly
the kind of figure a course invents by carrying it from another state, and a licensee must read the
current rule.

### 70.5 Ch. REEB 23 — REEB 25.033(19)(e)

**Ch. REEB 23** governs **disciplinary proceedings and the Board's disciplinary authority** — the
procedural chapter behind **§ 452.14**, which Module WI-10 Hour 62.1 set out.

What a licensee carries: **discipline is a real process with notice, a response period, and a hearing
right**, and the correct response to a complaint is to notify the supervising broker immediately,
preserve every record and change nothing, respond within the time given, and obtain counsel.

## Hour 71 — Ch. REEB 18: trust accounts and trust funds

### 71.1 The statutory anchor

**§ 452.133(1)(f)** — owed to **every party** — is the duty to **safeguard trust funds and other property
held as required by rules promulgated under s. 452.13(5)**. **Ch. REEB 18** is those rules.

**REEB 25.033(19)(d)** requires instruction on ch. REEB 18 and names five elements. This course teaches
what each is about and what a licensee does; **it states no requirement from the chapter's text.**

### 71.2 Definition of a trust account — REEB 25.033(19)(d)1

A **trust account** is an account maintained by a firm at a financial institution, **separate from the
firm's own operating funds**, into which money belonging to others and received in connection with real
estate transactions is deposited.

Its defining feature is that **the money in it is not the firm's.** Everything else about the rules
follows from that.

The two failures the entire scheme exists to prevent:

**Commingling** — mixing trust money with the firm's operating funds. It is not an accounting untidiness;
it destroys the separation the account exists to create.

**Conversion** — using trust money for any purpose other than the one it was entrusted for. A firm that
pays its rent from the trust account has converted client and customer funds, whatever the intention and
whatever was repaid.

### 71.3 Interest-bearing and non-interest bearing — REEB 25.033(19)(d)2

The rule requires instruction on both. The substantive questions a licensee must be able to identify:
**whether an account bears interest**; **who is entitled to the interest**, which is not automatically the
firm; **what the parties may agree**; and **what documentation is required.**

**This course states no rule about which account must be used or to whom interest belongs**, because
ch. REEB 18 has not been read. What a licensee must never do is **assume the interest belongs to the
firm.**

### 71.4 Definition of trust funds — REEB 25.033(19)(d)3

**Trust funds** are money belonging to others that comes into the firm's hands in connection with a real
estate transaction. In practice: **earnest money**; **rents collected for an owner**; **tenant security
deposits**; money advanced for expenses of a transaction; and other money the firm holds for a party.

**§ 452.133(1)(f) makes safeguarding them a duty owed to every party**, so the tenant whose deposit the
firm holds is owed it as much as the owner is.

### 71.5 When a trust account is required — REEB 25.033(19)(d)4

The rule requires instruction on **when** an account is required, which implies circumstances in which
one is not — for example where a firm never holds trust funds because another party always does.

**This course states no threshold or exception**, because the chapter has not been read. The operational
consequence a salesperson must know: **if you are handed money that is not the firm's, you must know
where it goes and by when, before you take it.**

### 71.6 Deposit of trust funds — REEB 25.033(19)(d)5

The rule requires instruction on the **deposit** of trust funds, which in every state's scheme means:
**by when**, **into which account**, and **with what record**.

**This course states no deadline.** What it states is the discipline, which does not depend on the
figure:

**A cheque received is delivered promptly** to the account or to the holder the contract names. A cheque
carried in a bag over a weekend is a problem in the making, and a cheque held to "see whether the deal
firms up" is worse.

**Every dollar is traceable**: from whom it was received, when, into which account it was deposited,
when, and its final disposition.

**Every owner and every deposit has its own running balance**, and the account's sufficiency is measured
against the **sum of the ledgers**, not the bank balance — Module WI-11 Hour 66.3.

**Reconcile monthly** and retain the reconciliation.

**A disputed deposit is never resolved by the licensee choosing a side.** It goes to the supervising
broker and, where necessary, to counsel, and it is disbursed only as the contract and the rules permit.

## Hour 72 — Marital property, mortgage banking, and escrow

### 72.1 Property rights of married persons — REEB 25.033(19)(f)

The rule requires instruction on **ss. 766.31, 766.51, 766.60 and 766.63**, and **REEB 25.033(5)(k)**
separately requires **homestead under ss. 706.01(7) and 766.605**.

**Wisconsin is a marital property state.** Ch. 766 is a community-property-style regime whose general
effect is that property acquired by spouses during marriage is presumptively **marital property** in
which each spouse holds an undivided one-half interest — subject to classification rules, **individual
property** categories, **marital property agreements**, and the **survivorship marital property** form.

**Ch. 766 has not been read from primary text for this project and this course states no rule from it.**
What it states is the practice consequence, which is unavoidable:

**A spouse may hold an interest in property, and a spouse's signature may be required to convey or to
encumber it, even where that spouse does not appear on the deed.** The **homestead** provisions make this
particularly acute for a residence, and **§ 706.02** requires the signature of each grantor.

**So the question is asked at listing, every time:** is the seller married; is the property the
homestead; is there a **marital property agreement**; and **who must sign?**

**And it is answered by the title company, the closing agent and counsel — never by the licensee.** A
Wisconsin listing signed by one spouse alone on a homestead may not be performable, and the time to
discover that is at intake.

### 72.2 Mortgage banking under s. 224.71 — REEB 25.033(19)(g)

**s. 224.71** sits in Wisconsin's mortgage banking provisions, which license and regulate **mortgage
bankers, mortgage brokers and mortgage loan originators**, alongside the federal **SAFE Act** framework.

Why a real estate salesperson needs it: **mortgage origination is a separately licensed activity.** A
real estate licensee who advises on loan products, takes an application, negotiates loan terms, or is
compensated for placing a loan may be engaging in it.

The boundary a salesperson holds: **describe categories, refer to lenders, and take nothing of value for
a referral without complying with § 452.133(3)(a) and (3)(c)** — no compensation from anyone other than
the licensee's client or firm without the **prior written consent of all parties**, and **written
disclosure** of any referral interest.

**This course states no requirement from s. 224.71**, which has not been read from primary text for this
project.

### 72.3 Escrow agreement procedures — REEB 25.033(19)(h)

The rule names five elements, and the distinction running through all of them is **when a separate escrow
agreement is required and when the transaction contract itself suffices.**

**1. Escrows requiring separate escrow agreements.** Where money is held for a purpose the transaction
contract does not itself provide for and govern, a **separate written escrow agreement** is needed —
naming the escrow agent, the funds, the condition, who decides whether it is met, the deadline, and what
happens to the money in each outcome.

**2. Pre-closing earnest money escrows.** Earnest money held between binding acceptance and closing.
Wisconsin's **approved offer** governs its holding and disbursement, so a separate agreement is generally
not required — the contract is the agreement.

**3. Post-closing escrows.** Money held **after** closing for work not completed, a condition not
satisfied, a repair pending, or an obligation continuing. **These almost always need their own written
agreement**, because the transaction contract has performed and no longer governs, and because the
parties' expectations at closing are usually less aligned than they believe.

**4. Escrows not requiring a separate escrow agreement.** Where the transaction contract already provides
the terms — the ordinary earnest money case — a separate agreement adds nothing.

**5. Drafting escrow agreements.** Which is the point at which a licensee stops. **Drafting an escrow
agreement is legal work.** A licensee identifies that an escrow is needed, identifies the questions it
must answer, and routes it to the **closing agent and counsel** — and where a Board-approved form covers
the situation, the approved form is used.

The four questions every escrow must answer, and which a licensee should be able to list: **what money**;
**held by whom**; **released on what condition, determined by whom**; and **what happens if the condition
is not met by a stated date.** An escrow that does not answer all four is an escrow that will be
disputed.

### 72.4 What a Wisconsin salesperson carries out of 72 hours

**The duties in § 452.133(1) are owed to every party**, client or not: honestly and fairly; reasonable
skill and care; **written** disclosure of material adverse facts the firm knows and the party cannot
reasonably discover; **confidentiality that continues after the transaction is complete**; accurate
market information on request; safeguarding trust funds; and objective presentation of proposals with
their advantages and disadvantages.

**Clients get five more** under § 452.133(2), of which **(am)** is the one that decides what a licensee
may advise on at all: matters **within the scope of the knowledge, skills and training required under
ch. 452**.

**§ 452.133(3) prohibits four things**, three of which are cured by **written consent or disclosure
obtained in advance** — and for self-dealing, obtained **in the transaction contract itself**.

**Wisconsin runs on Board-approved forms.** Complete them; do not draft.

**Wisconsin is a marital property state, a mortgage state with judicial foreclosure, a public land survey
state, and a state whose fair housing list is broader than the federal one** — and every one of those is
a place where a licensee arriving from elsewhere will be wrong unless they translate.

**And read the section, not a summary of it.** Everything this course could not read from primary text
was left unstated and the source named instead, because a remembered figure is the thing that goes wrong.

### Module WI-13 engagement check

**Question.** At closing the buyer will not accept the property because the seller has not finished a
deck the parties agreed would be complete. Everyone wants to close anyway and hold back $9,000 until it
is done. The seller is married; the wife signed nothing because "it's his rental property, not the
homestead". And your firm is asked to hold the $9,000. Work through it.

**Expected answer.**

**A holdback after closing is a post-closing escrow**, and it **needs its own written escrow agreement** —
the transaction contract has performed and no longer governs. The agreement must answer four questions:
**what money; held by whom; released on what condition and determined by whom; and what happens if the
deck is not finished by a stated date.** **Drafting it is legal work**, so it goes to the **closing agent
and counsel**, and if a Board-approved form covers it, that form is used. I identify the need and the
questions; I do not draft.

**If my firm holds the money it is trust money.** **§ 452.133(1)(f)** requires it to be safeguarded as
the rules under **s. 452.13(5)** — **ch. REEB 18** — require, in the firm's **trust account**, separate
from operating funds, with its own ledger and a traceable record from receipt to disposition. And if the
parties later disagree about whether the deck was finished, **the firm does not pick a side**: it goes to
the supervising broker and, where necessary, to counsel.

**The wife's position is not the seller's to characterise.** Wisconsin is a **marital property state**
under **ch. 766**, and property acquired during marriage may be **marital property** in which each spouse
holds an undivided one-half interest **regardless of whose name is on the deed** — and **§ 706.02**
requires the signature of each grantor. That the property is a rental rather than the homestead changes
the **homestead** analysis but does not by itself answer the marital property question. **The title
company and counsel decide it**, and it should have been raised at listing.

**And whatever I actually know about the deck, the holdback and the signature question, I disclose in
writing** under **§ 452.133(1)(c)** — to every party, not only to my client.
