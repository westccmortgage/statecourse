## Hour 67 — Public controls, planning, zoning, and codes

### 67.1 Public controls — REEB 25.033(17)(a)

Every public limitation traces to the four powers of Module WI-04 Hour 18.1. **Police power** is the
source of everything in this module: zoning, subdivision control, building codes, shoreland and
floodplain regulation, and environmental permitting — none of which is compensated, because use is
regulated rather than taken.

Wisconsin delegates the power to **counties, cities, villages and towns**, and the allocation between
them is itself a Wisconsin feature: **county zoning** governs in many unincorporated areas, **town**
authority varies with whether the town has adopted village powers and whether the county's ordinance
applies, and **shoreland zoning** is **county-administered** even inside some municipal boundaries.

**So the first question about any Wisconsin parcel is: which body regulates it?** A licensee who calls
the city about a property in the town has called the wrong office.

### 67.2 The comprehensive plan — REEB 25.033(17)(b)

**Amended by CR 23-075**, effective 9-1-24, and the version taught is the amended one.

Wisconsin requires local governments that exercise land-use authority to act **consistently with a
comprehensive plan** containing prescribed elements — housing, transportation, utilities and community
facilities, agricultural and natural resources, economic development, intergovernmental cooperation,
land use, and implementation.

Two consequences for brokerage. The plan is a **public document** stating where a community intends
growth, infrastructure and change — which is information a buyer of land or of a property whose value
depends on its surroundings should have. And **zoning must be consistent with it**, which makes the plan
evidence about the likelihood of a rezoning succeeding.

### 67.3 Zoning — REEB 25.033(17)(c)

Zoning divides a jurisdiction into districts and prescribes for each the permitted **uses** and the
**bulk** controls — minimum lot size, width and frontage, setbacks, height, lot coverage, impervious
surface, parking and often signage.

**The relief mechanisms, distinguished:**

A **variance** relieves a **dimensional** requirement where strict application causes **unnecessary
hardship** arising from the property itself — shape, topography, soils. It is granted by the **board of
adjustment or appeals**. **A variance does not authorise a use the district forbids**, and self-created
hardship and mere financial disadvantage are not grounds. Wisconsin's unnecessary-hardship standard is
applied strictly, and **this course states no element of it**.

A **conditional use permit** authorises a use the ordinance **lists as permissible subject to review and
conditions**. It is the ordinance operating as written, not an exception to it.

A **nonconforming use or structure** was lawful when established and became non-compliant on a later
ordinance change. It may generally continue, typically may not be **expanded**, and may be lost on
**abandonment or destruction**.

**In Wisconsin the nonconforming-structure question is acute on waterfront**, where a very large share of
the shoreland housing stock predates current setbacks. What may be done to such a structure — expanded,
replaced, rebuilt after a loss, relocated — is governed by **county shoreland zoning** and by state
minimum standards, and the answer differs between counties.

A **rezoning** amends the ordinance itself and is a **legislative** act of the governing body.

### 67.4 Building codes — REEB 25.033(17)(f)

Wisconsin administers a **statewide uniform dwelling code** for one- and two-family dwellings, and a
commercial building code, through the Department of Safety and Professional Services and delegated
municipal inspectors.

Practical consequences: **new construction requires permits and inspections**, and the **certificate of
occupancy** (where issued) is the document saying the building may lawfully be occupied. **An unpermitted
addition** is a code problem, a title and insurance problem, and a financing problem at once — and it is
discovered from the **building inspector's file**, not from the seller.

### 67.5 The licensee's boundary

Every question in this hour is answered by a public official and recorded in a public office. The
licensee's job is to know **that the question exists**, know **who answers it**, and send the client
there **in writing**.

**§ 452.133(2)(am)** limits advice to matters within the scope of the knowledge, skills and training
required under ch. 452, and zoning is outside it. **REEB 24.03**, competence in area of service, says the
same as a rule. Answering a zoning question from memory is how licensees create liability.

## Hour 68 — Subdivision, development, and private controls

### 68.1 Subdivision regulation — REEB 25.033(17)(d)

Wisconsin regulates the division of land at state and local level. **Ch. 236** is the state platting
statute, and municipalities and counties administer their own subdivision ordinances alongside it.

Two instruments a licensee must distinguish:

**A subdivision plat** — the instrument creating a subdivision as the statute defines it, reviewed by the
local approving authorities and by state agencies, and **recorded**.

**A certified survey map (CSM)** — Wisconsin's instrument for **smaller divisions**, prepared by a
surveyor, approved locally, and **recorded**. A great many Wisconsin parcels are described by reference
to a CSM, and a CSM is the ordinary route for splitting off a lot from a larger parcel.

**A lot does not exist until the plat or CSM creating it is recorded**, and **an unlawful division is a
title and marketability problem** — which is why a seller asking whether they can "just split off five
acres" is asking a question for the municipality, the county, and a surveyor, in writing, and never for
the licensee.

**This course states no lot count, acreage threshold, or time period from ch. 236 or from any local
ordinance**, because they have not been read from primary text for this project.

### 68.2 Land development and subdividing — REEB 25.033(17)(g) and (h)

The sequence: concept and feasibility; **comprehensive plan consistency and zoning**; preliminary plat or
CSM; **engineering and public improvement plans** for streets, water, sewer and stormwater; **state and
county permits** — erosion control, stormwater, wetland, floodplain, driveway access; **construction of
improvements**, usually secured by a bond or letter of credit; **final plat or CSM recorded**; and
**dedication** of streets and utilities.

The feasibility questions that decide a Wisconsin land deal, each with its office: **zoning and permitted
use** — the municipality or county, in writing; **subdivision status** — the approving authority;
**wastewater capacity**, which on unsewered land means **soil testing and a septic permit** through the
county; **water supply**; **access**, including a **driveway or entrance permit** from the town, county
or state highway authority; **wetlands, floodplain and shoreland**; **farmland preservation zoning**;
and **utility availability**, where "available" is not the same as "permitted".

### 68.3 Private land-use controls — REEB 25.033(17)(e)

Module WI-04 Hour 19.4 set them out: **restrictive covenants**, recorded so they bind successors,
enforced **privately** by an association or a neighbour rather than by the municipality, and capable of
being **stricter than zoning** — in which case the owner must satisfy both.

**Discriminatory covenants are void and must not be honoured.**

**Associations** — condominium, planned community, road and lake associations — hold both the enforcement
power and the assessment power, and a buyer needs the documents, the budget, the reserve position, the
minutes and the assessment history.

## Hour 69 — Environmental concerns

### 69.1 The eleven topics — REEB 25.033(18)

The rule requires instruction on **radon**, **asbestos**, **lead-based paint**, **toxic waste**,
**underground storage tanks under ch. ATCP 93**, **floodplains under s. 87.30 Stats. and ss. NR 116.01
and 116.06**, **flood insurance**, **wetlands under s. 23.32**, **farmland preservation under ss. 91.01
and 91.60 to 91.70**, and **disclosure documents** under **REEB 24.07** and ch. 709.
**Paragraph (18)(j) was repealed by CR 19-128**, effective 2-1-21, and is not taught.

**None of ATCP 93, s. 87.30, NR 116, s. 23.32 or ch. 91 has been read from primary text for this
project, and no requirement, threshold, distance or exemption from any of them is stated.** What follows
is what each topic is, why it matters in Wisconsin, and **who answers it**.

### 69.2 The topics, and the routing

**Radon — (18)(a).** A naturally occurring gas, common in Wisconsin soils, measured by testing and
addressed by mitigation, which is a defined trade. **Test in the inspection period; a certified tester
answers it.**

**Asbestos — (18)(b).** In insulation, flooring, siding, pipe wrap and textured finishes in older
housing. **A qualified assessor answers it, and disturbing it is regulated work.**

**Lead-based paint — (18)(c).** **Target housing built before 1978**: federal disclosure of known paint
and hazards, available records and reports, the EPA pamphlet, the prescribed contract warning and
acknowledgments, and — **for sales** — a **ten-day** opportunity to conduct a risk assessment or
inspection unless the parties agree otherwise in writing. The federal **Renovation, Repair and Painting**
rule requires certified firms for disturbing painted surfaces in target housing.

**Toxic waste — (18)(d).** Contamination from historic industrial, agricultural or commercial use.
Wisconsin's remediation and closure programmes are administered by the **Department of Natural
Resources**, and a property with a known or suspected release is an environmental consultant's and
counsel's question — never a licensee's.

**Underground storage tanks under ch. ATCP 93 — (18)(e).** Buried heating oil and fuel tanks are a
Wisconsin legacy issue. An abandoned tank never properly removed or closed is an environmental liability
attaching to the property. **The Department of Agriculture, Trade and Consumer Protection administers
ATCP 93**, and a suspected tank goes there and to a qualified contractor.

**Floodplains — (18)(f).** **s. 87.30 Stats.** and **NR 116.01 and 116.06** are the state floodplain
provisions; municipalities administer floodplain zoning. **Whether a parcel is in a mapped floodplain,
and what may be built or altered there, is answered by the municipality and by the mapping — in
writing.**

**Flood insurance — (18)(g).** **Flood is excluded from a standard homeowners policy.** Coverage comes
from the **National Flood Insurance Program** or the private market. A property in a **Special Flood
Hazard Area** faces a **lender requirement** on a federally regulated or insured loan. **Maps change**,
being outside a mapped area is not the same as not flooding, and NFIP coverage has limits and a waiting
period. An insurance professional and FEMA's mapping answer it.

**Wetlands — (18)(h).** **s. 23.32** is the wetland provision, and the **Department of Natural
Resources** administers wetland identification and permitting. A wetland can make a physically buildable
parcel unbuildable, and its presence is answered by mapping and by a **wetland delineation**.

**Farmland preservation — (18)(i).** **ss. 91.01 and 91.60 to 91.70.** Wisconsin's farmland preservation
programme ties **county farmland preservation zoning** and **farmland preservation agreements** to
income tax credits, and it restricts non-agricultural use and division of enrolled land. **The county
land conservation department and the Department of Agriculture, Trade and Consumer Protection answer
it**, and a buyer of enrolled land must understand the restriction and any consequence of withdrawal
**before closing** — which makes it a **contract term**, not an afterthought.

**Disclosure documents — (18)(k).** **REEB 24.07** and **ch. 709**, from Module WI-10 Hours 61 and 62.3.

### 69.3 The rule that governs the whole hour

**The licensee is not the person who determines an environmental condition.** The licensee is the person
who **knows the question exists, discloses what the firm actually knows, and routes the question to the
office or professional who answers it — in writing, with the contingency deadline stated.**

**§ 452.133(1)(c)** requires timely **written** disclosure of material adverse facts the firm knows and
the party cannot discover through reasonably vigilant observation. **REEB 24.07(3)** requires action on
information **suggesting** an adverse material fact. And **§ 452.133(2)(am)** and **REEB 24.03** together
mean that a licensee who characterises a condition has stepped outside their competence.

### Module WI-12 engagement check

**Question.** A buyer is under contract on 22 acres with a 1961 farmhouse, an old barn, a pole shed with
a rusted tank behind it, and a low wet area at the back that "dries out by July". The parcel is in county
farmland preservation zoning and the buyer intends to split off five acres for a second house. Build the
investigation list.

**Expected answer.** Every line names the question, the office, and the document.

**The split.** Whether five acres can be divided off is a **subdivision** question under **ch. 236** and
the county or town ordinance, and it is compounded by **farmland preservation zoning** under
**ss. 91.01 and 91.60 to 91.70**, which restricts non-agricultural use and division of enrolled land.
**County zoning, the county land conservation department, and DATCP — in writing.** And the answer may
carry a consequence for any **farmland preservation agreement** and its tax credits, which makes it a
**contract term** the buyer must resolve before closing.

**The wet area.** Potential **wetland** under **s. 23.32**, answered by DNR mapping and, if it matters, a
**wetland delineation**. A wetland can make the intended building site unbuildable, and "dries out by
July" is not a determination.

**The rusted tank.** Potential **underground or above-ground storage tank** under **ch. ATCP 93** —
DATCP and a qualified contractor — and, if there has been a release, a **DNR** remediation question and
an environmental consultant.

**The 1961 farmhouse.** Pre-1978 **target housing**: the federal **lead-based paint** disclosure,
records, pamphlet, contract acknowledgments, and the **ten-day** assessment opportunity. Plus
**asbestos** and **radon** testing in the inspection period.

**The second house.** **Wastewater** — soil testing and a **septic permit** through the county — and
**water supply**, **driveway access** and a possible **entrance permit**, and **zoning** for a second
dwelling on the parcel.

**Floodplain** status under **s. 87.30** and **NR 116** from the municipality and the mapping, and
**flood insurance** availability from an insurance professional.

**And what I disclose:** whatever the firm actually knows, **in writing**, under **§ 452.133(1)(c)** —
and I act on the rusted tank and the wet area as information **suggesting** adverse material facts under
**REEB 24.07(3)** rather than treating the seller's account of them as an answer.
