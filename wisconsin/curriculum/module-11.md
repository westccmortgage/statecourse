- Identify the risk-management controls a managed property requires.

## Hour 63 — Leasehold estates and lease documents

### 63.1 The four leasehold estates — REEB 25.033(15)(a)

**Estate for years** — a fixed term with a definite beginning and end. It **expires without notice**, and
its length is irrelevant to the name: a two-week lease is an estate for years.

**Periodic estate** — renews automatically for successive periods until one party gives notice.
Month-to-month tenancies are the common case.

**Estate at will** — possession with consent, no fixed period, terminable by either party subject to
statutory notice.

**Estate at sufferance** — a tenant who entered lawfully and stayed past the term without consent.
Distinguished from trespass only by the lawful entry, and removing such a tenant is a **legal process**,
not a self-help matter.

### 63.2 A lease is two things at once — REEB 25.033(15)(d)

A lease is a **conveyance** — it transfers a leasehold estate and the right of exclusive possession —
and a **contract** — it creates mutual promises. The first explains why a tenant may exclude even the
landlord except as the lease and the law allow; the second explains how breach is analysed.

A lease of stated length is a **conveyance** for the purposes of **ch. 706**, which is why the writing
and signature requirements Module WI-07 named reach leases as well as deeds.

### 63.3 Standard provisions — REEB 25.033(15)(b) and lease documents (15)(c)

A Wisconsin residential lease settles: the **parties**; the **premises**, including any storage, parking
or garage; the **term**; the **rent**, its amount, due date, place and method, and any late fee; the
**security deposit** and its treatment; **utilities**, and in a Wisconsin climate specifically **who pays
for heat**; **permitted use and occupancy limits**; **maintenance** responsibilities on each side; the
**landlord's right of entry** and the notice required; rules on **pets** — and separately the treatment
of **assistance animals**, which are not pets; **snow removal and lawn care**, allocated expressly;
**alterations**; **assignment and subletting**; **default and remedies**; **renewal or termination**; and
the **required disclosures**.

**Two Wisconsin documents belong with the lease** and are governed by ATCP 134, which Hour 65 takes:
the **check-in and check-out condition report** the tenant is entitled to complete, and the
**nonstandard rental provisions** document by which certain terms must be separately identified and
agreed rather than buried in the lease.

## Hour 64 — Legal principles, improvements, maintenance, breach

### 64.1 Assignment and sublease

**Assignment** transfers the tenant's **entire** remaining interest; the assignee comes into direct
relationship with the landlord. **Sublease** transfers **less than** the entire remaining interest — a
shorter period or part of the premises — and the original tenant becomes a landlord to the subtenant
while remaining a tenant to the landlord.

**In neither case is the original tenant automatically released.** Release requires the landlord's
agreement, which is a **novation**. A tenant who "assigned the lease and moved out" remains liable unless
the landlord released them in writing.

Most leases restrict both and require consent.

### 64.2 Improvements — REEB 25.033(15)(e)

**Trade fixtures** installed by a tenant for the tenant's trade or business remain the tenant's personal
property and must be removed before the term ends — after which they generally pass to the landlord by
**accession**.

**Tenant improvements** in a commercial lease are negotiated: who builds, who pays, what allowance the
landlord gives, what standard applies, and what happens at the end of the term.

**Alterations in a residential tenancy** are governed by the lease — and by the **reasonable
modification** rules from Module WI-10 Hour 57.2 where a disability is involved, under which the change
is at the tenant's expense and the landlord may where reasonable require restoration.

### 64.3 Maintenance — REEB 25.033(15)(f)

The allocation is a lease term sitting on a statutory floor. The landlord's obligations as to the
condition of the premises, and the tenant's as to their own conduct and the unit's care, are set by
Wisconsin law and cannot be reduced below the floor by agreement.

**This course states no Wisconsin habitability standard or repair obligation**, because the governing
provisions have not been read from primary text for this project.

What is certain and belongs to practice: **the record of what was reported and what was done is the
whole of the defence** in a habitability dispute. A request-to-completion tracking system with defined
response times, vendor accountability, and an escalation trigger when a scheduled visit does not happen
is not administration — it is the evidence.

### 64.4 Breach — REEB 25.033(15)(g)

**Self-help is unlawful.** Changing locks, removing possessions, or shutting off utilities to force a
tenant out is prohibited, and a licensee who advises a landlord client to do any of it has advised a
violation — against **§ 452.133(1)(b)**'s reasonable skill and care and against the licensee's duty to
comply with the law.

Possession is recovered through a **statutory judicial process**, and **this course states no notice
period, no cure right and no procedure**, because the provisions have not been read from primary text
for this project. They must be read from the current statutes, and where the consequence of getting it
wrong is the loss of a remedy, confirmed with counsel.

**A tenant's remedies** for a landlord's breach likewise come from statute and from the lease, and a
licensee does not advise on whether rent may be withheld.

## Hour 65 — Residential rental practices under ATCP 134

### 65.1 What ATCP 134 is — REEB 25.033(15)(h)

**Ch. ATCP 134** is the Wisconsin Department of Agriculture, Trade and Consumer Protection's rule on
**residential rental practices**, and REEB 25.033(15)(h) requires instruction on **ATCP 134.02, 134.06
and 134.09** specifically.

It is a **consumer protection rule**, which means two things a licensee must understand: a violation can
be enforced by DATCP and can support a private action, **independently of** anything the Real Estate
Examining Board does; and its requirements apply to a residential rental whether or not a licensee is
involved.

**ATCP 134 has not been read from primary text for this project, and this course states no requirement,
deadline, amount or form from it.** What follows is what each cited section governs and what a licensee
must therefore do.

### 65.2 The three sections the rule names

**ATCP 134.02 — definitions.** The section that decides **what the rule applies to** and what its terms
mean. Scope questions — whether a particular arrangement is a residential tenancy within the rule — are
answered here first.

**ATCP 134.06 — security deposits.** The section governing **the handling and return of security
deposits**, including what may be withheld, what accounting the tenant is entitled to, and the timing.
Security deposit disputes are the single most common residential rental complaint, and the rule is
prescriptive.

**ATCP 134.09 — prohibited practices.** The section listing practices a landlord may not engage in. It
is the operative list a property manager measures their own conduct against.

### 65.3 What ATCP 134 requires a licensee to do

**Read it.** A Wisconsin property manager who has not read ATCP 134.02, 134.06 and 134.09 in current
text is managing without knowing the rules that generate most of the complaints.

**Use compliant documents.** The check-in and check-out condition report, the nonstandard rental
provisions document, and the required disclosures are ATCP 134's, and using an out-of-state lease form
in Wisconsin is how a manager creates violations wholesale.

**Handle deposits by the rule, not by habit.** The amount that may be withheld, the itemisation the
tenant receives, and the deadline are prescribed. **A remembered figure is how a landlord loses.**

**Screen by written, objective, uniform criteria** applied identically — which is the fair housing
answer from Module WI-10 Hour 57.5 and the consumer-protection answer at the same time.

**And never advise on a specific ATCP question from memory.** The method: classify the tenancy, read the
current section, read the lease, and refer the legal conclusion.

## Hour 66 — Property management

### 66.1 Functions of a property manager — REEB 25.033(16)(a)

A property manager is engaged to **operate real property**: to produce the income the owner wants, to
preserve the asset, and to do both within the law.

The functions: **leasing** — marketing, screening, and executing leases; **rent collection** and
delinquency management; **maintenance**, routine and emergency; **vendor management**; **accounting and
reporting** to the owner; **budgeting**; **compliance** with fair housing, ATCP 134, local ordinances and
the licensing law; **risk management**; and **records**.

**Property management is brokerage.** A person managing real property for another for compensation is
providing brokerage services, and the firm's **§ 452.133** duties apply — including the seven owed to
**every party**, which in a managed property means **the tenants as well as the owner.**

That is worth stating plainly, because managers frequently assume the tenant is nobody to them. In
Wisconsin the tenant is a **party**, owed honesty and fairness, reasonable skill and care, written
disclosure of material adverse facts, confidentiality that survives, accurate market information,
safeguarding of trust funds, and objective presentation of proposals.

### 66.2 The management agreement — REEB 25.033(16)(b)

It is an **agency agreement**, so **§ 452.135(2)(a)** requires the **Disclosure to Clients** **not later
than the time the firm enters into it**.

Its essential terms: the **parties and the property**; the **term**; the **scope of authority** — what
the manager may spend without approval and the threshold above which the owner decides; **leasing
authority** and the criteria to be applied; **rent collection and enforcement** authority; **maintenance
and emergency** authority; **compensation** — the management fee, any leasing fee, and how each is
calculated and paid; the **reporting package** and its frequency; **insurance** requirements on both
sides; **indemnification**; **handling of the owner's funds and of tenant deposits**; and
**termination**.

### 66.3 Trust accounting — where managers lose licences

**§ 452.133(1)(f)** requires the firm to **safeguard trust funds and other property held**, as required
by rules under **s. 452.13(5)**, and **ch. REEB 18** governs trust accounts — Module WI-13.

Three rules a manager follows without exception:

**One owner's money is never used for another owner's property.** A pooled trust account may be
permitted; a **pooled ledger** is not. Every owner and every deposit has its own running balance, and
sufficiency is measured against the **sum of the ledgers**, not against the bank balance.

**Security deposits are the tenant's money held in trust**, not the owner's income, and they are not
available to fund operations.

**Reconcile monthly** — bank statement to book balance to the sum of the individual ledgers — and retain
the reconciliation. A reconciliation that has never been performed is what an audit finds.

### 66.4 Management considerations and the operating statement — REEB 25.033(16)(c)

The manager builds an annual budget on the Module WI-09 structure: **potential gross income**, less
**vacancy and collection loss**, giving **effective gross income**; less **operating expenses**, giving
**net operating income**. **Debt service and capital expenditures sit outside NOI**; **replacement
reserves** are inside it.

Reporting to the owner, monthly: rent roll and occupancy; collections and delinquency; income and
expense against budget; the **trust account balance and the owner's ledger**; maintenance completed and
outstanding; and lease expirations coming up.

**Treating a capital item as an operating expense understates NOI and misstates value**, which is why
the distinction matters beyond bookkeeping.

### 66.5 Renting and maintaining the property — REEB 25.033(16)(d)

**Marketing** under **§ 452.136** and REEB 24.04, with the fair housing rule from Module WI-10 Hour
57.5: describe the property, never the occupants.

**Screening** by written, objective, uniform criteria applied identically to every applicant, documented
for each — the only defensible practice under both fair housing law and ATCP 134.

**Leasing** on documents that comply with ATCP 134, with the check-in condition report completed.

**Maintenance** on a tracked request-to-completion path with defined response times, current vendor
insurance certificates on file, and an escalation trigger.

**Turnover** with a documented check-out inspection and a deposit accounting that follows **ATCP
134.06** rather than habit.

### 66.6 Risk management — REEB 25.033(16)(e)

**Written policies**, and a manager who knows what the firm's are.

**Documentation**, contemporaneous rather than reconstructed. A decision log entry records the date and
time, who said what by what channel, what was decided, **on what authority** — statute, rule, lease,
management agreement, or the owner's written instruction — who was notified, and the next step and its
date.

**Insurance** — the owner's property and liability coverage, the manager's own errors and omissions
coverage, and vendor certificates.

**Unlicensed assistants** — who may do administration and may not perform licensed activity.

**Escalation** to the supervising broker that people actually use.

**And the duty that most often surprises a manager: § 452.133(1)(c) is owed to tenants.** A material
adverse fact about the physical condition of the property that the firm knows and a prospective tenant
cannot discover through reasonably vigilant observation must be **timely disclosed in writing** — to
them, not only to the owner.

### Module WI-11 engagement check

**Question.** You manage a twelve-unit building. A tenant reports that the furnace in unit 6 is failing;
you schedule a vendor who does not attend, and nothing further happens for three weeks. The owner's
ledger holds $1,900 and the replacement quote is $6,400. The owner is unreachable. A prospective tenant
for unit 9 asks whether the building has any problems. Work through it.

**Expected answer.**

**The maintenance failure is the case.** What was reported, when, by what channel, what was done, and
when — and a text message seen and not logged is the failure that decides a habitability dispute. I log
everything retrospectively with dates and sources, get the heat working, write to the owner, and escalate
to my **supervising broker**. Whether the tenant has a remedy is a **legal** question I do not answer.

**I may not pay the $6,400 from the trust account.** The owner's ledger holds $1,900, and spending
$4,500 of other owners' and tenants' money is **conversion** — the trust account's balance must be
sufficient at all times against the **sum of the ledgers**, not the bank balance. **§ 452.133(1)(f)**
requires trust funds to be safeguarded as the rules under **s. 452.13(5)** require. The management
agreement should have fixed the emergency spending threshold and the procedure in advance; if it has
not, that is the conversation to have with the owner and the broker now.

**The prospective tenant for unit 9 is a party**, and **§ 452.133(1)(c)** requires the firm to **timely
disclose in writing** material adverse facts the firm knows that the party cannot discover through
reasonably vigilant observation. A failing furnace in unit 6 is not necessarily a material adverse fact
about unit 9 — but a building-wide heating problem, a systemic maintenance backlog, or anything
affecting unit 9's habitability is, and I must not answer the question with a reassurance I cannot
support. **§ 452.133(1)(a)** requires honesty and fairness to every party, and the tenant is one.

**And the screening of that prospective tenant** proceeds on the firm's **written, objective, uniform
criteria**, applied identically, documented — under fair housing law and under **ATCP 134.09**, which I
read in current text rather than recall.
