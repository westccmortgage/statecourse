# Wisconsin Salesperson Pre-License — Module WI-03

## Hours 13–17 · Listing agreements

**Board topic covered:** REEB 25.033(4) Listing agreements
**Clock hours:** 5 · **Hour allocation:** a StateCourse proposal for Board review; REEB 25.033
allocates no hours among its nineteen topics.
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No Wisconsin pre-license credit is available.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module the student will be able to:

- State what a listing contract is in Wisconsin and where its form comes from.
- Distinguish exclusive right to sell, exclusive agency, and open listings, and say what each pays.
- Identify the special provisions a Wisconsin listing carries and what each allocates.
- Explain how a listing terminates and what survives it.
- Describe a defensible pricing presentation and the two rules that bound it.
- Identify the disclosures that attach at listing and route each one.

## Hour 13 — Listing property and obtaining listings

### 13.1 What a listing is

A **listing contract** is the agency agreement between a seller and a **firm**. It is what makes the
seller a **client** rather than a party, and it is therefore what triggers the additional duties in
**§ 452.133(2)** on top of the seven owed to everyone.

Three consequences follow immediately:

**The client disclosure timing.** **§ 452.135(2)(a)** requires the firm to provide the client with
the written **Disclosure to Clients** statement **not later than the time the firm enters into an
agency agreement with the client.** Not at the first offer. Not afterwards.

**The firm is the party to it.** Wisconsin's duties run to and from the firm; the salesperson signs on
the firm's behalf.

**The form is prescribed.** REEB 25.033(4)(b) requires instruction on "listing agreements under ch.
REEB 16", and REEB 25.033(8)(f)1 lists listing agreements among the **forms used in real estate under
ch. REEB 16**. Wisconsin brokerage runs on **Board-approved forms**, and Module WI-06 takes the form
regime in full.

### 13.2 Obtaining listings — REEB 25.033(4)(e)

The rule requires instruction on how listings are obtained, and the professional content is mostly
about the boundaries.

**Prospecting and solicitation** are governed by federal telephone and email rules — Do Not Call, the
TCPA for automated calls and texts, and CAN-SPAM — and by the firm's own policy.

**Advertising to obtain listings** is governed by **§ 452.136** and **REEB 24.04**. Two provisions
bite immediately: **§ 452.136(1)** prohibits advertising that is **false, deceptive, or misleading**;
and **§ 452.136(3)** provides that a firm and its licensees **may not advertise a property unless the
firm is the listing firm or has the listing firm's consent**. So advertising someone else's listing
to generate seller leads is not permitted.

**§ 452.136(2)(a) and (b)** require a licensee to disclose the firm's name exactly as printed on the
licence, or a trade name previously filed with the department, to **clearly indicate that the firm is
a business enterprise and not a private party**, and to advertise **under the supervision of and in
the name of the firm**, with the firm's name **clear and conspicuous**.

**Claims made to win a listing** are the recurring exposure. "I can get you $X" is a representation
about future value that nobody can support, and it is a **misleading market values** problem under
**REEB 24.09** as well as a reasonable-skill-and-care problem under § 452.133(1)(b).

### 13.3 The seller intake

Before anything is signed, a Wisconsin listing intake should establish:

**Who owns the property**, from the register of deeds, and **who must sign to convey**. Wisconsin's
**marital property** regime — ch. 766, which REEB 25.033(19)(f) reaches through ss. 766.31, 766.51,
766.60 and 766.63 — and the **homestead** provisions at ss. 706.01(7) and 766.605 make this
substantive rather than clerical. **A spouse's signature may be required even where the spouse is not
on the deed**, and Module WI-13 takes it. This is a legal question, and it goes to the closing agent
and to counsel at listing, not at closing.

**Whether the property is in a condominium, planned community, or association**, and the documents.

**Whether the property is occupied by a tenant**, and every lease, deposit and rent record — because
a sale does not end a tenancy.

**What conveys and what does not** — Hour 2.2's list, including **leased** water treatment equipment
and fuel tanks.

**What the seller knows about the condition of the property**, which the seller records in the
**Real Estate Condition Report** under **ch. 709**. Hour 17 takes it.

**Whether there is anything the licensee will be required to disclose** under § 452.133(1)(c),
because that duty is owed to **every party** and begins now.

## Hour 14 — Listing agreements under ch. REEB 16, and the types of listing

### 14.1 Board-approved forms

**Ch. REEB 16** governs the forms used in real estate transactions, and **§ 452.05(1)(b)** is the
Board's power to approve them. The consequence for a salesperson is structural: **the listing
contract is a Board-approved form**, and the licensee **completes** it rather than drafting one.

Module WI-06 takes the whole regime, including what a licensee may and may not do to an approved
form. What belongs here is the habit: use the current approved form, complete every blank the
transaction requires, attach every addendum the form references, and never alter the form's printed
provisions.

### 14.2 The three types

**Exclusive right to sell.** The firm earns its fee if the property sells during the term, **whoever
procures the buyer** — including the seller. It is the ordinary residential listing, and it is what
justifies the firm's investment in marketing.

**Exclusive agency.** The firm earns its fee if the property sells during the term **unless the seller
sells it themselves** without any firm's involvement. It creates an obvious dispute risk about who
procured the buyer.

**Open listing.** A non-exclusive arrangement in which the firm earns a fee only if it procures the
buyer, and the seller may list with others and sell themselves. It is a **unilateral** contract until
performance.

A fourth arrangement is **prohibited or restricted**, and Wisconsin names it: a **net listing** —
where the seller receives a fixed sum and the firm keeps the excess — is a listed topic at REEB
25.033(13)(b)1.e, under **REEB 24.10**. Module WI-10 takes the rule. The reason is plain: a net
listing puts the firm's interest directly against the client's, which is irreconcilable with
§ 452.133(2)(a)'s loyalty duty.

### 14.3 What every listing must settle

**Parties** — the firm and every owner whose signature is needed.
**Property** — the legal description as well as the address, and the included and excluded items.
**Price and terms** the seller will accept, and any financing the seller will consider.
**Term** — a definite beginning and end.
**Compensation** — the amount or method, how and when it is earned and paid, and how it is shared
with a cooperating firm. Fees are negotiable; see Hour 12.3 on antitrust.
**Marketing authorised** — including multiple listing service submission, photographs, signage,
lockbox, open houses, and internet display.
**Agency structure** — single agency, or the firm's practice on multiple representation and
designated agency, consistent with Hour 11.
**Seller's representations and obligations**, including the condition report.
**Protection or override period**, and to whom it applies.
**Termination provisions.**

## Hour 15 — Special listing provisions

### 15.1 What "special provisions" means — REEB 25.033(4)(c)

The rule lists **special listing provisions** as a required topic. In practice these are the terms
that allocate risk beyond the basic bargain, and each of them is a question a licensee must be able
to explain without advising on its legal effect.

**Protection or override.** The firm's right to a fee where the property sells after the term ends to
a buyer the firm introduced during it. The provision defines **who is protected**, for **how long**,
and **what displaces it** — typically a subsequent listing with another firm.

**Lockbox and access authority**, and the seller's acknowledgement of the risks of access.

**Multiple listing service submission**, and any delayed or withheld marketing the seller directs —
which raises its own fair housing question, because withholding a property from general marketing can
have effects the seller may not intend.

**Cooperation and compensation** to firms working with buyers, and how the firm's fee is shared.

**Occupancy and tenancy**, where a tenant is in possession.

**Existing offers and prior negotiations** the seller had before listing, and how they are treated.

**Excluded parties** — buyers with whom the seller has already negotiated, who are carved out for a
defined period.

**Seller's warranties and indemnities** as to authority, title, and the accuracy of information
supplied to the firm.

**Personal property and leased equipment** — the list from Hour 2.2, and any bill of sale under REEB
25.033(8)(f)13.

**Arbitration.** REEB 25.033(8)(g) requires instruction on the **agreement to arbitrate real estate
transaction disputes under s. 788.015**, and a listing may carry one.

### 15.2 The licensee's boundary on special provisions

Wisconsin's answer is unusually clear.

**Completing an approved form**, including its optional and blank provisions, as the parties have
agreed, is licensed activity.

**Advising a client on which provisions to accept, and what they will do legally**, is limited by
**§ 452.133(2)(am)** to matters **within the scope of the knowledge, skills and training required
under ch. 452**. A question about whether an override provision is enforceable against a particular
buyer is outside it.

**Drafting bespoke language** to solve a legal problem is not licensed activity at all.

The professional sentence is the one § 452.135's own disclosure supplies: *an agent of the firm can
answer your questions about brokerage services, but if you need legal advice, tax advice, or a
professional home inspection, contact an attorney, tax advisor, or home inspector.*

## Hour 16 — Terminating listings

### 16.1 How a listing ends — REEB 25.033(4)(d)

**Performance.** The property sells and the transaction closes.

**Expiration.** The stated term runs out. A Wisconsin listing has a definite term, and a licensee who
continues to market after expiration is advertising a property the firm is no longer the listing firm
for — which **§ 452.136(3)** does not permit.

**Mutual agreement.** Both sides agree to end it, in writing, recording what happens to any
protection period and to any pending negotiations.

**Revocation by the seller** or **renunciation by the firm**, either of which may end the agency
while leaving contract consequences — a question for counsel rather than for the licensee.

**Operation of law** — death or incapacity of a party, bankruptcy, or destruction of the property.

**Cancellation agreement and mutual release.** REEB 25.033(8)(f)11 lists this among the Board-approved
forms, and it is the instrument by which a Wisconsin listing or offer is unwound cleanly.

### 16.2 What survives termination

**Confidentiality.** **§ 452.133(1)(d)** in terms: the firm **"shall continue to keep the information
confidential after the transaction is complete and after the firm is no longer providing brokerage
services to the party."** It does not expire, and it is owed to every party — not only to the former
client.

**Trust fund obligations.** § 452.133(1)(f) and the rules under s. 452.13(5) run until funds are
properly accounted for. Module WI-13.

**Records.** The firm's record obligations under the REEB rules continue; Module WI-13 names the
chapter and directs the licensee to the current rule rather than stating a retention period this
project has not read.

**Any protection period** the listing created, on its own terms.

### 16.3 The conversation when a seller wants out

A seller who wants to cancel is usually describing a service problem, and the professional response
is to escalate to the supervising broker before it becomes a dispute. What a licensee must not do is
argue about the fee: whether a fee is owed on a cancellation is a contract question, and it belongs
to the broker and, if necessary, to counsel.

## Hour 17 — Pricing, disclosures, and home warranties

### 17.1 Pricing the property — REEB 25.033(4)(f)

A defensible Wisconsin pricing presentation:

**Define the market area** by what actually changes buyer behaviour — the school district, the
municipal boundary that changes the mil rate, the lake, the highway.

**Use closed sales**, screened for arm's-length conditions. Active listings are unaccepted offers;
expired listings show what the market **refused**, which brackets the top.

**Adjust the comparables toward the subject** — never the subject — in the direction Module WI-09
develops: comparable better, subtract.

**Reconcile to a range**, weighted by gross adjustment, recency and location. Reconciliation is
judgment, not averaging.

**State the limits** — how many sales, how old, what could not be verified.

Two rules bound the whole exercise.

**§ 452.133(1)(e)** requires the firm to provide **accurate information about market conditions** on
request. Accurate means what the evidence shows.

**REEB 24.09 — misleading market values** — is a listed topic at REEB 25.033(13)(b)1.d, and Module
WI-10 takes it. A price presented to win a listing rather than to describe the evidence is the conduct
the rule addresses.

A licensee's comparative market analysis is **not an appraisal**. Wisconsin licenses appraisers
separately, and Module WI-09 states the boundary.

### 17.2 Disclosures at listing — REEB 25.033(4)(g)

**The Real Estate Condition Report — ch. 709.** REEB 25.033(14)(i) requires instruction on the
**seller's disclosure duties under ch. 709**, and REEB 25.033(8)(f)10.a lists the **seller condition
report** among the Board-approved disclosure forms. Wisconsin requires a seller of residential
property, subject to the chapter's exemptions, to furnish a condition report answering specific
questions about the property.

**This course states no ch. 709 deadline, exemption, or item**, because the chapter has not been read
from primary text for this project. What a licensee does is: give the report to the **seller** to
complete; do not complete it for them; do not coach toward "not aware"; read the completed report as
an investigation prompt list; and update it when things change.

**The licensee's own duty is separate and is not satisfied by the seller's report.**
**§ 452.133(1)(c)** requires the firm to timely disclose **in writing** all material adverse facts the
**firm** knows and the party does not know or cannot discover through reasonably vigilant
observation. A seller's report saying "not aware" does not discharge a licensee who knows.

**Federal lead-based paint.** For **target housing** built **before 1978**, the seller must disclose
known lead-based paint and hazards, provide available records and reports, give the EPA pamphlet,
include the prescribed warning and acknowledgments in the contract, and — **for sales** — give the
purchaser a **ten-day** opportunity to conduct a risk assessment or inspection unless the parties
agree in writing otherwise. REEB 25.033(18)(c) lists lead-based paint as a required topic in its own
right.

**Wisconsin condition questions** that recur at listing and belong on the investigation list:
**radon**, which REEB 25.033(18)(a) requires be taught and which is common in Wisconsin soils;
**private wells and septic systems**, and their permits and service records; **underground storage
tanks** under **ch. ATCP 93**; **floodplain** status under **s. 87.30** and **NR 116**; **wetlands**
under **s. 23.32**; **farmland preservation** under **ss. 91.01 and 91.60 to 91.70**; and
**shoreland zoning** on a waterfront property.

Each is a question to be raised, routed to the office that answers it, and recorded. None is answered
by the licensee.

### 17.3 Home warranties — REEB 25.033(4)(h)

A **home warranty** or residential service contract is a **contract**, not insurance. It covers repair
or replacement of specified systems and appliances, subject to exclusions, coverage caps, service
fees, and **pre-existing condition provisions**.

Three things a licensee must be able to say plainly:

**It is not a substitute for an inspection.** The defect an inspection would find is the one most
likely to be excluded as pre-existing.

**It covers what its schedule says and nothing else**, and the exclusions are where the answer lives.

**Offering one does not satisfy any disclosure duty.** § 452.133(1)(c) is unaffected by the presence
of a warranty.

And if the licensee or the firm receives anything of value in connection with a warranty provider,
**§ 452.133(3)(c)** requires written disclosure of the fact that the licensee may receive
compensation, or of an interest in the provider.

### Module WI-03 engagement check

**Question.** At a listing appointment the seller says: "Don't put the flooded basement on the
condition report — the sump pump fixed it, and the buyer's inspector will look anyway. And price it
at $429,000; the agent from the other firm said she could get that." What do you do?

**Expected answer.** Two separate refusals and one referral.

**The condition report is the seller's document and the seller completes it**, and I will not coach
the seller toward an answer. But my own duty is independent of theirs: **§ 452.133(1)(c)** requires
the **firm** to timely disclose **in writing** all material adverse facts the firm knows and that a
party does not know or cannot discover through reasonably vigilant observation. I now know the
basement has flooded, and a buyer cannot discover that by looking at a dry basement. **I will disclose
it in writing to every party**, and the seller's instruction cannot authorise otherwise — **§ 452.133(2)(c)**
limits the client's orders to those **not inconsistent with another duty under ch. 452**, and this
would be inconsistent. That "the sump pump fixed it" is a fact to state alongside it, not a reason to
omit it.

**On price**, I present the evidence — closed arm's-length comparables, adjusted toward the subject,
reconciled to a range, with my reasoning and the limits of the data stated — and I do not adopt
another licensee's figure. **§ 452.133(1)(e)** requires accurate information about market conditions,
and **REEB 24.09** addresses misleading market values. If the evidence supports $429,000 I will say
so and show why; if it does not, I will say that too, and the seller decides.

**And I would raise it with my supervising broker**, because a listing taken on a price the evidence
does not support, with a known condition the seller wants omitted, is a file that produces a
complaint.
