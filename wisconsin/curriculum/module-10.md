## Hour 56 — Equal opportunity in housing — REEB 25.033(12)(a) and (b)

### 56.1 The federal floor

The **federal Fair Housing Act** — Title VIII of the Civil Rights Act of 1968, amended in 1974 and 1988
— prohibits discrimination in the sale, rental and financing of dwellings on the basis of **race,
colour, religion, national origin, sex, familial status, and disability**. Seven classes.

The **Civil Rights Act of 1866** is older, narrower and in one respect stronger: it prohibits **racial**
discrimination in the sale or lease of all property and, as the Supreme Court held in *Jones v. Alfred
H. Mayer Co.* (1968), **carries no exemptions**. A transaction exempt from the Fair Housing Act is not
exempt from the 1866 Act where the discrimination is racial.

**Prohibited practices** under federal law: refusing to sell or rent after a bona fide offer, or refusing
to negotiate; discriminating in terms, conditions or privileges, or in services and facilities;
discriminatory advertising; representing that a dwelling is unavailable when it is not; **blockbusting**;
**steering**; and **redlining**.

**Enforcement** runs through HUD complaint and administrative proceedings, federal civil actions, and
Department of Justice pattern-or-practice cases.

### 56.2 Why a licensee's exposure is doubled in Wisconsin

**§ 452.133(1)(a)** requires brokerage services to be provided **honestly and fairly** to every party,
and **§ 452.133(1)(b)** requires **reasonable skill and care**. A fair housing violation by a Wisconsin
licensee is therefore both a fair housing matter and a matter under **ch. 452** — two forums, two
consequences.

## Hour 57 — The practices, disability, and s. 106.50

### 57.1 Blockbusting, steering, redlining — REEB 25.033(12)(c)

**Blockbusting** — inducing or attempting to induce a person to sell or rent by representations
regarding the entry or prospective entry into the neighbourhood of persons of a protected class. It is
prohibited whether or not the representation is true, and whether or not anyone sells.

**Steering** — directing prospects toward or away from particular areas, buildings or neighbourhoods on
a protected basis. It is the practice a licensee is most likely to commit **without intending to**,
because it usually arrives as a helpful answer to a friendly question.

The compliant answer to "what kind of neighbourhood is it?" names **objective sources the buyer consults
for themselves**: school district data and attendance boundaries, crime statistics published by the
municipality, census data, tax rates, commute times. And it offers to **show every property meeting the
buyer's stated criteria**, with the criteria recorded **in the buyer's own words**.

**Redlining** — denying loans, insurance or services, or varying their terms, based on the composition of
an area rather than on the applicant or the property.

### 57.2 Disability: accommodation and modification

**A reasonable accommodation** is a change to a **rule, practice, policy or service** necessary to afford
a person with a disability equal opportunity to use and enjoy a dwelling — waiving a no-pets policy for
an **assistance animal**, assigning a parking space nearer the door, permitting a payment date matching a
benefit date. **The housing provider bears the ordinary cost of making it.**

**A reasonable modification** is a **physical change** to the premises necessary to afford full
enjoyment — a ramp, grab bars, a widened doorway. **It is at the disabled person's expense**, and in a
rental the landlord may, where reasonable, condition permission on the tenant agreeing to restore the
interior.

Two rules follow: **a no-pets policy is not an answer to an assistance-animal request**, because an
assistance animal is not a pet; and **pet fees and pet deposits may not be charged for one** — though the
tenant remains responsible for damage and behaviour.

**Design and construction.** Covered multi-family dwellings built for first occupancy after **13 March
1991** must meet federal accessible design and construction requirements.

**The ADA is a different regime.** It governs **places of public accommodation** and commercial
facilities — a real estate office or leasing office open to the public is one. **Housing is governed by
fair housing law**, and ADA service-animal rules must not be imported into a housing accommodation
analysis.

### 57.3 Equal rights under s. 106.50 — REEB 25.033(12)(d)

**Wisconsin's open housing law is s. 106.50**, and **it protects more classes than federal law.**

**This course does not print the Wisconsin list**, because **s. 106.50 has not been read from primary
text for this project** and a protected-class list is the single worst thing for a course to approximate.
Legislatures amend them, and a licensee relying on a remembered version is relying on a list that may
have changed.

What a licensee must carry: **s. 106.50 is the section**; **Wisconsin's list is broader than the federal
seven**; the **Department of Workforce Development's Equal Rights Division** administers it; and **the
current list must be read from s. 106.50 directly** before any licensee relies on a remembered version.

That instruction is not a hedge. It is the only responsible way to teach a list this course has not read.

### 57.4 Organizations — REEB 25.033(12)(e)

The rule requires instruction on the organisations involved in equal opportunity in housing. A licensee
should know where a complaint goes and where guidance comes from: **HUD** and its Office of Fair Housing
and Equal Opportunity federally; the **Wisconsin Equal Rights Division** under s. 106.50; local fair
housing councils and municipal equal opportunity commissions, several of which in Wisconsin administer
local ordinances of their own; and the **Department of Justice** for pattern-or-practice enforcement.

**Local ordinances may protect classes the state list does not**, which is one more reason a licensee
reads rather than recalls.

### 57.5 Practice rules

**Describe the property, never the occupants or the desired occupants.** "Four bedrooms, fenced yard,
walk to the elementary school" describes the property. "Perfect for a young family" expresses a
familial-status preference; "quiet building, ideal for mature professionals" expresses preferences on
age and family status.

**Use written, objective, uniform criteria** in leasing and apply them identically to every applicant.
Deviating for one applicant is the fact pattern that produces liability, whatever the reason.

**Do not honour a discriminatory covenant.** Historic racial covenants remain in Wisconsin chains of
title; they are void, and repeating one as though it operated is itself a problem.

## Hour 58 — Ch. REEB 24 and the conduct rules

### 58.1 What ch. REEB 24 is — REEB 25.033(13)(a)

**Ch. REEB 24** is the Board's chapter on **ethical real estate practices**, and REEB 25.033(13) requires
instruction on it. It is a **rule**, not a trade association's code: it is Wisconsin law, and violating it
is a disciplinary matter before the Board.

**This course names each cited section and states its subject. It does not quote or paraphrase the text
of any of them**, because ch. REEB 24 has not been read from primary text for this project. A student
must read the chapter itself, and an instructor supplementing this module must obtain and date the
reading.

### 58.2 Competence in area of service — REEB 24.03

REEB 25.033(13)(b)1.a. The rule addresses a licensee's **competence in the area of service** they hold
themselves out to provide.

It aligns exactly with **§ 452.133(2)(am)**, which limits the duty to advise a client to matters **within
the scope of the knowledge, skills and training required under ch. 452**, and with the plain-language
statement in the **§ 452.135** disclosure: *if you need legal advice, tax advice, or a professional home
inspection, contact an attorney, tax advisor, or home inspector.*

Three practical consequences: a residential licensee who takes a commercial, farm or time-share
transaction outside their competence has a rule problem as well as a service problem; the answer to a
question outside one's competence is a **referral**, not an attempt; and the referral is put **in
writing**.

### 58.3 Tie-in arrangements — REEB 24.075

**REEB 24.075** prohibits a licensee from conditioning the sale of real estate owned by the licensee or controlled by an option, as described in the rule, on the buyer purchasing other real estate or listing the buyer's real estate with the licensee. The rule has specific builder-related provisions; read them before applying an exception.

Do not describe every commercial tie or package as automatically prohibited by this particular rule. Federal antitrust tying analysis and this Wisconsin real-estate conduct rule are related subjects with different elements.

### 58.4 Agreements in writing — REEB 24.08

REEB 25.033(13)(b)1.c. The rule addresses **agreements in writing**.

It sits alongside the statutory requirements a student has already met: **§ 240.10**, requiring a broker's
contract to be in writing; **§ 706.02**, requiring a conveyance to be in writing; **§ 452.135(2)(a)**,
requiring the client disclosure not later than the agency agreement; and the approved-form regime under
**ch. REEB 16**.

The habit it produces: **if it matters, it is written, signed, delivered and kept.**

### 58.5 Misleading market values — REEB 24.09

REEB 25.033(13)(b)1.d. The rule addresses **misleading market values**, and Module WI-09 Hour 55.3 set
out why it bites: a price presented to win a listing rather than to describe the evidence is the conduct
the rule addresses, and **§ 452.133(1)(e)** independently requires **accurate** information about market
conditions.

### 58.6 Net listings — REEB 24.10

A **net listing** is an arrangement in which the seller receives a fixed sum and the firm keeps the excess as its compensation. **REEB 24.10 strictly prohibits net listings.** It is not merely a matter to take to the supervising broker for consent, and the seller's proposal or informed agreement does not remove the prohibition. Use a lawful negotiated compensation arrangement instead.

## Hour 59 — Advertising

### 59.1 REEB 24.04 and § 452.136 — REEB 25.033(13)(b)2

The paragraph requires instruction on **advertising under s. REEB 24.04 and s. 452.136**, and **it was
amended by CR 23-075**, effective 9-1-24.

**REEB 24.04** is the Board's advertising rule; **this course names it and does not state its text**, for
the reason given at 58.1.

**§ 452.136 has been read**, and it is stated here in full.

### 59.2 § 452.136, applied

**(1) False advertising.** *A licensee may not advertise in a manner that is false, deceptive, or
misleading.*

**(1m) Advertising enhanced by technology.** *A licensee shall in all advertising disclose if the
advertising has been altered or modified using technology, **including artificial intelligence**, to
add, remove, or change elements of the property, that creates a false or misleading impression of the
property.*

**This subsection is created effective 1 January 2027 by 2025 Wis. Act 69.** It is stated here because
it is on the books and dated, and because it will govern practice within the working life of anyone
completing this course. Virtual staging, sky replacement, removing a neighbouring structure, and
generative editing of a listing photograph all fall within it where the result creates a false or
misleading impression. **Until the effective date, subsection (1) governs the same conduct** — an
altered photograph creating a false impression is misleading advertising whether or not (1m) is yet in
force.

**(2) Disclosure of name.**
**(a)** Except for advertisements for the rental of real estate owned by the licensee, a licensee shall
in all advertising disclose **the firm's name exactly as printed on the licence**, or a **trade name
previously filed** with the department, and shall **clearly indicate that the firm is a business
enterprise and not a private party.**
**(b)** Except for the same rental advertisements, a licensee associated with a firm shall advertise
**under the supervision of and in the name of the firm**, and **the firm's name as used in advertising
shall be clear and conspicuous.** The paragraph does not apply to a licensee engaged in independent
practice under s. 452.30(6).
**(c)** A licensee may advertise the **occasional sale of real estate owned by the licensee**, or engage
in the occasional solicitation of real estate for purchase by the licensee, without complying with (a)
and (b) — **provided the licensee clearly identifies themselves as a real estate licensee in the
advertisement.**

**(3) Advertising without an agency agreement prohibited.** A firm and its licensees **may not advertise
a property unless** the firm is the **listing firm**, or has **obtained consent to advertise from the
listing firm.**

**(4) Advertised price.** A licensee **may not advertise property at a price other than that agreed upon
with the owner** — except that the price may be stated as a **range or in general terms if it reflects
the agreed upon price.**

### 59.3 Auditing an advertisement

Six questions, in order:

**Is it false, deceptive or misleading?** (1)
**Has it been altered or modified using technology in a way that creates a false or misleading
impression, and is that disclosed?** (1m) from 1-1-27, and (1) before then.
**Does it disclose the firm's name exactly as licensed, or a filed trade name, and indicate the firm is
a business rather than a private party?** (2)(a)
**Is it under the firm's supervision and in the firm's name, clear and conspicuous?** (2)(b)
**Is the firm the listing firm, or does it have the listing firm's consent?** (3)
**Is the price the price agreed with the owner, or a range or general terms reflecting it?** (4)

And then the fair housing question from Hour 57.5: **does anything in it describe the occupants rather
than the property?**

### 59.4 Other marketing controls

**Do Not Call**, the **TCPA** for automated calls and texts, and **CAN-SPAM** govern solicitation.
**Copyright** — photographs, floor plans, drone footage and video are copyrighted works, usually owned by
the photographer unless the agreement says otherwise; reusing another firm's listing photographs after a
listing changes hands is infringement, not economy. And **§ 452.136(3)** independently bars advertising
another firm's listing without consent.

## Hour 60 — Written proposals, compensation, and interest

### 60.1 Completing and presenting written proposals — REEB 24.12 and 24.13

REEB 25.033(13)(b)3 requires instruction on **completing and presenting written proposals under ss. REEB
24.12 and 24.13**, and names five elements:

**a. Confidentiality.** Which aligns with **§ 452.133(1)(d)** — owed to every party, covering information
the firm obtains that it knows a reasonable person would want kept confidential, and **continuing after
the transaction is complete**.

**b. Drafting and submitting offers.** On the **Board-approved forms** under ch. REEB 16, completed
rather than drafted — Module WI-06 Hour 32.

**c. Prompt delivery to buyer.** A signed acceptance, counter-offer or notice reaching the buyer promptly.

**d. Fair presentation of offers.** Which aligns with **§ 452.133(1)(g)** — presenting proposals
**objectively and without bias**, disclosing their **advantages and disadvantages**.

**e. Prompt notification.** Telling a party promptly what has happened to their proposal — particularly
where a **multiple counter-proposal** has been used and buyers are waiting on a seller's selection.

**The REEB 24 text is not stated here**, for the reason at 58.1. What is stated is that each element has
a statutory partner in § 452.133, so a licensee who satisfies the statute is most of the way to
satisfying the rule — and must still read the rule.

### 60.2 Disclosure of compensation and interest — REEB 24.05 and § 452.133

REEB 25.033(13)(b)4 requires instruction on **disclosure of compensation and interest under s. REEB 24.05
and s. 452.133**, and **the introduction was amended by CR 23-075.** Four elements:

**a. Disclosing of interest.** **§ 452.133(3)(b)**: a licensee may not act in a transaction on their own
behalf, on behalf of immediate family where the firm is an individual, on behalf of their firm, or on
behalf of any organisation or entity **in which the licensee has an interest**, **without the prior
written consent of all parties** — and the consent must be obtained **in the offer to purchase, option,
lease, or other transaction contract.**

**b. Referral services.** **§ 452.133(3)(c)**: no referring, recommending or suggesting the services of a
person or entity **from which the licensee may receive compensation for a referral**, or **in which the
licensee has an interest**, unless the licensee **has disclosed that in writing.** The trigger is "may
receive", not "will receive".

**c. Disclosing of profits.** Where a licensee stands to profit from the transaction beyond the agreed
brokerage fee, the profit is disclosed.

**d. Disclosing of licensure.** A licensee dealing in their own account must **identify themselves as a
licensee** — which **§ 452.136(2)(c)** requires expressly in an advertisement for the occasional sale or
purchase of the licensee's own real estate.

And **§ 452.133(3)(a)** underlies all of it: no fee or compensation related to the transaction from
anyone other than the licensee's client or firm **without the prior written consent of all parties**.

## Hour 61 — Consumer protection: REEB 24.07

### 61.1 The structure — REEB 25.033(14)(a) to (f)

REEB 25.033(14) requires instruction on six provisions of **REEB 24.07**, and the structure of the rule
is worth learning even where its text is not quoted here:

**(1)(a) Property inspections.** REEB 25.033(14)(a).
**(1)(b) Inquiry by the listing firm.** REEB 25.033(14)(b).
**(2) Disclosure of material adverse facts.** REEB 25.033(14)(c).
**(3) Disclosure of material suggesting adverse material facts.** REEB 25.033(14)(d).
**(4) Disclosure of side agreements.** REEB 25.033(14)(e).
**(5) Reliance upon third parties.** REEB 25.033(14)(f).

**This course does not state the text of any of them**, because ch. REEB 24 has not been read from
primary text for this project. What it teaches is **what each provision is about, how it relates to
§ 452.133(1)(c), and what a licensee does** — and it instructs the student to read the rule.

### 61.2 What the six provisions do, and how they relate to the statute

**Wis. Stat. 452.133(1)(c)** and **REEB 24.07(2)** require timely written disclosure of known material adverse facts to parties who do not know or cannot discover them through reasonably vigilant observation, unless disclosure is prohibited by law. Apply the actual statutory wording rather than replacing it with a narrower “both conditions” test.

**REEB 24.07(1)(a)–(d)** supplies the licensee-inspection framework: a reasonably competent and diligent inspection of accessible structures and immediate surroundings; before listing execution, inspection plus seller inquiry with a written response requested; and the other licensee's inspection before making a written proposal, subject to access and the party's instruction to submit before access. In that last case inspect promptly afterward if access is provided. The rule excludes specified invasive, obstructed, or hazardous activity, including moving furniture, opening panels, operating equipment, and entering hazardous areas. It does not turn a licensee into an engineer or home inspector.

**REEB 24.07(3) was repealed effective March 1, 2026 by CR 25-077.** Its former stand-alone “material suggesting adverse material facts” rule must not be taught as operative law. REEB 25.033(14)(d) still contains a reference to that repealed paragraph: preserve the curricular topic and explain this stale cross-reference, but use current operative duties for conduct.

**REEB 24.07(4)** requires timely written disclosure to the financing institution or other secured lender if the licensee knows the parties have not disclosed their entire agreement to that lender. It is not a blanket statement that every side agreement is forbidden.

**REEB 24.07(5)** allows reliance on a qualified third party's written property-condition report if copies are given timely to all parties. **(6)** separately requires timely written disclosure of a material inconsistency between the licensee's inspection and the seller's or third party's information. **(7)** prohibits knowingly giving false property information to parties or another licensee.

### 61.3 The practical method

Distinguish observed information from an unsupported diagnosis. If the firm knows a material adverse fact, apply 452.133(1)(c) and 24.07(2). If an inspection reveals a material inconsistency with a seller's or qualified third party's statement, apply 24.07(6). If the cause or significance is uncertain, apply reasonable skill and care and the competence rules, describe observations accurately, and obtain appropriate inspection or assistance rather than certifying a technical conclusion.

**The repeal of 24.07(3) does not repeal the other inspection, honesty, care, competence, and disclosure duties.** A stain alone does not automatically prove a structural defect; uncertainty is also not permission to ignore a known fact or materially contradictory evidence. Track the agreed investigation and notice deadlines without inventing an additional statutory deadline.

For a lender side-agreement issue, disclose the known omission timely in writing as required by 24.07(4). For reliance under (5), obtain the qualified third party's written report and give copies to all parties in a timely manner.

## Hour 62 — Investigations, liability, and the seller's disclosure

### 62.1 Disclosures and inspections by licensees under § 452.23 — REEB 25.033(14)(g)

**Wis. Stat. 452.23 is “Disclosures and inspections by brokers and salespersons.”** It is not a grant of the Department's power to investigate licensees. It prohibits disclosures that constitute the specified unlawful discrimination, addresses information a licensee need not disclose under its stated conditions, and addresses qualified third-party reports.

Under **452.23(2)(b)**, a qualifying written third-party report provided to the person can remove the need for duplicate disclosure of information already disclosed there. Under **(3)** the licensee must disclose known facts that contradict such a report. Under **(4)** the licensee must exercise the care expected of a reasonably prudent person with the knowledge, skills, and training required for licensure. The statute's other exceptions and conditions must be checked in current text before being applied.

Board investigation and discipline are separate matters, including the applicable provisions of **452.14**. Preserve records and obtain appropriate advice for a real complaint; do not misidentify 452.23 as the disciplinary-investigation authority.

### 62.2 Civil liability for misrepresentation — REEB 25.033(14)(h)

Separate from Board discipline, a licensee and a firm can be **sued**.

**§ 452.139(2)** allocates responsibility: a **client is not liable** for a misrepresentation made by the
firm or its licensees **unless the client knew or should have known, or the firm or licensee is
repeating a misrepresentation made by the client**; and a firm is not liable for a **subagent's**
misrepresentation on the same conditions. **§ 452.139(2)(c)** preserves the firm's responsibility under
**s. 452.12(3)** for misrepresentations by its own associated licensees, and preserves a client's
liability for their own.

The exposure a licensee actually creates: **repeating the seller's statement as fact**. The moment a
licensee passes on "the roof is five years old" without attributing it and without qualification, the
statement becomes the firm's — and § 452.139(2)(a)'s protection for the client falls away precisely
because the licensee is repeating the client's misrepresentation.

The habit that prevents it: **attribute, qualify, and route.** *The seller states the roof was replaced
in 2019; I have not verified that; here is the permit record if one exists, and your inspector can
assess it.*

### 62.3 Seller's disclosure duties under ch. 709 — REEB 25.033(14)(i)

Wisconsin requires a seller of residential property, subject to the chapter's exemptions, to furnish a
**Real Estate Condition Report** — the form REEB 25.033(8)(f)10.a names.

**This course states no ch. 709 requirement, deadline, exemption or report item**, because the chapter
has not been read from primary text for this project.

What a licensee does, from Module WI-06 Hour 36.1: **give it to the seller and let the seller complete
it; do not coach toward "not aware"; read it as an investigation prompt list; deliver it as the
transaction requires; and update it when things change.**

**And the point that must not be missed: the seller's report does not discharge the firm's duty.**
**§ 452.133(1)(c)** requires the **firm** to disclose in writing material adverse facts **the firm**
knows. A seller's "not aware" is irrelevant to what the firm knows.

### Module WI-10 engagement check

**Question.** You list a house. The seller's condition report says "not aware" of any basement water. At
the first showing you notice efflorescence on the basement wall and a dehumidifier running, and the
seller mentions "the sump only runs in spring". A buyer's agent later asks you directly whether the
basement has ever taken water. Separately, your firm's marketing company has virtually removed the
neighbouring garage from the listing photographs. Work through everything.

**Expected answer.**

**The observations call for careful inquiry, not a fabricated diagnosis.** Efflorescence, a running dehumidifier, and a statement that a sump operates in spring do not by themselves prove that the basement floods or establish the cause. Record the actual observations and seller statements, seek competent inspection, and avoid saying either that flooding is proved or that the property is defect-free. Disclose known material adverse facts under 452.133(1)(c) and 24.07(2), and materially inconsistent information under 24.07(6), where their conditions are met. The former 24.07(3) provision is repealed; it is not a current independent basis for the answer.

**When the buyer's agent asks me directly**, I answer honestly — **§ 452.133(1)(a)** — and in writing,
and I do not soften it. If the seller instructs me not to, that instruction is not one I may follow:
**§ 452.133(2)(c)** limits a client's orders to those **not inconsistent with another duty under ch.
452**, and (1)(c) is such a duty.

**The photographs are advertising that has been altered using technology to remove an element of the
property in a way that creates a false impression.** **§ 452.136(1)** already makes misleading
advertising a violation, and **§ 452.136(1m)** — created effective **1 January 2027 by 2025 Wis. Act
69** — will require the alteration to be **disclosed**. Either way the images come down or are disclosed,
and I take it to my **supervising broker**, who is responsible for advertising under
**§ 452.136(2)(b)**.

## Extended treatment — the conduct rules applied

### E10.1 An advertisement, audited line by line

*"JUST LISTED — stunning lakefront retreat, 150 ft of pristine frontage, priced to sell at $549,000 (or
best offer)! Perfect for empty nesters or a quiet couple. Walk to St. Mary's. Call Dana 555-0100.
#LakeLife"* — the property is listed by another firm, the frontage is 120 feet on the plat, the seller
agreed a price of $575,000, and the photograph has had a neighbouring shed removed digitally.

**"150 ft of pristine frontage" against 120 feet on the plat** — **§ 452.136(1)**, false or misleading
advertising, and a **§ 452.133(1)(a)** honesty problem toward every party.

**"$549,000 (or best offer)" against an agreed $575,000** — **§ 452.136(4)**: a licensee may not
advertise property at a price other than that agreed with the owner. A range or general terms are
permitted **only if they reflect the agreed price**.

**The property is another firm's listing** — **§ 452.136(3)**: a firm may not advertise a property
unless it is the listing firm or has the listing firm's consent.

**No firm name** — **§ 452.136(2)(a)**: the firm's name exactly as licensed, or a filed trade name, and
a clear indication that the firm is a business enterprise rather than a private party. **(2)(b)**: under
the firm's supervision and in the firm's name, **clear and conspicuous**.

**The digitally removed shed** — **§ 452.136(1)** now, because an altered image creating a false
impression is misleading advertising; and **§ 452.136(1m)** from **1 January 2027**, which will require
the alteration to be **disclosed**.

**"Perfect for empty nesters or a quiet couple"** — a preference on **familial status** and, depending on
the reading, age. Describe the property, never the occupants.

**"Walk to St. Mary's"** — a reference to a place of worship. Wisconsin's own list under **s. 106.50** has
not been read for this course and no state rule is stated; the practice answer is that describing
proximity to a landmark is different from describing the community, and the safe formulation names
distance rather than affinity.

**A compliant rewrite:** *"Lakefront home, 120 feet of frontage, offered at $575,000. [Firm's licensed
name], [office contact information]. Contact Dana, 555-0100."* — posted only if the firm is the listing
firm or has the listing firm's written consent, and with any digital alteration disclosed.

### E10.2 Steering, recognised in ordinary conversation

Steering rarely arrives as a decision. It arrives as an answer.

*"Which schools are better?"* → Name the source — the district's own published data — and offer to show
everything meeting the buyer's criteria. Do not rank neighbourhoods.

*"Would we fit in there?"* → Ask what matters to them about a neighbourhood — commute, lot size, price,
walkability — and answer on those. Do not answer on who lives there.

*"Is it safe?"* → Name the municipality's published crime statistics. Do not characterise.

*"We'd like to be near people like us."* → Record the objective criteria they actually name and search on
those. Do not infer a protected characteristic and do not act on one.

*A licensee's own convenience* — showing only properties in areas they know well — becomes steering the
moment it correlates with a protected characteristic. **Record the buyer's criteria in the buyer's own
words** and show against them.

### E10.3 Material adverse facts: a working method

The current statutory test in 452.133(1)(c) concerns a material adverse fact the firm knows and that the party does not know **or** cannot discover through reasonably vigilant observation, unless disclosure is prohibited by law. Do not silently rewrite the statute's connector or assume that any visible clue resolves all hidden aspects of a known condition.

Classify the actual fact, the firm's knowledge, the recipient's information, and the required form and timing of disclosure. Distinguish that analysis from an uncertain observation that requires reasonable care and competent follow-up. REEB 24.07(3) was repealed on March 1, 2026; current 24.07(1), (2), (5), and (6), and 24.03 continue to govern their respective issues.

### E10.4 What a complaint file looks like

When a Board complaint arrives, the licensee's file either answers it or does not. What answers it:

**Contemporaneous notes** with dates, times and channels. **Every written disclosure**, with the date
given. **Every § 452.135 disclosure**, delivered and evidenced. **The referral record** — who was named,
when, in what form. **The advertising record**, including any timely written request to a third party who
controls a platform. **Every offer and its presentation**, with dates. **The trust account ledger** for
the transaction. And **the correspondence.**

What does not answer it: a reconstruction, a recollection, or an assertion that the licensee "always"
does something.

**And the response itself:** notify the supervising broker immediately; preserve everything and change
nothing; do not contact the complainant to argue; respond within the time given; obtain counsel.
