- Distinguish a subagent's duties from a firm's, and a firm from the licensees associated with it.
- Identify an antitrust exposure in a conversation before it becomes a case.

## Hour 6 — The firm, the licensee, and Wisconsin's idea of agency

### 6.1 Wisconsin licenses the firm

Wisconsin's brokerage duties run to and from the **firm**. § 452.133 is written that way throughout —
"a firm providing brokerage services to a party owes all of the following duties to the party" — and
the licensees associated with the firm provide those services on the firm's behalf.

A **salesperson** is licensed to provide brokerage services **associated with a firm**. The
salesperson's authority is derived from the firm's licence, and the firm's duties are what the
salesperson's conduct satisfies or breaches.

That framing matters practically. When a client asks "are you my agent?", the accurate Wisconsin
answer is that **the firm** is the agent, and the salesperson is the licensee through whom the firm
performs.

### 6.2 § 452.139(1) — what happened to the common law

> **The duties of a firm specified in this chapter or in rules promulgated under this chapter shall
> supersede duties or obligations under common law to the extent that those common law duties or
> obligations are inconsistent with the duties specified in this chapter or in rules promulgated
> under this chapter.**

Read it precisely, because it is narrower than Virginia's abrogation and wider than nothing.
Wisconsin does not delete the common law of agency; it makes **ch. 452 and its rules control where
the two are inconsistent.**

The instruction that follows: **answer a Wisconsin duty question from § 452.133 and the REEB rules
first.** Where a national text describes a fiduciary duty and the chapter says something different,
the chapter governs. Where the chapter is silent and the common law is not inconsistent with it, the
common law still speaks.

REEB 25.033(3)(a) and (c) require instruction on the "law of agency" and on "fiduciary duties" for
exactly this reason: a Wisconsin licensee needs the general framework in order to see where the
chapter has changed it.

### 6.3 Two more things § 452.139 does

**(2) Misrepresentation.** A **client is not liable** for a misrepresentation made in connection with
the provision of brokerage services by the firm or a licensee associated with it — **unless the
client knows or should have known of it, or the firm or licensee is repeating a misrepresentation
made by the client.** And a firm that retains another firm as a **subagent** is not liable for the
subagent's misrepresentation unless it knew or should have known, or the subagent is repeating a
misrepresentation the firm made. Nothing in the subsection limits a firm's responsibility under
**s. 452.12(3)** for misrepresentations by its own associated licensees, nor a client's liability for
their own misrepresentation.

**(3) Negligent hiring.** Where an associated licensee commits a crime or another wrongful act, the
firm may not be held civilly liable for hiring them in a negligent-hiring claim if the firm relied on
the department's investigations under **s. 440.03(13)** or on a Board determination — regardless of
whether the firm conducted its own investigation.

Both are protections, and both have conditions. A licensee should notice that the client's protection
in (2)(a) evaporates the moment the licensee is **repeating something the client said**, which is
precisely what happens when a seller's statement about the property is passed on without
qualification.

### 6.4 Client, customer, party

**Party** — anyone to whom the firm provides brokerage services in the transaction. **Every party is
owed the seven § 452.133(1) duties.**

**Client** — a party the firm represents under an **agency agreement**. A client is owed the seven
plus the five in § 452.133(2).

**Customer** — a party who is not the firm's client. Wisconsin's § 452.135(1)(a) Disclosure to
Customers exists to tell them, in plain language, exactly what they are owed.

**Subagent** — a firm providing brokerage services through another firm's client relationship, owed
and owing the sub. (1) duties but not the sub. (2) client duties.

## Hour 7 — The seven duties owed to every party

### 7.1 § 452.133(1), read in full

A firm providing brokerage services to a party owes that party:

**(a) Honestly and fairly.** The duty to provide brokerage services honestly and fairly.

**(b) Reasonable skill and care.** Not "utmost care", not a fiduciary standard borrowed from another
state — **reasonable skill and care**, and it is owed to a customer as much as to a client.

**(c) Material adverse facts, in writing.** The duty to **timely disclose in writing all material
adverse facts that the firm knows and that the party does not know or cannot discover through
reasonably vigilant observation**, unless disclosure of a material adverse fact is prohibited by law.

**(d) Confidentiality — and it survives.** The duty to keep confidential any information given to the
firm in confidence, **or any information obtained by the firm that the firm knows a reasonable person
would want to be kept confidential**, unless the information must be disclosed by law or the person
whose interests may be adversely affected specifically authorises the disclosure of particular
information. And then the sentence that distinguishes Wisconsin: **"The firm shall continue to keep
the information confidential after the transaction is complete and after the firm is no longer
providing brokerage services to the party."**

**(e) Accurate market information on request.** The duty to provide accurate information about market
conditions that affect the transaction, within a reasonable time after a request, unless disclosure
is prohibited by law.

**(f) Safeguard trust funds.** The duty to safeguard trust funds and other property held, as required
by rules promulgated under **s. 452.13(5)**. Module WI-13.

**(g) Objective presentation of proposals.** When the firm is negotiating on behalf of a party, the
duty to **present contract proposals in an objective and unbiased manner and disclose the advantages
and disadvantages of the proposals.**

### 7.2 Why (c) is the hardest duty in Wisconsin practice

Three elements have to be present, and each is worth isolating.

**"Material adverse facts."** The phrase is defined in ch. 452 and developed in **REEB 24.07**, which
Module WI-10 takes in full. What matters here is that it is not "anything a buyer would like to
know"; it is a defined class.

**"That the firm knows."** Actual knowledge of the **firm**, which includes what the associated
licensee knows in the course of the firm's business.

**"And that the party does not know or cannot discover through reasonably vigilant observation."**
This is the limb licensees misapply. A visibly sagging roofline is discoverable by reasonably vigilant
observation; a failed drain tile is not. The duty attaches to what is **known and not reasonably
discoverable**, which is why the licensee's file must record what was observed and what was told.

**"Timely" and "in writing."** Both. A material adverse fact disclosed orally at the closing table
satisfies neither.

### 7.3 Why (d) is the duty licensees breach without noticing

Two features are unusual.

**It reaches information the firm merely *obtained*** and knows a reasonable person would want kept
confidential — not only information given "in confidence". A licensee who learns from a neighbour
that the seller is divorcing has obtained such information.

**It continues after the transaction and after the relationship ends.** There is no expiry. The
former client whose circumstances become interesting to a new client three years later is still
protected, and the licensee who uses that knowledge has breached a live duty.

Release requires either that the law compels disclosure or that **the person whose interests may be
adversely affected specifically authorises the disclosure of particular information** — specific
authorisation of particular information, not a general waiver.

### 7.4 Duty (g), and what "objective and unbiased" costs

When negotiating on behalf of a party, the firm must present proposals **objectively** and **disclose
the advantages and disadvantages**. Both halves.

So a licensee presenting a seller with three offers must present the disadvantages of the one they
would prefer the seller to take, and the advantages of the one they would not. The duty is symmetric,
and it is the statutory answer to the licensee who presents a clean cash offer with enthusiasm and a
contingent offer with a sigh.

## Hour 8 — The additional duties owed to a client

### 8.1 § 452.133(2), read in full

A firm providing brokerage services to a **client** owes the sub. (1) duties **and**:

**(a) Loyalty**, by (1) placing the client's interests ahead of the firm's, and (2) placing the
client's interests ahead of the interests of persons in the transaction who are not the firm's
clients — **by not disclosing to them information or advice whose disclosure is contrary to a
client's interests, unless the disclosure is required by law.**

**(am) Information and advice, when requested**, on matters material to the client's transaction
**and within the scope of the knowledge, skills and training required under ch. 452.**

**(b) Disclosure to the client** of all information known by the firm that is material to the
transaction and not known by the client or discoverable through reasonably vigilant observation —
**except** confidential information under (1)(d) and other information whose disclosure is prohibited
by law.

**(c) Fulfilment of the agency agreement** and of any order of the client within its scope, that is
not inconsistent with another duty under ch. 452 or any other law.

**(d) Negotiation on behalf of the client.**

### 8.2 The boundary inside (am)

**(am) is the section that decides what a Wisconsin licensee may advise on.** The duty is to provide
information and advice on matters material to the transaction **and within the scope of the
knowledge, skills and training required under ch. 452**.

So the licensee owes advice on brokerage matters — pricing evidence, marketing, offer strategy,
deadlines, the forms — and owes **nothing** on legal, tax, engineering, appraisal, or lending
questions, because those are outside the chapter's scope. The § 452.135 **Disclosure to Customers**
says the same thing in plain language: *"An agent of the firm can answer your questions about
brokerage services, but if you need legal advice, tax advice, or a professional home inspection,
contact an attorney, tax advisor, or home inspector."*

**REEB 24.03**, competence in area of service, is the ethics rule that carries the same idea, and
Module WI-10 takes it.

### 8.3 Where (c) stops

The duty to fulfil the client's orders is expressly limited to orders **within the scope of the
agency agreement** and **not inconsistent with another duty under ch. 452 or any other law**.

So an instruction to withhold a material adverse fact is not an order a licensee may follow: it
conflicts with § 452.133(1)(c), which is owed to every party. An instruction to screen buyers on a
protected basis conflicts with fair housing law. A client's instruction cannot authorise either, and
the statute says so in terms rather than leaving it to inference.

### 8.4 The tension between (2)(a)2 and (1)(c), resolved

Students see a contradiction: loyalty says do not disclose information contrary to a client's
interests to a non-client; the material-adverse-facts duty says disclose in writing to every party.

The statute resolves it itself. **(2)(a)2 is expressly subject to "unless the disclosure is required
by law"**, and **(1)(c) is a legal requirement.** Material adverse facts are disclosed. Everything
else that would harm the client stays inside the client relationship.

That is the whole architecture in one sentence, and it is worth memorising: **material adverse facts
go to everyone; advantage does not.**

## Hour 9 — Prohibitions and subagency

### 9.1 § 452.133(3) — prohibited conduct

**(a) No compensation from a third person without prior written consent of all parties.** A licensee
may not accept any fee or compensation related to the transaction from any person other than the
licensee's client or firm **without the prior written consent of all parties to the transaction.**

**(b) No self-dealing without prior written consent of all parties.** A licensee may not act in a
transaction on their **own behalf**, on behalf of their **immediate family** where the firm is an
individual, on behalf of their **firm**, or on behalf of any **organisation or business entity in
which the licensee has an interest** — without the prior written consent of all parties. And the
statute prescribes how: **"For the purpose of complying with this paragraph, a licensee shall obtain
the written consent in the offer to purchase, option, lease, or other transaction contract."**

Note that: the consent is not a side letter. It goes **in the transaction contract**.

**(c) No undisclosed referral interest.** A licensee may not refer, recommend or suggest the services
of a person or entity from which the licensee **may receive compensation for a referral**, or in which
the licensee **has an interest**, unless the licensee **has disclosed that fact in writing**.

The trigger is "may receive" and "has an interest" — not "will receive". A licensee recommending an
affiliated title company, inspection service, or lender discloses in writing before the
recommendation is acted on.

**(d) No negotiating personal property unless related to the transaction**, with a Board-approved
form available under s. 452.05(1)(b) for conveying the seller's interest in personal property.

**Version warning.** The section as read carries effective-date notes for **2025 Wis. Act 69,
effective 1 January 2027**, amending (3)(a) and (3)(c). Before that date (3)(a) reads "other than the
licensee's client, **principal firm**, or firm", and (3)(c) cross-references **s. 452.19(1)** rather
than s. 452.19(1m)(a). Any statement here must be rechecked against the operative version before the
course is delivered on or after that date.

### 9.2 § 452.133(4) — the subagent

A **subagent** owes all parties to whom it provides brokerage services **the sub. (1) duties** — the
seven — **but does not owe the principal firm's clients the sub. (2) duties.**

And a subagent **may not**:
**1.** place its own interests ahead of the principal firm's clients in the transaction it was
engaged for; or
**2.** provide advice or opinions to parties in the transaction where doing so is **contrary to the
interests of the principal firm's clients**, unless required by law.

Subagency is a relationship a firm enters deliberately, in writing, and the second prohibition is
what makes it workable: a subagent may assist but may not advocate against the principal firm's
client.

### 9.3 What the prohibitions have in common

Three of the four turn on **written consent or written disclosure obtained in advance**. That is the
Wisconsin pattern throughout ch. 452: the remedy for a conflict is not avoidance, it is **disclosure
in writing, before the conduct, to everyone affected** — and, for self-dealing, **in the transaction
contract itself**.

A licensee who is unsure whether a conflict has arisen has a simple test: would a party be surprised
to learn this? If so, it goes in writing to the supervising broker first.

## Hour 10 — § 452.135 disclosure, and creating and terminating agency

### 10.1 The Disclosure to Customers — § 452.135(1)(a)

> **No firm, and no licensee associated with a firm, may negotiate on behalf of a party who is not
> the firm's client unless the firm, or a licensee associated with the firm, provides to the party a
> copy of** the written Disclosure to Customers statement the statute prints.

The statute prints the statement. Its substance, in the statute's own plain language, is the seven
duties from § 452.133(1): brokerage services **fairly and honestly**; **reasonable skill and care**;
**accurate information about market conditions** within a reasonable time on request; the duty to
**disclose in writing certain material adverse facts about a property**; the duty to **protect your
confidentiality**, with the firm not disclosing the customer's confidential information or that of
other parties unless the law requires it; the duty to **safeguard trust funds and other property**;
and the duty, **when negotiating, to present contract proposals in an objective and unbiased manner
and disclose the advantages and disadvantages**.

And it closes with the referral sentence Hour 8 quoted, and with the statement that **"This disclosure
is required by section 452.135 of the Wisconsin statutes and is for information only. It is a
plain-language summary of the duties to a customer under section 452.133(1)."**

Three things follow.

**The trigger is negotiating on behalf of a party who is not the firm's client.** It is not "meeting
someone", and it is not "showing a property" — it is negotiating on their behalf.

**It is a summary, not a source.** The duties come from § 452.133(1). The disclosure is a
plain-language restatement, and a licensee should be able to point to the statute behind each line.

**§ 452.135(1)(b)** extends it: a **subagent** must provide the same statement to any person who is
not the principal firm's client and who receives brokerage services from the subagent within the
scope of the subagency agreement.

### 10.2 The Disclosure to Clients — § 452.135(2)(a)

> Except as provided in par. (b), **a firm shall provide to a client a copy of the** written
> Disclosure to Clients statement **not later than the time the firm enters into an agency agreement
> with the client.**

The timing is the point: **not later than the time the agency agreement is entered into.** Not
afterwards, and not at the offer.

### 10.3 Creating agency — REEB 25.033(3)(b)

An agency relationship in Wisconsin arises from an **agency agreement**. REEB 25.033(3)(j)2 requires
instruction on "the requirement for an agency agreement and disclosure of agency under s. 452.135",
and Module WI-03 takes listing agreements and Module WI-06 takes the Board-approved forms — including
**buyer agency agreements** at REEB 25.033(8)(f)4.

What a licensee carries from this hour: representation follows from an agreement, the client
disclosure is delivered **no later than** that agreement, and the customer disclosure is delivered
before negotiating on behalf of a non-client.

### 10.4 Terminating agency — REEB 25.033(3)(b)

An agency relationship ends by **performance**, by **expiration** of its stated term, by **mutual
agreement**, by **revocation or renunciation** — each of which may carry contract consequences — or
by **operation of law**, including death or incapacity of a party, or destruction of the property.

Two duties do not end with it. **§ 452.133(1)(d) confidentiality continues after the transaction is
complete and after the firm is no longer providing brokerage services.** And **§ 452.133(1)(f)**
trust-fund obligations run until the funds are properly accounted for.

Module WI-03 takes **terminating listings** at REEB 25.033(4)(d) in the specific context of the
listing contract.

## Hour 11 — Multiple representation, designated agency, single agency

### 11.1 The three structures — REEB 25.033(3)(k)

The rule requires instruction on **multiple representation with designated agency**, **multiple
representation without designated agency**, and **single agency**. They are three ways a firm can
stand in a transaction, and a Wisconsin salesperson must be able to say which one they are in.

**Single agency.** The firm represents one client in the transaction. The other party is a
**customer**, owed the seven § 452.133(1) duties and no more.

**Multiple representation without designated agency.** The firm has an agency agreement with **more
than one client in the same transaction** and has **not** designated separate agents. The firm
represents both, and the practical consequence is that the firm's ability to advocate is
constrained — it cannot place one client's interests ahead of the other's, because both are clients
under § 452.133(2)(a).

**Multiple representation with designated agency.** The firm has agency agreements with more than one
client in the same transaction and has **designated** different associated licensees to represent
each. The designation is what allows each designated agent to advocate for their own client.

### 11.2 What is common to all three

**The seven duties in § 452.133(1) are owed to every party in every structure**, and they do not
change. Honesty and fairness, reasonable skill and care, written disclosure of material adverse
facts, confidentiality that survives, accurate market information, safeguarding trust funds, and
objective presentation of proposals.

What changes between the structures is the **advocacy** — who may negotiate on whose behalf and whose
interests may be placed ahead of whose.

### 11.3 Consent, and the practical conversation

Multiple representation is a structure a client agrees to. The agency agreement is where it is
agreed, the Disclosure to Clients under § 452.135(2) must reach the client **not later than** the
agreement, and the client must understand what changes.

The honest explanation, before consent rather than after: *if the firm ends up on both sides, what
you lose is not honesty or care or disclosure — those are owed to everyone by statute and do not
change. What you lose is the firm arguing for you against the other side on price and terms, because
they are also our client.*

A licensee who cannot say that sentence has not understood the structure well enough to obtain
informed consent to it.

### 11.4 What must never happen in multiple representation

**Confidential information does not cross.** § 452.133(1)(d) protects each party's confidences, and
the duty is owed to each of them separately. A firm that tells one client what the other client would
accept has breached a duty owed to the second, whatever the first client's interests were.

**Material adverse facts still cross**, because (1)(c) is a legal requirement and (2)(a)2's
non-disclosure duty is expressly subject to disclosure required by law.

## Hour 12 — Compensation, contractor status, antitrust, and firm relationships

### 12.1 Broker's and salesperson's compensation — REEB 25.033(3)(e)

Compensation is a term of the **agency agreement**. Three rules a salesperson must hold:

**A salesperson is paid by their firm.** Money flows to the firm, and the firm pays the associated
licensee under their agreement. A salesperson does not take a fee directly from a client.

**§ 452.133(3)(a) prohibits accepting any fee or compensation related to the transaction from any
person other than the licensee's client or firm without the prior written consent of all parties.**

**Who pays does not decide who is represented.** A seller paying a cooperating firm's fee does not
make that firm the seller's agent. Representation follows the agency agreement.

**Fees are negotiable**, and that is not a courtesy — it is the antitrust position, and 12.3 is why.

### 12.2 Independent contractor or employee — REEB 25.033(3)(g)

The rule requires instruction on the difference. It is a **tax, employment and supervision** question
at once, and a salesperson should understand three things.

The classification affects **tax treatment** — withholding, self-employment tax, and benefits — which
is the accountant's territory.

It affects **employment law** obligations, which is counsel's.

It **does not** affect the firm's **supervisory** obligations under ch. 452, and it does not affect
the duties § 452.133 places on the firm for the conduct of licensees associated with it. **§ 452.139(2)(c)**
expressly says nothing in that subsection limits a firm's responsibility under **s. 452.12(3)** for
misrepresentations made by an associated licensee.

So a licensee cannot reason from "I am an independent contractor" to "the firm is not responsible for
me". The tax label and the regulatory structure are different things.

### 12.3 Antitrust — REEB 25.033(3)(f)

The **Sherman Act** prohibits agreements in restraint of trade, and four patterns reach real estate
licensees directly:

**Price fixing.** Any agreement or understanding among **competing firms** about the fees they will
charge. **Commission rates are negotiable between a client and a firm, and no schedule is set by
law.** The conversation among competing licensees about "what everybody charges around here" is the
conversation that produces the case, and the agreement need not be written or even explicit.

**Market allocation.** Agreements among competitors to divide territories, property types, price
bands, or customers.

**Group boycott.** Agreements among competitors to refuse to deal with a particular firm — most often
a discount or non-traditional broker, or one whose business model the others dislike.

**Tying.** Conditioning the availability of one service on the purchase of another. Note that
Wisconsin also has an ethics rule directly on point: **REEB 24.075, tie-in arrangements**, listed at
REEB 25.033(13)(b)1.b and taken in Module WI-10.

**Antitrust exposure is created by conversation.** The safe habits are concrete: never discuss your
firm's fees with a competing firm; state your fee as your own firm's and as negotiable; never agree,
even informally, to avoid or disadvantage a competitor; and leave visibly any conversation that
starts down one of these roads, so that the leaving is remembered.

### 12.4 Firm-to-firm and licensee-to-licensee — REEB 25.033(3)(h) and (i)

Both paragraphs were **amended by CR 23-075**, effective 9-1-24, and the version taught here is the
amended one.

**Firm-to-firm** relationships are cooperation, subagency, and referral arrangements between
brokerage firms. Cooperation between the listing firm and a firm working with a buyer is the ordinary
case, and it does not by itself make either firm the other's agent. **Subagency** under
§ 452.133(4) is a specific relationship entered deliberately. **Referral** arrangements between firms
engage § 452.133(3)(c)'s written disclosure requirement where the licensee may receive compensation.

**§ 452.136(3)** bears directly on firm-to-firm conduct: **a firm and its licensees may not advertise
a property unless the firm is the listing firm or has obtained the listing firm's consent to
advertise it.**

**Licensee-to-licensee** relationships are those within and between firms — teams, mentoring, split
arrangements, and the relationships between designated agents inside one firm. Two constraints follow
from earlier hours: **§ 452.133(1)(d) confidentiality** is owed to each party separately, so
information does not move freely between licensees of the same firm where a party's confidence is at
stake; and **§ 452.133(3)(a)** governs any compensation flowing from anyone other than the licensee's
client or firm.

### 12.5 Responsibilities to third parties — REEB 25.033(3)(d)

The rule lists this separately, and in Wisconsin the answer is unusually clean: **the seven duties in
§ 452.133(1) are owed to every party to whom the firm provides brokerage services**, whether or not
they are a client. A third party who is a party to the transaction is not owed advocacy; they are
owed honesty and fairness, reasonable skill and care, written disclosure of material adverse facts,
confidentiality, accurate market information, safeguarding of trust funds, and objective presentation
of proposals.

That is a longer list than most states owe a customer, and it is the answer to almost every "what do
I owe the other side?" question a Wisconsin salesperson will ask.

### Module WI-02 engagement check

**Question.** Your firm has an agency agreement with a seller. A buyer, unrepresented, asks you to
write their offer. Before you do, they tell you privately that they would go $12,000 above their
opening number. You also know, from the seller, that the basement floods after heavy rain, and you
know that your brother-in-law's inspection company — in which you hold a ten percent interest — is
who you would normally recommend. Work through what you owe, to whom, and in what form.

**Expected answer.**

**The buyer is a party, not a client.** Before negotiating on their behalf, **§ 452.135(1)(a)**
requires the firm to provide the **Disclosure to Customers**. That comes first.

**The flooding is a material adverse fact** the firm knows and that the buyer cannot discover through
reasonably vigilant observation on a dry day. **§ 452.133(1)(c)** requires it be **timely disclosed
in writing** to the buyer. It is owed even though the buyer is not a client, and the seller's
interest in silence does not defeat it: **§ 452.133(2)(a)2's** non-disclosure duty to non-clients is
expressly subject to "unless the disclosure is required by law", and (1)(c) is such a requirement.

**The buyer's $12,000 is confidential.** **§ 452.133(1)(d)** protects information the firm obtains
that it knows a reasonable person would want kept confidential, and it is owed to the **buyer** as a
party. So I may not tell the seller — even though the seller is my client — unless the buyer
**specifically authorises the disclosure of that particular information** or the law requires it. And
the duty **continues after the transaction is complete.**

**The inspection company** engages **§ 452.133(3)(c)**: I may not refer, recommend or suggest the
services of an entity in which I have an interest, or from which I may receive compensation for a
referral, **unless I have disclosed that in writing.** The disclosure comes before the recommendation.

**And when I present the offer to the seller**, **§ 452.133(1)(g)** requires me to present the
proposal **objectively and without bias, disclosing its advantages and disadvantages** — both.
