May 1, 2025

I am pleased to present the 2024 Annual Report (“Report”) of summary statistical information
produced by the Department of Financial and Professional Regulation (“Department”) pursuant to
Public Acts 100-286 and
102-105. The Acts affirm a principle that continues to guide our work: a
criminal conviction alone should not stand in the way of an individual pursuing an occupational
license. We remain steadfast in our mission to eliminate unnecessary barriers to licensure, expand
economic opportunity, and evaluate every applicant on the full a rc of their history, including
evidence of rehabilitation and other mitigating factors.

This year, Illinois again set the standard for fair, individualized review. In 2024, we processed over
122,000 new license applications for the Divisions of Professional Regulation and Real Estate ;
roughly 1,400 of those applicants reported a past criminal conviction. Of those, virtually all whose
convictions did not legally bar them were awarded licenses. The small number of applicants who
chose not to complete the process, for instance, by requesting a hearing or working with our
enforcement staff, accounted for a few defaults. In total, 2 applicants were denied licensure this
year for conviction-related reasons, reflecting the narrow scope of exclusion already set by statute.

Our work dovetails from the Comprehensive Licensing Information to Minimize Barriers
(“CLIMB”) Task Force Report, which convened bipartisan legislators, policy experts,
community members, and Department staff to chart a path toward more transparent, accessible
licensing. While recognizing the critical role licensing plays in safeguarding public health and
safety, CLIMBS’s recommendations shine a spotlight on ways to open doors, particularly for low-
and moderate-income individuals, without compromising professional standards.

In addition to awarding licenses to applicants with criminal convictions, under the Cannabis
Regulation and Tax Act, Illinois pioneered a model approach to social equity in a newly regulated
industry. By removing employment barriers and incentivizing the hiring of pe ople with past
convictions, our cannabis licensing framework is delivering tangible opportunities to communities
long excluded from this emerging field.

Beyond licensing decisions, we continue to deepen our collaboration with the Illinois Department
of Corrections and lead statewide re-entry summits, ensuring that individuals preparing for release
know about and can access licensure pathways. Simultaneously, we are investing in critical
technologies to deliver faster, more user-friendly service for every applicant and licensee.

We have made tremendous progress, but our journey continues. Th is Report underscores our
unwavering commitment to fair ness, impartiality, and the thoughtful application of mitigating
factors, making clear that a past conviction is rarely, in and of itself, a barrier to licensure in Illinois.
We encourage every qualified individual with prior convictions to apply and join the ranks of
licensed professionals serving our communities. When more people can build careers and
contribute their skills, all of Illinois benefits.

Thank you for your interest in our work. We are proud of what we have achieved together and
energized by the opportunities that lie ahead.

INTRODUCTION

P.A. 100- 286 and P.A. 102- 105 amended the Civil Administrative Code of Illinois for the
Department requiring it to prepare, publicly announce, and publish an annual report for the
preceding calendar year that includes the following information for professions under the
Department’s jurisdiction:

• Number of applicants for a new license, certificate, or registration;
• Number of applicants who had a criminal conviction;
• Number of applicants who were granted a new license, certificate, registration;
• Number of applicants who were granted a new license, certificate, or registration with a
criminal conviction;
• Number of applicants who were denied a new license, certificate, or registration;
• Number of applicants who were denied a new license, certificate, or registration in part or
in whole because of a criminal conviction, including the types of criminal convictions;
• Number of licenses issued on probation with a criminal conviction;
• Number of licensees or certificate holders granted an expungement for a record of
discipline based on a conviction predating licensure, certification, or registration or a
criminal charge, arrest, or conviction that was dismissed, sealed, or expunged or di d not
arise from the regulated activity, as a share of the total of such expungement requests.

This report, as required by 20 ILCS 2105/2105-205, is to be completed no later than May 1 of each
year. This Report is made using applicant data from the period of January 01, 2024, through
December 31, 2024.  Please note an applicant who may have begun the application process during
this time may not have completed the process by December 31, 2024, or a decision may have been
issued on an application in 2024 that was submitted in the year prior.
