## Findings and disposition

### REF-IR-01 — Texas homestead VA equity cash-out was insufficiently distinguished

Priority: substantive teaching correction. Status: corrected by author and rechecked in module 08, T08U8/A52.

The initial A52 paired an existing conventional mortgage with a request for cash and directed investigation of a VA cash-out route. A general warning to consider Texas homestead restrictions did not teach the operative obstacle for a Texas homestead equity loan.

The official [Texas Attorney General Opinion KP-0183](https://www2.texasattorneygeneral.gov/opinions/opinions/51paxton/op/2018/kp0183.pdf), pages 3–4, interprets the VA guaranty as additional collateral prohibited for a Texas homestead equity loan by article XVI §50(a)(6)(H). Footnote 7 on page 3 expressly distinguishes traditional VA purchase-money and refinance loans outside subsection (a)(6). The [current constitutional text](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/CN/htm/CN.16.htm#16.50) retains the additional-collateral restriction. This is an Attorney General interpretation supported by the cited constitutional text, not a claim that the opinion is a new court judgment. Its historical discussion of other percentages should not be copied as current law.

The revised teaching now names that distinction, cites the opinion and current Constitution, and cautions against treating a federal product label as the Texas constitutional classification. Revised borrower B is expressly requesting a Texas homestead §50(a)(6) equity cash-out loan with a VA guaranty. Feedback identifies the obstacle without prohibiting every VA purchase or ordinary refinance. This resolves the identified instructional ambiguity on re-read. No conclusion was made about an unstated nonhomestead or direct-loan transaction.

### REF-IR-02 — General refinance break-even was not the IRRRL eligibility recoupment test

Priority: substantive teaching correction. Status: corrected by author and rechecked in module 08, T08U8/A52.

The earlier exercise used $4,200 of financed costs and $70 monthly savings, producing 60 months, but did not distinguish broad economic recovery from the applicable IRRRL included-fee test. A holding-period comparison alone could therefore leave the learner unable to evaluate the proposed program requirement.

[38 U.S.C. §3709(a)(1)–(3)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title38-section3709) provides a 36-month recoupment condition for covered fees and costs, with exclusions. [VA Circular 26-19-22](https://www.benefits.va.gov/HOMELOANS/documents/circulars/26_19_22.pdf), paragraph 3.a, page 2, explains the lower-P&I calculation using included costs whether financed or paid separately, and addresses same/higher-payment loans. Paragraph 3.d, pages 4–5, distinguishes a broader comparison-statement calculation. The statute and these distinctions were read. No proposed 2024 regulatory text was treated as an enacted final rule.

The revised example expressly makes the entire $4,200 an included-cost numerator, supplies a $70 P&I reduction, and correctly identifies 60 months as failing the stated 36-month condition. At 36 months savings are $2,520 and unrecovered included costs $1,680. The revised $2,100 alternative recoups in 30 months, with feedback expressly reserving all other eligibility requirements. These numbers were independently checked. A broad 60-month comparison containing excluded charges is not automatically the same legal result; the revision correctly avoids that overgeneralization by supplying the cost classification.

Priority: instructional-time evidence, not an observed time failure. Status: the specific A26/A27 workload omissions were addressed and re-read; observed-time validation remains separate. The following records the initial examples.

The inspected time plan assigns ten minutes to every applied exercise and two minutes to each primary formative item, while excluding exercise prose from teaching-word counts. - Module 05 A27 supplies an $18,000 purported gift with $300 monthly repayment for five years. Multiplication confirms $18,000, and feedback correctly calls for disclosure as debt. It contains no additional document reconciliation or independently developed alternative facts.

Both are useful learning checks. The concern is counting ten required minutes for each, not their correctness. Recommended disposition: add substantive, unit-relevant decision work with feedback—such as cash-flow/redemption comparisons for A26 and a documented gift/loan/source-of-funds reconciliation for A27—or produce appropriate pilot evidence. Do not lower a reading speed, count remediation twice, or claim an observed duration from a prompt's existence. No substitute duration has been asserted here.

The longer A20–A25/A28 activities and multi-stage comparisons elsewhere are materially deeper: they require calculations, changing assumptions, evidence tables, responsible-party questions, and correction memos. The four required scenarios each contain multiple graded tasks and explicit short-answer rubric elements. Thus this is a targeted weakness, not a claim that all activities are superficial. T05's modeled margin in the inspected table was approximately eleven minutes, so uncertainty in multiple required activity allowances is material to that topic's claimed allocation.
