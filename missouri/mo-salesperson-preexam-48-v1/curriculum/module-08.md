# Missouri Salesperson Pre-Examination Course — Module MO-08

## Hours 40–44 · Fair housing, federal law, land use, and environmental concerns

**Clock hours:** 5. A Missouri clock hour is **60 minutes containing at least 50 minutes of actual
instruction** and no more than 10 minutes of recess — 20 CSR 2250-7.070(2).
**Content basis:** a StateCourse organisation for Commission review. **20 CSR 2250-6.060(1) requires
the 48-hour course to be developed from the testing service's detailed content outline, which this
project has not read.**
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No Missouri pre-license credit is available.

## Learning objectives

1. Name the seven federal protected classes and describe the acts the Fair Housing Act prohibits.
2. Recognise steering, blockbusting and redlining in ordinary language, including when unintended.
3. Distinguish reasonable accommodation from reasonable modification and apply each.
4. Describe the advertising rule and audit a listing description against it.
5. Describe the ADA's application to places of public accommodation in a real estate context.
6. Identify the government and private controls on land use and route each correctly.
7. Recognise the common environmental concerns and state the disclosure obligation that attaches to
   pre-1978 housing.
8. State the licensee's boundary on every subject in this module.

## Hour 40 — The Fair Housing Act

### 40.1 The seven protected classes

Under the federal Fair Housing Act, as amended:

**Race. Colour. Religion. National origin. Sex. Familial status. Disability.**

Race and colour came first, in the Civil Rights Act of 1866, which prohibits **all** racial
discrimination in the sale or rental of property and has **no exemptions at all**. Religion, colour
and national origin were added by Title VIII of the Civil Rights Act of 1968; sex in 1974; and
familial status and disability — the statute's term is *handicap* — by the Fair Housing Amendments Act
of 1988.

**Familial status** means households with children under eighteen, and includes pregnant persons and
those in the process of securing custody. The principal exemption is qualified **housing for older
persons**.

**These seven are the federal floor.** State and local law may protect additional classes, and a
Missouri licensee's actual obligations are the sum of federal, Missouri and any applicable local
ordinance. **This course states no Missouri or local protected class**, because the Missouri Human
Rights Act and municipal ordinances were not read from primary text for this project. **Read them.**
The classes protected in Kansas City, in St. Louis and under state law are not necessarily the same
seven, and a licensee who works from the federal list alone will eventually be wrong.

### 40.2 What the Act prohibits

- Refusing to sell, rent or negotiate, or otherwise making housing unavailable, because of a protected
  characteristic.
- Discriminating in the terms, conditions, privileges, services or facilities.
- **Advertising** that indicates a preference, limitation or discrimination.
- Representing that housing is unavailable when it is available.
- **Blockbusting** — inducing sale or rental for profit by representations about the entry of persons
  of a protected class.
- **Steering** — directing prospects toward or away from areas on the basis of a protected
  characteristic.
- **Redlining** — discrimination in lending or insurance on the basis of the composition of an area.
- Denying access to a multiple listing service or other facility on a protected basis.
- **Coercing, intimidating, threatening or interfering** with a person exercising a fair housing
  right — or **retaliating** against someone who has asserted one or assisted another to.

### 40.3 The exemptions, and why they change nothing for you

The Act contains narrow exemptions — for certain single-family sales by an owner without a broker and
without discriminatory advertising, and for owner-occupied dwellings of up to four units.

**Every one of them is lost the moment a licensee is involved.** They also do not apply to race under
the 1866 Act, and they do not permit discriminatory advertising in any case. A licensee should know
the exemptions exist and should never expect to operate inside one.

### 40.4 Enforcement

A complaint may be filed with HUD, generally within one year of the alleged act, or a civil action may
be brought in federal court within two years. Remedies include actual and punitive damages, injunctive
relief, civil penalties and attorney's fees. The Department of Justice may bring a pattern-or-practice
action.

And a Missouri licensee faces a second track: § 339.730.1(6) and § 339.740.1(6), RSMo, make compliance
with **fair housing and civil rights statutes and regulations** a statutory duty of the limited agent
— so a fair housing violation is also a licence-law violation.

## Hour 41 — The practices, disability, and advertising

### 41.1 Steering, in ordinary conversation

Steering rarely announces itself. It usually arrives as a helpful remark:

> "You'd probably be more comfortable over on the east side."
> "That neighbourhood is really more for young families."
> "Let me show you where people like you tend to buy."
> "I'd stay north of the river if I were you — better element."

Every one of those is steering, and none of them names a protected class. Steering is also committed
by **omission** — by simply not showing or not mentioning areas that meet the buyer's stated criteria.

**The method that prevents it** is to answer with **data the buyer can verify** and to describe
**property**, never people:

- The buyer asks about schools → give them the district's published data and the state's report, and
  tell them to visit. Do not characterise a school as good or bad, because "good schools" functions as
  a proxy in practice.
- The buyer asks about crime → route them to the police department's published statistics.
- The buyer asks "what kind of people live there" → *"I can't characterise the people in an area, and
  I wouldn't be much good at it. Tell me what you need in a property and I'll search on that."*
- The buyer names their own protected characteristic and asks you to search on it → the answer is
  still no, gently and clearly. A buyer's own request does not create an exemption.

### 41.2 The client's discriminatory instruction

A seller says: *don't show it to anyone who would change the character of the street.* A landlord
says: *no children on the second floor.*

**Refuse. Say plainly that you cannot follow the instruction. Withdraw from the representation if it
stands.**

Three things that are **not** compliance: following the instruction while noting your objection in the
file; applying it "informally"; or asking the client to put it in writing. A written discriminatory
instruction is evidence **against** the licensee, not authority for them.

### 41.3 Disability: accommodation and modification

**Reasonable accommodation** — a change in a **rule, policy, practice or service** necessary to give a
person with a disability equal opportunity to use and enjoy a dwelling. Waiving a no-pets rule for an
assistance animal; a reserved accessible parking space; permitting a rent payment date that matches a
benefit deposit. Generally at the **housing provider's** expense.

**Reasonable modification** — a **physical change** to the premises. A grab bar, a ramp, a widened
doorway, a lowered counter. Generally at the **requesting person's** expense, and the provider may
require restoration of the interior at the end of the tenancy in appropriate cases.

**Rule-change versus physical-change is the whole distinction**, and it is tested constantly and
confused constantly.

**Assistance animals are not pets.** Pet rules, pet rent and pet deposits do not apply to them, and
the questions that may be asked about them are narrowly limited.

**Design and construction.** Covered multifamily dwellings built for first occupancy after March 1991
must meet the Act's accessibility requirements. A licensee marketing new multifamily should know the
requirement exists and route the compliance question to the developer and counsel.

**What may not be asked:** whether a prospect has a disability, the nature or severity of one, or to
see medical records.

### 41.4 Advertising

**Describe the property. Never the buyer, the tenant, or the neighbours.**

Problem phrases and why:

| Phrase | Problem |
|---|---|
| "Perfect for a young professional couple" | age, familial status, marital status |
| "Great family neighbourhood" | familial status |
| "Walk to St. Anne's" | religion |
| "Adult building" | familial status |
| "Must be able to climb stairs" | disability |
| "Exclusive area" | historically coded, and describes people |
| "Bachelor pad" | sex, familial status |
| "Safe neighbourhood" | frequently a proxy, and a claim you cannot support |

Acceptable in the same breath: "two bedrooms," "third floor, no lift," "0.4 miles from the Route 9
bus," "1,720 square feet," "attached two-car garage."

**Auditing an advertisement, line by line.** For every sentence ask three questions: **Who is
speaking?** — the firm must be identifiable, not disguised as a private party. **What is claimed?** —
and can it be supported by something written down. **Who is described?** — if the answer is a person
rather than a building, rewrite it.

And note the school-side rule that governs this course's own advertising: 20 CSR 2250-7.010(1)(L)5
requires every advertisement of an approved course to state **the number of hours of education credit
and the correct title of the course as stated in the Commission's approval letter**, and (L)6 forbids
advertising a course at all until it is approved in writing.

## Hour 42 — Other federal law

### 42.1 ECOA

The **Equal Credit Opportunity Act** prohibits discrimination in **credit** on the basis of race,
colour, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age (provided the applicant can contract), or
because income derives from a public assistance programme, or because the applicant has exercised a
right under the Consumer Credit Protection Act.

Note two classes ECOA covers that the Fair Housing Act does not: **marital status** and **age**. And
note that a rejected applicant is entitled to notice of the reasons.

### 42.2 The ADA

The **Americans with Disabilities Act** governs **places of public accommodation** and commercial
facilities — Title III — and employment — Title I. In a real estate context this reaches offices,
retail, restaurants and other commercial premises, requiring the removal of architectural barriers
where readily achievable.

**A real estate office is itself a place of public accommodation.** So is an open house held in a
commercial property.

The ADA and the Fair Housing Act cover different ground and are frequently confused. FHA is about
**housing**; ADA Title III is about **public accommodations**. Some property is both.

### 42.3 Antitrust

Four practices, and every one of them is a felony exposure rather than a licence problem:

**Price fixing** — any agreement or understanding among competing firms about commission rates or
fees. Commission is negotiable between the firm and its client, and **any** conversation with a
competitor about what "we" charge is a conversation to leave.

**Market allocation** — agreeing with competitors to divide territories, property types or customer
groups.

**Group boycott** — agreeing with competitors to refuse to deal with a particular broker or business
model.

**Tying** — conditioning the sale of one product or service on the purchase of another.

**The practical rule:** never discuss your firm's commission structure with a competitor, in any
setting, however casual. Do not attend the conversation. Leave it, and be seen to leave it.

### 42.4 Telephone, text and email marketing

The **Telephone Consumer Protection Act** and the **National Do Not Call Registry** govern
telemarketing calls, and the rules on autodialled calls and text messages are stricter than most
licensees assume. Firms must maintain an internal do-not-call list and honour requests. **CAN-SPAM**
governs commercial email, requiring accurate headers and subject lines, a physical postal address and
a working opt-out that is honoured promptly.

**This course states no do-not-call registration period, exemption or penalty amount**, because
neither the TCPA nor the FCC rules nor CAN-SPAM was read from primary text for this project. Prospecting
practice is set by the firm's policy, written with counsel, and followed.

## Hour 43 — Land use control

### 43.1 Public controls

**The comprehensive plan** is the municipality's long-range policy document. Zoning is meant to
implement it.

**Zoning** divides a jurisdiction into districts and regulates **use** and **dimension** — setbacks,
height, lot coverage, density. The instruments a licensee will hear named:

- **Variance** — permission to depart from a **dimensional** requirement because of a hardship arising
  from the property itself. Granted by a board of adjustment or appeals on its own standards.
- **Conditional or special use permit** — a use the ordinance contemplates subject to review and
  conditions.
- **Rezoning** — a legislative change to the district or the map.
- **Legal nonconforming use** — a use that lawfully predates the current ordinance and is allowed to
  continue **subject to limits** on expansion, on resumption after abandonment, and on rebuilding
  after destruction. Those limits are the point, and a buyer who assumes the use is permanent and
  unrestricted may find they cannot rebuild after a fire.

**Building codes** set minimum construction standards, enforced through permits and inspections. They
are separate from zoning, and a code-compliant building is **not** a defect-free building. A permit
history records what was applied for, not what was built; unpermitted work is a routine finding and is
routed to the municipality.

### 43.2 Subdivision regulation

Governs the division of land into lots and the standards for the plat, streets, utilities and
dedications. It is a municipal and county process, and a licensee working raw land routes plat
questions to the municipality, the county and a surveyor **before anything is marketed**.

**This course states no Missouri platting requirement**, because Missouri's subdivision chapter has not
been read from primary text for this project.

### 43.3 Private controls

**Restrictive covenants** recorded against the land, enforced by an association or by benefited owners.
As Module MO-02 Hour 9 established: they are **separate from zoning** and **the more restrictive
controls**.

And the necessary qualification: **a covenant that discriminates on a protected basis is unenforceable
and void.** Racially restrictive covenants appear in the older records of essentially every American
city, Missouri's included. They have no legal effect. A licensee who encounters one does not repeat
it, does not describe the neighbourhood by reference to it, and routes the question of removing it from
the record to counsel.

### 43.4 The licensee's boundary, stated once for the whole hour

**Identify the question, tell the client which authority answers it, and see that it is answered
before their contingencies run.** Never state what a municipality will permit. Never assume a current
use is permitted merely because it exists. Section 339.730.1(3)(d), RSMo — the duty to advise the
client to obtain expert advice on material matters beyond the licensee's expertise — is the statutory
expression of all of it.

## Hour 44 — Environmental concerns

### 44.1 The list, and the rule that governs all of it

The concerns a residential licensee will meet: **lead-based paint; radon; asbestos; mould; underground
storage tanks; methamphetamine contamination; well and septic systems; wetlands and floodplain;
formaldehyde; groundwater and soil contamination; and, in some Missouri markets, mine subsidence and
sinkholes.**

**One rule governs every item on that list: recognise, disclose, route.** Recognise the issue. Disclose
what you know or should know, under § 339.730.1(3)(c) and § 339.730.3, RSMo. Route the determination to
a qualified professional or the responsible agency. **A licensee assesses no environmental condition.**

### 44.2 Lead-based paint — the one with a federal obligation on the licensee

For **target housing built before 1978**, the federal Residential Lead-Based Paint Hazard Reduction Act
and its implementing rule require, in a sale:

- delivery of the EPA-approved **pamphlet**;
- **disclosure** of any known lead-based paint and hazards and delivery of any available records and
  reports;
- a **ten-day opportunity** for the purchaser to conduct a risk assessment or inspection, which the
  parties may agree in writing to change;
- an **attachment or disclosure form** signed by the parties and by the **agent**; and
- **record retention** of the completed disclosure.

**The agent's signature is on that form.** This is one of the very few places where a licensee is not a
bystander to someone else's disclosure obligation but a participant with an obligation of their own,
including an affirmative duty to ensure compliance.

**1978 is the line, and it is a line about the housing rather than about the paint.** A pre-1978 house
triggers the requirement whether or not anyone believes there is lead in it.

### 44.3 The others, briefly, with the routing

**Radon.** Missouri has areas of elevated radon potential. Testing is by a qualified tester; mitigation
by a qualified contractor. A licensee neither tests nor interprets a result.

**Asbestos.** Common in pre-1980 construction — insulation, floor tile, siding, pipe wrap. Intact
material is often best left undisturbed; disturbance is the risk. Renovation plans on an older house
are a routing trigger.

**Mould.** Route to a qualified professional. The underlying question is almost always a moisture
source, and a licensee who opines on health effects has strayed a long way outside the licence.

**Underground storage tanks.** Heating oil tanks on older properties and agricultural tanks on rural
land. Route to a qualified consultant and the responsible state agency.

**Methamphetamine contamination.** A serious and specific issue in parts of Missouri. Route to the
responsible agency and a qualified remediation professional; a known history is disclosed.

**Well and septic.** On rural Missouri property these decide habitability and financeability. Route to
the county health authority and a qualified inspector.

**Wetlands and floodplain.** An inventory map is an inventory, not a determination. Route to the
responsible agency and a consultant. **Flood insurance is a separate policy** — the standard homeowners
policy excludes flood, and a buyer in or near a mapped zone needs to be told to get a quote **early**,
because it can change what they can afford.

**Mine subsidence and sinkholes.** Real in parts of Missouri. Route to the responsible state agency, a
qualified engineer and the insurer.

### 44.4 Missouri's disclosure position, stated carefully

**This course states no Missouri seller disclosure requirement**, because whatever governs Missouri
residential seller disclosure practice was not read from primary text for this project.

What this course does state, because it **was** read, is the statutory duty: under § 339.730.1(3)(c) a
licensee discloses to the **client** all adverse material facts actually known or that should have been
known; under § 339.730.3 and § 339.740.3 a licensee discloses to any **customer** all adverse material
facts actually known or that should have been known; and under § 339.755(2)(d) a transaction broker
discloses to each party those of which the licensee has actual notice or knowledge. Under
§ 339.710(1)(a) **environmental hazards affecting the property** are on the statute's own list of
adverse material facts.

That duty is enough to decide almost every environmental question a licensee will face. **If you know
it, and it affects value, and the other side cannot reasonably discover it — disclose it, in writing.**
