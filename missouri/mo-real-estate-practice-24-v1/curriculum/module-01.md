# Missouri Real Estate Practice Course — Module MREP-01

## Hours 1–4 · The Broker Disclosure Form and the obligations of agents to clients and customers

**Commission topics covered:** 20 CSR 2250-6.060(2)(A) Broker Disclosure Form · (H) Obligations of
Agents to Clients and Customers
**Clock hours:** 4. A Missouri clock hour is **60 minutes containing at least 50 minutes of actual
instruction** and no more than 10 minutes of recess — 20 CSR 2250-7.070(2).
**Hour allocation:** a StateCourse proposal for Commission review. **20 CSR 2250-6.060(2) lists

**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No Missouri pre-license credit is available.

## What this course is, and how it differs from the 48-hour course

The 48-hour Salesperson Pre-Examination Course is built, under 20 CSR 2250-6.060(1), from the
**testing service's** detailed content outline. It teaches the body of knowledge the licensing
examination tests.

**This course is different in kind.** Under 20 CSR 2250-6.060(2) the Commission itself lists **sixteen
topics** the Missouri Real Estate Practice Course *must address*, and reading that list tells you what
it is for. Broker disclosure form. Listing a property. Buyer representation. Dual agent or transaction
broker. Termination of brokerage relationships. Compensation. Cooperating with other licensees.
Obligations of agents to clients and customers. Pricing property. Role of the appraiser. Types of
financing. Estimating closing costs. Contracts. Earnest money. Avoiding misrepresentation. Types of
construction.

Fourteen of the sixteen are about **what a Missouri licensee actually does in a transaction**, and
eight of them are about **brokerage relationships specifically**. The Commission is not asking for more
theory. It is asking that a person about to be licensed in Missouri knows how Missouri transactions
work and where the licensee's obligations sit.

**The topics are the Commission's; the hour allocation and the grouping into six modules are
StateCourse's**, and every module in this course says so.

## Learning objectives

1. State the three conditions that trigger the § 339.770.1 broker disclosure obligation and the one
   exception in § 339.770.2.
2. Describe what the broker disclosure form is for and route its content correctly.
3. Distinguish client from customer using the § 339.710 definitions.
4. Recite the six duties owed to a client under §§ 339.730.1 and 339.740.1.
5. State exactly what is owed to a customer and what is not.
6. Identify an adverse material fact under § 339.710(1), including the item that is not about the
   property.
7. Apply the confidentiality rule and its three exceptions.
8. Describe a firm's § 339.760 written policy and explain why you read it before your first
   appointment.

## Hour 1 — The Broker Disclosure Form — 20 CSR 2250-6.060(2)(A)

### 1.1 The statutory trigger, read exactly

Section 339.770.1, RSMo:

> In a residential real estate transaction, at the earliest practicable opportunity during or following
> the first substantial contact by the designated broker or the affiliated licensees with a seller,
> landlord, buyer, or tenant who has not entered into a written agreement for services as described in
> subdivision (5) of section 339.710, the licensee shall provide that person with a written copy of the
> current broker disclosure form which has been prescribed by the commission.

**Three conditions, and all three must be present:**

1. **Residential** real estate transaction. Commercial is outside this section. Note that § 339.710(7)
   defines commercial real estate as real estate **other than** one-to-four residential units or
   agricultural and horticultural property under § 137.016, and expressly excludes single-family units
   including condominiums and townhouses sold unit-by-unit.
2. **First substantial contact** — at the earliest practicable opportunity during or following it.
3. **The person has not entered into a written agreement for services.**

### 1.2 The exception

Section 339.770.2: where the person **has already** entered into a written agreement for services with
a designated broker, **no other licensee is required to make the disclosure.**

That is a narrow exception and it is easy to misapply. It applies where the person already has a
written agreement — not where they have merely been working with someone, not where they say they have
an agent, not where you assume they must.

### 1.3 What "first substantial contact" means, and what this course will not do

**The statute does not define it, and this course will not invent a definition.**

What the course can say is where the safe practice lies. Ministerial acts under § 339.710(19) — a
telephone inquiry about price or location, a question at an open house, setting a showing appointment —
are informative in nature and expressly do not rise to the level requiring a brokerage relationship. So
they are not, in themselves, substantial contact.

But the moment a conversation stops being about the availability of information and starts being about
**this person's transaction** — their needs, their finances, their motivation, what they should offer,
what they should do — you are past the point. **Give the form early.** Nobody has ever been disciplined
for giving the broker disclosure form too soon.

### 1.4 What is on the form, and where you get it

**This course does not reproduce the broker disclosure form, state its fields, or describe its layout,
because the Commission-prescribed form was not read from primary text for this project.**

What the statute requires is a written copy of **the current** form **prescribed by the Commission**.
Two operative words:

- **Current.** Forms are revised. The copy in your firm's drawer, or the PDF someone emailed you in
  your first week, may be superseded. Get it from the Commission and check that what you are handing
  out is what the Commission currently prescribes.
- **Prescribed by the Commission.** Not your firm's version of it, not a summary, not a form you have
  redesigned to look nicer.

And § 339.770.3 supplies the payoff for doing it right: **disclosures made in accordance with
§§ 339.710 to 339.860 are sufficient as a matter of law** to disclose brokerage relationships to the
public.

### 1.5 What the form is not

**It is not an agency agreement.** Giving someone the broker disclosure form does not make them your
client, and receiving it does not commit them to anything. It is a **disclosure** — it tells a consumer
what the relationships are so that they can decide which one they want.

**It is not a substitute for the written agreement.** Section 339.780 requires a written agreement
before you act in any brokerage capacity. The disclosure form and the written agreement are two
different documents doing two different jobs, and a licensee who has done the first and not the second
has not complied with § 339.780.

### 1.6 A practice routine

Before every first appointment with a person you have not worked with:

1. Is this **residential**?
2. Is this **first substantial contact** — or past it?
3. Do they **already have a written agreement** with a designated broker?

Residential, yes, no → **the current Commission broker disclosure form goes into their hands, in
writing, and the fact that you gave it goes into your file with the date.**

## Hour 2 — Client and customer — 20 CSR 2250-6.060(2)(H)

### 2.1 Two definitions that decide everything downstream

**Client** — § 339.710(6), RSMo: *a seller, landlord, buyer, or tenant who has entered into a brokerage
relationship with a licensee pursuant to sections 339.710 to 339.860.*

**Customer** — § 339.710(10): *an actual or potential seller, landlord, buyer, or tenant in a real
estate transaction in which a licensee is involved but who has not entered into a brokerage
relationship with the licensee.*

The difference is created by **entering into a brokerage relationship** — which, under § 339.780,
means a **written agreement** in every case except ministerial acts.

### 2.2 What does NOT create a client

This is where new licensees go wrong, and it is worth listing:

- **Not rapport.** You may have spent nine Saturdays with someone.
- **Not time.** You may have shown them thirty houses.
- **Not helpfulness.** You may have answered every question they ever had.
- **Not who pays.** Section 339.800.2 is explicit: *payment of compensation by itself shall not
  establish an agency relationship or transaction brokerage relationship between the party who paid the
  compensation and the designated broker or any affiliated licensee.*

**A written agreement creates a client. Nothing else does.**

The corollary is a hazard: a licensee who behaves like a buyer's agent for weeks without a written
agreement has created a person who **believes** they are a client, is legally a customer, and will be
furious when the difference surfaces. That is a complaint waiting to happen, and it is entirely
avoidable.

### 2.3 Ministerial acts, and why the category exists

Section 339.710(19) defines **ministerial acts** as acts *informative in nature* that *do not rise to
the level which requires the creation of a brokerage relationship*. The statute's own examples:

- responding to telephone inquiries about the availability and pricing of brokerage services;
- responding to telephone inquiries about the price or location of property;
- attending an open house and responding to questions about the property from a consumer;
- setting an appointment to view property;
- responding to questions of consumers walking into a licensee's office.

The category exists so that ordinary courtesy does not accidentally create a representation. Answer the
phone. Hold the open house. Set the showing.

**But note what ministerial acts do NOT suspend:** the duty every licensee owes every **customer** to
disclose adverse material facts. That duty does not wait for an agreement, and Hour 4 takes it up.

### 2.4 Read your firm's written policy first

Section 339.760: **every designated broker who has affiliated licensees shall adopt a written policy
which identifies and describes the relationships in which the designated broker and affiliated
licensees may engage** with any seller, landlord, buyer or tenant.

This is not paperwork. It tells you **which brokerage relationships you are permitted to offer**, and it
is not the same at every Missouri firm. Some offer dual agency; some route every in-house situation to
transaction brokerage; some appoint designated agents under § 339.820. You practise your firm's policy,
not your preference, and you read it on your first day rather than on the day it matters.

## Hour 3 — The six duties to a client

### 3.1 The list

Section 339.730.1 for a seller's or landlord's agent; § 339.740.1 for a buyer's or tenant's agent. They
are the same six, mirrored:

**1. To perform the terms of the written agreement made with the client.**

Which presupposes there is one, and that you have read it. The agreement is the first source of your
obligations and it can add to the statutory floor — § 339.780.8 expressly preserves the public's
freedom to contract for more.

**2. To exercise reasonable skill and care for the client.**

The most-cited duty and the least specific. In daily practice it is mostly unglamorous competence:
calendaring deadlines the day the contract is accepted, confirming things in writing, checking that
what you repeated is what the document says, raising a problem before it becomes one.

**3. To promote the interests of the client with the utmost good faith, loyalty, and fidelity**,
including four specified things:

- **(a) Seeking a price and terms acceptable to the client** — with the express carve-out that the
  licensee is **not obligated to seek additional offers** while the property is already subject to a
  contract for sale, or a lease or letter of intent to lease.
- **(b) Presenting all written offers to and from the client in a timely manner regardless of whether
  the property is subject to a contract** for sale or lease.
- **(c) Disclosing to the client all adverse material facts actually known or that should have been
  known** by the licensee.
- **(d) Advising the client to obtain expert advice** as to material matters about which the licensee
  knows but the specifics of which are beyond the licensee's expertise.

**4. To account in a timely manner for all money and property received.**

**5. To comply** with §§ 339.710–339.860, § 339.100.2, and the rules under them.

**6. To comply with applicable federal, state and local laws, rules, regulations and ordinances,
including fair housing and civil rights statutes and regulations.**

### 3.2 (3)(a) and (3)(b) read together

These two are a matched pair and they are misread constantly. Once a property is under contract you
**need not go looking for more offers** — that is (a)'s carve-out. But if a written offer arrives
anyway, **you must still present it** — that is (b), and its "regardless of whether the property is
subject to a contract" clause leaves no room.

Sellers ask about this constantly. The accurate answer is: *I am not required to market for further
offers while we are under contract, but if one arrives in writing I am required to bring it to you, and
what you do with it is a legal question about your existing contract that you should take to counsel.*

### 3.3 (3)(d) is the sentence that keeps you inside your licence

The duty is **not** to answer the legal, tax, engineering, environmental or appraisal question. It is to
**know the question exists and tell the client to get it answered by someone qualified.**

That is the entire obligation, and doing more than it is how licensees end up practising law. It is also
a genuine service: the client who is told in week one that they need a lawyer to look at the easement is
far better served than the client who is reassured in week one and finds out in week five.

### 3.4 Duty 6 is not decoration

Section 339.730.1(6) makes compliance with **fair housing and civil rights statutes and regulations** a
statutory duty of the limited agent under Missouri licence law. A fair housing violation by a Missouri
licensee is therefore **both** a fair housing matter **and** a licence-law matter, prosecuted on two
separate tracks by two separate authorities, neither of which waits for the other.

## Hour 4 — The customer sentence, adverse material facts, confidentiality

### 4.1 What is owed to a customer

Section 339.730.3 — and § 339.740.3 in the mirror — is the most consequential sentence in Missouri
brokerage law and it should be known by heart:

> A licensee acting as a seller's or landlord's agent **owes no duty or obligation to a customer, except
> that a licensee shall disclose to any customer all adverse material facts actually known or that
> should have been known** by the licensee.

The same subsection continues: the agent **owes no duty to conduct an independent inspection** or to
discover adverse material facts for the benefit of the customer, and **owes no duty to independently
verify** the accuracy or completeness of any statement made by the client or by any independent
inspector.

Section 339.740.3 adds the buyer-side analogue: a buyer's agent **owes no duty to conduct an independent
investigation of the client's financial condition for the benefit of the customer.**

**So the structure is: everything to the client; to the customer, honesty about adverse material facts.**

That residue is not small. **A licensee cannot conceal a known defect from the other side and then say
the buyer was not their client.** That defence does not exist in Missouri, and a licensee who tries it
has usually made their situation worse.

### 4.2 Adverse material fact — the definition, read exactly

Section 339.710(1): *a fact related to the property not reasonably ascertainable or known to a party
which negatively affects the value of the property.*

Three elements, each doing work. **Related to the property.** **Not reasonably ascertainable or known**
to the party — a fact a buyer can see for themselves is not concealed by not mentioning it. **Negatively
affects value.**

The statute's own list of what such facts **may** include:

- **(a)** environmental hazards affecting the property;
- **(b)** physical condition of the property which adversely affects value;
- **(c)** material defects in the property;
- **(d)** material defects in the **title** to the property;
- **(e)** **material limitation of the party's ability to perform under the terms of the contract.**

### 4.3 Item (e), which is not about the property at all

**(e) is about a PARTY, not a building.** A buyer whose financing has collapsed. A seller who cannot
deliver clear title. A party who has told you they cannot close.

Licensees skip it because the other four are about houses. It belongs on the list because a party's
inability to perform destroys value in a transaction as surely as a failed foundation does — and because
the licensee is usually the first person to find out.

### 4.4 The two standards

Missouri applies **two different disclosure standards** depending on the relationship:

- A **limited agent** — §§ 339.730.1(3)(c), 339.730.3, 339.740 — discloses adverse material facts
  **actually known or that should have been known.**
- A **transaction broker** — § 339.755(2)(d) — discloses those **of which the licensee has actual notice
  or knowledge.**

"Should have been known" is objective and makes **wilful ignorance unavailable**. A licensee who
arranges not to learn something has not escaped the duty.

**The professional method is not to litigate which standard covers a fact you already know. It is to put
it in writing, to everyone entitled to it, and move on.**

### 4.5 Confidentiality, and where it stops

**Confidential information** — § 339.710(9) — is information obtained from the client and designated
confidential by the client; information made confidential by §§ 339.710–339.860 or any other statute or
regulation; or written instructions from the client. **Unless** it is or becomes public by the client's
own words or conduct, or from a source other than the licensee.

Sections 339.730.2 and 339.740.2 forbid disclosing it **unless**:

1. disclosure is **required by statute, rule or regulation**;
2. **failure to disclose would constitute a misrepresentation**; or
3. disclosure is necessary to **defend the licensee** against an action of wrongful conduct in an
   administrative or judicial proceeding or before a professional committee.

And: **no cause of action arises against the licensee for making any required or permitted disclosure.**

**Exception 2 is where confidentiality meets adverse-material-fact disclosure, and it decides the hard
cases.** A client cannot make a fact confidential simply by asking. A seller who tells you in confidence
that the basement floods has not sealed your mouth — they have told you an adverse material fact, and
§ 339.730.3 requires you to disclose it to any customer.

The right response in the room is to say so at the time: *I have to tell you that this is something I'll
need to disclose. Let's talk about how we handle it.* Sellers accept that far better than they accept
finding out afterwards.

### 4.6 What survives the end of the relationship

Section 339.790.1: the relationships **commence on the effective date of the broker's agreement** and
continue until performance, completion, termination or expiration.

Section 339.790.2: after that, **no further duty except** (1) accounting in a timely manner for money
and property received during the relationship, and (2) **treating as confidential information provided
by the client during the relationship that may reasonably be expected to have a negative impact on the
client's real estate activity** — unless written consent, disclosure required by law, the information
became public otherwise than through the brokerage, or disclosure is needed to defend against an action
of wrongful conduct.

**Confidentiality outlives the relationship; the rest does not.** A licensee who learned that a divorce
was forcing a sale does not become free to mention it when the listing expires.
