# Missouri Real Estate Practice Course — Module MREP-03

## Hours 10–13 · Dual agency, transaction brokerage, termination, compensation, and cooperation

**Commission topics covered:** 20 CSR 2250-6.060(2)(D) Acting as a Dual Agent or Transaction Broker ·
(E) Termination of Brokerage Relationships · (F) Compensation · (G) Cooperating with Other Licensees
**Clock hours:** 4. A Missouri clock hour is **60 minutes containing at least 50 minutes of actual
instruction** — 20 CSR 2250-7.070(2).
**Hour allocation:** a StateCourse proposal for Commission review; 20 CSR 2250-6.060(2) allocates no
hours among its sixteen topics.
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No Missouri pre-license credit is available.

## Learning objectives

1. State how dual agency is created and recite the five items a dual agent may not disclose.
2. State what a dual agent MAY disclose, and why dual agency is not a vow of silence.
3. Describe the transaction broker's duties and the two ways they differ from a limited agent's.
4. Explain designated agency under § 339.820 and where its protection stops.
5. State when a brokerage relationship commences and exactly what survives its end.
6. Apply § 339.800 to a compensation question, including who may pay and what payment does not create.
7. Describe cooperation and subagency and state what a cooperating licensee owes each party.

## Hour 10 — Acting as a dual agent — 20 CSR 2250-6.060(2)(D)

### 10.1 Creation

Section 339.750.1, RSMo: **a licensee may act as a dual agent only with the consent of all parties to
the transaction. Consent shall be presumed by a written agreement pursuant to section 339.780.**

Section 339.780.4: before engaging in any of the § 339.010 activities, a designated broker intending to
act as a dual agent **shall enter into a written agreement with the seller and buyer** (or landlord and
tenant) permitting the broker to serve as a dual agent, and the agreement **shall include the § 339.750
duties and responsibilities and the terms of compensation**.

So dual agency is created **by written agreement with both sides, in advance**. It is not created by
circumstance, and a licensee who finds themselves on both sides without one has a problem rather than a
relationship.

### 10.2 What a dual agent is

Section 339.750.2: a dual agent is **a limited agent for both** the seller and buyer, or landlord and
tenant, **and shall have the duties and obligations required by §§ 339.730 and 339.740** unless the
section provides otherwise.

Both sets of duties. To both parties. Simultaneously. The statute does not pretend that undivided
loyalty is possible in that position — it narrows what a dual agent may say instead.

### 10.3 The five items — § 339.750.4

**Shall not be disclosed by a dual agent without the consent of the client to whom the information
pertains:**

1. That a **buyer or tenant is willing to pay more** than the purchase price or lease rate offered for
   the property.
2. That a **seller or landlord is willing to accept less** than the asking price or lease rate.
3. **What the motivating factors are** for any client buying, selling or leasing the property.
4. That a client **will agree to financing terms other than those offered.**
5. **The terms of any prior offers or counter offers** made by any party.

Learn these five. They are examinable and they are the practical content of dual agency.

Notice what they have in common: each is a piece of **negotiating advantage**. What stays in is the
information one side could use against the other in the negotiation. Everything else about the
transaction is on a different footing.

### 10.4 What a dual agent MAY disclose

Section 339.750.3: **except as provided in subsections 4 and 5, a dual agent may disclose any
information to one client that the licensee gains from the other client if the information is material
to the transaction** — unless it is confidential information as defined in § 339.710.

**Dual agency is not a vow of silence.** It is a specific list of five things that stay in, plus
confidential information. A material fact about the property does not become unmentionable because the
licensee is a dual agent — and in fact the adverse material fact duties under §§ 339.730 and 339.740
continue to apply on both sides.

Section 339.750.5: a dual agent shall not disclose to one client any **confidential information** about
the other unless disclosure is required by statute, rule or regulation, or **failure to disclose would
constitute a misrepresentation**, or disclosure is necessary to defend against an action of wrongful
conduct. **No cause of action arises for making any required or permitted disclosure, and a dual agent
does not terminate the dual agency by making one.**

### 10.5 No imputation

Section 339.750.6: **in a dual agency relationship there shall be no imputation of knowledge or
information between the client and the dual agent or among persons within an entity engaged as a dual
agent.**

Under general agency law, what the agent knows is imputed to the principal. Section 339.750.6 switches
that off for dual agency — otherwise every dual agency would immediately impute each side's confidences
to the other and the arrangement would be impossible.

## Hour 11 — Acting as a transaction broker — 20 CSR 2250-6.060(2)(D)

### 11.1 What a transaction broker is

Section 339.755.1: **a real estate licensee may provide real estate service to any party in a
prospective transaction without an agency or fiduciary relationship to one or more parties. Such
licensee shall be called a transaction broker.**

Section 339.710's definition adds the character of the role: a transaction broker **assists the parties
without an agency or fiduciary relationship to either party and is, therefore, neutral, serving neither
as an advocate or advisor for either party to the transaction.**

**Neutral. Not an advocate. Not an advisor.** That is the difference from limited agency, and it must be
explained to a consumer in those terms — because a consumer who thinks they have an advocate and has a
neutral facilitator has been misled about the most important feature of the arrangement.

### 11.2 The duties — § 339.755.2

1. **Perform the terms of any written or oral agreement** made with any party. Note that this is the one
   place in the Missouri scheme where the statute contemplates an **oral** agreement — though
   § 339.780.6 requires a **written** transaction brokerage agreement where the broker **expects to
   receive compensation from the party assisted.**
2. **Exercise reasonable skill, care and diligence**, including:
   - **(a)** presenting all written offers and counteroffers in a timely manner regardless of whether
     the property is subject to a contract, lease or letter of intent, unless the agreement provides
     otherwise;
   - **(b)** **informing the parties regarding the transaction and suggesting that they obtain expert
     advice** as to material matters beyond the broker's expertise;
   - **(c)** **accounting in a timely manner** for all money and property received;
   - **(d)** **disclosing to each party any adverse material facts of which the licensee has actual
     notice or knowledge**;
   - **(e)** **assisting the parties in complying with the terms and conditions of any contract.**

### 11.3 The two differences that matter

**First: the disclosure standard.** A limited agent discloses adverse material facts **actually known or
that should have been known** — §§ 339.730.1(3)(c), 339.730.3, 339.740. A transaction broker discloses
those **of which the licensee has actual notice or knowledge** — § 339.755(2)(d). The transaction
broker's standard is narrower.

**Second: no imputation, and only actual knowledge.** Section 339.755.7: **each party and the transaction
broker, including all persons within an entity engaged as the transaction broker, are considered to
possess only actual knowledge and information. There is no imputation of knowledge or information by
operation of law.**

And §§ 339.755.4 and .5: a transaction broker has **no duty to conduct an independent inspection or
investigation for adverse material facts**, and **no duty to conduct an independent investigation of the
buyer's financial condition.**

### 11.4 The five items again

Section 339.755.3 carries the **same five non-disclosure items** as dual agency, phrased as requiring
**the informed consent of the party or parties disclosing such information to the broker**, and adds
**any confidential information about the other party** unless disclosure is required by law or failure
to disclose would constitute **fraud or dishonest dealing**.

### 11.5 What a transaction broker may do, and the withdrawal right

Section 339.755.6: without breaching any obligation, a transaction broker may show alternative
properties, list competing properties, show properties to other prospective buyers, and **serve as a
single agent, subagent, designated agent, limited agent or disclosed dual agent for the same or
different parties in OTHER transactions.**

Section 339.755.8: a transaction broker may **cooperate with other brokers, and such cooperation does
not establish an agency or subagency relationship.**

Section 339.755.12: a licensee who refuses transaction broker status and wants to continue an agency
relationship with both parties **has the right to become a designated agent or a dual agent** as
§§ 339.730 to 339.860 provide.

Section 339.755.13: **a licensee may without liability withdraw from representing a client who has not
consented to a conversion to transaction brokerage.** The withdrawal does not prejudice the licensee's
ability to continue representing the other client in the transaction, or to represent the refusing
client in a different transaction.

Section 339.755.10: none of this alters the **broker's responsibility for the conduct and actions of a
licensee operating under the broker's licence.**

## Hour 12 — Designated agency, subagency, and cooperating with other licensees

### 12.1 Designated agency — § 339.820

**The in-house problem.** One firm has the listing. A buyer working with the same firm wants to make an
offer. Without a mechanism, the firm is on both sides and every licensee in it is caught.

**The mechanism** — § 339.820.1: a designated broker entering a limited agency agreement with a client
**may appoint in writing affiliated licensees as designated agents to the exclusion of all other
affiliated licensees.** The same applies to designated transaction brokers under a transaction brokerage
agreement.

One licensee is appointed to the seller; another to the buyer. Each owes the full duties. Section
339.830.1: designated agents and designated transaction brokers have **the same duties and
responsibilities to the client and customer under §§ 339.730 to 339.755 as the designated broker**,
except as § 339.820 provides.

**Where the protection stops** — § 339.820.2: a designated broker is **not** a dual agent or transaction
broker **solely because** of making an appointment — **but any licensee who personally represents both
the seller and buyer in a particular transaction IS a dual agent or a transaction broker** and must
comply accordingly.

**Read that twice.** The firm is protected by the appointment. **You are not, if both sides are yours
personally.** A licensee who has the listing and is also working with the buyer is a dual agent or a
transaction broker, and the appointment does not change it.

**The default rule** — § 339.820.1, final sentence: where an appointment has been made, **an affiliated
licensee assisting a party without a written agreement is presumed to be a transaction broker** to the
exclusion of all other affiliated licensees, unless a different status has been disclosed to or
established with that party.

Section 339.830.2: all licensees have **the same protections from vicarious liability** as their
designated broker.

### 12.2 Subagency

A **subagent** is a designated broker working for the client through the client's designated broker.

Section 339.730.5: a seller or landlord may **agree in writing** that other designated brokers may be
retained and compensated as subagents, and any such subagent is **a limited agent with the obligations
and responsibilities of §§ 339.730.1 to .4.** Section 339.740.5 mirrors it on the buyer side.

Section 339.780.5 sets out the three routes in: a written agreement with the client's designated broker;
or, where the client's broker has made a **unilateral offer of subagency**, by the second broker's act
of **disclosing to the customer that they are a subagent of the client**; or, where a § 339.820
appointment has been made, by an excluded affiliated licensee disclosing the same.

**The consequence for the person in front of you.** A subagent working with a buyer is **the seller's
agent.** The buyer is a **customer** — owed adverse material fact disclosure and nothing more. The buyer
must be told, and the disclosure route in § 339.780.5 makes telling them the very act that creates the
subagency.

### 12.3 Cooperating with other licensees — 20 CSR 2250-6.060(2)(G)

**Cooperation is not agency.** Two firms working the same transaction from opposite sides are
cooperating; neither becomes the other's agent, and neither owes the other's client the duties owed to
a client. Section 339.755.8 says so expressly for transaction brokers, and the same principle governs
generally: **the relationship is created by the written agreement, not by the cooperation.**

**What a cooperating licensee owes the other side's client:** the customer duty — **disclosure of all
adverse material facts actually known or that should have been known** (or, for a transaction broker,
of which the licensee has actual notice or knowledge).

**The professional standards of cooperation**, which are not statutory but are what makes the business
work:

- **Present offers promptly** and accurately, and confirm receipt.
- **Communicate deadlines** and changes in writing.
- **Do not go around the other licensee to their client.** If a client contacts you directly, tell the
  other licensee.
- **Do not disparage** the other side's client or licensee to your own client. It is unprofessional and
  it can be defamatory.
- **Settle compensation in writing, in advance.** Cooperative compensation arrangements are agreements
  and they are documented like any other.

## Hour 13 — Termination and compensation

### 13.1 Termination of brokerage relationships — 20 CSR 2250-6.060(2)(E)

**When it starts.** Section 339.790.1: the relationships **commence on the effective date of the real
estate broker's agreement and continue until performance, completion, termination or expiration of that
agreement.**

**How it ends.** By **performance** — the transaction closes. By **expiration** — the term runs out. By
**termination** — the parties end it. By **operation of law** — destruction of the property, death or
incapacity in some circumstances, bankruptcy.

**Power versus right, again.** A client has the **power** to revoke the agency at any time. Whether they
have the **right** to do so without liability is a question about the contract, and it goes to the
designated broker and counsel. A licensee who answers it has given legal advice.

### 13.2 What survives — § 339.790.2

After termination, expiration, completion or performance, the broker and the affiliated licensee **owe
no further duty or obligation, EXCEPT the duties of:**

1. **Accounting in a timely manner for all money and property related to, and received during, the
   relationship**; and
2. **Treating as confidential information provided by the client during the course of the relationship
   that may reasonably be expected to have a negative impact on the client's real estate activity** —
   unless:
   - **(a)** the client to whom the information pertains grants **written consent**;
   - **(b)** disclosure is **required by law**;
   - **(c)** the information **is or becomes public** by the client's words or conduct or from a source
     other than the brokerage or the affiliated licensee; or
   - **(d)** disclosure is **necessary to defend** the designated broker or an affiliated licensee
     against an action of wrongful conduct in an administrative or judicial proceeding or before a
     professional committee.

**Two things survive; everything else stops.** The confidentiality obligation is the one licensees most
often forget, and the one whose breach is most obvious after the fact.

A concrete case: a listing expires. Six months later you are working with a buyer interested in that
same house, now listed with another firm. You know the former seller's motivation, their bottom line and
their divorce. **All of that is still confidential.** Section 339.790.2(2) does not expire with the
listing.

### 13.3 Compensation — 20 CSR 2250-6.060(2)(F) and § 339.800

**§ 339.800.1** — compensation may be paid by the **seller, the landlord, the buyer, the tenant, or a
third party**, or by **sharing between designated brokers.**

**§ 339.800.2** — **payment of compensation by itself shall not establish an agency relationship or
transaction brokerage relationship** between the party who paid and the designated broker or any
affiliated licensee. **Who pays does not decide whom you represent.**

**§ 339.800.3 and .4** — a seller or landlord may agree that the designated broker may **share** the
compensation with another designated broker; a buyer or tenant may agree the same.

**§ 339.800.5** — a designated broker may be compensated by **more than one party** for services in a
transaction **with the knowledge of all the parties at or before the time of entering into a written
contract** to buy, sell or lease. **At or before** — disclosure after the fact does not satisfy it.

**§ 339.800.6** — nothing in the section relieves the licensee of the requirement to obtain a **written
agreement** for brokerage services or another written agreement addressing compensation.

### 13.4 Three things a licensee must know about their own pay

**Commission is negotiable.** It is not set by any board, association, multiple listing service or rule,
and any suggestion that a rate is standard or customary is an antitrust exposure, not a shorthand.

**A salesperson is paid by their designated broker.** Money moves from the closing to the broker and from
the broker to the affiliated licensee. A salesperson does not accept compensation directly from a party.

**Every compensation arrangement is in writing, in advance.** Between the firm and the client. Between
cooperating firms. Between the firm and the affiliated licensee. Section 339.800.6 requires the first
two, and the third is what stops a disagreement about a split from becoming a dispute about the file.
