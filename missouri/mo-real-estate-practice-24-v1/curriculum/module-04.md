# Missouri Real Estate Practice Course — Module MREP-04

## Hours 14–17 · Pricing property, the role of the appraiser, and types of construction

**Commission topics covered:** 20 CSR 2250-6.060(2)(I) Pricing Property · (J) Role of the Appraiser ·
(P) Types of Construction
**Clock hours:** 4. A Missouri clock hour is **60 minutes containing at least 50 minutes of actual
instruction** — 20 CSR 2250-7.070(2).

hours among its sixteen topics.
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft

## Learning objectives

1. Build a comparative market analysis with the adjustments in the correct direction and reconcile to a
   range rather than an average.
2. Select and reject comparables on stated grounds.
3. State the boundary between a licensee's market analysis and an appraisal, and describe how each is
   labelled.
4. Describe the appraiser's role, who engages the appraiser and why, and what happens on a low appraisal.
5. Identify the principal construction types and components a residential licensee meets, and describe
   each accurately without assessing condition.
6. Recognise construction-related conditions that must be disclosed or routed.

## Hour 14 — Pricing property — 20 CSR 2250-6.060(2)(I)

### 14.1 What the licensee is actually producing

A **comparative market analysis** is the licensee's presentation of market evidence supporting a pricing
decision. It is not an opinion of value produced under an appraiser's licence and it must never be
labelled, described or presented as one.

Four rules govern every CMA you will ever produce:

1. **Present it in writing**, with the comparables and the adjustments shown. A CMA delivered orally
   leaves no record of what was actually presented, which harms the client and, when a dispute arrives,
   the licensee.
2. **Present a range**, not a single figure. The market is not that precise and pretending it is
   overstates what the evidence supports.
3. **Say plainly that the price is the seller's decision.**
4. **Label it for what it is.**

### 14.2 Selecting comparables

**Use:** recent **closed** sales; similar in size, age, style, condition and location; in the same
market area and ideally the same submarket; arm's-length transactions between unrelated parties.

**Do not use, or use only with explanation:** current **listings**, which are asking prices and prove
only what someone hoped for; **pending** sales, whose prices are not yet known; **distressed** sales,
unless the subject is itself distressed; sales between family members or otherwise not arm's-length.

**The quality test:** the fewer and smaller the adjustments a comparable needs, the better it is. A
comparable requiring $60,000 of gross adjustment is telling you it is not really comparable.

### 14.3 The adjustment rule

**Adjust the COMPARABLE, never the subject.** The subject is what is being valued; it has no price yet
and there is nothing on it to adjust.

- Comparable **superior** to the subject → adjust the comparable **DOWN**.
- Comparable **inferior** to the subject → adjust the comparable **UP**.

Getting the direction wrong moves the answer **twice** as far as leaving the adjustment out. It is the
single most common error in the work and it is worth checking every line before you present.

### 14.4 What may and may not be said about price

**May:** *Here are five closed sales, here are the adjustments and why, here is the range they support,
and here is what I would expect at each price within it. The decision is yours.*

**May not:** confirm a figure the licensee did not derive — an automated online valuation, a neighbour's
number, an old appraisal; state a future value; guarantee a sale price or a time on market; or describe
the analysis as an appraisal.

Two Missouri provisions bear directly. **Section 339.730.1(2), RSMo** — reasonable skill and care — and
confirming a valuation you did not derive is not care. **Section 339.730.1(3)(d)** — the duty to advise
the client to obtain expert advice as to material matters beyond the licensee's expertise — which a
valuation the licensee cannot support squarely is.

And the school-side rule that describes the same principle from the other direction:
20 CSR 2250-7.010(1)(L)3 forbids a school to permit anyone associated with it to **provide any
misleading information to the public.**

### 14.5 The seller who arrives with a number

Three sources, and the accurate response to each:

**An automated online valuation.** A model's output from public and listing data. It has never been
inside the house and cannot see condition, finish or the neighbour's junkyard. Evidence to weigh, not a
valuation. Do not adopt it; do not dismiss it; put your own analysis beside it.

**An old appraisal.** A 2019 appraisal is a 2019 opinion, produced for a purpose that may not be this
one, on a property that may since have changed.

**A neighbour's figure.** Ask what it is based on. It is almost always somebody's **list price**, which
is an asking price and is evidence of nothing but hope.

## Hour 15 — A worked comparative market analysis

### 15.1 The subject

*1,940 sq ft two-storey, 4 bed, 2.5 bath, attached 2-car garage, unfinished basement, 0.28 acre, built
2004, no recent updates.*

### 15.2 The grid

| | Comp A | Comp B | Comp C | Comp D |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Sale price | $348,000 | $312,000 | $379,500 | $335,000 |
| Sold | 2 months ago | 6 months ago | 1 month ago | 9 months ago |
| Market conditions +0.35%/mo | +$2,436 | +$6,552 | +$1,328 | +$10,553 |
| Living area | 2,010 (+70) | 1,830 (−110) | 2,120 (+180) | 1,905 (−35) |
| Area adj. at $58/sq ft | −$4,060 | +$6,380 | −$10,440 | +$2,030 |
| Bathrooms | 2.5 | 2 (−0.5) | 3.5 (+1) | 2.5 |
| Bath adj. at $7,000 each | $0 | +$3,500 | −$7,000 | $0 |
| Basement | unfinished | unfinished | finished | unfinished |
| Basement adj. | $0 | $0 | −$16,000 | $0 |
| Kitchen | original | original | renovated | renovated |
| Kitchen adj. | $0 | $0 | −$22,000 | −$22,000 |
| **Gross adjustment** | **$6,496** | **$16,432** | **$56,768** | **$34,583** |
| **Net adjustment** | **−$1,624** | **+$16,432** | **−$54,112** | **−$9,417** |
| **Adjusted price** | **$346,376** | **$328,432** | **$325,388** | **$325,583** |

### 15.3 Checking the directions

Do this every time, line by line:

- **Comp A** is larger — superior — so the area adjustment is **negative**. Correct.
- **Comp B** is smaller and has fewer baths — inferior on both — so both adjustments are **positive**.
  Correct.
- **Comp C** is larger, has more baths, has a finished basement and a renovated kitchen — superior on all
  four — so all four are **negative**. Correct.
- **Comp D** is slightly smaller — inferior — so **positive**; and has a renovated kitchen — superior —
  so **negative**. Correct.

**Market conditions are positive on every comparable** because the market has risen since each sale;
each would sell for more today. Time adjustments are applied before the physical adjustments.

### 15.4 Reconciling — and why it is not averaging

The four adjusted figures: **$346,376 · $328,432 · $325,388 · $325,583.**

The arithmetic mean is $331,445. **That number tells you nothing about which evidence was strongest**,
which is why reconciliation is judgment rather than averaging.

Weigh them:

- **Comp C** required **$56,768 of gross adjustment** — the most by a wide margin — and needed
  adjusting on four separate characteristics. Least reliable.
- **Comp D** sold **nine months ago** and required $34,583 gross. Second least reliable.
- **Comp B** sold six months ago and required $16,432. Moderate.
- **Comp A** sold **two months ago** and required only **$6,496 gross**. **Most reliable by a distance.**

**A defensible conclusion:** the weight of the evidence supports roughly **$332,000 to $345,000**, with
Comp A arguing for the upper part of that range and the three heavily adjusted comparables clustering
near $326,000 in a way that deserves attention rather than dismissal.

**And the honest thing to tell the seller:** three of four comparables land within $3,000 of each other
around $326,000 while the single most reliable one lands at $346,000, and the difference is largely the
kitchen. That is worth a sentence in the presentation, because it tells the seller something real about
their choices: renovate, price into the cluster, or price at the top and expect the market to test it.

**A CMA that hides the disagreement between its own comparables is not evidence. It is decoration.**

## Hour 16 — The role of the appraiser — 20 CSR 2250-6.060(2)(J)

### 16.1 What an appraiser is

An **appraiser** is separately licensed or certified and works under their own professional standards.
An appraisal is an **opinion of value** produced under that licence, for a stated purpose, as of a
stated date, for a stated interest in a property.

**A real estate salesperson is not an appraiser and does not perform appraisals.** The two roles are
distinct in licence, in standards, in liability and in what the product may be used for.

**This course states no Missouri appraiser licensing requirement**, because Missouri's appraiser
licensing chapter was not read from primary text for this project.

### 16.2 Who engages the appraiser, and why it matters

In a financed purchase the appraisal is ordered **by or for the lender**, to establish that the
collateral supports the loan. The **appraiser's client is the lender**, not the buyer — even where the
buyer pays for the report, which they usually do.

The consequence is one buyers regularly misunderstand: **an appraisal is not an inspection and is not a
condition report for the buyer's benefit.** It looks at condition only so far as condition affects value
for the lender's purpose. A buyer who declines an inspection because "the appraiser will look at it" has
made a serious mistake, and the licensee should say so plainly.

### 16.3 The three approaches, briefly

**Sales comparison** — the dominant approach for owner-occupied residential property, because that is
how the market behaves. Its logic is the same as the CMA's in Hour 15, applied under professional
standards.

**Cost** — land value plus the reproduction or replacement cost of the improvements less accrued
depreciation. Governs new construction and special-purpose property where comparables are thin.

**Income** — net operating income divided by a capitalisation rate. Governs income-producing property.

**Reconciliation** into a final opinion is judgment about which evidence is strongest. Again: not an
average.

### 16.4 The low appraisal

The single most common appraisal event in residential practice, and one a licensee must be able to
handle calmly.

**What has happened:** the lender will lend on the **lesser of price or appraised value**, so a gap has
opened between the contract price and what the lender will finance. The buyer must now cover the
difference in cash, or the price must move, or the parties must find another route, or the contract
must end according to whatever the appraisal contingency says.

**What the licensee does:**

- **Read the appraisal contingency** and its deadline before saying anything to anyone.
- **Review the report for factual errors** — wrong square footage, wrong bedroom count, a comparable
  that is not comparable, a missed permit. Factual errors are the legitimate basis for a
  reconsideration of value, and they are submitted **through the lender**, not by telephoning the
  appraiser.
- **Present the options to the client** neutrally, and route the decision.
- **Do not attempt to influence the appraiser.** Pressure on an appraiser to reach a number is
  misconduct, and in a federally related transaction it engages appraiser independence rules directly.

**What a licensee may properly do** is provide the appraiser with **factual information** — a list of
improvements with dates and costs, a survey, a plat, the seller's disclosure, comparable sales the
licensee believes relevant. Providing facts is legitimate. Providing a target is not.

## Hour 17 — Types of construction — 20 CSR 2250-6.060(2)(P)

### 17.1 Why this topic is on the Commission's list

Because a licensee describes buildings for a living, and a description that is wrong is a
misrepresentation whether or not it was intended to be. This hour is vocabulary and it exists so that
you can say accurate things about a house and recognise when a question is beyond you.

**The rule for the whole hour: describe, do not assess.** A licensee identifies what something is. A
qualified professional says whether it is sound.

### 17.2 Foundations and structure

**Foundation types:** full basement — common in much of Missouri; crawl space; slab on grade; pier and
beam. Materials: poured concrete, concrete block, stone in older buildings, brick.

**Framing:** platform framing, the modern standard, where each storey is built on a platform; balloon
framing, found in older houses, where studs run continuously from sill to roof; post and beam; and
structural masonry, where the walls carry the load.

**Conditions to route, never to assess:** foundation cracking or displacement; water in a basement or
crawl space; sagging floors; doors and windows out of square. Every one of those goes to a qualified
inspector or engineer, and any of them that you know about goes into the disclosure.

### 17.3 Roofing, cladding, and openings

**Roof coverings:** asphalt shingle in its several grades; wood shake; metal; tile; slate; and
membrane systems on low-slope roofs. Each has a different expected service life, and **a licensee does
not state the remaining life of a roof.**

**Roof shapes:** gable, hip, gambrel, mansard, shed, flat.

**Cladding:** brick veneer, stone, wood siding, vinyl, fibre cement, stucco, and synthetic stucco —
which has a claim history significant enough that a property clad in it is routed to a specialist
inspector as a matter of course.

**Windows:** single, double or triple glazed; wood, vinyl, aluminium or clad. **Aluminium wiring**,
**single-pane windows**, and **original 1970s systems** are all facts a buyer would want to know.

### 17.4 Systems

**Heating and cooling:** forced air furnace with split-system air conditioning; heat pump; boiler with
radiators or in-floor tubing; mini-split. Fuel: natural gas, propane, electric, oil.

**Electrical:** the service size in amps; the panel type; copper or **aluminium** branch wiring;
**knob-and-tube** in older houses. Certain panel brands have known histories and some insurers decline
them, which is a fact with a direct financial consequence for a buyer.

**Plumbing:** copper, PEX, CPVC, galvanised steel — which corrodes internally and reduces flow — and
**cast iron or clay drains**, common in older Missouri housing stock and a source of expensive
surprises. Water supply: municipal or **private well**. Waste: municipal sewer or **septic**.

**On rural Missouri property, well and septic decide habitability and financeability.** They are routed
to the county health authority and a qualified inspector, early, because a failed septic can stop a
loan.

### 17.5 Measurement and description

**Square footage** is measured to a standard, and different standards produce different numbers.
Finished basement space is generally reported separately from above-grade living area, and combining
them inflates the figure in a way that a buyer, an appraiser and a lender will each treat as an error.

**Where the figure comes from matters.** A tax record is an assessment record, not a measurement. A
prior listing is somebody else's figure and may have been wrong then. **Say where the number came from,
or measure it.**

**Age** comes from the record and is stated as such. **"Updated," "renovated" and "new"** are claims:
say what was done and when, and where you know, say who did it and whether it was permitted.

### 17.6 The disclosure connection

Everything in this hour connects back to § 339.710(1), RSMo. **Physical condition of the property which
adversely affects value** and **material defects in the property** are on the statute's own list of
adverse material facts — and under §§ 339.730.1(3)(c), 339.730.3 and 339.740.3 they are disclosed to
clients and customers alike, actually known **or that should have been known.**

"Should have been known" is why this vocabulary matters. A licensee who cannot recognise that a stain
running down a basement wall is worth asking about has a harder time explaining that they did not know.
