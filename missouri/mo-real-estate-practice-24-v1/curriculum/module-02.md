## Hour 5 — Types of seller agency agreements — 20 CSR 2250-6.060(2)(B)1

### 5.1 Three types, distinguished by one question

The question is: **in what circumstances is the firm owed a commission?**

**Exclusive right to sell.** The firm is owed on a sale during the term **regardless of who procures
the buyer — including the seller.** This is the ordinary residential listing, and the reason it is
ordinary is that it removes the firm's incentive problem: the firm can spend money marketing without
worrying that the seller's cousin will appear and eliminate the fee.

**Exclusive agency.** The firm is the only firm authorised, **but the seller reserves the right to sell
to a buyer they find themselves without owing a commission.** This produces an obvious dispute: who
found the buyer? The answer needs to be settled in the agreement, in writing, before it becomes an
argument — usually by naming the reserved prospects.

**Open listing.** A **non-exclusive** authorisation. The seller may give it to any number of firms and
**only the firm that procures the buyer is paid.** The seller may also sell themselves and owe nothing.
It is a unilateral contract: the seller promises to pay whoever performs, and nobody is obliged to try.

### 5.2 Net listings

A **net listing** is an arrangement in which the seller receives a fixed net amount and the firm keeps
everything above it.

The structural problem is that the licensee is **paid more the worse the seller does on price** — the
exact inversion of the § 339.730.1(3) duty to promote the client's interests with the utmost good
faith, loyalty and fidelity, which includes **seeking a price and terms acceptable to the client**.

**This course states no Missouri rule permitting or prohibiting net listings**, because 20 CSR 2250
chapters 1 through 5 and 8 and § 339.100, RSMo, were not read from primary text for this project.
Whatever the chapters say, the professional position does not depend on it: this is not an arrangement
a licensee treats as an ordinary fee structure, and any proposal for one goes to the designated broker
before it goes anywhere else.

### 5.3 Who enters the agreement

Section 339.780.1: **all written agreements for brokerage services shall be entered into by the
designated broker** on behalf of that broker and the affiliated licensees — **except that the designated
broker may authorise affiliated licensees in writing** to enter into the written agreements on the
broker's behalf.

So a salesperson taking a listing is signing **on the designated broker's behalf, under the broker's
written authority.** Know that the authority exists and know its scope. The listing is the firm's
contract with the seller, not yours.

### 5.4 Termination, and what a seller can and cannot do

A listing ends by **expiry**, by **performance**, by **mutual agreement**, or by **operation of law** —
destruction of the property, death or incapacity of a party in some circumstances.

**And the distinction that a licensee must state accurately:** a seller has the **power** to revoke the
agency at any time, but that is not the same as a **right** to do so without liability. Whether the firm
has a claim is a legal question between the seller and the firm, and it goes to the designated broker
and counsel. A licensee who tells a seller "you can cancel whenever you like, no consequences" has given
legal advice, and probably wrong legal advice.

Protection or override clauses — providing for a commission where a buyer introduced during the term
purchases after it — may survive termination on their own terms, and their terms are read rather than
assumed.

## Hour 6 — Elements of a seller agency agreement — 20 CSR 2250-6.060(2)(B)2

### 6.1 What § 339.780.2 requires

Read it exactly. Before engaging in any of the activities enumerated in § 339.010, a designated broker
intending to establish a limited agency relationship with a seller or landlord **shall enter into a
written agency agreement with the party to be represented**, and:

> The agreement shall include a licensee's duties and responsibilities specified in section 339.730 and
> the terms of compensation and shall specify whether an offer of subagency may be made to any other
> designated broker.

**Three mandatory contents:**

1. **The § 339.730 duties and responsibilities.** They go in the agreement, not merely in the licensee's
   head. A seller reading their listing agreement is entitled to see what they are owed.
2. **The terms of compensation.**
3. **Whether an offer of subagency may be made** to any other designated broker. This one is
   specifically named, and it is the one most likely to be overlooked.

### 6.2 The commercial elements

Beyond the statutory minimum, every listing must settle:

- **The parties.** Every person who must sign to convey. Missouri's marital property rules and the
  possibility of a life estate, a trust or an estate all mean this is not answered by looking at the
  deed. **Route it to counsel and the title company at listing**, not at closing.
- **The property**, with a legal description taken from the deed or the title company — never from a tax
  record, a prior listing or memory.
- **The price**, which is the seller's decision.
- **The term**, with a definite end date.
- **The compensation and how it is earned** — the event that triggers it.
- **Included and excluded items**, in two written lists.
- **Authorisations**: marketing, photography, lockbox, sign, showings, internet display and any opt-outs.
- **Any reserved prospects**, if the type is exclusive agency.
- **The seller's disclosure obligations**, and the licensee's own separate obligation to disclose adverse
  material facts to clients and customers alike.

### 6.3 Pricing, and the boundary

The comparative market analysis is presented **in writing**, with the comparables and the adjustments
shown, as **evidence supporting a range**. The price is the seller's decision.

**It is not an appraisal**, it is not labelled or described as one, and it is not presented in a way that
lets a client believe it is one. That boundary is a § 339.730.1(3)(d) matter — the duty to advise the
client to obtain expert advice as to material matters beyond the licensee's expertise — and Module
MREP-04 takes it up in full.

Taking a listing you know is unsupportably priced is a competence problem before it is a marketing
problem. The professional response is to put the evidence in front of the seller in writing, let them
decide, and record what you presented.

## Hour 7 — The listing appointment, in sequence

### 7.1 The sequence

This hour is the practical assembly of everything in Module MREP-01 and Hours 5 and 6.

**Before you arrive:**
- Check your firm's § 339.760 written policy for what relationships you may offer.
- Pull the tax record, the plat, the prior sales and the comparables.
- Have the **current Commission broker disclosure form** with you.

**On arrival, in order:**

1. **The broker disclosure form**, if the three § 339.770.1 conditions are met — residential, first
   substantial contact, no existing written agreement. Give it, note that you gave it, and date the note.
2. **Explain the relationship options** your firm permits, and what each means.
3. **Walk the property.** Settle the included and excluded items as you go. Ask specifically about leased
   equipment, tenant-installed items, recent construction and any unpaid or disputed contractor.
4. **Ask the ownership questions.** Who is on the deed; is anyone deceased; is there a trust, an estate,
   a life estate, a divorce; is there a tenant. Route every complication to counsel and the title company
   rather than resolving it in the driveway.
5. **Present the pricing evidence in writing**, as a range, with the adjustments shown.
6. **Complete the agreement** — all three § 339.780.2 mandatory contents, plus the commercial elements.
7. **Explain the seller's disclosure obligations** and your own separate duty to disclose adverse
   material facts to everyone entitled to them.
8. **Confirm in writing** what was decided, the same day.

### 7.2 The four questions worth asking that most licensees do not

- **"Is any equipment on the property leased or financed?"** Propane tank, water treatment, solar,
  security, sometimes HVAC. A buyer cannot discover this by looking, and a licensee who did not ask has
  a "should have known" problem under § 339.730.1(3)(c).
- **"Has any work been done in the last year, and is everyone paid?"** Recent construction plus a payment
  dispute is a construction lien risk, and lien priority may run from a date earlier than any filing.
  **This course states no Missouri mechanic's lien period**, because that chapter was not read from
  primary text for this project — route it to the title company and counsel, at listing.
- **"Is there anything about this property a buyer would want to know and could not find out by
  looking?"** This is the adverse material fact question in plain language, and it works.
- **"Who else has to sign?"** Asked at listing, this is a five-minute conversation. Asked at closing, it
  is a postponement.

### 7.3 What goes in the file

Everything. The date the disclosure form was given. The pricing evidence you actually presented. The
included and excluded lists. Every material conversation, confirmed by email the same day.

You write every file as though someone else will read it, because someone may — 20 CSR 2250-7.090 gives
the Commission investigatory powers over schools and the statute gives it comparable powers over
licensees, and a file that records what happened is the only defence that has ever worked.

## Hour 8 — Types of buyer agency agreements — 20 CSR 2250-6.060(2)(C)1

### 8.1 The types

**Exclusive buyer agency.** The buyer agrees to work with one firm for a term and in a defined area or
property type. The firm is owed compensation on a purchase within the term regardless of who found the
property, on the terms the agreement states.

**Non-exclusive buyer agency.** The buyer may work with more than one firm; compensation follows only
where this firm's work produced the transaction. It is workable and it is fragile, and the fragility
falls on the licensee.

**Designated agency.** Under § 339.820.1 a designated broker entering a limited agency agreement may
**appoint in writing affiliated licensees as designated agents to the exclusion of all other affiliated
licensees** — which is how a Missouri firm represents both a seller and a buyer in the same transaction
without the whole firm becoming a dual agent. Module MREP-03 takes this up in detail.

**Transaction brokerage** is not buyer agency at all. A transaction broker under § 339.755 assists
without an agency or fiduciary relationship and is **neutral, serving neither as advocate nor advisor**.
It is a different product and the buyer must understand which one they are getting.

### 8.2 The ministerial acts carve-out, and why it matters here more than anywhere

Section 339.780.3: before engaging in any of the § 339.010 activities, **except ministerial acts as
defined in § 339.710**, a designated broker acting as a single agent for a buyer or tenant **shall enter
into a written agency agreement** with the buyer or tenant.

**Ministerial acts do not require an agreement.** You may answer the phone about price and location, hold
an open house and answer questions, and set a showing appointment.

**But that carve-out ends where the ministerial acts do.** The hazard in buyer work is drift: a licensee
who shows twenty houses, discusses the buyer's finances, advises on what to offer and negotiates on their
behalf, all without an agreement, has been acting as an agent without one — and has created a person who
believes they are a client, is legally a customer, and will be extremely unhappy when the difference
surfaces.

**Have the conversation early.** Explain the relationship options, explain what each means, and get the
agreement signed before the work becomes representation.

## Hour 9 — Elements of a buyer agency agreement — 20 CSR 2250-6.060(2)(C)2

### 9.1 What § 339.780.3 requires

> The agreement shall include a licensee's duties and responsibilities specified in section 339.740 and
> the terms of compensation.

**Two mandatory contents:** the § 339.740 duties, and the terms of compensation. Note that the seller-side
requirement in § 339.780.2 has a third item — whether an offer of subagency may be made — and the
buyer-side requirement does not.

### 9.2 The commercial elements

- **The parties** and the **term**.
- **The scope** — geographic area, property type, price range. A buyer agency agreement covering "any
  real estate anywhere" is a different commitment from one covering a defined search, and the buyer should
  understand which they signed.
- **Compensation: how much, from whom, and what happens if the seller's side pays some or all of it.**
  This is the element most likely to produce a dispute and it must be settled explicitly, in numbers.
- **Whether the agreement is exclusive**, and what happens if the buyer purchases through another firm.
- **Dual agency or designated agency consent**, if the firm's policy contemplates it and the buyer agrees
  in advance — noting that under § 339.750.1 dual agency requires the consent of **all** parties and
  consent is presumed by a written agreement under § 339.780.
- **Any protection period** after termination.

### 9.3 Compensation: the sentence that must be said out loud

Section 339.800.2: **payment of compensation by itself shall not establish an agency relationship or
transaction brokerage relationship** between the party who paid and the designated broker or any
affiliated licensee.

**Who pays does not decide whom you represent.** The written agreement decides.

This has to be said to buyers explicitly, because the intuition runs the other way. A buyer whose agent
is compensated through the transaction may assume they are therefore not really represented, or the
reverse. Section 339.800.1 permits compensation from the seller, the landlord, the buyer, the tenant, a
third party, or by sharing between designated brokers — and **none of those choices changes the agency.**

Section 339.800.5 adds that a designated broker may be compensated by more than one party **with the
knowledge of all the parties at or before the time of entering into a written contract** to buy, sell or
lease. At or before — not afterwards.

And § 339.800.6: none of this relieves the licensee of the requirement to obtain a written agreement for
brokerage services or another written agreement addressing compensation.

### 9.4 The § 339.780.7 minimum services

Every **exclusive** brokerage agreement — on either side — must specify that the broker, through the
broker or one or more affiliated licensees, will provide **at a minimum**:

1. **Accepting delivery of and presenting** to the client or customer offers and counteroffers to buy,
   sell or lease.
2. **Assisting** the client or customer in developing, communicating, negotiating and presenting offers,
   counteroffers and notices that relate to them — **until a lease or purchase agreement is signed and
   all contingencies are satisfied or waived.**
3. **Answering** the client's or customer's questions relating to the offers, counteroffers, notices and
   contingencies.

Note the reach of item 2: **through contingency satisfaction, not to contract signature.** A firm that
regards its work as done when the contract is signed has not provided the statutory minimum.

Section 339.780.8 preserves the freedom to contract for **more**: nothing in the section prohibits the
public from entering into written contracts containing duties, obligations or responsibilities in
addition to those specified.
