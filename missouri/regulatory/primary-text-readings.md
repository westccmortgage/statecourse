## The structural fact that shapes the whole Missouri build

**20 CSR 2250-6.060(1)** — read in full:

> Schools accredited to teach the forty-eight (48)-hour Salesperson Pre-Examination Course and the
> forty-eight (48)-hour Broker Pre-Examination Course shall develop these courses based on the
> detailed content outline as provided by the professional testing service contracted to administer
> the examination for the Missouri Real Estate Commission. This detailed content outline shall be
> based on the testing service's most recent job analysis of real estate professionals.

Read that twice, because it is unlike every other state in this project. **The Commission does not
publish a topic list for the 48-hour Salesperson Pre-Examination Course.** It delegates the content
to a document produced by a private contractor — the testing service's detailed content outline,
derived from that service's job analysis.

Two consequences, and both are release gates:

1. **The detailed content outline has NOT been read for this project.** It is a testing-service
   document distributed through the Commission's exam-information page and the service's candidate
   handbook, and this project has not obtained the operative current version. Therefore **no document
   in this build may claim that the 48-hour course's topic list is the Commission's, or the testing
   service's.** The nine modules and the hours allocated to them are StateCourse's own organisation
   of the general body of salesperson pre-license knowledge, and every file says so.
2. **The course cannot be submitted until the outline is read and the course reconciled against it.**
   20 CSR 2250-7.070(18) requires that courses be taught in adherence to the outline on file with the
   Commission, and 20 CSR 2250-7.010(1)(F) requires the course to include the subjects set out in
   20 CSR 2250-6.060 — which for this course means whatever the testing service's outline contains.
   A course built without reading it is a draft, not a submission.

**20 CSR 2250-6.060(2)** — the 24-hour Missouri Real Estate Practice Course. Here the rule DOES fix
the topics, and it fixes sixteen of them. Transcribed exactly:

> (A) Broker Disclosure Form;
> (B) Listing a Property. 1. Types of seller agency agreements. 2. Elements of seller agency agreements;
> (C) Buyer Representation. 1. Types of buyer agency agreements. 2. Elements of buyer agency agreements;
> (D) Acting as a Dual Agent or Transaction Broker;
> (E) Termination of Brokerage Relationships;
> (F) Compensation;
> (G) Cooperating with Other Licensees;
> (H) Obligations of Agents to Clients and Customers;
> (I) Pricing Property;
> (J) Role of the Appraiser;
> (K) Types of Financing;
> (L) Estimating Closing Costs;
> (M) Contracts;
> (N) Earnest Money;
> (O) Avoiding Misrepresentation; and
> (P) Types of Construction.

**The rule allocates no hours among the sixteen.** So for the MREP course the topic list is the
Commission's and the hour allocation is StateCourse's, and the documents must keep those apart —
the same discipline Wisconsin requires, for the same reason.

Authority: sections 339.090 and 339.120, RSMo Supp. 2005. Originally 4 CSR 250-6.060, filed
6 April 2006, effective 30 September 2006; moved to 20 CSR 2250-6.060 effective 28 August 2006.

## 20 CSR 2250-7 — Schools

### 7.070(2) — what an hour means

> For the purpose of the course offerings by accredited real estate schools, an hour means sixty (60)
> minutes, at least fifty (50) minutes of which shall be devoted to actual instruction and no more
> than ten (10) minutes of which shall be devoted to a recess. Times allotted for supervised
> examinations may be regarded as hours of instruction.

Three operative facts in one subsection. A Missouri hour is **60 minutes containing at least 50
minutes of instruction** — not the 60 minutes of instruction Maine and Wisconsin require, and not
Virginia's 50-minute hour either. Recess is capped at 10 minutes. And **supervised examination time
may count as instruction**, which is a permission, not a licence to let assessment displace teaching.

### 7.070 — the rest, as read

- **(1)** A school must be accredited **before offering or advertising** a course for credit.
- **(5)** Course approval runs for the duration of the license period for which approval is sought.
- **(6)** Applications for course approval are submitted **at least ninety (90) days** before the
  course is first expected to be offered, with a complete outline showing all subjects covered. The
  Commission responds in writing **within forty-five (45) days** of a properly completed application.
  The "no fewer than three (3) unique learning objectives per course hour" requirement in this
  subsection is stated **for continuing education courses**; it is not stated for pre-license courses.
  This build meets it for every hour anyway, because it is good practice and costs nothing — but no
  document may describe it as a pre-license requirement.
- **(7)** A course found deficient gets written notice and **no more than sixty (60) days** to correct.
- **(10)** No classroom pre-examination or MREP course may exceed **eight (8) hours per day** and must
  provide **at least a half-hour break after four (4) hours**. This governs classroom delivery.
- **(12)** Classroom dates, times and locations submitted electronically **at least ten (10) days**
  before each offering.
- **(13)** Advertising for a distance course **shall clearly describe all course requirements**
  including satisfactory completion of a final examination.
- **(14)** Classroom students must be physically present for **one hundred percent (100%)** of actual
  classroom instruction; for good cause the school may in its discretion allow absence of up to
  **ten percent (10%)** of required hours and still issue a certificate of attendance, with the
  duration of absence documented.
- **(17)** Within **ten (10) days** of course completion the school electronically submits a complete
  and accurate list of attendees who satisfactorily completed the course.
- **(18)** All courses **must be taught in adherence to the outline on file**. A substantive change
  requires a revised outline filed **at least thirty (30) days** in advance and **written approval
  before implementation**; the Commission responds within **twenty (20) days**.

### 7.080 — distance delivery, which is what StateCourse is

- **(1)(B)** For each pre-examination course, MREP course or CE course of **more than three hours**:
  **two final examination forms — primary and alternate — of at least fifty (50) multiple choice
  items each, with no duplication of items**, and a key showing correct responses. Examinations must
  reflect sound test development practices and **must measure knowledge of the subject matter of the
  entire course as set out in the learning objectives**.
- **(1)(C)** A copy of all examination questions and the answer key, and where a question pool is
  used, **an explanation of how pooled items are selected**.
- **(1)(D)** A statement setting out **the methods the school will use to assure examinations are
  maintained securely**.
- **(1)(E)** Within **ten (10) days** following administration of the final examination, the school
  delivers to the student **who achieves a score of at least seventy-five percent (75%)** a
  certificate of course completion on a Commission-prescribed form.
- **(3)** A **course introduction statement** must be provided to the student setting out the dates
  during which the course is approved, the terms and conditions under which the final examination
  will be administered including review of the completed workbook, and **a list of the specific
  learning objectives referenced in the final examination**.
- **(4)** By **31 July and 31 January**, statistical information on completion times for the preceding
  period must be provided to the Commission. **If the statistics indicate that at least fifty percent
  (50%) of students took less time to complete the course than identified on the course submission**,
  the school has **ten (10) days** either to withdraw the course or to file an amended outline.

That last one is worth stating plainly: Missouri measures whether students actually spend the hours
the school claimed. A course whose stated hours are not the hours students take is a course the
Commission requires to be withdrawn or corrected. It is the strongest anti-inflation provision this
project has read in any state.

### 7.010(1) — accreditation standards, as read

- **(E)** The school **shall enter into a contract with each student** enrolling in a pre-examination
  or MREP course, identifying tuition, the refund policy for dismissal or voluntary or hardship
  withdrawal, any additional fee for supplies or materials, **the standard for issuance of a
  certificate of satisfactory completion**, and other material matters.
- **(D)** The school **shall advise all students, prior to contractual obligation, that certain
  criminal convictions may prevent the student from receiving or retaining a real estate license.**
- **(F)** The courses offered **shall include the subjects set forth in 20 CSR 2250-6.060**.
- **(H)** For distance courses, **an instructor must respond to each student's inquiries by the end of
  the next regular business day.**
- **(I)** A certificate of satisfactory completion is issued **on a form prescribed by the commission**.
- **(K)1–6** Record maintenance. Each student's record includes the course undertaken, dates of
  attendance and areas of study completed satisfactorily. Distance schools **additionally maintain
  records of all final examinations and examination administration**. Student records are kept for a
  **minimum of four (4) years**, available for inspection by the student or the Commission during
  regular business hours, or provided to the Commission by certified mail **within thirty (30) days**
  of written request. On closure the owner has **ten (10) days** to give the Commission the contact
  information for the custodian of records for the previous four years.
- **(L)2** No school shall advertise or imply that it is **recommended, endorsed or approved by the
  commission** — though an accredited school may indicate what courses it has been approved to teach.
- **(L)4** No school shall permit anyone associated with it **to make any warranties or guarantees
  that a student will pass the real estate license examination** by taking its courses.
- **(L)5** Every advertisement of an approved course must state **the number of hours of education
  credit** and **the correct title of the course as stated in the course approval letter**.
- **(L)6** **No course shall be announced or advertised until it is approved by the commission, in
  writing, for credit.**
- **(5)** Initial accreditation is valid for no more than one year and expires **31 March**; renewal
  is submitted no earlier than 60 days before and no later than the expiration date, and a renewed
  accreditation expires 31 March of the second odd-numbered year following. **Failure to renew before
  expiration voids all course approvals.**

### 7.090(5) — grounds for a complaint to the Administrative Hearing Commission

Read in full. Two are structural for a school of this kind:

- **(E)** an accumulative **first-time-examinee pass rate below forty percent (40%) for three of the
  last six months** for the school's Salesperson or Broker Pre-Examination Course;
- **(K)** **awarding credit to any student who has either not taken the course or who has failed to
  satisfactorily complete it.**

(K) is the reason the course engine's completion gates are server-authoritative and the reason no
payment event may set a completion state.

### 7.060 — instructors

> Each school is responsible for hiring only appropriately trained instructors with practical
> experience or formal education/training on the subject matter being presented.

The whole rule. Missouri sets no instructor licence, hour count or credential list here.

## Chapter 339, RSMo — statute

### 339.040 — licence qualifications (read 2026-08-20, text effective 28 August 2020)

- **.3** Each applicant is at least eighteen and pays the examination fee.
- **.6** Each application for a salesperson licence shall include **a certificate from a school
  accredited by the commission under section 339.045 that the applicant has, within six months prior
  to the date of application, successfully completed the prescribed salesperson curriculum** — with a
  Commission discretion to waive all or part on proof of other educational background or experience.

**The six-month window is the operative fact a student needs and almost never hears.** Completing the
education and then waiting eight months to apply means doing it again. Every Missouri student-facing
document in this build states it.

- **.8** Every active licensee provides on request evidence of **twelve hours** of approved real
  estate instruction in the preceding two years. That is continuing education, not pre-license, and
  no document may blur them.

### 339.710 — definitions (read in relevant part)

- **"Adverse material fact"** — *a fact related to the property not reasonably ascertainable or known
  to a party which negatively affects the value of the property*, and may include environmental
  hazards, physical condition adversely affecting value, material defects in the property, material
  defects in title, and **material limitation of the party's ability to perform under the terms of
  the contract**. That last item is easy to miss and is not about the property at all.
- **"Client"** — a seller, landlord, buyer or tenant **who has entered into a brokerage relationship**.
- **"Customer"** — an actual or potential seller, landlord, buyer or tenant in a transaction in which
  a licensee is involved **who has not entered into a brokerage relationship** with the licensee.
- **"Confidential information"** — information obtained from the client and designated confidential by
  the client, information made confidential by ss. 339.710–339.860 or any other statute or regulation,
  or written instructions from the client — unless it is or becomes public by the client's words or
  conduct or from a source other than the licensee.
- **"Limited agent"** — a licensee whose duties to a client are those set forth in ss. 339.730–339.750.
- **"Ministerial acts"** — acts *informative in nature* that *do not rise to the level which requires
  the creation of a brokerage relationship*, including responding to telephone inquiries about the
  availability and pricing of brokerage services or the price or location of property, attending an
  open house and answering questions about the property, setting an appointment to view property, and
  responding to questions of consumers walking into an office.
- **"Designated agent"**, **"designated broker"**, **"affiliated licensee"**, **"transaction broker"**
  and **"commercial real estate"** were also read; commercial real estate is defined as real estate
  other than one-to-four residential units or agricultural/horticultural property under s. 137.016.

### 339.730 — seller's or landlord's agent as limited agent

Six duties to the client: perform the written agreement; **exercise reasonable skill and care**;
promote the client's interests **with the utmost good faith, loyalty and fidelity** — including
seeking acceptable price and terms, **presenting all written offers to and from the client in a
timely manner regardless of whether the property is already under contract**, **disclosing to the
client all adverse material facts actually known or that should have been known**, and **advising the
client to obtain expert advice** on material matters beyond the licensee's expertise; **account in a
timely manner** for all money and property received; comply with ss. 339.710–339.860 and s. 339.100.2
and the rules; and comply with applicable federal, state and local law **including fair housing and
civil rights statutes and regulations**.

**.3** is the sentence that decides most examination questions and most complaints: a seller's or
landlord's agent **owes no duty or obligation to a customer, except that the licensee shall disclose
to any customer all adverse material facts actually known or that should have been known.** No duty
to inspect independently, no duty to discover adverse facts for the customer, no duty to verify the
client's or an inspector's statements.

**.4** The agent may show alternative properties and list competing properties without breach.

### 339.740 — buyer's or tenant's agent

The mirror of 339.730, with the same six duties and the same customer rule. **.3** adds that a
buyer's or tenant's agent **owes no duty to conduct an independent investigation of the client's
financial condition for the benefit of the customer.**

### 339.750 — dual agency

- **.1** A licensee may act as a dual agent **only with the consent of all parties**; consent is
  presumed by a written agreement under s. 339.780.
- **.2** A dual agent is a limited agent for both sides with the duties of ss. 339.730 and 339.740.
- **.4** **Five items a dual agent shall not disclose without the consent of the client to whom they
  pertain**, transcribed: that a buyer or tenant **is willing to pay more** than the price offered;
  that a seller or landlord **is willing to accept less** than the asking price; **what the motivating
  factors are** for any client buying, selling or leasing; that a client **will agree to financing
  terms other than those offered**; and **the terms of any prior offers or counter offers** made by
  any party.
- **.6** **In a dual agency relationship there shall be no imputation of knowledge or information**
  between the client and the dual agent or among persons within an entity engaged as a dual agent.

### 339.755 — transaction broker

A licensee may serve any party **without an agency or fiduciary relationship**, and is then **neutral,
serving neither as advocate nor advisor**. Duties include performing any written or oral agreement;
exercising **reasonable skill, care and diligence**; presenting all written offers and counteroffers
timely; informing the parties and **suggesting expert advice** on matters beyond the broker's
expertise; accounting timely; **disclosing to each party any adverse material facts of which the
licensee has actual notice or knowledge**; and assisting the parties in complying with the contract.

Note the difference in the disclosure standard and record it: an agent under 339.730/.740 discloses
adverse material facts **actually known or that should have been known**; a transaction broker under
339.755(2)(d) discloses those **of which the licensee has actual notice or knowledge**. The
transaction broker also **has no duty to conduct an independent inspection or investigation for
adverse material facts** and **no duty to investigate the buyer's financial condition**.

**.3** carries the same five non-disclosure items as dual agency, phrased as requiring the informed
consent of the party who disclosed them, plus confidential information about the other party.

**.7** **Each party and the transaction broker are considered to possess only actual knowledge and
information. There is no imputation of knowledge or information by operation of law.**

**.13** A licensee may **without liability withdraw** from representing a client who has not consented
to conversion to transaction brokerage.

### 339.760 — the written policy

> Every designated broker who has affiliated licensees shall adopt a written policy which identifies
> and describes the relationships in which the designated broker and affiliated licensees may engage
> with any seller, landlord, buyer, or tenant as part of any real estate brokerage activities.

### 339.770 — the Broker Disclosure Form

**.1** In a **residential** real estate transaction, **at the earliest practicable opportunity during
or following the first substantial contact** with a seller, landlord, buyer or tenant who has not
entered into a written agreement for services, the licensee **shall provide that person with a
written copy of the current broker disclosure form prescribed by the commission**.

**.2** Where the person has already entered into a written agreement for services with a designated
broker, **no other licensee is required to make the disclosure**.

**.3** Disclosures made in accordance with ss. 339.710–339.860 **shall be sufficient as a matter of
law** to disclose brokerage relationships to the public.

**The form itself is a Commission-prescribed form and has NOT been read for this project.** No
document in this build reproduces it, states its fields, or describes its layout. What the course
teaches is the statutory trigger — first substantial contact, residential, no existing written
agreement — and the routing: use the **current** Commission form, obtained from the Commission.

### 339.780 — written agreements (text effective 28 August 2025 — the most recently amended section read)

All written agreements for brokerage services are entered into **by the designated broker**, unless
the designated broker authorises affiliated licensees in writing. Then, **before engaging in any of
the activities enumerated in s. 339.010**:

- **.2** a limited agency relationship with a seller or landlord requires a **written agency agreement**
  including the s. 339.730 duties, the terms of compensation, and **whether an offer of subagency may
  be made**;
- **.3** acting as a **single agent for a buyer or tenant** requires a written agency agreement
  including the s. 339.740 duties and the terms of compensation — **except for ministerial acts**;
- **.4** acting as a **dual agent** requires a written agreement with both sides including the s.
  339.750 duties and terms of compensation;
- **.5** acting as a **subagent** requires a written agreement with the client's designated broker,
  with two routes by disclosure where a unilateral offer of subagency or a s. 339.820 appointment
  exists;
- **.6** a **transaction broker** who expects compensation from the party assisted enters a **written
  transaction brokerage agreement** including the s. 339.755 duties and terms of compensation.

**.7** Every **exclusive** brokerage agreement must specify that the broker will provide at minimum:
accepting delivery of and presenting offers and counteroffers; assisting in developing,
communicating, negotiating and presenting offers, counteroffers and notices **until a lease or
purchase agreement is signed and all contingencies are satisfied or waived**; and answering the
client's or customer's questions about offers, counteroffers, notices and contingencies.

**This section carries a 2025 amendment (H.B. 595 & 343 merged with H.B. 596). It was read in its
current effective text on 2026-08-20 and must be re-read at submission**, because a section amended
this recently is the one most likely to move again.

### 339.790 — commencement and what survives termination

**.1** The relationships commence **on the effective date of the broker's agreement** and continue
until performance, completion, termination or expiration.

**.2** After termination the broker and affiliated licensee **owe no further duty except**: accounting
in a timely manner for money and property received during the relationship; and **treating as
confidential information provided by the client during the relationship that may reasonably be
expected to have a negative impact on the client's real estate activity** — unless written consent,
disclosure required by law, the information became public other than through the brokerage, or
disclosure is needed to defend against an action of wrongful conduct.

### 339.800 — compensation

**.1** Compensation may be paid by seller, landlord, buyer, tenant or a third party, or shared between
designated brokers. **.2** **Payment of compensation by itself shall not establish an agency
relationship or transaction brokerage relationship.** **.5** A designated broker may be compensated by
more than one party **with the knowledge of all parties at or before the time of entering into a
written contract** to buy, sell or lease. **.6** Nothing in the section relieves the licensee of the
requirement to obtain a written agreement.

### 339.820 and 339.830 — designated agency

**339.820.1** A designated broker entering a limited agency agreement **may appoint in writing
affiliated licensees as designated agents to the exclusion of all other affiliated licensees**, and
may do the same for designated transaction brokers under a transaction brokerage agreement. Where an
appointment has been made, **an affiliated licensee assisting a party without a written agreement is
presumed to be a transaction broker** to the exclusion of all other affiliated licensees, unless a
different status has been disclosed or established.

**339.820.2** A designated broker **is not a dual agent or transaction broker solely because of making
an appointment** — but any licensee who personally represents both sides in a particular transaction
**is** a dual agent or transaction broker and must comply accordingly.

**339.830.1** Designated agents and designated transaction brokers have **the same duties and
responsibilities to client and customer under ss. 339.730–339.755 as the designated broker**, except
as provided in s. 339.820. **.2** All licensees have the same protections from vicarious liability as
their designated broker.

## What was NOT read, and is therefore stated nowhere in this build

Named explicitly, because an unnamed gap becomes an invented fact:

- **The testing service's detailed content outline** for the 48-hour Salesperson Pre-Examination
  Course. This is the single largest gap in the Missouri build and it is a submission gate, not a
  detail. See the top of this file.
- **The Commission-prescribed Broker Disclosure Form** and every other Commission-prescribed form —
  the certificate of course completion form, the attendance forms, the school and course application
  forms. No document reproduces or describes any of them.
- **20 CSR 2250 chapters 1 through 5 and 8**, including the licensing, conduct and trust-account
  chapters. No figure, deadline or threshold from them appears anywhere in this build.
- **Section 339.100** (grounds for discipline) and **section 339.105** (trust accounts) beyond the
  cross-reference in 339.730(1)(5). Their content is not stated.
- **Section 339.010** (definition of the activities requiring a licence) beyond its use as a
  cross-reference in 339.780. The enumerated activities are not listed.
- **Sections 339.045, 339.090 and 339.120**, cited as rulemaking authority throughout Chapter 7 but
  not read in their own text.
- **Missouri's seller disclosure practice.** Missouri has no statutory residential seller disclosure
  form of the kind Wisconsin's ch. 709 supplies, and this project has not read whatever governs the
  practice. **This course states no Missouri seller disclosure requirement.** What it teaches is the
  statutory adverse-material-fact duty under ss. 339.730(1)(3)(c), 339.730.3, 339.740 and 339.755,
  which was read, and it routes the rest.
- **Missouri's foreclosure, landlord-tenant, condominium, subdivision, environmental and
  construction-lien chapters.** None was read. No figure, period or threshold from any of them is
  stated anywhere in this build; where a course reaches one of these subjects the authored answer is
  the routing.
