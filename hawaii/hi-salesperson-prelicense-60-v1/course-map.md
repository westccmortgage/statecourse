# Hawaiʻi Salesperson Prelicensing — 60-hour Course Map

**Course ID:** HI-SALES-60-v1  
**Status:** Development draft. Not submitted or approved.  
**Delivery design:** Online independent study; instructor and regulatory review required before submission or enrollment.

## Controlling requirements

- The Hawaii Real Estate Commission's current public salesperson curriculum is the June 2020 curriculum, effective January 1, 2021. It contains 18 required sections.
- Hawaiʻi requires a 60-hour salesperson prelicensing course; an independent-study course must reasonably require at least 60 hours.
- The school must be registered and approved before offering the course. The school's registration materials require a timed outline, curriculum objectives, and coverage of every area in the Commission curriculum.
- This course map allocates 60 instructional hours across 15 four-hour units. Unit allocation is StateCourse's design proposal; the Commission curriculum does not prescribe this allocation.

## Learning outcomes

On completion, a student should be able to:

1. Explain Hawaiʻi-specific land history and the present-day consequences for ownership, land records, and transactions.
2. Identify the licensing rules, duties, supervision structure, and conduct requirements that apply to Hawaiʻi real estate licensees.
3. Analyze property interests, title, land use, financing, contracts, escrow, disclosures, valuation, leasing, and tax questions using Hawaiʻi examples.
4. Recognize when a question requires a Hawaiʻi attorney, tax professional, surveyor, appraiser, lender, or public agency.
5. Perform common real estate calculations and document transaction decisions clearly.

## Timed curriculum

Each unit is designed for 240 minutes of instructional work. The allocation includes guided reading, worked examples, retrieval practice, and the applied task. It is not a claim that the regulator has approved the timing.

| Unit | Hours | Commission curriculum coverage | Outcomes and applied task |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 01 | 4 | 1. Introduction and Hawaiian history | Describe Hawaiʻi's real estate profession and market; distinguish mokupuni, moku, ahupuaʻa, ʻili, lele, and kuleana concepts. **Task:** annotate a fictional parcel history and identify questions that require land-record research. |
| 02 | 4 | 2. Laws, land use, and controls | Distinguish state and county land-use roles; interpret zoning, permits, shoreline/SMA, environmental, and private controls at a basic level. **Task:** prepare a due-diligence issue list for a fictional coastal parcel. |
| 03 | 4 | 3. Hawaiʻi licensing laws | Explain HRS Chapter 467, HAR Chapter 99, DCCA/PVL/REB/Commission/RICO roles, licensing, supervision, and discipline. **Task:** classify activities in short scenarios as licensed, exempt, or requiring escalation. |
| 04 | 4 | 3–4. Licensing laws and agency | Explain client/customer, fiduciary duties, agency formation, dual representation and written consent, conflicts, and broker responsibility. **Task:** draft a relationship and disclosure timeline for a fictional transaction. |
| 05 | 4 | 4–5. Agency and property rights | Apply Hawaiʻi agency concepts to sale, lease, and management; distinguish estates, ownership interests, and encumbrances. **Task:** compare two fictional ownership records and identify limits on what the licensee may conclude. |
| 06 | 4 | 6–7. Transfers and encumbrances | Compare conveyance instruments, recording, title evidence, liens, easements, restrictions, and other encumbrances. **Task:** build a title-and-transfer checklist from a fictional preliminary title report. |
| 07 | 4 | 8. Contracts | Identify contract formation, authority, performance, contingencies, breach, remedies, and Hawaiʻi-specific transaction issues. **Task:** mark a fictional offer for missing terms and questions for the supervising broker or attorney. |
| 08 | 4 | 8 and 17. Contracts and Hawaiʻi forms | Read Hawaiʻi transaction forms as documents, not legal advice; identify parties, dates, deadlines, disclosures, and signature gaps. **Task:** create a deadline calendar from a fictional executed agreement. |
| 09 | 4 | 9. Closing and settlement | Explain escrow roles, prorations, settlement statements, recording sequence, and funds handling. **Task:** reconcile a fictional closing statement and show each calculation. |
| 10 | 4 | 10. Disclosures | Identify material facts, required disclosures, investigation limits, adverse facts, and escalation obligations. **Task:** prepare a fact log and disclosure decision tree for a fictional property. |
| 11 | 4 | 11. Valuation and market analysis | Distinguish price, cost, and value; use comparable sales and market indicators without presenting an appraisal. **Task:** build a comparative market analysis and explain its limitations. |
| 12 | 4 | 12 and 18. Financing and mathematics | Explain common financing structures and perform loan, interest, commission, prorations, area, and percentage calculations. **Task:** complete a transaction worksheet with checked units and formulas. |
| 13 | 4 | 13. Property management and leasing | Explain lease terms, management duties, tenant communication, maintenance, funds, and risk. **Task:** triage a fictional property-management file and identify urgent escalation items. |
| 14 | 4 | 14–16. Ownership, discrimination, and taxes | Compare ownership forms; apply fair-housing and equal-service principles; identify basic property-tax concepts and when tax advice is needed. **Task:** review fictional marketing copy and a property-tax question for compliance and referral. |
| 15 | 4 | 17–18 and integrated review | Apply Hawaiʻi forms and calculations to an end-to-end transaction; demonstrate source use and professional boundaries. **Task:** submit a complete transaction file index, issue log, and calculation check. |

## Assessment architecture

- **Unit practice:** short retrieval checks after each topic, with explanations and a second attempt after remediation.
- **Applied assignments:** one original scenario-based deliverable per unit; rubric scores accuracy, source use, reasoning, disclosure/escalation, and completeness.
- **Cumulative course examination:** two independently authored forms, balanced across the 18 required curriculum sections and the current PSI salesperson exam content outline. This is a StateCourse course examination, not PSI's licensing examination and not a reproduction of PSI items.
- **Open items before release:** confirm the Commission's current minimum exam length/pass standard and online/independent-study controls; obtain qualified Hawaiʻi instructor review; reconcile every lesson and assessment to the official curriculum and current law.
