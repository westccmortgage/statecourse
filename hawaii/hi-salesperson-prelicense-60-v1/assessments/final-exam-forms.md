# Hawaiʻi Salesperson Prelicensing — Cumulative Course Exam Forms

**Course ID:** HI-SALES-60-v1  
**Status:** Development draft. These are original StateCourse questions, not official Hawaiʻi or PSI examination items.  
**Administration proposal:** Closed-book, 30 items per form, one point per item. Suggested course threshold: 75%, subject to instructor and Commission review. This suggested threshold is not represented as a Hawaiʻi regulatory requirement.  
**Source alignment:** Hawaiʻi Commission's salesperson curriculum and the PSI Hawaiʻi Candidate Information Bulletin effective January 16, 2026. Confirm item counts, timing, pass score, retake/remediation, and delivery controls before submission or use.

## Form A

Choose the best answer.

1. A buyer asks whether long family occupancy proves ownership.  
A. Yes, occupancy always establishes title. B. No; verify current ownership through appropriate records and qualified review. C. Yes, if taxes were paid. D. The listing is enough.

2. A client asks if a proposed use is allowed on an agricultural parcel.  
A. Promise approval based on lot size. B. Check official state/county controls and refer entitlement questions to planning professionals. C. Rely on a neighboring use. D. Assume the seller's statement is binding.

3. A parcel description in a draft deed conflicts with a prior recorded deed.  
A. Use whichever is shorter. B. Correct it from memory. C. Flag it and seek correction/verification from the responsible title or document professional. D. Ask the buyer to choose.

4. A buyer asks a licensee representing the seller to recommend the buyer's offer price.  
A. Clarify the licensee's role and duties before giving advice. B. Share confidential seller information. C. Act as the buyer's agent without disclosure. D. Promise the seller will accept.

5. What best distinguishes a client from a customer?  
A. A client has an agency relationship; a customer may receive limited services without that relationship. B. They are always identical. C. A customer is always a buyer. D. A client must be a licensee.

6. A licensee considers dual representation. Which is essential?  
A. Informed consent and the required written documentation. B. Verbal consent from one party. C. A larger deposit. D. A friendly relationship between parties.

7. A recorded easement appears in a title report. The licensee should:  
A. Declare it unenforceable. B. Identify it, provide the report, and refer legal interpretation to counsel. C. Remove it from the file. D. Promise it has no effect.

8. Which evidence best establishes a current parcel boundary?  
A. A listing photo. B. A fence line alone. C. Appropriate recorded survey/land records reviewed by qualified professionals. D. A neighbor's recollection.

9. An offer has an unclear term about an appliance.  
A. Assume it conveys. B. Flag the ambiguity and have the parties clarify it in writing. C. Promise it will stay. D. Wait until closing.

10. A party asks whether a breach permits rescission.  
A. Give a binding legal opinion. B. Document the facts and refer the legal question to an attorney. C. Tell the party to stop performing. D. Delete the notices.

11. A signed form is missing a date that controls a deadline.  
A. Guess from an email. B. Verify the controlling record and correct the file through the proper process. C. Use upload date. D. Leave the gap unrecorded.

12. A deadline calendar should be based on:  
A. The earliest date from all drafts. B. Controlling executed documents and verified triggering events. C. The agent's memory. D. A verbal summary only.

13. A tax proration does not match the source bill.  
A. Adjust it silently. B. Show the calculation and source, then request correction from the responsible settlement professional. C. Hide the difference in another fee. D. Tell the client not to review.

14. A client's funds are offered to the licensee for temporary holding in a personal account.  
A. Accept if documented later. B. Follow trust-fund law and brokerage controls; do not commingle. C. Hold cash in a desk. D. Use the account for one day.

15. A seller reports a repaired leak. The licensee should:  
A. Describe the property as defect-free. B. Accurately document the report, obtain available records, and follow applicable disclosure procedures. C. Omit it if repaired. D. Tell the buyer not to investigate.

16. A neighbor makes an unverified encroachment allegation.  
A. Present it as proven. B. Record it as unverified, identify verification steps, and follow disclosure/escalation duties. C. Delete the message. D. Decide the boundary from a map screenshot.

17. A CMA should be described as:  
A. A guaranteed sale price. B. A broker's market analysis with stated assumptions and limitations, not an appraisal. C. A legal title opinion. D. A lender's appraisal.

18. The best comparable sale is generally:  
A. The highest-priced property nearby. B. A recent, similar sale with reliable data and explainable differences. C. Any property with the same bedroom count. D. An unsold listing.

19. A $600,000 loan on a $750,000 purchase has a loan-to-price ratio of:  
A. 60%. B. 75%. C. 80%. D. 125%.

20. One point on a $400,000 loan equals:  
A. $400. B. $4,000. C. $40,000. D. $400,000.

21. A tenant reports an active water leak. The first response should be to:  
A. Wait for the next monthly meeting. B. Follow emergency/broker procedures, route repair action promptly, and document. C. Promise rent abatement. D. Tell the tenant to deduct repairs.

22. A verbal request to change a lease should be:  
A. Accepted as binding without documentation. B. Routed through the broker and documented in the required written form if agreed. C. Added by the licensee to the signed lease. D. Ignored.

23. A housing provider asks the licensee to discourage families with children.  
A. Follow the instruction. B. Refuse discriminatory treatment and escalate under fair-housing law and brokerage policy. C. Ask the family to apply elsewhere. D. Use coded advertising.

24. A buyer asks whether changing ownership will reduce taxes.  
A. Promise a reduction. B. Refer the tax question to a qualified tax professional. C. Copy another client's outcome. D. Say transfers are always tax-free.

25. A coastal parcel may be subject to shoreline and environmental controls. The licensee should:  
A. Decide the boundary visually. B. Identify the questions and refer to official agencies and qualified specialists. C. Promise a building permit. D. Ignore it if the house exists.

26. A condominium listing uses “exclusive ownership of all common areas.” What should the licensee do?  
A. Repeat the phrase as fact. B. Check governing documents and correct the description to match the recorded ownership interests. C. Assume the seller is correct. D. Omit condominium documents.

27. A closing file includes an older unsigned disclosure and a newer signed addendum.  
A. Use the old disclosure. B. Reconcile versions and confirm the controlling executed documents. C. Keep both without review. D. Delete the addendum.

28. A buyer asks whether a recorded restriction is valid.  
A. Decide validity based on age. B. Refer legal interpretation to an attorney while preserving and sharing the document. C. Tell the buyer to disregard it. D. Ask the seller to interpret it.

29. A 2,400-square-foot property is priced at $1,200,000. Price per square foot is:  
A. $200. B. $400. C. $500. D. $2,000.

30. Why should an instructor log remediation and resubmission?  
A. It replaces the course record. B. It documents learning, assessment, and completion decisions for the correct course version. C. It guarantees regulatory approval. D. It removes the need for a final exam.

### Form A key

1 B — Occupancy alone does not establish record title.  
2 B — Verify controls through official sources and qualified review.  
3 C — Do not alter legal descriptions from memory.  
4 A — Role clarity comes before advice.  
5 A — Agency status determines duties and scope.  
6 A — Informed consent and required writing are central.  
7 B — A licensee identifies and routes, not opines on enforceability.  
8 C — Boundary conclusions require proper records/survey work.  
9 B — Ambiguous terms should be resolved in writing.  
10 B — Legal remedies are for counsel.  
11 B — Deadlines require verified controlling records.  
12 B — Calendar from executed documents and verified events.  
13 B — Transparent calculation and correction by the responsible professional.  
14 B — Follow trust-fund controls; no commingling.  
15 B — Record facts and follow disclosure process.  
16 B — Preserve the allegation's unverified status and route it.  
17 B — A CMA is not an appraisal or guarantee.  
18 B — Similarity and reliable data matter.  
19 C — 600,000 ÷ 750,000 = 80%.  
20 B — 1% × 400,000 = 4,000.  
21 B — Safety, prompt routing, and records.  
22 B — Changes require authorization and proper written documentation.  
23 B — Equal service and escalation.  
24 B — Tax consequences require qualified tax advice.  
25 B — Identify and route technical/legal issues.  
26 B — Verify condominium ownership and common elements.  
27 B — Use the current controlling signed record.  
28 B — Preserve the document and refer legal interpretation.  
29 C — 1,200,000 ÷ 2,400 = 500.  
30 B — Traceable course-version and learning records.

## Form B

Choose the best answer.

1. A seller says a parcel's historical land division proves the present lot line.  
A. Accept it as conclusive. B. Use it as context, then verify the current legal description and records. C. Use the listing map. D. Ask the buyer to decide.

2. A buyer wants to build an additional dwelling.  
A. Promise it is allowed. B. Identify applicable state/county land-use, permit, and utility questions and refer for official verification. C. Rely on a nearby dwelling. D. Assume residential zoning permits any number of units.

3. A salesperson is asked to perform a task that may be regulated.  
A. Check the actual activity against current licensing law and exceptions. B. Decide from the person's title. C. Assume unpaid work is always exempt. D. Ignore the issue if the broker is busy.

4. A consumer asks who handles a complaint about licensee conduct.  
A. Identify the appropriate DCCA/REB/Commission/RICO path based on the issue. B. Send every complaint to the county tax office. C. Use a trade association as regulator. D. Ignore it until a lawsuit.

5. A licensee receives confidential information from a buyer while representing the seller.  
A. Share it to help negotiations. B. Stop and clarify role, duties, and permitted handling with the supervising broker. C. Put it in public remarks. D. Send it to other buyers.

6. Which best describes dual representation?  
A. One licensee or firm represents opposing interests subject to applicable law, disclosure, and informed consent. B. A licensee represents only the seller. C. A transaction has two offers. D. A buyer works with two agents.

7. A title report lists a lien.  
A. Promise it will be removed. B. Record the issue and route it to escrow/title/counsel for resolution. C. Delete the item. D. Tell the buyer the lien is invalid.

8. A fence appears beyond the surveyed line.  
A. Treat the fence as the boundary. B. Flag the discrepancy and refer to the surveyor/title professional. C. Move the fence. D. Close without disclosure.

9. An offer includes inconsistent dates in two sections.  
A. Choose the date that benefits the client. B. Flag the conflict and have the parties resolve it through the proper written process. C. Ignore one section. D. Use the date the agent prefers.

10. A contract party asks what a court would award after breach.  
A. Give a definite prediction. B. Refer to an attorney and provide an accurate factual record. C. Threaten the other party. D. Tell the party to stop all payments.

11. A deadline-triggering document arrives after the calendar was prepared.  
A. Keep the calendar unchanged. B. Reconcile the controlling signed version and update reminders with audit trail. C. Erase the old calendar. D. Use the email timestamp without checking the document.

12. Why preserve superseded drafts?  
A. They replace signed forms. B. They may document the transaction history, but must be clearly distinguished from controlling documents. C. They prove acceptance. D. They should be sent to the public.

13. A closing credit differs from the negotiated agreement.  
A. Change the agreement. B. Identify the discrepancy and ask the responsible parties to reconcile it before closing. C. Hide it in escrow charges. D. Tell the client it is immaterial.

14. A licensee receives a deposit.  
A. Place it in a personal account temporarily. B. Follow the applicable trust-fund handling and brokerage procedures and document receipt. C. Give it to the seller directly. D. Wait to report it.

15. A seller says a wall was repaired but has no records.  
A. State the repair is complete and code-compliant. B. Record the statement and identify documentation/inspection questions without making a technical conclusion. C. Omit the issue. D. Guarantee no future problem.

16. A buyer reports a material fact after reviewing disclosures.  
A. Ignore it because disclosures were signed. B. Record and route the new information under the disclosure and supervision process. C. Delete the message. D. Tell the buyer it is too late.

17. A property has fewer recent comparable sales than expected.  
A. Invent an adjustment. B. Explain data limits, broaden carefully with transparent reasoning, and avoid a guaranteed value. C. Use only the highest sale. D. Say the CMA is an appraisal.

18. A comparable has a materially different location and condition.  
A. Use it without comment. B. Explain the differences and whether an adjustment is supportable; otherwise exclude it. C. Assume all differences cancel. D. Set the value from its list price.

19. A $540,000 loan on a $900,000 price has an LTV ratio of:  
A. 40%. B. 60%. C. 75%. D. 166.7%.

20. Two discount points on a $350,000 loan equal:  
A. $700. B. $3,500. C. $7,000. D. $70,000.

21. A tenant reports a possible electrical hazard.  
A. Wait for the next inspection. B. Follow urgent safety procedures, notify responsible parties promptly, and document the report. C. Tell the tenant to repair it. D. Promise the landlord will pay a particular amount.

22. A landlord asks a licensee to keep a tenant's maintenance request off the record.  
A. Comply. B. Preserve accurate records and follow broker/management procedures. C. Delete the message. D. Move the request to a personal phone.

23. An advertisement says “perfect for young couples only.”  
A. Publish it. B. Revise to neutral property features and ensure equal access; escalate any fair-housing concern. C. Add “no families” in smaller type. D. Target only one demographic.

24. A buyer asks for legal advice about taking title jointly.  
A. Recommend the form with the lowest taxes. B. Explain the licensee's role and refer to an attorney and tax professional as appropriate. C. Copy the seller's vesting. D. Choose a form for the buyer.

25. The property has an older cesspool and uncertain sewer connection.  
A. Assume it is compliant because it is in use. B. Identify records, inspection, and agency questions; refer to qualified professionals. C. Promise it can remain. D. Ignore it if there is no odor.

26. A condo association has rules that may restrict rentals.  
A. Rely on the listing remarks. B. Review current governing documents and refer legal interpretation as needed. C. Promise rentals are unrestricted. D. Assume rules are unenforceable.

27. A buyer's financing addendum changes a deadline.  
A. Keep the old calendar. B. Reconcile the executed addendum and update the deadline record. C. Use the original offer only. D. Assume the lender will track it.

28. A client asks whether a shoreline certification is sufficient for a planned project.  
A. Confirm based on the date on the document. B. Refer the current status and project applicability to the responsible agency and qualified professionals. C. Use a prior neighbor's permit. D. Promise approval.

29. A $1,050,000 sale has a 5% commission. What is the gross commission before any split or other agreement?  
A. $5,250. B. $52,500. C. $105,000. D. $525,000.

30. A student fails a unit practice check.  
A. Issue completion anyway. B. Provide remediation tied to missed objectives and re-assess according to the approved course policy. C. Delete the attempt. D. Reduce the course hours.
