# Hawaiʻi Salesperson Prelicensing — Unit Practice Bank

**Course ID:** HI-SALES-60-v1  
**Status:** Original practice questions for development. Not a state licensing examination.  
**Use:** 30 formative items, two per unit. Students should explain the reasoning, then review the rationale. Legal and regulatory answers must be checked against the current official source before release.

## Unit 01 — Hawaiʻi real estate and land history

**1.** A listing says a parcel is “family land” and therefore needs no title review. What is the best response?  
A. Accept the statement if the family has occupied the property for many years.  
B. Explain that occupancy alone does not establish current record title; identify the records and professional review needed.  
C. Tell the buyer the property is government land.  
D. Remove the parcel from consideration without research.  
**Answer: B.** The course's land-history unit teaches that historical tenure and current ownership records are related but not interchangeable. A licensee should identify research needs and avoid a title conclusion.

**2.** Which approach best distinguishes historical Hawaiʻi land divisions from a modern parcel description?  
A. Treat each term as a current legal lot identifier.  
B. Use the terms as historical context, then verify the modern parcel using official land and county records.  
C. Substitute a neighborhood name for a legal description.  
D. Assume the historical system has no present relevance.  
**Answer: B.** Historical context can inform research, but a transaction requires verification of present records and the legal description.

## Unit 02 — Land use and controls

**3.** A buyer asks whether an agricultural-zoned parcel can be used for a proposed dwelling. What should the licensee do first?  
A. Promise the use is allowed because the parcel is large.  
B. Check the applicable state and county sources and direct the buyer to planning officials and qualified professionals.  
C. Rely on a neighboring property's use.  
D. Treat the seller's opinion as approval.  
**Answer: B.** Use rights depend on governing classifications, county controls, permits, and facts. The licensee should not guarantee entitlement.

**4.** A coastal property has an uncertain shoreline boundary. Which next step is most appropriate?  
A. Estimate the boundary from a listing photograph.  
B. Identify the issue, locate the relevant official process, and refer the buyer to qualified survey/shoreline professionals.  
C. Assume the shoreline is fixed by the visible vegetation.  
D. Ignore the issue unless construction is planned.  
**Answer: B.** The course emphasizes shoreline certification and specialist verification rather than visual assumptions.

## Unit 03 — Licensing and regulator roles

**5.** A person is paid to negotiate a real estate transaction for another person. What is the best course response?  
A. Determine whether the activity is regulated, check the current licensing law and exceptions, and escalate if unclear.  
B. Assume payment means the person is automatically exempt.  
C. Assume all negotiations are unregulated.  
D. Decide based only on the person's job title.  
**Answer: A.** Licensing turns on the law and the actual activity, not a title alone.

**6.** A consumer reports suspected licensee misconduct. Which regulator pathway is the best starting point?  
A. DCCA's Real Estate Branch and the applicable complaint process, with the specific roles of the Commission and RICO verified.  
B. The county planning office for every issue.  
C. The licensee's social media page.  
D. A private trade group as the licensing authority.  
**Answer: A.** The course distinguishes Commission, PVL/REB, DCCA, and RICO functions; the correct route depends on the issue.

## Unit 04 — Agency

**7.** A buyer asks a licensee who is working with the seller to recommend an offer price. What should the licensee do?  
A. Provide confidential seller information to help the buyer.  
B. Clarify the licensee's role and duties, avoid acting beyond that role, and follow the required disclosure/consent process.  
C. Say the buyer automatically became a client.  
D. Give a binding legal opinion on agency.  
**Answer: B.** Role clarity, duties, disclosure, and consent must be handled before advice that could create or exceed an agency relationship.

**8.** Before a licensee undertakes dual representation, what is central to the course analysis?  
A. Informed written consent and compliance with Hawaiʻi's applicable rules.  
B. Verbal permission from only one party.  
C. The licensee's belief that both parties are friendly.  
D. A higher commission.  
**Answer: A.** The Hawaiʻi curriculum specifically addresses dual representation and written agreement/consent requirements; exact current law must be verified.

## Unit 05 — Property rights and interests

**9.** A buyer sees a path across a parcel and asks whether it is a public right of way. What is the most responsible answer?  
A. Confirm it is public because people use it.  
B. Identify possible easement/access questions and verify them through records and qualified review.  
C. State that no easement exists unless a sign is posted.  
D. Advise the buyer to block it immediately.  
**Answer: B.** Visible use alone does not establish the legal status of an access right.

**10.** A condominium listing describes “oceanfront ownership.” Which question should be checked before describing the buyer's rights?  
A. Whether the unit's ownership interest, common elements, and association restrictions match the statement.  
B. Whether the unit is furnished.  
C. Whether the building has a pool.  
D. Whether the seller has a mortgage.  
**Answer: A.** Ownership form and governing documents define what is owned and what restrictions apply.

## Unit 06 — Transfer and encumbrances

**11.** A preliminary title report lists a recorded easement. What should the licensee do?  
A. Interpret enforceability and advise the buyer whether to proceed.  
B. Explain that the report identifies a recorded item, provide it to the appropriate parties, and refer legal interpretation to counsel.  
C. Delete it from the transaction file.  
D. Tell the buyer it has no practical effect.  
**Answer: B.** A licensee should identify the issue and route legal interpretation to a qualified professional.

**12.** A deed draft contains a legal description that differs from the prior recorded deed. What is the best next step?  
A. Correct it from memory.  
B. Stop relying on the draft, flag the inconsistency, and seek correction from the preparer/title professional.  
C. Use the shorter description.  
D. Ask the buyer to choose one.  
**Answer: B.** Legal-description inconsistencies require verification by the responsible document professional.

## Unit 07 — Contracts

**13.** A purchase agreement includes an unclear appliance term. What should the licensee do?  
A. Assume all appliances convey.  
B. Flag the ambiguity and have the parties resolve it through the proper written process.  
C. Promise the item will remain.  
D. Wait until closing to mention it.  
**Answer: B.** Material terms should be clear in the transaction documents, not left to assumption.

**14.** A party asks a licensee to explain what legal remedy applies after an alleged breach. What is the appropriate response?  
A. Select the remedy and tell the party to pursue it.  
B. Explain the factual timeline the licensee can document and refer legal advice to an attorney.  
C. Delete the communications.  
D. Tell the other party the contract is void.  
**Answer: B.** The licensee should preserve facts and refer legal interpretation.

## Unit 08 — Hawaiʻi forms

**15.** A signed transaction form has a missing date next to a deadline-triggering event. What should happen?  
A. Guess the date from an email timestamp.  
B. Flag the gap, verify the controlling document and event, and ask the responsible parties to correct the record.  
C. Leave it blank and continue.  
D. Use the date the file was uploaded.  
**Answer: B.** Deadline tracking must be traceable to the signed record and the governing form.

**16.** Why keep a document index with version, parties, and received date?  
A. It replaces the signed transaction documents.  
B. It supports accurate retrieval, deadline management, and a defensible transaction record.  
C. It proves every disclosure was legally sufficient.  
D. It eliminates the need for supervision.  
**Answer: B.** Good recordkeeping supports workflow but does not replace legal compliance or supervision.

## Unit 09 — Closing and settlement

**17.** A settlement statement's tax proration does not match the underlying bill. What should the licensee do?  
A. Change the amount to make the statement balance.  
B. Show the calculation and source, then ask escrow/authorized professionals to resolve the discrepancy.  
C. Hide the difference in a fee line.  
D. Tell the buyer not to review it.  
**Answer: B.** The discrepancy should be transparent and routed to the responsible settlement professional.

**18.** A client asks a licensee to hold transaction funds in a personal account. What is the appropriate response?  
A. Agree if the client trusts the licensee.  
B. Follow the applicable trust-fund rules and brokerage procedures; do not commingle funds.  
C. Accept cash and document it later.  
D. Use the account temporarily.  
**Answer: B.** The Hawaiʻi curriculum covers trust-fund handling; funds must follow applicable law and controls.

## Unit 10 — Disclosures

**19.** A seller reports a prior leak but says it was “fixed.” What is the best first action?  
A. Remove the issue from the disclosure record.  
B. Record the seller's statement accurately, obtain available documentation, and follow the applicable disclosure and escalation process.  
C. Describe the property as defect-free.  
D. Tell the buyer not to ask about it.  
**Answer: B.** A licensee should preserve facts and disclosures without minimizing or making unsupported conclusions.

**20.** A neighbor makes an unverified boundary complaint. What should the licensee do?  
A. Present it as proven encroachment.  
B. Record it as an unverified report, identify verification steps, and disclose/escalate as required.  
C. Delete it because it came from a neighbor.  
D. Decide the boundary using a map screenshot.  
**Answer: B.** Accurate qualification and verification protect the transaction record and prevent overstatement.

## Unit 11 — Valuation and market analysis

**21.** A client asks for a “guaranteed value” based on three comparable sales. What is the correct response?  
A. Guarantee a sale price.  
B. Explain that a CMA is a market analysis with assumptions and limitations, not an appraisal or guarantee.  
C. Use the highest comparable only.  
D. Add a premium because the property is attractive.  
**Answer: B.** The licensee must represent the scope and limitations of the analysis accurately.

**22.** Which comparable is generally most useful?  
A. A recent, similar property with explainable differences and reliable data.  
B. The most expensive sale in the county.  
C. Any property with the same bedroom count.  
D. A listing that has not sold but supports the target price.  
**Answer: A.** Similarity, recency, location, and reliable adjustments matter more than a single matching feature.

## Unit 12 — Financing and math

**23.** A buyer borrows $720,000 against a $900,000 purchase price. What is the loan-to-price ratio?  
A. 8%  
B. 20%  
C. 80%  
D. 125%  
**Answer: C.** $720,000 ÷ $900,000 = 0.80, or 80%.

**24.** A $500,000 loan has a one-point charge. What is the charge before any other fees?  
A. $500  
B. $5,000  
C. $50,000  
D. $500,000  
**Answer: B.** One point is 1% of the loan amount: $500,000 × 0.01 = $5,000.

## Unit 13 — Property management and leasing

**25.** A tenant reports an active water leak late at night. What is the first priority?  
A. Wait until the next routine office meeting.  
B. Follow the emergency response and broker policy, communicate promptly, document the report, and route repair action to the responsible party.  
C. Promise the tenant a rent reduction.  
D. Tell the tenant to repair it and deduct any cost.  
**Answer: B.** Safety, prompt routing, and accurate records come first; legal or lease remedies require proper review.

**26.** A tenant asks the licensee to change a lease clause verbally. What should the licensee do?  
A. Agree and rely on the conversation.  
B. Refer the request through the broker and ensure any agreed change is handled in the required written form.  
C. Tell the tenant the landlord must accept.  
D. Alter the signed lease without authorization.  
**Answer: B.** Changes should be authorized and documented in the proper written process.

## Unit 14 — Ownership, fair housing, and taxes

**27.** A housing provider asks the licensee to discourage families with children from viewing a unit. What is the best response?  
A. Follow the instruction to protect the relationship.  
B. Refuse discriminatory screening, provide equal service, and escalate under fair-housing law and brokerage policy.  
C. Ask the family whether they can keep children quiet.  
D. Advertise the unit as “adult preferred.”  
**Answer: B.** Equal housing service and fair-housing requirements prohibit discriminatory treatment; specific facts should be escalated.

**28.** A buyer asks whether a proposed ownership transfer will reduce their taxes. What should the licensee do?  
A. Estimate the tax savings as a fact.  
B. Explain the transaction information the licensee can provide and refer tax consequences to a qualified tax professional.  
C. Use another client's result.  
D. Tell the buyer all transfers are tax-free.  
**Answer: B.** Tax consequences are fact-specific and require qualified tax advice.

## Unit 15 — Integrated practice

**29.** A transaction file contains an unsigned disclosure, a deadline calendar based on an old draft, and a new financing addendum. What should happen first?  
A. Close the file because all documents are present.  
B. Reconcile the signed version, update the calendar from controlling documents, and notify the supervising broker and parties as appropriate.  
C. Use the earliest date from any version.  
D. Ignore the addendum if it arrived by email.  
**Answer: B.** The file must reflect the controlling executed documents and current deadlines.

**30.** A client asks a licensee to decide whether a shoreline permit is legally sufficient. What is the best response?  
A. Decide based on the property's current use.  
B. Identify the permit and refer the legal/technical sufficiency question to the appropriate agency and qualified professionals.  
C. Confirm sufficiency if the seller has a copy.  
D. Recommend proceeding without review.  
**Answer: B.** The licensee can identify and route the issue, but should not make unsupported legal or technical determinations.
