# Oregon Broker Prelicense — 150-Hour Curriculum Map (Draft)

**Status:** Original course-design draft for educator and provider review; not approved and not available for Oregon credit.  
**Audience:** Oregon broker applicants.  
**Delivery:** Draft for classroom-equivalent/self-paced adaptation. Confirm the provider's school license, Oregon Real Estate Agency (REA) course approval, delivery requirements, examinations, and annual renewal before use.

## Regulator requirements captured

Oregon REA states broker applicants complete seven required prelicense courses. Only Oregon community colleges, colleges, universities, or Oregon private career schools licensed through HECC may offer them; the courses require REA approval. OAR 863-022-0010 lists required course hours, subjects, final-exam item counts, and the private-career-school passing score of 75% for each final. REA states providers must verify the applicant's license application, notify PSI upon successful completion, issue a certificate, and renew course approval annually.

Primary references:
- REA course-provider page: https://www.oregon.gov/rea/educators/pre-license/Pages/broker-education.aspx
- Oregon Administrative Rules, OAR 863-022-0010: https://secure.sos.state.or.us/oard/viewSingleRule.action?ruleVrsnRsn=287127
- Distance-learning provider rule (verify current text and applicability): https://secure.sos.state.or.us/oard/

Before any offering, the provider must verify the current official rules, the precise scope of applicable examination controls, and current REA instructions. This draft does not establish provider eligibility or approval.

## Course 1: Real Estate Law — 30 hours

**Official final specification:** 60 items; 75% passing score for private career schools.

| Unit | Hours | Learning targets |
|---|---:|---|
| Law sources and licensing framework | 4 | Distinguish statute, rule, agency guidance, and local regulation; identify licensee scope |
| Property interests and ownership | 5 | Compare estates, co-ownership, fixtures, and limitations on ownership |
| Land description and public records | 4 | Read basic legal descriptions and identify record/title questions |
| Land-use controls | 4 | Identify zoning, building, environmental, and private restrictions requiring verification |
| Agency and fiduciary duties | 5 | Apply duties, disclosure, confidentiality, and conflict handling |
| Fair housing and consumer protection | 4 | Identify discriminatory conduct, advertising risks, fraud, and privacy concerns |
| Remedies, risk, and dispute pathways | 4 | Identify when to preserve records, notify a principal broker, or refer to counsel |

**Applied assignments:** classify property interests and title questions; analyze an agency/conflict scenario; revise a discriminatory advertisement; prepare an evidence-based legal issue referral memo.

## Shared course design

Each unit includes an original lesson manuscript, worked transaction example, short retrieval practice, applied task, and feedback rubric. Assignment rubric: legal/topic accuracy 40%, analysis/application 30%, documentation/communication 20%, professional boundaries 10%. The assignment briefs above are seeds; each course needs student-facing instructions, source packet, scoring rubric, and instructor feedback before instructional release.

## Oregon source and release controls

- Keep Oregon law materials separated from national-core content and other states' law.
- Record exact rule version/effective date used in each lesson and exam item.
- Check rule changes before each cohort; retire outdated forms, deadlines, fees, and agency procedures.
- Provider must confirm HECC school licensing, REA approval, course titles/hours, distance-learning evidence, exam configuration, applicant verification, PSI reporting, certificate, and annual renewal.
- Do not advertise enrollment, course credit, or approval before written approval and applicable provider authorization are confirmed.
