# Oregon Broker Prelicense — Applied Assignment Workbook (Draft)

Draft for instructor review and conversion to course-platform activities. These assignments are formative and do not replace the required final exam for each approved course.

## Common student instructions

Use only the fictional facts supplied in each case. Separate verified facts from assumptions. Cite the rule, contract term, or course concept used. Do not give legal, tax, lending, engineering, or inspection advice outside a licensee's role. State what must be referred to the principal broker or a qualified professional. Keep client data confidential.

**Scoring rubric (10 points each):** accuracy and source use (4); analysis and completeness (3); clear, professional communication (2); appropriate boundaries/referral (1). Instructor feedback must identify the specific correction and next practice step.

## Real Estate Law — Parcel and rights file

**Case:** A fictional 2-acre parcel has a deed, a recorded utility easement, a fence that appears to cross a boundary, an unverified short-term-rental claim in an old listing, and a buyer interested in building an accessory structure.

**Deliverables:**  
1. One-page fact/evidence table with source and confidence level.  
2. Parcel-rights diagram identifying the easement and possible encroachment as unverified issues.  
3. Checklist of questions for title, survey, local planning, and buyer due diligence.  
4. Neutral client update that does not promise buildability or rental legality.

**Rubric emphasis:** Distinguish ownership evidence from use permission; do not infer survey or zoning results.

## Oregon Real Estate Practice — Listing launch file

**Case:** Seller authorizes marketing a home. The seller supplies square footage from an old listing, says a roof was repaired, and wants the ad to state “perfect for families.” No current measurement or repair receipt is available.

**Deliverables:**  
1. Verification checklist and follow-up questions.  
2. Revised objective ad using only supportable facts.  
3. Seller approval/authorization log and next-step schedule.  
4. Escalation note for unresolved claims.

**Rubric emphasis:** Accurate, authorized advertising; fair-housing-safe language; source traceability.

## Real Estate Finance — Offer financing comparison

**Case:** Two offers have different down payments, financing contingencies, estimated closing dates, and seller credits. Loan estimates are fictional and incomplete.

**Deliverables:**  
1. Side-by-side comparison separating contract terms from lender estimates.  
2. List of missing information and uncertainties.  
3. Calendar of financing milestones.  
4. Neutral seller-facing summary; do not predict loan approval or recommend a product.

**Rubric emphasis:** Correct arithmetic from supplied data; no guarantee; appropriate lender referral.

## Contracts — Deadline and amendment review

**Case:** A fictional purchase agreement includes inspection, financing, title review, and closing dates. The parties agree by phone to move one date, but no written amendment exists.

**Deliverables:**  
1. Deadline calendar with source clause, responsible party, reminder date, and evidence of completion.  
2. Identify the oral date change as not yet documented.  
3. Draft a communication to the principal broker describing the issue and requesting direction.  
4. List what authorized written documentation is needed before treating the date as changed.

**Rubric emphasis:** Do not alter a signed contract or assume an extension.

## Agency Law — Representation and conflict map

**Case:** A prospective buyer asks a licensee for confidential strategy while the licensee already has a relationship with the seller of a property the buyer wants.

**Deliverables:**  
1. Parties-and-relationships map.  
2. Identify actual and potential conflicts and what facts are missing.  
3. Explain what must be disclosed and when, citing course materials.  
4. Prepare a broker-consultation note and a neutral client update.

**Rubric emphasis:** No unauthorized disclosure of confidential information; supervision and informed choices.

## Property Management — Maintenance and records log

**Case:** Tenant reports water intrusion, a smoke alarm chirp, and a broken exterior lock. Owner asks the manager to “wait until next month.” No inspection has occurred.

**Deliverables:**  
1. Time-stamped issue log separating report, observation, and action.  
2. Immediate follow-up questions and escalation path.  
3. Tenant communication acknowledging receipt without diagnosing cause.  
4. Owner update requesting authorization and qualified assessment.

**Rubric emphasis:** No unsupported habitability conclusion; prompt routing under applicable law and management policy.

## Real Estate Brokerage — Trust-fund exception and corrective action

**Case:** A fictional brokerage reconciliation shows a deposit receipt entered one day later than the bank record; the file is missing a receipt acknowledgment; a staff member suggests changing the date to match.

**Deliverables:**  
1. Exception report preserving actual dates and source documents.  
2. Notify/escalate plan following brokerage and applicable Oregon requirements.  
3. Proposed corrective-action checklist: reconciliation, documentation, staff coaching, follow-up review.  
4. Short client-safe explanation if one is required by the supervising broker.

**Rubric emphasis:** Never backdate or fabricate; preserve records; immediate principal-broker escalation.
