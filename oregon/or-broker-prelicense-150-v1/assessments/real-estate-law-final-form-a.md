# Oregon Real Estate Law — Final Examination Form A (Draft)

**Course:** Oregon Broker Prelicense, Real Estate Law, 30 clock-hours.  
**Exam length:** 60 questions, as required by OAR 863-022-0010(2)(a).  
**Passing standard:** At least 75% for an approved private career school (45 correct of 60).  
**Status:** Original draft questions, not agency questions. Instructor and course owner must review against the complete current Oregon-approved course outline and law/rule sources before use. Secure and separately store the answer key in the delivery system.

Official rule: https://secure.sos.state.or.us/oard/viewSingleRule.action?ruleVrsnRsn=224867  
Rule 863-022-0010 specifies the 30-hour course, 60-item final, and 75% private-school pass mark. This file supplies a draft question form only; it does not establish provider or course approval.

## Questions

1. Which statement best describes real property?  
A. Land and items permanently attached, subject to applicable law. B. Any item located inside a home. C. Only the soil beneath a building. D. Any item listed in an advertisement.

2. A seller and buyer disagree whether a removable appliance is included. The best transaction document is:  
A. A neighbor's statement. B. A clear written term identifying the appliance. C. An oral promise to discuss later. D. The tax assessor's record.

3. A fixture is most commonly distinguished from personal property by considering:  
A. The color and age of the item. B. How it is attached, adapted, and intended by the parties. C. The item's purchase price alone. D. Whether it appears in a photograph.

4. An appurtenant easement generally:  
A. Gives ownership of the burdened parcel. B. Benefits a parcel and may pass with that parcel, according to its terms. C. Automatically expires at sale. D. Replaces zoning approval.

5. A legal description is inconsistent with a street address. A licensee should:  
A. Rewrite it using an online map. B. Flag it and seek authoritative title/survey review. C. Ignore it if the parcel looks right. D. Ask the buyer to choose one.

6. A deed is best understood as:  
A. An instrument used to convey an interest in real property. B. A loan approval. C. An inspection report. D. A zoning permit.

7. Recording an instrument generally serves to:  
A. Guarantee the property's physical condition. B. Place the instrument in public records and provide notice under recording law. C. Create a building permit. D. Resolve every title defect.

8. Which professional is best suited to determine a disputed boundary location?  
A. A real estate photographer. B. A qualified land surveyor. C. A lender. D. A property manager.

9. A title commitment is primarily used to:  
A. Report proposed title insurance coverage and identified requirements/exceptions. B. Guarantee future appreciation. C. Certify building-code compliance. D. Replace a survey in all cases.

10. A recorded lien may affect title because it:  
A. Is a claim or encumbrance against property or an interest in it. B. Automatically transfers ownership to the lienholder. C. Eliminates all easements. D. Confirms market value.

11. Zoning rules are generally adopted by:  
A. Local government land-use authorities. B. A private appraiser. C. The buyer's lender. D. The listing brokerage.

12. A buyer asks whether a parcel can be used for a particular business. The best response is to:  
A. Promise the use based on a prior listing. B. Confirm with the local authority and review applicable restrictions. C. Rely solely on the seller's opinion. D. Treat a neighboring use as approval.

13. A private deed restriction may:  
A. Impose use limits separate from zoning, subject to enforceability and law. B. Replace all public law. C. Automatically create a mortgage. D. Guarantee a permit.

14. An encroachment is most accurately described as:  
A. A possible physical intrusion across a property boundary. B. A lender's appraisal adjustment. C. A recorded deed transfer. D. A zoning variance.

15. Which statement about a survey is most appropriate?  
A. It can provide professional evidence about boundaries and improvements. B. It guarantees zoning compliance. C. It is always identical to a title report. D. It establishes market value.

16. A buyer wants to add an accessory structure. The licensee should recommend verifying:  
A. Only the seller's preferred closing date. B. Zoning, permits, title restrictions, utilities, and other applicable requirements with authorities/professionals. C. The color of nearby homes. D. The property's online estimate.

17. A life estate generally describes:  
A. A present possessory interest measured by a person's life, with future interests also possible. B. A short-term lease only. C. A mortgage. D. A zoning designation.

18. Tenancy in common generally permits:  
A. Co-owners to hold undivided interests that may be unequal, subject to governing law and agreement. B. Only equal shares. C. No transfer of an owner's interest. D. Automatic survivorship in every case.

19. The legal effect of survivorship in co-ownership depends on:  
A. The recorded instrument and applicable law. B. The street address. C. The listing price. D. The lender's advertising.

20. A trust holding real property may involve:  
A. A trustee holding or managing property under the trust terms. B. An automatic sale at maturity. C. A zoning permit. D. An appraisal waiver.

21. A contract generally requires:  
A. Mutual assent and other applicable formation elements. B. A recording stamp in every case. C. A lender's approval before any offer exists. D. A survey in every case.

22. An offer should be communicated to the other party by:  
A. A method permitted by the contract and applicable rules, with evidence retained. B. An anonymous social-media post. C. A neighbor. D. A property sign.

23. A counteroffer generally:  
A. Rejects or changes the prior offer and proposes different terms. B. Automatically accepts every original term. C. Is a title policy. D. Transfers possession immediately.

24. A contract amendment is best documented by:  
A. An authorized written document signed as required. B. An agent's handwritten change after signing. C. A calendar note alone. D. A verbal discussion not recorded.

25. A contingency is best described as:  
A. A contract condition that may affect a party's performance rights under its terms. B. An appraisal. C. A deed. D. A property tax.

26. A deadline is approaching and the client has not instructed the agent. The agent should:  
A. Promptly alert the client, explain the deadline, and document instructions. B. Decide for the client. C. Extend the deadline unilaterally. D. Delete the deadline from the calendar.

27. A party asks the licensee to backdate an amendment. The licensee should:  
A. Refuse and consult the supervising broker. B. Comply if the client asks. C. Change the date only in the copy sent to escrow. D. Ask the other party to initial later.

28. A contract term is ambiguous. The licensee should:  
A. Make a legal ruling. B. Flag it and seek principal-broker/legal guidance as appropriate. C. Choose the interpretation benefiting the licensee. D. Ignore it.

29. A contract's integration clause usually signals that:  
A. The written agreement may be intended as the parties' complete agreement on covered terms. B. No signatures are needed. C. Oral changes always control. D. The property is insured.

30. Specific performance is generally a remedy that may seek:  
A. A court order requiring performance of a contract, when legally available. B. A zoning change. C. An appraisal. D. Automatic loan approval.

31. Agency is a relationship in which an agent may act on behalf of a principal, subject to:  
A. Applicable law, authority, and duties. B. Only the agent's preferences. C. The property's assessed value. D. A lender's underwriting.

32. A fiduciary duty of loyalty generally requires an agent to:  
A. Put the client's lawful interests ahead of the agent's own, within the agency relationship. B. Conceal all material facts from everyone. C. Guarantee a result. D. Avoid documenting instructions.

33. Confidentiality generally protects:  
A. Client information that is confidential under law and the agency relationship, subject to required disclosure and other limits. B. Every fact about a property from all parties. C. Information already public in all cases. D. A licensee from recordkeeping.

34. An agent discovers a personal financial interest in a transaction. The agent should:  
A. Promptly disclose as required and seek broker guidance. B. Hide it unless asked. C. Destroy related documents. D. Ask the other party not to mention it.

35. A licensee's authority to bind a client is:  
A. Limited by the client's authorization and applicable law. B. Unlimited once a listing is signed. C. Created by a property sign. D. The same as a court's authority.

36. A person is a customer rather than a client when:  
A. No agency representation relationship has been established, though other duties may still apply. B. They have visited a property. C. They have submitted an offer. D. They have a lender.

37. A dual or multiple representation situation raises the need to:  
A. Identify applicable law and disclosure/consent requirements and consult the principal broker. B. Automatically disclose one client's confidential strategy. C. Ignore conflicts. D. Promise equal outcomes.

38. A client gives an instruction inconsistent with law. The agent should:  
A. Decline the unlawful action and consult the supervising broker. B. Follow it because the client is principal. C. Ask another agent to do it. D. Delete the instruction.

39. A material fact is best handled by:  
A. Following applicable disclosure law and form requirements, and accurately distinguishing known facts from assumptions. B. Suppressing it if inconvenient. C. Sharing only with the preferred buyer. D. Replacing it with a guess.

40. An agency disclosure form primarily helps:  
A. Explain the parties' agency relationships and required information. B. Guarantee contract performance. C. Set an appraisal value. D. Transfer title.

41. Federal fair-housing law prohibits discrimination in covered housing transactions based on protected characteristics. A licensee should:  
A. Apply housing criteria consistently and avoid discriminatory conduct or advertising. B. Screen buyers by religion. C. Recommend areas based on race. D. Describe who should live in a property.

42. Which advertisement is most objective?  
A. “Perfect for young families.” B. “Ideal for Christians.” C. “Two-bedroom home near bus service and a public park.” D. “No children.”

43. Steering refers generally to:  
A. Directing a person toward or away from housing based on protected characteristics. B. Explaining a property's square footage. C. Preparing a closing calendar. D. Reviewing a title commitment.

44. A client asks for a neighborhood recommendation based on the residents' race. The licensee should:  
A. Decline demographic steering and offer objective property/location criteria. B. Provide a racial breakdown. C. Ask the seller to choose. D. End all service without explanation.

45. An accommodation request related to disability should be handled by:  
A. Following fair-housing duties and brokerage process, and consulting qualified resources where needed. B. Rejecting every request. C. Asking for unnecessary medical details. D. Promising a legal outcome.

46. Antitrust laws generally prohibit agreements that unreasonably restrain competition, such as:  
A. Competitors agreeing to fix commission rates. B. A buyer comparing homes. C. A seller choosing an asking price independently. D. An agent keeping records.

47. Competitors discuss setting a uniform commission. The licensee should:  
A. Refuse to participate and consult the broker. B. Agree if everyone benefits. C. Put it in a group text. D. Ask a client to approve it.

48. An advertisement uses another brokerage's copyrighted photo without permission. The safest action is to:  
A. Obtain proper authorization or use permitted materials. B. Remove the watermark and publish. C. Assume public access means free use. D. Credit the photographer after publication only.

49. A licensee receives sensitive client data. Appropriate practice is to:  
A. Use approved secure systems and restrict access to those who need it. B. Post it in an open team channel. C. Store it indefinitely on a personal device. D. Forward it to every vendor.

50. A wire instruction changes by email shortly before closing. The agent should:  
A. Independently verify through a known trusted phone number and alert the client to fraud risk. B. Forward the change without verification. C. Reply asking for a password. D. Assume the sender is authentic.

51. Which is a reliable way to confirm current Oregon license law or rule text?  
A. Consult the current official statute/rule source and verify effective date. B. Rely only on an old blog. C. Use another state's course. D. Ask an anonymous forum.

52. A salesperson is uncertain whether an activity is within license scope. The best next step is:  
A. Stop, consult the principal broker, and verify governing law before acting. B. Proceed if the customer agrees. C. Ask the client to assume risk. D. Copy a competitor.

53. An unlicensed assistant may perform a task only if:  
A. The task is permitted for an unlicensed person under current law and brokerage supervision. B. The assistant has worked there for a year. C. The client consents. D. The task is done after hours.

54. A brokerage record should be:  
A. Accurate, retained under applicable requirements, and available to authorized reviewers. B. Changed to match memory. C. Deleted when a deal closes. D. Shared publicly.

55. A complaint alleges mishandling of client funds. The agent should:  
A. Preserve relevant records and promptly notify the principal broker. B. Alter the ledger. C. Contact witnesses to coordinate stories. D. Delete messages.

56. An earnest-money dispute arises. The licensee should:  
A. Follow the contract, applicable law, brokerage procedures, and principal-broker direction. B. Decide who deserves the funds. C. Pay one party from personal funds. D. Ignore written instructions.

57. A licensee becomes aware of possible fraud. The appropriate response is to:  
A. Preserve evidence, avoid further misrepresentation, and escalate to the principal broker/appropriate authority. B. Help complete the transaction without comment. C. Delete evidence. D. Warn only a preferred party.

58. A statutory deadline is uncertain. The agent should:  
A. Verify it from current official sources and consult the broker; do not guess. B. Use a deadline from a different state. C. Estimate based on memory. D. Omit it from the calendar.

59. A course provider's final examination for this Oregon course must contain:  
A. 20 questions. B. 30 questions. C. 40 questions. D. 60 questions.

60. For a private career school student to receive credit for this course, the final-exam score must be at least:  
A. 60%. B. 70%. C. 75%. D. 80%.
