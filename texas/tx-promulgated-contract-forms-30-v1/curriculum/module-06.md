# Texas Promulgated Contract Forms — Module 06

The Remaining Promulgated Forms. The required comparison set is [resale 20-19](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/one-four-family-residential-contract-resale), [condominium resale 30-18](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/residential-condominium-contract-resale-0), [Farm and Ranch 25-17](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/farm-and-ranch-contract-0), [Unimproved Property 9-18](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/unimproved-property-contract-0), [New Home Incomplete Construction 23-20](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/new-home-contract-incomplete-construction-0), and [New Home Completed Construction 24-20](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/new-home-contract-completed-construction-0). These six currently listed contracts are effective July 1, 2026. The form number and actual terms must be checked again when TREC revises them.

## T06U1 — Overview of Forms

Select a form by the legal and physical transaction, not its marketing nickname. A “townhouse” may be a condominium unit or a fee-simple lot with an attached house. A “ranch-style home” may be an ordinary suburban resale. A rural parcel may involve agricultural equipment, crops, surface leases, mineral interests, acreage adjustment, and rollback taxes. Identify those facts before deciding that the familiar residential form is sufficient.

Current Chapter 537 identifies the adopted form and intended use. Sections 537.20, .28, .30, .31, .33, and .35 govern the unimproved, resale, incomplete-new-home, completed-new-home, farm-and-ranch, and condominium families respectively. A project involving commercial development, construction closing before completion, new condominium sales, or other complexity may not fit these standard forms. Apply §537.11's exception and attorney-review rules rather than forcing the facts into an unsuitable document.

Common structure creates efficiency but also traps. Several forms share price, money delivery, title, default, and notice concepts, yet paragraph letters, exceptions, attachments, and property-specific duties differ. A checklist copied from resale can incorrectly call a condominium objection paragraph “6D” or overlook a farm acreage adjustment. Build a form-specific field map every time. Similar headings are a prompt to compare, not proof of identical obligations.

### Statute reading exercise: Verify all six form families

Open the six official pages and the corresponding Chapter 537 adoption rules. Create a matrix of form number, current effective date, intended transaction, one explicit limit, and official source. Then place these six files in the matrix: occupied single-family resale; condominium unit resale; working cattle acreage; vacant platted residential lot; unfinished builder home to close after completion; completed never-occupied builder home.

For each, identify a fact that would force reconsideration, such as condominium ownership, a commercial purpose, or closing before completion. Explain the difference between a form's date and the 2016 course-outline date. Check the matrix directly against the source before continuing.

Feedback: the course outline supplies the subjects; current adoption supplies the transaction forms. Each file has a likely indicated family, subject to the actual facts and exceptions. The unfinished-home form expressly excludes closing before construction completion and condominium transactions. A warehouse or new condominium is not automatically covered by one of these six simply because the course teaches six contracts.

### Applied exercise: Diagnose a misleading property label

Three listings say “townhome.” A conveys Unit 9 with an undivided interest in common elements under a recorded condominium declaration. B conveys Lot 9, Block 2, with the seller owning the land beneath an attached residence; a property owners association maintains the entry. C is an unfinished condominium unit sold by the developer. All will be used as residences.

Prepare a selection memo for each identifying the legal fact, likely form path, and missing documents. Explain why the common marketing word does not control and why the presence of an association does not make B a condominium. For C, identify the need for the appropriate developer/attorney instrument and statutory analysis instead of choosing a resale condominium form automatically.

Feedback: A fits condominium resale; B may fit ordinary resale with the appropriate association addendum; C requires analysis of a new condominium transaction. Read the deed/declaration and actual ownership regime. An association is not synonymous with condominium ownership. The memo should identify where counsel and title evidence are needed rather than declaring a legal regime solely from a listing photograph.

## T06U2 — Differences Between 1-4 Family Residential Contract & Other Contracts

The property definition changes with ownership. The condominium form identifies the unit, building, project, declaration, common elements, limited common elements, and assigned parking. The farm form includes agricultural/residential improvements and accessories, crops, and an attached description suitable for the rural tract. The unimproved form's intended subject has no physical buildings, additions, or fixtures. New-home forms address completed or future improvements and their construction/warranty/insulation information.

Title and property-condition mechanisms must match those definitions. Condominium document and resale-certificate delivery can trigger buyer rights separate from ordinary title commitment or option deadlines. Farm property can have surface leases and acreage-price adjustment, with cost consequences that depend on selected fields. New construction has written change orders, allowances, completion criteria, and selected finishes that an existing-home repair paragraph cannot adequately represent.

Read even familiar late pages. The current Farm and Ranch form includes brokerage fee sections with stated restrictions; do not transplant the resale contact-only assumptions to every other form. A separate fee agreement or selected contribution may make signing a particular fee section inappropriate according to its instruction. A complete review asks what each form actually says, including its warning above signatures.

### Statute reading exercise: Produce a paragraph crosswalk

Compare paragraphs 2, 6, 7, 12, and 13 of all six current forms. Build a worksheet with five rows per form: property description, title/documents, condition/construction, brokerage/expense contributions, and prorations/special taxes. For each row name one identical concept and one material difference or a confirmed absence of difference. Use pinpoint paragraph letters, not a general citation to the PDF.

Then trace the farm form's fee-section instruction and the resale contact-only page. Explain why copying a signature instruction between the two without reading is unsafe. Mark each finding as verified from the actual form, and preserve unresolved legal questions for direct counsel review.

Feedback: identify the condominium documents in paragraph 2 and its form-specific objection letters; farm crops, surface leases, acreage adjustment, and rollback allocation; incomplete-construction documents/allowances/completion; and completed-new-home warranty/insulation provisions. The crosswalk is successful only if it records actual provisions rather than filling every cell with “same as resale.” Fee/contact areas must be read on their own terms.

### Applied exercise: Replace a copied checklist

A staff member reuses a resale checklist for a condominium. It demands a seller T-47 survey package, ignores declaration/bylaws/rules and certificate, labels objections as 6D, and tells the buyer association approval is irrelevant because price is agreed. The supplied condominium declaration has a right of first refusal. No documents or association certification have yet been received.

Write a corrected checklist using 30-18 paragraphs 2B–D and 6. Identify each copied assumption that must be removed or verified, the required document-delivery blanks, the certificate-age requirement, and the right-of-refusal mechanism. Explain what happens to the effective-date analysis under the actual clause if the required certification arrives. Do not invent a waiver by the association.

Feedback: condominium-specific documents and certificate are central. Under 2D, the stated certification that refusal rights were complied with and not exercised/waived affects the effective date; failure within the negotiated period or exercise has the printed termination consequence. The current certificate must meet the form's age and content requirements. Use 30-18's title objection paragraph, not resale numbering. A price agreement does not erase the declaration's stated process.

## T06U3 — Residential Condominium Contract

Paragraph 2A identifies the unit together with its interests in common elements and appurtenant limited common elements. Assigned parking needs accurate identification. A seller's use of space P-14 does not prove ownership of it or the right to assign it. Read the declaration, plat, amendments, and any assignment. Storage, balconies, garages, and rooftop rights can have similar distinctions. Describe the interest verified by the documents; do not promise exclusive ownership when there is only a license to use.

Paragraph 2B addresses declaration, bylaws, and association rules as Documents. It records receipt or requires delivery within agreed days and supplies the stated seven-day termination right after receipt, while retaining statutory rights. Paragraph 2C separately addresses the Resale Certificate, which must be prepared no more than three months before delivery and include at least the information required by Property Code §82.157. A certificate is not the declaration; neither substitutes for the other. The form's special affidavit/waiver choice requires its stated facts and agreement; it is not a shortcut for an agent who forgot to order the certificate.

Read [Property Code §§82.156–.157](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/PR/htm/PR.82.htm#82.157) with the contract. Documents reveal governance, assessments, litigation, insurance, and use restrictions relevant to affordability and intended use. A lender's project approval is a different determination from the buyer's satisfaction with those records. Association right-of-refusal provisions under 2D may change the effective-date sequence and the contract's status. Calendar each receipt and decision independently.

### Statute reading exercise: The two condo delivery records

Read 30-18 paragraph 2 and Property Code §82.157. Seller Asha offers Unit 204, Building C, Lake Heron Condominiums, in Dallas County, with parking P-22 assigned by the declaration. Buyer Basil has not received Documents or Certificate. The parties specify ten days for Documents and twelve for Certificate. Effective date is October 1; Documents arrive October 8; Certificate arrives October 12 but was prepared June 1. No right of first refusal exists.

Complete the paragraph 2 field map and separate receipt/deadline entries. Identify the problem with the certificate date and the professional/association follow-up needed. Extract five categories of certificate information that can affect the purchase. Explain why the Documents' receipt date does not also become the certificate receipt date.

Feedback: the June 1 certificate is more than three months old on October 12 and does not meet the form's age requirement. Obtain a compliant certificate and legal advice if timing/rights are disputed. The Documents and Certificate have distinct receipt and termination provisions. Relevant information includes assessments, association finances, litigation, insurance, and other statutory required items. The parking assignment must trace to the condominium records, not verbal assurances.

### Applied exercise: Evaluate a condo special assessment

Buyer Celeste receives a current certificate disclosing $410 monthly regular dues, an approved $6,000 roof assessment payable in six $1,000 installments, and pending litigation over a common wall. The certificate arrives during the applicable contract review period. Celeste's lender has not approved the project. The listing had described dues as $350 and “no assessments.” Seller confirms the certificate is accurate. No allocation of the special assessment has been resolved beyond the actual contract wording.

Prepare a corrected cost summary separating recurring dues and special assessment. Identify the discrepancy to correct in communications, documents counsel should review, lender follow-up, and buyer decision deadline. Read the actual contract allocation before stating who pays particular installments. Draft a factual explanation that neither promises lender approval nor treats litigation as automatic cancellation.

Feedback: use verified $410 dues and the disclosed $6,000 assessment schedule; correct the inaccurate representation. Contract allocation and legal advice determine the particular payment issue, and the buyer's document rights must be tracked. Project approval is separate. The existence of litigation matters but does not automatically prove default, bad title, or a right outside the actual agreement.

### Applied exercise: Parking and limited common elements

Seller Devon uses two garage spaces but the declaration assigns only P-8 to the unit. P-9 is rented under a revocable annual arrangement with the association; no transfer approval exists. The draft says “two owned parking spaces included.” Buyer Elise requires two long-term spaces. The offer is not accepted.

Create an interest/document/transferability chart, correct the unsupported draft claim, and list association questions and buyer decisions. Identify the 30-18 field for assigned parking and distinguish it from an attorney-reviewed arrangement for the second space. Explain why the agent cannot create a permanent real-property right by typing P-9 into the parking blank.

Feedback: P-8 is documented as assigned; P-9's rental/license status and transfer conditions require investigation. Correct the misleading ownership statement before presentation. The buyer may choose different terms or seek legal advice about a satisfactory arrangement. Contract language cannot convey rights the seller does not possess. The worksheet must keep the verified and desired interests separate.

## T06U4 — Farm and Ranch Contract

The farm form's property definition includes crops and rural improvements/accessories. Unless otherwise agreed in writing, seller may harvest growing crops until delivery of possession under paragraph 2D. If parties intend a different harvest arrangement, obtain proper written treatment; do not assume a crop belongs to buyer merely because closing happened before possession under a leaseback. Equipment ownership and exclusions require careful identification, including leased tanks or machinery.

Paragraph 3D allows selected survey-based price adjustment. Record reference acreage, dollars per acre, a termination-notice period for a price change greater than ten percent, and how the adjustment affects cash/financing. Ten percent is a change in sales price under this clause, not a generic permission to ignore acreage errors. If adjustment is not selected, do not assume a new survey automatically reprices the sale. Survey acceptance, title objections, access, and boundaries remain separate matters.

Paragraph 4 includes natural-resource and surface-lease treatment. Surface-only grazing or hunting rights can affect access and buyer use even when no residence is leased. Obtain the actual instruments. Mineral, water, and timber reservations require appropriate addenda and expert/counsel advice; a fence does not establish the subsurface estate. Paragraph 13B allocates rollback-type assessments based on whether the sale/buyer's later use or seller's preclosing use/change caused them, and the obligation survives closing. The contract allocation does not decide the taxing authority's assessment or exact statutory amount.

### Statute reading exercise: Read the rural differences in context

Read 25-17 paragraphs 2, 3D, 4, 7's water disclosure, and 13B. A 50-acre cattle tract includes a barn, fences, owned stock-water tanks, growing hay, a three-year grazing lease, and a severed mineral interest shown in title documents. The parties consider adjustment at $12,000 per acre and buyer's planned conversion of part of the land to nonagricultural use. Seller has supplied no water disclosure despite a known well.

Make a rural intake matrix linking each fact to the form location, needed document, buyer decision, and professional review. Distinguish existing mineral ownership from seller's proposed new reservation. Identify the rollback allocation principle without computing taxes from absent data. Explain why a residential resale checklist would omit at least three material issues.

Feedback: address crops, equipment, acreage adjustment, surface lease, mineral/title evidence, known well/water disclosure, and use-related tax consequences. A severed interest is not a new reservation of something seller owns. Tax and title professionals must establish actual consequences. The buyer's intended use makes leases, access, and water critical, not optional background.

### Applied exercise: Calculate an acreage adjustment

The accepted farm contract states $720,000 for reference acreage 60 acres. Paragraph 3D selects adjustment at $12,000 per acre, adjustment entirely to cash under 3A, and five days for notice if price changes more than ten percent. Original cash is $180,000 and financing $540,000. Survey shows 57.5 acres. In an independent alternative, the survey shows 53 acres. No other term changes and neither party has elected termination.

Calculate acreage difference, dollar adjustment, adjusted price, percent price change, and cash/financing for both cases. Identify whether the special termination threshold is implicated and what additional receipt/date evidence is required. Explain why the five-day period must not be invented from survey preparation date when the clause uses receipt.

Feedback: 57.5 acres reduces price by $30,000 to $690,000, a 4.1667% decrease; cash becomes $150,000. At 53 acres, reduction is $84,000 to $636,000, an 11.6667% decrease; cash would be $96,000 if the transaction proceeds. The latter exceeds ten percent and requires analysis of the stated notice option from actual receipt; it is not automatic termination. Financing remains $540,000 under the selected adjustment, subject to actual lender requirements and any needed agreement changes.

### Applied exercise: Harvest and a surface lease

Farm seller Finn closes November 1 but retains possession through November 15 under a written arrangement. Hay is growing; there is no different crop agreement. A grazing tenant has a written lease through December 31 covering the north pasture. Buyer Gia plans immediate fencing work across that pasture and assumes “all leases disappear when I buy.”

Prepare a possession/harvest/lease-rights timeline, identify the documents requiring review, and list questions for seller, tenant, buyer, and counsel. Explain the paragraph 2D crop default and why closing alone does not prove vacant possession of the leased pasture. Draft a factual correction to the buyer's assumption without interpreting the grazing lease's termination or assignment clauses beyond supplied facts.

Feedback: seller's default harvest right runs to delivery of possession absent a different written agreement. The surface lease is a separate instrument and must be reviewed for rights and transfer; sale does not supply a blanket conclusion that it vanished. The buyer needs actual lease and possession information before planning access or construction. Do not draft a unilateral cancellation of the tenant's rights.

## T06U5 — Unimproved Property Contract

Form 9-18 addresses unimproved property for intended one-to-four-family residential use under its adoption rule. Its official description refers to property without physical buildings, additions, or fixtures, commonly platted land. Do not use it mechanically for industrial land, an existing farmhouse, or a parcel whose intended transaction exceeds that scope. Verify the legal description and what actually exists on the land.

Vacant does not mean ready to build. Paragraph 7A specifically advises buyer to determine utility availability suitable to the buyer's needs. Existing utilities and future service availability are different. A nearby line does not guarantee a connection, capacity, affordable extension, or service by the planned date. Ask the utility/provider and permitting authorities. Access, restrictions, floodplain, environmental conditions, setbacks, and sewage disposal may matter to intended use.

Use the form's title and survey objection choices, environmental addendum if agreed, and applicable district/coastal/utility notices. Do not invent a feasibility contingency in special provisions. If the buyer requires a custom development condition that available forms do not address, direct attorney involvement is appropriate before execution. A title policy generally is not a building permit or a promise of utility service.

### Statute reading exercise: Review a vacant-lot file

Read 9-18 paragraphs 2, 6, 7, and the attachment list. Lot 16, Block A, Cedar Cove Addition is vacant and intended for one residence. It is within a water service area, has no meter, and requires an on-site wastewater permit. A recorded drainage easement crosses the proposed building pad. Buyer Hugo has a preliminary sketch but no approved plans or specialist report.

Produce a due-diligence map identifying each source of uncertainty, the relevant contract mechanism, the professional/agency responsible for verification, and the deadline or negotiated condition needed. Explain why “utilities nearby” is inadequate. Identify appropriate disclosure and environmental-document checks and distinguish a preliminary sketch from a registered survey or approved permit.

Feedback: verify utility capacity/cost/timing, wastewater feasibility, drainage easement and use restrictions, survey, permits, and any applicable notices. The form's existing access/condition/title provisions must be read; they do not guarantee every proposed project. Obtain a suitable negotiated investigation structure and direct legal assistance for bespoke conditions. The map should name concrete records to obtain, not simply “do due diligence.”

### Applied exercise: Utility facts versus marketing claims

Lot seller Isabel advertises “water and sewer available.” The provider states water connection requires a $9,800 extension and six months, while no public sewer service is planned. Buyer Jalen wants to start building in sixty days and assumed a $1,500 meter charge. No independent sewage feasibility review has occurred. The offer is still a draft.

Create a verified cost/timing comparison and correct the unsupported advertisement/offer assumptions. Identify provider documents and permitting questions required before buyer decides. Explain which desired protections fit an existing form or addendum and which may need attorney drafting. Do not state that the lot is unbuildable or that the seller must pay all extensions without agreement.

Feedback: report the actual quoted water cost and timing, absence of public sewer, and unresolved alternative wastewater feasibility. The buyer's objective may not fit the known service schedule, but buildability requires professional/agency determinations. An agent must correct material statements and help obtain appropriate documents rather than transform expectations into an unsupported legal guarantee.

## T06U6 — New Home Contracts

Form 23-20 is for incomplete new construction to close after completion; its notice excludes condominium transactions and closings before completion. Paragraph 7B incorporates signed construction documents: plans/specifications, finish schedules, change orders, and allowances. A model-home tour is not an adequate substitute. Paragraph 7C requires written change orders and addresses cost increases, decreases, and unused allowances. Price and financing must stay consistent with agreed changes and lender requirements.

The incomplete form also addresses buyer selections, start and substantial-completion timing, specified delays, substitution of comparable materials, express warranties and assignable manufacturer warranties, and insulation information. Substantial completion refers to final inspection/approval by applicable governmental authorities and lender under the form. It is not merely the builder saying work looks nearly finished. Read the closing interaction; the stated delays do not create unlimited extensions beyond closing.

Form 24-20 is for completed new construction not previously occupied. It includes acceptance/repair choices, warranty and insulation information, and work-completion duties. New does not mean no inspection is needed or that every defect is covered by an unlimited warranty. Identify the actual written warranty and what law supplies; an agent should not invent an express warranty. Owner-required builder forms may fall within a §537.11 exception, but complex provisions still call for direct counsel review.

### Statute reading exercise: Compare new-home construction duties

Read 23-20 paragraph 7B–G and 24-20 paragraph 7A–F. In File A a builder's home lacks flooring and final approvals, and the buyer will choose counters from a $7,000 allowance. In File B a never-occupied completed home has final approvals and a damaged interior door to be repaired before closing. Both have written manufacturer warranties and insulation specifications.

Prepare parallel field/document maps identifying the correct form, incorporated documents, cost-adjustment treatment, completion evidence, warranty transfer, and insulation record. Identify one condition that would make the incomplete form unsuitable. Explain why a buyer selection above allowance must be documented and how an unused allowance differs from a verbal gift by the builder.

Feedback: File A needs 23-20 with signed construction documents and written change orders; File B fits 24-20 with the specific repair and relevant warranty/insulation provisions. Closing before construction completion is outside 23-20's stated use. The cost adjustment follows the actual clause, with price and financing reconciled rather than an undocumented side payment. Warranty and final approval are different evidence.

### Applied exercise: Change orders and allowances

Incomplete-home contract price is $540,000, cash $108,000, financing $432,000. Signed construction documents include a $7,000 counter allowance. Buyer selects $8,600 counters and the parties' written instructions require the $1,600 excess paid separately by buyer as allowed in the cost-adjustment field. A separate written change order removes $3,000 of work; lender instructs the resulting price reduction be allocated 20% to cash and 80% to financing. No other changes exist.

Calculate adjusted price, cash, financing, and separate upgrade obligation. Produce a change-order ledger linked to signed documents and lender instruction. Identify what must be checked so that the separate payment is disclosed properly and not counted twice in price. Explain why an agent must not treat a texted upgrade request as the complete signed construction-document change.

Feedback: price becomes $537,000; cash $107,400 and financing $429,600; the separately agreed upgrade excess is $1,600 under the supplied payment treatment. Document and disclose it properly to lender/closing professionals. The price-reduction and upgrade-payment branches are distinct; avoid double counting. Written change orders and the parties' agreement govern rather than the agent's estimate of what the builder probably intended.

## T06U7 — Case Study/Workshop

The form-selection workshop starts before a contract is completed. Prepare an applicability memorandum with four distinct findings: the legal estate being sold, the property's present use and physical status, what the parties intend to transfer, and when closing and possession will occur. A memorandum that says only “residential property” has not answered enough. A condominium has governing-document and common-element issues even when its building looks like a house. A vacant tract has service and access questions even when a future house is planned. A new home may need construction obligations that cannot be reconstructed by borrowing a resale repair clause.

For each file, open the current applicable form and build its own issue index. Do not replace names and dollar amounts in an old completed form and assume the result is appropriate. Carry verified facts across documents, but carry paragraph references and deadlines only after checking the new form. If the form's scope warning excludes the intended closing arrangement, stop that selection and obtain an appropriate authorized contract; crossing out the warning does not cure the mismatch.

The review artifact should distinguish an incorrect selection from an incomplete field and an unresolved outside decision. A wrong ownership regime calls for correcting the contract approach. An unfilled negotiated cost allocation calls for principal instructions. A lender's unresolved survey acceptance calls for lender/title follow-up. A disputed title or mineral-right conclusion calls for legal analysis. These are different defects and should not all be marked “ask seller.” The following files require you to identify the defect, choose the proper source, state the correction or missing information, and preserve a record that a second reviewer can reproduce.

### Applied exercise: Build a condominium offer package

Seller Karina sells Unit 11, Building D, River Finch Condominiums, Austin, Travis County, with parking P-11 and appurtenant storage S-11 verified in the declaration. Buyer Landon offers $330,000, $66,000 savings and $264,000 conventional financing. Documents are received, Certificate is not received and is due ten days after acceptance. There is no right of first refusal. Earnest money is $3,500, option fee $250, option seven days, closing November 30, possession at closing/funding, seller ordinary-expense contribution $2,000, and no brokerage contribution. Buyer chooses inspections; no specific seller repairs are yet agreed.

Produce a form-specific field map and attachment list, identify missing loan parameters and title/company/notice information, and prepare the certificate review tracker. Explain why the ordinary association addendum for non-condominium subdivisions is not a substitute for condominium paragraph 2. Mark unresolved blanks rather than inserting defaults.

Feedback: use 30-18 and its separate Documents/Certificate choices with correct unit, parking, and storage interests. The $330,000 equation balances, but complete financing and title/notice information remains necessary. Condominium documents and the statutory certificate framework cannot be replaced with a generic association addendum. The worksheet must be internally consistent without pretending all intake facts are present.

### Applied exercise: Build a rural transaction risk memorandum

Seller Mina offers 42 acres described by a verified attached metes-and-bounds survey. Price is $840,000, no acreage-price adjustment selected. Buyer Nolan proposes $336,000 cash and $504,000 financing. There is a hunting surface lease through February 28, a known stock well, an existing oil lease delivered to buyer, and growing hay. Seller wants to retain half of seller's currently owned mineral interest; the extent of current ownership is unresolved. Buyer plans a residence and grazing use.

Prepare a 25-17 package map identifying existing facts, proposed reservation, lease/document delivery, water disclosure, crop default, and tax questions. Explain what must be verified before a reservation can be described and why no acreage-price adjustment should be assumed. Draft direct questions for title/counsel concerning mineral ownership and surface-use consequences without writing a reservation clause yourself.

Feedback: keep the existing oil lease and proposed reservation distinct. Determine seller's actual owned interest, use the appropriate mineral addendum if suitable, and obtain counsel's advice on unusual matters. Address hunting rights, well disclosure, crop harvest through possession, and tax-use implications. The selected no-adjustment choice means a later survey alone does not activate 3D's price formula.

### Applied exercise: Reject the unsuitable shortcut

A developer asks the agent to use 23-20 for a buyer who will close next month on the land and unfinished shell, then separately hire the developer to finish construction over six months. The property is residential, but construction will not be complete at closing. Buyer also wants an enforceable fixed-price guarantee, delay damages, and an escrow holdback drafted by the agent. No attorney has prepared the arrangement.

Write a suitability review quoting only the relevant short form-use limit, identify each separate transaction component, and prepare a direct attorney/financial/closing referral list. Explain why familiar residential price fields do not cure the form's excluded use and why special provisions cannot be used to invent all requested construction remedies. Propose the administrative next step: gather documents and instructions for appropriate professional preparation.

Feedback: 23-20 expressly is not for closings before completion. Land acquisition, construction obligations, financing, security, holdbacks, and remedies need a suitable instrument and direct professional review. The agent should not force the deal into the wrong form or compose penalty clauses. A correct review remains helpful by organizing concrete facts and documents for the professionals who can structure it.

### Module recap

Review your six-form matrix and all field maps. For each form, state the ownership/use facts that select it, a distinctive provision, an important limitation, and the required evidence for its special conditions. Recalculate the farm and new-home worksheets and confirm their chosen cash/financing allocations. You are prepared for the topic quiz when you can recognize when to use a specialized form and when the proposed transaction needs a different professionally prepared instrument.
