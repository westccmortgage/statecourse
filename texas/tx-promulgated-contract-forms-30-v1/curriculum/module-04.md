# Texas Promulgated Contract Forms — Module 04

Covenants, Commitments and Notices. Work with [resale 20-19](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/one-four-family-residential-contract-resale), paragraphs 5–7, and the linked official forms in each unit. Build a document-delivery ledger as well as a calendar. A deadline cannot be verified merely because a sender says the document was prepared; identify receipt, completeness, and the actual contractual trigger.

## T04U1 — Earnest Money Requirement

Paragraph 5A requires the specified earnest money and option fee to be delivered to the named escrow agent within three days after effective date. They are payable to escrow and may be combined. Additional earnest money has its own amount and days blank. The express extension applies if the last delivery day for those payments falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or qualifying legal holiday. Record payment type, amount, delivery evidence, recipient, and any returned or insufficient payment; a promise to initiate a wire is not the same as verified delivery.

The form applies received amounts first to option fee, then earnest money, then additional earnest money. Partial delivery can therefore leave the earnest money short even if the buyer intended the payment mainly as a deposit. Under paragraph 5C, failure to deliver earnest money in time gives seller the stated termination/remedy rights by notice before buyer delivers it. The contract does not say late earnest money automatically means there never was a contract.

Earnest money is credited through closing or disposed of under the applicable termination and escrow provisions. It is not automatically the broker's money or automatically seller's money after a disagreement. Do not promise release without the actual contract and escrow process. Accurate records protect everyone when deposit receipt, timeliness, or entitlement is disputed.

### Applied exercise: Partial payment allocation

Effective date is Monday, October 5, 2026; no relevant holiday affects the three-day period. The contract calls for $4,000 earnest money and $350 option fee. Buyer delivers one $4,000 payment to the named escrow agent on October 7 and mistakenly writes “earnest money only” in the memo. The option period is eight days. The remaining $350 arrives October 9. No seller notice has yet been sent, and assume the payments clear.

Prepare a ledger showing contractual allocation, deficiency, delivery deadline, and later cure event. Identify the difference between the buyer's memo and paragraph 5A(3), and locate the seller's notice-before-delivery language. Write a same-day factual alert at the end of October 8 that states what is missing without declaring automatic termination. Separately record the option-expiration date and local-time requirement.

Feedback: first allocate $350 to option fee and $3,650 to earnest money; $350 earnest money remains missing at the October 8 delivery deadline. October 9 is later delivery, so the seller's paragraph 5C rights and timing must be evaluated on actual notice facts. The option period ends October 13 at 5:00 p.m. local time at the property on the supplied count. The money shortage is not an automatic erasure of formation or an instruction for the broker to decide a disputed remedy.

## T04U2 — Option Fee Requirement

The option is an unrestricted buyer termination right for the agreed period when its requirements are met. Paragraph 5B states notice must be given by 5:00 p.m. local time where the property is located. Paragraph 5D removes the unrestricted right if no option-fee amount is stated or the fee is not timely delivered. That result is different from automatic termination of the purchase agreement. The buyer may still have other specific rights if their separate conditions are met.

Option fee is credited to the sales price at closing but is not refunded on a timely option termination under paragraph 5B. Escrow may release it to seller as authorized by paragraph 5A. Do not send it directly to seller merely because that was an older practice remembered from a prior form. The current document governs the current offer. Record the option-payment and option-notice deadlines separately.

The agent should help the buyer order investigations early enough to make an informed choice. Inspection reports received after the option deadline do not reopen that option automatically. A repair request does not suspend it; a seller's pending answer does not extend it. If the buyer wants an extension, use a properly agreed amendment and keep the original deadline active until the change is effective.

### Applied exercise: A late fee and an unrelated right

The parties accept on October 1. Option fee is $250, option period seven days, and all money is due under paragraph 5. Buyer timely pays earnest money but sends the option fee on October 6, after the applicable deadline; assume no extension applies. On October 7 the inspection reveals a failed sewer line. The contract is subject to a separate financing addendum, but the lender has made no property determination. Seller has not agreed to repairs or an option extension.

Write a rights inventory distinguishing the lost unrestricted option, the inspection/access right, potential negotiated amendment, and any financing issue still requiring facts. Draft an urgent factual explanation to buyer and a broker/counsel referral. Explain why the inspection finding alone does not establish a financing termination. Do not label the whole contract terminated or guarantee that another contingency will rescue the buyer.

Feedback: late option-fee delivery means no unrestricted paragraph 5 termination right on these stipulated facts. Inspection and negotiation remain distinct. Financing requires its own lender determination, evidence, timing, and applicable provisions; a failed sewer line in a general inspection is not the same as lender property disapproval. The agent communicates the status accurately and obtains appropriate advice before any asserted termination.

## T04U3 — Title Commitment, Title Policy & Endorsements

A commitment is the title insurer's undertaking to issue a policy subject to stated requirements and exceptions. It is not the issued policy and is not a broker's certificate of clear title. Review the parties, estate/interest, description, exceptions, and requirements with the buyer and refer legal effect to counsel. A title policy insures against covered loss under its terms; exclusions and exceptions matter. A lender policy protects the lender's interest and does not replace an owner's policy for the buyer.

Paragraph 6A identifies who pays for the owner policy and the title company, and lists permitted exceptions. The survey/area-and-boundary exception choice may change coverage and cost; the buyer must understand the choice and obtain title advice. Do not describe an endorsement or amended exception as unlimited boundary insurance. Paragraph 6B measures delivery from the title company's receipt of the contract, with the stated extension up to fifteen days or three days before closing, whichever is earlier. The buyer must receive both the commitment and legible exception documents as required.

Paragraph 6D has its own objection mechanism. Its initial objection deadline is the earlier of closing or the specified days after buyer receives the commitment, exception documents, and survey. Missing one of the required components may matter. Schedule C requirements are not waived by the buyer's failure to object. Timely objections trigger the stated cure period and potential closing extension, followed by a separate buyer termination/waiver choice if objections remain uncured. New matters in revised documents can create a new objection period for those new matters. These are several clocks, not one.

### Statute reading exercise: Build the title-document clock

Read 20-19 paragraphs 6A–D. Escrow/title receives a contract October 2; closing is November 10. Buyer receives the commitment October 15, exception documents October 17, and the required survey October 20. The objection blank is five days. A recorded driveway easement not among the specified exceptions appears in the supplied materials. Buyer gives an appropriate written objection October 23. Assume seller receives it that day and the title issue is within the objection provision.

Map initial delivery, initial objection, cure, and after-cure election as separate rows, each with trigger, calculation, actor, and evidence. Identify the earliest initial objection deadline on these facts. Read the cure and closing-extension interaction rather than importing the money-delivery weekend rule. State what counsel should review about the easement and why the agent should not call it invalid.

Feedback: receipt of the final required component on October 20 makes the five-day date October 25, earlier than November 10. The October 23 objection is timely on the supplied facts. The fifteen-day cure period runs from seller receipt under 6D; the form then supplies the buyer's five-day election after an uncured period and any necessary closing extension. The agent identifies the issue and exact clause; counsel evaluates legal effect and the buyer's proposed use. Schedule C remains a separate nonwaiver point.

### Applied exercise: Commitment versus assurance

Seller asks agent Noah to tell buyer the title is “guaranteed perfect.” The commitment names a prior owner, lists a recorded access restriction, requires release of a $190,000 seller lien in Schedule C, and excludes matters not covered by its terms. The owner-policy box names seller as payer. Buyer thinks the seller-paid policy also pays for every future fence dispute.

Prepare a correction request for the named-insured/owner information, a requirements checklist for the lien release, and a referral question concerning the access restriction. Draft a plain-language explanation distinguishing commitment, owner policy, lender policy, exceptions, and coverage decisions. Identify the relevant paragraph 6A coverage choice to discuss with the title professional without promising the result.

Feedback: the commitment is conditional evidence of intended coverage, not a universal title warranty. Correct identity information, satisfy closing requirements, and obtain review of the access restriction. Who pays does not enlarge the insurance. The buyer's fence-dispute assumption must be checked against the actual policy and survey-related choice. The broker should not opine on title validity or promise insurance for an uncovered matter.

## T04U4 — Survey

Choose one survey route in paragraph 6C. An existing-survey route requires both the survey and the TDI residential affidavit or declaration within the stated time. If seller fails to deliver both, buyer obtains a new survey at seller expense under the provision. If seller timely delivers the package but title or lender rejects it, the separate selected payer controls. The factual difference between nondelivery and rejection changes the cost result.

The buyer-new-survey route and seller-new-survey route have different duties. Under buyer's route, the form includes deemed receipt and the stated limit on financing termination if buyer fails to obtain the survey. A survey must be by a registered professional land surveyor acceptable to title and lender. An old sketch, tax map, or online boundary overlay is not automatically an acceptable survey. Alterations since the survey can be critical.

### Applied exercise: Who pays for the replacement survey?

In File A, paragraph 6C(1) requires the existing survey and affidavit/declaration in eight days. Seller delivers the survey on day five but no affidavit or declaration by day eight. The rejection-payer box is buyer. In File B, seller delivers both valid documents on day five, but the lender rejects the old survey because a new garage was added; the rejection-payer box is also buyer. A new survey costs $780 in either file.

Write a separate cost determination for each using the actual paragraph, state the delivery deadline for the replacement, and identify the evidence distinguishing the branches. Explain why the selected rejection payer does not decide File A. Draft a neutral request for a current survey and required supporting documents, including who orders it and who the contract makes responsible for its cost.

Feedback: File A is missing required seller delivery, so the form places the new survey expense on seller. File B is rejection after delivery, so the selected buyer allocation applies. Under 6C(1) the new survey is to be obtained no later than three days before closing. Preserve dated receipt of each document and the lender/title rejection. Do not invoice the wrong party by using a single “survey rejected” label for both files.

## T04U5 — Affidavit & Notice to Prospective Buyer

The TDI [T-47 affidavit](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/title/documents/formT-47.pdf) and [T-47.1 declaration](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/title/documents/form-t-47-1.pdf) are different authorized ways of supplying the required existing-survey statements. The declaration is an unsworn declaration under the applicable law rather than a notarized affidavit. Read its certifications and identify the survey and property correctly. Neither form creates a new survey or guarantees a title company will accept an old one. The person with knowledge must answer accurately, including changes affecting the survey.

The current [Notice to Prospective Buyer, 58-0](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/notice-prospective-buyer-0), replaces the older OP-C designation. It addresses the written advice to obtain an attorney's abstract examination or a title policy and alerts the buyer to specified district notices. It is approved for use where a sale contract has not been promulgated by TREC and says it should be presented before the buyer signs an offer. On the resale form, paragraph 6E(1) supplies the title-advice language. Acknowledging general notice does not replace the seller's property-specific statutory utility or improvement-district notice.

### Statute reading exercise: Four documents, four jobs

Read T-47, T-47.1, Form 58-0, and resale paragraphs 6C and 6E. Seller Iris has a 2019 survey and knows a pool was installed in 2022. Buyer Leo receives an owner-prepared contract without the TREC title-advice text. The property is in a utility district and public improvement district. No district-specific notices have been provided.

Create a purpose/signer/trigger/limitation chart for the two survey statements, title-advice notice, and statutory district notices. Identify the pool information that must not be omitted from the survey statement. Explain what the broker must do before the offer under the notice's instruction and what remains for the seller concerning districts. Do not certify that the old survey is acceptable.

Feedback: the affidavit/declaration addresses knowledge concerning the existing survey, while the buyer notice addresses advice and alerts. Pool construction needs accurate disclosure; title/lender decides survey acceptance. General 58-0 delivery does not supply the particular district's tax, indebtedness, assessment, or statutory acknowledgment information. The owner-prepared-contract exception does not erase the broker's statutory written title-advice duty.

## T04U6 — Property Condition: Inspection, Acceptance, Repairs

Paragraph 7A provides access at reasonable times for buyer and authorized inspectors, while seller maintains existing utilities. Hydrostatic testing requires separate written seller authorization; an inspection appointment is not that authorization. The buyer selects qualified inspectors within applicable licensing rules. An inspection is evidence about condition, not a title opinion, lender approval, or guarantee of future performance.

“As Is” in paragraph 7D describes acceptance of present condition with the stated exceptions. It does not prevent inspection, later repair negotiation, or timely option termination where that right exists. If selecting acceptance conditioned on specific repairs, identify actual repairs and treatments. The form warns against general phrases such as “subject to inspections.” A broad phrase does not supply an inspection contingency or explain what must be fixed.

Distinguish agreed repairs, lender-required repairs, and optional buyer upgrades. Paragraph 7E generally does not obligate either party to pay for lender-required repairs absent agreement; it states consequences if they do not agree and supplies the buyer's separate right where cost exceeds five percent of the price. Paragraph 7F regulates agreed repairs, including completion before closing, qualified repair providers, permits, documentation, and transfer of transferable warranties. An agent should not certify building compliance or draft a new remedy.

### Applied exercise: Authorize the investigation correctly

Buyer Mae's inspector proposes a general inspection, sewer camera observation, and a hydrostatic plumbing test on a 1992 house. The seller has permitted ordinary inspection access but has not signed separate hydrostatic authorization. Existing water and electricity are off. The buyer's option ends in five days. The inspector cannot complete a meaningful inspection without utilities.

Prepare an inspection action plan with permission status, responsible actor, utility requirement, scheduling deadline, and evidence for each task. Identify the official [hydrostatic testing addendum](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/addendum-authorizing-hydrostatic-testing-0) and the separate approval needed. Write a status note explaining why an inspection appointment does not authorize every testing method and why the option clock must still be tracked.

Feedback: obtain seller's separate written hydrostatic permission before that test; verify the scope of all requested methods with the qualified inspector. Seller's existing-utilities duty is in 7A. Schedule promptly, communicate limitations, and let buyer decide under existing rights before expiry. Do not conduct unauthorized testing, imply inspection extends the option, or give engineering assurances from the agent's own observation.

### Applied exercise: Turn a report into specific repair instructions

An inspection identifies a leaking main bathroom angle-stop valve, an inoperative garage-door safety reversal, a loose handrail at the rear steps, and cosmetic paint scuffs. The buyer requests repairs to the first three and accepts the paint. Seller agrees to have appropriately qualified trades complete them before closing and provide the paragraph 7F documentation. The parties do not agree to any new warranty or open-ended satisfaction contingency.

Prepare a factual repair list identifying location, condition, and agreed corrective work suitable for the form's repair fields; do not write a new legal standard or remedy. Create a completion-evidence checklist and walkthrough plan. Compare the list with “fix all inspection items” and explain three ambiguities that phrase would create. Identify when attorney drafting would be necessary if the parties request a bespoke warranty.

Feedback: specific agreed work should identify the three distinct conditions and locations while excluding the unagreed paint upgrade. The inspector/trade provides technical scope if needed. Completion includes permits where required, appropriate qualification, scope/payment documentation, and transferable warranties under the form. “All inspection items” can incorporate observations, maintenance suggestions, and matters not actually negotiated. A new warranty or remedy exceeds informational completion.

## T04U7 — Seller's Disclosure & Lead Based Paint Disclosure

Texas Property Code §5.008 generally requires the seller's notice for covered previously occupied single-dwelling residential property, with specified exemptions. Determine the applicable exemption from actual facts rather than assuming every estate, investor, or never-occupied-by-this-seller transaction is exempt. A seller's absence of personal occupancy is not permission to fabricate “unknown” answers or omit known facts. The notice reports seller knowledge; it is not a warranty or a substitute for inspection. The agent's independent duty concerning known significant defects is not erased by a seller exemption.

Use current [Seller's Disclosure Notice 55-1](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/sellers-disclosure-notice). Paragraph 7B of resale separately records whether buyer received the notice, has not received it with an agreed delivery period, or seller is not required to provide it. Its nondelivery and postreceipt termination provisions must be read exactly. A late notice can create a different window from an expired option. Record actual buyer receipt and do not backdate a signature to conceal late delivery.

For most pre-1978 target housing, federal lead-disclosure requirements call for known information and available reports, the approved pamphlet, required acknowledgments, and the buyer's inspection/risk-assessment opportunity, subject to applicable exclusions and lawful waiver/modification rules. The [EPA explanation](https://www.epa.gov/lead/real-estate-disclosures-about-potential-lead-hazards) is the primary agency guide. Lack of known lead is not proof that no disclosure process is needed. The current TREC [56-0](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/addendum-sellers-disclosure-information-lead-based-paint-and-lead-based-paint-hazards-0) separately provides a waiver choice or inspection within ten days and termination notice within fourteen days if the specified hazard is present. Do not confuse that contractual fourteen-day point with the ten-day investigation opportunity or an option period.

### Statute reading exercise: Three disclosure timelines

Read [Property Code §5.008](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/PR/htm/PR.5.htm#5.008), resale 7B, and 56-0 B–F. A covered 1968 house is contracted September 8; closing is October 1. Seller's notice was not received at acceptance, is promised in five days, and actually reaches buyer September 14. The option expires September 15. Before the buyer becomes obligated, the federal lead information and pamphlet are delivered and the parties select 56-0's inspection option. No statutory exemption or lead waiver applies.

Build separate calendars for seller-notice delivery/postreceipt rights, option, lead investigation, and lead termination. Identify the required lead records and signatures and the fact that would support the selected lead termination. Explain why “option expired” does not answer every disclosure question. Record the actual receipt date, not merely the seller's form-signing date.

Feedback: the promised seller-notice delivery and actual receipt differ. Resale 7B(2) supplies a seven-day postreceipt window or prior closing, whichever occurs first, while the option has its own date. Under the selected 56-0 wording, the ten-day inspection point is September 18 and the fourteen-day notice point September 22, subject to the stated presence condition. The lead process includes known information, available reports, pamphlet, acknowledgments, and certifications; receipt does not itself certify that a hazard exists.

### Applied exercise: Complete the 2026 water-disclosure decision

Resale 20-19 paragraph 7I is new to many older course materials. Seller Nolan says the house receives municipal water and selects “not required.” His documents show an unused well behind the garage and a recorded groundwater lease. Buyer has received no Seller's Water Disclosure, Form 61-0. Closing is thirty days away and the draft has not been accepted.

Read 7I's five cumulative conditions for the “not required” choice. Create a condition-by-condition worksheet, identify the two supplied facts contradicting that choice, and map the correct received/not-received decision based on actual delivery. Draft a factual request for the seller-completed current disclosure and a delivery tracker. Explain why municipal service alone is insufficient and why the broker should not opine on the buyer's ability to pump groundwater.

Feedback: all five conditions must be true for 7I(3), including no known well and no known severance/sale/lease of groundwater rights. Municipal service does not cure the well or lease facts. If the disclosure has not been received, use the actual delivery status and agreed period rather than claiming receipt. The form supplies specified nondelivery/postreceipt rights; legal water rights and permitted use require appropriate expert and attorney review.

## T04U8 — Environmental Assessment & Threatened or Endangered Species & Wetlands Addendum

Form [28-2](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/environmental-assessment-threatened-endangered-species-and-wetlands-addendum) permits selected buyer-paid investigations by the specified specialists: environmental assessment, threatened/endangered species or habitat, and wetlands. Its negotiated days run after effective date. Termination requires both a copy of a selected report that adversely affects property use and notice within that period; an unsupported fear or social-media photograph is not the stated report.

An ordinary property inspection may not investigate soil contamination, wetlands jurisdiction, or habitat. Identify the intended use and specialist needed early. An agent should not determine that a wet area is legally a wetland or promise that habitat protections do not apply. The addendum creates contractual investigation and termination mechanics; it does not issue a permit or remove environmental law.

### Applied exercise: Use the right report in time

Buyer Opal intends to build an additional residence on a wooded two-acre property. The accepted 28-2 selects wetlands and threatened/endangered-species reports with 24 days after effective date. On day 19 a qualified natural-resources professional issues a selected report finding habitat restrictions that adversely affect the intended building area. A landscaper separately texts “looks dry to me.” Buyer instructs termination on day 21. No extension or waiver exists.

Prepare a report-and-notice checklist, identify the professional and selected investigation, map the 24-day deadline, and draft a factual explanation of the report's role. Explain why the landscaper's text neither replaces nor negates the specialist report. Identify what counsel should review if seller disputes adverse effect. Do not guarantee permitting outcomes or omit the report from the notice package.

Feedback: the stated contractual path calls for the selected adverse-use report plus timely notice, with earnest-money refund under the addendum. The informal text does not answer the professional or legal questions. Preserve the intended-use record, report, buyer instruction, and delivery evidence; refer disputed entitlement to counsel. Completing the worksheet requires checking both the type of investigation selected and the actual deadline.

## T04U9 — Residential Service Contract

Paragraph 7H permits the buyer to purchase a residential service contract from an appropriately licensed provider/administrator and provides a negotiated seller reimbursement cap. Purchase is optional. A service contract has coverage, exclusions, limitations, fees, and claims procedures. It is not equivalent to property insurance, an inspection, a seller's promised repair, or a guarantee that any failed system will be replaced. Review actual coverage before recommending a product based solely on price.

The seller reimbursement is a cap on the described cost, not automatically a cash credit for an unpurchased plan. A broker's relationship or compensation connected with the service company needs the applicable disclosure and legal analysis; use the TREC [Disclosure of Relationship with Residential Service Company, RSC-4](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/disclosure-relationship-residential-service-company) where applicable and follow the governing rule. Do not represent optional purchase as a lender or TREC requirement without a genuine source.

### Applied exercise: Compare cost and coverage honestly

The contract states a $650 maximum seller reimbursement under 7H. Buyer chooses a $720 plan with a $100 service-call fee, a $1,500 HVAC annual limit, and an exclusion for known preexisting failures. The inspection already reports an inoperative compressor. Seller separately promised to repair that compressor before closing. Agent receives a disclosed marketing relationship payment from the company, but the applicable relationship disclosure has not yet been furnished.

Prepare a cost worksheet, coverage-question list, and separate repair-completion checklist. Calculate the purchase-price portion above the stated reimbursement cap. Draft a buyer explanation distinguishing the plan from the seller's repair obligation and identify the disclosure task to review with the broker. Do not promise the plan will replace the compressor or treat the $100 fee as part of the original plan price without checking terms.

Feedback: the plan price exceeds the cap by $70; service-call and coverage limits are additional terms to understand. Known preexisting failure raises the exclusion; it does not excuse the seller's agreed repair. Handle the relationship disclosure and applicable compensation rules before an uninformed recommendation. The worksheet should show separate contract obligations, product terms, and disclosure duties rather than one combined “warranty protection” label.

### Module recap

Audit your ledger for five distinctions: earnest money versus option fee; money delivery versus option notice; commitment versus policy; existing-survey nondelivery versus rejection; seller's notice versus lead and water disclosures. Review each independent worksheet and source exercise, correct deadlines against the exact paragraphs, and identify any question needing title, trade, environmental, insurance, or legal expertise. You should be able to explain both the required evidence and what that evidence does not establish.
