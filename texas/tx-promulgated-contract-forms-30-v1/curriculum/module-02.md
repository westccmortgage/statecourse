# Texas Promulgated Contract Forms — Module 02

Laws, Rules and Regulations. Required work in this module is an audit of authority: the authority to act as broker, select a form, fill a field, communicate an offer, or make a decision. Keep the current [Texas Occupations Code Chapter 1101](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/OC/htm/OC.1101.htm) and [TREC rules](https://www.trec.texas.gov/agency-information/rules-and-laws/trec-rules) open. Record the subsection for each conclusion. The statutes and rules supply professional limits; the parties supply transaction choices within those limits.

## T02U1 — Texas Real Estate License Act

Chapter 1101 is the Real Estate License Act. It defines brokerage activity, establishes licensing and supervisory requirements, authorizes the Commission, regulates conduct, and supplies enforcement mechanisms. Form preparation is one part of licensed brokerage. A license does not make the holder an attorney, title examiner, lender, engineer, tax adviser, or principal in the client's transaction. A signature line for broker contact information is not authority to sign the seller's conveyance promise.

Section 1101.155 addresses the Commission's power concerning contract forms. Section 1101.654 addresses unauthorized practice of law and recognizes specified form-completion activity incidental to brokerage. Read that statute together with current 22 TAC §537.11, which gives the detailed mandatory-use and practice-of-law rules. Selecting a familiar form is insufficient if a different form is required for the transaction. Completing a proper form is insufficient if an agent inserts impermissible legal drafting.

Section 1101.652 identifies conduct that can support discipline. Contract work creates recurring risks: negligence or incompetence; material misrepresentation or failure to disclose a known significant defect; failure to make representation clear; handling another person's money improperly; using unauthorized sale terms; and failing to provide a signed document to its signer on request. An honest agent can still work incompetently by using an obsolete form, overlooking a contingency, or losing a time-sensitive notice. A good system anticipates these errors instead of relying on memory.

Representation must be established and disclosed independently of who pays. A broker may represent the seller, the buyer, or act as an intermediary under statutory requirements. The intermediary arrangement requires the prescribed written consent, including who will pay the broker, and restrictions on disclosure and advice. The contract's broker contact information records the capacity; it does not create the underlying consent. The Information About Brokerage Services notice explains relationships and duties; it is not itself a representation agreement.

The 2026 buyer requirements are part of current contract intake. Under §1101.563 a license holder performing brokerage for a prospective buyer of residential real property must have the required written agreement before showing residential property or, if no showing will occur, before presenting an offer on the buyer's behalf, subject to the statutory owner-representative exception. The agreement must include its services, termination date, exclusivity status, representation or showing-only status as applicable, compensation amount or rate and how determined, and a conspicuous statement that compensation is not set by law and is fully negotiable.

A showing-only agreement under §§1101.562–.563 is a narrow arrangement. It is nonexclusive, lasts no more than fourteen days, and cannot include advice or other brokerage acts for the prospect. Providing additional services requires a separate agreement. Calling a contract “showing-only” while negotiating price for the buyer does not preserve that limited status. Do not transfer the residential-buyer requirement indiscriminately to every tenant or commercial transaction; identify the scope of each subsection.

Money and documents require separate controls. Funds payable to the contractual escrow agent should be delivered as the contract directs. If a broker receives trust funds, applicable trust-account rules, written authorization, accounting, and proper disposition matter. Keeping a check in a desk to help a client postpone a deadline is not faithful performance. Give principals copies of what they sign and preserve the accepted version with its attachments and communications. The file should make the sequence understandable to someone who was not present.

### Statute reading exercise: Locate the disciplinary risk

Read §1101.652(a-1)(3), (b)(1), (3), (4), (7), (9), (10), (20), (28), and (29). In one hypothetical file, broker Vera knows a structural pier has failed but calls the issue cosmetic. She changes seller's minimum accepted price from $390,000 to $380,000 without instructions, asks the buyer to write the deposit to her personal account, and refuses to send buyer a copy of the signed offer.

Build a four-row conduct/subsection/evidence/correction chart. Separate misrepresentation from failure to disclose, unauthorized price instructions from a negotiation recommendation, and trust-money accounting from money ownership. Identify the statutory written title-advice duty and the printed contract location that addresses it. Do not decide a penalty; identify the conduct and preserve facts.

Feedback: known significant structural information implicates the disclosure and misrepresentation provisions; unauthorized terms implicate (b)(20); personal handling of trust funds raises the separate accounting/commingling restrictions; refusing requested signed documents implicates (b)(28). The written title advice is in (b)(29), reflected in resale paragraph 6E(1). Actual discipline depends on established facts and process. The exercise requires a specific subsection, not merely “TRELA violation.”

### Statute reading exercise: Read the 2026 buyer-agreement boundary

Read §§1101.562 and 1101.563 in full. Prepare a decision tree for these three prospective buyers: Farah wants a private residential showing with no representation and no advice; Micah wants the agent to submit an offer on a residential property he has already toured alone; Ruth asks the seller's listing agent for access to the listed house and receives disclosure that the agent represents the seller. Assume no hidden prior agreements or additional acts.

For each branch identify the statutory agreement/disclosure requirement, when it occurs, and the limit on services. For Farah list the showing-only duration and exclusivity limits and explain what must occur before the agent recommends offer terms. For Micah identify the before-offer trigger. For Ruth identify the owner-representative exception without turning it into buyer representation. Compare your tree to every condition in both sections.

Feedback: Farah's showing-only path requires satisfying the four no-representation/no-advice conditions, the appropriate advance disclosure or notice, and the residential written agreement with its fourteen-day and nonexclusive limits. Additional brokerage calls for a separate agreement. Micah triggers the required agreement before presenting the offer. Ruth's listing agent must clearly disclose the seller representation; the exception does not make Ruth a client or permit misleading her about representation. A compliant tree must distinguish showing-only from representing a buyer and from an owner representative showing the owner's property.

### Applied exercise: Repair the intake file

Residential buyer Daniel has not toured 4410 Lantern Bend. Associate Tessa plans to show it at 3:00 p.m. Her only document is an IABS notice sent yesterday. Her notes promise price advice, inspection strategy, and negotiation. The proposed agreement has a 30-day showing-only term, says “exclusive,” and compensation “whatever seller offers.” Tessa's broker represents neither owner nor buyer yet.

Prepare a missing-information list and a corrected service-path recommendation for Daniel to consider before the showing. Identify five reasons the proposed document does not fit the stated services. State what must be definite concerning compensation without inventing a rate. Draft a file note distinguishing delivery of IABS from obtaining the appropriate agreement. Do not backdate any agreement.

Feedback: the described services exceed showing-only. A showing-only agreement cannot be exclusive or exceed fourteen days; unspecified compensation is not a substitute for the amount/rate and determination required by §1101.563. The representation status must match the services. The parties must negotiate a compliant agreement, including definite termination and negotiability language, before the applicable trigger. IABS delivery alone supplies neither service authority nor the required agreement.

### Applied exercise: Capacity is a separate field

Seller Kiran and buyer Mei each signed representation agreements with the same brokerage. Neither document contains written intermediary consent. One associate is working with each. The buyer's offer names the firm in the intermediary contact section. The seller offers to contribute $7,500 toward the buyer's brokerage obligation under resale paragraph 12B(1).

Create a three-part audit of representation, consent, and compensation. List the facts that cannot be inferred from the two associates, the contract contact page, or the contribution. Explain the needed escalation to the broker before negotiation proceeds. Write a client-facing explanation of why a seller contribution does not by itself determine whom the buyer's broker represents.

Feedback: two associates are not substitutes for the statutory intermediary process and written consents. Contact-page entries report an established capacity; they do not establish it. Seller contribution addresses payment toward an obligation under a separate agreement and is not representation consent. The broker must resolve whether and how lawful intermediary service will occur. The agent should not create a traditional dual-agency arrangement by using a familiar label.

## T02U2 — Unauthorized Practice of Law, Examples of Common Mistakes

Current §537.11 prohibits practicing law, legal advice, opinions on the legal effect of contract forms, and title-validity opinions. It specifically prohibits drafting or recommending language defining or affecting principals' rights, obligations, or remedies, including escalation, appraisal, and contingency clauses. “My broker has always used it” and “the client asked for protection” are not exceptions permitting agent drafting. An online clause library is not independent legal representation for the client.

An informational item completes a blank, discloses a fact, or provides instructions. A specific repair description entered into a form's designated repair field is different from inventing a new remedy if repairs disappoint the buyer. The agent may explain the meaning of informational choices, but must not promise what a court will enforce. When another form is approved for mandatory use for that purpose, the agent must not replace it by adding an informational item elsewhere.

There is a carefully limited rule for a principal's specific written instructions: §537.11(d)(2) allows adding or striking language as directed, with conspicuous changes. This is not permission for the license holder to originate or recommend the clause and obtain a confirming instruction. Preserve the principal's original direction, show the edits visibly, and obtain needed agreement. Unusual matters or instruments to be acknowledged and recorded call for advising the principal to consult an attorney before execution.

An agent may employ counsel to represent the agent, and may reimburse a principal for legal fees. The rule prohibits obtaining legal advice, directly or indirectly, for a principal in the transaction in which the agent acts as agent. Facilitate the principal's direct consultation; do not treat the brokerage attorney as automatically representing every client. A transaction attorney needs to know whom the attorney represents and the client's facts.

### Statute reading exercise: Four kinds of editing

Read §537.11(b)–(f) and resale paragraph 11. Classify these edits: entering “September 30, 2026” in the closing-date field from both parties' instructions; composing “buyer may cancel whenever insurance is unsatisfactory” in special provisions; conspicuously striking text specifically identified in the principal's own written instruction; and replacing a mandatory mineral addendum with one sentence in special provisions.

For each, identify the controlling subsection, what the agent may do, and what additional facts or professional assistance may be required. Write a short explanation of why even a well-intended contingency changes rights and remedies. Then produce a recordkeeping checklist for the specifically instructed strike-through, including the original written instruction and assent by the affected parties.

Feedback: the closing-date entry is informational completion. An agent-authored insurance escape clause is legal drafting. The specific principal-written direction fits the narrow conspicuous-edit rule if its conditions are satisfied, but does not make the agent its author or adviser. Replacing a mandatory addendum with a special-provisions sentence violates the separate-form restriction. Required advice that an instrument is binding and advice to consult counsel on unusual matters remain important.

### Applied exercise: The attractive escalation clause

Buyer Ellis wants to offer $470,000 and automatically beat competing offers by $2,000 up to $492,000. The agent finds a paragraph online allowing the listing broker to disclose competing offer prices and bind Ellis to the cap without further signatures. Ellis says, “Just make it enforceable; I trust you.” There is no principal-written clause or attorney-approved transaction-specific direction.

Identify every new right or obligation the proposed clause would introduce, the evidence issues it leaves unanswered, and the professional boundary. Draft a response that still helps Ellis decide a lawful next step: communicate an ordinary offer on definite terms, or seek direct attorney advice about a bespoke structure. Prepare the factual questions counsel would need, without recommending legal wording.

Feedback: the clause affects price, proof, disclosure, acceptance, and assent. It cannot be supplied by an agent under the label of filling a blank. Trust in the agent does not alter §537.11. A competent response presents the business objective and available process, refers legal drafting directly to counsel, and keeps an ordinary offer's terms definite if that is the buyer's choice. The agent must not send the copied clause for signature as if client approval cures authorship.

## T02U3 — The Broker-Lawyer Committee

The Texas Real Estate Broker-Lawyer Committee develops and recommends standard contract forms. Under §§1101.251–.253 it includes six broker members appointed by the Commission, six lawyer members appointed by the State Bar president, and one public member appointed by the governor. Its combination of professional perspectives is intended to support usable standard transaction forms. The Commission adopts forms; a committee draft or recommendation is not itself a mandatory form.

Follow the distinction from draft to adoption to effective use. A proposed form may be available for comment while the current form remains in effect. A form's printed revision date may differ from its effective date on TREC's listing. Save the official PDF, form number, effective-date evidence, and source. Do not identify currency merely by the most recent date printed somewhere in the file. Likewise, an old outline such as PCF-0 fixes the subjects to teach but does not require use of outdated operational forms.

Not every TREC-published form is mandatory. Current Chapter 537 includes forms approved for voluntary use as well as mandatory forms. A form's title, logo, or existence on the website does not answer that legal status. Check the adoption rule and intended transaction. A voluntary notice can still be a helpful way to satisfy a substantive disclosure duty, but do not tell a principal that the particular form is mandatory unless the controlling provision says so.

### Applied exercise: A version-control card

Your broker has three PDFs: a 20-18 resale form, a 20-19 committee discussion draft, and the 20-19 PDF linked from TREC's current resale page. The current page says effective July 1, 2026; the published PDF bears a May 4, 2026 revision date. The file will be offered September 21, 2026. No owner-required alternative form applies.

Create a version-control card stating the selected form, official source, rule, effective date, printed revision date, date checked, and rejected alternatives. Explain why the discussion draft is not interchangeable with the adopted PDF and why the May date is not proof of May mandatory use. Identify what should be checked again if the form is used after a later rule change.

Feedback: use the official current 20-19 linked by TREC and the current adoption rule §537.28. Preserve both distinct dates and their functions. Retire the obsolete transaction template for new offers under current requirements, while retaining older executed files as evidence of what was actually used. A later revision requires a new currency check, not editing an old signed contract to resemble the new form.

## T02U4 — Use of Promulgated Forms

Under §537.11(a), a license holder negotiating covered real-property transactions uses the Commission form approved for mandatory use for that transaction unless an exception applies. The stated exceptions include acting solely as a principal, a United States agency requiring another form, an owner-prepared form or attorney-prepared form required by the owner, and a transaction without a Commission mandatory form where the specified permissible alternatives are used. The details of the fourth exception matter; it does not allow the broker to draft a new commercial purchase agreement.

Choose the transaction family first. A resale condominium uses its own contract. A farm-and-ranch transaction needs its specialized property and agricultural treatment. A new home under construction requires construction provisions not found in the ordinary resale contract. Vacant land intended for one-to-four-family residential use may fit the Unimproved Property Contract; that does not make the form suitable for every commercial development. An unusual mixed transaction should be reviewed with counsel before an instrument is executed.

Preserve the form's text and format. Section 537.11(h) permits reproduction with the published text, sizing, spacing, and pagination intact, subject to the stated logo/outside-border and screen-scaling exceptions. Course worksheets are field maps, not substitute execution forms. Students should complete the official PDF rather than sign a redesigned educational table. Technology can help fill blanks and track attachments, but it must not silently delete warning text or reflow the executed document.

### Statute reading exercise: Test the exception, not the preference

Read §537.11(a) and (h). Analyze four proposed transactions: a license holder selling only the holder's own home; a federal agency requiring its own sale instrument; a commercial warehouse transaction for which there is no TREC mandatory purchase form; and a standard residential resale where the agent prefers a private form because it is shorter. For the warehouse, assume the proposed form is prepared by a Texas attorney and contains the required identification, approved-use statement, restrictions, and applicable explanations.

Write the applicable exception or mandatory-form outcome for each. Then evaluate software that reproduces the entire TREC form but removes page breaks and replaces twelve pages with six. Identify the rule concern separately from the form-selection concern. Explain why an exception allowing an alternative form is not a general exception allowing legal advice.

Feedback: the first three may fit their specified exceptions on the supplied facts; preference for a shorter form does not. Reformatting the executed TREC document contrary to its text-and-format requirement is a separate problem. Even when a lawful alternative form is used, the agent's practice-of-law boundaries remain. A good answer states the conditions of the exception rather than simply marking “allowed.”

### Applied exercise: A four-property selection memorandum

Compare these hypothetical purchases: Unit 308 in a condominium regime; a completed new home being sold by its builder; an existing detached duplex with no condominium regime; and an unimproved five-acre parcel intended solely for a four-bedroom residence. All are ordinary agent-represented transactions with no required alternative form and no unusual matter yet identified.

For each property identify the current TREC contract family, one feature requiring that choice, and an intake question that could change it. Locate the corresponding form's official page and verify its number and effective date. Then explain why the detached duplex does not become a condominium because it has an association, and why acreage alone does not make the vacant parcel a farm-and-ranch transaction.

Feedback: residential condominium resale, completed new home, one-to-four-family resale, and unimproved residential property are the indicated families on the supplied facts. Legal ownership regime, construction status, use, leases, agricultural interests, and transaction complexity can change the analysis. Form choice must follow facts, not the agent's folder labels or a marketing description such as “townhome.”

## T02U5 — Presenting Offers and Multiple Offers

The principal decides which terms to accept. Under §535.156, a license holder presents offers promptly unless the principal has instructed otherwise as the rule permits; an existing contract does not by itself erase the presentation duty. Under §1101.652(b)(20), offering unauthorized terms is a disciplinary concern. A broker's duty is to communicate and explain business features accurately, protect confidential information, and follow lawful instructions, not to select a winner based on the broker's compensation or personal preference.

Compare price with material terms: financing, approval conditions, option period, earnest money, closing, possession, contingencies, and seller contributions. A high price with an unresolved sale contingency is not equivalent to a lower price without it. A cash label does not justify representing undisclosed borrowing as cash. A reliable comparison states what the documents actually provide and identifies missing information.

Multiple-offer communication requires care. Do not invent competing offers, misstate their terms, promise secrecy you cannot guarantee, or disclose confidential information without authority. Discuss the seller's chosen process and lawful instructions with the supervising broker. The seller may choose among offers within legal limits; fair-housing restrictions still apply. Avoid collecting personal buyer narratives for discriminatory selection. Transaction terms provide the appropriate comparison.

### Applied exercise: Compare three offers accurately

Seller Ana receives three complete offers at 10:00 a.m. A offers $510,000, conventional financing of 90%, $5,000 earnest money, ten-day option, and a $9,000 seller expense contribution. B offers $500,000, documented cash, $10,000 earnest money, seven-day option, and no requested contribution. C offers $520,000, 80% financing, $7,500 earnest money, ten-day option, and a sale-of-other-property contingency. All close November 16 and provide possession at closing and funding. None supplies buyer brokerage contribution beyond what is stated, and no seller selection instruction limits presentation.

Prepare a neutral comparison table and a list of questions requiring documents or seller decisions. Calculate price less the stated seller expense contribution for A and B, clearly label it a limited comparison rather than net proceeds, and explain why C's higher number does not resolve its contingency risk. Draft a prompt presentation message without selecting the winner or describing protected buyer characteristics.

Feedback: A's limited figure is $501,000; B's $500,000. Those are not closing-net calculations because other expenses and obligations remain. C's price is $520,000 but the condition needs separate evaluation. Promptly present all three with complete attachments; the seller chooses after relevant advice. Avoid implying that finance labels guarantee funding or that an agent may suppress an offer because it seems weaker.

## T02U6 — When does the Offer Become a Contract?

Track final acceptance of the same terms and its communication. Form 20-19 directs the broker to insert the date of final acceptance as the effective date. A signature may occur before communication; the first signature date is not invariably the effective date. In a counteroffer sequence, the changed term must be accepted by the other party. A broker writing a date in the blank does not create an agreement that the evidence does not support.

Prepare a complete package before presenting it: property, parties, economic terms, selected alternatives, required attachments, signatures, and legible modifications. If the seller returns a counteroffer, compare it against the prior version and disclose every change to the buyer. Preserve the version actually accepted and the communication establishing final acceptance. Do not overwrite an earlier offer and lose the evidence of negotiation.

Different transactions can present difficult communication questions, revocations, competing acceptances, authority disputes, or electronic errors. Record exact facts and refer those questions promptly to counsel. An agent should not assume that a text saying “looks good” is full acceptance of every page when the sender asks for one more change. Likewise, later delivery to escrow may be administratively important without being the formation event.

### Applied exercise: Reconstruct final acceptance

At 9:00 a.m. Tuesday, buyer Amina signs an offer at $352,000 with closing October 28. At 1:00 p.m., seller Bruno signs but changes price to $357,000 and closing to October 30. At 3:00 p.m., Amina initials the price but changes closing to November 2. At 6:00 p.m., Bruno initials November 2. On Wednesday at 8:15 a.m., the seller's agent communicates Bruno's unqualified acceptance of that exact package to the buyer's authorized agent. No earlier acceptance communication or revocation occurred. Escrow receives it Thursday.

Build a version table and identify the final price, closing date, and effective date on these stipulated facts. Explain why neither Tuesday's first signature nor Thursday's escrow receipt supplies the date. List the evidence needed before entering the effective date, and calculate the three-day payment delivery count without assuming weekend/holiday facts that the problem does not supply.

Feedback: final terms are $357,000 and November 2; the stipulated final-acceptance communication occurs Wednesday, making that the effective date in this simplified sequence. Count Thursday, Friday, Saturday, then apply paragraph 5's actual weekend/holiday rule after verifying the calendar. Retain every negotiated version and the acceptance communication. A correct answer does not invent authority or infer an earlier communication absent from the facts.

### Applied exercise: Preserve a clean offer trail without concealing edits

Seller Ezra receives version A of an offer with a $468,000 price, November 19 closing, a $7,000 ordinary-expense request, and a $6,000 brokerage contribution. Version B raises price to $473,000, removes the ordinary-expense request, leaves the brokerage contribution unchanged, and changes closing to November 23. The seller signs B without further change and final acceptance is communicated as stipulated. A staff member proposes replacing the stored A file with B, marking A as accepted, and telling the buyer that only price changed. The title company has not yet received either version.

Prepare a four-field version comparison, a truthful principal communication, and an archive index identifying which version is accepted and which is superseded. List the evidence supporting final acceptance, identify the undisclosed changes in the staff member's statement, and specify what escrow should receive. Explain why version preservation does not authorize distributing confidential negotiation information to unrelated people. Then identify the supervising broker's role in correcting the proposed recordkeeping failure without fabricating a receipt or acceptance time.

Feedback: the accepted B changes price, closing, and the ordinary-expense request; the brokerage contribution stays $6,000. Preserve A as an unaccepted prior version and B as the accepted document, with the actual acceptance communication and complete attachments. Send the complete accepted package and accurately describe its changes. Do not relabel history, hide a material change, or invent escrow receipt. The broker's supervision and an accurate confidential file support compliance; they are not substitutes for the principal's actual agreement.
