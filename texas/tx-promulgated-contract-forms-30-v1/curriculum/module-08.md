# Texas Promulgated Contract Forms — Module 08

Other Real Estate Matters. Accurate form completion does not cure dishonest facts, discriminatory instructions, improper fees, or missing disclosures. This module applies the [License Act](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/OC/htm/OC.1101.htm), [TREC rules](https://www.trec.texas.gov/agency-information/rules-and-laws/trec-rules), and the linked primary statutes to concrete transaction decisions. Every exercise is hypothetical and requires an independent written response before feedback.

## T08U1 — Real Estate Fraud

Fraud involves legal elements that must be established in a particular claim; it is not a label for every mistake. For license practice, the immediate standards are concrete. Section 1101.652 addresses dishonesty, misrepresentation, false promises, known significant defects, unauthorized terms, inaccurate accounting, and negligent/incompetent conduct. A transaction can be professionally improper even before a court determines common-law fraud. Do not wait for a completed lawsuit before correcting a known false statement.

Forms can be used to disguise a false transaction. Examples include misstated cash sources, concealed credits, a fake lease to satisfy underwriting, an inflated repair invoice, a forged signature, or a side agreement kept from the lender and closing professional. The document title does not make a false statement true. Refuse to create or transmit a materially false record, preserve evidence, inform the broker, and obtain appropriate legal guidance about the actual incident. Correct an error transparently rather than altering an executed document silently.

Known property defects require particular care. A seller's disclosure notice does not immunize an agent who independently knows a significant structural defect and conceals it. An “as-is” selection does not license misrepresentation. If the agent knows a roof repair failed, merely forwarding an older disclosure saying “repaired” can create a misleading impression. Record what is actually known, its source, what has changed, and what appropriate disclosure/review is required.

Wire fraud exploits trusted-looking instructions. An email with the correct property address and closing amount may still have a compromised sender or changed bank details. Verify funding instructions through a previously established, independently known channel with the closing professional. Never verify a changed bank account by calling only the phone number supplied in the suspicious email. If money has been sent to the wrong place, escalate immediately to the financial institution, broker, closing professional, and appropriate authorities through the incident process; speed and accurate facts matter.

Distinguish a recommendation from a guarantee. “An inspector can assess the foundation” is a process statement. “This house will never move” is an unsupported assurance. “The seller will consider your request” is not acceptance. “We have three offers” must be true if stated. Sales pressure never justifies inventing urgency, hiding a defect, or backdating a required agreement. Honest uncertainty is preferable to a fact the agent cannot support.

### Statute reading exercise: Match false documents to conduct rules

Read §1101.652(b)(1)–(6), (20), and (24), and [Business & Commerce Code §17.46](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/BC/htm/BC.17.htm#17.46). Broker Alia is asked to show $20,000 more cash than buyer has, create a $5,000 repair invoice for work never performed, and assure lender that a relative occupies the house under a lease that does not exist. Separately, the seller admits a significant foundation defect omitted from the notice.

Prepare a conduct/evidence/rule/correction matrix for all four matters. Distinguish the licensing concerns from a final judicial determination of fraud or consumer-law liability. Draft a refusal to produce false paperwork that still identifies legitimate alternatives: accurate sources, actual repairs, true occupancy documentation, and appropriate disclosure. Record what should be preserved for broker/counsel review.

Feedback: the requested records are materially false and can implicate multiple conduct provisions; §17.46 supplies additional consumer-law concerns whose elements and applicability require legal analysis. Correct the facts and involve the broker/counsel. The foundation information requires attention independent of the requested financial falsification. Do not create a “practice version” of false records for use in the live transaction.

### Applied exercise: Verify a changed wire instruction

Buyer Ben is due to send $81,450 to title tomorrow. At 4:45 p.m. an email using the closer's display name directs funds to a new account and says to call a new number if needed. The message knows the property address and earnest-money amount. The buyer has the original title-company phone number from the verified opening package. No funds have been sent.

Prepare a verification sequence identifying the trusted channel, data to compare, parties to alert, and evidence to preserve. Draft a short message to buyer that gives concrete next steps without repeating sensitive account details. Explain why knowledge of the transaction and a familiar display name do not authenticate the change. State what the immediate escalation would be if funds had already been sent.

Feedback: independently contact the known title office/closer using previously verified information and authenticate the actual instructions before transfer. Preserve the suspicious message and alert the broker/title team. Do not use only the new number or reply to the potentially compromised thread. If funds moved, promptly contact the sending institution and incident-response contacts; do not promise recovery or delay until the next business day to investigate.

### Applied exercise: Correct a known defect record

Seller Cora's older disclosure says a roof leak was repaired in 2024. Yesterday agent Dev saw active water entering the same area during rain and received the seller's message that the repair failed last month. Cora asks Dev to send only the old form and call the ceiling stain cosmetic. Buyer has not yet signed an offer.

Prepare a factual chronology, identify the misleading statements to avoid, and draft the broker/seller follow-up needed to provide accurate information. Distinguish the seller's notice responsibilities from Dev's independent known-defect duty. Identify qualified inspection/repair evidence to obtain without diagnosing the roof yourself. Explain why accepting property as-is would not authorize concealment.

Feedback: the active leak and seller admission are current known facts that cannot be disguised by forwarding stale wording. Correct or supplement the disclosure process appropriately and disclose known significant information as required. An agent need not diagnose engineering cause to report what was observed and learned. An as-is bargain does not turn a false cosmetic description into an acceptable statement.

## T08U2 — Broker's Fees

Broker compensation is negotiated; no TREC contract sets a universal percentage. Section 1101.563 requires the residential buyer agreement to state the amount/rate and determination and conspicuous negotiability language within its scope. Resale paragraph 12B repeats the negotiability principle and distinguishes each party's separate obligation from a contribution paid by the other party. A seller contribution does not automatically cap or increase the buyer's promised fee. Read both instruments and avoid charging beyond the actual agreement.

Compensation also has a lawful route. Section 1101.651 generally prohibits compensation for brokerage acts to an unlicensed person and controls how sales agents receive and pay transaction compensation through the sponsoring broker, with stated out-of-state-broker rules. Calling a commission a “consulting bonus” does not change the act performed. Section 1101.652(b)(8) requires full knowledge and consent for compensation from more than one party. Disclosure is not a cure for every prohibited payment; the underlying activity and law still matter.

Settlement-service referrals can raise separate federal RESPA restrictions where applicable. Do not assume an association membership or a disclosed referral label authorizes payment for steering a covered settlement service. The provider's actual services, compensation, relationship, disclosures, and the governing law need examination. Route uncertain payments to the broker and counsel before promising them. The fee agreement should be understandable to the consumer and accurately reflected in closing records.

### Applied exercise: A fee request outside the agreement

Buyer Etta's written representation agreement specifies $9,200 total brokerage compensation for the covered purchase, with no other fee. Seller contributes $7,800 under the accepted contract. A sales associate asks Etta to pay the remaining $1,400 plus a new $600 “processing fee” directly to the associate's personal account. No amendment or broker authorization exists. A neighbor also requests $500 for negotiating the buyer's price despite holding no real-estate license.

Prepare a compensation ledger and identify the unsupported fee, improper payment route, and unlicensed-activity issue. Draft a factual explanation of the agreed remaining obligation and questions for the sponsoring broker. Explain why relabeling the extra amounts does not resolve the agreement or licensing problem. Do not assume any commission rate is mandatory.

Feedback: the supplied remaining contractual amount is $1,400; the new $600 is not authorized by the stated agreement. The sales agent's direct personal-account demand conflicts with the statutory broker compensation route. The neighbor's paid negotiation is not transformed into a permissible referral by calling it a bonus. Broker and counsel must evaluate the actual conduct and payment before it is made.

## T08U3 — Fair Housing Laws

Federal Fair Housing Act protections in 42 U.S.C. §3604 and the Texas Fair Housing Act in Property Code Chapter 301 apply to covered sales, rentals, terms, services, representations of availability, advertising, and related conduct. Protected categories in these core provisions include race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, and disability. Texas license-conduct provisions and §531.19 also expressly address ancestry. Identify applicable federal, state, and local law rather than assuming one short list resolves every jurisdictional issue.

Discrimination can enter form work through apparently routine questions: asking whether buyers have children to decide which offer to present, requesting a photograph to select a purchaser, steering families away from a subdivision, or adding a restrictive occupancy sentence based on protected status. Section 531.19 reaches asking, responding to, facilitating inquiries, or making disclosures intended to indicate prohibited preferences. An agent must not simply relay a discriminatory request under the excuse of acting as a messenger.

Compare offers on lawful transaction terms: price, financing, contingencies, timing, and ability to perform. A buyer's religion or ancestry is not a financing term. An owner's preference does not authorize the agent to discriminate. Exemptions are specific, limited, and do not necessarily remove advertising restrictions or separate licensing duties. The owner-sale exemption in §301.041 includes conditions concerning broker involvement; it is not a blanket exemption for every owner of one house.

Disability law can require reasonable accommodations in rules or services and reasonable modifications as provided by the governing law. Evaluate an actual request individually through the appropriate process. A policy against animals or alterations does not end the inquiry when disability-related accommodation or modification is implicated. Avoid unnecessary medical-detail requests or assumptions from appearance. The broker should use current legal guidance and obtain counsel for disputed or unusual requests. A course example cannot determine every accommodation's reasonableness.

### Statute reading exercise: Instructions an agent cannot carry out

Read 22 TAC §531.19, Occupations Code §1101.652(b)(32), and [Property Code §§301.021–.025 and .041](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/PR/htm/PR.301.htm). Seller Fia asks the agent to obtain each buyer's ancestry, whether they have children, and whether anyone has a disability, then to show only offers from households she prefers. The property is marketed through a broker, and no applicable exemption has been established.

Create a request/rule/allowed-alternative table. Draft a response explaining the permissible financial and contractual comparison process. Identify why facilitating the questions itself matters even before an offer is rejected. Analyze the limits of an unsupported owner-exemption claim and distinguish equal treatment from an appropriate disability-accommodation process.

Feedback: the agent must not solicit or facilitate the stated discriminatory selection data. Offer comparison should use legitimate transaction factors. Broker involvement is material to the cited owner-sale exemption's conditions, and separate license duties remain. Accommodation requests are evaluated under their own legal standards, not used as reasons to exclude a buyer. A lawful alternative gives the seller useful contract information without protected-characteristic screening.

### Applied exercise: Review a temporary-lease restriction

Seller Gita will lease back for fourteen days after closing. Buyer/landlord Hamid asks to add “no children allowed on the property at any time” because he worries about damage. Gita's family includes two children, and the parties have agreed to ordinary residential occupancy. No senior-housing exemption or other lawful basis is supplied. The agent is also asked to add an automatic $5,000 penalty if a child visits.

Prepare a review identifying familial-status concerns, legal-drafting concerns, and legitimate property-care issues that can be addressed through lawful agreed terms. Draft a response and broker/counsel referral. Explain why a fourteen-day term does not itself authorize discriminatory language, and why an agent cannot cure the clause by replacing “children” with a coded phrase aimed at the same group.

Feedback: a blanket child exclusion and targeted penalty raise protected-status and drafting problems. Address actual occupancy, condition, deposit, permitted use, and damage responsibility through applicable lawful provisions and advice. Short duration does not erase all fair-housing or licensing duties. The agent should not recommend a disguised discriminatory substitute or assume the owner's concern creates an exemption.

### Applied exercise: An accommodation request is not an automatic rejection

During negotiation of an otherwise suitable temporary residential lease, a prospective occupant with a disability requests an exception to a no-animals policy for an assistance animal. The applicant has not asked for structural work, and no facts establish direct threat, property damage, or an applicable exemption. The owner instructs the agent to reject the request because all animals are forbidden and suggests asking for the applicant's complete medical records. The broker has a lawful accommodation-review process, but the agent has not used it.

Read Property Code §301.025 and prepare a decision log separating the stated policy, requested accommodation, known facts, missing information, and the professional responsible for review. Draft a neutral acknowledgment that does not promise approval or demand an unrestricted medical history. Identify the owner's two unsupported assumptions and the questions to route to the broker or counsel. Distinguish an accommodation involving a rule from a physical modification and explain why an agent should not invent an exemption merely because the lease is temporary.

Feedback: an otherwise applicable rule can require reasonable accommodation when necessary for a person with a disability to use and enjoy a dwelling. The blanket no-animals rule is not itself a complete legal answer. Use the lawful review process, seek only appropriate information, and avoid unsupported disability inquiries or definitive entitlement decisions on incomplete facts. No structural modification was requested here. Duration alone does not establish an exemption. Preserve the request and response and obtain appropriate review rather than transmitting the owner's categorical rejection.

## T08U4 — Other Disclosures (Required, Permitted, Prohibited)

Classify information by actual authority. Required disclosure can include representation, license holder's personal interest under §535.144/resale paragraph 8, known significant defects, seller notices, lead information, and applicable water/district/coastal notices. The transaction facts determine which duty applies. A signature acknowledging one notice is not proof all required notices were delivered.

Some information has no inquiry/disclosure duty under a specific statute. Section 1101.556 addresses certain occupant health information and death by natural causes, suicide, or accident unrelated to property condition. That provision must not be converted into “all deaths and hazards may be concealed.” An accident caused by the property's condition raises a different question. Also distinguish no duty from affirmative permission to facilitate disability discrimination; §531.19 remains relevant to health-related inquiries.

Confidential information and discriminatory information have separate limits. For an intermediary, §1101.651(d) restricts revealing bargaining positions and confidential information except under its stated exceptions. A party's written instruction does not authorize hiding material property-condition information contrary to the statute. A broker can disclose required facts while protecting unrelated personal financial or medical details. When duties appear to conflict, obtain prompt broker/counsel guidance and preserve the actual request rather than guessing.

### Applied exercise: Classify six disclosure requests

An intermediary's file contains: seller's private minimum price; a known foundation failure; buyer's private maximum price; seller's adult child's medical diagnosis unrelated to condition; a disclosed agent ownership interest above ten percent in the seller entity; and a report that a person died naturally in the home years ago. No separate written authority to disclose negotiation limits exists. No court order is supplied.

Build a six-row matrix identifying required, protected/restricted, no statutory inquiry duty, or further-analysis status with a pinpoint source. Explain the property-condition exception separately from the negotiation-price rules. Draft two safe responses to requests for the minimum price and medical diagnosis without supplying the confidential facts. Identify why “everything in the file is confidential” is also inaccurate.

Feedback: bargaining positions have specific intermediary restrictions; material condition cannot be suppressed under a blanket confidentiality claim; license-holder interest has the applicable written disclosure duty; health-related discriminatory inquiry raises §531.19 and §1101.556; natural death is within the cited no-duty provision. Do not overgeneralize any category. The actual information and statutory exception control.

## T08U5 — Review Occupancy Standards

Texas Property Code §92.010 states, subject to its exceptions, a maximum number of adults a landlord may allow equal to three times the number of bedrooms. It defines adult as eighteen or older and bedroom as an area intended for sleeping, excluding the listed nonbedroom spaces such as kitchen, living room, bathroom, utility room, and storage. It is not a command that every landlord impose exactly three persons per bedroom, and children are not counted as adults under that section.

The section includes exceptions where state or federal fair-housing law requires a higher rate and for the specified temporary sanctuary from family violence for no more than one month. Read the whole provision before applying a number. Other building/safety standards, actual dwelling configuration, fair-housing considerations, and local rules may matter. A familiar “two per bedroom” practice is not a universal safe harbor that overrides all facts and law.

An occupancy policy should address legitimate space and safety needs consistently, not act as a proxy for excluding families with children, people with disabilities, or a national-origin group. Do not turn a living room into a legal bedroom on an application merely to make the arithmetic work. When an exception or accommodation may apply, gather the appropriate facts respectfully and obtain qualified guidance. The agent is not a building official and should not certify a room as compliant sleeping space from a photograph.

### Applied exercise: Count the statutory adults accurately

A two-bedroom rental application lists three adults aged 42, 39, and 20, and three children aged 16, 10, and 7. The landlord says six occupants exceed Texas's “two adults per bedroom law.” A second independent application lists seven adults for a two-bedroom dwelling; one seeks temporary sanctuary from family violence for three weeks. No other safety or local-rule determination has been made, and the statutory sanctuary facts are assumed genuine for this exercise.

Read §92.010 and compute the base adult count and threshold for both files. Identify the landlord's incorrect statement and the second file's exception needing review. Explain why the calculation does not by itself resolve every lawful occupancy policy or building requirement. Draft a factual intake note without labeling the children's presence a statutory adult-count violation.

Feedback: the first file has three statutory adults, below the base six-adult maximum; the children do not become adults for this calculation. The second exceeds the base but raises the express temporary-sanctuary exception within the one-month limit. Fair-housing, legitimate safety standards, and other applicable law still require evaluation. The statute is neither a universal two-person rule nor permission to fabricate additional bedrooms.
