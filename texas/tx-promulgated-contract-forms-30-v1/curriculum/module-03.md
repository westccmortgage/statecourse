# Texas Promulgated Contract Forms — Module 03

Parties, Property and Financing. In this module you build the first part of an offer and choose the financing documents that make the stated price credible. Use the current official PDFs, not a rebuilt execution form. Required sources are [resale 20-19](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/one-four-family-residential-contract-resale), [Non-Realty Items 57-0](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/non-realty-items-addendum), [Third Party Financing 40-11](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/third-party-financing-addendum-0), [Loan Assumption 41-3](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/loan-assumption-addendum-0), [Seller Financing 26-8](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/seller-financing-addendum-0), and [Release/Restoration 12-3](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/addendum-release-liability-assumed-loan-andor-restoration-sellers-va-entitlement). A field map is a learning worksheet; the parties would sign the official documents in a real transaction.

## T03U1 — Information Needed to Complete Contract Forms (including financing costs

The official outline leaves this title's parenthesis unfinished. The subject is complete: collect identities, authority, property, money sources, loan terms, costs, and deadlines before presenting a supposed final bargain. Paragraph 1 names the actual parties. A nickname, team name, or employee's name may not identify the owner. Compare the seller with title information and confirm who will sign for a trust or entity. Do not assume that a married seller's assertion resolves all homestead or signature issues; counsel and the closing professional determine required signatures from the facts.

Paragraph 2 defines the property through land, improvements, accessories, exclusions, and reservations. A promised refrigerator and a reserved chandelier require different analysis. Fixtures and listed accessories included by the form pass unless properly excluded or otherwise addressed; additional personal property uses the appropriate non-realty document. Fixtures may also be leased. A seller cannot promise to convey ownership of leased solar equipment merely because it is physically attached to the home. Paragraph 4 calls for residential, fixture, and natural-resource lease treatment.

The sales-price equation in paragraph 3 is cash plus all disclosed financing. The form's definition of cash excludes proceeds of borrowing or sale of other real property except as disclosed. “Cash offer” must therefore be checked against actual sources. Earnest money and option fee are not an extra addition to the sales price; their credits and disposition follow the contract. Closing funds also include expenses, prorations, and other adjustments, so paragraph 3A alone is not the buyer's complete cash-to-close estimate.

Financing costs include more than interest. The financing addendum identifies principal, term, rate limits, and origination charges as applicable. The buyer's Loan Estimate supplies lender disclosures; it does not rewrite the offer's agreed limits. Identify financed premiums separately where the form excludes them from principal figures. Do not assume an origination percentage is the total settlement cost. Lender, title, insurance, tax, and brokerage amounts must be reconciled without inventing rates or guaranteeing approval.

### Applied exercise: A complete intake sheet

Sellers Arlo and Beatriz Vega own Lot 12, Block 4, Copper Willow Addition, City of San Antonio, Bexar County, Texas, known as 118 Copper Willow Lane, 78230. Buyers Celia Hart and Nikhil Rao will both take title. Price is $425,000; savings fund $85,000 and a conventional first mortgage funds $340,000. The buyers have no sale contingency or other borrowing. The built-in oven stays, the dining chandelier is retained by sellers, and a freestanding freezer will be sold separately for $500. The solar panels are leased; sellers provide a $95/month agreement with 84 months remaining. No residential or natural-resource lease exists.

Prepare a field map for paragraphs 1–4 showing every supplied fact, the separate document needed for the freezer and solar lease, and the unresolved information needed before completing the lease addendum. Compute paragraph 3 and list at least four cash-to-close categories not included in $85,000. Explain why the solar panels should not be described as seller-owned equipment.

Feedback: use both sellers and both buyers, the full supplied land description, $85,000 cash, $340,000 financing, and $425,000 total. Address the chandelier under exclusions and the freezer under the non-realty document. The fixture lease needs its own review and addendum; obtain assignment/consent terms, documents, deadlines, and the buyer's informed decisions. Title expenses, lender charges, insurance/reserves, taxes/prorations, and brokerage obligations can affect cash needed. A good answer does not silently convert leased equipment into owned property.

### Applied exercise: Cash-source reconciliation

Buyer Darius proposes a $600,000 purchase “all cash.” His source sheet shows $280,000 savings, $120,000 from a new unsecured line of credit, and $200,000 expected from the sale of his current home. That sale has not closed. The proposed offer has no financing addendum, no sale-of-other-property addendum, and paragraph 3A states $600,000. He asks the agent to leave the sources out because the seller prefers cash.

Reconcile the three sources and identify what is disclosed and undisclosed. Explain the paragraph 3A definition in plain language, identify the financing and sale-contingency questions requiring a buyer decision and appropriate documents, and draft a factual response to the requested omission. Do not assume that every source creates a termination right; distinguish disclosure from a negotiated contingency.

Feedback: the sources total $600,000, but their nature does not match an unqualified savings-only description. Borrowing and sale proceeds require accurate treatment under the actual contract; conditions and addenda must reflect the buyer's instructions and agreed bargain. A source disclosure is not automatically a sale contingency. The agent must not conceal material financing facts or promise that a lender will approve the funds. The correct work product lists questions and routes each source into the proper contract analysis.

## T03U2 — Review Elements & Practice 1-4 Family Residential Contract (Resale), Non-Realty Items Addendum

The resale form covers an ordinary resale of one-to-four-family residential property and expressly is not for condominium transactions. Its opening page creates an integrated definition: land, improvements, and accessories collectively make up the property. Read the included-item lists before drafting exclusions. The refrigerator, stove, window coverings, wall-mounted television bracket, and smart controls may be treated differently by the actual wording and installation facts. Do not rely on “everything attached stays” as a complete reading of the form because listed accessories can be included even when not fixtures.

Specific exclusions identify what seller retains and must remove before possession. Avoid vague phrases such as “seller's personal items” when the object is an included fixture or accessory. If the seller intends to remove a chandelier, identify it distinctly and resolve resulting repair or replacement instructions using appropriate fields and professional advice. Mineral or other reserved interests belong in an attached addendum, not merely the household-property exclusions blank.

The current Non-Realty Items Addendum is 57-0, a voluntary-use form in the current TREC inventory; the older outline's generic title remains the curriculum heading. Non-realty items are separately identified, with their agreed consideration and condition provisions. They do not become part of the lender's real-estate collateral because the buyer wants to finance everything together. Give the lender and closing professional accurate information about the transaction rather than hiding a personal-property price inside the real-estate price.

Paragraph 10B of 20-19 also matters when property changes hands: smart-device access must be transferred and seller access removed as stated. Identify physical devices, relevant software/app controls, and existing fixture leases. Account-transfer capability is a factual question; an agent should not promise that a subscription or license will transfer without checking its terms. Security and privacy at possession require a deliberate handoff.

### Statute reading exercise: Mark the property boundary

Read resale paragraphs 2, 4, and 10B, then the current Non-Realty Items Addendum. At 81 Birch Crossing the seller owns a built-in dishwasher, an attached television mount, a television hung on that mount, a freestanding washer, curtain rods and curtains, a hard-wired doorbell camera, and its paid cloud subscription. The proposed contract has no exclusions and no non-realty attachment. The seller wants to keep the television and washer; the buyer wants the mount, window coverings, and operational doorbell.

Create an item-by-item matrix with form classification, whether facts are sufficient, proposed document location, and transfer question. Do not reproduce the entire official form; mark it and prepare a field map. Explain the difference between transferring physical hardware and transferring access. Add a final entry for a leased propane tank not previously disclosed and identify the new document work it requires.

Feedback: read the actual inclusion lists, which expressly include mounts/brackets and curtains/rods, and analyze the television and washer separately. The doorbell's hardware and account/control handoff require separate attention; the cloud subscription's transferability cannot be assumed. The leased tank belongs in the fixture-lease review. A strong answer distinguishes what the form includes from what an additional personal-property agreement or lease assignment must establish.

### Applied exercise: Correct the property draft

Using the Copper Willow facts from the intake worksheet, a draft says “all appliances excluded,” describes the freezer as included real estate for $500, leaves the chandelier out of exclusions, and checks “no leases.” The sellers confirm they intend to retain only the dining chandelier and sell the freezer; the built-in oven stays. The leased panels remain subject to their owner's rights.

Prepare a redline instruction sheet identifying each incorrect field, the factual correction, and the supporting instruction. Produce a separate inventory for the non-realty transaction with identifying details the parties still need, such as make, model, serial number, and condition. Write a delivery checklist for the complete offer package, including the financing and fixture-lease attachments. Explain why deleting “all appliances excluded” without verifying the remaining item list would be incomplete review.

Feedback: correct the blanket exclusion, identify the chandelier, remove the freezer from the real-estate price equation unless the parties properly revise the bargain, and address the lease truthfully. The buyer must understand the actual panel arrangement. The package should contain the official resale form, relevant financing, fixture-lease, and non-realty documents with consistent property identification. The correction sheet records instructions rather than silently changing an already signed offer.

## T03U3 — Review Elements & Practice Third Party Financing Addendum & Loan Assumption Addendum

Third-party financing involves funds supplied by a lender other than seller or buyer. Form 40-11 requires prompt application and every reasonable effort to obtain the stated financing, including providing requested information. Its financing categories contain different fields. Complete only the applicable selections from actual instructions and lender information; do not populate every category or assume a conventional field fits a VA loan. Other-financing provisions include a specific property-approval waiver choice that requires careful explanation and the buyer's decision.

Buyer Approval and Property Approval are separate. Buyer Approval concerns availability of the stated loan terms and the lender's asset, income, and credit requirements. If the applicable subject-to box is selected, the buyer's termination under paragraph 2A requires both notice and a lender's written reasons within the negotiated days after effective date. If the buyer does not terminate under that provision, the contract is no longer subject to that approval condition. A prequalification letter is not a contractual extension.

Property Approval concerns lender underwriting of the property, including appraisal, insurability, and required repairs. Paragraph 2B generally requires the notice and lender statement on or before the third day before closing. Read any applicable waiver and the rest of the contract; failure to obtain a survey under resale paragraph 6C(2), for example, has its own stated limit on use of that financing provision. FHA/VA paragraph 4 supplies a separate required provision and expressly does not use paragraph 2B's three-day termination-notice requirement. Do not flatten these into one generic “financing deadline.”

An assumption under Form 41-3 is a different financing transaction: the buyer assumes an existing note and obligations under its deed of trust. Obtain actual documents and lender statements. The addendum addresses credit documentation and seller approval, balance variation, assumption fees/rate limits, noteholder consent, seller protection, and tax/insurance escrow. A quoted attractive rate is not proof that the noteholder will allow transfer or release the seller. Assumption consent, seller release, and restoration of VA entitlement must be examined separately.

### Statute reading exercise: Compare the two approval paths

Read 40-11 paragraphs 1, 2, and 4 and 41-3 paragraphs A–E. Prepare a comparison using these supplied facts: a new $288,000 conventional loan, 30-year term, 6.5% maximum interest for all 30 years, origination-charge limit 1.5%, buyer-approval period 18 days; alternatively, assumption of a $251,000 existing first lien with a $1,720 current total monthly payment, maximum assumption fee $1,200, maximum permitted interest 4%, and credit documentation due in seven days.

Map each fact to the appropriate form field and identify the different approval actors. Write the separate notices and supporting evidence required for failure of buyer approval in the new-loan path and failure of noteholder consent in the assumption path. Explain why $251,000 cannot simply replace $288,000 without reconciling price and cash. Include the source paragraph for each finding.

Feedback: new-loan underwriting uses the buyer/property distinction and the agreed loan parameters; assumption additionally involves seller's credit review and noteholder consent under its actual terms. The new-loan buyer-approval termination requires notice plus lender reasons within the selected period. Noteholder failure to consent under 41-3E gives either party the stated termination choice by notice. Different financing principal requires corresponding cash or price adjustment, not a hidden mismatch.

### Applied exercise: Complete conventional financing and costs

Use Copper Willow's $340,000 first mortgage. The parties instruct a 30-year term, interest not exceeding 6.75% for 30 years, origination charges not exceeding 1.25%, and a contract subject to buyer approval within 21 days. Effective date is October 6, 2026; closing is November 10. No FHA/VA or other-financing provision applies. The Loan Estimate later shows origination charges of $4,760, while the lender is willing to revise them to $4,100. No other lender determination is provided.

Complete a field map for 40-11, calculate the dollar origination cap, and compare both lender amounts to it. Create distinct buyer-approval and property-approval deadline entries, including the required supporting statement. Write a follow-up explaining that a cost mismatch calls for prompt resolution and does not automatically terminate the contract. Identify which terms would require an agreed amendment if the buyer accepts different limits.

Feedback: the cap is $4,250; $4,760 exceeds it and $4,100 is within it. Buyer-approval period ends October 27 on the stated count; the ordinary property-approval notice point is November 7, the third day before November 10, subject to actual provisions and facts. Do not assume a weekend extension absent language applying to that deadline. The agent obtains lender clarification and the buyer's decision, preserving the original negotiated condition.

### Applied exercise: Balance variance in an assumption

Price is $370,000 with an assumed first lien stated at $250,000 and $120,000 cash. The assumption addendum selects adjustment of cash payable at closing, with a $2,000 total-variance threshold. The actual principal balance at closing is $248,600. The lender consents on unchanged terms and charges $900, within the $1,000 allowed fee. Seller has not yet obtained a release of liability.

Recalculate cash and show why the price remains the same. Compare the $1,400 variance with the threshold, and distinguish the fee from principal. Prepare a closing inquiry identifying release, loan-document, and escrow-balance evidence still needed. Then analyze a separate version with actual balance $247,000 and no other party election yet made: identify the threshold issue and the addendum's next decision without announcing automatic termination.

Feedback: cash increases to $121,400 when the assumed debt falls, preserving $370,000 total. The variance is within $2,000. With $247,000 debt, cash would be $123,000, but the variance exceeds the stated threshold and invokes the specified right/election mechanism in 41-3C. Consent to assumption does not itself release seller liability; inspect the actual release and any required security. An assumption fee is a cost, not part of the principal balance variance calculation.

## T03U4 — Review Elements & Practice Seller Financing Addendum

Form 26-8 applies when the seller finances all or part of the price. Its opening notice directs consultation with an attorney and financial professional because lending arrangements may be regulated and create accounting or reporting obligations. The addendum describes agreed financing; it is not a substitute for a lawful note, deed of trust, servicing arrangement, and compliance analysis. Do not assure a seller that one occasional loan is exempt from every federal and state requirement.

Credit documentation and approval have distinct consequences. If required documents are not delivered, the seller's specified timely termination before complete delivery directs earnest money to seller. If documents are delivered but the seller timely disapproves credit, the specified termination refunds earnest money to buyer. Those are different facts. The agent should track the documents actually received, not label a package “complete” because one PDF arrived.

The note fields establish principal, interest, and payment structure. One lump sum, monthly installments, and interest-only followed by installments create different obligations. An amortization schedule may have payments calculated over thirty years with the remaining balance due in five; that is not a fully amortizing five-year loan. Explain the field choices, obtain agreed figures from qualified sources, and ensure that the principal reconciles to resale paragraph 3B. Do not calculate a number and guarantee its legal or financial suitability.

The deed-of-trust choices address transfer consent, insurance naming seller as mortgagee loss payee, tax/insurance escrow, servicing, and prior-lien default. Follow the exact choices and avoid contradictory checkmarks. Seller's financial security depends on more than an interest rate: lien priority, borrower capacity, insurance, servicing, default rights, and existing debt all matter. A seller may need direct counsel on due-on-sale, wraparound, or consumer-credit issues before agreeing.

### Statute reading exercise: The seller-finance approval branches

Read every provision of 26-8, marking paragraphs A, B, C, and D in separate colors. Seller Faye finances $160,000 of a $200,000 purchase. Credit report, employment verification, deposit verification, and a current financial statement are due six days after effective date. On day six only the credit report and employment verification arrive. On day eight the remaining required documents arrive. No notice has been delivered. In a separate branch, all documents arrive on day six and the seller finds the credit unacceptable on day nine.

Write the delivery ledger, identify the distinct termination windows and money consequences from paragraph B, and explain why the agent may not merge the two branches. Then identify all note and security choices still missing from the supplied facts. Draft a referral list for direct attorney/financial consultation addressing the opening notice rather than inventing completed loan documents.

Feedback: before complete delivery in the first branch, paragraph B supplies seller's stated nondelivery termination path; complete delivery changes the analysis. In the second branch, timely credit disapproval uses the seven-day period measured from expiration of the delivery period or actual delivery, whichever is later, with earnest money refunded to buyer. Rate, maturity/payment structure, transfer consent, insurance, escrow, and servicing remain unresolved. The form itself directs professional consultation.

### Applied exercise: Assemble an internally consistent seller note

Buyer Imani and seller Grant agree on $280,000 price, $70,000 cash, and $210,000 seller financing. Their attorney and financial professional supply these hypothetical agreed terms: 6% annual interest, interest-only monthly payments for 12 months, then $1,800 monthly including interest beginning 13 months after note date and continuing for 48 months when the remaining balance is due. Consent is required for transfers under the selected form provision; casualty insurance naming seller is required; tax/insurance escrow is required with a third-party servicer at buyer's expense. No superior lien exists. Credit documents selected are credit report and deposit verification, due seven days after effective date.

Map all fields in 26-8 and the corresponding resale financing amount. Calculate the initial monthly interest-only payment, identify the balloon risk, and create a checklist for the attorney-prepared note and security instrument. Explain why “$1,800 for 48 months” does not prove the debt is paid off. Do not calculate or promise the final balloon from an incomplete payment-history assumption.

Feedback: $210,000 × 6% ÷ 12 equals $1,050 initially. The later payment schedule has a remaining-balance maturity, not a representation of full amortization. Check the interest-only option and associated later-installment fields as supplied, and the stated D choices. Keep the $210,000 in paragraph 3B and $70,000 in 3A. Actual note drafting, legal compliance, and final amortization confirmation belong to the retained professionals.

## T03U5 — Review Elements & Practice Addendum for Release of Liability on Assumed Loan and/or Restoration of Seller's VA Entitlement

Form 12-3 separates release of seller's liability from restoration of VA entitlement. Neither follows merely from the buyer taking title or agreeing to assume payments. The release section calls for seller and buyer to apply within the stated days and provide required information. It offers a choice of contract termination with earnest money refund if approval has not occurred by closing, or proceeding without delaying closing. The choice must reflect the parties' instructions, not the agent's preference for saving the transaction.

The restoration section has its own application period and closing consequence. The form's notice states the buyer must be a veteran, have sufficient unused entitlement, and otherwise qualify for VA restoration in this assumption setting; it tells the parties to use both A and B when restoration is desired. A broker should not certify VA eligibility or promise restoration. Obtain written lender/VA determinations and distinguish submitted, under review, and approved.

Seller pays the cost of securing release and restoration under the form. The deed must contain any assumption clause required by the lender or agency. This calls for coordination with the closing attorney and title professional, not broker drafting. If the seller is willing to close without release, make sure the selected contractual choice is understood with direct legal advice about continuing liability.

### Applied exercise: Two approvals at the same closing

Seller Hector's VA-backed note will be assumed by buyer Jo. Their 12-3 selects both release and entitlement restoration, applications due five days after effective date, and termination/refund if either required approval is absent at closing. Both submit complete applications on day four. Three days before closing the lender approves assumption and release, while VA restoration remains pending. Jo is a veteran, but no eligibility or unused-entitlement determination has been supplied.

Make a three-row status chart for assumption consent, release, and restoration. Map the selected application deadlines and closing consequences to A and B. Draft a status message explaining what has and has not been approved, what evidence remains necessary, and who can evaluate eligibility. Explain why the release letter cannot be copied into the restoration box as proof. Do not assume an extension or change the selected consequence.

Feedback: assumption approval, liability release, and entitlement restoration are separate. Pending restoration has not satisfied the selected closing condition. The parties may consider lawful agreed changes with appropriate advice, but the original form does not become an automatic extension. The lender/VA must supply the actual determinations; veteran status alone does not prove sufficient unused entitlement or all qualification conditions.

## T03U6 — Case Study/ Workshop

This workshop requires an offer package and a review memorandum. It applies the earlier units to one coherent file but introduces new decisions. First make your own work, then compare feedback. Do not combine incompatible financing alternatives into one offer. A change in financing source is a new branch to analyze, not permission to overwrite a buyer's accepted instructions.

### Applied exercise: Draft the initial offer field map

Buyers Soren Bell and Uma Ellis offer $480,000 to seller Yara Finch for Lot 22, Block B, Meadow Reed Addition, City of Austin, Travis County, Texas, 2608 Meadow Reed Drive, 78748. Verified savings provide $96,000 and a $384,000 conventional first mortgage provides the balance. Agreed offer limits are 30 years, maximum 6.8% interest for 30 years, maximum origination charges 1%, and buyer approval within 20 days. Earnest money is $6,000, option fee $400, option ten days, closing November 23, 2026. Escrow is Prairie Oak Title, 400 Congress Avenue, Austin, Texas 78701. A freestanding wine refrigerator is separately purchased for $700; the built-in range stays; a brass hallway chandelier is excluded. Seller owns all fixtures and there are no leases.

Produce a paragraph 1–5 field map, finance-addendum field map, and non-realty attachment inventory. Reconcile all figures, show the origination cap, identify remaining whole-contract information needed before presentation, and explain that the proposed closing date does not supply an effective date. Mark the work as hypothetical and unexecuted.

Feedback: price reconciles $96,000 + $384,000; the origination cap is $3,840. The refrigerator's separate $700 does not silently increase paragraph 3C; identify its agreed transaction treatment in the proper document. The chandelier must be specifically excluded. The form remains incomplete until title, survey, condition, notices, contributions, possession, attachments, and party instructions are resolved. No effective date exists merely because the offer proposes November 23 closing.

### Applied exercise: Audit the changed financing branch

Before acceptance, Uma asks instead to assume Yara's $290,000 loan at its existing 3.8% rate and pay the remaining price from verified savings. The lender has not consented. Current payment including reserves is $1,930. The parties propose an assumption-fee cap of $1,100, a rate cap of 3.8%, cash adjustment for balance variance with a $2,500 threshold, and seven-day credit-document delivery. Yara requires liability release by closing with termination/refund if it is absent. This is a conventional loan, so VA entitlement is irrelevant.

Create a new package list that replaces the incompatible initial financing choice, recalculates cash, and maps the assumption and release fields. Identify the documents the seller must supply, all approvals still unresolved, and tax/insurance escrow questions. Write a comparison explaining the change in funding risk. Do not retain the conventional new-loan addendum by habit when that loan is no longer part of the proposed bargain.

Feedback: cash becomes $190,000 against the $290,000 assumption. Use 41-3 and the applicable liability-release section of 12-3, with no VA restoration selection. Obtain notes, deeds of trust, recent statements, actual balance, lender consent, release, and escrow information. A lower historic rate does not prove transfer permission. The original financing package remains a superseded proposal, preserved for history but clearly distinct from the new offer.

### Applied exercise: Explain a preclosing financing failure

Return to the initial conventional branch, now accepted October 20 with closing November 23 and buyer approval within twenty days. On November 6 the lender states in writing that the borrowers' income cannot support the agreed loan. On November 7 the buyer instructs timely termination under the buyer-approval provision. Assume the agreement still is subject to that provision, no amendment changes it, and the written lender statement gives the reasons. The property appraisal was satisfactory; escrow holds the $6,000 earnest money and the $400 option fee has been released to seller.

Prepare a notice-package checklist, identify the clause and applicable deadline, and explain why satisfactory appraisal does not cure missing buyer approval. Write a money-status note distinguishing earnest money from the option fee and direct escrow release procedures. Identify the delivery evidence to retain. Do not use an expired option as the stated basis when the supplied facts identify a different right.

Feedback: twenty days after October 20 is November 9 on the stipulated calculation; the buyer's November 7 instruction can be acted on under the stated 2A condition with both notice and lender reasons delivered as required. Appraisal is a property issue and does not solve income qualification. The financing clause calls for earnest-money refund on proper termination; it does not turn the already released option fee into refundable earnest money. Preserve notice, attachments, delivery, instructions, and escrow correspondence.

### Module recap

Review your field maps as one complete audit trail. Every dollar in price must have a disclosed source; every financing choice must have the correct applicable document; every approval must identify its actor and deadline. Explain assumption consent, seller release, and entitlement restoration without using them as synonyms. Check the current forms' warnings and signatures, correct your independent worksheets, and prepare for the topic quiz. No worksheet authorizes real-world execution without complete transaction facts and the appropriate professional review.
