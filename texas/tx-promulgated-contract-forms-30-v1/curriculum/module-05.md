# Texas Promulgated Contract Forms — Module 05

Closing, Possession and More. The required sources are [resale 20-19](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/one-four-family-residential-contract-resale), paragraphs 8–23 and its execution/contact/receipt pages; [Seller's Temporary Residential Lease 15-7](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/sellers-temporary-residential-lease); [Buyer's Temporary Residential Lease 16-7](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/buyers-temporary-residential-lease); and the current [Amendment 39-11](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/amendment). Closing is a coordinated performance event. It must not be reduced to an appointment at which someone signs whatever has arrived.

## T05U1 — Closing Paragraph & Buyer's Possession Paragraphs in Contract

Paragraph 9A states an on-or-before closing date, with the specified interaction for objections cured or waived under paragraph 6D. Read both paragraphs before telling anyone the original calendar date remains controlling. Paragraph 9B identifies seller's deed/title and tax documentation, buyer's good funds, necessary closing documents, satisfaction of liens except properly assumed loans, and transfer-fee treatment. The title professional prepares the settlement process; each party remains responsible for the promised performance.

Possession in paragraph 10A is a separate selection: at closing and funding, or under a written temporary lease. Signing before funding does not satisfy a closing-and-funding possession trigger. Buyer possession before closing and seller possession afterward require the correct written arrangement; otherwise the form describes a tenancy at sufferance. Insurance coverage may change when ownership and occupancy separate. Refer both parties to their insurers before the change, rather than promising a homeowner policy continues unchanged.

Paragraph 10B adds a practical modern handoff. At possession, seller provides access information necessary to operate and manage smart devices and removes seller's connections and access as specified. A key receipt should include gate, garage, alarm, doorbell, thermostat, and other agreed control information, while protecting passwords and personal data. Do not publicly email an entire security credential list to every transaction participant.

### Applied exercise: Closing-day authority to release keys

Buyer Owen signs on Monday morning, seller Petra signs Monday afternoon, and the lender funds Tuesday at 2:00 p.m. The contract selects possession at closing and funding with no lease. On Monday evening Owen's movers arrive and ask the agent for the lockbox code. Petra's personal account still controls the garage app and doorbell. Both parties expect Tuesday possession when funding is confirmed.

Prepare a possession checklist with the actual trigger, confirmation source, key/control inventory, insurance referral, and access-removal evidence. Draft Monday's factual response to the movers and Tuesday's transfer record. Explain why an agent's desire to avoid moving charges cannot substitute for an authorized lease or changed agreement.

Feedback: Monday signatures alone do not authorize the selected possession. Confirm closing and funding before Tuesday release, coordinate the smart-device transfer/removal, and document delivery securely. Movers are not parties authorized to change the agreement. If the parties wish to change possession, use a proper written arrangement with appropriate advice before access, not a retroactive note after occupancy.

### Applied exercise: A closing-document readiness table

Closing is October 29. The settlement file has buyer's signed loan papers, an unsigned seller deed, a payoff quote expiring October 27, proof of earnest money, an unverified wire instruction email, and no final repair invoice. No document establishes that the lien is assumed. Seller promised completed roof flashing repairs and required documentation before closing.

Prepare a readiness table marking each item complete, incomplete, expired, or requiring verification. Assign each next step to the appropriate party/professional. Identify the contract provisions governing deed, lien satisfaction, repair evidence, and good funds. Draft a concise status message that identifies blockers without claiming the sale failed or the title is defective. Include an independent verification step for wire instructions using a known trusted contact.

Feedback: obtain the executed deed through the closing professional, an updated payoff, verified good-funds instructions, and repair evidence. Loan papers and deposit receipt prove only their own events. An expiring payoff can change the amount needed; it is not a reason to fabricate a payoff. Verify wiring details through an independently known channel before funds move. The buyer and seller receive accurate status and make needed decisions with counsel if deadlines or remedies are at issue.

## T05U2 — Special Provisions

Paragraph 11 is for additional informational items, not a workshop for drafting legal protections. Current §537.11 defines informational items and expressly limits agent-authored language affecting rights, obligations, or remedies. A factual delivery instruction is different from a new termination right. A repair field or mandatory addendum should be used for its intended subject rather than repeating competing terms in special provisions.

Consistency is part of accuracy. If paragraph 9 says October 30 and special provisions say November 15, the document may create an interpretive dispute instead of a clear extension. If the standard financing addendum governs appraisal and another sentence announces a different deadline, the agent has introduced a potential conflict. Refer proposed bespoke language to the principal's attorney and preserve specific written directions for any allowed insertion. Never discourage attorney review because “the form already protects everybody.”

### Applied exercise: Route six requested terms

Buyer asks for: price $398,000; closing November 12; a seller contribution of $5,000 toward ordinary buyer expenses; $8,000 toward buyer's broker obligation; a seven-day option extension for an additional $200; and a new right to cancel if a nearby road ever becomes busier. The transaction is already under contract. No change has yet been accepted.

Build a routing sheet naming the applicable amendment/contract field for each standard change and identifying the bespoke request requiring direct legal assistance. Explain why brokerage contribution must not be hidden in the ordinary expense field under 20-19. List all original deadlines to keep active while the amendment is pending and the evidence needed before updating the transaction tracker.

Feedback: use current amendment choices and the corresponding price, closing, expense, brokerage, and option provisions, checking the exact 39-11 fields. The speculative road condition is a new legal contingency, not informational completion. Paragraph 12A(1)(b) excludes brokerage contributions now handled in 12B. Pending negotiations do not change existing terms; accepted written changes and their communication must be recorded.

## T05U3 — Seller's Temporary Lease Form & Buyer's Temporary Lease Form

The seller's temporary lease, 15-7, is for seller occupancy no more than ninety days after closing. Buyer becomes landlord and seller becomes tenant. The term begins on closing and funding and ends at the stated point, subject to the lease. Daily rent excludes the day of closing and funding and the anticipated rent is paid at funding. The deposit is security for lease obligations, distinct from purchase earnest money. The lease supplies its own deposit accounting, utility, pet, maintenance, inspection, default, holdover, notice, and attorney provisions.

The buyer's temporary lease, 16-7, is for buyer occupancy no more than ninety days before closing. Seller is landlord and buyer is tenant. The lease has a commencement date, anticipated rent paid at commencement, adjustment if actual term differs, and a termination provision tied to the contract and other stated events. The form expressly says rent is not applied to purchase-contract items. Do not casually promise that rent becomes a down payment. Deposit treatment must be read for whether closing occurs or the lease ends beforehand.

In both forms, identify all parties, property, dates, daily amounts, deposits, utilities, pets, holdover damages, and notice addresses from agreed instructions. Their repair provisions are subject to the Texas Property Code; a brief lease does not authorize ignoring nonwaivable duties. Current forms warn that changed possession can alter insurance coverage. Ninety-day intended use does not mean every short arrangement is suitable for the form, especially unusual commercial occupancy or an indefinite stay. Longer or unusual arrangements require appropriate forms and legal review.

### Statute reading exercise: Reverse the roles correctly

Read 15-7 and 16-7 in full. In File A, buyers Quinn and Rhea close and fund October 15; seller Silas stays through October 25 at $125/day, $2,000 deposit, all utilities paid by Silas, one cat allowed, and holdover damages $250/day. In File B, seller Tara lets buyer Uriel occupy October 10 before anticipated October 20 closing at $90/day, $1,500 deposit, utilities paid by Uriel, no pets, and $180/day holdover damages. No special modifications exist.

Prepare a field map for each form, identifying landlord/tenant, correct commencement/ending mechanism, rent-payment timing, deposit, utilities, pets, and holdover. Compare the insurance, inspection, repairs, and termination provisions in writing. For File B, identify the exact lease clause governing a sale that never closes rather than inventing a fixed end date.

Feedback: A uses 15-7 with the buyers as landlords and seller as tenant; B uses 16-7 with seller as landlord and buyer as tenant. Roles follow occupancy and ownership timing, not who originally hired the agent. Rent and deposit remain separate amounts. Use the actual printed termination mechanism in 16-7 and do not assume failure to close gives the occupant a continuing right to stay.

### Applied exercise: Calculate seller lease rent and holdover

Use File A from the source review. Closing and funding actually occur October 15. The stated temporary lease ends October 25, and the parties agree this exercise's rental count includes October 16 through October 25. Silas remains through two additional full days after termination. The lease's daily rent is $125 and its holdover damages are $250 per day. No agreed extension exists. Silas provides a forwarding address after surrender; no deposit deduction has yet been determined.

Calculate ordinary rent and the stated two-day holdover amount separately. Prepare a ledger identifying the deposit as security rather than automatic liquidated rent. Draft a possession follow-up and identify who should advise on removal or disputed remedies. Explain why paying holdover damages does not itself renew the lease.

Feedback: ten charged rental days produce $1,250; two stated holdover days produce $500 under the supplied rate. The $2,000 deposit is not automatically forfeited; follow the lease's accounting and applicable law. Holdover payment is not consent to extension. The landlord needs appropriate legal advice for enforcement; the agent should not attempt self-help removal or change locks based on a course calculation.

### Applied exercise: Early occupancy without the right documents

Buyer Veda asks to move into seller Wren's home seven days before closing. The purchase contract still selects closing-and-funding possession. The lender has not cleared the loan, buyer proposes to paint two rooms immediately, and neither insurer has reviewed early occupancy. Wren says “fine” in a phone call but no lease terms, deposit, rent, maintenance arrangement, or default provisions have been agreed.

Prepare an early-occupancy risk and information checklist, identify the contract/lease changes needed, and distinguish who approves alterations, insurance, and financing. Draft a neutral response explaining the missing agreement work. List the consequences that must be understood if the purchase fails. Do not treat the phone call as a fully completed 16-7 or improvise a lease in special provisions.

Feedback: obtain the appropriate written possession arrangement with complete agreed terms before access. The proposed painting raises alteration consent, condition, insurance, and failed-closing issues. A pending loan can fail while a tenancy exists. The principals and their professionals decide whether the arrangement is acceptable; an agent cannot guarantee coverage or erase occupancy rights by writing “buyer assumes all risk.”

## T05U4 — Settlement & Other Expenses

Paragraph 12A assigns seller and buyer expenses and gives a separate seller cap toward buyer expenses. In 20-19 that cap expressly excludes brokerage compensation and contributions under 12B. Paragraph 12B states compensation is fully negotiable and not set by law. Each party's obligation to its own broker is in separate written agreements; selected seller-to-buyer-broker or buyer-to-seller-broker contributions apply toward those obligations without changing them. Read the compensation agreement and contract together rather than assuming one replaces the other.

Separate a contractual cap, actual eligible expense, lender limitation, and broker obligation. A $6,000 concession does not guarantee the buyer can use all $6,000 if actual eligible costs are lower or loan rules restrict it. Nor may unused ordinary expense credit be silently diverted to brokerage compensation. Confirm with the lender and closing professional. Paragraph 12C addresses prohibited buyer charges in governmental loan programs and the priority application of the stated concession; it does not make the agent the authority on every loan-program rule.

Settlement math should show gross price, applicable credits/debits, deposits, financing, prorations, and net cash. Avoid a misleading “net” figure that omits taxes or compensation. Use actual signed terms and verified invoices. If the statement disagrees with the contract, investigate and obtain a corrected statement or an authorized agreement change; do not simply tell the buyer the title company has the final say over negotiated terms.

### Statute reading exercise: Separate the two contribution fields

Read 20-19 paragraphs 12A–C and a hypothetical separate buyer representation agreement requiring $10,500 buyer brokerage compensation. Price is $420,000. The sales contract has $6,000 in 12A(1)(b) and a seller contribution of 2% of price in 12B(1). Eligible ordinary buyer expenses total $5,500; no lender limitation changes these figures. No other compensation credits or amendments exist.

Prepare a three-ledger worksheet for ordinary expenses, seller brokerage contribution, and the buyer's remaining brokerage obligation. Calculate each amount and explain why unused ordinary concession does not automatically move to brokerage. Identify who must approve any desired new arrangement and what must be checked with the lender. Write a buyer explanation that does not call 2% a standard rate.

Feedback: seller pays $5,500 eligible ordinary expenses under the $6,000 cap, not an automatic $6,000 cash gift. Two percent of $420,000 is $8,400 toward the $10,500 brokerage obligation, leaving $2,100 absent other agreed credits. The unspent $500 is not automatically brokerage money because 12A excludes it. Compensation is negotiated; any changed contribution or obligation needs the appropriate agreement and lender review.

### Applied exercise: Correct a settlement estimate

For that same transaction, the draft statement charges seller $6,000 ordinary concession although only $5,500 eligible costs are shown, applies a $9,000 brokerage contribution, and reduces buyer brokerage obligation to $9,000 despite no changed representation agreement. It also omits buyer's verified $4,000 earnest-money credit. Assume all other listed amounts are correct and no additional expenses or credits exist.

Create a correction table for each discrepancy, with correct amount, signed source, and party/professional to contact. Explain which errors are statement corrections and which would require new agreement if the parties actually want the draft amounts. Calculate the difference in each affected line without inventing a complete cash-to-close total from partial facts.

Feedback: ordinary eligible concession should be $5,500, brokerage contribution $8,400, contractual brokerage obligation remains $10,500, and verified earnest money must receive the proper credit. A desired $9,000 contribution or reduced broker fee requires the appropriate written change rather than a silent ledger edit. The exercise supplies partial settlement figures, so a final cash-to-close number would be fabricated.

## T05U5 — Prorations, Casualty Loss, Default & Mediation of Contract

Paragraph 13 prorates taxes, interest, rents, and regular periodic charges through closing. Estimated tax proration is adjusted when actual statements become available if amounts differ. Use the agreed day-count basis and identify who is charged or credited. Seller's current tax exemption may not reflect the buyer's future tax situation. Do not present an estimate as a guarantee of next year's taxes or appraised value.

Paragraph 14 requires seller to restore casualty damage to its previous condition as soon as reasonably possible and by closing. If restoration fails due to factors beyond seller's control, the printed buyer alternatives include termination/refund, an extension up to fifteen days, or damaged-condition acceptance with an allowed insurance-proceeds assignment and deductible credit. These are alternatives under specific facts. A casualty is not automatically a buyer-default event or a requirement to accept whatever insurance pays.

Paragraph 15's default remedies are distinct from ordinary contingency termination. Paragraph 16 requires unresolved buyer/seller contract disputes to go to mutually acceptable mediation after informal discussion, with costs shared equally, while preserving equitable relief as stated. Mediation is not a decision imposed by the mediator in the same manner as a judgment. Paragraph 17 addresses reasonable attorney fees and proceeding costs for the stated prevailing participants; it is not a broker's promise that counsel will be free.

### Applied exercise: Proration and a casualty election

Part A: annual taxes are estimated at $7,300, use a 365-day basis, and the agreed settlement count allocates 273 days to seller. Taxes are unpaid and buyer will pay the annual bill. Part B is a separate file: a storm damages the roof after effective date; seller cannot restore by closing because materials are unavailable, a factor stipulated beyond seller's control. The insurer permits assignment of proceeds; deductible is $2,500. Buyer has not chosen an alternative.

Calculate the seller tax debit/buyer credit and explain the later true-up. For Part B create an alternatives chart from paragraph 14, identifying what must be confirmed before damaged-condition acceptance and who chooses. Explain why a broker should not unilaterally select the fifteen-day extension or promise insurance coverage beyond the actual carrier decision.

Feedback: $7,300 ÷ 365 = $20/day; 273 days gives $5,460 seller debit and buyer credit on the supplied convention. Actual taxes can require adjustment. The casualty alternatives belong to buyer under the stipulated clause conditions; assignment requires carrier permission and the deductible credit must be addressed. This is separate from declaring default. Counsel and insurer should resolve disputed legal or coverage issues before a choice is implemented.

## T05U6 — Escrow Provisions

Paragraph 18 explains that escrow is not a party responsible for buyer's or seller's performance. It may require collected good funds, handle authorized expenses, and follow its release and demand procedure. At closing earnest money is applied first to cash down payment, then buyer expenses, with excess refunded. Without closing, authorized expenses and release requirements must be examined rather than treating every dollar as instantly payable on demand.

The demand process and objection receipt matter. A written demand by one party is copied to the other; the form describes fifteen days for written objection and permits specified disbursement if no objection is received. Paragraph 18E distinguishes effectiveness of escrow notices from receipt of an objection to a demand. “May disburse” is not “must wire immediately.” Wrongful refusal to sign a release has separate paragraph 18D consequences; an agent should not pronounce refusal wrongful without the relevant facts and legal advice.

### Applied exercise: An objected-to earnest-money demand

Seller sends escrow a written demand for $5,500 earnest money after a disputed termination. Escrow forwards it as required. Buyer delivers a written objection that escrow actually receives within the fifteen-day period. Seller asks the agent to say the funds must still be released because the buyer did not sign the seller's preferred release. No court order or mutual settlement exists.

Prepare a chronology showing demand, forwarding, actual objection receipt, and present dispute. Identify the paragraph preventing treatment of this as an unopposed demand. Draft an escrow-status message and a counsel referral preserving both parties' positions. Explain why receipt evidence is critical and why the agent should not characterize refusal as wrongful simply because one party demands it.

Feedback: timely received objection means the unopposed-demand disbursement condition is not established. Escrow follows the governing agreement and applicable process; the broker cannot adjudicate entitlement. Preserve the objection and proof of receipt. A release's wording, underlying termination, authorized expenses, and possible remedies require proper review, not an automatic payment instruction from the agent.

## T05U7 — Other Contract Provisions

Paragraph 19 states the contract's covenants, representations, and warranties survive closing; an untrue seller representation at closing puts seller in default under its wording. It also permits continued showings and receipt, negotiation, and acceptance of backup offers unless prohibited by written agreement. Do not tell a seller that a signed primary contract automatically forbids every backup discussion, and do not treat a backup as a replacement for the primary without its own terms.

Paragraph 20 addresses governmental requirements, including foreign-person tax withholding and requested reporting information. Citizenship, ethnicity, accent, and national origin do not establish tax-residency status. Refer the actual FIRPTA analysis to qualified tax and closing professionals. The buyer may have withholding duties under applicable law; a seller's assertion that withholding “is only the title company's problem” is unsafe. The course does not supply tax rates or exemptions for a real transaction without verified facts.

Paragraph 21 governs written notices and provides party and agent contact fields. Accurate contacts are functional contract data. Paragraph 22 integrates the agreement, requires written changes, and organizes financial, lease, testing, statutory, and other attachments. It expressly warns that missing applicable statutory disclosures can create remedies. Paragraph 23 directs attorney consultation before signing. These late pages are not optional administrative decoration; they can determine how substantive rights are exercised.

### Applied exercise: A late-page audit

The draft's buyer notice email is missing one letter; seller notice address is an old property; 22 checks a fixture-lease addendum that is not attached; a required district notice is absent; and buyer's attorney has not been offered the chance to review an unusual water-right reservation. Seller says all the “important money pages” are complete. The draft is not signed.

Create a five-row defect/location/consequence/repair table. Identify how to verify each contact, who supplies the missing attachment/notice, and which matter needs direct legal review. Explain why checking an attachment box does not incorporate a nonexistent agreed document. Prepare an accurate remaining-work message without claiming the draft is ready for signatures.

Feedback: correct verified notice contacts, obtain and review actual attachments, satisfy applicable district notice requirements, and facilitate direct counsel review of the unusual reservation before execution. Paragraphs 21–23 affect delivery, completeness, and advice. A form that adds up financially can still be incomplete or dangerous to present as final. Preserve the corrected version and all attached documents together.

## T05U8 — Executing the Contract & Finalizing the Agreement

Final review works in two directions. Read from the first page forward for the bargain; then read from signatures and attachments backward to ensure every referenced term is actually part of the accepted package. Verify exact parties and capacities, all material changes, selected alternatives, agreed blanks, legibility, attachments, final-acceptance evidence, and current form version. Do not fill an unresolved choice with a customary value simply because the software requires a field.

On 20-19 the broker contact page says to print names and not sign. Parties sign the execution page; escrow completes the appropriate receipt acknowledgments for what escrow receives. These are different functions. A broker contact entry is not a compensation agreement, a party signature, or a new representation consent. The effective date is final acceptance, supported by the actual communication sequence. Do not backdate it to align with a lender's preferred schedule.

After acceptance distribute the complete accepted package, confirm escrow receipt and payment deadlines, and initialize the performance calendar. Keep amendments, notices, evidence, and superseded drafts distinguishable. An electronic-signature audit trail can support attribution and timing but must be reviewed with the actual record; technology does not cure the wrong party signing or an absent attachment.

### Applied exercise: Final package quality check

Buyer Ximena and seller Yusuf accept a twelve-page 20-19 packet. Pages 1–10 name Lot 4; one attached finance form names a different street number; the seller's spouse is a required signer according to the closing attorney but has not signed; the broker signs the contact-only page; and escrow's receipt is blank because nothing has yet been delivered. The effective-date blank was prefilled a day before final acceptance.

Prepare an issue list separating incorrect data, missing required signature, unnecessary broker signature, unearned receipt, and wrong date. State the evidence and professional instruction required to resolve each. Explain why you must preserve the actual signed record and obtain a proper corrected agreement rather than quietly edit it after signatures. Draft a distribution checklist for the final verified package.

Feedback: the mismatched attachment and required signer need actual correction and assent; broker contact information is printed rather than signed; escrow alone acknowledges actual receipt; effective date follows real final acceptance. Silent post-signature changes would compromise the evidence. The attorney's supplied required-signature determination controls this hypothetical; do not substitute an agent's marital-status assumption.

## T05U9 — Case Study/Workshop

The workshop is a closing simulation, not a final examination. Produce separate artifacts for each stage. The facts below use a new transaction so that you must organize the workflow yourself. The assessed scenario assigned to this topic is a separate decision task and is not a second time credit for these worksheets.

### Statute reading exercise: Read the whole closing sequence

Read 20-19 paragraphs 9–23 in order and then trace every cross-reference back to its target. For a $460,000 purchase closing November 18, list every provision that might affect date, funds, possession, disclosures, notices, or post-closing obligations. The parties have $92,000 cash and $368,000 financing, $5,000 earnest money, $350 option fee, seller ordinary-expense cap $4,000, seller brokerage contribution $9,000, seller leaseback through November 24, and an agreed repair not yet documented.

Build a two-page checklist organized by before closing, at closing/funding, at possession, and after closing. Each line needs an actor, document, contractual citation, and evidence of completion. Identify which amounts cannot be combined and which details remain missing. Mark at least three cross-references that would be missed by reviewing only the closing paragraph.

Feedback: trace title objections into closing, repairs into completion/remedies, possession into lease and device access, expense/contribution fields into separate agreements, and escrow into good-funds/deposit handling. Include proration true-up and surviving representations after closing. Missing lease rate/deposit/terms, actual expenses, repair evidence, and complete notices are unresolved facts, not invitations to fill customary numbers.

### Applied exercise: Construct the buyer's simplified cash ledger

For this simulation only, all lender limitations have been checked. Price $460,000; loan proceeds $368,000; earnest money $5,000; option fee $350; eligible buyer ordinary expenses $6,200; seller ordinary contribution $4,000; buyer brokerage obligation $11,000; seller brokerage contribution $9,000; seller tax credit to buyer $5,900. No other charges or credits exist in this simplified worksheet. Do not include the separate seller lease rent or deposit in buyer purchase cash.

Compute the cash needed from buyer at closing, showing each line and its sign. Explain why the loan, earnest money, option fee, expense concession, brokerage contribution, and proration are six different entries. Prepare a verification note that labels the result a simplified exercise rather than an actual Closing Disclosure.

Feedback: starting with $92,000 price cash, subtract $5,000 earnest money and $350 option credit; add $2,200 uncovered ordinary expenses and $2,000 remaining brokerage obligation; subtract $5,900 seller tax credit. Result is $84,950. The number is valid only for the supplied closed set of facts. It is not a quote for a live purchase; actual settlement requires all verified charges, credits, funds, and program requirements.

### Applied exercise: Deliver possession after a leaseback

Prepare a surrender-condition record distinguishing observation from a legal conclusion, a device handoff checklist, and a deposit-accounting follow-up. Feedback: six days at $140 equals $840. Document the condition and remaining possession, obtain lawful instructions and advice, and follow the lease/deposit process. A broken window may support a claim but the exercise does not decide causation or allowable deduction. Smart-device access is part of the practical possession work. Partial occupancy is not automatically a renewed lease and does not authorize self-help eviction.

### Applied exercise: Resolve a closing-date change consistently

On November 16 the parties agree to move closing from November 18 to November 20 because a repair will be completed November 19. They want seller's lease to end November 24 unchanged, ordinary daily rent still $140, and all other terms unchanged. The lender and title company confirm they can perform November 20. Feedback: the new anticipated lease rent is four days × $140 = $560 if closing/funding occurs November 20 and the end date remains November 24. The amendment is proposed until properly agreed; distribute the accepted version and recheck the financing, title, repairs, and possession deadlines against their actual wording.

### Module recap

Review all closing artifacts against the signed-source principle: every amount, date, and authority must trace to the actual agreement or verified document. Explain why signature, closing, funding, and possession may occur separately; why ordinary expense contribution is different from brokerage contribution; and why a demand does not decide a disputed deposit. Correct your independent calculations and field maps, then complete the topic's separate scenario and quiz. Preserve the limitations of the simplified financial exercises when discussing real files.
