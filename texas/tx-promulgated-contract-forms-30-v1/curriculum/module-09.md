# Texas Promulgated Contract Forms — Module 09

Practice Makes Perfect. This required capstone workshop integrates the complete contract file. It is adapted for individual distance learning: complete every role's written artifact yourself, then compare feedback and correct the artifact. A study partner may discuss the reasoning, but no group attendance is assumed and another person's answer does not replace your work. These worksheets are formative instruction. The separately assigned assessed scenario and topic quiz have different facts and must be completed independently.

## T09U1 — Group Work Shop/Case Study/Discussion

Your role is the buyer's supervised sales agent in a hypothetical Texas residential resale. You have a valid current residential buyer representation agreement authorizing the services, with a definite termination date, negotiated compensation, and required disclosures. Seller has a separate listing broker; no intermediary or personal-interest disclosure is involved. Both sides are competent adult principals with verified authority. These supplied assumptions keep attention on the contract file; in a real transaction they would need evidence.

The objective is to create a coherent file, not merely fill every blank. A coherent file can explain why a form was selected, where each fact came from, who made each choice, when agreement occurred, what performance remains, and how a notice or payment was verified. An unanswered question should remain visible. A form with a plausible but invented value is less reliable than an uncompleted draft accompanied by a clear request for the missing instruction.

Use current official [resale 20-19](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/one-four-family-residential-contract-resale), [financing 40-11](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/third-party-financing-addendum-0), [association 36-11](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/addendum-property-subject-mandatory-membership-property-owners-association), [lead 56-0](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/addendum-sellers-disclosure-information-lead-based-paint-and-lead-based-paint-hazards-0), [non-realty 57-0](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/non-realty-items-addendum), and [amendment 39-11](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/amendment). Other documents are added only if the facts call for them. Do not print or sign a redesigned course worksheet as if it were the promulgated form. Preserve TREC's official form text and formatting.

Work in passes. The first pass is identity and property: match the title description, actual parties, ownership regime, included items, exclusions, leases, and reservations. The second is money: reconcile price and financing, keep earnest money and option fee separate, distinguish ordinary expenses and brokerage contributions, and identify costs still needing outside confirmation. The third is conditions and disclosures: record actual receipt and whether a specific addendum changes the default. The fourth is performance: assign dates, actors, evidence, and notice methods. The fifth is integrity: match all attachments, signatures, modifications, and the actual final-acceptance communication.

The current July 2026 contract must be read as a complete document. An older checklist can omit water disclosure, additional attachments, current compensation fields, or current broker-contact instructions. It can also import the wrong rule from another form. Current source control means checking the official version and preserving the one actually used; it does not mean changing old executed records to resemble today's form. The student should be able to show which version supports every field map in this workshop.

Keep a separate event log. A document can be signed one day, sent another, and received later. Some provisions depend on delivery; others use receipt, title-company receipt, final acceptance, or a changed effective date. The log should record the actor, date/time, document version, recipient, method, and proof. If the sender's claim and recipient's evidence disagree, do not choose whichever date saves the deadline. Preserve both and escalate the dispute promptly. The legal consequences may require counsel.

When money is involved, show the equation. Price cash plus financing must equal price. Closing cash then accounts for verified deposits, expenses, contributions, prorations, and other adjustments. A reduced loan amount can increase buyer cash or require an amended price; it cannot be silently ignored. A seller contribution may be limited by actual eligible expenses or the lender. A personal-property payment may need separate disclosure and cannot simply be hidden inside a lender's collateral value. Every figure needs a label and a source.

When repairs are involved, separate observations, technical assessment, agreement, and completion. The inspector identifies conditions within the inspector's scope. Qualified trades supply proposed work and cost. The parties decide what work they agree to require. The contract describes completion, permits, documentation, and available remedies. The agent tracks that process and communicates facts. The agent does not promise that a foundation is safe, a repair complies with every code, or a party will win litigation if a repair fails.

Disclosure work is not one universal checklist marked “done” at acceptance. Seller's notice, lead information, water disclosure, association documents, district notices, personal-interest disclosure, representation disclosure, and IABS have different actors and triggers. A property's age matters to lead analysis; municipal water alone does not necessarily establish the current water-disclosure exception; an association does not automatically make the property a condominium. The student's job is to make each conclusion traceable to actual supplied facts and the appropriate provision.

An apparent problem does not always produce the same remedy. A missing option fee affects the unrestricted right under paragraph 5; missing earnest money invokes a different seller mechanism. A title problem can involve objection and cure. A financing problem can involve buyer approval, property approval, or FHA/VA protections. A casualty has its own alternatives. A proposed amendment does not suspend existing terms. A termination notice records a right; escrow release follows a separate procedure. Identifying the wrong mechanism can be more damaging than failing to name the form number.

Communication should be useful and precise. Tell the principal what is known, what document says, what deadline matters, what choices belong to the principal, and what question requires a professional. Avoid statements such as “you are protected” without explaining the relevant conditions. Avoid declarations that a contract is void, an estate has clear title, or a party must accept a settlement unless a proper authority has resolved the issue. A well-prepared referral includes documents and factual questions so counsel can act efficiently.

In a group classroom, students might divide buyer, seller, escrow, and reviewer roles. Here you complete all four perspectives to make the distance exercise equivalent in substance. As buyer's agent, prepare the proposed choices. As seller's reviewer, identify what the proposal asks seller to do. As closing coordinator, identify which documents and funds are needed. As auditor, find contradictions. Do not reuse an earlier role's conclusions uncritically; challenge them against the source. The final work product is a corrected set of worksheets with an explanation of changes.

Before reading any feedback, write your own answer. After reading it, annotate each error with the governing provision and a corrected explanation. If your answer differs because the exercise lacks a fact, identify the missing fact and an appropriate source instead of assuming the model answer gives permission to fabricate. A correct unknown is a valuable result. The point of the workshop is to develop a dependable professional process that remains useful when real facts are incomplete.

### Statute reading exercise: Establish the complete transaction map

Read the official resale form in full, then the listed applicable addenda. Hypothetical sellers Arden and Briar Lee convey Lot 18, Block 3, Silver Wren Addition, City of Fort Worth, Tarrant County, Texas, known as 821 Silver Wren Lane, 76109, a 1974 detached resale home. Buyers Cassia Reed and Dorian Moss offer $450,000: $90,000 verified savings and $360,000 conventional financing. Both sides intend no natural-resource, residential, or fixture leases and no reservations; seller retains a named dining-room crystal chandelier. Buyer separately purchases a freestanding washer/dryer set for $900. Seller's disclosure notice is already delivered; available lead reports and the federal pamphlet are delivered before the buyer becomes obligated; buyer selects the lead inspection option. No well, pond/lake/tank, known surface-water permit, or severed/sold/leased groundwater rights exists; the property receives water only from the City of Fort Worth. Seller supplies that accurate paragraph 7I information. The property is subject to Silver Wren Property Owners Association. No statutory district/coastal/propane notice trigger is supplied.

Prepare an issue-to-form map for every fact. Identify which claims still would need verification in a live file and which attachments follow from the supplied assumptions. Read the forms' warnings and mark any instruction that prevents using a form for the wrong context. Do not fill the remaining negotiated fields until the next exercise supplies them.

Feedback: 20-19 fits the stipulated ownership/use; 40-11, 36-11, 56-0, and 57-0 address the supplied financing, association, lead, and personal-property subjects. Specify the chandelier exclusion. The cumulative supplied facts support the current water-disclosure no-delivery choice with the named utility; municipal service alone would not. No supplied facts authorize inventing leases, mineral reservations, or district notices, but a live file must verify them.

### Applied exercise: Complete the economic and delivery fields

For Silver Wren, the parties instruct a 30-year conventional first loan, maximum 6.6% annual rate for 30 years, maximum 1.5% origination charges, and a buyer-approval condition of 22 days. Earnest money is $5,500; option fee $350; option ten days. No additional earnest money is required initially. Escrow/title is Mockingbird Title, 100 Commerce Street, Fort Worth, Texas 76102. Seller pays the owner policy; survey exception is amended as the selected form allows at seller expense. Existing survey plus T-47/T-47.1 is due in seven days; seller pays for replacement if the timely package is rejected. Title objections: five days; intended use: single-family residence. Closing is November 23, 2026; possession at closing and funding. Buyer accepts as-is with no initial specific repair agreement. Seller ordinary buyer-expense cap is $4,500 and seller contribution toward buyer brokerage is $8,000. Buyer brokerage obligation under the separate agreement is $10,500. Residential-service reimbursement cap is $650. Association route A1: seller obtains/pays/delivers in twelve days; buyer C transfer-charge cap $700; seller pays D title-required information if D becomes applicable. All complete notice contacts will be supplied by the parties before presentation; none are invented here.

Create a field map for every supplied choice and identify every remaining unresolved blank. Calculate origination-charge dollar cap and the buyer's uncovered brokerage obligation. Reconcile the $900 personal-property price separately. Prepare an attachment inventory with signature and property-consistency checks, not real signatures.

Feedback: price balances $90,000 + $360,000; origination cap is $5,400; uncovered brokerage obligation is $2,500 on the supplied separate fee and contribution. The $4,500 ordinary expense cap cannot automatically be used for that brokerage gap. Keep the $900 additional personal-property consideration distinct. Obtain actual contact details and any still-missing principal instructions rather than substitute fake addresses in a live instrument.

### Applied exercise: Record final acceptance and build the first calendar

The parties complete the offer and all required attachments, with final unqualified acceptance communicated by the seller's agent to buyer's authorized agent on October 20, 2026, at 4:10 p.m. No earlier communication establishes acceptance. Escrow receives the contract October 21. Assume no relevant holiday changes the stated money delivery date. October 20 is Tuesday. Use the initial Silver Wren terms without any amendments yet.

Build a calendar for earnest/option delivery, option termination, existing survey package, buyer approval, association delivery, title commitment from escrow receipt, lead inspection, lead termination if its presence condition is met, and closing. For each state trigger, calculation, actor, evidence, and any clause-specific cutoff. Do not apply paragraph 5's weekend extension to every other deadline.

Feedback: effective date October 20; initial payments October 23; ten-day option October 30 at 5:00 p.m. local property time; survey package October 27; buyer approval November 11; association twelve-day delivery November 1; initial title commitment twenty days after title receipt November 10, subject to its actual extension rule; lead inspection October 30 and applicable fourteen-day notice November 3; closing November 23. The association and other calculations require their own actual wording and receipt records. A correct calendar identifies later receipt-based periods separately.

### Applied exercise: Handle new document receipts

In Silver Wren, seller timely supplies existing survey and declaration October 25, but lender rejects them October 27. Buyer receives title commitment November 2 and all exception documents November 4. A new acceptable survey arrives November 6. Buyer receives complete association information October 29, showing a previously unknown $1,200 special charge classified by the association and contract as a paragraph C transfer charge; the ordinary dues/proration amount is separate. No adverse material change after receipt is supplied.

Update the survey cost and ordering task, title-objection receipt trigger and deadline, association review deadline, and C charge allocation. Prepare the supporting evidence list and explain why the existing survey's preparation date is irrelevant to the new-survey receipt trigger. Do not treat the transfer charge as regular dues or use the condo seven-day period.

Feedback: seller pays replacement under the selected rejection choice. Final title component arrives November 6, so the five-day objection date is November 11, earlier than closing. Association three-day postreceipt point is November 1, subject to the form's earlier-closing limit. Buyer pays $700 of C charges and seller $500 excess. Actual statements and receipt evidence must support classifications and dates.

### Applied exercise: Negotiate repairs without losing the option

The October 27 inspection finds a leaking kitchen drain and a failed garage safety-reversal sensor. Buyer asks for both repairs, a price reduction to $447,000, and closing moved to November 25. Seller agrees only to the two repairs through qualified trades with permits if required and paragraph 7F documentation. On October 29 both parties sign and communicate acceptance of a current amendment containing only the agreed repairs; it does not change price, closing, or option. No lead hazard is found by the properly qualified lead inspector.

Prepare a requested-versus-accepted change table, repair evidence checklist, and updated calendar. Explain which original terms remain and why the repair amendment does not extend the option. Identify the buyer's remaining business decision before October 30 at 5:00 p.m. without recommending termination or promising the repair outcome. Record the lead result without falsely marking a lead-based termination right exercised.

Feedback: price remains $450,000; closing November 23; option October 30. Only the specific repairs became agreed obligations. Maintain the required provider/permit/documentation checks. A satisfactory lead result does not create the form's hazard-based termination fact. The buyer chooses whether to proceed or timely exercise an available right, with legal advice if needed; the agent should not invent an automatic extension while waiting for repairs.

### Applied exercise: Audit loan and closing figures

By November 10 the lender confirms buyer approval on the agreed terms; property approval is also established. For this simplified closing worksheet, eligible ordinary buyer expenses total $7,100; actual residential service plan costs $620; seller reimburses it separately under 7H within the $650 cap; buyer broker fee is $10,500 with $8,000 seller contribution; seller tax proration credit is $6,200; earnest money $5,500 and option fee $350 are fully credited. Loan proceeds remain $360,000. The $900 non-realty payment is paid separately outside this purchase cash worksheet and accurately disclosed. No other purchase charges/credits apply.

Calculate purchase cash needed at closing line by line, showing ordinary-expense contribution and service-plan reimbursement separately so the plan is not counted twice. Explain what the approval confirmations do and do not establish about closing/funding. Prepare a reconciliation request if the statement instead uses the entire $650 service cap despite actual cost $620.

Feedback: begin with $90,000 price cash; subtract $5,500 earnest and $350 option; add $2,600 uncovered ordinary expenses; add $2,500 remaining brokerage obligation; subtract $6,200 tax credit. The separately reimbursed $620 service plan nets to zero within this worksheet. Purchase cash is $83,050; the separate $900 personal-property payment is not silently included. Actual reimbursement is $620, not the cap. Approval is not evidence funds have already disbursed.

### Applied exercise: Final walkthrough and a new factual problem

At November 22 walkthrough, the drain is no longer leaking and seller supplies paid invoices from appropriately qualified providers plus any required permits and transferable warranty records for both repairs. The garage sensor still fails the inspector's operational check. Seller says the buyer must close because an invoice proves repair. Closing remains November 23; no extension or waiver has been signed. The buyer wants advice about the available choices.

Prepare a factual completion matrix distinguishing documents from observed function. Locate paragraph 7F's remedies/extension provision, identify who chooses and who supplies technical/legal advice, and draft an urgent buyer/broker status note. Explain why neither the invoice nor the agent's observation alone is a complete technical determination. Preserve the inspector's report and communications.

Feedback: documentation is necessary but does not erase the reported continuing failure. Obtain qualified clarification, communicate the actual status, and review the buyer's paragraph 7F choices, including the specified extension up to five days if necessary for completion or remedies under 15. The buyer's decision and any legal interpretation must be handled appropriately. Do not announce an automatic extension or pressure the buyer to sign a false completion acknowledgment.

### Applied exercise: Close, transfer access, and archive accurately

Assume the parties lawfully resolve the sensor issue with verified completion before closing; no date change is needed. They sign and fund November 23. Possession is delivered the same afternoon with the required property condition. Seller provides all keys and smart-device access and confirms seller's personal connections are removed. Escrow supplies final statement and receipts. In February, the actual tax bill differs from the closing estimate, requiring a $240 adjustment under the agreed proration allocation. No release waives that obligation.

Create a closing/possession record and an archive index containing accepted contract, attachments, amendments, notices, receipt evidence, inspections, repair verification, disclosures, approval communications, settlement, and access-transfer confirmation. Add a post-closing proration follow-up entry. Explain why closing does not justify deleting superseded drafts or ignoring surviving obligations, and why credentials should be stored/transmitted securely rather than in a public shared folder.

Feedback: preserve the actual documentary sequence and distinguish final from superseded records. The $240 adjustment follows paragraph 13 and the actual allocation; do not assume every post-closing claim vanished. Record verified access transfer without exposing personal credentials unnecessarily. Retention and security follow applicable broker policies and law; a course archive index is not permission to destroy records prematurely.

### Applied exercise: Reviewer challenge and correction memorandum

An independent reviewer of your Silver Wren packet flags six statements: “The contract became effective when escrow received it”; “The $4,500 seller expense cap pays the buyer's broker gap”; “The seven-day condo review applies to the subdivision”; “The unsigned price reduction became binding during inspection”; “An invoice conclusively proves every repair”; and “The option-fee amount is refunded with earnest money whenever buyer terminates.”

Write a six-point correction memorandum. For each statement supply the accurate rule, pinpoint source, relevant Silver Wren fact, and a practical record correction. Identify any statement that lacks enough facts for a universal conclusion. Then reread your eight prior worksheets and mark any occurrence of the same misconception. This final reviewer pass must produce corrections, not merely a list of statements you recognize as wrong.

Feedback: use final acceptance October 20; separate 12A and 12B; use 36-11's actual three-day mechanism; only accepted repair changes occurred; repair completion needs the actual contractual/technical evidence; option fee and earnest money have distinct disposition rules and the applicable termination clause matters. Reject universal refund claims. The corrected packet should show the evidence behind each conclusion and preserve original records with transparent corrections.

### Applied exercise: Restore an audit trail after a communications error

This final quality-control exercise uses a separate post-closing training file, not a new Silver Wren contract event. The archived accepted price is $326,000 and the actual amendment changes only the closing date. A coordinator accidentally uploads an unaccepted $320,000 draft as the final contract and marks a lender email dated February 8 as received February 6. The original accepted document, actual amendment, and email headers remain available; no evidence supports the earlier receipt date. A reviewer has already relied on the mislabeled record. No party authorized a price change, and no legal conclusion about missed rights has been reached.

Prepare an incident correction note identifying each inaccurate field, its reliable source, who needs corrected information, and how the original and correction will remain auditable. Reconcile the correct price and amended closing without erasing the uploaded draft. List which reports or calendars must be recalculated from February 8 and which legal questions require prompt broker/counsel review rather than a backdated entry. Explain how access controls and version labels could prevent recurrence. Finally draft a factual notification to the reviewer that acknowledges the error without asserting that no one was affected.

Feedback: accepted price remains $326,000; only the accepted closing change belongs in the operative terms. Restore the actual February 8 receipt evidence and recheck every calculation that depended on it. Preserve the original records and transparent correction rather than deleting the discrepancy or changing metadata to make a deadline appear timely. Inform affected authorized participants accurately, route potential legal consequences promptly, and avoid an unsupported assurance that the error was harmless. Version control supports the substance of contract work: a correct rule applied to a false document or date still produces a wrong result.
