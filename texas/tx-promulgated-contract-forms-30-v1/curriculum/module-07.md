# Texas Promulgated Contract Forms — Module 07

Promulgated Addenda, Notices and Other Forms. These documents have different jobs: add terms, provide required information, exercise a right, record receipt, or settle obligations. A title does not supply authority. In every worksheet identify the underlying contract, current form, factual trigger, selected choices, required actor, deadline, and delivery evidence. Required reading uses TREC's official form pages linked in each unit and the current [rules](https://www.trec.texas.gov/agency-information/rules-and-laws/trec-rules).

## T07U1 — Addendum for Sale of Other Property by Buyer

Form [10-6](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/addendum-sale-other-property-buyer) makes the purchase contingent on buyer's receipt of proceeds from the identified other property by the stated date, which should not be later than the purchase closing date. Listing the other house, accepting an offer on it, or signing its closing documents is not the same as receiving its proceeds. If the contingency is not satisfied or waived by the date, the form states automatic termination and earnest-money refund.

The seller's acceptance of another written offer can initiate the stated waiver requirement. Seller must notify buyer both of acceptance and the demand to waive. Buyer waives by written notice and the specified additional earnest-money deposit, not by reassurance that another closing is likely. Time is expressly essential. If buyer waives and then fails to close solely because those proceeds are unavailable, the addendum describes default. A broker must not recommend waiver without understanding the buyer's alternate funds and lender position.

### Statute reading exercise: Read the entire sale contingency

Read 10-6 A–E. Buyer Adina needs proceeds from 90 Linden Walk by November 15; purchase closing is November 20. The waiver response blank is two days after seller notice and additional earnest money is $3,000. Seller accepts another written offer and gives the complete required notice November 2. Adina's existing buyer has merely signed loan documents; proceeds have not arrived. Adina sends a waiver email November 3 but does not deliver additional earnest money.

Create a chronology and identify the two waiver acts, the contingency's original date, and the deadline created by seller's notice. Explain whether the email alone meets paragraph C and what facts must be obtained immediately. Write a buyer explanation of the risk of waiving without replacement funds. Do not treat the other buyer's signed loan papers as received proceeds.

Feedback: paragraph C requires notice and additional deposit; one alone is incomplete. The stated two-day count leads to November 4, subject to the actual governing terms and notice facts. The original November 15 condition remains distinct from the accelerated waiver mechanism. The exercise calls for urgent factual review and appropriate advice, not an invented grace period or a promise that funds will arrive.

### Applied exercise: A seller notice that omits a required component

Seller Belen texts buyer Cyrus, “We accepted another offer today.” The accepted 10-6 requires the seller to notify buyer of acceptance and that seller requires waiver. The message says nothing about requiring waiver. Cyrus's agent immediately calendarizes the waiver deadline and tells him he must waive that night. The actual addendum specifies three days, not hours. No other notice exists.

Prepare an evidence-based notice review, identify both missing/incorrect assumptions, and draft a factual clarification request. Explain why agents should not decide a contested notice's legal sufficiency from a casual message. List the buyer's funding information and instructions needed if a compliant waiver demand is later received. Preserve the original text without rewriting it as a complete notice.

Feedback: the supplied message does not expressly include the demand component described in B, and the response period was misstated. Obtain prompt clarification and legal review if rights are disputed. Never manufacture a deadline to pressure a waiver. If proper notice arrives, analyze the actual three-day period and both waiver requirements from the accepted form.

## T07U2 — Addendum for Back-Up Contract

The current [11-9](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/addendum-back-contract), effective July 1, 2026, has specific additional earnest-money and additional-option-fee provisions. The backup contract is binding upon execution; original earnest money and option fee are due under its paragraph 5. Other performance is generally deferred as the addendum states while the first-contract termination condition remains. Do not tell a backup buyer that nothing is binding and no deposit is due until first position.

The amended effective date for performance is the date seller delivers notice of first-contract termination to buyer. It is not necessarily the day the first contract actually terminated. The new [Seller's Notice to Buyer of Removal of Contingency, 62-0](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/sellers-notice-buyer-removal-contingency-under-addendum-back-contract) provides a current notice tool. The backup addendum also has a deadline for first-contract termination, and states an amendment of the first contract does not terminate it. Preserve both contracts' distinct dates and records.

Additional payments after amended effective date have their own fields and extension/allocation consequences. Received additional money first satisfies the additional option fee, then additional earnest money. A missed additional option fee affects the unrestricted termination right; late additional earnest money invokes the stated seller notice-before-delivery remedy. If buyer has an unrestricted termination right, paragraph J describes its operation from original effective date through amended effective date and expiration. Read this provision with the actual paid fees and option period.

### Applied exercise: Build a backup calendar

Backup buyer Dena and seller Emil execute October 3. Original earnest money $4,000 and option fee $200 are paid timely. The first-contract termination deadline is October 25. First contract terminates October 14; seller delivers notice to Dena October 16. Backup addendum A(2) requires $2,000 additional earnest money and $150 additional option fee within three days of amended effective date. No holiday affects the relevant date. Closing will follow the actual contract terms; do not invent a new closing date.

Create original-date and amended-date tracks, calculate the additional-payment deadline, and state how a $2,000 combined payment would allocate. Identify what is still missing if only that amount arrives. Explain why October 14 is not the amended effective date on the supplied facts and why the original payments were due before first position.

Feedback: amended effective date is October 16 delivery; three days leads to October 19. A $2,000 combined additional payment first pays $150 option fee and $1,850 additional earnest money, leaving $150 earnest money short. Original payment duties arise as stated even while backup. Maintain actual evidence of notice delivery and check the entire contract for performance dates affected by the amended date.

## T07U3 — Addendum for Reservation of Oil, Gas & Other Minerals

Form [44-3](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/addendum-reservation-oil-gas-and-other-minerals-0) is used when seller reserves all or a portion of seller's mineral estate. Its definition includes specified minerals and associated royalty, executive, access, exploration/development, production, payment, and related rights. It expressly excludes water and listed surface materials from the defined estate, while addressing reasonable use of surface materials for mineral activity. Do not treat it as a general water-right reservation form.

The reservation percentage or fraction applies to the mineral estate seller owns, not necessarily the entire mineral estate beneath the land. Reserving half of seller's one-quarter interest retains one-eighth of the whole. The separate surface-right waiver choice affects seller's rights described in the form; it does not waive rights belonging to other mineral owners or lessees who are not parties. Failure to complete that choice has a stated deemed election. Precise ownership and legal effect call for an attorney with mineral expertise.

### Statute reading exercise: Trace ownership and surface choices

Read 44-3 A–D and its notices. Title counsel confirms seller Farid owns a one-half undivided mineral interest. Farid wishes to reserve one-half of the interest he owns and waive the stated access/reasonable-use rights. An existing mineral lessee is not a party. Seller knows that lessee's contact information. No other reservation exists.

Calculate the fraction of the entire estate retained and conveyed by Farid, map the reservation and surface-waiver selections, and identify the contact-information duty. Explain why Farid's waiver does not eliminate the lessee's rights and why the form cannot establish title ownership on its own. Add a separate warning against inserting groundwater into this mineral definition.

Feedback: Farid retains one-quarter of the whole and conveys his other one-quarter, on the supplied title determination. Choose the partial reservation and stated waiver from actual instructions. Because he does not reserve all his interest, paragraph D calls for known existing-lessee contact information within seven days. The nonparty lessee's rights are unaffected by seller's election. Water is excluded from this form's defined Mineral Estate.

### Applied exercise: An unsupported “all minerals” claim

Seller Gemma says “I own all minerals because my parents owned the land,” but an old deed excepts a mineral interest of uncertain extent. Buyer Hale asks the agent to guarantee that a seller surface waiver prevents drilling anywhere on the parcel. The offer is unsigned and no mineral title opinion exists.

Prepare a missing-title-evidence list, direct questions for mineral counsel, and a factual buyer explanation. Identify why neither the seller's family history nor a form checkbox establishes complete ownership or binds nonparties. Propose the next administrative step for obtaining documents and instructions without drafting a bespoke severance or guaranteeing that drilling cannot occur.

Feedback: obtain deeds, reservations, leases, and qualified title analysis. The agent may identify the uncertainty and appropriate current addendum, but cannot guarantee the mineral estate or a nonparty's conduct. A seller waiver has the form's limited scope. The correct workflow makes the actual interest reviewable before the parties agree to a reservation.

## T07U4 — Buyers Termination of Contract

The current [Notice of Buyer's Termination of Contract, 38-8](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/notice-buyers-termination-contract) records the buyer's exercise of an identified termination basis. The form is not a universal cancellation entitlement. Check the actual accepted agreement and the facts before selecting a reason. Some provisions require supporting documents, such as lender reasons or an environmental report, in addition to notice. A bare notice may leave the requirement incomplete.

Preserve buyer's instruction, correct parties/property, chosen basis, relevant deadlines, attachments, and delivery evidence. Use paragraph 21 and any specific notice requirement; escrow objections have a separate receipt rule. Do not select a familiar option reason after the option expired simply because another valid right might exist. If a new current-contract right is not a printed checkbox, follow the applicable form and legal advice rather than inventing a false checked reason.

### Applied exercise: Notice package quality review

Buyer Inez has a valid lender property-disapproval statement under an applicable 40-11 paragraph 2B. Closing is November 30 and buyer instructs termination November 26. The statement specifically identifies unacceptable insurability. The draft notice incorrectly checks unrestricted option, omits the lender statement, and is addressed only to an assistant not identified as the party or agent in the agreement. The option expired two weeks earlier.

Write a correction checklist for basis, deadline, supporting document, recipient, property, signatures/instructions, and proof. Calculate the ordinary third-day-before-closing point and explain why the supplied November 26 instruction still requires proper delivery. Draft a factual money-status note distinguishing contractual refund entitlement from escrow's release process.

Feedback: use the actual property-approval basis and required lender statement, with notice under the agreement by the applicable November 27 point. An internal instruction is not completed delivery. Correct recipient and evidence; do not rely on the expired option checkbox. Proper termination may call for earnest-money refund, while escrow follows its separate release/demand procedures.

## T07U5 — Mutual Termination of Contract

The outline's “Mutual Termination” is a legal event and document function, not proof that TREC currently publishes a mandatory form with that title. The current inventory must be consulted. Parties may agree to terminate and settle their rights using a suitable authorized instrument, with counsel where legal drafting or disputed claims are involved. The agent must not compose a settlement, release of claims, or new indemnity in special provisions.

Separate agreement to end performance from agreement about money, expenses, claims, and escrow's release. A release can do more than return a deposit; read the actual document and advise direct counsel review when appropriate. One party's proposed release is not mutual consent. A unilateral termination based on an existing right is different and should not be misdescribed as requiring mutual permission merely because escrow seeks release paperwork.

### Applied exercise: What has actually been agreed?

Buyer Jory and seller Kesia each email that they want to end the purchase. Jory wants the $7,000 deposit returned; Kesia demands $2,000 for alleged losses. Escrow requests an agreed release. An agent drafts “all claims waived forever” and tells Jory to sign because it is merely an administrative receipt. No party has agreed to the money split or claim waiver.

Prepare an agreement-status table for future performance, earnest money, expenses, claims, and escrow instructions. Identify the unauthorized drafting concern and draft a neutral next-step message requesting direct legal review and an appropriate agreed instrument. Explain why the desire to end the deal does not prove consent to every release term.

Feedback: ending performance may be a shared objective, but money and claims remain unresolved. A broad claim waiver is substantive legal language, not an administrative receipt. Counsel should help the principals resolve and document their actual agreement; escrow then follows valid instructions and governing procedures. Do not infer consent from pressure to close the file quickly.

## T07U6 — Addendum for Property Located Seaward of the Gulf Intracoastal Waterway, Addendum for Coastal Area Property

The [seaward addendum 34-4](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/addendum-property-located-seaward-gulf-intercoastal-waterway) and [coastal-area addendum 33-2](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/addendum-coastal-area-property) address different statutory geographic triggers and notices. Read Texas Natural Resources Code §§61.025 and 33.135 and the actual forms. One concerns property seaward of the Gulf Intracoastal Waterway and coastal/public-beach issues; the other concerns property adjoining or sharing a boundary with tidally influenced state waters. A view of the sea does not, by itself, establish either trigger.

The notices warn of matters such as public rights, shifting boundaries, erosion, and regulatory limitations. They are not a survey, building permit, flood-insurance determination, or guarantee of permanent private beachfront. Identify location from reliable maps/survey and appropriate authorities. A property may require multiple applicable notices; do not choose one merely because its title sounds broader. Missing required notices may have legal consequences that need direct advice.

### Applied exercise: Match geography to notice work

File A is stipulated seaward of the Gulf Intracoastal Waterway. File B is stipulated landward of that waterway but shares a boundary with tidally influenced state waters. File C has an inland lake view with no tidal influence. No other geographic trigger is established. Buyers in all files ask whether the lot line can ever move.

Read the two current forms and statutory trigger sections. Prepare a notice-selection and verification chart for A–C, list additional location facts needed for any overlapping requirement, and draft a factual explanation of why a notice cannot guarantee permanent boundaries. Identify survey, coastal authority, insurer, and attorney roles without declaring a lot buildable or unbuildable.

Feedback: analyze A under the seaward trigger and B under the coastal-area trigger; C's lake view alone does not satisfy these supplied triggers. Additional facts may establish overlapping notices for A. The forms disclose risks and legal context; qualified survey and legal review determine boundary and public-right issues. The buyer's permanent-boundary question cannot be answered by checking a form box.

## T07U7 — Addendum for Property Subject to Mandatory Membership in an Owner's Association

Current [36-11](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/addendum-property-subject-mandatory-membership-property-owners-association) is not for condominiums. It defines Subdivision Information to include restrictions, bylaws, rules, and resale certificate under Property Code §207.003. It offers four distinct delivery/receipt choices: seller obtains and delivers; buyer obtains; buyer already received/approved with an updated-certificate choice; or buyer does not require delivery. Each has different timing and consequences. Do not check “received” because the listing includes an annual-dues number.

Under seller-delivery A1, the form supplies the stated three-day postreceipt or preclosing termination choice and a nondelivery remedy. Under buyer-obtains A2, deemed receipt can occur at the specified date even if buyer delays ordering. A3's requested updated certificate has its own ten-day delivery period after seller receives buyer's payment. Material changes in B have separate notice/termination treatment. Read the actual selection rather than applying the seven-day condominium rule to this form.

Paragraph C caps buyer's specified association transfer fees/deposits/reserves, with seller paying excess, and excludes regular prorated periodic charges and A/D costs. Paragraph D addresses cost of title-required information when buyer does not require the information/update. Current wording requires payment before title orders information. Association repair responsibility is another issue: seller cannot promise to compel an association repair without verifying authority and agreement.

### Statute reading exercise: Association documents and fee buckets

Read 36-11 A–D and [Property Code §207.003](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/PR/htm/PR.207.htm#207.003). Select A1: seller obtains/pays/delivers in ten days. Buyer receives complete information October 12; closing is October 30. Paragraph C buyer cap is $700. Association transfer-related charges within C total $950; regular quarterly dues prorated under paragraph 13 are $300; the A1 resale-package cost is $375.

Create three cost buckets and allocate each from the stated selections. Calculate the postreceipt decision date and identify what would change under A2's deemed-receipt rule. Explain why the $300 regular dues and $375 package cannot be thrown into C to exhaust the cap. Draft a delivery-and-fee inquiry to the closing professional.

Feedback: buyer pays up to $700 of C charges and seller $250 excess; regular dues follow proration rather than C; seller pays the A1 package. Three days after October 12 is October 15, subject to earlier closing if applicable. A2 changes receipt analysis by its deemed-date language. Accurate cost classification precedes arithmetic.

### Applied exercise: A material association change

Buyer Livia received subdivision information showing no special assessment and chose A3 without an update. Before closing, seller learns the association approved a major new assessment and prohibited the buyer's intended short-term rental use. Seller asks the agent to keep quiet because the buyer already approved the papers. Assume these are material adverse changes, not merely rumors, and no waiver changes paragraph B.

Prepare a change-notice record identifying source, date learned, corrected information, buyer delivery, and applicable rights to review. Explain why prior approval does not erase paragraph B. Identify any association repair or lender questions arising from the new facts and route disputed legal effects to counsel. Do not silently replace the old certificate in the record and conceal the change history.

Feedback: seller must promptly notify buyer of known material changes under B; the stated adverse-change right requires the actual provision and preclosing notice. Prior A3 approval is not a blanket waiver of later B facts. Preserve both versions, disclose accurately, and let buyer obtain advice and decide under the agreement. An agent must not assist concealment to protect the closing.

## T07U8 — Short Sale Addendum

Form [45-2](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/short-sale-addendum-0) defines its short-sale arrangement with specific lender consent and agreement: consent to the sale, acceptance of seller's net proceeds in full satisfaction of seller liability, and a recordable lien release. A lender letter merely permitting release of its lien while retaining a deficiency may not satisfy that agreed definition. Seller needs direct legal and tax advice about actual liability and consequences. Never promise forgiven debt or harmless credit effects.

The contract is binding upon execution and original earnest money/option fee are payable as required. Seller promptly applies and makes reasonable efforts to obtain the defined agreement, supplying required documents. Other performance is deferred as stated. Seller's notice of obtaining consent/agreement changes the effective date for performance; refusal or withdrawal before closing/funding has the stated termination consequence. If there is more than one secured loan or lienholder, the addendum applies to each. One approval is not all approvals.

### Applied exercise: Read the lender letter for what it approves

Seller Malik has a first mortgage and a second lien. First lender's letter accepts a proposed payment to release its lien but expressly reserves the right to collect the remaining debt. The second lienholder has not responded. The accepted 45-2 requires its defined Consent and Agreement by November 20. Buyer Nora paid earnest money and option fee timely and asks whether the sale is fully approved.

Create a lender-by-lender matrix for sale consent, full satisfaction of liability, recordable lien release, conditions, and status. Explain the first letter's mismatch with A2 and the missing second response. Draft a factual status message and legal/tax referral without predicting forgiveness or credit impact. Identify the deadline and notice evidence required before using an amended effective date.

Feedback: the supplied first letter does not establish every component of the addendum's defined agreement; its reserved deficiency is material. The second approval remains absent. Do not announce full approval or reset dates based on an incomplete letter. Seller's counsel and lenders must resolve terms, and buyer receives accurate status under the actual deadline and notice provisions.

## T07U9 — Noncontract Forms:

The outline places three untimed subentries within this thirty-minute unit: Promulgated Resale Certificates, Consumer Information Form, and Information About Brokerage Services Form. Their function is information, not a sale promise. A resale certificate reports association information supplied under its statutory framework. Use [Condominium Resale Certificate 32-5](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/condominium-resale-certificate-0) for its context and [Subdivision Information/Resale Certificate 37-5](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/subdivision-information-including-resale-certificate-property-subject-mandatory-membership) for its context. An agent must not invent the association's financial, insurance, litigation, or assessment answers.

The Consumer Protection Notice under §531.18 tells consumers about regulation and complaint resources and has display/link requirements. It is not a contract requiring the consumer to retain a broker, and a posted copy does not replace transaction-specific disclosures. The Information About Brokerage Services notice under §531.20 and §1101.558 explains brokerage relationships and duties and has its own delivery/link requirements. Current IABS 1-2 reflects current Texas law. Neither notice is a substitute for §1101.563's required residential buyer agreement.

Disclose whom the license holder represents as the statute requires at first contact with the other party, and provide the written notice at the applicable substantive-dialogue trigger with its stated exceptions. A conversation restricted to scheduling differs from advising about a specific transaction, but a website link alone does not automatically prove every transaction delivery duty was satisfied. Record how, when, and to whom the required notice was furnished. The broker's contact details must be accurate.

### Statute reading exercise: Notices without accidental agency

Read §§531.18 and 531.20, Occupations Code §1101.558, and current IABS. Prospect Orin emails a broker merely to ask open-house hours; later he calls for advice about how much to offer on that specific home. The broker represents the seller. Prospect Pia instead contacts an independent buyer broker for representation and a private residential showing. Neither has signed a representation agreement, and the only prior material is the firm's homepage footer link.

Prepare a contact-stage matrix identifying representation disclosure, IABS delivery analysis, consumer-notice display role, and the separate buyer-agreement requirement where applicable. Explain what each document establishes and does not establish. Record a compliant factual workflow for both prospects without silently converting the seller's broker into Orin's buyer agent.

Feedback: the broker must make seller representation clear to Orin; substantive discussion triggers the applicable IABS analysis, with actual statutory exceptions considered. Pia's buyer-service path requires the appropriate agreement before the residential showing. A homepage link is relevant to website duties but not a universal substitute for the actual conversation delivery requirement. None of the informational notices alone creates buyer representation.

### Applied exercise: Audit two resale certificates

Association staff supplies a condominium certificate for a fee-simple subdivision lot, leaves the litigation field blank, and reports the seller's monthly payment rather than the association's regular assessment schedule. Buyer Quinn has a 36-11 seller-delivery agreement. The seller's agent proposes to fill “none” for litigation and use the condominium certificate because it looks official.

Identify the wrong-context form, missing authorized information, and delivery-completeness concern. Prepare an association correction request and buyer status note. Explain why the agent cannot certify “none” from silence and why receiving a document with a TREC logo does not prove the subdivision information package is complete. Keep the actual receipt history intact while corrected materials are requested.

Feedback: obtain the appropriate subdivision information/certificate from the responsible association source. The agent does not know that litigation is absent and must not invent an answer. Correct form context and complete required information are necessary; 36-11 includes restrictions/bylaws/rules as well as certificate. Counsel should address disputed deadline consequences if documents were deficient.

## T07U10 — Non-Realty Items

Current voluntary [57-0](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/non-realty-items-addendum) concerns additional personal property not already included by the contract's property paragraph. Identify each item carefully, its agreed additional consideration, seller ownership free of encumbrances, and the form's absence of a condition/future-performance warranty. A leased item cannot truthfully be represented as owned free of encumbrances. The short official unit reinforces the earlier field work; it is not a second credit for the same exercise.

As a quick self-check, explain why a financed refrigerator subject to an unreleased security interest requires investigation before seller signs an ownership representation. State why a used appliance's separate conveyance does not guarantee future operation. This check is part of teaching prose, not a separately timed activity.

## T07U11 — Case Study/Workshop

An addendum packet is a system of selected conditions, not a stack of standard attachments. Start with the base contract, then identify exactly what each attachment changes or adds. A backup condition postpones specified performance but retains original money duties. A sale-of-other-property condition requires the buyer's actual proceeds or proper waiver, not merely another signed sale agreement. An association addendum has its own document and cost routes. A mineral reservation concerns the seller's owned estate and selected rights; it cannot establish ownership that title evidence does not support.

Use two linked records for this workshop. The attachment inventory records form version, property identification, principal selections, consideration, signatures, and consistency with the base agreement. The event ledger records original acceptance, document delivery, waiver demand, required money, termination of another contract, seller notice, amended effective date, and any later notice. Linking the records lets a reviewer trace each calculated date back to the document that creates it. An attachment checkbox alone cannot prove that its missing terms were agreed.

When several conditions interact, identify the order without assuming that one event satisfies another. Receipt of a seller's backup notice may change a date but does not itself pay additional money. A buyer's waiver message may express intent but does not prove the additional earnest money was delivered. A termination can end a purchase duty without resolving a contested escrow distribution. The following exercises require separate findings for each step. If a fact is missing, state precisely what record or authorized decision is needed instead of inventing a convenient event.

### Applied exercise: Assemble an association and sale-contingency package

Buyers Rafi and Sonia offer $390,000 on seller Teo's fee-simple home in Aspen Gate Association. They need proceeds from 714 Morning Dove Road by November 25; purchase closing is November 30. They choose a three-day waiver response after a complete seller notice and $4,000 additional earnest money to waive. Association information is not received; seller will obtain/pay/deliver it in twelve days. Buyer transfer-fee cap is $600. No mineral reservation, coastal issue, or short sale is known.

Prepare field maps for 10-6 and 36-11, identify the actual association name/contact still needed, and build distinct sale/waiver/document-review/fee ledgers. Explain why the buyer's sale contingency date should not be later than the purchase closing. List the attachments and notice evidence required before presenting a complete offer.

Feedback: use the specified dates, response days, additional deposit, seller-delivery route, and $600 C cap. Identify incomplete association contact/data rather than inventing it. The receipt of sale proceeds, complete waiver, association information, and closing are distinct events. The two addenda do different jobs; one does not cure a missing condition in the other.

### Applied exercise: Advance the backup without inventing termination

Seller Uma has an accepted first contract and an accepted 11-9 backup with buyer Vale. The first buyer requests an amendment extending closing ten days; seller accepts that amendment. Vale's agent says this makes the original contract “different” and therefore terminated, immediately declares the backup in first position, and invoices the additional option fee. No first-contract termination or seller notice to Vale has occurred.

Use 11-9 G–J to prepare a status memorandum, identify the erroneous inference, and list evidence needed to establish an amended effective date. Draft a correction to the premature fee demand without suggesting original backup payments were unnecessary. Explain what to monitor as the first-contract-termination deadline approaches.

Feedback: paragraph I expressly says amendment/modification does not terminate the first contract. No supplied termination or delivered notice establishes first position or amended effective date. Original backup payment duties remain as stated, while additional-payment triggers require the actual amended date. Monitor the first-contract deadline and authentic notice, not rumor or changes in the primary bargain.

### Applied exercise: Review three attempted shortcuts

In separate files: Willa uses the mineral addendum to “reserve all well water”; Xander sends a short-sale approval letter that releases only one of two liens; and Yvonne replaces a required seaward notice with a map screenshot and the phrase “buyer aware of beach.” The offers are not yet signed, and no professional opinion resolves the missing issues.

Write three mini-memos identifying document function, missing fact or authority, appropriate official source, and next professional referral. Explain why each shortcut fails even though it mentions the right general subject. Produce a corrected package checklist for each without composing legal reservation or waiver language.

Feedback: 44-3's mineral definition excludes water; short-sale requirements apply to every relevant lienholder and include defined liability/release terms; required coastal notice language cannot be replaced by a casual map annotation. Match documents to actual legal function. The agent can organize and explain informational choices but should not invent substitute legal protections.

### Applied exercise: A termination and release handoff

Buyer Zora properly terminates under the stipulated applicable 36-11 material-adverse-change provision before closing, with written notice delivered as required. Escrow holds $4,800 earnest money and asks for release paperwork. Seller agrees the transaction has ended but disputes $300 of authorized escrow expenses. No settlement or order resolves the expense dispute, and the agent wants to mark the buyer paid in full immediately.

Prepare separate records for contract status, termination evidence, gross deposit, expense issue, release status, and actual disbursement. Explain why contractual termination can be established while money accounting remains unfinished. Draft a neutral handoff to escrow and counsel without deciding the expense dispute or falsifying the receipt record.

Feedback: record termination and delivery truthfully, keep the $4,800 held amount distinct from disputed expense deductions, and follow paragraph 18 and appropriate legal resolution. A completed termination notice is not proof of a completed bank transfer. The learning goal is a coherent file that does not conflate ending a contract, releasing escrow, and settling claims.

### Module recap

Sort every document studied into its actual function and explain its trigger. Review your source maps and independent worksheets for confused pairs: sale contingency and backup; assumption release and short-sale satisfaction; mineral and water rights; condominium and subdivision certificate; IABS and representation agreement; termination and release. Correct each error against the actual form before the topic quiz. Current form names and numbers matter, but the controlling task is using the correct instrument for the actual facts.
