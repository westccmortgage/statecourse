# Texas Promulgated Contract Forms — Module 01

Contract Law Overview. This module supplies the vocabulary needed to read a Texas sale contract without treating a form as a guarantee. Work on a saved, unexecuted copy of the current [One to Four Family Residential Contract (Resale), 20-19](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/one-four-family-residential-contract-resale). Every person, property, communication, and transaction in an exercise is hypothetical. Do not submit these exercises to a title company or obtain real signatures. Your written answers and corrections are the work product; looking at a model answer is not completion.

All teaching, source reviews, applied exercises, and the recap below are required. Additional practice after the optional heading is not required and is not assessed. Exercise time includes reading that exercise, making the required decisions independently, checking feedback, and correcting the work. It does not also count as first-pass teaching prose.

## T01U1 — Elements of a Valid Contract

A contract is an enforceable agreement. In a typical sale the buyer promises payment and the seller promises conveyance, with many additional promises about title, property condition, access, closing, and documents. Competent parties, lawful purpose, mutual assent, consideration, and sufficiently definite terms are the basic checkpoints. The writing must also satisfy the requirements that apply to this kind of transaction. A useful checkpoint is a question the file can answer, not a slogan: who is buying; who owns and can convey; what property is included; what each promises; and where the final agreement is recorded.

Mutual assent concerns the same terms. A buyer who signs an offer for $418,000 and a seller who changes it to $428,000 have not yet assented to the same bargain. A counteroffer calls for the other party's acceptance. Initials help document assent to changes; they do not cure an unaccepted change by themselves. The agent must identify the last offer, its exact attachments, the signatures, and communication of acceptance. An agent's confidence that the parties will agree is not evidence that they have agreed.

Consideration is the bargained exchange, not necessarily the earnest-money check. The exchange of enforceable promises can support a bilateral sale contract. Earnest money is a separate contractual performance requirement. It is dangerous to tell a seller there cannot be a contract until escrow receives money. Paragraph 5 of the current contract specifies consequences for late delivery; those consequences would be unnecessary if a contract never existed before delivery. The option fee serves another purpose and has its own consequences.

A legal description must identify the land sufficiently. A street address is useful for delivery and recognition, but the land blank calls for lot, block, addition, city, county, and an address or an attached description. A listing record is not a substitute for verified title information. Names require the same care: a person negotiating for an estate, trust, partnership, or company may not be the owner or the authorized signer. Obtain the appropriate evidence and refer legal-capacity or title questions to the principal's attorney and title professionals.

Texas Business & Commerce Code §26.01 requires a signed writing for specified transactions, including a contract for sale of real estate. The party against whom enforcement is sought, or a person lawfully authorized to sign for that party, matters. The electronic-transaction rules in Chapter 322 recognize electronic records and signatures when their requirements apply; they do not dispense with agreement, authority, or necessary terms. A typed name in an unrelated email should not be treated as universal proof of assent.

### Statute reading exercise: A writing that can be traced

Open [Texas Business & Commerce Code §26.01](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/BC/htm/BC.26.htm#26.01) and [§§322.005 and 322.007](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/BC/htm/BC.322.htm#322.007). Elena agrees to buy Omar's vacant residential lot for $91,000. They orally agree on price and closing. Omar emails a photograph of an unsigned form saying, “Here is what we discussed.” Elena signs the form electronically. Omar has not signed or otherwise communicated acceptance. Both have agreed to conduct this transaction electronically. No exception to the statute of frauds has been established.

Write four findings: the applicable category in §26.01(b); whose signature would matter in an action against Omar; what §322.007 does for an electronic signature; and what the electronic statute does not establish from these facts. Then draft a factual file note requesting the missing acceptance evidence, without announcing a legal outcome. Compare the actual statutory words with your note and revise any overstatement.

Feedback: a real-estate sale is within §26.01(b)(4). Elena's electronic signature can be recognized, but it is not Omar's signature or proof of his agreement. Electronic recognition solves a medium problem, not a missing-assent problem. The file note should state which version Elena signed and that Omar's acceptance remains unverified. Whether an unusual email, conduct, agency relationship, or exception supports enforcement is legal analysis for counsel. Do not label Omar bound merely because the form was sent from his address.

### Applied exercise: Build an elements checklist

Priya Shah and Luis Ortega offer $366,000 for Lot 7, Block C, Willow Mesa Addition, City of Denton, Denton County, Texas, known as 710 Willow Mesa Drive, 76201. The seller shown on the title report is Meadow Finch LLC. Its employee signs “Alex Green, sales coordinator.” The offer identifies only Priya as buyer, omits the lot and block, shows $73,200 cash and $292,800 financing, and has no seller acceptance. Luis will also take title and borrow. The LLC has supplied no authority documents.

Prepare a six-row intake chart identifying the intended parties, verified owner, unresolved authority, property information to reconcile, price arithmetic, and present offer status. For every unresolved item, name the person or document that can supply the answer. Write a two-sentence explanation to the buyer of why signing this draft would not repair the omissions. Do not invent an LLC resolution or sign for Luis.

Feedback: include both intended purchasers, verify the LLC's exact name and authorized signer, reconcile the property with title evidence, and retain the correct $366,000 sum. The employee's job title is not authority to convey. No seller acceptance is established. A corrected offer can be prepared from verified instructions; an agent cannot cure authority by adding “authorized” after a name. A complete checklist should distinguish information already known from information still needed.

## T01U2 — Valid/Void/Unenforceable/Voidable

These words answer different questions. A valid contract satisfies formation requirements. A void arrangement has no legal effect as a contract; an agreement to carry out an unlawful purpose illustrates the concern. A voidable contract is subject to avoidance by a party with a legally recognized ground, such as circumstances involving incapacity, fraud, or duress. It is not automatically a nullity. An unenforceable agreement may encounter a legal obstacle to enforcement, such as the applicable writing requirement, although the parties appeared to reach agreement.

Classification depends on facts and law. A typo is not automatically a void contract. A missed deadline is not automatically a void contract. A known defect in a house does not, by itself, classify the purchase agreement as voidable. Separate the alleged problem, evidence, controlling provision, and remedy. The agent can explain a printed choice and gather evidence, but should not pronounce a disputed contract invalid or advise a principal to disregard it. The course uses simplified hypotheticals to teach categories, not to create a checklist for declaring real transactions unenforceable.

Read the remedies in the contract as alternatives rather than labels of formation. Paragraph 15 addresses failures to comply with an existing contract. A party's termination under an existing right may end future performance without asserting that no contract ever existed. A release may settle money and claims. Neither should be confused with the formation question.

### Applied exercise: Categorize without overclaiming

Consider four independent files. A: competent adults sign and accept a lawful $240,000 residential purchase with definite terms. B: parties agree that payment is solely for an unlawful transfer, and counsel determines the agreement void for illegality. C: a purchaser alleges coercion and counsel identifies a potential right to avoid the contract, but no resolution has occurred. D: a real-estate sale agreement is entirely oral and no applicable exception to the writing requirement is established.

Make a chart with the most appropriate teaching category, the fact supporting it, an uncertainty to preserve, and a safe next administrative step. Then explain why “the seller missed closing” cannot be inserted as a fifth example of “void” without further analysis. Prepare a client-facing two-sentence distinction between a formation defect and failure to perform a formed agreement.

Feedback: A illustrates valid; B void on the expressly supplied legal determination; C potentially voidable, not conclusively avoided; D a potential unenforceability problem. Missing closing typically directs attention to the existing agreement and remedies, not erasure of formation. Preserve counsel's role in evaluating exceptions and contested facts. A good answer does not promise that the oral agreement can never matter under any circumstances.

## T01U3 — Executed and Executory Contracts

The word executed has two common uses. A document is often called executed when signed. In a performance classification, an executed contract is one whose required performance has been completed; an executory contract still has obligations remaining. Ask which meaning a speaker intends. In the signature block of Form 20-19, the execution date is the effective date of final acceptance, not a representation that all closing duties have been performed.

After acceptance a sale agreement generally remains executory through inspections, document delivery, financing, and closing. Signing a closing packet does not necessarily mean funding has occurred. A transaction may close while specified obligations survive: the tax-proration adjustment under paragraph 13 is a practical example. “Closed” is therefore not a reason to destroy records or ignore an express surviving obligation.

The contract and deed perform different functions. The contract establishes the promised transaction and conditions; the deed conveys the interest described when properly executed and delivered. The broker should not draft a deed or give an opinion on its legal sufficiency. Track what must be delivered and identify the professionals preparing it. A receipt from escrow proves receipt of the identified payment, not that every duty in the sale agreement is complete.

### Applied exercise: A status report with four timestamps

On October 1, the parties finally accept a resale contract. On October 3, escrow receives the earnest money and option fee. On October 29, each party signs the closing documents. The lender disburses on October 30 and escrow confirms funding that afternoon. Possession is due at closing and funding. On February 2, the parties receive the current-year tax statement showing the estimate used at closing was $640 too low; paragraph 13 requires an adjustment.

Draw a five-event timeline. For each event state what it establishes and one thing it does not establish. Write the possession instruction for October 29 and identify the post-closing obligation on February 2. Finish by rewriting this inaccurate file note: “Executed October 1; everything is performed; release keys October 29 because they signed.”

Feedback: acceptance establishes the contract's effective date; escrow receipt establishes identified delivery; signing does not establish funding; confirmed closing and funding trigger the selected possession provision; the tax reconciliation remains to be handled under the contract. Correct the note to distinguish signed, accepted, closed, funded, and performed. The problem does not give a temporary lease, so one cannot be assumed.

### Applied exercise: Outstanding promises after acceptance

For a second purchase, assume final acceptance on November 3, closing November 30, and an option period ending November 10. The seller must supply the existing survey and affidavit in seven days and repair a named broken bedroom window before closing. The buyer must apply for financing promptly under the attached financing addendum. On November 7 the survey is delivered without its affidavit, the window remains broken, and the buyer has not applied. No party has given a termination notice.

Produce a performance tracker with columns for actor, promise, trigger, due point, current evidence, and required follow-up. Identify which obligations are not yet overdue on the given facts and which require investigation now. Explain why incomplete performance on November 7 does not allow an agent to declare the whole agreement “unexecuted and therefore nonexistent.” State what additional financing facts are needed before advising any remedy.

Feedback: the survey package is incomplete even though part arrived, but its seven-day delivery period has not yet ended; the repair deadline is closing; the financing application obligation calls for prompt attention. The agreement already exists on the stipulated facts. Use the actual addendum and counsel for disputed consequences. The tracker records facts and deadlines; it does not invent a grace period or a right to terminate.

## T01U4 — Bilateral vs. Unilateral

In a bilateral agreement, a promise is exchanged for a promise. The standard resale sale agreement expresses reciprocal promises in paragraph 1. Neither party can simply change its mind without an applicable right or consequence. In a unilateral arrangement, acceptance is invited through performance rather than a return promise. Classification is about how the obligation is undertaken, not the number of people signing a paper.

An option must be read with particular care. An option can give a party a right to act within a specified period without requiring that action. But the termination option in paragraph 5 exists inside an otherwise bilateral purchase contract. It permits the buyer to terminate under its terms; it does not change every obligation in the sale into an optional promise. A buyer who has paid for a termination option still must handle notices correctly and may still owe other performance until the contract is terminated.

A buyer's right and a buyer's promise may appear in adjoining sentences. Circle operative verbs: must, shall, may, and may terminate. The distinction helps a student identify a duty, permission, or remedial election. The word may is not a universal escape hatch: it is linked to the facts, timing, and notice described in the specific provision.

### Applied exercise: Separate promises from permissions

Use Form 20-19 paragraphs 1, 5A, 5B, 7A, and 9B. Hypothetical buyer Jonah agrees to buy for $305,000, promises $3,000 earnest money and a $250 option fee, receives a seven-day option period, and chooses whether to hire an inspector. The seller promises access and existing utilities; closing is set for December 10. Both have accepted the same agreement.

Create an eight-entry list of promises, permissions, and conditioned rights from these paragraphs. For each entry identify its actor and the form language that supports your category. Explain why Jonah's choice not to inspect does not erase his promise to close. Then write a plain-language explanation of the relationship between the bilateral sale and the termination option.

Feedback: the reciprocal purchase and conveyance promises, money delivery, access/utilities, and closing performance are duties under the identified provisions. Inspection is a permitted buyer action; option termination is a right conditioned on its requirements. The classification exercise should never imply that the buyer must terminate or must order an inspection. Choosing not to inspect is not equivalent to exercising the option.

## T01U5 — Reasonable Time vs. Time is of the Essence

A reasonable-time standard depends on context; a time-is-of-the-essence provision emphasizes strict compliance. Do not use one general “Texas deadline rule” across the contract. Paragraph 5 expressly makes time essential and supplies a weekend and legal-holiday extension for specified money deliveries. Its option notice deadline is 5:00 p.m. local time where the property is located. That money-delivery extension does not silently extend the option period itself.

Dates can run from final acceptance, actual receipt of a document, title-company receipt, or another event. Record the trigger before counting. Count days after the trigger rather than treating the trigger as day one where the form says “after.” A calendar should show both the calculated deadline and its supporting evidence: acceptance email, delivery receipt, or notice. If a required fact is unknown, mark the deadline unresolved and obtain evidence urgently. Guessing creates an appearance of certainty without protection.

Time calculations do not authorize the agent to decide a disputed legal question. The important operational response is early performance, verified notice channels, and prompt advice to consult counsel if rights may have expired. Do not wait for the final afternoon to test an unfamiliar electronic delivery method.

### Applied exercise: Two deadlines, two rules

Assume final acceptance on Friday, October 2, 2026. The option period is nine days after the effective date; the option fee is $300 and earnest money $5,000. No relevant legal holiday affects these dates. Buyer plans to deliver both payments and wishes to preserve the option. October 5 is Monday, October 11 is Sunday, and October 12 is Monday. A colleague says both deadlines move to October 12 because the option ends Sunday.

Use paragraph 5 to create a day-by-day count for the delivery period and the option period. State the recipient and payee for the money, the time and locality for option notice, and whether the colleague's extension applies. Draft a factual reminder with both deadlines and a plan to obtain receipts. Explain what would change if the last money-delivery day, instead, fell on a qualifying holiday.

Feedback: the three-day payment delivery deadline is October 5. The nine-day option period ends October 11 at 5:00 p.m. local time at the property; the money-delivery extension does not move that option deadline. Money is delivered to the named escrow agent and payable to escrow. A qualifying holiday on the money deadline would activate the express payment extension. The hypothetical supplies the weekday and holiday facts; independently check the actual calendar and statutory holiday definition in a live file.

## T01U6 — Amendments and Addenda

An addendum supplements the agreement with terms for a specific condition or subject. An amendment changes an existing agreement. The label is useful but the actual language controls. A financing addendum often accompanies the offer; an amendment after inspection may change repairs, price, closing, or option terms. An unsigned proposed amendment does not automatically suspend existing deadlines.

Use the current [Amendment to Contract, 39-11](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/amendment) and the appropriate current addendum. Locate an existing field before considering special provisions. A standard form is not a collection of optional paragraphs to rewrite freely. Under 22 TAC §537.11, an agent may explain informational choices and complete authorized fields but may not draft or recommend language defining rights, obligations, or remedies. A principal's specific written direction is treated separately by the rule; it is not permission for the agent to compose a clause and ask the principal to adopt it.

Attachments must travel with the offer that is accepted. Checking an addendum box but failing to attach the document creates a missing-terms problem, not a completed agreement on whatever a party later remembers. In a revision, make the actual changes conspicuous, secure the necessary agreement, identify the contract by parties and property, and preserve both original and amended versions. Cross-check amounts and dates after a change.

### Applied exercise: The unsigned repair proposal

Marta and Chen have an accepted $412,000 contract with an option ending October 18 at 5:00 p.m. They ask seller Rosa on October 16 to reduce the price to $406,000, repair the leaking kitchen supply valve through a licensed plumber, and move closing from November 4 to November 9. Rosa signs only a message saying she will consider repairs. No amendment is signed by both parties. At noon on October 18, Marta believes the pending negotiation extends the option.

Prepare a change log with original term, requested term, accepted status, and consequence of leaving it unsigned. Identify the relevant fields in the current amendment for the proposed changes without drafting a new legal contingency. Write a buyer reminder distinguishing the negotiation from the existing option deadline, and list the documents to preserve if a fully accepted amendment arrives at 2:00 p.m.

Feedback: the original price, closing date, and option deadline remain the documented terms absent an effective amendment or other legally operative event. Rosa's willingness to consider is not acceptance of all requests. The buyer must choose whether to act under existing rights in time, with counsel for legal advice. Preserve the accepted amendment, communication of acceptance, and revised tracker; never overwrite the original to make it appear it contained the new terms all along.

## T01U7 — Performance of a Contract

Performance means carrying out the actual agreement. A professional transaction tracker ties each task to a provision, a responsible party, a trigger, a deadline, proof, and a next step. It must track the buyer's duties and seller's duties independently. An unanswered request to the title company does not transfer the seller's obligation to a broker, and it does not prove timely delivery to the buyer.

Performance often requires coordination without authority to decide for a party. An agent can schedule an inspection, request repair documentation, send reminders, and identify a missing signature. The principal chooses whether to accept a proposed repair or waive a right. Attorneys advise on disputed remedies. Lenders decide loan approval. Title professionals identify requirements for issuance and closing. The agent should make these handoffs explicit, early, and documented.

Specific performance is a remedy requesting that a court require contractual performance; it is not a service a broker can promise to deliver. Liquidated damages are a contractually specified alternative in the seller's default remedies under paragraph 15. A right to seek relief does not guarantee success, entitlement, or recovery. Do not replace the form's alternative structure with an assurance that the seller can automatically keep earnest money and compel closing at the same time.

### Statute reading exercise: Read the default provision against the rule

Read Form 20-19 paragraph 15 and [22 TAC §537.11(b), (c), and (e)](https://www.trec.texas.gov/agency-information/rules-and-laws/trec-rules). Buyer Aaron fails to appear on the stipulated closing date and sends “I found a better house.” Seller Dalia asks the agent to tell escrow to pay her the $6,000 earnest money and to file a suit forcing the sale. Assume no termination right has been exercised; do not assume the entire facts of default or defenses have been adjudicated.

Diagram the alternatives printed for seller and buyer. Then write a response identifying the factual failure, explaining where the alternatives appear, and referring legal election and disputed entitlement to counsel. Identify two sentences the agent should not say and why each crosses the rule. Finally list the evidence counsel needs: signed agreement and attachments, deadline history, notices, and performance records.

Feedback: paragraph 15 describes alternative paths, including enforcement/other relief or termination and earnest money as liquidated damages for buyer default. The agent should not promise that both requested outcomes automatically follow or issue an unsupported determination to escrow. Rule §537.11 permits explanation of informational items; it prohibits legal advice and opinions on legal effect. Preserving the record and arranging direct consultation serves the principal without substituting the agent's legal judgment.

### Applied exercise: Repair completion is more than a photograph

Seller Nia agreed to replace a failed water heater before a November 20 closing. The contract has no alternative repair arrangement. On November 19 the seller sends a photo of a new unit but no invoice, permit information, paid receipt, or transferable warranty. The installer is described only as “a friend.” The buyer's walkthrough notes an active leak. Paragraph 7F governs agreed repair completion.

Prepare a verification checklist and a request for the missing evidence. Separate factual observations from conclusions requiring a licensed trade, inspector, or attorney. Identify the printed closing-extension and remedy language for incomplete agreed repairs and explain who chooses whether to invoke it. Write a concise closing-status note that does not assert that a photo proves compliance.

Feedback: investigate licensing or commercial-trade status as applicable, required permits, scope and payment documentation, transferable warranties, and actual completion. A photo does not resolve the observed leak or documentation duties. Paragraph 7F provides the buyer specified alternatives if agreed repairs are not completed, including extending closing up to five days if necessary; it is not an unlimited automatic extension. The buyer receives the facts and legal advice as needed before choosing an action.

## T01U8 — Statute of Limitations

A limitation period limits the time for bringing a claim. It is different from a contractual notice deadline, option period, or closing date. Texas Civil Practice & Remedies Code §16.004 includes a four-year period for specified actions, including debt and fraud; §16.051 supplies a four-year residual period for actions without another express limitation. Different claims and circumstances can involve different statutes, accrual rules, tolling, or defenses. Do not teach “every real-estate lawsuit is four years.”

A ten-day option does not become four years because a party mentions limitations. A limitations issue is also not permission to delay protecting records. A broker should promptly refer a threatened claim, notify appropriate supervisory and insurance contacts under applicable procedures, and preserve evidence. Whether a particular suit is timely is legal advice, not a date that an agent should calculate from a generic course memory.

### Applied exercise: Three clocks in one file

An accepted agreement has a financing-approval deadline November 12, a closing date December 1, and a dispute raised two years later alleging a material misrepresentation. A colleague tells the buyer to ignore November 12 because “Texas gives four years.” The facts do not establish when any claim accrued or which legal theories apply.

Create three separate clock entries: the contractual financing deadline, closing performance, and possible litigation limitation. State the source needed for each and the consequence of treating them as interchangeable. Prepare an immediate action list for the November 10 file and a separate preservation/referral list for the later claim. Do not calculate an ultimate lawsuit deadline on absent facts.

Feedback: the attached financing addendum controls its notice and approval deadlines, the sale contract controls closing, and applicable statutes and legal analysis control claims. A limitation period does not extend contractual conditions. The later file should preserve original documents, communications, and records and route the claim promptly to counsel and the broker; it should not state the claim is timely or barred from the supplied facts.

## T01U9 — Reasons for Termination

Identify a termination's source before preparing its notice: an unrestricted option, a financing provision, delayed disclosure, a title-document provision, casualty, default, or mutual agreement. The same notice form cannot create the right it records. A notice should identify the contract and valid applicable basis; it must be delivered as the contract requires within any applicable period. A receipt or release involving escrow money is a separate instrument with a separate purpose.

Termination by agreement requires actual agreement. One party writing “we mutually terminate” does not make the other party agree. Conversely, a unilateral right properly exercised under the contract does not necessarily require the other party's consent just because the other party disagrees. The agent gathers the contract, facts, and delivery evidence and refers contested rights to counsel. The escrow holder's ability to release money follows the governing instructions and procedures, not the agent's prediction of who deserves it.

### Applied exercise: Select a basis before a notice

File A: buyer has a paid, unexpired option and instructs the agent in writing to terminate today. File B: the option expired yesterday; buyer merely dislikes the paint, and no other contractual basis is supplied. File C: seller and buyer both agree to end their transaction but disagree over the $4,500 deposit. File D: buyer has a lender rejection letter but the financing addendum and notice deadline have not yet been reviewed.

For each file record whether the supplied facts identify a basis, what additional document or decision is needed, how delivery would be verified, and whether deposit release is resolved. Draft a factual communication for A and an internal follow-up for D; do not compose legal settlement language for C. Explain why using the correct termination-form title is insufficient by itself.

Feedback: A supplies an option basis subject to the actual contract and timely delivery. B supplies no current right from the stated dislike. C calls for an agreed resolution of termination and money; do not assume the deposit disposition. D requires the actual financing clause, applicable deadline, required supporting evidence, and legal review if disputed. The form documents a choice under authority; it does not supply absent authority.

### Module recap

Without looking back, define formation, performance, termination, and deposit release in separate sentences. Use your marked Form 20-19 to identify a promise, a permission, a notice deadline, and a remedy. Check your ten applied worksheets and two source-review responses for statements that decide a disputed legal issue. Correct those statements into factual descriptions and appropriate referrals. Then explain why a paid option, an accepted contract, a signed closing packet, and escrow funding are four different events. You are ready for the topic quiz when you can support each answer with the relevant required unit and identify the missing facts in an incomplete file.

## Optional reference: Deeper reading on electronic transactions

Texas Business & Commerce Code Chapter 322 also addresses attribution, errors, retention, and the time/place of sending and receiving electronic records. Read those sections if your work involves unusual electronic-signature disputes. No required assessment depends on material in this optional section. Do not infer that the chapter overrides the specific agreed notice terms or resolves forgery and authority disputes automatically.
