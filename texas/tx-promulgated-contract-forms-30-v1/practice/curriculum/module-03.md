## Inspect, disclose, and document repairs

Paragraph 7A grants inspection-related access but separately requires the seller’s written authorization for hydrostatic testing. General permission to inspect should not be stretched into permission for a separately controlled test. Use the applicable authorization document and obtain the seller’s actual instruction.

Paragraph 7D distinguishes taking the property As Is from agreeing to specific repairs. As Is does not eliminate inspection or negotiation of a later repair amendment. A blank saying “subject to inspections” does not identify repair work. Describe the actual agreed repair or treatment; do not insert a promise that “everything will be perfect.”

Lender-required repairs have a separate paragraph, 7E. Neither party is automatically required to pay unless agreed in writing. If they do not agree to pay, the form states a termination consequence. It also gives a buyer choice if the cost exceeds 5% of sales price. For a $420,000 transaction, 5% is $21,000. A $23,100 repair requirement equals 5.5% and exceeds that threshold. This arithmetic establishes only the comparison; the contractual facts and procedure still matter.

Paragraph 7F requires agreed repairs before closing unless otherwise agreed in writing, required permits, appropriate licensed or commercially engaged repair persons, and scope/payment evidence. A seller’s undocumented assurance is not the full repair package. Check warranties that can transfer as well.

Read the current water-disclosure provision. Paragraph 7I uses TREC’s Seller’s Disclosure about Groundwater and Surface Water Rights, currently form 61-0. The nondelivery option in 7I(3) requires all listed conditions. City water alone is insufficient if the seller knows of an old well. The condition concerning a well applies whether the well is in use or not. This 2026 change is a concrete reason to reject an old form merely because it worked last year.

The ordinary Seller’s Disclosure Notice in paragraph 7B is separate from the water disclosure. Where 7B(2) is selected, nondelivery before closing and later delivery have different stated termination mechanics. Identify which notice is missing; do not treat every disclosure as interchangeable.
