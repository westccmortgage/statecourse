## Compute deadlines and distinguish the two deposits

Paragraph 5A initially requires delivery of earnest money and the option fee to the named escrow agent within three days after the effective date. They may be separate payments or one combined payment. Read the named payee and address. Do not rely on an older workflow that sent the option fee elsewhere.

For a Thursday, September 10, 2026 effective date, day one is Friday, day two Saturday and day three Sunday. Under paragraph 5A(2), a payment-delivery deadline falling on a Saturday, Sunday or defined legal holiday moves to the next day that is none of those. With no Monday holiday, delivery is due Monday, September 14. The 2026 form defines the applicable legal holiday by reference to specified Texas Government Code provisions. Do not substitute the company’s holiday calendar without checking the contract definition.

That extension is attached to payment delivery. The paid option termination deadline in paragraph 5B is a different rule: notice by 5 p.m. local time where the property is located within the agreed option period. The payment weekend extension does not expressly extend the option notice deadline. A Sunday option end is not automatically moved to Monday. Store both date and time in the transaction calendar.

Payments also have an allocation order. Suppose the option fee is $300 and earnest money is $3,000, but escrow receives $500. The form applies $300 to the option fee and $200 to earnest money, leaving $2,800 earnest money outstanding. A single combined receipt does not prove both obligations were fully satisfied.

An omitted option-fee amount or untimely fee removes the unrestricted option right under paragraph 5D. Late earnest money has different consequences under 5C, including a seller notice sequence before the buyer delivers it. Never collapse the two payments into one generic “deposit deadline.”
