## Complete closing and avoid blank-field mistakes

Paragraph 10A controls possession. If possession is upon closing and funding, completion of the signing appointment alone does not satisfy that combined condition. Seller occupancy after closing should be handled through the appropriate written seller temporary lease. Early buyer occupancy calls for a different arrangement. Insurance should be reviewed before possession changes.

The smart-device clause is operational, not decoration. At possession the seller must deliver the needed access information and terminate the specified access from the seller’s personal devices. A buyer who receives keys but cannot control the locks and cameras has an unresolved handoff item.

Keep concessions and compensation fields separate. Paragraph 12A(1)(b) supplies a seller contribution toward buyer expenses other than brokerage compensation/contributions. Paragraph 12B separately addresses brokerage contributions, and states that compensation is negotiable and governed by separate written agreements. A $6,000 expense contribution is not automatically a $6,000 broker-compensation authorization.

A final document review should compare names and property identification across documents; read the selected boxes; locate negotiated blanks; compare price and financing totals; reconcile attachments; verify signatures; and record effective date, deadlines and delivery contacts. If both incompatible possession boxes are checked, obtain the parties’ resolution rather than privately choosing the more convenient one. If an amendment is only a draft, do not present it as an executed term.

In the question set, calculate the amount or deadline yourself before revealing the explanation. When an answer depends on a paragraph, open it and identify the specific words controlling the outcome. This is a practice workflow, not certification that a real transaction document is legally adequate.
