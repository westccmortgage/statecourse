# Questions for the Texas Real Estate Commission

**Prepared:** 2026-08-27; updated 2026-09-02
**From:** StateCourse — prospective qualifying education provider, distance education only
**Status of every question below: REGULATOR CLARIFICATION. None is answered by inference.**

This memo lists only what current authority does not resolve. Where a source answers a question, the
question is not here.

---

## 0. What has since been resolved, and what has not

**Most of this memo's original premise is gone.** When it was first written on 2026-08-24 it opened
by saying that no Texas rule or statute could be obtained. That was too broad and has been corrected:

- **Texas statutes are held.** Twenty-five chapters and four federal sources, retrieved from the
  Texas Legislative Council's static layer and the federal publishers.
- **Final adopted §§535.62 and 535.65 are held**, from the Secretary of State's 28 August 2026 Texas
  Register. Both have been effective since 31 August 2026.
- **Proctoring and closed-book are resolved.** The 2025 adoption removed the blanket requirements;
  StateCourse selects no human proctor and no proctoring fee while preserving identity assurance.
- **The 10-minute inactivity treatment is resolved as provider policy.** It is retained from TREC's
  still-published 2023 distance checklist without being described as a current rule requirement.
- **§ 535.65(a)(4) answers most of question 4 below**, and the answer is recorded there.

**What remains genuinely unobtainable here** is the codified current text of eight administrative
rules — §§ 535.55, 535.60, 535.61, 535.63, 535.64, 535.66, 535.67 and 535.101 — because the
Secretary of State's portal serves only a JavaScript application and no browser can reach it from
the build environment. That is an export task, not a question for the Commission, and it is listed
at `texas/submission/human-export-list.md`.

**The questions below are what is still genuinely open.**

## 1. Filing channel and delivery of the original surety bond

TREC's provider page asks for an **original $20,000 bond bearing an original or raised seal** and
labels the process “Apply by Mail.” The application link resolves to the REALM portal, and the
current REALM FAQ says education-provider applications are managed online.

**Question.** For the QE Provider Initial Application effective 09/03/2025, must a new applicant
submit through REALM or by mail, and how must the original bond be delivered? Specifically:

- Is a scan uploaded in REALM sufficient to complete the filing?
- Must the original paper bond also be delivered to the Commission, and if so to what address and by
  what deadline relative to the online filing?
- Does the Commission issue a separate instruction after the online application is submitted?

**Why it is not inferred.** A raised seal does not survive a scan, so an upload cannot obviously be
what "original or raised seal" means; but the filing is online, so paper cannot obviously be the
whole answer either. Both readings are available and the Commission's practice decides it.

## 2. The $10,000 reserve account

**Question.** Does the Commission require any of the following for the reserve account?

- a particular type of institution;
- a Texas location or a Texas-chartered bank;
- a restriction, hold, pledge or Commission control over the funds;
- a particular account titling;

**Why it is not inferred.** This build states none of these, because no source read here states any

## 4. May a commercial service be the designated Texas resident?

**Partly answered by the rule, and the answer narrows the question.** Final adopted
§ 535.65(a)(4), published in the Texas Register on 28 August 2026 and effective 31 August 2026:

> *"If a provider approved by the Commission does not maintain a fixed office in Texas for the
> duration of the provider's approval to offer courses, the provider shall designate a resident of
> this state as attorney-in-fact to accept service of process and act as custodian of any records in
> Texas that the provider is required to maintain by this section. A power-of-attorney designating
> the resident must be filed with the Commission in a form acceptable to the Commission."*

So the rule already contemplates **one designated resident holding both roles**, which was the part
this memo previously did not assume. StateCourse intends to be **distance education only** and will
not operate a Texas classroom.

**What is still open.** May that "resident of this state" be a **commercial registered-agent or
compliance service** rather than a natural person, and what **form of power-of-attorney** does the
Commission accept? The rule says "in a form acceptable to the Commission" and does not publish one.

## 5. What "records maintained in Texas" means when the records are electronic

**Question.** Which provider records must be physically or legally maintained in Texas when the
learning management system, the student records, the examinations and the completion records are
electronic and hosted outside Texas? Does a Texas-resident records custodian with access satisfy
the requirement, or must a copy reside on media in Texas?

## 6. Closed — proctoring

The final 2025 adoption says the blanket qualifying-course proctoring requirement was removed and,
as a result, closed-book examinations are no longer required. StateCourse's application therefore
proposes no human proctor and no proctoring fee, while retaining the identity-assurance and
exam-administration controls required by §535.65. Reopen only if the current course application asks
for a course-specific approved method inconsistent with this design.

---

## 7. Closed as provider policy — inactivity timeout

TREC's 04/10/2023 distance-course checklist, still linked from the current course page, says to log
the student out or stop the course timer after **10 minutes** of inactivity. StateCourse adopts that
value as conservative provider policy and does not claim it is contained in the current rule.

---

## 8. Course application fee for a distance-only course

The fee schedule effective 15 December 2025 sets, for qualifying real estate courses, a $50 base fee
plus **$10 per credit hour** for content and examination review, **$10 per credit hour** for
classroom delivery design and presentation review, and **$20 per credit hour** for distance education
delivery design and presentation review.

**Question.** For a 30-hour course offered by distance education only, is the fee **$950** — base
plus content and distance components — or does the classroom component apply regardless of the
delivery methods sought?

**Whatever REALM calculates controls.** This build records $950 as its planning figure and will
report the difference rather than reconcile it silently.
