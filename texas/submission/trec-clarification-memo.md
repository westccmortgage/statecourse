# Questions for the Texas Real Estate Commission

**Prepared:** 2026-08-27
**From:** StateCourse — prospective qualifying education provider, distance education only
**Status of every question below: REGULATOR CLARIFICATION. None is answered by inference.**

This memo lists only what current authority does not resolve. Where a source answers a question, the
question is not here.

---

## 0. A retrieval problem that precedes the rest

**The adopted text of 22 TAC Chapter 535 could not be obtained.** The Secretary of State's legacy
rule reader at `texreg.sos.state.tx.us` returns a "This Site Has Moved" page for every section, and
the replacement portal at `texas-sos.appianportalsgov.com` serves only a JavaScript shell to a
non-browser client. `statutes.capitol.texas.gov` behaves the same way for the Occupations Code.

This is recorded because it shapes everything below: **the questions in this memo are questions that
remain after reading what could be read, and there may be others that the rule text itself would
have answered.** Retrieval by a person with a browser is the first item on the provider checklist.

## 1. Delivery of the original surety bond when the application is filed online

TREC's provider page asks for an **original $20,000 bond bearing an original or raised seal**. The
application link resolves to the REALM/Accela portal, so the filing is online.

**Question.** How must a new applicant deliver the original bond? Specifically:

- Is a scan uploaded in REALM sufficient to complete the filing?
- Must the original paper bond also be delivered to the Commission, and if so to what address and by
  what deadline relative to the online filing?
- Does the Commission issue a separate instruction after the online application is submitted?

**Why it is not inferred.** A raised seal does not survive a scan, so an upload cannot obviously be
what "original or raised seal" means; but the filing is online, so paper cannot obviously be the
whole answer either. Both readings are available and the Commission's practice decides it.

## 2. The $10,000 reserve account

**Question.** Does the Commission require any of the following for the reserve account?

- a particular type of institution;
- a Texas location or a Texas-chartered bank;
- a restriction, hold, pledge or Commission control over the funds;
- a particular account titling;
- any limitation on the provider's access to the balance.

**Why it is not inferred.** This build states none of these, because no source read here states any
of them. If the answer is "none of the above", the build will say so with the Commission's answer
behind it rather than by silence.

## 4. Attorney-in-fact and records custodian, for a provider with no Texas office

StateCourse intends to be **distance education only** and will not operate a Texas classroom.

**Question.** May a commercial Texas registered-agent or compliance service serve as **both**

- the attorney-in-fact who accepts service of process, **and**
- the Texas records custodian?

**Why it is not inferred.** A registered agent's statutory function is service of process. Custody of
education records is a different function with different duties, and this project does not assume
one service satisfies both roles merely because a vendor offers both.

---

## 5. What "records maintained in Texas" means when the records are electronic

**Question.** Which provider records must be physically or legally maintained in Texas when the
learning management system, the student records, the examinations and the completion records are
electronic and hosted outside Texas? Does a Texas-resident custodian with access satisfy the
requirement, or must a copy reside on media in Texas?

## 6. Proctoring, and the difference between a rule and a provider policy

Historical Commission guidance and older distance-course checklists required proctored, closed-book
qualifying-course examinations. Later rulemaking is understood to have removed the blanket
requirement.

**Question.** Does the current REALM distance qualifying-course application impose any examination
proctoring requirement beyond the identity-assurance and examination-administration requirements of
§ 535.65?

**Why it matters to this provider.** StateCourse will not describe a voluntary proctoring choice as a
Commission mandate. If proctoring is a provider policy, it will be labelled a provider policy, its
procedure and any fee disclosed in the enrolment agreement, and the method represented in the
application. If the Commission requires it, the build follows the Commission. **What the build will
not do is assert either without the Commission's own current text.**

---

## 7. Inactivity timeout for asynchronous qualifying courses

An older checklist is understood to have specified a **10-minute** inactivity timeout.

**Question.** Does the current REALM distance qualifying-course application require a specific
inactivity timeout value for asynchronous qualifying courses?

**Why it is not inferred.** If StateCourse adopts a 10-minute timeout, it will be recorded as
**provider policy** unless a current authority requires it. A conservative provider choice presented
as a Commission requirement is a misstatement of the Commission's rules, which is the failure mode
this project treats most seriously.

---

## 8. Course application fee for a distance-only course

The fee schedule effective 15 December 2025 sets, for qualifying real estate courses, a $50 base fee
plus **$10 per credit hour** for content and examination review, **$10 per credit hour** for
classroom delivery design and presentation review, and **$20 per credit hour** for distance education
delivery design and presentation review.

**Question.** For a 30-hour course offered by distance education only, is the fee **$950** — base
plus content and distance components — or does the classroom component apply regardless of the
delivery methods sought?

**Whatever REALM calculates controls.** This build records $950 as its planning figure and will
report the difference rather than reconcile it silently.
