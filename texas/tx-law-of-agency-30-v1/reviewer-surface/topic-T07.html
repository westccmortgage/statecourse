Creation and Termination of Agency

Topic 7 of 12 · 85 prescribed minutes

How and When Agency is Created

Almost every disciplinary case and almost every lawsuit begins with a disagreement about one

question: whose agent were you? Duties attach to a relationship, so if you cannot say precisely

when the relationship began, you cannot say when the duties began.

Occupations Code § 1101.557(a), taught in Module 02, fixes the moment: "A broker who represents a

party in a real estate transaction or who lists real estate for sale under an exclusive agreement for

a party is that party's agent." Two independent triggers, joined by "or." The second is the

comfortable one — an exclusive listing, signed and sitting in the file. The first is the dangerous

one: it describes what you are actually doing, and it contains no signature requirement at all.

Two structural points follow, and both are taught elsewhere. The agency runs to the broker, not to

you: § 1101.351(c) bars a sales agent from brokerage "unless the sales agent is sponsored by a

licensed broker and is acting for that broker," and 22 TAC § 535.2 requires listings to be "solicited

and accepted in a broker's name" (Module 09). And the conduct that pulls you in is the list of

brokerage acts in § 1101.002(1)(A) with the definition of showing in 22 TAC § 535.4(c) (Module 01),

whose thresholds sit very low — attempting to negotiate, offering to aid in locating. Opening a

lockbox is a regulated act.

Question 1 of 3

A license holder works a residential buyer for six weeks with nothing signed — pulling comparables, advising on price, drafting an offer — and then argues that the absence of paperwork means there was no relationship and therefore no problem. State the position under the held sources.

Three provisions run in different directions and all three cut against the licensee. Section 1101.557(a) makes a broker who “represents a party” that party's agent, with no signature in the sentence, and 22 TAC § 531.2 attaches fiduciary status to a license holder “while acting as an agent for another” — the trigger is acting, not signing. Section 1101.652(b)(34), effective January 1, 2026, makes failing to enter into the written agreement § 1101.563 requires a ground for discipline in its own right, so the absence of the writing is the violation rather than a defence to one. And § 1101.806(c) bars the fee action, because there is no writing signed by the party who would be sued. Full exposure, no enforceable fee, and a disciplinary count.

Question 2 of 3

On Monday you email your sponsoring broker your resignation. An offer on one of your listings is due to be presented on Tuesday. Apply § 1101.367(a) and § 1101.351(c).

Section 1101.367(a) is exact: “When the relationship of a sales agent with the sales agent's sponsoring broker terminates, the terminating party shall immediately notify in writing both the other party and the commission. On receiving the written notice, the commission shall place the sales agent license on inactive status.” The duty falls on whichever side ended it, the notice is written, it goes to two recipients, and the timing word is “immediately.” The consequence is automatic and § 1101.351(c) then bars the agent from engaging or attempting to engage in brokerage, because sponsorship is a condition of acting at all. The listing itself stays with the firm, since 22 TAC § 535.2 requires listings to be accepted in a broker's name.

Question 3 of 3

A listing terminated unsold months ago, but earnest money from a dead contract is still sitting in the brokerage trust account and the broker has since become entitled to a commission paid into the same account by mistake. Which statement about the surviving duties is correct?

22 TAC § 535.146(c)(5) requires that if a broker acquires ownership of trust money held in a trust account, “including entitlement to compensation, such money must be removed from the trust account not later the 30th day after the date the broker acquires ownership of the money” — a rule that runs against the broker precisely after the work is done, because leaving earned commission in a trust account is a commingling risk, and § 535.146(b)(5) makes placing trust money in a personal or operating account prima facie evidence of commingling. Section 535.146(c)(6) requires a documentary record of each deposit or withdrawal and an accounting to each beneficiary “at least monthly if there has been any activity in the account” — the trigger is activity, not an open transaction.

Duties of Agency that Survive Termination

A listing you held expired three months ago and the seller did not relist with you. During the listing the seller told you privately that they would have taken $12,000 under the asking price to close before the school year. A buyer's agent working the same street now asks you, in conversation, what the seller would have accepted. May you answer?

Section 1101.651(d) forbids disclosing that a party will accept a price other than the one submitted, and its own text fixes no period after which the prohibition lapses. No held source states a duration for the confidentiality obligation, and that absence is recorded in this course rather than filled by assumption. The safe reading, and the one the statute's silence supports, is that the duty does not expire with the agreement.

Optional practice — the earnest money after the deal dies. This exercise is optional. The

required work in this topic tests what survives termination as a matter of duty; this one is date

arithmetic on the disbursement deadlines in 22 TAC § 535.146(d), which no required question in this

course asks, and it puts you in the position of a broker holding money two people have demanded in

writing.

Bright Star Realty in Killeen holds $6,000 of earnest money in its designated trust account on a

contract for 2209 Comanche Gap Road. The contract terminates unperformed on May 29. On June 12 the

buyer delivers a written demand for return of the full $6,000. On June 18 the seller emails Bright

Star's broker demanding the same $6,000 as liquidated damages. The listing agreement, signed

January 15 with a termination date of July 15, provides for total compensation of 3 percent to the

listing broker on a list price of $335,000.

1. Under 22 TAC § 535.146(d)(2), what is the deadline for Bright Star to pay the trust money to the

party entitled to it, and from what date does it run? Give the date.

2. The broker cannot tell who is entitled. What does § 535.146(d)(5) permit, and is it mandatory or

permissive? Quote the operative words.

3. Suppose instead that on June 20 both parties sign a written agreement splitting the earnest money

$4,000 to the buyer and $2,000 to the seller, and Bright Star receives it that day. Under

§ 535.146(d)(3), by what date must Bright Star pay, and what additional step does § 535.146(d)(4)

require?

4. The property later sells within the listing term at $335,000 and Bright Star's 3 percent is paid at

closing into the trust account by mistake on September 4. Compute the amount, and state the deadline

under § 535.146(c)(5) for removing it from the trust account. Give the date.

5. The transaction closes September 4, 2026. Under 22 TAC § 535.2, through what date must Bright Star

maintain the listing agreement and the substantive communications with the parties? Give the date,

and name two other categories on that rule's list that must be kept for the same period.
