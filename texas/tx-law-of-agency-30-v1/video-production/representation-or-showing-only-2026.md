## 1. Learning objective

By the end of this video the student can:

1. state that, since 1 January 2026, a Texas license holder working with a residential buyer occupies
   one of exactly two lawful postures — representation under a written agreement, or a § 1101.562
   showing without representation — and that the posture is chosen **before** the showing, in writing;
2. state both § 1101.563(b) triggers in the right order: before showing any residential real property,
   or, if none will be shown, before presenting an offer on the buyer's behalf;
3. list the four § 1101.562(a) conditions and explain the consequence of their being joined by "and";
4. state the two limits § 1101.563(e) puts on a showing-only agreement — not exclusive, not more than
   fourteen days;
5. apply the § 1101.562(b) gate: which disclosure is owed turns on whether the broker represents the
   owner; and
6. name the only three things a broker showing without representation may **confirm** under
   § 1101.562(c), and distinguish confirming from evaluating.

**What text cannot do here.** This is a decision made against a clock, and the clock is the part that
fails in practice. The prose in Module 05 states every rule correctly, but it states them as rules;
the student still has to assemble them into an order of operations — choose posture, paper it, make
the right disclosure, then show, and say almost nothing while showing. Ordering is what video is for.
The second thing text cannot supply is the *moment* — the hallway question the license holder is not
allowed to answer, and the sentence they need ready before it arrives. That is an act of professional
judgment, delivered as one, by a person, on camera.

> transactions generally. And does not perform any other act of real estate brokerage for them. Four
> conditions, joined by and. Satisfy three and you are not inside this section at all.
>
> Read the paragraph underneath, because the non-representation posture is papered too. Before showing
> a residential property to an unrepresented prospective buyer, a license holder must enter into a
> written agreement that contains the information required by section 1101.563. The agreement may not
> be exclusive and must be limited to no more than 14 days.
>
> One more gate stands before any showing without representation, and which side you are on decides it.
> Represent the owner, and you make the representation disclosure — orally or in writing — under
> section 1101.558(b). Represent nobody, and you deliver the written notice under section
> 1101.558(b-1). That is the IABS.
>
> Here is where this fails, and it is never at the paperwork. It fails in a hallway, when someone you
> are not representing asks whether the price is fair. Section 1101.562(c) lets you confirm the size,
> price and terms. Confirm — not evaluate. Have the sentence ready before you need it. I can't give you
> an opinion on that, because I'm showing you this house without representing you. Then stop talking.
