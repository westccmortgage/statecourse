# Texas Law of Contracts — Module 11

## TREC Topic: Common Contract Mistakes

You will integrate the course’s rules in complete transaction files, distinguish administrative coordination from legal judgment, and identify common failures before a deadline or unsupported assurance harms the client.

This is student instruction aligned to the LOC-0 topic. Legal propositions identify primary authority; worked examples are hypothetical. ## T11U1 — Contract Examples

A contract example becomes useful when it requires a sequence of decisions, not merely identification of a paragraph number. Begin with the parties and their capacity, the property and interest to be conveyed, the proposed exchange, the form and addenda, and the evidence of agreement. Then identify each remaining obligation, condition, deadline, notice, and remedy. Finally, distinguish what the license holder can explain or complete from what requires the principal’s decision or legal advice.

In the first example, Elena agrees to purchase a resale residence with third-party financing. The seller’s name in the proposed contract matches an individual shown in a prior deed, but a title search identifies a later transfer to a trust. The buyer’s lender has supplied a prequalification letter, and the seller’s disclosure notice reports a repaired foundation. A survey is available but does not depict a newly enclosed patio. These are four distinct issues. Party and authority questions concern who can bind the owner and convey title. Financing concerns the buyer and property approvals under the signed addendum. The disclosure concerns known property facts and the buyer’s investigation. The survey concerns the physical and legal depiction of the property and the contract’s survey process.

The agent’s first response should be an organized inquiry rather than a broad assurance that the paperwork is routine. Ask the title company and appropriate counsel to identify the current owner and required authority documents. Confirm the lender’s actual approval stage. Deliver the disclosure accurately and help the buyer obtain the desired professional investigation while contractual rights remain available. Compare the survey with the current condition and identify the applicable survey obligations. None of those steps authorizes the agent to declare that the trust is valid, the foundation is sound, or the enclosed patio complies with restrictions.

Suppose the buyer signs the offered terms, but the seller changes the closing date before signing. The file must establish whether the buyer accepted that change and whether acceptance was communicated as required. A signature somewhere on each page is not a substitute for mutual agreement to the same terms. Preserve the version history and communications. Do not calculate performance deadlines from an assumed effective date while the evidence of agreement remains unresolved. Refer a disputed formation question to the broker and counsel.

Once the agreement is established, create a calendar tied to the actual provisions. The money-delivery duties, termination-option period if agreed, financing-approval provisions, survey and title review, closing, and possession are not one general inspection period. Their triggers, recipients, delivery methods, and consequences can differ. A request for a lender update does not preserve an expiring contractual right. A repair negotiation does not itself extend an option period. The agent should identify the needed decision early enough for the buyer to obtain advice and act through the prescribed process.

In the second example, Marco buys a small residential rental property with existing tenants. The marketing sheet lists monthly rent, but the seller’s ledger shows one tenant has prepaid two months and another has an unpaid balance. The lease contains an extension provision. A security deposit is held for each tenant. The buyer intends to move into a unit immediately after closing. Those facts require more than copying the advertised rent into an investment calculation.

Obtain the leases, amendments, relevant notices, deposit information, and income records through the authorized process. The lease term and possession rights may prevent the intended immediate occupancy. A security deposit is not seller income to be divided as rent. Prepaid rent and delinquent rent require different accounting treatment under the agreement. The parties should determine which interests and obligations transfer, how the closing figures address them, and what information and notices the law requires. The broker should not promise that a sale automatically ends a tenant’s lease.

Suppose the buyer proposes a side letter stating that the seller will remove the tenant before closing and pay any resulting claim. That document creates rights, duties, and potential indemnity. It is not merely an informational entry in an authorized form. Under 22 TAC § 537.11, the license holder must stay within the rule’s permissions and prohibitions. The parties need appropriate legal drafting and advice. Calling the letter an addendum does not make an agent-written indemnity permissible.

The buyer’s financing raises another issue. If the lender underwrites based on rental income, the lender needs accurate information about the actual leases and payment history. The agent should not omit the delinquent balance to preserve approval or treat the buyer’s future occupancy plan as irrelevant. Accurate transaction information should travel through authorized channels, with sensitive personal data protected. Whether the loan qualifies is the lender’s determination; whether the buyer wishes to proceed is the buyer’s decision under the agreement and applicable advice.

In the third example, a seller proposes owner financing for a residential property while retaining an existing mortgage. The buyer suggests signing a lease with an option to purchase and applying part of each monthly payment toward the price. The seller says that title will transfer only after the buyer makes payments for several years. The label attached to the packet does not settle its legal classification. The combined documents and actual operation must be evaluated, including whether Property Code Chapter 5, Subchapter D applies to an executory contract.

The agent should identify the existing debt, proposed payment flow, contemplated conveyance date, security arrangement, insurance, taxes, and default provisions. Existing financing may have restrictions that cannot be ignored merely because payments will continue. Seller-financing regulation and applicable loan-originator requirements also demand proper analysis. A familiar deed-of-trust structure, a wrap arrangement, and a contract for deed are not interchangeable products. They place title, debt, security, and default rights in different relationships.

Do not prepare a homemade forfeiture clause or assure the seller that the buyer can be evicted whenever a payment is late. The applicable statutory protections and remedies depend on the actual transaction. Section 5.062 addresses applicability, and the following provisions impose particular requirements and remedies. The appropriate response is timely referral to qualified counsel and other required professionals before execution, with a complete factual record of the proposed arrangement. The agent can help identify the parties’ business objectives without choosing a legal device to avoid protections.

In the fourth example, a conventional resale transaction approaches closing. The buyer completed inspections and agreed to proceed. A signed amendment specifies repairs, but the final walkthrough reveals that one listed item is unfinished. The seller has already moved, and the buyer’s moving truck is scheduled for the next morning. The lender has authorized documents but has not confirmed funding. The agent receives an email purporting to change the closing company’s wire instructions.

Separate the issues. The repair obligation requires comparison with the signed amendment, evidence of performance, and the buyer’s decision with appropriate advice. The moving schedule does not amend the contract or justify an unsupported assurance about remedies. Funding status requires confirmation from the authorized closing participant. The wire change requires independent verification using a trusted contact channel under the broker’s fraud-prevention procedures. Neither pressure nor convenience eliminates any of these checks.

If the parties agree to a different repair arrangement, it must be documented through an authorized process before the parties rely on it. A closing statement credit is not sufficient evidence that every related contract obligation has been released. If the buyer considers refusing to close, the possible default and remedy consequences require legal advice. The agent should preserve the chronology, communicate accurately, and avoid directing a unilateral legal strategy.

Across all four examples, use a four-part file note: the observed fact, the document or authority implicated, the person responsible for the next decision, and the evidence needed to close the issue. “Seller will handle it” lacks an identified obligation and completion evidence. “Title company requested a certified authority document; seller’s counsel is obtaining it; closing officer will confirm acceptance by the stated checkpoint” makes the unresolved step visible. This is a coordination technique, not a guarantee of legal sufficiency.

The primary authorities for these examples are [22 TAC §§ 535.2 and 537.11](https://www.trec.texas.gov/agency-information/rules-and-laws/trec-rules), [Property Code §§ 5.008 and 5.062–5.085](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/PR/htm/PR.5.htm), and the applicable current [TREC contract and addenda](https://www.trec.texas.gov/agency-information/contracts). The exact executed documents, not this hypothetical summary, determine a real file’s contractual duties.

## T11U2 — Common Areas of Concern

The most important common concern is often a category error: treating one document, participant, or event as proof of something it does not establish. A seller’s disclosure is not a warranty or a substitute for investigation. A lender’s prequalification is not final approval. A recorded deed is not a promise that no competing rights exist. An inspection report is not an automatic repair amendment. A closing appointment is not funding. These distinctions let the agent ask a focused question rather than repeat a reassuring but unsupported label.
### Adapted teaching: Recognizing recurring complaint patterns and responding to an error

Risk management in Texas brokerage is not a philosophy. It is the practice of knowing, in advance,
which specific things go wrong, what the Commission does about them, what a court does about them,
and what you are supposed to do on the day you realise you are the one who got it wrong. Almost all
of the law in this unit belongs to other modules — the disciplinary catalogue and the penalty scheme
to Law of Agency Module 10, the Recovery Trust Account and the DTPA to Law of Agency Module 11, the allocation of
misrepresentation liability to Law of Agency Module 03. What is here is where each of them meets your file.

**How a complaint reaches you.** Section 1101.204, sets the frame: a signed,
written, non-anonymous complaint, submitted within four years of the incident, with reasonable cause
for an investigation, and prompt written notice to the license holder under investigation. Rule
535.141 then triages it.

Two consequences to absorb now. A complaint that names a sales agent but not the sponsoring broker
is, under 22 TAC § 535.141, also a complaint against that broker for the limited purpose of
determining the broker's involvement and whether the broker met the broker's professional
responsibilities — and § 1101.803, makes the broker liable to the Commission,
the public and clients for your conduct. Your mistake is your broker's problem by operation of
statute, which is why brokers write policies and why you follow them. Second, § 1101.652(a)(4) makes
it an independent ground for discipline to fail to provide, within a reasonable time, information
the Commission requests in connection with a complaint. Ignoring a request can create a separate disciplinary issue even when the underlying complaint is disputed.

**The recurring fact patterns, and where in the week each one is created.** Section 1101.652(b) is
the catalogue, and Law of Agency Module 10 teaches it subdivision by subdivision. Read as a working list, the
patterns you will actually meet are six, and each has a moment on the calendar when it is created:

*Silence about a defect* — § 1101.652(b)(3) and (b)(4), misrepresenting or failing to disclose a
significant defect known to the license holder. Created at the listing appointment, when the seller
mentions something in conversation that never reaches the § 5.008 notice or the file.

*Advertising* — § 1101.652(b)(23), operationalised by 22 TAC §§ 535.154 and 535.155. Created the
moment you press publish. The published advertisement can supply important evidence; the applicable rule, context, and required elements still must be evaluated.

*Money* — § 1101.652(b)(9), (10), (30) and (31), operationalised by 22 TAC § 535.146. The relevant duty and deadline depend on the particular handling of funds, the governing agreement, and the rule; bank records can supply evidence.

*Compensation and undisclosed interest* — § 1101.652(b)(8), (11), (13) and (16), with § 1101.651 and
22 TAC §§ 535.147 and 535.148. Created when the payment is arranged, not when it is received.

*Authority and interference* — § 1101.652(b)(18) through (b)(22): a sign without written consent,
offering property without the owner's knowledge and consent, offering on unauthorised terms,
inducing a party to break a contract in order to substitute a new one, and negotiating with a party
you know is under an exclusive agency with another broker. On that last one, 22 TAC § 535.153,
taught in Law of Agency Module 04, draws the line precisely: soliciting a listing from an owner whose property is
subject to another broker's exclusive listing is permitted; negotiating the present transaction is
not.

*Paperwork that was supposed to exist* — § 1101.652(a-1)(3), (b)(12), (b)(28), (b)(29) and (b)(34):
the wrong form, no termination date, no copy provided on request, no written title advisory, no
buyer agreement under § 1101.563. Every one of these is created by an omission on a day when nothing
appeared to go wrong.

Discrimination sits across all of them, at § 1101.652(b)(32) and 22 TAC § 531.19, taught in Module
10 alongside the federal fair housing provisions.

**What it costs.** The Commission's remedies are cumulative, not alternative, and Law of Agency Module 10 teaches
the scheme: suspension or revocation, an administrative penalty capped by § 1101.702(a) at 5,000
dollars "for each violation" with "Each day a violation continues or occurs" capable of being a
separate violation, and the three published ranges of rule 535.191. Note § 1101.702(b), which puts
"efforts to correct the violation" among the factors that set the amount — the reason the last
section of this unit is worth more to you than any other. Beyond penalties there are a cease and
desist order under § 1101.759, an injunctive action under § 1101.751, and, for a wilful violation of
the chapter or a Commission order, a Class A misdemeanour under § 1101.756.

One consequence lands on your calendar rather than in a tier table. Under 22 TAC § 535.141, a person
whose licence is suspended "may not during the period of any suspension perform, attempt to perform,
or advertise to perform any act for which a license is required", and must give written notice "not
later than the third day before the date of the suspension" — a sales agent to the sponsoring
broker; a broker to sponsored sales agents and to any entity for which the person is designated
broker; and, where the person is contractually obliged to perform licensed services, to all other
parties to that contract.

**Beyond the Commission.** Three exposures run alongside discipline, and all three are taught
elsewhere. The Real Estate Recovery Trust Account, §§ 1101.601–.610 and Law of Agency Module 11, pays an aggrieved
person's actual damages after a judgment goes unsatisfied, subject to the caps that module sets out,
and then looks to you: it is your licence that answers for the reimbursement. The DTPA, Business &
Commerce Code ch. 17 and Law of Agency Module 11, adds mental anguish and up to treble damages for knowing or
intentional conduct, and the consumer's attorney's fees under § 17.50(d). And § 1101.805, taught in
Law of Agency Module 03, allocates responsibility for misrepresentation between you and your client on two
elements — knowledge of the falsity and failure to disclose that knowledge. The practical reading:
you are not an insurer of your seller's statements, but the moment you know a statement is false,
staying quiet makes it yours. Suit for fraud or breach of fiduciary duty must be brought within four
years, Civil Practice and Remedies Code § 16.004(a) — the same four years for which your file must
survive.

**The day you realise you got it wrong.** This is the part of risk management that is almost never
taught, and it is the part you will actually need. There is no held Texas source that prescribes a
self-correction procedure, and none is invented here. What the held sources do provide is a set of
obligations that, taken together, guide a responsible response.

Tell your broker immediately. Rule 535.2 makes the broker responsible for your authorized acts and
for trust funds, and § 1101.803 makes the broker liable to the Commission, the public and clients
for your conduct; concealing a problem from the person who is legally answerable for it converts a
mistake into a betrayal.

Correct the record in writing, and correct it toward everyone who relied on it. Rule 535.156 imposes
an affirmative duty to keep the principal informed of significant information applicable to the
transaction and a duty to convey accurate information to members of the public with whom you deal.
If you quoted the wrong square footage in an advertisement, the fix is a corrected writing to the
people who saw it, not a quiet edit.

If money is involved, use the notice rules that already exist. Under 22 TAC § 535.146 a broker whose
trust-money check is dishonoured "shall immediately notify all parties to the transaction in
writing", must notify all parties in writing of any disbursement made on a written demand or a
subsequent written agreement, and may interplead where entitlement genuinely cannot be determined.

Do not touch the file except to add to it. Section 1101.652(a-1)(2) makes it a ground for discipline
to fail or refuse to produce records for inspection; 22 TAC § 535.2 requires the broker to keep the
eight listed record categories four years in a format readily available to the Commission; and the
only excuse the rule recognises for missing records is destruction "by an 'Act of God' such as a
natural disaster or fire not intentionally caused by the broker".

Answer the Commission. Section 1101.652(a)(4) makes failure to provide requested information within
a reasonable time its own ground for discipline, and § 1101.702(b) lists "efforts to correct the
violation" among the factors that set the penalty amount. Prompt correction is not merely decent; it
is statutorily relevant to what you will pay.

And do not offer legal conclusions about your own mistake to the person affected by it. That is
exactly the advice 22 TAC § 537.11 forbids you to give, and it is the moment when the person harmed
should be talking to a lawyer — whom you may not discourage them from employing.


Another concern is the quiet loss of the operative document. Files can contain an initial offer, a counteroffer, unsigned revisions, a signed contract, several addenda, and an amendment. A participant may inadvertently work from the wrong attachment. Maintain a clear current set and retain the historical versions. Record which change was accepted and when. Do not delete prior versions to make the file look simpler; the chronology may be essential to understanding what happened.

Deadlines require similar discipline. A software reminder is only as reliable as its input and interpretation. The actual provision may use a particular trigger, date, time, recipient, or delivery method. A calendar may treat business days differently from the contract or applicable federal rule. Review the underlying language and obtain advice where interpretation is needed. If the parties amend a date, determine which dependent tasks change and preserve the source of that update.

An issue can remain unresolved despite repeated communication. Several emails asking whether a repair is finished do not prove performance. Repeated lender updates do not establish property approval. A promise to provide a release is not the release itself. Close a task only when the required evidence exists and the proper participant has accepted it where acceptance is relevant. Administrative optimism should not change the status from pending to complete.

The handling of uncertainty also matters. A competent statement can be narrow: “The document reports this fact; we have not yet received the requested supporting record.” It is not necessary to guess in order to sound helpful. Where a legal question arises, identify it clearly for counsel rather than hiding it in a vague referral. For example, ask whether the proposed signing authority is sufficient for the named owner and conveyance, not simply whether the contract looks fine.

Finally, distinguish a client’s informed choice from an agent’s substitution of judgment. The client may decide to accept a risk, negotiate a change, obtain additional investigation, or exercise an available right. The agent should communicate material information accurately and within the permitted role, document the decision, and carry out lawful instructions. The file should not suggest that the agent guaranteed a legal outcome or pressured the client to waive a protection to preserve a commission.

The completed course should leave a practical habit: identify the legal relationship before applying the vocabulary, find the controlling document before calculating the calendar, and secure evidence before declaring the task finished. Where the work crosses into legal advice, specialized evaluation, or regulated lending activity, make an effective referral while continuing appropriate coordination. That boundary is part of competent service, not a withdrawal from service.


### Statute reading exercise: Separate completion from legal drafting

Read 22 TAC § 537.11. A seller asks the agent to write a clause making the buyer responsible for all future tenant claims. Identify which part of the request involves creation or allocation of legal rights, what the agent may explain about the form-use boundary, and what referral is needed. Do not draft the requested clause.

Feedback: The proposed indemnity allocates substantive liability; it is not an informational entry. The license holder must apply the rule’s restrictions and refer drafting and legal-effect questions to counsel. The agent may gather accurate business facts and coordinate delivery of the attorney-prepared material through the authorized process.

### Applied exercise: Four facts, four workstreams

For Elena’s example, prepare four file-note entries: trust ownership, financing stage, foundation disclosure, and inaccurate survey depiction. For each, identify the evidence needed, the participant responsible, and the contract deadline that must be checked. Explain why completing one entry does not close the others.

Feedback: Authority requires title and legal evidence; financing requires the lender’s actual approval status and addendum review; condition requires delivery and professional investigation; survey requires the specified documents and review process. Each entry has a distinct source and completion test. A single “due diligence complete” label conceals unresolved work.

### Applied exercise: Closing pressure and unresolved performance

For the fourth example, write a chronological action record addressing the unfinished repair, funding uncertainty, and changed wire instructions. Identify decisions reserved for the buyer, legal questions requiring counsel, and confirmations needed from closing participants. Do not invent an extension or tell either party which remedy to elect.

Feedback: Compare the repair evidence with the signed amendment, communicate promptly, and obtain the buyer’s decision with legal advice where needed. Confirm actual funding status independently of signing. Verify wire instructions through a trusted established channel. Any negotiated change requires an authorized documented agreement. Preserve the record and do not let the moving schedule substitute for legal or financial confirmation.


### Module recap

A complete contract file combines accurate party and property identification, authorized documents, mutual agreement, a provision-based calendar, evidence of performance, and properly assigned decisions. Integrated examples demonstrate that financing, condition, title, possession, and legal remedies remain separate questions even when they arise on the same day. Common failures become easier to prevent when the agent distinguishes a fact from an assumption and a coordination task from legal advice.
