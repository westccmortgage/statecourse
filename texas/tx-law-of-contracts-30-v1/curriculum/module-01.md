### Learning objectives

After completing this module, you should be able to:

1. classify the vocabulary used in a Texas real estate contract problem by authority layer and apply the statutory definitions of broker, license holder, real estate, sales agent, and brokerage;
2. trace a contract-form obligation from the Texas Real Estate License Act through TREC's delegated rulemaking authority to the discipline provision that gives the obligation consequences;
3. separate permitted form completion and factual explanation from prohibited drafting, legal advice, and opinions about legal effect or title;
4. explain who sits on the Texas Real Estate Broker-Lawyer Committee, what the Legislature directs it to do, and what it does not do;
5. select a permitted form source for a stated transaction and apply the mandatory-form exceptions and document-handling rules in the held TREC reproduction; and
6. analyze a real-estate fact pattern through the definitions, actionable conduct, broker exemption, exceptions, causation, reliance, and remedies of the DTPA sections read for this topic.

This module begins with an operating principle that will recur throughout the course: **a document is not an authority merely because it looks official, and an authority does not answer a question merely because it is nearby.** A TREC contract form can establish what that form says. It cannot, by itself, establish what a statute says. A Commission rule can govern a license holder's conduct. It does not become a clause in the parties' contract unless the law and the contract make it so. A statute can authorize the Commission to adopt rules without supplying the detailed rule that the Commission later adopts. A guidance page can help you find a form without becoming the legal source of the mandatory-use obligation.

Those distinctions matter because Texas license holders work at the junction of several different relationships. The buyer and seller may be negotiating a private contract. One or both may have a representation agreement with a broker. A sales agent acts for a sponsoring broker. The Legislature regulates brokerage through Occupations Code chapter 1101. TREC administers that chapter and publishes rules and forms. Attorneys advise principals about legal effect and draft transaction-specific legal language. A title company, lender, inspector, surveyor, and closing agent may each have a separate professional role. A single set of facts can therefore produce a contract question, a licensing question, a disclosure question, and a consumer-protection question at the same time. Competent practice begins by asking which question is being answered and which source has authority to answer it.

The module does not teach a license holder to diagnose litigation or to replace counsel. It teaches a safer and more exact skill: recognize the legal boundary, use the correct instrument, enter authorized factual information accurately, explain choices without predicting legal consequences, document the principal's instruction, and refer the principal to an attorney when the issue crosses the line.

## Unit: Key Terms

Legal vocabulary is useful only when you know who supplied the definition. Some terms in a contract are defined by that contract. Some are defined by a statute for a particular chapter. Some are used as professional shorthand without a universal statutory definition. The same everyday word may carry different consequences in different sources. For that reason, the first question should not be merely “What does this word mean?” It should be “What does this word mean **in the source that controls this question**?”

### The authority ladder

For this topic, use the following working ladder:

1. **Texas statute.** The Legislature enacted Occupations Code chapter 1101 and Business and Commerce Code chapter 17. A statutory definition applies within the scope the statute gives it. Section 1101.001 supplies the short title “The Real Estate License Act,” commonly shortened to TRELA. Section 17.45 opens with “As used in this subchapter,” which is a warning not to export its definitions carelessly into unrelated law.
2. **Administrative rule.** Occupations Code § 1101.151 authorizes TREC to adopt and enforce rules necessary to administer chapters 1101 and 1102 and to establish conduct and ethics standards. The held TREC reproduction of 22 TAC § 537.11 gives detailed rules about standard contract forms and unauthorized practice. The rule is more specific than the delegation that authorizes it, but the held copy is the Commission's reproduction rather than a completed comparison against the Secretary of State's codified text.
3. **Adopted form.** A TREC form is an instrument designed for a defined transaction. Its paragraphs, notices, choices, addenda checklist, signature blocks, and revision date matter. A form may be approved for mandatory use, approved for voluntary use, or outside the form set altogether. Never infer the category from the form's polished appearance.
4. **Official guidance.** A TREC webpage may organize links and explain agency practice. It is useful, but it does not displace the statute, rule, or instrument when those are the source of the proposition.
5. **Provider method.** A checklist or decision tree in this course is a training method. It is not a TREC rule. When a method is ours, the course says so.

The ladder is not a claim that every higher line defeats every lower line in every dispute. It is a reading discipline. If the question is whether a license holder must use a mandatory form, start with the statute and rule, then identify the correct form. If the question is what paragraph 5 of Form 20-19 requires, open Form 20-19. If the question is what a party should write into special provisions to achieve a novel legal result, stop: the answer is not a provider checklist and is not within a license holder's drafting authority.

### Statutory people and activities

Occupations Code § 1101.002 supplies the basic actors. A **license holder** is a broker or sales agent licensed under chapter 1101. A **sales agent** is a person sponsored by a licensed broker to perform an act described in the broker definition. Section 1101.351 adds the operating consequence: a sales agent may not engage or attempt to engage in brokerage unless sponsored by a licensed broker and acting for that broker. The sales agent does not become an independent contract-form professional merely because the student knows how a form works.

The statute's broker definition is functional. It does not begin with a job title. It begins with a person who acts for another in exchange for a commission or other valuable consideration, or with the expectation of receiving it, and performs one of the enumerated acts. Those acts include selling, exchanging, purchasing, or leasing real estate; offering or negotiating; listing; auctioning; dealing in options, including lease-purchase options; helping locate real estate; procuring prospects or property; controlling acceptance or deposit of rent from a resident of a single-family residential unit; providing a qualifying written price analysis that is not called an appraisal; and short-sale advice. Section 1101.004 restates the functional idea: brokerage occurs when a person expects valuable consideration and directly or indirectly performs, offers, attempts, or agrees to perform a listed act for another, whether the act is the entire transaction or only part of it.

This structure yields several practice rules.

- **Expectation matters.** The definition does not require successful collection of a fee. A person who expected consideration cannot escape the definition merely because the transaction failed or the fee was never paid.
- **One listed act can be enough.** The opening clause refers to one of the following acts. A person need not perform the entire bundle.
- **Substance defeats title.** Calling someone a consultant, coordinator, transaction helper, or investment specialist does not change the statutory analysis if the person performs the defined conduct for another with the required compensation expectation.
- **The security-interest exclusion matters.** Section 1101.002 defines real estate as an interest in real property, including a leasehold, but excludes an interest given as security for performance of an obligation. That helps distinguish brokerage of a property interest from the creation of a security interest, although other licensing law may still govern financing activity.
- **Not every property-related act is brokerage.** Section 1101.004(b) says construction, remodeling, or repair; participation as a principal, partner, or financial manager in a real estate investment; and entering an obligation secured by real property do not, by those activities alone, make a person a broker. “By itself” is the important limit. Add negotiation for another for expected compensation and the analysis changes.

### Contract, form, addendum, and amendment

The following are course working terms, not quotations from a universal Texas definitions section.

- A **contract**, as a neutral working term here, is an agreement through which parties express commitments. Later topics classify agreements as valid, void, voidable, or unenforceable; using the word in this module is not a conclusion that a court will enforce it.
- A **contract form** is a structured instrument used to record an agreement. Completing a form does not guarantee that the agreement is valid, wise, complete, or suited to unusual facts.
- An **addendum** is a separate document attached to and incorporated into a contract to address a subject the base form assigns to that addendum. The correct addendum is part of the contract when the parties make it so.
- An **amendment** changes an existing contract after it has become effective. It is not the same thing as correcting a draft before acceptance.
- A **notice** communicates an election, fact, demand, or other matter under a contract or law. A notice is not automatically an amendment. For example, a termination notice may exercise a contractual right without rewriting the contract.
- An **informational item** records a fact called for by the form. Names, dates, addresses, prices, legal descriptions, checkbox elections, and other entries may be informational depending on the form and context. The label does not authorize a license holder to invent a legal clause.
- **Special provisions** is a form location, not a license to practice law. A blank line can create more risk, not more authority.

These distinctions prevent a common mistake: treating every document that touches a contract as interchangeable. If the parties need to change the closing date after the effective date, that is an amendment problem. If the buyer is exercising a termination right already granted by the contract, that is usually a notice problem. If the base form instructs the parties to attach a financing addendum for a selected financing arrangement, that is an addendum problem. If a principal wants an original remedy or contingency drafted for unique facts, that is an attorney problem.

### “Promulgated” and “mandatory” are not magic labels

License holders often use “promulgated form” as shorthand. The held TREC reproduction of § 537.11 speaks more precisely about forms **approved for mandatory use by the Commission** for a type of transaction. Section 1101.155 says the Commission may require use of forms prepared by the Broker-Lawyer Committee and adopted by the Commission. The committee also prepares forms that the Commission may approve for voluntary use. Therefore, ask two separate questions:

1. Is this an authentic current form from an authorized source?
2. Is it mandatory for this transaction, voluntary, or inapplicable?

An authentic TREC form can be the wrong form. An old revision can be an authentic historical form and still be wrong for a current transaction. A current form can be voluntary rather than mandatory. A mandatory form can be inapplicable because the transaction falls within a stated exception. Accuracy requires the form number, revision, transaction type, and legal category—not merely the logo.

## Unit: Texas Real Estate License Act

Tex. Occupations Code § 1101.001 gives chapter 1101 the short title “The Real Estate License Act,” or TRELA. This module does not claim a chapter-wide read. Its held section reads are narrower: they cover the brokerage vocabulary, licensing entry gate, Commission administration and form authority, Broker-Lawyer Committee, qualifying-course approval, specified disciplinary grounds, and unauthorized-practice boundary actually used below. In this course, those exact provisions supply the statutory frame around a license holder's use of private contracts.

### Statute, rule, and contract operate on different subjects

Imagine a resale transaction using Form 20-19. Three layers operate at once:

- The **contract** allocates rights and duties between the buyer and seller according to its terms and applicable law.
- **TRELA and Commission rules** regulate what the license holder may do while helping the parties create and carry out the transaction.
- Other **statutes and law** govern subjects such as seller disclosures, electronic signatures, title, financing, and remedies.

A violation at one layer does not automatically answer every question at another. A license holder can commit a disciplinary violation even if the buyer and seller still have an enforceable contract. A contract can fail or be terminated without proving that the license holder violated TRELA. A license holder can use the correct form and still enter a fact inaccurately. Conversely, a license holder can understand what the parties want and still lack authority to draft the clause needed to accomplish it.

Section 1101.151 explains why Commission rules matter. The Commission must administer chapters 1101 and 1102. It may adopt and enforce rules necessary to administer them and establish conduct and ethics standards. That delegation is the statutory bridge to Commission rules. The bridge should be visible in your analysis. Do not say vaguely that “TREC law” requires something. Say which part comes from the Legislature and which comes from the Commission's rule.

### Licensing is an entry gate, not a drafting license

Section 1101.351 generally prohibits acting or representing oneself as a broker, sales agent, or residential rental locator without a license. It also says a sales agent must be sponsored by and act for a licensed broker. The license authorizes brokerage within the statutory and regulatory boundaries. It does not confer a law license. That is why the same chapter that requires a real estate license also commands suspension or revocation for specified unauthorized practice of law in § 1101.654.

This distinction answers a frequent misconception: “I am licensed, so I can write the deal the client requests.” A real estate license permits regulated brokerage. It does not transform the license holder into the principal's legal drafter. Form competence means knowing when to use a form, how to complete authorized informational choices accurately, when the form does not fit, and when to stop.

### Discipline gives the form rules consequences

The form-use obligation is not aspirational. Occupations Code § 1101.652(a-1)(3) authorizes discipline when a license holder fails to use a contract form required by the Commission under § 1101.155. Section 1101.652 also contains conduct grounds that can overlap a contract problem: negligence or incompetence, dishonest or bad-faith conduct, material misrepresentation, failure to disclose a known significant defect, a false promise likely to influence entry into an agreement, failure to account for money, inducing a party to break a sale or lease contract to substitute another, failure to provide a signer a requested copy of a transaction document, and other listed conduct.

Do not collapse these grounds into a slogan that every contract mistake causes revocation. Read the elements. Some grounds require knowledge; some require a particular context; some apply to conduct while engaged in brokerage; some use discretionary language about what the Commission may do. The course will return to specific grounds when their subject appears. The important Topic 01 point is structural: the Legislature authorized form rules and separately attached disciplinary consequences to defined failures.

### A method for reading a TRELA problem

Use this five-part analysis:

1. **Actor.** Is the person a broker, sales agent, license holder, principal, attorney, owner, or someone outside chapter 1101? Cite the definition or status provision.
2. **Activity.** What exactly is the person doing—showing, negotiating, completing a form, explaining an informational item, recommending legal language, holding money, or giving an opinion about title?
3. **Authority.** Which statute or rule permits, requires, or prohibits the activity?
4. **Instrument.** Which current form, addendum, amendment, or notice fits the transaction? Does the form itself direct attachment of another instrument?
5. **Consequence and response.** Is the problem a form-selection correction, a need for broker supervision, a required attorney referral, a disclosure issue, a contract right, a disciplinary risk, or more than one?

Example: a buyer asks a sales agent to write a custom clause requiring the seller to pay “all future foundation expenses.” The actor is a sponsored sales agent. The activity is not merely entering a price or date; it is drafting a broad future obligation and remedy allocation. The held TREC reproduction of § 537.11 prohibits drafting or recommending contract language defining or affecting rights, obligations, or remedies. The agent should not solve the issue by changing the adjective or moving the sentence to special provisions. The response is to involve the sponsoring broker and advise the principal to consult an attorney. If an attorney prepares language and the applicable form/rule conditions permit its use, the parties can decide whether to accept it.

Example: a seller tells the listing agent the agreed refrigerator exclusion and the current form provides a field for exclusions. Entering the factual item as instructed is different from inventing a seller-favorable remedy if the refrigerator remains at closing. The first can be form completion; the second changes legal consequences.

## Unit: Unauthorized Practice of Law

The boundary between real estate brokerage and legal practice is the operational center of Topic 01. License holders are expected to use contract forms competently. They are not authorized to become transaction counsel simply because a form has blank space.

Two sources work together. Occupations Code § 1101.654 supplies the statutory discipline rule and safe harbor. The held TREC reproduction of 22 TAC § 537.11 supplies detailed conduct rules. Read both; neither is a substitute for the other.

### The statutory rule

Section 1101.654(a) requires suspension or revocation when a non-attorney license or certificate holder, for consideration or in connection with the regulated relationship, does either specified act:

- drafts an instrument other than a § 1101.155 form that transfers or otherwise affects an interest in real property; or
- advises a person about an instrument's validity or legal sufficiency, or about the validity of title.

Subsection (b) then states a safe harbor for a license or certificate holder who completes a contract form for a sale, exchange, option, or lease incidentally to acting as a broker when the form fits one of three categories: Commission-adopted for that transaction, prepared by a Texas attorney and approved by the attorney for that transaction type, or prepared by the owner or an attorney and required by the owner.

The safe harbor is not unlimited. “Completes” is not “redrafts.” “Incidental to acting as a broker” is not a separate legal-document business. A form approved for one transaction type does not become approved for another. The statute does not authorize the license holder to advise whether the resulting language is legally sufficient.

### The detailed rule boundary

The held TREC reproduction of § 537.11 lists prohibited conduct. A license holder may not practice law; directly or indirectly give or attempt to give legal advice; advise about the legal effect of forms or other instruments affecting title; opine on title status or validity; draft or recommend language defining or affecting a principal's rights, obligations, or remedies, including escalation, appraisal, or contingency clauses; add informational items when the Commission has approved another mandatory form for that purpose; discourage a principal from employing an attorney; or obtain legal advice from an attorney for a principal in a transaction where the license holder acts as agent.

The last prohibition is easy to misunderstand. A license holder can employ an attorney to represent the license holder. A license holder can reimburse a principal for attorney fees. But the license holder should not become the intermediary who obtains legal advice **for** the principal. The principal and attorney need their own professional relationship, with the attorney answering to the principal.

The rule preserves important brokerage functions. It does not limit the fiduciary obligation to disclose known pertinent facts. It does not prohibit explaining the meaning of informational items or choices in a form, so long as the explanation does not become law practice or legal advice. It allows adding informational items to an authorized form. It also allows a change specifically instructed in writing by a principal when the change is made conspicuous—for example by underlining additions or striking through deletions. But a principal's written instruction does not convert the license holder into the author of legal language. The safer use of that provision is a principal supplying the exact change after advice from the principal's attorney, with the license holder recording it conspicuously as instructed.

### Explanation versus legal advice

The distinction is factual and contextual, but the following comparison helps.

| Usually factual form explanation | Legal advice or drafting risk |
| --- | --- |
| “This checkbox selects the third-party financing addendum.” | “Choose this because it guarantees you can recover every cost.” |
| “Paragraph 9 states the closing date entered here.” | “This wording makes time of the essence in every circumstance.” |
| “The form asks the parties to identify exclusions.” | “Write this sentence so the fixture is legally excluded even if title law says otherwise.” |
| “The termination notice contains several reasons; we must identify the right actually being exercised.” | “Use this reason even though the facts do not fit; the seller probably cannot challenge it.” |
| “The form provides a space for a factual legal description.” | “This description is legally sufficient to convey every interest you intend.” |

The left column describes the document. The right column predicts legal effect, selects a legal strategy, or designs new rights. A license holder can and should know the form thoroughly. Competence is not silence. But the competent explanation stays tethered to what the form says and does not promise a court's conclusion.

### Blank space does not enlarge authority

Special-provisions fields create the most persistent trap. The form may provide room for factual statements or business details that do not belong elsewhere. That does not authorize a license holder to draft a novel contingency, remedy, waiver, release, indemnity, escalation mechanism, appraisal formula, or title conclusion. Moving a prohibited clause from an email into a form does not cure it. Asking an artificial-intelligence system to draft the sentence does not cure it. Copying language from another transaction does not cure it. A clause can be grammatically polished and legally dangerous.

Use a three-question screen:

1. Is the entry a fact or business term expressly called for by the form, or does it define legal rights, duties, remedies, waivers, or conditions?
2. Has the Commission supplied another mandatory form or addendum for this purpose?
3. Did the principal supply the exact change in writing, preferably after independent legal advice, or is the license holder creating or recommending it?

If the entry changes legal consequences, if another mandatory form exists, or if the license holder is the author of a new legal solution, stop and escalate.

### The attorney referral is an affirmative duty in unusual matters

The held TREC reproduction directs a license holder to advise principals to consult an attorney when a transaction involves unusual matters that should be reviewed before execution or when the instrument must be acknowledged and filed of record. It also directs the license holder to advise principals that the instrument they are about to execute is binding on them.

This is more than a generic disclaimer at the bottom of every email. A useful referral identifies the reason without supplying the legal answer: “Your requested survival clause allocates obligations after closing and the standard form does not resolve the unusual term you want. I cannot draft or advise on its legal effect. Please have your Texas real estate attorney prepare or approve the language before you sign.” Document that advice and give the principal a genuine opportunity to obtain counsel.

Do not select the attorney for the purpose of controlling the advice. Do not summarize the attorney's conclusion as though it were yours. Do not tell the other party that “our lawyer says this is enforceable” unless the lawyer represents the person to whom that advice is attributed and communication is authorized. Each principal should understand whom the attorney represents.

## Unit: The Broker-Lawyer Committee

The Texas Real Estate Broker-Lawyer Committee is not a private nickname for TREC staff. It is a statutory committee created in Occupations Code chapter 1101. Section 1101.251 defines “committee” for the subchapter. Section 1101.252 gives it thirteen members: six appointed by the Commission, six members of the State Bar of Texas appointed by the State Bar president, and one public member appointed by the governor. Section 1101.253 establishes staggered six-year terms and rules for holdover service and vacancies.

The appointing structure matters, but do not add labels the statute does not supply. Section 1101.252 identifies six Commission appointees, six State Bar members, and one public member; it does not say that every Commission appointee must be a broker or state a separate rationale for each appointing category. The structure places Commission, State Bar, and public appointments in one statutory committee whose assigned work concerns both practical transaction instruments and legal rights. Membership on the committee does not make a Commission appointee a lawyer or a State Bar appointee a Commission official. The committee acts as a committee within the statutory assignment.

### Drafting and adoption are separate acts

Section 1101.254 directs the committee to draft and revise contract forms capable of being standardized, for two named purposes: expediting real estate transactions and minimizing controversy. The forms must contain safeguards adequate to protect the principals. Section 1101.155 then authorizes the Commission to adopt rules in the public interest requiring license holders to use committee-prepared forms.

This produces a sequence:

1. The Legislature creates the committee and states its mission.
2. The committee drafts or revises a standardizable form.
3. The Commission considers and adopts or approves the form through its authority.
4. The applicable rule determines mandatory or voluntary use and the circumstances.
5. The license holder selects the current form for the actual transaction and completes it within the practice boundary.

Skipping a step creates bad analysis. A committee draft is not automatically a mandatory Commission form. Commission adoption does not make a form appropriate for every transaction. Mandatory-use status does not authorize creative modification. Proper form use does not guarantee that unusual facts need no attorney.

### Standardization is a risk-control system

Standardization reduces avoidable variation. Defined paragraphs appear in consistent locations. Addenda are integrated through checklists. Notices and warnings are repeated. Signature and receipt fields capture evidence. Revision control allows a brokerage to identify obsolete files. Training can focus on a shared instrument rather than dozens of local forms.

But standardization also creates cognitive risk. Familiarity can turn into automatic completion. A license holder may remember an old revision, assume a paragraph stayed the same, or select a familiar form before screening the transaction. The safeguard is a disciplined workflow: identify transaction type, identify parties and property, screen for mandatory form and exceptions, verify current revision, identify required addenda, enter facts from reliable source documents, obtain instructions, review for internal consistency, provide copies, and preserve the file.

The committee's statutory mission to minimize controversy does not promise zero controversy. A standard form cannot predict every property, financing arrangement, title issue, or personal objective. Standardization is strongest where a transaction can actually be standardized. The moment facts become unusual, the attorney-referral rule becomes more important, not less.

### Applied exercise: map the institution, authority, and artifact

For each statement, identify whether it belongs to the Legislature, Broker-Lawyer Committee, Commission, license holder, principal, or attorney. More than one may participate, but name the actor responsible for the stated act.

1. Creates the thirteen-member committee.
2. Drafts and revises forms capable of standardization.
3. Requires safeguards adequate to protect principals.
4. Adopts a rule requiring use of a form for a transaction type.
5. Decides which current approved form applies to today's facts.
6. Supplies the price, closing date, and other negotiated business choices.
7. Drafts a unique post-closing indemnity for one principal.
8. Ensures the selected form revision is current in the brokerage's form library.
9. Decides whether to accept a proposed contract.
10. Advises a principal about the legal consequences of a novel clause.

**Model analysis.** The Legislature performs item 1 and imposes the duties in items 2 and 3. The committee performs the drafting and revision in item 2 under that mandate. The Commission acts in item 4. The broker's supervised workflow and license holder perform items 5 and 8, with the principal making the business decisions and accepting or rejecting the transaction in items 6 and 9. A principal's attorney performs items 7 and 10. The license holder can communicate the principal's decisions and explain informational choices but should not take ownership of the legal result.

## Unit: Use of Promulgated Contract Forms

This unit converts the authority structure into a transaction workflow. The held TREC reproduction of § 537.11 begins with a mandatory-form command: when a license holder negotiates a contract binding the sale, exchange, option, or lease of an interest in real property, the license holder must use only contract forms approved for mandatory use by the Commission for that type of transaction, subject to stated exceptions.

### First classify the transaction

Form selection begins before opening a template. Ask:

- Is the license holder acting as agent or solely as principal?
- Is the transaction a sale, exchange, option, or lease of a real-property interest?
- What property type is involved—one-to-four family resale, condominium, farm and ranch, unimproved land, new construction, or something else?
- Does a federal agency require a different form?
- Has the owner prepared a form or required an attorney-prepared form?
- Has the Commission approved a form for mandatory use for this transaction type?
- Do the financing, title, property condition, disclosure, association, environmental, or other facts trigger an addendum or notice?

Do not select a form solely from the street address or marketing label. A residential-looking property can contain facts that change the form analysis. A condominium unit is not handled merely as a generic house. A farm-and-ranch transaction can involve reservations and improvements that the resale form does not organize the same way. New construction can present completion and warranty issues. The brokerage's intake process should capture the facts before a sales agent sends a draft.

### Apply the four mandatory-form exceptions exactly

The held reproduction identifies four categories outside the opening mandatory-form command:

1. **Solely a principal.** The license holder functions solely as a principal, not as agent. Do not treat this as a general exemption from disclosure duties or other law. It answers the form-use rule's stated category.
2. **Federal agency form.** An agency of the United States requires a different form. The requirement matters; a license holder's preference for another form is not enough.
3. **Owner or owner-required attorney form.** The property owner prepared the form, or an attorney prepared it and the property owner requires it. Preserve evidence of the owner's requirement.
4. **No Commission form approved for mandatory use.** The license holder may use a qualifying attorney- or trade-association-prepared form, or a Broker-Lawyer Committee form approved for voluntary use, within the detailed conditions.

Exception analysis is not “I found a form online.” It is a fact-and-source analysis. If the owner sends a form, confirm that it actually came from the owner and document the instruction. If a federal program requires a document, identify the requirement. If no mandatory form exists, verify the preparer and the required content rather than relying on a filename.

### When no mandatory form exists

Under the held reproduction, a license holder can use a form prepared by a Texas attorney or by a trade association in consultation with a Texas attorney if it contains the listed information: the preparer's identity; the broker or trade association for whom it was prepared; the transaction type for which use was approved; restrictions on use; and, when applicable, an explanation of how it changes rights, obligations, or remedies under a Commission mandatory form together with a statement that license holders may not practice law or give legal advice.

Those conditions perform several functions. They identify who took responsibility for legal drafting. They prevent a form created for one transaction from drifting into another. They expose restrictions. They warn when a form changes the allocation a Commission form would otherwise make. A brokerage should not strip the attribution or limitation page from the document and then treat the remainder as a generic template.

The other option is a form prepared by the Broker-Lawyer Committee and approved by the Commission for voluntary use. “Voluntary” does not mean casual. The form still must fit the transaction, be current, and be completed within the same legal-practice boundaries.

### Never use a blank in place of an approved addendum

The rule reproduction specifically prohibits adding informational items to a Commission-approved form if the Commission approved another form for mandatory use for that purpose. This rule fights a predictable shortcut: placing financing, appraisal, sale-of-other-property, back-up, or other terms into free text when the form system supplies a specific addendum.

Why does the shortcut matter? The addendum contains a coordinated set of definitions, deadlines, notices, elections, and consequences. A sentence improvised in special provisions may omit part of the mechanism or conflict with the base contract. The license holder also crosses from entering information into designing legal rights.

Use the form's own addenda checklist as a control, but do not let the checklist replace screening. A box does not decide whether the underlying facts trigger the addendum. Read the current form and addendum. Ask the principal the business question. If the fit is uncertain or the principal wants a result the form does not provide, refer to counsel.

### Current form, complete form, internally consistent form

The course holds the current TREC form set as acquired on August 31, 2026 and a page-level read of eight priority instruments. The One to Four Family Residential Contract (Resale), Form 20-19, is held and read page by page. The read confirms its structure: parties and property; sales price and financing; earnest money and termination option; title, survey, objections, and notices; property condition and disclosures; closing and possession; expenses; prorations; casualty; default; mediation; escrow; representations; notices; addenda; signatures; broker information; and receipts.

That read does **not** authorize use of Form 20-19 in every sale. It does not replace current TREC instructions or broker supervision. It establishes what the held instrument contains so the course can teach it accurately later.

When a form is selected, verify:

- the form number and revision date;
- every page is present;
- names match the parties' legal identities and source documents;
- the property description is drawn from an appropriate source and is consistent across documents;
- numbers reconcile across price, financing, deposits, credits, and expenses;
- dates and time periods do not conflict;
- checkbox elections do not contradict inserted text;
- referenced addenda are attached and attached addenda are identified;
- special provisions do not duplicate or conflict with a mandatory addendum;
- signature, effective-date, broker-information, and receipt fields are handled in the proper sequence; and
- the provider workflow delivers each signer a completed copy and preserves delivery evidence. Separately, Occupations Code § 1101.652(b)(28) makes it a disciplinary ground to fail or refuse, on request, to provide a signed copy of a document relating to a transaction.

This is quality control, not legal advice. You are testing the document for completeness and internal consistency, not promising enforceability.

### Explaining choices without choosing for the principal

A form may present elections. The license holder can identify the available choices, explain where the form records them, describe the factual information needed, and communicate offers and counteroffers. The principal makes the business decision. The license holder should not convert explanation into a legal recommendation outside competence.

Compare:

- “The form provides option A and option B; here is what each paragraph says and where your selection is entered.”
- “Option A is legally safer for you and guarantees the court will award your deposit.”

The first stays with the instrument. The second predicts legal effect and outcome. A principal who asks “Which one protects me from every possible claim?” is asking for legal advice even if the question points to a checkbox.

### Written instructions and conspicuous changes

The held reproduction provides that, when specifically instructed in writing by a principal, a license holder may add language or strike language as long as the change is conspicuous, such as through underlining or strike-through. Apply the provision narrowly and document the provenance.

A strong file should show:

1. the principal's exact written instruction;
2. the date and transaction;
3. the version of the document before the change;
4. the conspicuous change in the document;
5. any broker review required by policy;
6. the attorney referral when legal effect or unusual matter was involved; and
7. the copy delivered to the principal and other party.

Do not paraphrase an unclear instruction into legal language. Ask the principal to clarify in writing or obtain attorney-prepared language. Do not hide changes in a clean retyped document when the rule calls for the change to be conspicuous. Do not use the procedure to defeat another mandatory form.

### Advise that the instrument is binding

The held reproduction directs the license holder to advise principals that the instrument they are about to execute is binding. That advice should occur before execution, not after the electronic-signature notification arrives. It should not be diluted into “you can always get out during the option period.” Contract rights depend on the actual instrument and facts. A termination option may require timely notice and payment mechanics. Other contingencies have their own conditions. The accurate message is that signing creates legal obligations and the principal should understand the document and obtain legal advice about questions of effect.

### Reproduction is controlled

The held reproduction says Commission-approved forms may be reproduced, including by software, only when text and format—including sizing, spacing, and pagination—are identical to the published version, subject to limited allowances for a business name or logo outside the border and scaling for smaller-screen viewing when the final executed copy otherwise complies.

This matters in a digital workflow. A software vendor cannot silently rewrite warnings, reorder paragraphs, compress pages, remove a receipt, or insert brokerage marketing into the form body. Mobile display may scale, but the executed copy must preserve the required instrument. A brokerage should compare its generated PDF against the official current PDF and repeat that validation after a vendor update.

### Provider workflow: document control from intake to archive

The following workflow is StateCourse methodology, not a TREC-prescribed checklist. It translates the statutory and rule boundaries into repeatable quality control. A brokerage may impose additional supervision and retention requirements.

**1. Open a source record before opening a draft.** Record the transaction type, property type, representation role, principal, other party, financing path, occupancy, known association status, and any unusual request. Identify the source that answers each screening question. “The last transaction used this form” is not a source. If an exception is claimed, record the fact supporting it: sole-principal capacity, federal requirement, owner-prepared form, owner-required attorney form, or absence of a mandatory form with a qualifying alternative.

**2. Pull from a controlled form library.** The library should identify form number, title, revision, status, official source URL, acquisition date, and hash or another reliable version control. Lock obsolete versions against new use while retaining them for historical files. A filename like `resale-final.pdf` is inadequate because “final” describes someone's past workflow, not the Commission's revision. The course's acquisition catalog performs this control for held TREC forms: it pairs each official PDF with its source page, date, bytes, and cryptographic hash.

**3. Build an attachment matrix.** List every addendum, notice, disclosure, exhibit, and source document that the base form or facts may require. For each, mark “required,” “not applicable,” or “requires broker/attorney decision,” with the reason. Compare the matrix with the base form's addenda checklist. The two lists should reconcile, but neither should be treated as self-executing. A checked box without an attachment is a defect; an attachment not incorporated where required is also a defect.

**4. Separate facts from drafted legal solutions.** Create two columns for requested entries. In the first, place facts the current form calls for: party names, price, dates, addresses, described property, selected choices, and amounts. In the second, place requests that allocate rights, create conditions, waive duties, define default, expand remedies, or survive closing. The second column receives broker and attorney review. If the license holder cannot confidently classify an entry, that uncertainty is itself a reason to escalate rather than guess.

**5. Record information provenance.** For each significant factual entry, preserve where it came from. Party names may come from identification and the parties' instruction; legal description from an appropriate title or deed source; association information from the applicable documents; financing figures from the negotiated offer and lender information; exclusions from the seller's instruction. Do not silently “correct” a principal's legal name, property description, or business term from memory. Surface conflicts to the principal and broker.

**6. Run an internal-consistency pass.** Read the assembled package as one instrument set. Check that the financing selection matches the addendum; cash amounts reconcile; dates are possible; the option and earnest-money entries do not conflict; addenda referenced are attached; attached documents identify the same parties and property; amendments refer to the correct effective contract; notices identify a real contractual or statutory basis; and signature blocks belong to the correct version. This pass is not an opinion on legal sufficiency. It is the document equivalent of verifying that every page and field belongs to the same transaction.

**7. Run the practice-boundary pass.** Highlight every free-text addition, deletion, and nonstandard attachment. For each one, identify its author and authority. If a principal supplied an exact written instruction, preserve it and make the change conspicuous as the held rule reproduction requires. If an attorney prepared language, identify whom the attorney represents and the transaction type for which it was approved. If the license holder created or recommended language affecting rights or remedies, stop the process and correct it before circulation.

**8. Run the communication pass.** Before signature, make sure the principal received the complete assembled package, not merely the signature pages. Identify every attachment. Advise that the instrument is binding. Invite questions about factual entries and form choices. Route legal-effect questions to counsel without delaying until after acceptance. Avoid statements such as “this is standard, so you do not need to read it” or “you can always cancel.” Standardization and cancellation rights have limits.

**9. Control versions during negotiation.** Give each circulated draft a clear version marker or transaction-platform history. When a counterproposal changes a field, compare the whole returned instrument rather than assuming only the discussed item changed. Preserve the accepted version and evidence of the effective-date process separately from rejected drafts. Do not assemble a “clean copy” from pieces of different versions unless the parties and authorized professionals have created and reviewed that instrument through a proper process.

**10. Deliver and archive.** Provide the required copies promptly, including attachments and receipts. Preserve the exact executed package in a format that can be reopened. Retain the principal's written instructions, attorney-prepared language, form-source record, delivery evidence, and any corrected version. A later reviewer should be able to answer: Which official form was used? Why did it fit? Which exception applied, if any? Who supplied each nonstandard change? What did the principal receive and when?

This workflow catches four different defect classes. A **selection defect** uses the wrong form or exception. A **completion defect** enters facts inaccurately or leaves required choices unresolved. A **practice defect** crosses into legal drafting or advice. A **record defect** leaves the brokerage unable to prove what occurred. Fixing one does not cure the others. The correct current form can still be completed inaccurately; perfect recordkeeping does not legalize unauthorized drafting; attorney-prepared language does not cure use of an obsolete mandatory form.

### Counteroffers, amendments, and notices need separate version logic

The form package also changes character over time. Before an agreement becomes effective, parties may exchange offers and counteroffers. After an effective contract exists, a negotiated change belongs in a proper amendment rather than in a silent rewrite of the original. An election under an existing right may require a notice rather than an amendment. The exact legal effect is taught with the relevant instruments later, but Topic 01 establishes the control rule: **identify the document's function before selecting its template.**

A transaction file should never make a reviewer guess whether a marked-up contract was a rejected draft, an accepted counteroffer, or an amendment after effectiveness. Label drafts, preserve communications, and use the correct current instrument. If a party asks to “just change the original PDF so it looks clean,” explain that transaction history and signatures cannot be rewritten casually. Involve the broker and counsel when the proper document or effect is uncertain.

### Accessibility and completeness are part of form fidelity

An electronic form can preserve its visual appearance and still fail a user if fields are unlabeled, reading order is scrambled, signatures cannot be completed with assistive technology, or the final PDF omits accessible text. The held § 537.11 reproduction speaks expressly to identical text and format and allows scaling for small screens; it does not resolve every accessibility implementation detail. StateCourse's provider method therefore treats accessibility as an additional delivery control, not as permission to alter required form content. Test the platform with keyboard navigation and screen readers, preserve the official text, and provide an accessible path to review the entire instrument before signature.

Form fidelity also includes page completeness. Some official instruments carry broker-information and receipt pages after the party signature page. Stopping at the signatures can omit operational evidence. The priority-form register records every page precisely so authoring and later document-lab work cannot quietly forget the last page.

### Applied exercise: select the form source before touching the form

For each fact pattern, identify the first source question, the likely form-source category, and the action the license holder must avoid. Do not draft a clause.

1. A licensed broker sells a personally owned vacant lot and acts solely as principal.
2. A seller instructs the listing broker in writing to use a contract prepared by the seller's Texas attorney.
3. A buyer wants an appraisal-gap clause, and a current mandatory addendum addresses the subject.
4. A federal agency requires its own contract form for the transaction.
5. No Commission mandatory form exists; the broker has a form prepared by a Texas attorney for this transaction type, but the attribution page was removed.
6. A sales agent finds a prior revision of Form 20-19 in an old transaction folder.
7. The correct current base form is selected, but the financing paragraph selects third-party financing and the addendum is absent.
8. A principal emails exact attorney-prepared language and specifically instructs the agent to insert it; the agent plans to retype the document without showing the addition.
9. A buyer asks whether a provision guarantees specific performance.
10. A seller asks the agent to explain where the form identifies excluded accessories.
11. A brokerage software vendor compresses a twelve-page official form into nine pages and moves warnings.
12. The parties want an unusual post-closing leaseback term that the available standard instrument does not address adequately.

**Model analysis.** Item 1 requires careful application of the principal exception, not a conclusion that all law disappears. Item 2 fits the owner-required attorney-form category if the facts and instruction are documented. Item 3 requires the approved form rather than improvised free text. Item 4 turns on the federal requirement. Item 5 is not ready for use because the required provenance and limitations are missing. Item 6 requires current-form verification. Item 7 is incomplete and internally inconsistent. Item 8 fails the conspicuous-change control even though written instruction exists. Item 9 asks for legal effect and belongs with counsel. Item 10 is a factual form explanation. Item 11 violates the reproduction control. Item 12 triggers attorney review rather than invention by the license holder.

### Scenario: the “simple” custom contingency

**Facts.** Maya represents a buyer considering a one-to-four family resale. The current mandatory resale form applies. The buyer wants the purchase to depend on the buyer's employer approving remote work, the buyer selling a cryptocurrency holding above a stated value, and the buyer being “fully satisfied with all neighborhood conditions.” The buyer asks Maya to put one sentence in special provisions: “Contract is contingent on Buyer's sole satisfaction with employment, finances, and neighborhood.” The listing agent says everyone uses language like that and asks Maya to send the offer tonight. No current TREC addendum in the brokerage library appears to implement all three conditions together.

**Tasks.** Identify the form, licensing, and legal-practice questions. State what Maya may explain, what Maya must not draft or promise, what should be documented, and the next safe step.

**Model analysis.** Maya can identify the current resale form, explain its informational fields and existing choices, and screen whether any approved addendum addresses part of the buyer's objectives. She should not draft or recommend a broad satisfaction contingency. The proposed sentence creates conditions affecting both parties' rights and remedies, uses subjective standards, and combines unrelated risks. The held § 537.11 reproduction specifically identifies contingency clauses within the drafting prohibition. Because no mandatory form appears to solve the full request and the matter is unusual, Maya should involve her sponsoring broker and advise the buyer to consult a Texas real estate attorney. The buyer or attorney should communicate exact, approved language if the buyer still wants to proceed. Maya should document the request, the boundary explanation, the attorney referral, any written instruction, the current form revision, and the final document delivered. Speed and the other agent's custom do not enlarge Maya's authority.

**Passing standard.** A passing response separates the private business objective from the legal drafting task, cites § 1101.654 and the held § 537.11 reproduction, refuses to invent language, checks for a mandatory form, and routes the unusual matter to independent counsel without telling the buyer whether the desired contingency will be enforceable.

## Unit: Deceptive Trade Practices Consumer Protection Act (DTPA)

The Texas DTPA is Business and Commerce Code chapter 17, subchapter E. It is not a general label for anything unfair. A useful analysis moves through defined gates: consumer status, actionable conduct, exemption, exception, causation and reliance where required, and remedy. Skipping a gate produces overstatement.

### Gate one: consumer and transaction scope

Section 17.45 defines **goods** to include real property purchased or leased for use. **Services** include work, labor, or service purchased or leased for use, including services connected to a sale or repair of goods. A **consumer** is a person or qualifying entity that seeks or acquires goods or services by purchase or lease, subject to the large-business exclusion stated in the definition. “Trade” and “commerce” include advertising, offering, sale, lease, or distribution of property and other things of value affecting Texans.

Consumer status is not established merely because someone was treated badly. Ask what the claimant sought or acquired and by what transaction. The goods-or-services objective matters. A brokerage service can be a service; real property can be a good within this subchapter's definition. The exact claim and relationship still matter.

Section 17.45 also defines an **unconscionable action or course of action** as one that, to a consumer's detriment, takes advantage of the consumer's lack of knowledge, ability, experience, or capacity to a grossly unfair degree. “Grossly” is a meaningful limit; every unfavorable bargain is not automatically unconscionable.

The mental-state definitions matter at the remedy stage. “Knowingly” requires actual awareness of the falsity, deception, unfairness, or other specified matter, although actual awareness may be inferred from objective manifestations. “Intentionally” adds specific intent that the consumer detrimentally rely or remain detrimentally ignorant, with the statutory inference routes. Do not use “intentional” as a casual synonym for “the person did the act.”

### Gate two: identify actionable conduct

Section 17.46(a) declares false, misleading, or deceptive acts in trade or commerce unlawful. For private relief under § 17.50(a)(1), subsection (d) limits the relevant phrase to specifically enumerated acts in § 17.46(b). Therefore, identify the subdivision rather than saying only “this seems deceptive.”

Real estate examples commonly implicate:

- § 17.46(b)(5): representing sponsorship, approval, characteristics, uses, benefits, or quantities that do not exist, or misrepresenting a person's status or affiliation;
- § 17.46(b)(7): representing a standard, quality, or grade different from the truth;
- § 17.46(b)(12): representing that an agreement has rights, remedies, or obligations it does not have or that law prohibits;
- § 17.46(b)(14): misrepresenting an agent's authority to negotiate final consumer-transaction terms; and
- § 17.46(b)(24): intentionally withholding known information to induce a transaction the consumer would not have entered had the information been disclosed.

Subdivision (24) has several elements. The information concerns goods or services. It was known at the time. There was a failure to disclose. The failure was intended to induce the consumer into a transaction. The consumer would not have entered had the information been disclosed. A complaint that “the agent should have known” is not the same as the knowledge and intent specified in this subdivision, though other law may address negligence.

Contract descriptions can create DTPA risk. Telling a consumer that a form guarantees a remedy it does not provide may implicate subdivision (12). Claiming that a license holder has authority to bind a principal when the license holder does not may implicate subdivision (14). Claiming that a course or provider is TREC-approved when it is not could implicate subdivision (5), which is one reason this course carefully states its approval status.

### Gate three: test the broker exemption and its exceptions

Section 17.49(i) generally excludes a claim against a chapter 1101 broker or salesperson arising from an act or omission while acting as a broker or salesperson. But the subsection immediately gives three exceptions:

1. an express misrepresentation of material fact that cannot be characterized as advice, judgment, or opinion;
2. a failure to disclose information in violation of § 17.46(b)(24); or
3. an unconscionable action or course of action that cannot be characterized as advice, judgment, or opinion.

The correct conclusion is not “brokers are exempt from the DTPA” and not “brokers are always covered.” Identify whether the claim arises from the person acting as broker or salesperson, then test each exception. The professional-services exemption in § 17.49(c) follows a similar advice/judgment/opinion structure with listed exceptions, but subsection (i) specifically addresses chapter 1101 license holders and should be read directly.

Two transaction-size provisions also belong in the exemption gate, especially for commercial facts. Section 17.49(f) excludes a claim arising from a written contract only when all three stated conditions are satisfied: the related transaction or project involves total consumer consideration of more than $100,000; the consumer was represented in negotiation by independent legal counsel not identified, suggested, or selected by the defendant or the defendant's agent; and the contract does not involve the consumer's residence. Section 17.49(g) separately excludes a cause of action arising from a transaction or related project involving more than $500,000 in total consumer consideration, again excepting a cause of action involving the consumer's residence. The thresholds are “more than,” not “at least.” Do not apply either provision from price alone; identify the claim, aggregation, counsel, and residence facts the applicable subsection actually requires.

Example: a buyer says a broker's pricing opinion was wrong. The claim may fall within advice, judgment, or opinion, depending on facts. Change the facts: the broker expressly states that the roof was replaced last month despite knowing it was not. That is an express factual representation, not merely advice. Change them again: the broker knows of repeated flooding, hides the reports to induce the buyer to sign, and the buyer would not have signed if told. Section 17.46(b)(24) and the exception in § 17.49(i)(2) become central.

### Gate four: causation and reliance

Section 17.50(a) allows a consumer action when one of four categories is a producing cause of economic damages or mental-anguish damages: an enumerated § 17.46(b) practice relied on to the consumer's detriment, breach of warranty, an unconscionable action, or a specified Insurance Code violation.

For a laundry-list claim under § 17.50(a)(1), detrimental reliance is express. Identify the representation or omission, the decision it affected, and the loss allegedly produced. Do not assume that the mere existence of an inaccurate statement proves every element. Causation is a link between conduct and damages, not just chronology.

This analytical discipline improves transaction practice even before litigation. A license holder should preserve the source of factual statements, retain disclosure delivery evidence, document corrections, avoid unsupported promises, and provide copies. Good files reduce both the likelihood of harm and the uncertainty about what occurred.

### Gate five: remedy tier

Section 17.50(b) begins with economic damages for a prevailing consumer. If the fact finder finds knowing conduct, mental-anguish damages may be available and the trier may award additional damages within the statutory limit tied to economic damages. Intentional conduct permits the more severe additional-damages computation stated in the section. Other remedies include injunction, restoration of money or property, and other proper relief. A prevailing consumer receives court costs and reasonable and necessary attorney's fees under subsection (d). A defendant can obtain fees and costs when the court finds the action groundless in fact or law, brought in bad faith, or brought for harassment under subsection (c).

Do not quote “treble damages” as an automatic result. The statute uses mental-state findings and caps; the trier of fact “may” award additional damages. Identify the actual tier and base. A transaction professional should recognize exposure and preserve facts, not calculate a litigation recovery for a client.

### DTPA and TRELA can overlap without merging

Suppose a license holder knowingly gives a buyer a false factual statement about a significant defect to induce the purchase. TRELA's disciplinary provisions may apply. DTPA analysis may also apply if the consumer, conduct, exemption exception, reliance, causation, and damages gates are met. A contract remedy may exist too. These are separate routes with different elements and decision-makers.

Avoid the phrase “This violates DTPA and voids the contract.” The cited sections do not establish an automatic voiding rule for every violation. Section 17.50 lists remedies. Contract validity and rescission questions require the applicable law and facts. The correct professional response is to correct the record, disclose as required, involve the broker, preserve evidence, and direct the principal to counsel for legal advice.

### Applied exercise: run the DTPA gates

Analyze each scenario in this order: consumer; goods or services; asserted conduct and subdivision; broker exemption; exception; reliance or causation; potential remedy tier. State what additional fact is needed.

1. A buyer purchases a residence after a license holder says, without any basis, “TREC inspected and approved this roof.”
2. A broker gives a supported comparative market analysis that later proves optimistic.
3. A listing agent knows the garage flooded three times, removes the water-remediation invoices, and tells the buyer the garage has never taken water so the buyer will sign.
4. A sales agent says a termination notice automatically guarantees return of earnest money in every dispute.
5. A buyer dislikes the negotiated price after comparable sales decline but identifies no representation, warranty, unconscionable act, or other actionable conduct.
6. A school advertises that its Texas course is TREC approved when the course has never been submitted.

**Model analysis.** Scenario 1 involves real property and possibly services, a specific approval representation under § 17.46(b)(5), and a factual statement rather than mere opinion; consumer reliance and producing cause still require proof. Scenario 2 may fit advice, judgment, or opinion and the broker exemption unless additional facts show an express material misrepresentation or another exception. Scenario 3 squarely raises § 17.46(b)(24), express misrepresentation, and the § 17.49(i) exceptions; knowledge, intent, consumer decision, and damage must be established. Scenario 4 may implicate § 17.46(b)(12), but the listener, reliance, context, and loss matter. Scenario 5 shows that economic disappointment alone does not identify an actionable category. Scenario 6 can implicate false approval or status under subdivision (5); it also illustrates why approval claims must be tied to actual regulator evidence.

### Misconception check

Mark each statement accurate or inaccurate, then repair every inaccurate statement.

1. “Because I hold a real estate license, special provisions allows me to draft any contingency my client requests.”
2. “A TREC form is mandatory whenever it has a TREC form number.”
3. “The Broker-Lawyer Committee drafts standardizable forms; the Commission decides adoption and mandatory or voluntary status through its authority.”
4. “A principal's written instruction can support a conspicuous change, but it does not authorize the license holder to invent legal language or bypass another mandatory form.”
5. “The DTPA never applies to a broker because § 17.49(i) has a broker exemption.”
6. “An electronic platform may change the text and pagination of a Commission form if all parties click consent.”
7. “A form can establish what its paragraph says, but a statute or rule supplies the external legal obligation.”
8. “A license holder should advise a principal to consult an attorney when unusual matters require legal review before execution.”

**Answers.** Statements 3, 4, 7, and 8 are accurate. Statement 1 ignores § 1101.654 and the drafting prohibition in the held § 537.11 reproduction. Statement 2 ignores transaction fit, mandatory versus voluntary status, current revision, and exceptions. Statement 5 ignores the three exceptions in § 17.49(i). Statement 6 conflicts with the reproduction controls in the held rule copy; party consent does not rewrite the license holder's regulatory duty.

### Module recap

Texas contract practice is a source-and-role discipline.

- TRELA is Occupations Code chapter 1101. It defines brokerage actors, gives TREC administrative and rulemaking authority, governs licensing, and supplies discipline.
- The held TREC reproduction of 22 TAC § 537.11 governs mandatory form use and the practice boundary in detail. Its codified Secretary of State text has not been compared, and that limitation remains open.
- A real estate license authorizes regulated brokerage, not law practice. Section 1101.654 and the rule reproduction separate form completion and factual explanation from drafting and legal opinions.
- The Broker-Lawyer Committee has thirteen members and a statutory mission to draft and revise standardizable forms that expedite transactions, minimize controversy, and protect principals. The Commission's adoption decision is a separate step.
- Form selection requires transaction classification, current-version verification, exception analysis, required addenda, accurate informational entries, internal-consistency review, copy delivery, and preservation.
- A blank line does not enlarge authority. Another mandatory addendum cannot be replaced with free text. A principal's exact written instruction must be documented and made conspicuous, and unusual legal matters go to independent counsel.
- DTPA analysis proceeds through consumer status, actionable conduct, broker exemption and exceptions, causation and reliance, and remedy tier. Neither “brokers are exempt” nor “everything unfair is DTPA” is accurate.

Before advancing, you should be able to look at any proposed contract action and say: **who is acting, what the act is, which source controls it, which current instrument fits, what the license holder may explain, and where attorney work begins.** That is the foundation for every later topic in Law of Contracts.
