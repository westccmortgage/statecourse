## T03U1 — Elements of a Valid Contract

A useful formation analysis asks whether competent parties objectively agreed on sufficiently definite terms for a lawful bargain supported by consideration. Real-estate transactions also require attention to the applicable writing and signature rules. Do not treat this list as a magic checklist in which any signature cures every other problem. The person may have signed the wrong version, the offer may have expired, the signer may lack authority, or an essential matter may remain for future negotiation.

Mutual assent is assessed from the parties’ outward words and conduct in context. A party’s private hope is not a communicated term. An agent’s enthusiasm is not the principal’s acceptance. A message saying “the price sounds good; we still need agreement on the property and financing” differs from unqualified assent to a complete offer. The practical file therefore preserves the full offer, all proposed changes, evidence of authority, signatures, and communications. An isolated screenshot can omit the language that changes the result.

Definiteness concerns what the parties have actually agreed to do. In a land sale, identify the parties, the property, the price or agreed means of determining it, and the other essential bargain terms required in context. The court must be able to understand the obligation rather than create a bargain the parties did not make. A promise to “work out a fair price later” raises a different issue from a stated price with a routine arithmetic calculation. The license holder should identify missing business terms early and use authorized instruments; a contested enforceability conclusion belongs to counsel.

Consideration is bargained-for legal value. In an ordinary bilateral sale, the seller’s promise to convey and the buyer’s promise to pay support the bargain. Earnest money is an agreed deposit with its own delivery and default consequences. A failure to deposit it does not justify the automatic announcement that no contract ever existed. Review the actual form’s provision, identify the unmet duty and available response, and preserve the timeline. A promise motivated only by gratitude for a completed past favor is not automatically a new bargained-for exchange.

Capacity and authority require different checks. Capacity concerns the party’s legal ability to enter the transaction. Authority concerns one person’s power to bind another. An adult may possess personal capacity but lack authority to sign for an LLC. A power of attorney may exist but not authorize the proposed transaction. A person may have authority to negotiate while lacking authority to accept the final terms. Record what is known and refer disputed legal capacity or authority before execution.

Lawful purpose is likewise more than a seller saying “we do this all the time.” The parties cannot make prohibited conduct lawful by agreement. A term violating public policy or a statute may affect a provision, a remedy, or the whole arrangement depending on the governing law. A license holder should not draft an arrangement intended to avoid a statutory protection and should not promise that an unlawful provision becomes enforceable when both parties initial it.

The signed-writing issue is distinct. [Business & Commerce Code § 26.01(a), (b)(4)–(6)](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/BC/htm/BC.26.htm) applies to real-estate sales, leases longer than one year, and agreements not to be performed within one year from making. [Property Code § 5.021](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/PR/htm/PR.5.htm) addresses conveyance of the specified estates. [Occupations Code § 1101.806(c)](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/OC/htm/OC.1101.htm) separately governs an action to recover a real-estate sale or purchase commission. Meeting one writing requirement does not prove compliance with the others.

The Supreme Court of Texas’s decision in [Chalker Energy Partners III, LLC v. Le Norman Operating LLC, No. 18-0352, opinion pages 7–13](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1445957/180352.pdf) illustrates the importance of the parties’ stated conditions of formation. The parties had agreed that no transaction agreement would exist before an executed and delivered definitive agreement. Their later emails did not satisfy that condition. The practical lesson is to read negotiations in their contractual context rather than equating an enthusiastic message with a completed bargain.

In a hypothetical residential file, a buyer signs an offer at $412,000 with a specified financing addendum. The seller signs at $418,000 and removes the financing protection. That is a materially different proposal. The buyer’s earlier signature on the original version does not establish acceptance of the seller’s changes. The agent should communicate the counteroffer accurately and preserve each version. Writing the seller’s signature date into an effective-date blank cannot manufacture agreement.

Now change the facts: the seller signs the exact offer but the buyer’s broker receives the signed copy only after a disputed withdrawal message. The contract question turns on the actual communications, governing terms, and applicable law. Do not decide it from the order of files in a phone gallery. Preserve metadata, recipients, attachments, and the parties’ instructions, notify the broker promptly, and advise legal review. Accurate evidence is the agent’s most useful contribution when formation is contested.

### Formation evidence under changing facts

A formation analysis is strongest when it isolates the fact that changes the outcome. Start with an identified buyer, an identified seller with authority, a sufficiently described parcel, an agreed price, an authorized written contract, and evidence that both parties accepted the same terms. That is the working baseline. Do not assume that adding a signature preserves the baseline when the signature accompanies changed terms or a qualification.

Change only the property description. The buyer intends to acquire the house and the adjacent vacant lot, while the written offer identifies only the house lot. The buyer’s private intention cannot expand the signed description. A marketing photograph showing both lots does not necessarily establish that both were offered and accepted. The agent should identify the discrepancy before execution, obtain the correct source documents and instructions, and use the authorized process. If the parties already signed, a legal question about the bargain and any correction must be handled with counsel rather than a quiet edit.

Change only the signer. The owner is an LLC, but the signature is supplied by an employee who ordinarily schedules maintenance. The employee’s adulthood and familiarity with the property establish neither actual authority nor every basis on which authority might be asserted. Ask for appropriate authority evidence through title and counsel. An email address using the company’s domain is not by itself a legal determination that the employee can sell company real estate.

Change only the negotiation language. The parties agree on a price in a message but expressly state that neither will be bound until a definitive agreement is executed and delivered. The Chalker decision illustrates why that condition matters. The agent should not strip the price message from its surrounding context and announce a sale. Conversely, the case should not be overread as a rule that every negotiation requires a later ceremonial document. The actual language and legal context determine whether the parties made a condition of formation.

Change only the deposit. Both parties have agreed and signed, but the buyer has not delivered the required earnest money. The question is now about the agreed delivery duty and its consequences, unless a particular provision makes deposit delivery a formation condition. Consideration and deposit are not interchangeable words. The seller’s and buyer’s exchanged promises may supply consideration while the buyer separately fails a money-delivery obligation. The agent should consult the actual form, calendar the relevant response, and avoid the unsupported shorthand that an unfunded deposit always means no contract.

Change only the purpose. The proposed agreement requires the buyer to engage in conduct prohibited by law. Mutual enthusiasm does not make the purpose lawful. The legal consequence can depend on the statute, public policy, severability, and other doctrines. The agent should not draft an evasive label or promise that a disclaimer will solve the problem. An informed legal referral should identify the proposed conduct and the provision believed relevant, not conceal it behind the description “creative terms.”

These variations demonstrate why the formation file contains more than a final PDF. Preserve instructions, versions, attachments, communications of acceptance, authority evidence, and the chronology of disputed events. A factual record cannot guarantee enforceability, but it allows the proper legal decision-maker to evaluate the actual bargain. An agent’s reconstructed memory months later is a weaker substitute for contemporaneous evidence.

## T03U2 — Express or Implied Contracts

An express agreement states its terms in words. The words may be spoken, written on paper, or communicated electronically, subject to any applicable legal form requirement. An implied-in-fact agreement is inferred from conduct showing actual agreement. The distinction concerns how assent is manifested; it does not decide whether the agreement is enforceable or whether a professional complied with a mandatory writing rule.

For example, a person requests a service, the provider describes the price, and the person tells the provider to begin. The agreement is express even if nobody signs. Where a person knowingly accepts a requested service in circumstances demonstrating an expectation of payment, conduct may help establish agreement. But the precise facts matter: unwanted work performed without a request is not automatically a contract merely because the worker sends an invoice. An obligation imposed by law to prevent unjust enrichment is analytically different from an implied-in-fact agreement; it should not be casually described as proof that the parties contracted.

In real estate, these distinctions are important because duties and enforceable fees do not always arise together. Brokerage conduct can create responsibilities while a missing signed commission agreement prevents a collection action. A license holder must not use “implied agreement” as a workaround for § 1101.563’s current residential buyer-agreement requirement. The law can recognize consequences of conduct without approving the absence of required paperwork.
### Adapted teaching: Three different writing rules answer three different questions

### The writings that make a promise enforceable — a different question

Here is the distinction that separates a licensee who understands Texas law from one who has memorised
a list. A writing that TRELA requires *before you act* is not the same thing as a writing the law
requires *before you can sue*. Section 1101.563 is the first kind. Two other provisions are the
second kind, and they operate whether or not you complied with § 1101.563.

Occupations Code § 1101.806(c) provides: "A person may not maintain an action in this state to recover
a commission for the sale or purchase of real estate unless the promise or agreement on which the
action is based, or a memorandum, is in writing and signed by the party against whom the action is
brought or by a person authorized by that party to sign the document." That is the commission statute
of frauds, and it lives in the License Act, not in the Business & Commerce Code. Notice exactly who
must have signed: "the party against whom the action is brought." Your own signature proves nothing. A
seller in Waco who shakes your hand, tells three neighbors you have the listing, and then sells to
his brother-in-law has given you nothing you can sue on, because he never signed. The rest of
§ 1101.806 — the licensure gate in subsection (b), the carve-out in (a) for agreements to share
compensation among license holders, and the forfeiture in (d) — is taught in Law of Agency Module 04.

Then there is the general statute of frauds. Business & Commerce Code § 26.01(a) provides that "[a]
promise or agreement described in Subsection (b) of this section is not enforceable unless the promise
or agreement, or a memorandum of it, is (1) in writing; and (2) signed by the person to be charged
with the promise or agreement or by someone lawfully authorized to sign for him." Subsection (b)
applies that rule to, among other things, "a contract for the sale of real estate," "a lease of real
estate for a term longer than one year," and "an agreement which is not to be performed within one
year from the date of making the agreement." The last of those has teeth for listings: a fifteen-month
exclusive right to sell agreed orally in Midland is unenforceable on its face.

Now read § 26.01(b)(7) with care, because this is where courses routinely misstate Texas law.
Subdivision (7) covers "a promise or agreement to pay a commission for the sale or purchase of: (A) an
oil or gas mining lease; (B) an oil or gas royalty; (C) minerals; or (D) a mineral interest." That
subdivision is about *mineral* commissions. It is not the source of the writing requirement for an
ordinary residential commission; § 1101.806(c) is. If you are asked where Texas law requires a
commission agreement to be in writing, the honest answer names both provisions and keeps them
straight: § 1101.806(c) for the sale or purchase of real estate generally, § 26.01(b)(7) for
commissions on the mineral interests it lists.

Three writings, three questions, three different failures: § 1101.563 answers *may I act*, and
failing it is a disciplinary count; § 1101.806(c) answers *may I sue for the fee*, and failing it
loses the suit; § 26.01 answers *is the promise enforceable at all*. A file can satisfy one and fail
another.


Electronic communication remains an express form of communication. The Texas Uniform Electronic Transactions Act, [Business & Commerce Code §§ 322.005, 322.007, and 322.009](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/BC/htm/BC.322.htm), addresses agreement to transact electronically, legal recognition of electronic records and signatures, and attribution. It does not make every email a sufficient contract. The content must still demonstrate the required agreement, the signature must be attributable as applicable, and the record must satisfy the relevant substantive law.

A typed name in an email, a software signature, and a scanned handwritten signature may each require examination in context. The agent should retain the actual signed record and any audit information, verify intended recipients, and avoid modifying the document after signature. Do not declare electronic contracting invalid because no pen was used; do not declare it valid merely because a platform displays a green completion icon.

Compare two communications. “I accept the attached counteroffer unchanged” refers to a specific set of terms and expresses assent. “Looks good, send something for our lawyer” may indicate continued negotiation or a request for documentation. Neither phrase should be analyzed without the entire exchange and any stated conditions. The lesson of Chalker is not that email never binds. It is that context and the parties’ express requirements matter.

The safest operating practice is to make the intended transaction clear through the required authorized documents and complete communications. Avoid accidental ambiguity about whether a conversation is preliminary, a counterproposal, an instruction to an agent, or final acceptance. The agent should not invent legal labels to repair a disputed history. The file should preserve the history accurately and direct legal interpretation to counsel.

A worked commission example shows the limits of the classification. An owner orally asks a broker to find a buyer and later praises the broker’s work. The broker finds a purchaser, but the owner refuses the commission. Evidence of conversation or conduct may show that services were requested. The commission collection question nevertheless encounters § 1101.806(c)’s signed-writing requirement. The owner’s appreciation is not the statutory memorandum. A prudent brokerage establishes its written agreement before undertaking the work instead of hoping an implied-contract theory will cure a missing signature.

## T03U3 — Unilateral or Bilateral Contracts

This classification asks what response the offer seeks. A bilateral bargain exchanges promises: each party undertakes an obligation. A unilateral offer seeks acceptance by a specified act or performance. It is inaccurate to count signatures or people and call the result bilateral. Two people can sign an option document without both promising to buy and sell immediately. A sales agreement can contain many signatures while remaining a bilateral exchange of promises.

The Supreme Court of Texas distinguishes mutual promises from acceptance by performance in [Vanegas v. American Energy Services, No. 07-0520, opinion pages 5–6](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/819887/OpinionsFY2010.pdf#page=315). That case concerned an alleged employee bonus promise, not a land conveyance, so its contract-structure explanation does not remove real-estate writing requirements. It gives the conceptual rule: identify whether the requested acceptance is a return promise or the specified performance, then apply the additional law governing the particular transaction.

An ordinary purchase agreement illustrates bilateral obligation. The buyer promises to pay and perform the buyer’s other obligations; the seller promises to convey and perform the seller’s obligations. Closing remains in the future, but the promises can already bind the parties. Financing, title, inspection, or sale-of-other-property provisions may condition or qualify performance. Conditional duties are still duties governed by the bargain; a contingency does not necessarily mean that neither side has a contract.

An option requires a more careful explanation than the slogan “one-sided contract.” The optionor undertakes to keep a specified offer available on stated conditions, commonly in exchange for an option fee. The optionee ordinarily has a choice whether to exercise the purchase right. The option agreement itself may contain enforceable obligations concerning payment, notice, access, confidentiality, or other matters. Before exercise, the optionee has not necessarily promised to buy. A proper exercise can activate the purchase obligations on the option’s terms.

The Supreme Court of Texas explains this structure in [North Shore Energy v. Harkins, No. 14-0552, opinion page 12](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1436267/140552.pdf): an option combines an underlying transaction awaiting acceptance with a promise to preserve the opportunity to accept. The option itself is not a conveyance of title or possession. Read any separate access permission on its own terms rather than assuming that the purchase election makes the holder the current owner.

Read the event that creates the duty. If a landowner promises to sell on stated terms if the holder exercises by a specified date, the holder needs to understand the exercise method, recipient, timing, and consequences. A message expressing interest in continuing negotiations may not be exercise. Mailing, sending, and receipt can differ; the governing language matters. Do not equate paying an option fee with giving the later purchase-election notice. Those are separate acts.

The termination option within a TREC residential sales contract also needs precise classification. The parties already have a sales agreement. The buyer purchases a stated right to terminate during the negotiated option period if its conditions are met. Exercising that termination right ends a purchase agreement under its terms; it is not the same as exercising a stand-alone option to create purchase obligations. Both arrangements use the word “option,” but their direction is different: one can lead into a sale obligation, the other can provide an exit from one.

A reward example makes unilateral structure visible without the complexities of property transfer. An owner offers a stated payment to the person who returns a lost item under specified conditions. The requested act, rather than a promise to search, is the identified acceptance. A bilateral search-services agreement would instead exchange a promise to undertake a defined search for a promise to pay. The actual language determines the structure, not the parties’ later characterization when the result disappoints.

Apply this to brokerage with caution. A listing agreement’s compensation may depend on specified results, but that does not mean the broker has no service duties before the result occurs. Read the written agreement and the broker’s regulatory duties separately. A term requiring a ready, willing, and able purchaser or a completed sale may affect when compensation is earned; it does not itself eliminate the broker’s obligations of competence, disclosure, or performance of promised services. The signed document controls the contractual event and the law controls the professional duties.

In a hypothetical ranch option, the holder pays $6,000 for a 90-day right to purchase at a stated price. At day 50 the holder decides not to proceed. Whether the fee is refunded, credited, or retained depends on the instrument; there is no universal result derived solely from the word “option.” If the holder elects to purchase, the agreed exercise mechanics and purchase terms must be followed. If the holder asks an agent to add new mineral or financing terms while exercising, the agent should identify that a proposed change may be a negotiation rather than performance of the existing option and seek counsel.

This distinction prevents a common communication error: “You signed, so you must buy.” A signature proves an act of signing; the document establishes what was promised. The client may have signed a showing-only agreement, a confidentiality agreement, an option, a purchase contract, or a disclosure acknowledgment. Read the obligation, not just the signature panel. [Occupations Code § 1101.654 and 22 TAC § 537.11](https://www.trec.texas.gov/agency-information/rules-and-laws/trec-rules) still limit the license holder’s advice on the legal effect of a particular instrument.

## T03U4 — Valid, Void, Voidable & Unenforceable Contracts

These terms answer different questions. A valid agreement satisfies the relevant requirements and has legal effect. A void arrangement lacks legal effect from the outset for the reason the governing law identifies. A voidable agreement can be avoided by a protected party under an applicable doctrine; it is not automatically nonexistent. An unenforceable promise may reflect real agreement but encounter a legal bar to enforcement. The label should follow the legal analysis, not replace it.

The statute of frauds supplies a clear statutory example. Under Business & Commerce Code § 26.01, the listed promise or agreement is not enforceable unless the required writing and signature exist. An oral conversation about selling land may therefore present an enforceability problem even when both people sincerely intended a sale. It is misleading to say that the oral promise is necessarily void in every respect. Exceptions, defenses, restitution, and other doctrines can require legal analysis. The professional workflow is to obtain the required signed writing, not rely on a possible litigation exception.

Voidability also requires care. Fraud, duress, undue influence, mistake, and incapacity can present different grounds for relief with different elements and consequences. A party’s assertion that “I was pressured” does not itself resolve those elements. Ordinary bargaining pressure is not automatically legal duress. A disappointing purchase is not automatically fraud. A buyer’s unilateral miscalculation is not the same as a qualifying mutual mistake about a basic fact. Counsel must evaluate the facts, legal standard, available remedy, timing, and any ratification or waiver issues.

Age and legal capacity should not be reduced to a universal slogan about minors. General contract principles recognize protective rules, but statutory exceptions and the nature of the transaction matter. An agent encountering a minor, a guardianship, or a person whose capacity is in doubt should promptly involve the broker and appropriate counsel. Do not accept the other party’s reassurance that a parent’s signature or a later birthday automatically cures everything. The document and authority must be examined before promises and funds are committed.

Illegality can affect a clause as well as a whole arrangement. For example, [Business & Commerce Code § 17.42](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/BC/htm/BC.17.htm) declares consumer waivers of the DTPA contrary to public policy and unenforceable and void except under its specified requirements. A casual waiver sentence does not become effective merely because the consumer initials it. The statute’s prescribed form, counsel, bargaining-position, and other conditions matter. The effect of an invalid clause on the remaining agreement is a legal question, not something an agent should resolve by deleting a page.

Distinguish the enforceability of a purchase promise from the enforceability of a fee promise. A complete buyer-seller contract does not establish a broker’s separate right to a commission. Conversely, a proper signed representation agreement does not make an unsigned land-sale agreement enforceable against the seller. Section 1101.806 addresses the commission action; § 26.01 addresses the listed substantive promises. The person against whom enforcement is sought and the particular promise being enforced must be identified.

A worked classification uses four files. File one contains a complete signed residential agreement with future closing obligations: it may be valid and executory. File two contains an oral two-year lease: the statutory writing problem is apparent. File three contains a forged seller signature: authenticity and authority are fundamental issues requiring immediate legal and broker involvement. File four contains a buyer’s assertion that a material known defect was concealed: the assertion raises possible disclosure and consent issues but does not authorize the agent to declare the contract rescinded. The response differs because the defect differs.

Be precise when a client wants to “cancel.” The word can refer to withdrawal of an unaccepted offer, exercise of an existing contractual termination right, a mutual agreement ending the contract, or a legal remedy for a formation defect. Each route has a different actor, timing, notice, and consequence. If the agent says merely “we cancelled,” a future reader cannot tell what occurred. Record the actual instrument or notice, the asserted authority, the recipient, and the delivery evidence.

Finally, legal uncertainty should not be hidden by optimistic paperwork. A disputed signature or capacity issue is not cured by adding an effective date. A suspected invalid clause is not cured by calling it customary. Protect the client’s decision process by stating the known facts accurately, preserving rights through timely competent action, and referring legal interpretation. The purpose of this vocabulary is to recognize the correct question early enough to act responsibly.

### Classifying the problem before proposing a response

Imagine that three buyers each say, “The contract is invalid.” The first never agreed to the seller’s changed price. The second agreed orally to buy land but signed no sufficient writing. The third signed a complete agreement and now alleges that the seller knowingly concealed a material condition. These statements require different inquiries. The first concerns assent to the same bargain. The second raises a statutory enforceability barrier. The third can involve disclosure duties and potential remedies even though an agreement was formed.

The agent should not translate all three statements into the same termination notice. First gather the documents and factual chronology. Identify what the client is asserting and what evidence supports it. Ask whether a presently available contractual right has a deadline that requires immediate attention. Obtain legal advice concerning the asserted defect and the proposed response. A mistaken legal label can lead a party to stop performing when the agreement remains enforceable.

For the oral sale, § 26.01 supplies the writing rule, but an agent should not advise that the absence of a signed writing eliminates every possible legal consequence. Litigation doctrines and remedies can require additional analysis. The professional lesson is preventive: secure the appropriate signed agreement at the proper time. A course classification is an aid to recognizing the issue, not a substitute for counsel’s analysis of exceptions or equitable claims.

For the alleged concealment, determine what was said, who knew what, when the information became available, and what the client did in reliance. A seller’s statement and a license holder’s independent knowledge present related but distinct questions. Preserve reports and communications. Do not alter an earlier disclosure to make the history appear consistent. A later correction should identify the new information and be communicated through an appropriate documented process.

For the unaccepted price change, compare the exact versions. Did the seller make a counterproposal? Did the buyer accept that proposal? Was acceptance communicated under the applicable terms and law? Was a relevant withdrawal or rejection communicated? The agent should not treat initials as a ritual that decides all these questions regardless of context. Initials can be evidence of assent to a change; they are not a substitute for understanding what change was accepted.

A legal classification can also change over time. A party who has a right to avoid an agreement may act in a way that raises ratification, waiver, or other issues. A party may miss a contractual election period. A later signed memorandum may matter to a writing analysis. These consequences depend on governing law and facts. The agent should therefore refer the issue promptly, not assume that a label assigned on Monday remains a complete description after the client acts on Friday.

This is why careful status language helps. Say that a client disputes formation, asserts a disclosure violation, or has requested advice about a writing requirement. Avoid declaring the contract void, rescinded, or unenforceable unless the appropriate legal determination supports that statement. Record the client’s instructions accurately and distinguish them from a court’s conclusion or an attorney’s advice.

## T03U5 — Executed or Executory Contracts

In classification vocabulary, an executory contract still has performance outstanding; an executed contract has been performed. In everyday transaction speech, however, “executed” often means “signed.” Those meanings must be kept separate. A fully signed sales contract awaiting closing is executed in the signature sense and still executory in the performance sense. Say “fully signed” or “fully performed” when either meaning could be misunderstood.

The relevant performance can be divided by party and obligation. The buyer may have paid earnest money while the seller still owes a title commitment and both owe closing performance. The seller may have completed repairs while the buyer still awaits lender approval. A deposit receipt shows that one duty was performed; it does not show the entire contract has been performed. The same is true of a signed disclosure acknowledgment or an appraisal report.

At closing, the transfer of funds and the conveyance ordinarily perform central obligations, but the document may contain obligations that continue. A temporary lease can govern later possession. A agreed post-closing duty can survive if the governing documents and law provide. An unresolved proration adjustment may remain. Do not announce that every obligation disappeared when the deed was signed; identify the particular duty and its governing language.

The narrower Texas statutory use of “executory contract” deserves explicit notice. [Property Code Chapter 5, Subchapter D](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/PR/htm/PR.5.htm) governs specified executory conveyance arrangements commonly associated with contracts for deed and related residential arrangements. The applicable sections contain scope, disclosure, recording, payment, and enforcement protections. It is incorrect to apply every Subchapter D rule to every ordinary purchase agreement merely because performance remains in the future. It is equally incorrect to avoid Subchapter D by renaming a covered arrangement “rent to own.” The later contract-for-deed and lease-purchase units examine that specialized subject.

One express coverage provision illustrates the distinction: Property Code § 5.062(c) excludes an executory contract that provides for deed delivery within 180 days after final execution. Read the promised delivery terms and the statutory provision rather than supplying a different threshold from habit. Subsection (a) addresses residential use and specified lease-option arrangements, while other subsections contain additional exclusions and limited applications. A correct classification therefore records the particular subsection and supporting facts. Exclusion from Subchapter D is not exemption from every contract, licensing, disclosure, or conveyance rule.

In a worked chronology, the parties sign on Monday, the deposit arrives Wednesday, the buyer inspects the next week, and closing is scheduled for the following month. At each point the agreement may be fully signed while still partly unperformed. If the buyer asks whether “executed” means funds have been released, the answer must distinguish signature completion from closing and funding. The agent should consult the actual closing confirmation, not infer funding from the document’s status in a signature service.

Performance status also affects document management. Keep the accepted contract as the record of promises, the amendment as the record of agreed changes, the notice as the record of an exercised right, and the closing documents as evidence of completed steps. Replacing all four with a folder labelled “done” erases the distinctions needed for a dispute or audit. The file should explain what obligation was due, what act occurred, and what remains.

Classification can now be applied along independent dimensions. A written residential purchase contract can be express, bilateral, valid, and executory at once. If it is fully performed, the last classification changes without changing how it was formed. An express option can give one party an election without an immediate duty to buy. An oral agreement can be express yet face a writing-based enforcement barrier. Learn to ask each dimension separately, then explain the practical consequence.

The most useful final review is not a memorized label. It is a short explanation: “The parties have signed the same purchase terms and communicated acceptance; closing remains due; the agreement therefore still has future performance obligations.” That explanation identifies evidence, event, and consequence. If a key fact is absent, say what must be established instead of guessing. A correct statement of uncertainty is more useful than an incorrect categorical conclusion.

### One agreement, several independent classifications

Use a four-column mental record for a hypothetical written purchase agreement. In the first column, identify expression: its terms are stated in words, so it is express. In the second, identify the promised exchange: buyer and seller undertake future duties, so the ordinary sale is bilateral. In the third, identify legal status: formation and enforceability must be evaluated from the facts and law. In the fourth, identify performance: if closing remains due, the agreement is executory in the general classification sense.

Now suppose the buyer pays the deposit. The performance column changes because one obligation is complete. The expression column does not change from express to implied. The exchange does not become unilateral merely because the buyer performed first. The entire agreement does not become fully performed while conveyance and payment of the remaining price are outstanding. These independent classifications prevent a common examination and practice error: allowing one completed act to answer an unrelated legal question.

After closing, identify any surviving duties separately. A temporary lease creates its own possession and performance questions. A later adjustment provision may require action after the main exchange. A party’s possible claim about earlier conduct is not erased merely because a folder is archived. The governing documents and law determine what remains. Precise completion records should identify completed conveyance and funding while listing any continuing obligations.

Finally, keep the specialized statutory label separate. Property Code Subchapter D does not apply to every agreement with future performance simply because the word executory appears in a textbook definition. Its applicability provisions must be read. The conceptual classification helps describe performance status; the statute defines the transactions governed by its protections. A competent agent recognizes both meanings and avoids applying the specialized remedies to an ordinary purchase contract without the required legal analysis.

Feedback: Section 26.01 covers the sale and the lease longer than one year. Section 1101.806(c) addresses the real-estate sale/purchase commission action; § 26.01(b)(7) separately names specified mineral commissions. Section 5.021 addresses conveyance. These are overlapping but distinct inquiries. A signature on the deed does not retroactively prove that every separate fee or earlier promise satisfied its own requirement.

### Applied exercise: “Executed” at three different moments

At 9 a.m. a signature platform marks the sales contract complete. At 10 a.m. acceptance is communicated. Three weeks later the parties sign closing documents, but lender funding is delayed. Explain which facts establish signatures, which concern formation, and which concern performance. Draft a two-sentence status update that avoids using “executed” ambiguously.

Feedback: The platform status concerns signatures, subject to version and authority checks. Communicated acceptance concerns formation under the applicable agreement and law. Signing closing documents does not prove funding or completion of every duty. A useful update says the parties have signed the same contract and acceptance was communicated; the closing documents are signed but funding confirmation remains outstanding. It does not announce complete performance from a signature icon.


### Module recap

Ask four independent questions: how was agreement expressed, what response or promise was sought, does the law recognize and permit enforcement of the bargain, and what performance remains? Then identify the particular writing rule. Use precise language for signature completion, formation, termination, closing, and funding. The same document can be express, bilateral, valid, and still executory.
