## Compare rate, cost, and monthly payments

The interest rate measures the charge for borrowing principal. APR is a broader cost measure that reflects specified charges, including points and certain fees. Compare like products and terms. A lower note rate with substantial upfront costs may not be cheaper over the borrower’s expected holding period. The [CFPB rate-versus-APR explanation](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-the-difference-between-a-mortgage-interest-rate-and-an-apr-en-135/) also warns that an ARM’s APR is not its maximum possible interest rate. Review index, margin, adjustment timing and caps separately.

For a simple points comparison, suppose paying $3,600 more upfront reduces the quoted payment by $75 a month. Divide $3,600 by $75: payment savings recover the initial amount in 48 months. This simplified break-even ignores time value, tax effects, principal-balance differences, future refinance and other changes. It is useful to expose the tradeoff, not to guarantee an optimal decision.

Rate-linked lender credits reverse the ordinary discount-points tradeoff: lower closing cash in return for a higher quoted rate. The lender may have other reasons for a credit, so ask what produced the figure. The [CFPB points-and-credits guidance](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-should-i-use-lender-credits-and-points-also-called-discount-points-en-136/) recommends comparing the choices across likely holding periods. A credit is not automatically free money and is not the same as reducing the property’s price.

Payment components must also be separated. For $2,100 principal and interest, $450 escrow and $95 mortgage insurance, the disclosed components total $2,645. Principal and interest alone understates that total by $545. Read whether taxes, insurance, assessments or dues are excluded from escrow. A non-escrowed bill still needs a budget.

A balloon payment means a large balance is due at maturity compared with regular installments. Do not assume the borrower can certainly refinance then. A fixed rate does not by itself establish full amortization or eliminate a balloon; review the actual loan terms and projected payments.
