# Module 10: Defaults & Foreclosures

REF-0 topic T10 suggests 85 minutes. Default response depends on the contract, loan type, property, applicable federal protections, Texas law, and any court orders. An agent can recognize urgency, document facts, and make appropriate referrals; the agent should not improvise a foreclosure deadline or promise a debt release.

## T10U1 — Defaults

Default is failure to satisfy an obligation under the loan documents and law. Missed payments are common, but unpaid taxes, inadequate insurance, prohibited transfers, or other covenant breaches can matter. A late charge, default, acceleration, foreclosure notice, sale, and eviction are different events. A grace period for a late charge is not necessarily a right to delay payment without other consequences.

Review the payment history, notices, servicer identity, loan documents, and the borrower's circumstances. Contact the servicer promptly through a verified channel. Housing counseling and legal assistance can be important. Beware of rescue schemes asking for upfront money, deed transfers, or instructions to stop communicating with the servicer. A pending assistance request is not always an automatic cancellation of a sale.

For covered loans and servicers, Regulation X §1024.41 restricts the first foreclosure notice or filing before the borrower is more than 120 days delinquent, subject to specified exceptions. Loss-mitigation application timing and completeness can trigger additional protections. Do not apply this provision as though it covers every loan, every servicer, and every foreclosure step without examining scope and exceptions.

### Applied exercise: A67 — Default triage

A homeowner receives a notice and says a modification request was submitted but cannot locate acknowledgment. Prepare a triage checklist: notice date/type, sale date if any, loan/servicer coverage, payment history, application completeness, and urgent counsel/servicer contact. Explain why saying “your application automatically stops everything” is unsafe.

Organize the records into known facts, unverified assertions, and urgent missing items. The homeowner has a transmission receipt but no acknowledgment specifying completeness. Draft one inquiry asking the servicer for status and missing documents, and another requesting prompt legal review of the actual notice and scheduled event. Explain why the agent should preserve dates without announcing a legal stay or discarding notices while waiting.

Feedback: protections depend on the governing rule and facts, including completeness and timing. Obtain records and prompt qualified review; do not let a reassurance replace action on a deadline.

Additional feedback: A receipt can show transmission without proving a complete application or establishing every protection. The responsible reviewer needs the actual loan, servicer, history, notices, dates, and application contents. The client message should encourage prompt action and qualified assistance while avoiding either an unsupported protection promise or an unsupported claim that no protection exists.

## T10U2 — Adjustments & Modifications

Forbearance temporarily changes payment expectations; it does not ordinarily forgive the underlying debt. A repayment plan spreads arrears over specified payments. A deferral places eligible amounts at a later due date under its terms. A modification changes contractual loan terms. A refinance creates a new loan. A short sale requires approval to sell for less than amounts otherwise due, and a deed in lieu transfers property under an agreement. Each has different eligibility, credit, tax, lien, and personal-liability consequences.

Get the actual agreement. A trial payment plan may require successful completion and further conditions before a permanent modification. A reduced payment can result from term extension or deferred principal rather than debt forgiveness. A short-sale approval should be examined for release of the lien and treatment of any remaining personal obligation; the two are not automatically identical.

### Applied exercise: A68 — Workout comparison

A borrower owes $6,000 arrears. Plan A adds $500 to each of twelve payments; Plan B defers $6,000 to a later event without current additional installments under the stipulated agreement. Calculate the immediate payment effect and remaining deferred amount after twelve compliant months. Name the term that would establish forgiveness if it existed.

Feedback: A adds $500 monthly and cures $6,000 over the year; B adds no current installment but leaves $6,000 deferred. Forgiveness requires an actual cancellation/release provision, not the absence of a current bill.

## T10U3 — Types of Foreclosures

Judicial foreclosure proceeds through a court action and order. A Texas nonjudicial sale under a contractual power of sale must comply with the instrument and Property Code §51.002 and other applicable law. Certain liens, including qualifying Texas home-equity liens, require court authorization even where a subsequent sale uses power-of-sale procedures. Tax foreclosure and other lien types have their own statutes; do not import an ordinary mortgage rule into every forced sale.

Section 51.002 generally provides public auction on the first Tuesday between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m., with its January 1/July 4 exception and designated-location provisions. It generally requires at least 21 days' sale notice through the specified posting, filing, and certified-mail steps. For property used as the debtor's residence, subsection (d) requires at least 20 days to cure before sale notice is given. The statute specifies counting conventions and exceptions. Loan documents may provide additional rights, and federal law can impose additional restrictions.

Foreclosure is not the same as immediate lawful physical removal. Possession may require separate process. Bankruptcy can impose an automatic stay; military-service protections may require additional analysis. Junior interests, surviving liens, redemption rights for particular lien types, and title defects make auction purchases legally complex. Obtain qualified legal/title review rather than promising that every auction buyer receives a vacant property free of all liens.

### Statute reading exercise: S08 — Texas sale requirements

Read Property Code §51.002(a)–(e), (g)–(i) and Regulation X §1024.41(f). Make separate columns for Texas sale mechanics, Texas residential cure notice, and covered federal pre-foreclosure restrictions. Explain why satisfying 21 days' sale notice alone does not prove the entire foreclosure is lawful.

Feedback: notice mechanics, cure opportunity, authority, contractual conditions, federal coverage, and court orders must all be considered. The statute's first-Tuesday rule has express exceptions; a generic calendar calculation should not be used as legal advice.

### Applied exercise: A85 — Auction buyer's question list

An investor client wants to bid at a Texas nonjudicial foreclosure sale of an occupied house. The posted notice names the sale date and the trustee. The investor says, "If I win, I get the keys that afternoon and every lien is gone." Separate that sentence into its claims. For each claim, write the question that must be answered before bidding and who can answer it. Questions to cover:
- what interest is actually being sold and which senior or surviving liens remain;
- whether the sale is being conducted under the instrument and Property Code §51.002, and whether a court order is required for this lien type;
- whether any federal restriction, bankruptcy stay or military-service issue could affect the sale;
- what separate process may be needed to obtain possession from the occupant.

Then explain why a correct first-Tuesday date and a 21-day notice period still do not establish that the bidder will receive clear title and immediate possession.

Feedback: "keys that afternoon" conflates foreclosure with lawful possession, which may require separate process. "Every lien is gone" ignores the sale's actual subject: a junior foreclosure does not ordinarily eliminate a senior lien, and other interests can survive. Notice timing is one requirement among several: authority under the instrument, the residential cure notice, the lien type's requirements, federal coverage and court orders all matter.

Additional feedback: the agent's role is to surface these questions and to route title and legal review before money is committed. It is not to certify a sale as valid or to promise a vacant, lien-free property. Keep the bidder's assumptions in writing next to the evidence that answers or contradicts each one.

## T10U4 — Deficiency Judgments

A deficiency is an amount remaining after applying foreclosure proceeds and required credits to the debt, but a shortfall is not automatically an enforceable judgment. Texas Property Code §51.003 generally requires an action following a §51.002 sale within two years and permits the defendant to request a fair-market-value determination. The statutory offset considers value and surviving secured claims as specified. Applicable nonrecourse protections, defenses, agreements, and insurance credits can change the result.

For a simplified example, debt is $280,000, foreclosure price $220,000, court-determined fair market value $250,000, and no surviving lien or other adjustment applies. Initial shortfall is $60,000. The value above sale price is $30,000, yielding a $30,000 amount after the stated offset. This is a statutory-mechanics illustration, not a court judgment or a conclusion that every loan permits personal recovery.

The fair-value offset is not the only statutory credit question. Property Code §51.003(d) addresses money the lender receives from a private mortgage guaranty insurer: the lender must credit that money to the borrower's account before bringing its own deficiency action. The provision also distinguishes the insurer's exercise of its own subrogation rights. A reviewer should identify the payment recipient, amount, ledger treatment, and claimant instead of assuming an insurance payment either belongs outside the accounting or releases every possible claim. Request the actual insurer and lender records and refer the legal effect to counsel. Do not subtract the same credit twice.

### Applied exercise: A69 — Offset calculation

Assume debt $310,000, sale price $245,000, court-determined fair market value $275,000, and no surviving liens or other adjustments. Calculate initial shortfall, offset, and remaining amount. List two legal facts needed before calling the result collectible.

Feedback: $65,000 shortfall, $30,000 offset, $35,000 remaining. Personal liability/recourse, timely action, applicable protections, competent value evidence, required credits, and defenses matter.

## T10U5 — Tax Impacts of Foreclosure

Foreclosure can involve both a disposition of property and cancellation-of-debt questions. Recourse and nonrecourse debt are treated differently for federal tax calculations. Adjusted basis, amount realized, debt discharged, fair market value, and applicable exclusions can all matter. A Form 1099-C or 1099-A is information to reconcile, not a self-contained determination of tax owed.

IRS Publication 4681 explains canceled debt, foreclosures, repossessions, and abandonment. The publication currently available during this review is labeled for 2025; a taxpayer's 2026 event requires current tax-year law. Do not promise that all principal-residence debt cancellation is tax-free or that a past temporary exclusion automatically continues. Bankruptcy and insolvency exclusions have their own requirements and possible tax-attribute consequences. Refer the homeowner to a qualified tax professional with the loan, sale, settlement, and basis records.

### Applied exercise: A70 — Tax referral packet

Create a document checklist for a homeowner after foreclosure: original purchase/basis records, improvement costs, loan history, recourse information, foreclosure sale/value documents, lender statements, cancellation forms, and facts relevant to any exclusion. Explain why the foreclosure sale price alone cannot establish all federal tax consequences.

Prepare two separate worksheet headings: disposition of the property and possible cancellation of debt. Under each, place the records that could affect the calculation. Then compare two borrowers with the same sale price but different adjusted bases and recourse status; explain why equal sale prices need not produce equal tax outcomes. Draft a referral note identifying the event year and any information form discrepancy without resolving an exclusion yourself.

Feedback: debt treatment and tax basis create separate calculations. A sale can produce a gain/loss analysis while cancellation can raise another issue; exclusions cannot be assumed from the property's former use alone.

Additional feedback: Basis, amount realized, debt treatment, and recourse facts can differ despite the same sale price. Form 1099-C is evidence to reconcile, not an automatic tax exemption or proof that the entire reported amount is taxable. The packet should identify the applicable tax year, retain source records, and ask the tax professional to apply current law to the actual facts.
