# Module 06: Instruments of Real Estate Finance

REF-0 topic T06 suggests 170 minutes. Read the obligation and the security together. The instructional excerpts below are invented examples for analysis, not execution-ready legal forms. Texas statutes govern the Texas-specific discussion; a license holder should identify issues and refer drafting and enforceability questions to counsel.

## T06U1 — Encumbrances & Liens

An encumbrance burdens property rights. A lien secures an obligation against property; an easement or restrictive covenant can burden use without being a debt lien. A mortgage lender examines both. An easement that limits construction can affect value even though no creditor receives payment from it. A tax lien may affect priority even though the owner did not voluntarily sign a mortgage to create it.

Priority determines the order of competing claims under applicable law and documents. Recording time is important, but “first recorded always wins” is incomplete. Statutory priority, notice, purchase-money rules, subordination agreements, and other exceptions require analysis. A junior loan can be current while foreclosure of a senior lien still threatens the junior creditor's security. Conversely, a junior foreclosure does not ordinarily eliminate a senior lien merely because a new owner buys at the sale.

Separate the existence of a claim from its amount and rank. A title search can reveal an instrument, but a current payoff may be needed to determine what remains due. A recorded amount may be an original maximum rather than today's balance. A release can be missing even when a debt was paid. An obligation can be disputed without disappearing from the title-review process. The professional task is to identify the recorded interest, obtain the appropriate evidence, and ask the title professional or counsel how it must be treated. Do not erase an inconvenient lien from a closing worksheet because a party says it is obsolete.

Priority arithmetic assumes a legally determined order; it does not establish that order. In a hypothetical $300,000 distribution, allocating $260,000 to a first claim leaves $40,000 for lower claims before costs. If a superior tax claim or sale expense must be paid first, the residual changes. If the sale is of a junior interest subject to a senior lien, the bidder's economics are different from a sale discharging that senior debt. State the sale assumption before computing proceeds, and do not turn a classroom waterfall into an opinion on the enforceability of a particular claim.

### Applied exercise: A29 — Priority proceeds

In a simplified sale with no costs or special statutory claims, proceeds are $310,000, the senior debt is $275,000, and the junior debt is $50,000. Allocate proceeds and calculate the junior shortfall. Explain why this arithmetic alone does not establish a personal deficiency judgment.

Feedback: senior receives $275,000; junior receives $35,000 and has a $15,000 shortfall. Personal liability, applicable law, remedies, and credits still must be analyzed. The example assumes priority; it does not prove it.

## T06U2 — The Note & Deed of Trust (Trust Deed)

The promissory note states the debt and repayment promise. A deed of trust creates security in identified property and typically gives a trustee a power of sale under its terms and law. The borrower granting security is commonly called trustor or grantor; the lender or holder benefits from the security; the trustee exercises the specified authority. The trustee is not the beneficiary simply because the trustee conducts a sale.

The note should identify principal, interest, payment schedule, maturity, late charges, default, and applicable prepayment terms. The deed of trust commonly addresses taxes, insurance, occupancy, maintenance, transfer, protection of security, acceleration, and enforcement. Signing a note can create personal liability; owning an interest in pledged property and signing security documents does not automatically mean that every signatory is personally liable on the note. Read each person's capacity and signature.

Texas homestead protections require special attention. Article XVI §50 permits liens for specified categories, including purchase money and qualifying home-equity credit, subject to their conditions. A homestead is not immune from every foreclosure, and a lender cannot make every debt enforceable against it merely by labeling the document a deed of trust. Qualifying §50(a)(6) home-equity credit carries distinctive constitutional restrictions, including the combined 80% value limitation, nonrecourse protection subject to its terms, and court-order requirement for foreclosure. A purchase-money mortgage is not automatically the same category.

Three signatures can serve three different purposes. A borrower may promise repayment as note maker. An owner may grant a lien on that owner's property interest. An authorized entity representative may sign on behalf of the entity rather than as an individual borrower. A guarantor's separate promise, if any, needs its own review. The heading next to a signature and the operative language matter. A real estate agent should report inconsistencies in names, ownership, authority, and capacity rather than conclude that everyone who attends closing assumes the same debt.

The word “trustee” is context-sensitive. A deed-of-trust trustee is not automatically the trustee of a family living trust, the owner of the loan, or a general financial adviser to the borrower. Texas Property Code §51.0001 defines roles for chapter 51, while §§51.0074–.0075 address power-of-sale duties and authority. The security instrument and governing law define the role; familiarity with a different type of trust does not supply missing powers. This prevents a common misunderstanding in which a borrower directs every servicing question to the named trustee instead of the servicer.

### Statute reading exercise: S05 — Homestead classification

Read Texas Constitution article XVI §50(a)(1), (a)(6)(B), (C), and (D), and the surrounding conditions. Compare a purchase-money lien with a proposed new home-equity lien on an existing homestead. For a hypothetical $400,000 value, $250,000 existing secured debt, and $90,000 proposed new home-equity credit, calculate the combined ratio and identify the excess above 80%. Do not treat the ratio as the only condition.

Feedback: total secured debt is $340,000, or 85%; an 80% cap permits $320,000 combined, leaving $70,000 for new credit under this simplified calculation. The proposal exceeds that amount by $20,000. Other constitutional conditions, allowed fees, documents, timing, and lender eligibility remain relevant.

## T06U3 — The Note & Mortgage

A mortgage is another security instrument. The mortgagor grants the security; the mortgagee receives it. The note and mortgage still answer separate questions: what is owed, and what property secures it? Across states, mortgage law and foreclosure procedures differ. Do not carry a procedure learned from another state's mortgage into a Texas deed-of-trust transaction.

Payoff and release also differ. A payoff statement specifies the amount needed to satisfy the obligation as of a date, often with daily interest and other authorized amounts. A release discharges the security of record. Paying the final scheduled installment does not justify ignoring whether the release was properly obtained and recorded. Likewise, releasing a lien does not necessarily release every separate obligation unless the agreement and law provide that result.

A payoff reconciliation needs more than principal and a guessed daily rate. Identify the statement's good-through date, per-diem convention, authorized advances or fees, pending payments, and the verified delivery instructions. A payment mailed but not credited can require investigation. An escrow balance may be refunded separately instead of automatically netted from payoff. Interest may continue until funds are received under the lender's procedure, not merely until the buyer authorizes a wire. Record the source of the statement and obtain a refreshed amount when timing changes; never modify the lender's payoff in the agent's own handwriting as though that binds the creditor.

### Applied exercise: A30 — Payoff review

A hypothetical payoff is $182,400 through September 15 with $29 daily interest after that date. Funds arrive September 18 under the stated example's three-extra-day convention. Calculate the amount and list the recordable document to request after satisfaction. Explain why an old statement showing principal alone is inadequate.

The settlement team proposes sending the old amount and correcting any shortfall later. Write an exception note explaining the amount/date mismatch, the need to authenticate payoff instructions, and the difference between receipt of money, satisfaction of debt, and recording a release. Then suppose arrival slips one more day under the same convention; update the amount and identify who must confirm it before sending funds.

Feedback: $182,487. The file needs the appropriate release/satisfaction instrument and recording follow-through. Principal alone omits accrued interest and authorized payoff items; use the actual lender's calculation and date convention.

Additional feedback: One further day adds $29, making the illustrated amount $182,516. A lender's actual payoff terms control; an unresolved shortfall can prevent satisfaction and release. Use a trusted contact to verify instructions and track the recordable release after confirmed satisfaction rather than treating the wire receipt as the release itself.

## T06U4 — Contract for Deed (Land Contract )

An executory land contract often provides possession and installment payments before the seller delivers a deed. It is not safely understood as an ordinary lease with an informal promise. Texas Property Code chapter 5, subchapter D applies to specified residential executory transactions and contains applicability provisions and exceptions. Some lease-option combinations are included. Review §5.062 before deciding which protections govern.

The subchapter addresses notices, disclosures, recording, annual accounting, title transfer, and default remedies. Section 5.079 provides that a recorded executory contract has the effect specified there of a deed with a vendor's lien. Section 5.064 does not permit simple forfeiture where §5.066 applies or the contract has been recorded. Equity-protection rules can require sale procedures rather than confiscation of the buyer's accumulated interest. A seller should not assume that retaining paper title authorizes immediate eviction after one missed payment.

An executory-contract review begins before default. The buyer needs to understand the interest acquired, existing debt or liens, property condition, tax and insurance responsibilities, price, financing charges, payment schedule, and how title will be conveyed. Those questions are especially important when a household assumes it is becoming an owner immediately but the documents postpone the deed. An informal receipt book does not replace the applicable disclosure, recording, and accounting requirements. The agent should not draft a contract-for-deed arrangement to avoid underwriting or closing oversight; the statutory regime and legal consequences warrant competent counsel.

Look for contradictions between the seller's promises and third-party rights. If the seller remains obligated on an underlying loan, the buyer's timely payments to the seller do not prove that the underlying creditor has been paid. A tax delinquency or insurance lapse can threaten the property regardless of the buyer's understanding. A title and legal review should identify existing claims, transfer restrictions, required consent, and the proposed payment-control arrangement. These questions matter even when the seller and buyer know each other well. Personal trust is not a substitute for reliable records and lawful structure.

### Statute reading exercise: S06 — Executory contract remedies

Read §§5.062, 5.064–5.066, 5.076, 5.077, and 5.079. Prepare an issue list for a seller proposing to terminate a recorded residential contract for deed and retain every payment. Identify applicability, recording effect, default notice, purchaser equity, and accounting questions. Do not calculate a deadline without first selecting the applicable provision.

Feedback: recording and equity protections change remedies. The file requires legal analysis; the seller cannot infer an unrestricted forfeiture remedy from the contract's label. A sound answer distinguishes the statutory scheme from an ordinary monthly lease.

### Statute reading exercise: S16 — Information before an executory commitment

Read the applicable portions of [Texas Property Code §§5.069–5.072](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/PR/htm/PR.5.htm), after revisiting §5.062 for scope. Focus on property-condition information, tax and insurance information, financing terms, and written-contract requirements. Create an intake checklist for a hypothetical residential buyer told only, “Pay $20,000 down and $1,400 monthly; we will discuss the deed later.” The seller has not supplied the interest rate, remaining term, underlying-lien information, property-condition disclosures, or tax/insurance records. Match each missing category to the relevant section or mark it for separate title/legal inquiry. Feedback: the checklist should expose the missing economic and property information, not merely ask the buyer to initial the short statement. Applicability must be established before claiming a statutory remedy. The course does not authorize the student to supply missing legal language. A strong answer records which questions are directly addressed by the cited provisions and which require additional title or counsel review. Do not infer a zero interest rate, clear title, or an immediate deed from silence.

## T06U5 — Subordinate Finance Instruments

Subordinate financing is secured behind another lien under the actual priority arrangement. It may supply part of a down payment, assistance, improvements, or other permitted purposes. The first lender must receive accurate information about simultaneous financing, monthly payments, deferred obligations, and the source of funds. Undisclosed secondary debt can distort both combined LTV and repayment analysis.

A subordination agreement can change priority by consent. A refinancing first lender may require an existing second lender to subordinate to the new first mortgage. Paying off the old first does not automatically give the replacement lender the priority it expects. A HELOC also requires attention to the credit-line limit and potential draws, not merely today's balance. Wrap financing leaves existing underlying debt in place while the new obligation includes it economically; it introduces payment, due-on-sale, disclosure, licensing, and foreclosure risks requiring specialized review.

A refinance can create an unexpected priority problem because the new first mortgage is a new instrument. If an existing second is not paid off, its holder may need to execute an acceptable subordination agreement. The old first lender's release does not itself make every replacement loan first in rank. Equitable or statutory doctrines may require legal analysis, but an agent should not rely on an assumed remedy instead of the title and lender requirements. Obtain the actual subordination terms, including any limit on principal, future advances, rate, or modifications, and confirm that they match the proposed loan.

### Applied exercise: A31 — Combined leverage

On a $500,000 value basis, calculate first LTV for a $375,000 first mortgage and combined LTV after a $50,000 second. Explain how the analysis changes if the second is a revolving line with $50,000 drawn and a $90,000 limit.

Feedback: first LTV is 75% and combined outstanding-balance LTV is 85%. A program may also require a ratio using the full credit line: ($375,000 + $90,000) ÷ $500,000 = 93%. Use the program's defined ratios, not an invented universal rule.

## T06U6 — Special Provisions in Mortgage Lending Instruments

Acceleration makes the remaining debt due after the conditions for that remedy are satisfied. An alienation or due-on-sale clause addresses specified transfers. A prepayment clause addresses early repayment, which is a different event. An assumption provision concerns whether another person may take responsibility subject to lender and program requirements. Taking title “subject to” debt does not by itself release the seller or create the same personal promise as an approved assumption.

Other provisions include escrow, late charges, payment application, protective advances, assignment of rents, cross-default, release clauses, and reinstatement rights. A blanket lien may cover several parcels; a partial-release provision determines whether one parcel can be released and on what terms. A lender's oral reassurance is not a substitute for reading a material provision and obtaining an authorized written change.

Translate provisions into events. A due-on-sale clause concerns a transfer event; a default clause concerns specified nonperformance; a balloon concerns scheduled maturity; and a prepayment provision concerns payment before maturity. Several can apply near the same time without being synonyms. If an owner sells before a balloon comes due, the payoff and transfer terms both deserve review. If a transferee pays installments without an approved assumption, that payment history alone does not establish release of the original maker. The creditor's written agreement and applicable law, not the parties' informal label, control the legal result.

### Applied exercise: A32 — Clause classification

Classify: the whole debt becomes due after uncured default; the lender reviews a sale to a new buyer; a fee may apply to voluntary early payoff; one lot can be released for a stated payment. Explain the distinct business consequence of each.

Apply the clauses to a two-parcel project with a $600,000 balance. The stipulated agreement permits release of parcel A after a $175,000 principal payment while parcel B remains collateral; sale also requires lender transfer review. Compute the remaining principal if the payment is accepted. Draft the two separate lender requests and explain why a parcel release is not a release of the borrower's personal obligation or of all remaining collateral.

Feedback: acceleration, due-on-sale/transfer review, prepayment, and partial release. A transfer review does not necessarily mean an assumption is approved, and partial release of one parcel does not release all collateral.

Additional feedback: The simplified balance becomes $425,000. Obtain the specified parcel release and the separate transfer determination. A partial-release clause does not by itself approve an assumption, release a guarantee, or excuse remaining debt. Exact documents and applicable law govern the actual parties' continuing obligations.

## T06U7 — Review of Promissory Note Form

For this unit, analyze an instructional note summary: principal $240,000; hypothetical fixed annual rate 6%; monthly principal and interest $1,438.92; first installment October 1; 360 monthly installments; late charge and acceleration only as provided in the actual signed documents and law. The summary is intentionally not a complete contract. At monthly rate 0.5%, first-month interest is $1,200; first payment reduces principal by $238.92; new balance is $239,761.08, subject to the loan's actual accrual convention and rounding.

Check internal consistency. A stated payment that amortizes over 30 years does not prove a 30-year maturity if another clause requires a balloon after five years. A fixed rate does not fix taxes, insurance, or escrow requirements. Identify the borrower, payee, amount, payment dates, maturity, rate-change provisions if any, default provisions, prepayment rights, and signature capacity. Resolve blanks, contradictions, and missing attachments before execution.

A reliable note worksheet has a validation column. Confirm that the periodic rate matches the annual rate and payment frequency. Confirm whether interest uses monthly amortization or another accrual convention. Confirm that the calculated final balance is consistent with maturity. Confirm whether the first interval is regular or includes odd-day interest handled at closing. A small difference caused by rounding should be identified, while a large unexplained payment difference should be escalated. Copying a payment from a sales summary into an amortization calculator without checking its assumptions can conceal a balloon, an interest-only period, or omitted charges.

### Applied exercise: A33 — Payment application

Using the summary, calculate the second month's interest and principal reduction to cents under monthly accrual. Then explain why sending an extra $1,000 should be accompanied by clear lender-compliant principal-application instructions.

Feedback: second interest is $1,198.81; principal reduction is $240.11; ending balance is $239,520.97. Actual servicing rules determine application. Extra funds should not be assumed to replace every future scheduled payment.

## T06U8 — Review of Trust Form

An instructional deed-of-trust review begins with the parties and legal description, then the secured obligation, grants and covenants, default and notice provisions, trustee authority, signatures, acknowledgments, and recording. A street address alone may not adequately identify the legal estate. Confirm that the grantor owns the interest being pledged and that spouses, entities, trusts, or estates have appropriate authority and signatures.

Compare the deed of trust with the note, title commitment, contract, and closing instructions. Principal, borrower identity, property, lien priority, and riders should agree. A rider may materially alter an otherwise familiar form, for example for adjustable rates, planned unit developments, or rents. A real estate professional can spot discrepancies and request correction without drafting legal language or deciding a disputed title question.

Security instruments may also contain continuing promises about maintaining the property, paying specified charges, and supplying evidence of required insurance. These collateral-protection covenants are different from the note's scheduled principal-and-interest promise. A file can be current on installments while an insurance or preservation issue remains unresolved. Read the actual covenant, applicable notice/protective-advance provisions, and evidence required; do not assume a fixed rate or a correctly recorded instrument proves ongoing compliance. The agent identifies the discrepancy and routes it to the responsible lender or legal reviewer rather than independently imposing a remedy.

### Applied exercise: A34 — Cross-document exception report

The note names Pine LLC as borrower; the deed of trust names its member individually; the title commitment shows Pine LLC owns the property; an unsigned rider provides a balloon; the closing summary calls the loan fully amortizing. Write an exception report identifying the contradictions and the professionals who must resolve them before funding.

Feedback: borrower/grantor authority and identity, collateral ownership, rider execution, and maturity terms need reconciliation. Do not choose the most favorable document and disregard the others. Lender, title professional, and counsel should resolve the legal documents.

### Statute reading exercise: S17 — Identify the instrument actors

Read [Texas Property Code §51.0001 and §§51.0074–51.0075](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/PR/htm/PR.51.htm). Build a role table for mortgagor, mortgagee, mortgage servicer, trustee, and substitute trustee. Match the statute's definitions and authority to these hypothetical facts: Oak Lending is the named beneficiary; River Servicing is the authenticated payment recipient acting for the mortgagee; Dana grants the security; an authorized substitute trustee is appointed by a written instrument. Identify what document you would request to verify each role. Then correct the statement, “The trustee must be Dana's personal fiduciary and owns the debt because the trustee can conduct a sale.” Do not determine a live foreclosure's validity from this exercise.

Feedback: the role table separates granting security, holding the specified mortgagee interest, servicing payments, and exercising sale authority. The chapter's trustee-duty provision does not make the trustee the mortgagor's or mortgagee's fiduciary. Appointment and instrument authority require evidence. A correct answer cites the relevant statutory definition and does not assume that the loan owner and servicer must be different or must always be the same entity.

### Applied exercise: A75 — Reconcile the instrument package

Review an original hypothetical package for a two-parcel purchase. The contract names buyer Mesquite Homes LLC and parcels A and B. The title commitment names that LLC as proposed insured owner and lists a $45,000 existing second lien. The new note states $360,000 principal, 6% annual interest, and $2,158.38 monthly P&I with a five-year maturity but 30-year amortization. The deed of trust names the LLC's manager individually as grantor, describes only parcel A, and says it secures a $350,000 note. The sales summary calls the loan “fully paid in five years.” A draft subordination agreement permits a new first lien of no more than $340,000. The loan's partial-release terms have not been provided.

Prepare an exception register with at least six distinct contradictions or missing facts. For the first payment, calculate interest and principal reduction under monthly accrual. State why that payment does not retire the loan in five years. For each exception assign a reviewer: lender for loan terms, title professional for insured estate and requirements, and counsel for entity authority, legal language, and enforceability. Draft a concise hold-for-review recommendation that does not rewrite the instruments or promise the loan cannot close under any corrected structure.

Feedback: issues include entity versus individual grantor, omitted parcel, $360,000/$350,000 secured-amount conflict, five-year maturity versus 30-year amortization, inaccurate sales summary, $340,000 subordination ceiling, treatment of the $45,000 second, and missing partial-release terms. First interest is $1,800; principal reduction is $358.38. A positive balance remains at five-year maturity. The appropriate outcome is documented correction and review, not choosing whichever page supports the desired result. The exercise's payment is rounded and does not substitute for the lender's actual schedule.
