# Module 02: Money & the Monetary System

REF-0 topic T02 suggests 100 minutes. This module distinguishes the institutions that issue money, conduct monetary policy, insure deposits, and supply liquidity to lenders. Its examples explain mechanisms; they do not predict rates or investment returns.

## T02U1 — What is Money & The Federal Reserve System

Money serves as a medium of exchange, unit of account, and store of value. A bank deposit is a claim on a bank and can function as money in payments. A house, bond, or stock may be valuable without being money that a seller routinely accepts for settlement. Liquidity describes the ability to meet obligations or convert an asset to usable funds without unacceptable loss. Net worth and liquidity are different: a borrower can have substantial home equity yet lack the cash required at closing.

The Federal Reserve is the United States central bank. Its structure includes the Board of Governors, twelve regional Reserve Banks, and the Federal Open Market Committee. The FOMC makes monetary-policy decisions; Reserve Banks perform operational functions such as payment services and lending to eligible depository institutions. The Federal Reserve is not a consumer mortgage lender and does not approve a buyer's credit application.

Banks connect funding sources with credit demand, subject to capital, liquidity, supervision, risk management, and legal constraints. A bank's ability to lend should not be explained solely as a mechanical multiple of cash in a vault. Deposits, borrowing, asset sales, payment flows, credit demand, and prudential requirements all matter. Distinguish bank capital, which absorbs losses, from liquid assets, which meet cash demands. An institution can encounter liquidity pressure even when its assets are expected to exceed liabilities over time.

### Applied exercise: A06 — Classify the balance sheet

A hypothetical lender holds $12 million in cash and $88 million in loans; it owes $90 million to depositors and other creditors. Calculate its accounting equity. If $18 million in deposits leave immediately, identify the liquidity gap before asset sales or new borrowing. Explain why the equity calculation does not itself solve the withdrawal problem.

Feedback: assets are $100 million and liabilities $90 million, so equity is $10 million. Cash is $6 million short of the requested withdrawals. Selling assets, collecting payments, or obtaining funding may supply liquidity, each with constraints and possible costs. Equity is not a separate $10 million cash account available to add automatically to the $12 million cash balance.

## T02U2 — Instruments of Credit Policy

The Federal Reserve's published tool inventory includes open market operations, the discount window, interest on reserve balances, overnight reverse repurchase operations, reserve requirements, and other liquidity tools. Their functions differ. Buying or selling securities changes the central bank balance sheet and reserve conditions. Interest paid on reserve balances affects incentives for banks holding reserve balances. The discount window supplies collateralized central bank credit to eligible institutions subject to its terms. A reverse repurchase operation temporarily exchanges securities and cash under an agreement to reverse the transaction.

Do not teach that changing reserve requirements is the only or routine way monetary policy currently operates. A list of historically important tools is not evidence that each is being changed today. The relevant operating framework and tool settings must be verified from the Federal Reserve's current materials. This course asks you to understand functions, not memorize an unverified current target or reserve percentage.

Transmission takes time and is uncertain. Policy affects short-term funding conditions; expectations influence longer-term yields; financial institutions price credit; households and businesses respond. Mortgage-backed security investors also consider how quickly borrowers may prepay. A fixed mortgage's long cash-flow horizon and prepayment option make its pricing different from an overnight interbank transaction. A policy announcement and a mortgage quote are related through markets, not through an automatic one-for-one formula.

Distinguish an administered rate from a market yield. An administered rate is set for an identified facility or balance under the Federal Reserve's operating framework. A market yield emerges from trading and expectations about future cash flows and risk. Even when they move together, they are not the same contractual number. A lender funding a long-term mortgage must also consider the possibility that borrowers refinance early, the cost of hedging a pipeline, and what investors will pay for the resulting asset. Those considerations help explain why a retail quote can move before an announced policy decision or move differently afterward.

Be cautious with the textbook claim that every dollar of reserves creates an exact fixed multiple of mortgage lending. That mechanical illustration omits capital, liquidity requirements, borrower demand, risk appetite, funding costs, and underwriting constraints. A bank with additional liquidity need not find an eligible borrower for every available dollar. A bank with strong loan demand still must fund its commitments and manage loss risk. The useful question is which constraint is binding in the actual institution and transaction. This course uses balance-sheet arithmetic to organize the facts, not to promise a fixed economy-wide lending multiplier.

## T02U3 — The U.S. Treasury & U.S. Mint

The Treasury manages federal finances and issues Treasury securities to finance authorized government borrowing. The United States Mint produces coins; the Bureau of Engraving and Printing produces paper currency. The Federal Reserve participates in distributing currency and operating payment infrastructure. These separate functions explain why “printing money,” fiscal spending, debt issuance, and monetary policy are not interchangeable descriptions of one agency's action.

A Treasury security is an investment obligation; a Treasury yield is a market return associated with its price and cash flows. Mortgage lenders and investors may use Treasury yields as reference points, but a mortgage carries different cash-flow, prepayment, credit, servicing, and liquidity characteristics. A quoted spread is a comparison, not a universal fee imposed by the Treasury. For example, a hypothetical mortgage yield of 6.4% compared with a 4.3% reference Treasury yield has a 2.1 percentage-point spread, or 210 basis points. That calculation does not show that all 210 points are lender profit.

### Applied exercise: A08 — Agency and spread check

Correct these statements in a short memo: “The Mint approves Treasury auctions”; “The Treasury's benchmark yield is the buyer's mortgage rate”; “A spread of 1.85 percentage points means 1.85 basis points.” Include a worked conversion of 75 basis points into a percentage-point change.

Add a dated hypothetical comparison: a reference yield rises from 4.10% to 4.45%, while the mortgage quote rises from 6.30% to 6.55%. Calculate each movement and both mortgage/reference spreads. Write two sentences separating the observed numbers from an unproved causal claim. Identify the source needed for Treasury borrowing responsibility and the separate source needed for the actual mortgage quote.

Feedback: Treasury borrowing, coin production, and consumer mortgage pricing are different functions. One percentage point is 100 basis points, so 1.85 points equals 185 basis points and 75 basis points equals 0.75 percentage point. A move from 6% to 6.75% is a 75-basis-point increase, not a 75% rate.

Additional feedback: The reference moves 35 basis points and the mortgage moves 25. The spread narrows from 220 to 210 basis points; the mortgage still becomes more expensive at the quoted rate. A narrower spread does not mean a lower absolute coupon. Agency information verifies institutional responsibility; a lender offer verifies borrower-specific pricing.

## T02U4 — The Federal Deposit Insurance Corp (FDIC)

FDIC deposit insurance protects eligible deposits at insured banks within applicable limits and ownership categories. The standard coverage is $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category as verified from FDIC's current explanation. Multiple accounts in the same ownership category at the same bank are aggregated; opening a second savings account does not automatically double coverage. Different branches of the same insured bank are not separate banks for this purpose.

Deposit insurance is not insurance against a borrower's mortgage default and is not a guarantee of the value of stocks, bonds, mutual funds, or mortgage-backed securities sold through a bank. Nor does an insured bank's failure forgive outstanding borrower debts. For an actual depositor's coverage question, identify legal ownership, account category, beneficiaries where relevant, and the insured institution; use FDIC's official tools rather than estimating from account nicknames.

### Applied exercise: A09 — Deposit protection

Jordan has a $170,000 checking account and $110,000 savings account, both solely owned without beneficiaries at the same FDIC-insured bank. Jordan also owns $40,000 in a mutual fund purchased through that bank. Determine the combined single-account deposits, the amount exceeding standard coverage in that category, and whether the mutual fund is an insured deposit. State the missing information if Jordan later adds a trust account.

Feedback: deposits aggregate to $280,000; $30,000 exceeds the standard single-account coverage. The mutual fund is not FDIC-insured. A trust analysis requires the actual ownership and beneficiary facts under the current trust rules, not simply the word “trust” on a statement. This exercise does not advise restructuring accounts.

## T02U5 — The Federal Home Loan Bank(FHLB) System

The Federal Home Loan Bank System supplies liquidity mainly through secured advances to its members. FHFA describes eleven regional FHLBanks and their Office of Finance. Each regional bank is a government-chartered, member-owned corporation. Eligible collateral and membership requirements govern access; the system is not a walk-in source of first mortgages for individual homebuyers.

An advance is a loan to a member institution. If a member pledges qualifying mortgage assets and borrows funds, its obligation to the FHLBank is separate from each homeowner's obligation to the member. Funding can help the member manage the difference between when assets pay cash and when liabilities require cash. Collateral protects the advance; it does not remove the homeowner's repayment duty or make every mortgage on the member's books government-guaranteed.

Distinguish an advance from a mortgage sale. In a simplified advance example, the member still owns the mortgage asset and owes a secured funding obligation. In a sale, the mortgage asset moves to the purchaser subject to the transaction's terms. Both can supply liquidity, but their balance-sheet and risk consequences differ. Affordable housing and community investment programs are additional system activities whose terms must be checked for a particular project.

### Applied exercise: A10 — Funding alternatives

A member bank needs $8 million of liquidity. Compare borrowing an $8 million secured FHLBank advance with selling $8 million of mortgage loans at par in a simplified no-fee example. Draw the direction of cash and identify the asset or liability that remains after each transaction. Explain why neither event means a retail borrower receives a free grant.

Feedback: the advance increases cash and a funding liability while pledged assets remain owned subject to the security arrangement. A true sale exchanges mortgage assets for cash, subject to sale terms and any retained obligations. Homeowners still owe their contractual debt. Actual transactions require accounting and legal analysis beyond this simplified comparison.

## T02U6 — Information Resources

Start with the agency that performs the function. Use Federal Reserve policy statements and tool explanations for monetary policy; Treasury and its bureaus for federal finances, securities, and currency production; FDIC for bank insurance; NCUA for federally insured credit-union share insurance; and FHFA/FHLBank materials for system structure and advances. A source's institutional authority is part of its meaning: an FDIC insurance page does not establish FHA underwriting standards.

Separate a durable institutional explanation from a changing figure. The difference between a loan sale and an advance is a conceptual distinction. A policy target, a securities yield, an assistance allocation, or a lender's price changes over time. When communicating a changing figure, include the effective or observation date and verify that the source is current. If a webpage cannot be retrieved, record the retrieval failure; do not treat a blank page as evidence that a law or program has ceased to exist.
