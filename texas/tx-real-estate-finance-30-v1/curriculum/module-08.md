# Module 08: Government Loans

REF-0 topic T08 suggests 215 minutes. Government involvement changes risk allocation and program requirements; it does not remove underwriting. HUD's current handbook information page lists an August 12, 2026 update. Use the applicable effective provisions and current mortgagee letters for an actual file. Workshop premiums, fees, ratios, and rates below are expressly supplied hypothetical values, not current program quotations.

## T08U1 — Federal Housing Administration (FHA)

FHA is part of HUD and insures eligible mortgages made by approved lenders. The originating lender supplies the loan, and FHA insurance covers specified lender losses under program conditions. The borrower still signs a note and security instrument, makes required payments, maintains the property, and complies with occupancy and other obligations. Insurance endorsement does not warrant market value or the absence of defects.

Program administration matters. HUD publishes policy in Handbook 4000.1 and updates through identified policy materials. Lenders must determine the applicable effective date and required documentation. A lender overlay can be more restrictive than a program minimum; the overlay should not be described as a universal FHA statutory rule. A borrower turned down by one lender has not necessarily received a determination that no FHA-insured loan could ever be available.

Use a layered rule review for an actual FHA file. First identify the program and transaction type. Then locate the applicable handbook section, its effective provision, and any later mortgagee letter affecting that subject. Finally identify the lender's own permissible overlay and the facts the underwriter still needs. A general consumer page is useful orientation but may not carry every operational update. A search result that quotes an old limit should not override a later effective policy source. Keep a note explaining which version governs the file instead of silently mixing requirements from different dates.

The distinction became concrete during this course's September 2026 source review: HUD's general program overview and later 203(k) policy material did not use the same historical maximum-rehabilitation figure. The course therefore does not publish an unsourced permanent limit. It directs the learner to the operative handbook and mortgagee letters. This is not merely a citation habit. A contractor's work plan, borrower cash need, and expected closing timeline can all be wrong if based on a superseded program condition. Current-policy verification belongs before promising financing, not after a buyer signs a construction agreement.

### Applied exercise: A45 — Insurance identity

Explain the roles of buyer, lender, FHA, appraiser, and servicer in an FHA purchase. Identify who owes the monthly installment and who decides whether the lender's remaining underwriting conditions are met.

Review three assertions in a proposed buyer email: insurance makes an FHA loan risk-free to the household; a completed appraisal guarantees no defects; and counseling substitutes for underwriting. Write a correction for each and identify the document or professional needed for the unanswered question. Then prepare an ownership budget category list showing at least four obligations that remain after the loan is insured.

Feedback: the borrower owes the installment; the lender determines its credit approval and program compliance. FHA insurance does not turn the appraiser into a home inspector or the servicer into the owner in every case.

Additional feedback: The borrower still owes the note and faces ownership expenses. Appraisal and independent inspection serve different purposes. Counseling helps with informed decisions but does not replace lender qualification. Taxes, insurance, repairs, utilities, association obligations where applicable, and debt payments need separate budget treatment; a program label does not pay them.

## T08U2 — FHA - Insured Loan Program

An FHA-insured mortgage must satisfy program rules for borrower, property, amount, documentation, and insurance. Mortgage insurance can include an upfront amount and periodic premiums. The method of payment, financing eligibility, premium amount, and duration depend on the program and loan circumstances. Do not apply conventional PMI cancellation rules to FHA mortgage insurance.

Separate base mortgage from any financed upfront premium. A hypothetical base loan of $250,000 with an assumed upfront premium of $4,375 would produce $254,375 financed principal if the entire premium is financed and the scenario permits it. The payment calculation uses the resulting note principal, not merely the base amount. A financed fee reduces cash needed today but increases debt and interest-bearing principal.

Build the transaction in layers: acquisition price, borrower funds toward acquisition, base mortgage, permitted financed insurance, other financed amounts if any, and total note principal. Then build cash to close separately from costs, prepaids, escrow, credits, and deposits. Mixing these layers creates common errors. A financed premium does not reduce the purchase price; a gift does not increase monthly earnings; a seller credit does not necessarily satisfy every required borrower contribution; and an escrow deposit does not reduce principal. The lender must apply the actual program restrictions to the sources and uses.

If a borrower can pay a premium in cash, the comparison involves both payment and liquidity. Financing it preserves cash today but increases the interest-bearing balance. Paying it in cash lowers debt but leaves less money for repairs and emergencies. Neither choice is automatically best for every household. Use the actual available options, verified eligibility, term, rate, and intended holding period. A small payment difference can correspond to a meaningful reserve difference, while a larger financed balance also changes the amount owed at sale or refinancing.

Handbook 4000.1, II.A.2.e.i(A), printed page 182 in the August 12, 2026 edition, requires the upfront premium to be entirely financed or entirely paid in cash. When financed, the exception is the portion below $1; the mortgage amount is rounded down to the nearest whole dollar whether the premium is financed or cash-paid. This narrow rounding treatment does not authorize an elective half-financed premium. When the premium is paid entirely in cash, adding that same premium to note principal as financed would duplicate its funding treatment. Reconcile the base mortgage, actual cash payment, and note amount across the lender and settlement records. A correct total requires consistent program treatment, not just a mathematically correct addition.

## T08U3 — Underwriting Guidelines

Underwriting examines verified income, employment when applicable, assets, credit, liabilities, housing expenses, property eligibility, and program requirements. Automated underwriting findings are conditional instructions based on the submitted data; inaccurate inputs cannot produce a reliable approval. A manual review has its own standards. Lender overlays, program rules, and legal requirements should be identified separately.

Income stability and continuation matter as well as amount. A wage statement, overtime history, self-employment income, and retirement income require different evidence. Assets must be available and from acceptable documented sources. Debts cannot be omitted because they are inconvenient. Qualifying ratios use the program's definitions and do not represent a personal recommendation to spend the maximum permitted amount.

Treat automated findings as a controlled output tied to specific inputs. If employment, income, liabilities, assets, occupancy, or property facts change, the lender must determine the necessary re-evaluation. A screenshot of an earlier favorable result is not evidence that changed facts remain eligible. A processing checklist should identify the data version, unresolved documentation, and who is authorized to clear each condition. The real estate professional's useful contribution is prompt, accurate coordination and clear status reporting, not an independent declaration that an automated result has waived the program rules.

Avoid conflating household cash flow with eligible income. A household may receive irregular transfers that are not accepted as qualifying income, or have lawful stable income requiring specific documentation. Likewise, a deduction used in a household budget may not be a debt counted in a lender's ratio, while a deferred obligation can still require program-specific treatment. Both analyses matter, but they answer different questions. The lender determines qualification using its applicable standards; the household must still decide whether the payment leaves room for its actual living costs and goals.

FHA Connection provides a concrete example of the difference between an automated category and a final decision. When changed data produces the message “TOTAL CASE RESCORED AS REFER,” the explanation calls for manual underwriting. A referral is neither a permanent denial nor permission to preserve an outdated favorable result. The lender must use the corrected file and the required manual process to make its final credit decision. Record the changed inputs and route the result to the authorized underwriter. Source: [FHA Connection, rescored Refer explanation](https://entp.hud.gov/common/f17error-pop.cfm?message=TOTAL+CASE+RESCORED+AS+REFER%3B+MANUAL+UNDERWRITING+REQUIRED%3B+ZFHA+INVALID&program=F17EUC).

## T08U4 — Most Frequently Used FHA Loans

Section 203(b) is associated with FHA's basic single-family mortgage insurance program. Section 203(k) combines eligible purchase or refinancing with rehabilitation under program controls. A Home Equity Conversion Mortgage (HECM) is FHA's reverse-mortgage program for eligible older homeowners, with counseling and other requirements. Product purpose matters: a rehabilitation project, ordinary purchase, and reverse-mortgage transaction do not use identical underwriting or disbursement rules.

For rehabilitation lending, work scope, contractor qualifications, inspections, draws, contingency, and completion requirements affect feasibility. For a reverse mortgage, the borrower retains obligations such as taxes, insurance, property maintenance, and occupancy; repayment can be triggered by specified events. “No required monthly principal-and-interest payment” must never be shortened to “no homeowner obligations.” Consult current HUD program materials for eligibility and limits.

Rehabilitation financing adds completion risk to ordinary purchase risk. Before relying on a proposed loan, identify eligible work, cost estimates, contractor arrangements, permits where needed, completion time, contingency funds, inspection requirements, and draw administration. A contractor wanting full payment before work and a lender releasing funds under completion controls can create a practical conflict even when the total budget fits. The solution is an approved, workable plan, not a side agreement hiding an advance from the lender. A change in scope should be documented and reviewed for its cost, eligibility, timing, and collateral effect.

The Limited and Standard 203(k) structures are not interchangeable labels. HUD Mortgagee Letter 2026-06, effective immediately on June 23, 2026, addresses draw procedures and distinguishes the programs. For example, its revised Limited structure permits at most four draw requests per contractor under the stated sequence and allows specified separate disbursements within a draw. This does not authorize every proposed renovation or waive other conditions. A lender must use the applicable current policy and actual work scope. The course's lab below stipulates an eligible work plan so that arithmetic does not masquerade as program approval.

## T08U5 — Direct Endorsement

Direct Endorsement allows qualified FHA-approved mortgagees to underwrite eligible loans and submit them for insurance endorsement under HUD's requirements. It is delegated lender responsibility, not permission to ignore FHA standards. The lender's underwriter must document the decision; quality control and potential enforcement remain relevant.

Distinguish an underwriter's approval, loan closing, and insurance endorsement. A file can encounter a defect between stages. A real estate agent should communicate the lender's actual status rather than announce that HUD individually reviewed every document before the buyer signed. Questions about a lender's authority should be verified from approved-lender resources and the applicable handbook provisions.

Delegation makes documented responsibility more important, not less. HUD's FHA Connection explanation states that the lender is responsible for ensuring its employed DE underwriters meet the applicable qualifications and are registered through the Underwriter Registry. Do not describe that registry as HUD individually approving today's loan file. A real estate professional generally does not need access to a lender's internal systems to communicate accurately. Ask the lender for its actual approval status and remaining conditions, and avoid unsupported statements about the underwriter's credentials or an endorsement not yet issued.

## T08U6 — FHA Contributions to Real Estate Finance

Mortgage insurance can support lending with borrower equity levels or risk characteristics a lender might otherwise reject, within program requirements. FHA's standardized requirements and insurance mechanism have influenced residential finance. That role should be understood alongside the costs of insurance, underwriting discipline, borrower obligations, and potential losses from default.

Evaluate a program through access, affordability, sustainability, and risk allocation. A small initial cash requirement can improve access but leaves less equity cushion. Insurance can protect lenders while borrowers still face foreclosure. Comparisons should use actual available alternatives with aligned assumptions, not unsupported claims that FHA is always cheapest or only for first-time buyers.

Access and resilience should be evaluated together. A household may be able to preserve savings with lower initial equity, yet the insurance cost and slower creation of equity can affect future options. A household making a larger down payment may have a smaller loan but inadequate liquid reserves. Compare both the closing position and a future adverse event. An agent should not steer a buyer into a program solely because it appears easiest to market or because a preferred lender offers compensation. Provide accurate program information and let verified options, lawful underwriting, and the consumer's objectives guide the decision.

## T08U7 — VA Loan Guarantee Program

VA-backed purchase loans are made by private lenders for eligible borrowers under VA requirements. A Certificate of Eligibility establishes an entitlement-related eligibility component; it is not lender credit approval. The lender evaluates credit, income, occupancy, property, entitlement, and other applicable conditions. VA's guaranty reduces specified lender risk; it does not transfer the borrower's payment duty to VA.

VA-backed loans do not require PMI or FHA-style MIP. A funding fee may apply, with exemptions and amounts depending on the borrower's circumstances and transaction. Never assume every veteran owes the same fee or every veteran is exempt. Entitlement and loan-limit questions require current VA-specific guidance; a generic mortgage limit should not be copied into a VA explanation without checking its applicability.

VA underwriting considers residual income as well as debt obligations. Residual income is money remaining after specified deductions and obligations under VA's method; it is not simply gross income less the mortgage payment. A VA appraisal and Notice of Value address program valuation/property matters but are not a comprehensive home inspection. If price exceeds value, entitlement or a no-down-payment marketing phrase does not automatically fund the difference.

The current VA underwriting regulation, 38 CFR §36.4340, treats DTI and residual income as related but distinct standards, with conditions for review of marginal cases. It expressly does not turn satisfaction of those measures into automatic approval. Residual analysis incorporates specified deductions, shelter costs, obligations, and household circumstances. A gross-income-minus-P&I shortcut leaves out important inputs. When a file is marginal, a relevant compensating factor must address the actual weakness; a favorable characteristic is not a universal substitute for another missing requirement.

VA purchase planning also requires the household to distinguish loan eligibility from the contract's economics. If a seller will not reduce a price exceeding the supported value, the buyer needs a lawful, documented way to address the gap or a contract decision with appropriate advice. A no-monthly-MI feature does not pay taxes, insurance, closing charges, or a value gap. A funding-fee exemption needs verification through the proper process, not an agent's inference from military service alone. The final comparison should use the borrower-specific documented fee status and actual loan terms.

## T08U8 — Additional VA Loan Programs

An Interest Rate Reduction Refinance Loan (IRRRL) refinances an existing VA-backed loan under its requirements and may reduce payments or stabilize an adjustable rate. The federal VA cash-out program is a different category and can reach certain non-VA debt, but the federal label does not establish a permissible Texas homestead transaction. A Native American Direct Loan is a separate direct-loan program for eligible borrowers and property arrangements; it should not be described as the same private-lender guaranty transaction.

Texas Constitution art. XVI §50(a)(6)(H) limits a qualifying homestead equity loan to the homestead as collateral. Attorney General Opinion KP-0183, pages 3–4, explains why the VA guaranty constitutes additional collateral and prevents a VA-guaranteed Texas homestead equity cash-out loan under that provision. Do not route such a borrower to a VA-guaranteed equity loan or claim that a lower loan amount cures the additional-collateral problem. Footnote 7 distinguishes traditional VA purchase-money and refinance loans outside §50(a)(6); the conclusion is not a ban on every VA purchase, ordinary refinance, or transaction merely carrying a federal cash-out label. The lender and counsel must classify the actual proceeds, existing lien, property and legal structure. Source: [KP-0183](https://www2.texasattorneygeneral.gov/opinions/opinions/51paxton/op/2018/kp0183.pdf), read with the [current Constitution §50](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/CN/htm/CN.16.htm).

Refinancing still has costs. Financing costs into the balance reduces immediate cash but increases debt. “Skip payments” marketing can obscure accrued interest and loan-cost consequences. For an IRRRL with a second lien, priority arrangements must be addressed. A program's federal availability does not displace the Texas classification above.

Separate general economic break-even from the IRRRL fee-recoupment requirement. Under [38 U.S.C. §3709(a)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A38+section%3A3709+edition%3Aprelim%29) and [VA Circular 26-19-22, paragraph 3.a](https://www.benefits.va.gov/HOMELOANS/documents/circulars/26_19_22.pdf), an IRRRL with lower monthly P&I must recoup the included fees, closing costs and expenses within 36 months. The statutory calculation excludes taxes, escrow and chapter 37 fees such as the VA funding fee; included charges matter whether financed or paid separately. The circular also addresses same/higher-payment loans and identifies a broader comparison-statement calculation in paragraph 3.d. Do not substitute that broader disclosure number for the statutory calculation or assume passing a simple ratio satisfies all net-benefit, seasoning and other requirements. The lender must classify actual charges and apply the operative rule.

An IRRRL comparison should identify the existing loan, proposed structure, benefit, costs, balance change, and applicable requirements. A borrower moving from an ARM to a fixed rate may be seeking stability rather than the lowest initial rate. A cash-out transaction introduces different proceeds and underwriting questions. A direct-loan program also has a different creditor relationship from a private-lender guaranteed loan. The label “VA refinance” is therefore not enough to select forms, determine eligibility, or calculate legal requirements. Obtain the actual product and source before using a generalized checklist.
