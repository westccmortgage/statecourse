# Module 09: Lender Loan Processes

REF-0 topic T09 suggests 220 minutes. This module assembles borrower, collateral, title, and closing evidence into a coherent loan file. All specimen facts are invented; never use real private borrower data for these exercises.

## T09U1 — Qualifying the Borrower

Qualification examines willingness and capacity to repay, eligible assets, and the complete transaction. Gross income is not always qualifying income. Variable earnings, self-employment, retirement, rental income, and support income can require distinct documentation and calculations. Assets need verification of ownership, access, source, and acceptable use. Debts include obligations the applicable program requires even when the borrower does not volunteer them.

Debt-to-income ratio divides defined monthly debt by eligible gross monthly income. A hypothetical $2,400 housing obligation plus $600 other debt against $7,500 income gives 40% DTI. That is an arithmetic result, not a universal approval standard. Residual-income and household-budget analyses address different questions. Consistent criteria and lawful treatment of income sources are essential under fair-lending law.

Build a qualifying-income worksheet from sources rather than from the most favorable number on a document. Salary, hourly wages, overtime, commissions, self-employment, retirement income, and other lawful sources can require different evidence and calculations. A gross bank deposit may include transfers, reimbursement, or borrowed funds rather than income. A profitable business's revenue is not automatically the owner's qualifying income. The lender determines eligible amounts under applicable standards; the agent can identify inconsistencies, ask for a current status, and avoid characterizing an unsupported figure as verified.

Use the same date and scope when comparing the application with supporting records. A pay raise effective next month and a pay stub from last month describe different periods. A debt paid off after the report date requires evidence of payoff and its effect under the lender's rules. A line of credit with no current balance may still be relevant to the complete file. Keep a changes log so that a corrected figure reaches underwriting, disclosures, and closing as appropriate. A file is not accurate merely because each document was accurate at some different point in time.

### Applied exercise: A55 — Ratio reconciliation

Income is initially listed as $8,000 but eligible verified income is $7,400; housing is $2,300; other debt is $720. Calculate both DTIs and draft a correction explaining why the application and underwriting inputs must agree.

Prepare a revised monthly budget alongside the underwriting calculation. Assume required household living costs outside the stated debt numerator are $3,800 and the household actually receives $6,000 spendable income after deductions. Compute the remaining cash after $3,020 debt/housing obligations and living costs. Explain why gross eligible-income DTI and spendable household cash answer different questions.

Feedback: 37.75% and approximately 40.81%. Do not preserve a favorable ratio by leaving unsupported income in the system.

Additional feedback: The budget is $6,000 − $3,020 − $3,800 = negative $820. The lender's 40.81% ratio does not eliminate that supplied budget deficit. The memo should preserve the verified gross-income calculation while separately identifying the household's cash constraint and opportunities for accurate review; do not change the ratio denominator to force a desired result.

## T09U2 — Qualifying the Collateral

Collateral review considers value, condition, eligibility, marketability, legal use, hazards, insurance, and the interest pledged. An appraisal is an opinion of value for a defined assignment. A home inspection evaluates condition under its scope. A survey addresses boundaries and improvements under its scope. Title review addresses legal ownership and encumbrances. None substitutes fully for the others.

A lower appraisal can increase required cash, change LTV, or affect program eligibility. If a program uses the lower of price and value, a $320,000 price and $300,000 appraisal with 90% maximum financing supports $270,000, leaving $50,000 of price to fund before costs. The buyer may have contractual rights, but an appraisal result does not itself rewrite the purchase contract.

An appraisal condition can affect the funding plan even when the opinion of value exceeds price. If the appraisal assumes completion of a repair, the lender may need completion evidence or an eligible alternative structure before funding. A buyer's inspection contingency and the lender's collateral requirement can operate on different timelines. The lender may accept a property that the buyer nevertheless finds unsuitable, or the buyer may accept a condition that the lender will not finance without correction. Explain both decision channels and preserve contract deadlines rather than treating lender approval as a substitute for the buyer's due diligence.

### Applied exercise: A56 — Value shortfall

For a $450,000 price, $430,000 value, and hypothetical 80% maximum purchase LTV, calculate supported loan and price gap. Identify valuation-review, financing, and contract questions without promising that the seller must reduce the price.

Separate the $106,000 price contribution into the down payment on supported value and the excess price above value. Then test a negotiated price of $435,000 with the same $430,000 supported value and unchanged maximum LTV. Recompute the loan and required price contribution. Draft two questions for the lender and one for contract counsel or the authorized broker concerning actual rights and deadlines.

Feedback: loan $344,000; price gap $106,000. A lender review, documented alternatives, and the actual contract determine next steps.

Additional feedback: At the original price, $86,000 is 20% of the $430,000 value and $20,000 is the excess price. At $435,000 price, the loan remains $344,000 and contribution becomes $91,000 before costs. Arithmetic does not create a seller duty to amend the contract or waive financing deadlines.

## T09U3 — Review of Sample Credit Report

A credit report can include identifying information, tradelines, balances, limits, payment history, collection information, inquiries, and other permitted data. It is evidence to evaluate, not a complete autobiography or a substitute for application disclosures. Credit scores are model-specific summaries; two scores may differ because of data, timing, or model. Never promise a particular score increase from a generic action.

Sample report: revolving account balance $2,400, limit $8,000, minimum payment $80; auto loan balance $11,000, payment $360; student loan reported payment $0; a recent mortgage inquiry; and an address unfamiliar to the borrower. Revolving utilization is 30%. The $0 student payment requires program-specific treatment, not automatic exclusion. The inquiry may indicate shopping or new debt; verify. An unfamiliar address may be an error or identity concern; investigate through authorized channels.

Reconcile balances and required payments separately. A $10,000 installment balance does not imply a $10,000 monthly debt, and a revolving account's balance divided by an arbitrary number of months is not automatically the program's required payment. If the report shows zero or omits a payment, obtain the applicable statement and lender treatment. A co-signed obligation may require analysis even if another person usually pays it. A recently closed account does not necessarily remove past payment history. These distinctions keep a credit review grounded in obligations rather than assumptions designed to improve a ratio.

Use a secure correction process for suspected report errors. Identify the exact tradeline or identifying information, obtain the borrower's explanation, and route the matter through authorized lender and reporting-agency procedures. Do not promise that a dispute will produce a particular score by a contract deadline. A lender may need to consider dispute status under its rules, while the consumer has separate rights under applicable law. Retain factual records without circulating full reports to people who do not need them. Data minimization is especially important when a report contains information about a nonborrower or an unrelated account.

Aggregate revolving utilization uses total relevant balances divided by total relevant limits, not the unweighted average of individual account percentages. Suppose one card has a $900 balance and $3,000 limit, while another has a $2,100 balance and $7,000 limit. Combined utilization is $3,000 ÷ $10,000 = 30%. If limits differ and the individual percentages differ, a simple average gives each card equal weight even though the credit capacities differ. State which measure you are computing. Neither aggregate nor account utilization, standing alone, determines an actual credit score or mortgage approval.

### Applied exercise: A57 — Credit exception list

Prepare an exception list for the sample report identifying known monthly payments, unknown obligations, possible error, and permissible next questions. Explain why the lender cannot treat a disputed report entry as proven fraud without review.

Feedback: known payments total $440; student-loan treatment and any new debt need resolution. Use accurate dispute and verification procedures, protect personal data, and document the result.

## T09U4 — Review of Uniform Residential Loan Application

The Uniform Residential Loan Application (URLA), associated with Fannie Mae Form 1003 and Freddie Mac Form 65, organizes borrower, employment/income, assets, liabilities, real estate, loan/property, declarations, acknowledgments, and demographic information under the current form's structure. It is a signed representation of facts, not a place to omit inconvenient obligations.

Read declarations carefully for occupancy, other financed property, borrowed funds, obligations, and transaction interests. Loan-application demographics serve legally defined monitoring purposes and must be collected and handled according to applicable instructions, not used for prohibited discrimination. An application can change as facts are verified; obtain appropriate corrections and acknowledgments instead of preserving obsolete data.

Review the application as a connected set of statements. Declared occupancy should match the transaction's known purpose. The real-estate-owned section should reconcile with mortgages and rental income disclosed elsewhere. Funds described as gifts should not simultaneously appear as repayable personal loans without explanation. Ownership of a business may affect how employment and income are evaluated. These are consistency checks, not invitations to choose the more favorable answer. The borrower and lender should correct the actual facts through the appropriate process and maintain the required acknowledgments.

### Applied exercise: A58 — Application cross-check

The URLA states owner occupancy and $25,000 savings, while the contract identifies an investment purchase and bank evidence shows $15,000 savings plus a $10,000 unsecured loan. Identify corrections to occupancy, assets, and liabilities and explain who must re-evaluate the file.

Create a three-column correction sheet showing the inaccurate entry, the truthful replacement, and supporting evidence. The unsecured loan requires $220 monthly payments. Identify the affected liabilities and underwriting calculation without adding loan proceeds to income. Draft a neutral correction message that states facts and requests lender review, then explain why merely changing an occupancy checkbox without reporting intended use and debt is incomplete.

Feedback: disclose the actual use, accurate owned funds, and borrowed obligation. The lender must evaluate the corrected transaction; a signed initial application does not authorize retaining errors.

Additional feedback: Record the actual investment intent, $15,000 owned savings, and the $10,000 borrowing with its $220 obligation, subject to the lender's source eligibility rules. The lender may need a different product or financing arrangement. Accurate totals cannot cure a misrepresented purpose or unsupported source.

### Statute reading exercise: S23 — Follow facts through the URLA

Open Freddie Mac's [current joint-GSE URLA resources](https://sf.freddiemac.com/tools-learning/uniform-mortgage-data-program/ulad), then read the Borrower Information form and the relevant Form 1003 instructions for employment/income, assets/liabilities, real estate, loan/property information, and declarations. Use only fictitious data. Feedback: the exercise is a cross-reference task, not a signature-ready application. The owned rental and its obligations must be reconciled, borrowed cash cannot be mislabeled a gift, and intended occupancy must be accurate. Variable income needs lender analysis and evidence. A correct map identifies the form's actual current section labels and flags inconsistent related entries. Do not send a fictitious application to a lender or enter invented borrower information into a live application system.

## T09U5 — Review of Request for Verification of Deposit

A deposit verification confirms identified account information through an authorized source. It may report account ownership, balances, and other requested facts. Bank statements, electronic verification, and a traditional verification form have different fields and limitations. A balance verification alone may not explain a recent deposit, prove that funds are unborrowed, or establish immediate access.

Authenticate the source and use appropriate borrower authorization. A document forwarded through an unknown email address may need independent verification. Evaluate significant deposits according to the applicable program; legitimate transfers between accounts should be documented rather than counted twice as new money.

An asset ledger should show ownership, date, gross balance, restrictions, verified transfers, intended closing use, and reserves remaining. A retirement account's statement balance may not equal accessible cash after withdrawal rules or other adjustments. Funds held for an entity may not be the individual's unrestricted asset. A gift source requires the applicable documentation and donor rules. Avoid double counting an earnest deposit already withdrawn from the same account or adding a transfer to both sending and receiving balances from mismatched dates. Reconciliation is a timing problem as well as an arithmetic problem.

### Applied exercise: A59 — Deposit trail

Account A loses $12,000 on Monday and account B gains $12,000 on Tuesday; both belong to the borrower. A worksheet adds both balances from different dates and claims $12,000 extra assets. Explain the error and the evidence needed to reconcile it.

Use a dated ledger: account A begins at $20,000 and account B at $5,000; the $12,000 transfer is the only movement. Calculate both aligned ending balances and total assets. Compare that result with a worksheet using A's beginning balance plus B's ending balance. Then identify transfer references and statement dates needed to prove the reconciliation.

Feedback: the transfer is one pool of money, not two assets. Use aligned statement dates and transfer records to avoid double counting.

Additional feedback: Aligned ending balances are A $8,000 and B $17,000, totaling $25,000. The mixed-date worksheet totals $37,000 and overstates funds by $12,000. The correction needs evidence of the single transfer and aligned balances; it does not establish that every remaining dollar is from an acceptable source or available for closing.

## T09U6 — Review of Request for Verification of Employment

Employment verification confirms facts such as status, dates, compensation, and variable earnings under the lender's process. Verification of employment and calculation of qualifying income are related but distinct. A current job does not prove that every recent bonus will recur. A pay stub can show gross and net amounts while a tax return and business analysis may be needed for self-employment.

Late changes matter. A job loss, change from salary to commission, or unpaid leave can affect repayment analysis. Verify through authorized independent contacts and update the lender promptly. Do not create an employer letter or choose only the most favorable pay period to misrepresent a pattern.

Independent verification reduces the risk of relying on a fabricated contact. Use the lender's approved process to authenticate the employer or verification provider; a telephone number supplied only in a suspicious letter may not be enough. Record what was verified and when, without turning a limited confirmation into a broader promise. An employer can confirm current employment without guaranteeing future earnings for the mortgage term. Likewise, a processor's receipt of a document is not the same as an underwriter's acceptance of the income calculation. Status language should make that distinction visible.

### Applied exercise: A60 — Employment change

Three days before signing, the borrower reports that a salaried job ended and a commission-only position begins next month. Draft a factual update and identify why the earlier approval cannot simply be assumed unchanged.

The new employer projects $9,000 monthly gross commissions but guarantees no salary; the borrower has not yet earned commissions there. Build an evidence checklist distinguishing projected revenue, actual earnings, expenses if relevant, employment terms, and applicable program history requirements. Draft a status message to the transaction team using only necessary information, and identify contract timing concerns for the responsible broker or counsel.

Feedback: income amount, history, stability, documentation, and program eligibility require re-evaluation. Concealment is not an acceptable response to a closing deadline.

Additional feedback: A forecast is not verified recurring eligible income. The lender must decide whether and how the new source can be used, with appropriate supporting evidence. Do not circulate the borrower's complete employment records to unnecessary recipients or declare a categorical denial solely from the course example; communicate the unresolved qualification and timing issue.

## T09U7 — Review of Uniform Residential Appraisal Report

Review an appraisal by identifying the subject property, rights appraised, effective date, intended use/users, assumptions, condition, neighborhood/market analysis, site, improvements, comparable sales, adjustments, reconciliation, and certification. An adjustment should reflect a supported market difference; it is not permission to force the conclusion to the contract price. A report “subject to” repairs or completion has conditions that must be resolved under the lender's requirements.

The official course form predates current appraisal-data changes. Fannie Mae's current UAD page states a November 2, 2026 UAD 3.6 mandate and describes a dynamic URAR replacing legacy forms, with transition details and updated FAQs. As of this course's September 7, 2026 verification, the transition is active. Teach the analytical fields and applicable delivery version; do not tell students every future report must always be a legacy Form 1004 layout.

Instructional comparison: subject is 1,800 square feet. Comparable A sold at $350,000 and receives a supported net +$10,000 adjustment; B sold at $365,000 and receives −$8,000; C sold at $355,000 and receives +$3,000. Adjusted indications are $360,000, $357,000, and $358,000. Their simple mean is $358,333.33, but an appraiser reconciles reliability and comparability rather than being required to average mechanically.

Read the reconciliation as an explanation, not just a final number. A comparable with similar location, condition, design, and recent sale evidence may be more persuasive than another requiring substantial uncertain adjustments. The smallest numerical adjustment is not automatically best if the underlying data are unreliable. Ask whether the report explains the choice of comparables, support for adjustments, market conditions, and weight given to the approaches used. Fannie Mae's current Selling Guide B4-1.3-11 describes a reasoned reconciliation and allows a properly explained weighted-average technique rather than an unexplained mechanical average. This is a program-specific source, not permission for a sales agent to independently revalue the property.

Identify whether a number is an observed fact, an assumption, an adjustment, or a conclusion. The comparable's sale price is a transaction fact subject to verification. A concession adjustment is an analytical response to financing or other terms. An assumed completed repair is a condition of the value conclusion. The final opinion synthesizes evidence at a particular effective date. Confusing these layers leads to weak review requests such as “raise value by the repair invoice amount” without market support. A repair's cost and its contribution to value need not be equal.

When asking for reconsideration, provide specific factual concerns through the lender's permitted process. Examples include an incorrect room count, an omitted relevant sale, an inconsistent legal description, or an unsupported assumption about condition. Explain the evidence without demanding that the appraiser reach the contract price or threatening future work. A corrected fact may change value, leave it unchanged, or require further analysis. The consumer's right to a copy of a valuation and the appraiser's independent judgment are compatible: access to the report is not authority to dictate the result.

### Applied exercise: A61 — Appraisal review memo

Use the three comparables to calculate adjusted indications and mean, then identify three questions a reviewer should ask before accepting a $390,000 conclusion. Include the effective date, market support for adjustments, and conditions/assumptions. Explain how to raise an error without directing a predetermined value.

Feedback: request factual correction or supported review through the lender's process. Contract price alone does not justify the unexplained conclusion. Valuation independence prohibits inappropriate pressure; sound review asks for evidence.

### Statute reading exercise: S07 — Valuation rights

Read Regulation B §1002.14(a) and Regulation Z §1026.42(c). Explain the consumer's right to copies of appraisals/valuations for covered first-lien dwelling applications and the difference between permissible factual review and coercion. Identify the timing question and any waiver conditions for an actual closing.

Feedback: copies and timing obligations are governed by the actual rule; paying for an appraisal does not authorize changing its conclusion. A timing waiver, when permitted, does not waive the right to receive a copy.

## T09U8 — Required Lender Notices

Notice requirements depend on transaction type and event. Examples include Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure, ECOA action notices, appraisal-copy notices and delivery, servicing-transfer notices, and applicable ARM or other product disclosures. One signed disclosure packet is not proof that every required notice was timely delivered. Record triggering event, coverage, deadline, delivery method, and evidence.

Under Regulation B §1002.9, the completed-application action-notice rule generally uses 30 days; incomplete applications have separate procedures. A counteroffer and adverse action may require different handling. A borrower should receive the reasons or notice of rights required by the rule, not an unexplained assertion that a computer rejected the file.

A notice register should retain both the triggering fact and evidence of delivery. “Sent to the buyer” can mean mailing, electronic transmission, personal delivery, or merely preparing a document for signature; the rule may treat those events differently. A business-day definition used for one waiting period may not govern another deadline. A calendar entry should identify the source provision and the person responsible for calculation. When a change occurs near closing, update the register instead of assuming the original scheduled date proves compliance. The agent can coordinate while the creditor determines its legal obligations.

### Applied exercise: A62 — Notice register

Create a notice register for application receipt, a completed application, appraisal completion, a scheduled consummation, and a later servicing transfer. Name the governing source to consult and the evidence to retain. Do not reuse “three business days” as a universal deadline.

Feedback: each event has its own rule and definition. An accurate register distinguishes delivery, mailing, receipt, and consummation where the applicable requirement does.

## T09U9 — Qualifying the Title

Title qualification asks whether the borrower can grant the estate and priority the lender requires. Review ownership, authority, legal description, existing liens, restrictions, easements, judgments, marital/homestead issues, and other exceptions. A title insurance commitment is a conditional promise to issue coverage when requirements are met; it is not an unconditional statement that no defect exists.

An owner's title policy and a lender's policy protect different insured interests. The lender's policy does not automatically protect the buyer's equity. Insurance also contains exclusions, exceptions, conditions, and limits. A survey and other documents can reveal matters requiring title review; “the loan was approved” is not a reason to disregard a boundary or ownership issue.

A lender's title requirements also interact with the property description and intended use. A borrower may own the surface estate while mineral-related exceptions remain. A recorded access easement may be essential to lawful access yet contain maintenance obligations or use limits. An entity's manager may need evidence of authority beyond a business card. These matters call for review of the actual instruments and applicable law. Do not equate a title commitment with an abstract or legal opinion on every ownership question. The commitment specifies a proposed insurance contract, its conditions, and excluded risks.

### Applied exercise: A63 — Ownership authority

The seller died after signing the contract, and a relative offers to sign closing documents without estate authority. Identify the title and authority issues and explain why lender credit approval does not resolve them.

Add that the relative supplies a power of attorney signed by the seller before death and says it proves present authority. Prepare a factual referral packet containing the contract, death information, ownership record, offered authority document, and proposed signing names. Explain why title and counsel must determine actual authority rather than letting the agent choose a signature format. Identify the separate lender concern if the delay exceeds a rate-lock period.

Feedback: ownership succession, estate authority, required parties, and insurability need title/counsel review. Family relationship alone does not establish authority to convey.

Additional feedback: Possession of a document does not establish that it remains effective or authorizes the proposed act after the owner's death. The estate, title, and legal facts require qualified review. A lock extension is a separate lender/pricing question; neither a prior credit approval nor a lock resolves ownership succession.

## T09U10 — Review of the Title Commitment

Texas title commitments commonly organize transaction/coverage information in Schedule A, exceptions in Schedule B, requirements in Schedule C, and specified disclosures in Schedule D under the applicable promulgated form. Read the current form and every incorporated exception. A requirement to pay and release an existing lien is different from an exception that will remain in the policy.

Instructional commitment: A names the proposed insured lender and legal description; B lists a utility easement; C requires release of a $190,000 prior deed of trust and evidence of entity authority; D supplies required title-company disclosures. Paying the old loan addresses a C requirement. It does not erase the B easement. A proposed policy amount should match the intended coverage and transaction documents.

Read all schedules with the effective date and policy type in view. The commitment can identify requirements that must be resolved to the insurer's satisfaction, exceptions that remain unless changed, and conditions limiting the promise to issue a policy. A new matter arising after the effective date can require additional attention. Request the actual recorded documents underlying material exceptions, not just the short index description. The lender's willingness to accept an exception does not necessarily mean the buyer's intended improvement or use is unaffected. The buyer may need counsel, a surveyor, or other specialist even if financing can proceed.

### Applied exercise: A64 — Commitment checklist

Make a before-closing checklist from the specimen: obtain a payoff, arrange release, verify entity authority, review the easement's effect, and compare legal descriptions. State which items require satisfaction and which require understanding or acceptable treatment as exceptions.

Feedback: payoff/release and authority are requirements; the easement needs review and resolution or acceptance under the lender's standards. Never promise that title insurance removes the physical easement.

### Statute reading exercise: S24 — Read the Texas commitment itself

Read TDI's currently linked [Form T-7, Commitment for Title Insurance](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/title/documents/form_t-07.pdf), including the information pages, conditions, and Schedules A–D. Confirm its presence in TDI's Basic Manual Section II rather than treating the form's older effective date as proof it is obsolete. Create a schedule map stating each schedule's purpose and the difference between an exception, exclusion, requirement, and condition. Apply it to a hypothetical prior deed of trust requiring release, an easement listed as an exception, a mismatch in proposed insured name, and an undisclosed new lien discovered before policy issuance. Identify what should be sent to the title professional and what may require counsel. Do not alter the promulgated form.

Feedback: the prior lien's required treatment is different from a continuing easement exception; proposed insured and estate information must be accurate; newly discovered matters need disclosure and review. The form explains that the commitment is a contract to issue a policy under its terms, not a general title opinion. A good map includes limitations as well as schedules. It does not promise that paying the premium removes a physical easement or insures every excluded risk.

## T09U11 — Lender Closing Costs

Distinguish charges for credit, third-party services, prepaid expenses, escrow reserves, government recording, and credits. A consumer can compare lender-controlled costs while also evaluating services and estimated future expenses. A “no closing cost” offer may use lender credits funded through pricing or finance costs into the loan; it does not mean every service is free.

Check charges paid before closing and charges paid by another party. Avoid double counting and test whether credits meet program restrictions. A lower cash-to-close amount can result from a larger loan rather than lower costs. The Closing Disclosure should allow the consumer to see who pays and how cash is calculated.

### Applied exercise: A65 — Cost comparison

Offer A has $3,200 lender costs and no credit. Offer B has $3,700 lender costs and a $1,500 lender credit but a higher rate. Calculate net lender costs and identify the additional calculation needed to compare a planned five-year holding period.

For a simplified equal-balance comparison, assume Offer B's payment is $28 higher each month. Compute the extra payments over 24 and 60 months and offset them against B's $1,000 upfront saving. Draft a two-horizon explanation. Then remove the equal-balance assumption and name the loan details needed for a complete five-year comparison.

Feedback: A $3,200; B $2,200. Compare periodic payments, remaining balances, and expected exit over the same period. Upfront savings alone do not settle the decision.

Additional feedback: At 24 months the extra payments are $672, leaving B ahead by $328 under the assumptions. At 60 months they are $1,680, making B $680 more costly. Break-even is about 35.71 months. Actual amortization, financed fees, payoff costs, and the likelihood of the planned exit must be examined before making a recommendation.

## T09U12 — Case Study/Workshop

Assemble a complete hypothetical file: price $380,000; appraised value $375,000; proposed first loan $300,000; subordinate assistance $15,000 with $0 periodic payment and repayment at sale; verified monthly income $8,000; first-loan P&I supplied as $1,896.20; taxes $500 monthly; insurance $200; association dues $80; other debt $680; closing costs $8,500; lender credit $1,000; seller credit $3,000; deposit $7,500. A $9,000 unexplained bank deposit appears shortly before closing. Title requires release of the seller's old lien.

First LTV on the lower value basis is 80%; combined outstanding-balance LTV is 84%. Price contribution is $65,000 after both loans. Net closing costs are $7,500 after the lender credit. Remaining cash to close is $62,000 after seller and deposit credits. Housing expense is $2,676.20 and DTI is $3,356.20 ÷ $8,000 ≈41.95%. Deferred assistance is still debt even with no periodic payment in this stipulated calculation.

### Applied exercise: A66 — Funding recommendation

Prepare a conditional-status memo with five parts: ratios, cash to close, source-of-funds exception, title requirement, and notice/delivery review. Then assume the $9,000 deposit is an undisclosed personal loan with a $250 monthly payment. Recalculate DTI and explain the required file corrections.

Feedback: corrected DTI is $3,606.20 ÷ $8,000 ≈45.08%. Disclose the debt, document the source, update application/underwriting, resolve title release, and verify disclosures before funding. The exercise does not authorize the agent to make the lender's final approval decision.

### Applied exercise: A81 — One file, reconciled sources and obligations

Analyze an original hypothetical file using the lender's supplied eligible-income amount of $7,650 monthly. Housing expense is $2,480. Known debt payments are auto $390 and revolving $125. The application omits a $210 monthly personal loan. The latest aligned account records show checking $18,000 and savings $42,000; an earlier savings statement showed $52,000 before a $10,000 transfer into checking. A documented earnest deposit of $6,000 has already left these current balances. Required remaining cash to close is $48,500. A separate $5,000 donor transfer has not yet arrived and its eligibility is unverified.

Calculate DTI before and after adding the omitted obligation. Reconcile current verified cash and reserves after closing without double counting the old statement, the transfer, or the earnest deposit. Calculate what reserves would be if the separate gift actually arrives and is accepted, labeling that as conditional. Write an exception memo assigning debt correction, source verification, updated underwriting, and cash-to-close confirmation to the responsible lender/settlement reviewers. Do not count the possible gift as monthly income.

Feedback: before correction, debt is $2,995 and DTI about 39.15%; after correction, debt is $3,205 and DTI about 41.90%. Current funds total $60,000, leaving $11,500 after the supplied remaining cash requirement. If the distinct $5,000 gift arrives and is eligible, reserves would be $16,500. Adding the old savings balance or subtracting earnest money a second time would distort the ledger. The memo must preserve the conditional status of the gift and require correction of the undisclosed loan.

### Applied exercise: A82 — Collateral and title decision packet

An original hypothetical purchase has a $410,000 price and a $400,000 appraisal made subject to completion of identified repairs. The lender's stipulated maximum loan is 85% of the lower price/value basis. Three comparable indications in the report are $398,000, $405,000, and $402,000; no mechanical averaging rule is supplied. The title commitment names the buyer correctly but describes Lot 42 while the contract and survey describe Lot 24. Schedule C requires release of a $220,000 seller loan; Schedule B contains an easement crossing the proposed garage location. The seller offers a receipt for repairs but no lender-accepted completion evidence.

Calculate the maximum loan and price contribution before costs. Prepare separate appraisal, repair, title-description, prior-lien, and intended-use exception entries. Explain why calculating the comparables' average would not resolve the title mismatch or automatically replace the appraiser's conclusion. Draft an evidence request and identify the lender, appraiser-review channel, title professional, surveyor, and counsel roles as appropriate. State the contract-deadline issue without assuming a right to extend or terminate.

Feedback: maximum loan is $340,000 and price contribution $70,000 before costs. The value opinion's repair condition requires lender-accepted resolution; a receipt alone may be insufficient. The lot mismatch, lien release, and easement effect are independent issues. Comparable indications require supported reconciliation, not an agent-directed target. A correct packet separates facts, missing evidence, decision makers, and time-sensitive contract questions rather than declaring “approved because appraisal exceeds the loan.”
