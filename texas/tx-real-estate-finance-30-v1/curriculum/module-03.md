# Module 03: Additional Government Influence

REF-0 topic T03 suggests 200 minutes. You will identify regulatory responsibilities, compare assistance structures, and examine the Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure. A completed disclosure informs a consumer; it does not itself approve a loan or eliminate a lender's other duties.

## T03U1 — U.S. Dept. of Housing & Urban Development (HUD)

HUD's work includes housing programs, FHA mortgage insurance, housing counseling, and fair-housing enforcement. FHA is part of HUD. FHA insurance protects approved lenders against specified losses on eligible loans; the homeowner still owes the debt. HUD is not the retail creditor on an ordinary lender-originated FHA purchase mortgage. Program insurance, a housing grant, rental assistance, and a loan guarantee are different mechanisms and should not be advertised as interchangeable benefits.

Fair housing reaches residential financing as well as sales and rentals. Credit decisions must use lawful, consistently applied criteria. An agent should offer financing information without steering households toward or away from lenders or neighborhoods because of a protected characteristic. A statement that a particular neighborhood is “not appropriate for your family” cannot be justified as financing advice. Ask about the consumer's stated financial objectives and refer questions to qualified professionals using consistent practices.

Housing counseling can help a consumer prepare a budget, understand purchase obligations, or respond to mortgage distress. It is not a promise of eligibility or a substitute for legal representation when a foreclosure deadline is near. Verify that an organization is HUD-approved through HUD's own resources before presenting that status as a credential.

An effective referral to a counselor begins with the consumer's problem. A first-time buyer may need to compare a proposed payment with a household budget; an existing owner may need help organizing delinquency records; a consumer facing discrimination may need a complaint channel. Those are different assignments. The agent should explain what the referred organization actually does, verify contact information independently, and avoid representing counseling as an approval shortcut. Preserve the consumer's control over sensitive information: an introduction does not authorize sending an entire loan application, tax return, or credit report without an appropriate lawful process.

Distinguish access from suitability. A program designed to broaden access can still leave a household exposed to maintenance costs, property-tax changes, insurance deductibles, and income interruptions. An agent who discusses those costs is not undermining the program. The agent is helping the household understand ownership while allowing the lender and program administrator to apply their standards. Likewise, a property satisfying an insurance program's requirements is not certified to meet every buyer preference or free of defects. The financing decision, property inspection, insurance decision, and purchase decision remain separate.

### Applied exercise: A11 — Identify the government role

Classify these events: an approved lender funds an FHA loan; an eligible household obtains assistance under a local program; a counselor helps prepare a budget; a consumer reports discriminatory loan treatment. Write who should handle each next step and explain which event guarantees the household can make future payments.

Continue with an exception report. The counselor supplies a completed budget, the lender still needs an appraisal repair verification, and a local assistance administrator has not reserved funds. For each document, identify what it establishes and what it does not. Allocate the next action to the household, lender, or program administrator. Draft a client update that does not describe counseling completion as loan approval or unreserved assistance as available cash.

Feedback: lender funding, program administration, counseling, and fair-housing enforcement are distinct. None guarantees future repayment. FHA insurance does not pay every borrower's monthly bill or make the property defect-free.

Additional feedback: The budget supports planning, not credit approval. The lender must resolve the property condition; the administrator must establish actual assistance availability and conditions. The household should preserve an alternative funding plan and avoid assuming the closing date is secure from these documents alone.

## T03U2 — Significant Federal Legislation (CRA, TILA, RESPA, SAFE, APA, Dodd-Frank)

The Community Reinvestment Act encourages covered banks to help meet community credit needs, including low- and moderate-income communities, consistent with safe and sound operations. It does not instruct banks to approve every applicant or disregard repayment capacity. The Truth in Lending Act, implemented through Regulation Z, standardizes credit-cost disclosures and imposes substantive protections for covered credit. The Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act, implemented through Regulation X, addresses settlement practices and servicing protections for covered mortgage loans.

RESPA section 8 and Regulation X §1024.14 prohibit giving or accepting a thing of value under an agreement or understanding for referrals of settlement-service business involving a federally related mortgage loan. Payments for actual services can be lawful under the rule's conditions; calling a payment “marketing” does not establish that services were performed or that the payment is permitted. An agent offered $250 per closed loan for sending borrowers should recognize the referral-payment problem and seek compliance review before accepting it. The consumer's consent does not automatically legalize a prohibited kickback.

The SAFE Act establishes a framework for mortgage loan originator licensing and registration. Whether a person needs a state license, federal registration, or falls within an exemption depends on the person's activities and employer. A real estate license alone does not resolve that question. Seller financing also requires analysis: Regulation Z has specific seller-financer exclusions from the loan-originator definition with different conditions, and state licensing rules remain relevant. “Only one transaction” is not a universal exemption from all consumer-credit law.

Dodd-Frank changed mortgage regulation, including ability-to-repay and loan-originator standards. For a covered transaction, Regulation Z §1026.43 generally requires a reasonable, good-faith repayment determination using verified information and the factors specified by the rule. The rule addresses income/assets, employment when relied on, mortgage payments, simultaneous loans, mortgage-related obligations, current debts, debt-to-income or residual income, and credit history. A qualified mortgage is a defined regulatory category with conditions and legal consequences; it is not a government guarantee that a borrower will never default. Do not teach an obsolete universal DTI cutoff as the current definition of every qualified mortgage.

ECOA and Regulation B prohibit discrimination in credit and establish application and notice rules. Covered bases include race, color, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age subject to the statute's conditions, public-assistance income, and good-faith exercise of Consumer Credit Protection Act rights. A lender may evaluate whether income is reliable and likely to continue using lawful standards; it cannot reject an application simply because the income comes from public assistance. Regulation B §1002.9 generally requires action notification within 30 days after a completed application, with rules for incomplete applications and other situations. Specific reasons or the applicable right to request them must be provided as required; “you did not qualify” is not automatically an adequate explanation.

REF-0 prints “APA” without defining it. This module addresses the Administrative Procedure Act's rulemaking framework as the ordinary federal-law meaning of that abbreviation, while preserving the form's ambiguity in the source ledger. An agency proposal, adopted final rule, effective date, compliance date, and court order are different events. Publication of a proposed rule does not alone change today's borrower duties. Always verify the operative source and relevant implementation date; route unresolved legal questions to counsel or the responsible regulator.

Use a coverage-first method when a transaction raises several statutes. Identify the property, purpose of credit, parties, lien type, occupancy, and activity at issue. Then identify the relevant rule and exceptions. A residential-looking property does not prove that every loan secured by it has the same consumer purpose. A loan described as “private” does not prove that no consumer law applies. A real estate agent should recognize these questions and route them for qualified review rather than invent a categorical exemption. Different rules can define application, business day, creditor, and loan originator differently for their own purposes.

The sequence matters in a mixed problem. Suppose a borrower receives a timely disclosure, but the file contains a fabricated employment letter and a prohibited referral payment. Accurate disclosure has not made the false document true or converted the payment into lawful compensation. Conversely, a sound ability-to-repay analysis does not excuse a missed notice or discriminatory treatment. A useful issue list has separate entries for disclosure, substantive credit standards, fair lending, compensation, licensing, and record integrity. Assign an owner to each unresolved issue and record the evidence required for resolution.

Consider a lender requesting a statement about overtime. The lender may need reliable evidence of income it intends to use. The agent should not write that overtime is guaranteed because the buyer hopes it will continue. A proper file identifies the actual pay history and employer evidence for the lender's evaluation. The same discipline applies to public-assistance income: using lawful reliability and continuance criteria is different from excluding the source because of a stereotype. Standardized requests help reveal inconsistent treatment, but a checklist is not a defense when its criteria themselves violate the applicable rule.

Marketing arrangements require the same fact discipline. A brochure may have measurable design, printing, distribution, and advertising services. That does not establish that any amount a lender pays is reasonable compensation for those services. A payment tied to closings or referral volume raises different questions from a documented fee for actual work. Preserve deliverables, the payment basis, and the agreement for compliance review. Avoid advising that removing the word “referral” from an invoice changes the underlying economic arrangement. Substance and the applicable exception matter more than the label.

### Statute reading exercise: S02 — Referral payments

Feedback: TILA/Regulation Z, RESPA/Regulation X, ECOA/Regulation B, SAFE/state licensing, and ability-to-repay analysis address different conduct. ### Statute reading exercise: S12 — Reasons and incomplete applications

Read [Regulation B §1002.9(a), (b)(2), and (c)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/9/). Prepare a notice decision sheet for three hypothetical events: a completed application is denied because of documented delinquent credit obligations; an application lacks a consumer-supplied document and the creditor follows the incomplete-application procedure; an employee sends only “our standards were not met.” Identify the section to consult, the relevant timing trigger, and the missing content. The credit score's contributing factors need not be the actual reasons the creditor denied the application. Your sheet should separate these duties and direct any ambiguous file to the creditor's compliance reviewer, not rewrite a notice in the agent's own name.

## T03U3 — State & Local Programs (incl. TDHCA)

TDHCA's Texas Homebuyer Program offers mortgage and down-payment/closing-cost assistance through participating lenders. Its current program information distinguishes My First Texas Home and My Choice Texas Home. A first-time-buyer label, veteran provision, geographic requirement, income limit, purchase-price limit, education requirement, or funding reservation may matter. Verify the exact program and current terms before stating eligibility. Available program funds and a lender's ability to reserve them are not permanent facts.

Assistance may be a grant, repayable second lien, deferred-payment loan, or forgivable obligation conditioned on events such as continued ownership or occupancy. “No monthly payment” does not mean “no debt.” A deferred second can become due when the property is sold, refinanced, transferred, or no longer occupied according to its documents. Assistance can reduce immediate cash need while adding a future obligation or affecting refinance options.

Compare total economics, not just the assistance amount. A hypothetical $10,000 deferred second paired with a higher first-loan rate can have different costs from a smaller grant paired with a lower rate. Read repayment triggers, forgiveness schedule, allowed combinations, fees, and first-mortgage terms. Texas Veterans Land Board programs are state programs and should not be confused with the federal VA guarantee. City and county assistance can impose separate rules, including compatibility limits.

An assistance comparison should show both a closing ledger and an exit ledger. The closing ledger identifies funds available today, who supplies them, and whether the first lender accepts the source. The exit ledger identifies obligations surviving closing and the events that make them payable. A program can have no scheduled monthly payment and still materially reduce the owner's proceeds on a sale. If a forgiveness schedule is conditional, show the conditions rather than assuming the maximum forgiveness at the outset. The lender also needs the correct subordinate-financing terms in its underwriting and closing package.

Reservation and eligibility are separate milestones. A program webpage describes a product; a lender's reservation concerns funding for an identified file; underwriting determines whether the actual borrower and property satisfy applicable standards. One document may expire or require updating while another remains valid. Before a contract deadline, obtain a status statement specifying which milestones are complete and which are outstanding. If income, occupancy, household composition, price, or first-loan terms change, ask the administrator whether the change affects the assistance. Do not treat an earlier eligibility estimate as a permanent entitlement.

### Applied exercise: A13 — Assistance comparison

Offer A supplies a hypothetical $9,000 deferred repayable second due at sale, with no periodic payment. Offer B supplies a $6,000 grant with no repayment obligation under the stated example facts. Assume identical first-loan terms and fees. Calculate immediate cash saved and debt remaining at a sale three years later under each option. List the documents needed before treating either offer as available.

Feedback: A saves $9,000 now but leaves $9,000 due later; B saves $6,000 and leaves no assistance debt under the stipulated facts. Actual program documents, eligibility determination, funding reservation, and lender approval are necessary. Do not convert a conditional forgiveness program into an unconditional grant.

## T03U4 — Agricultural Lending

Agricultural credit evaluates an operating business as well as land. Revenue can depend on seasonal production, commodity prices, weather, input costs, water access, leases, and management. Repayment schedules may align with production cycles instead of a household's monthly wages. A loan to buy farmland differs from a line financing seed, feed, inventory, or equipment; the term and collateral should fit the use of proceeds and cash-flow cycle.

USDA's Farm Service Agency offers direct and guaranteed farm ownership and operating programs for eligible farmers and ranchers. A direct loan is made by FSA; a guaranteed loan is made by an approved lender with federal backing subject to program terms. Farmer Mac serves the secondary market for eligible agricultural and rural credit. Neither fact means every acreage purchase qualifies as a farm loan. A residence on rural land may instead be evaluated under an eligible rural-housing program, whose borrower, occupancy, property, and income rules differ.

Seasonality changes the meaning of an annual surplus. An operation may receive most sales proceeds in autumn but pay input costs months earlier. It can show positive annual cash flow while lacking cash for spring planting or a midsummer installment. A working-capital line bridges timing; it does not solve a recurring annual loss. Match loan proceeds to their purpose and distinguish an operating cycle from a long-lived land investment. Repeatedly refinancing ordinary operating losses into land debt may hide deterioration by extending the repayment period rather than repairing the business economics.

A stress analysis should name correlated risks. Drought may lower crop revenue while increasing feed or irrigation expense. Falling commodity prices can reduce receipts without reducing an existing debt installment. A property appraisal may incorporate land characteristics that do not translate into immediate operating cash. Evidence requests can include production records, leases, water access, insurance, inventory, projected timing of receipts, and existing obligations. These are finance questions, not a license to give agronomic or legal advice. Refer specialized property-right, environmental, tax, and production issues to the appropriate professionals.

### Applied exercise: A14 — Farm cash flow

A hypothetical operation expects $240,000 annual receipts, $158,000 operating cash expenses, $32,000 household withdrawals, and $38,000 annual debt payments. Calculate cash available after operating expenses and withdrawals, then the remaining cushion after debt service. Reduce receipts by 15% with other figures unchanged. Explain what the lender needs beyond acreage value.

Feedback: $50,000 is initially available for debt service, leaving $12,000. Reduced receipts are $204,000; available cash is $14,000, creating a $24,000 debt-service shortfall. Production history, prices, expenses, other obligations, reserves, insurance, and management evidence affect the analysis. Land value alone does not produce the cash needed on a payment date.

## T03U5 — Review of Loan Estimate

The Loan Estimate presents key terms and estimated costs for covered transactions. Start with the applicant, property, purpose, product, loan type, amount, term, rate, and rate-lock information. Page one identifies whether the amount, rate, or monthly principal and interest can change, and highlights prepayment penalties and balloon payments where applicable. Projected payments can include mortgage insurance and estimated escrow; taxes or other charges outside escrow remain real obligations.

Page two separates loan costs from other costs and shows lender credits. Origination charges, services the consumer cannot shop for, and services the consumer can shop for deserve separate attention. Prepaid interest and initial escrow deposits are not the same as an origination fee. Cash to close combines down payment and closing costs with financed amounts, deposits, seller credits, and other adjustments. Page three supplies comparisons and other considerations, including APR and total interest percentage. APR incorporates covered finance charges and is not the note rate or a simple sum of fees divided by one year's payments.

For covered transactions, §1026.19(e) generally requires delivery or mailing within three business days after application and generally at least seven business days before consummation. For this purpose, the six application elements are name, income, Social Security number to obtain a credit report, property address, estimated property value, and mortgage amount sought. A creditor cannot delay the Loan Estimate by demanding verification documents after receiving these elements. Before receipt and intent to proceed, fees generally cannot be imposed except a bona fide, reasonable credit-report fee. Different business-day definitions apply to different requirements; have the lender calculate the actual deadline.

A Loan Estimate is not a commitment to lend. Changed circumstances and other specified events may permit revised estimates under the rule's timing and good-faith provisions. Do not promise that every number can never change or that a lender may change any number merely by issuing a new document.

Compare offers in layers. First align principal, loan purpose, term, product, lock period, occupancy, and estimated closing date. Then compare the note rate and points or lender credits. Next compare lender-controlled charges and required services, noting which estimates depend on a consumer's selection. Finally compare taxes, insurance, prepaids, and initial escrow on consistent assumptions. A lender showing a lower insurance estimate has not necessarily offered cheaper credit. A higher lender credit may accompany a higher rate. A longer term may lower payment while leaving more debt at a planned sale. Ask each lender to explain the actual tradeoff rather than choosing the smallest cash-to-close number in isolation.

Do not treat the federal good-faith framework as one blanket percentage applying to every closing charge. Regulation Z separates categories and specifies when a revised estimate can be used. Some permitted-shopping charges and recording fees can be evaluated as an aggregate category under specified conditions. Certain other estimates, such as prepaid interest and property insurance, operate under a best-information standard. The creditor's or affiliate's role and the consumer's provider selection can matter. When a charge changes, ask for the original classification, the reason, any permissible revision, and the required timing. The lender or compliance professional should determine any cure.

A comparison can also separate nonrecoverable costs from money collected for future obligations. An escrow deposit remains part of cash needed at closing, but it serves a different function from a charge for originating the loan. A prepaid premium buys coverage for a stated period. Prepaid interest covers a specified interval. Removing these rows from the cash ledger because they are not origination profit would understate cash required. Counting every escrow dollar as an incremental permanent financing fee could overstate the economic cost of one alternative. Use two clearly labeled analyses instead of forcing one total to answer both questions.

## T03U6 — Review of Closing Statement

REF-0 calls this unit “Closing Statement.” For a covered modern mortgage, the principal federal closing form is the Closing Disclosure. Some transactions use other statements, including HUD-1 where applicable; the form must match the transaction. A settlement statement that allocates debits and credits does not substitute for every required federal disclosure.

The Closing Disclosure reports final terms and costs. Compare its loan terms, projected payments, closing costs, and cash to close with the latest Loan Estimate and contract. Inspect who pays each charge, whether an item was paid before closing, and whether credits are shown once. Review the transaction summaries, loan disclosures, calculations, and contact information. A seller credit cannot be assumed to cover any charge regardless of loan-program restrictions.

Under §1026.19(f), the consumer generally must receive the Closing Disclosure at least three business days before consummation. A new waiting period is required for the specified changes: an inaccurate APR requiring correction under the rule, a loan-product change, or addition of a prepayment penalty. Many other changes can be corrected without restarting that three-day period, but the creditor must still deliver accurate required disclosures. Consummation is when the consumer becomes contractually obligated under state law, not a synonym to apply indiscriminately to signing, funding, recording, and possession.

Wire instructions deserve an independent verification process. Use a previously verified contact number, not a phone number embedded in a last-minute change email. A correct Closing Disclosure does not authenticate a fraudulent email. If figures or instructions conflict, pause the transfer and contact the responsible lender or settlement professional through a trusted channel.

A disciplined closing review uses a changes register. For every changed row, identify the prior amount, final amount, payer, paid-at or paid-before-closing status, reason, supporting document, and cash effect. Keep legal disclosure consequences in a separate column. A small dollar change can coexist with a significant product change; a large paid-before-closing item can have no additional effect on the wire still needed. The size of the cash difference is not the rule for deciding whether a new waiting period applies.

Trace every credit to its origin. Earnest money is the buyer's money already delivered, not a discount from the lender. A seller credit is governed by the contract and applicable financing limits. A lender credit is part of the loan pricing or agreed cost treatment. A tax proration allocates an obligation between parties under the transaction's rules and assumptions. An unexplained credit can conceal an incorrect ledger or an undisclosed agreement. If the final amount is lower than expected, investigate the reason with the same care used for an increase; favorable arithmetic does not make an inaccurate document acceptable.

A timeline should use defined events rather than the ambiguous phrase “closing day.” The contract may address possession and performance deadlines; the lender tracks consummation and its disclosure duties; the settlement professional tracks signing, receipt of funds, disbursement, and recording under applicable instructions. These can happen on different dates. If the buyer wants keys before an unresolved funding condition clears, the agent should not imply that a signed Closing Disclosure proves money has been disbursed. Confirm the actual status and obtain appropriate advice about any proposed possession arrangement.

Keep the lender's timing determination in writing when material terms change. The agent can supply the revised contract information and coordinate scheduling, but cannot waive a federal waiting requirement by promising that everyone agrees to move faster. A deadline that creates inconvenience is not automatically the rule's specified emergency. Ask the creditor to identify the applicable provision and required process. This protects the accuracy of the transaction record without asking an agent to practice law or make the lender's compliance decision.

### Applied exercise: A16 — Closing change analysis

Continue A15. The final appraisal charge rises $100; the lender adds a $100 credit; the seller credit remains $2,500; a $400 survey was paid before closing and is newly shown among costs and paid-before-closing amounts. Calculate the effect on cash still due at closing, assuming the survey entry is properly offset and no other changes. Separately classify an added prepayment penalty and a corrected seller mailing address for waiting-period review.

Feedback: the appraisal increase and added lender credit offset. The survey adds $400 to total costs but is offset as already paid, so remaining cash stays $32,900. An added prepayment penalty is one of the specified restart events. A mailing-address correction alone is not one of those three events. The lender remains responsible for applying the rule to the actual file.

### Applied exercise: A73 — Closing exception register

Begin with an agreed remaining cash-to-close figure of $28,600. Review six proposed changes, all hypothetical and otherwise independent: a $300 appraisal increase offset by a new $300 lender credit; $1,200 additional initial escrow with no offset; a $500 inspection already paid by the buyer, newly shown in both total costs and paid-before-closing; a $700 reduction in a seller credit; an added prepayment penalty; and replacement of a fixed-rate product with an adjustable-rate product. The lender has not yet delivered corrected documents. The buyer also receives a changed-wire email.

Create a six-row changes register, compute the revised cash requirement from the supplied monetary changes, and identify the two nonmonetary product/protection changes requiring specific waiting-period review. Write a message to the lender requesting accurate corrected disclosures and its timing determination. Write a separate message to the settlement contact asking for authenticated wire confirmation through a previously trusted channel. Explain why an agent cannot cure the legal timing issue merely by collecting the buyer's signature on a generic waiver.

Feedback: appraisal and lender credit offset; the already-paid inspection has no additional remaining-cash effect; escrow adds $1,200 and the lost seller credit adds $700. Revised cash is $30,500 before any further changes. Addition of a prepayment penalty and a loan-product change are specified new-period events under §1026.19(f)(2)(ii). A generic convenience waiver is not the rule's emergency procedure. Correct review distinguishes accounting, disclosure, contractual obligations, and authentication rather than describing all concerns as a single “closing delay.”
