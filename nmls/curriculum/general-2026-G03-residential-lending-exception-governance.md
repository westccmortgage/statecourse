## Purpose and non-duplication

“Exception approved” is meaningful only if the approver can identify what rule is being excepted, what authority permits a departure, which facts support it and what obligations remain. A senior title, a signed consumer request or a low-risk loan does not answer those questions. The professional skill is to separate an authorized policy decision from an attempt to cancel law through paperwork.

This lesson applies that distinction to new residential-lending cases: a tightly defined internal reserve-policy request; legally required versus optional escrow; escrow-cushion limits and cancellation conditions; financing a credit-insurance premium; and proposed contractual arbitration or waiver terms. It does not repeat E01's matched discrimination cases, E02's appraisal-coercion/referral investigations, G01's file handoffs, G02's multi-creditor closing and rescission exercises, or N02's non-QM repayment calculations. The core obligations remain applicable, but their already-authored instruction does not earn a second allocation here.

Assume consumer-purpose residential mortgage credit. The escrow case expressly stipulates a higher-priced first-lien mortgage on the consumer's principal dwelling subject to §1026.35(b)(1), with no applicable exemption. The escrow-account-analysis example also stipulates a federally related mortgage loan subject to Regulation X §1024.17. The credit-insurance and contract cases concern covered closed-end dwelling-secured consumer credit. Do not carry these conclusions into business credit, an unrelated insurance product, every investor program or every state without analyzing coverage.

NMLS recognizes CE electives, but that recognition is not approval of this manuscript or its category allocation. G03 is intended only where the eventual approved course architecture and a learner's agency requirements actually call for it. [S1]

### Learning objectives

The learner should be able to identify the source and limits of exception authority; distinguish an incomplete request from an impermissible request; test required escrow and cancellation conditions; compute a cushion limit without confusing it with the full initial deposit; explain why borrower consent does not permit prohibited credit-insurance financing or mandatory arbitration terms; and write a decision that preserves remaining conditions rather than falsely declaring the entire loan approved.

## 1. G03-U01 — Name the authority before deciding the request

An exception log often records a reason such as “strong file,” “good customer” or “manager approval.” None identifies the controlling requirement. Before discussing the merits, state what the requester wants the institution to do differently and which source created the existing restriction.

There are at least four distinct sources. A federal or state legal requirement may prescribe a prohibition, a minimum or a conditioned exception. A contract or program agreement may restrict the loan or authorize a specific waiver process. An investor may impose delivery conditions without creating a universal federal rule. An institution may set an internal threshold and delegate limited authority to depart from it. These sources can apply simultaneously. Satisfying one does not remove the others.

An effective first sentence is precise: “This request asks the named policy authority to accept a $1,000 reserve shortfall under fictional policy RP-3; it does not request departure from a statute, investor restriction or repayment determination.” That sentence gives a reviewer something to test. “All exceptions approved” does not.

### Case A: a genuinely internal reserve threshold

Pinehaven Mortgage's fictional RP-3 policy requires three months of the policy-defined housing payment to remain in eligible liquid reserves after closing. It delegates authority to the credit-policy chair to approve a reserve shortfall of up to $1,200, with an independently documented evaluation of the policy's specified compensating factors. For this exercise, legal and product review confirms that this is solely Pinehaven's additional internal requirement; no law, insurer, investor or contract separately requires this reserve amount. The chair cannot change evidence, waive law or approve matters outside this delegation.

The applicant, Remy Ortega, has a policy-defined monthly housing payment of $2,400. Three months is $7,200. Supported eligible reserves are $6,200, leaving a **$1,000 shortfall**. The originator may submit a bounded policy request; the requested amount falls within the stated $1,200 delegation. That arithmetic does not require the chair to grant it. The stipulated compensating-factor review still must occur, and the loan still needs every other applicable determination.

The legal requirement to make a reasonable, good-faith ability-to-repay determination on a covered loan is not replaced by Pinehaven's reserve arithmetic. Conversely, this fictional three-month reserve rule is not taught as a federal minimum. An authorized departure from one institution's added screen is not automatically a waiver of federal law. [S6: §1026.43(c)(1); original RP-3 assumptions]

Now change eligible reserves to $5,800. The shortfall is $1,400, outside the stated delegation. The chair's job title does not enlarge the $1,200 limit. Unless the institution's actual governance supplies a lawful further route, the request cannot be approved under RP-3 as described. Do not reduce the housing-payment input or reclassify unavailable funds just to bring the request inside the permitted amount.

### Scope and expiration are part of the decision

A complete fictional decision might approve only a specific reserve amount for identified loan terms and documented assets, subject to other conditions. If the loan amount, payment or available reserves later change, the earlier decision does not automatically cover the new request. An approval can be narrow without being evasive: it identifies what was authorized and what still needs review.

Case control EG-1, designed for this course, asks for six fields: requested departure; exact requirement/version; transaction coverage; delegated decision authority; supporting evidence and reasons; and remaining conditions/review trigger. These fields are an instructional control, not federally prescribed software or a claim that StateCourse has implemented a live exception system.

**Activity:** Compare Remy's $1,000 and $1,400 requests. State which can be considered by the described chair, which cannot, and why neither request is a complete loan-approval decision. Then rewrite “excellent customer” as a statement identifying the policy's actual required evidence without inventing what that evidence shows.

## 2. G03-U02 — Escrow requests do not all use the same rule

### Case B: “The customer accepts the risk”

Tessa Bell's proposed loan is expressly stipulated to be a higher-priced mortgage loan secured by a first lien on her principal dwelling and subject to §1026.35(b)(1). No exemption under paragraph (b)(2) applies. The creditor requires the mortgage-related insurance included in this case. Tessa would prefer to pay property taxes and insurance directly, and signs a statement accepting responsibility for missed payments. The branch manager requests an escrow waiver because Tessa has substantial savings.

Under the stipulated coverage, the creditor may not extend the loan unless the required escrow account is established before consummation. The consumer's preference and financial capacity do not supply an exemption absent from the rule. The branch manager cannot transform a covered mandatory escrow account into an optional one by accepting a signed risk statement. [S2: §1026.35(b)(1)]

The correct conclusion is limited: this **proposed no-escrow structure** does not satisfy the stipulated rule. It is not necessarily a denial of every loan Tessa might lawfully obtain. Staff may explain the required escrow or investigate another genuinely eligible structure through authorized channels, without misclassifying the current product to evade its rule. The consumer should not be promised that changing a pricing field will necessarily remove every escrow requirement.

Paragraph (b)(2) has specific transaction and creditor exemptions. Their existence matters, but an exemption requires the actual facts and every applicable condition. This lesson does not use an outdated small-creditor asset number, assume every rural loan is exempt or infer qualification from a lender's marketing name. Conversely, a loan outside §1026.35(b)(1) can still require escrow under another law, program or agreement. [S2: §1026.35(b)(2), comment 35(b)(1)-3]

### Case C: cancellation of an existing required account

An existing account is different from a pre-consummation waiver. Ellis Chen requests cancellation on September 10, 2026 for an otherwise qualifying escrow account required by §1026.35(b)(1). The loan was consummated September 10, 2020, remains outstanding and the consumer is current, not delinquent or in default. The creditor/servicer has no reason to know of another subordinate lien beyond the one specified below.

The general rule permits cancellation upon termination of the underlying debt, or receipt of a consumer request no earlier than five years after consummation, subject to the additional conditions for request-based cancellation. Those conditions include unpaid principal below eighty percent of the property's original value and the consumer not currently being delinquent or in default. These are minimum restrictions, not a promise that every agreement must allow cancellation at the first possible moment. [S2: §1026.35(b)(3), comments -1/-2]

“Original value” for this test is the lesser of the original contract sales price, if any, and the appraised value at consummation. It is not today's optimistic estimate. In the less-than-eighty-percent determination, the creditor or servicer must count any subordinate lien of which it **has reason to know**; the standard is not limited to liens it has already confirmed through actual knowledge. [S2: comment 35(b)(3)-3]

The same official interpretation permits a specific evidentiary route: if the consumer certifies in writing that the equity in the property securing the debt is unencumbered by a subordinate lien, the creditor or servicer may rely on that certification unless it has **actual knowledge to the contrary**. Do not rewrite this permission as a universal requirement for a new title search, or claim that the certification may be relied on despite actual contrary knowledge. In Ellis's original example below, the creditor/servicer actually knows of the stated subordinate balance; a contradictory no-subordinate-lien certification would not erase it. [S2: comment 35(b)(3)-3]

Ellis's original purchase price was $320,000 and original appraised value $330,000. Original value is therefore $320,000; eighty percent is $256,000. The first-loan unpaid principal is $237,000 and a known subordinate lien has $19,000 outstanding. For the stated test, $237,000 + $19,000 = $256,000, exactly eighty percent. The rule requires **less than** eighty percent; equality does not pass.

Now suppose verified subordinate debt is reduced to $18,000 with all other facts unchanged. The combined amount is $255,000, or **79.6875%** of $320,000, which passes this one balance condition. More than five years have elapsed and current status is stipulated. The creditor/servicer can now complete the remaining legal and agreement analysis; this lesson still does not command immediate cancellation or erase applicable notices, program requirements or servicing procedures.

### Test the condition, not an anecdote

A colleague says another customer had escrow removed after two years. That history does not establish that the other loan was covered by this rule, that the action complied with it, or that Ellis has the same rights. Another colleague says the new appraisal values Ellis's home at $400,000. That would make a different ratio look favorable, but it is not the defined original-value denominator for this cancellation test.

The originator should communicate the actual boundary: “This account has specific cancellation conditions. We must establish the original value, evaluate counted liens using the permitted evidence, and check payment status and the governing agreement. A current property estimate alone does not establish eligibility.” A promise that “we waive escrow for every good customer after a year” should not appear in an origination explanation for a loan subject to the five-year restriction.

**Activity:** Prepare a decision for the $19,000 subordinate-lien version. Identify the passed elapsed-time condition and the failed strict balance condition separately. Then add a counterfactual: even if the balance condition passes, current delinquency would independently prevent request-based cancellation under the cited rule. Do not combine independent checks into an averaged risk score.

## 3. G03-U03 — A cushion limit is not a total-deposit limit

### Case D: a request for extra protection

Pinehaven is establishing an escrow account for a federally related mortgage loan. Supported estimated annual disbursements are $7,200, all on an ordinary annual cycle. No shortage-repayment complication exists. The governing mortgage document permits the RESPA maximum, and no applicable law requires a lower amount. The account specialist proposes a $1,800 cushion because the consumer prefers “extra protection.”

Regulation X limits the cushion to no more than one-sixth of estimated annual disbursements. Here $7,200 ÷ 6 = **$1,200**. A $1,800 **required cushion** exceeds that limit by $600. Consumer preference does not give the servicer permission to require an amount beyond the applicable maximum. [S3: §1024.17(c)(1), (c)(5)]

Do not confuse this question with voluntary payments under a separately analyzed lawful arrangement; the case is an amount the servicer proposes to **require** as the cushion. Nor is a maximum always a mandatory charge. The regulation allows a smaller cushion or none; a lower mortgage-document or applicable legal limit governs when required. If the contract here instead limits the cushion to one ordinary **monthly escrow payment**, that limit is $7,200 ÷ 12 = $600, not $1,200. This escrow payment excludes the loan's principal-and-interest payment; the case supplies no shortage or other adjustment. A federal ceiling is not permission to disregard a lower binding limit. [S3: §1024.17(c)(1)(ii), (c)(8), (d)(1)]

### Why the total initial deposit may be different

The specialist responds, “Our closing worksheet needs a $2,700 escrow deposit, so it must be illegal if the limit is $1,200.” That conclusion confuses the cushion with the money needed to pay scheduled disbursements. At establishment, the account analysis determines the base amount needed so that projected balances can cover the relevant disbursements, and the permissible cushion is a separate addition. The rule requires aggregate accounting, not a fixed number of months collected independently for every bill. [S3: §1024.17(c)(1)(i), (c)(2), (c)(4), (d)]

Assume a correct aggregate analysis of the actual schedule supports a $1,500 base initial deposit, before any cushion. Adding a permissible $1,200 cushion gives **$2,700**. On those express assumptions, exceeding $1,200 in total initial deposits does not itself establish an excess cushion. Conversely, writing “base deposit” on an unsupported extra $600 does not make it valid. The calculation must support the allocation.

The two checks have different evidence. To evaluate the cushion, find the annual disbursement estimate and the applicable cap. To evaluate the initial deposit, find the actual timing of expected payments and disbursements, the aggregate trial balance and the permitted cushion. A reviewer who checks only the deposit total can either pass an impermissible cushion or reject a properly calculated initial deposit.

### Known bills are not optional inputs

The rule also addresses estimates. If the servicer knows an escrow item's charge for the next computation year, it must use that amount. If it does not know, the regulation specifies permitted estimation methods, including a preceding-year basis and relevant limits; unassessed new construction has a particular comparable-property approach. The point is not to forecast property taxes optimistically so the loan appears less expensive. [S3: §1024.17(c)(7)]

In Case D, suppose a final known annual insurance premium is $1,650, but an earlier quote was $1,350. An “exception” to keep using the old quote is not an ordinary discretionary reduction of the cushion. It changes a required input contrary to the known-charge rule. The specialist must update the disbursement estimate and rerun the analysis, not ask a manager to bless an obsolete number.

This lesson is not a complete servicing course: annual statements, shortages, deficiencies, refunds, disbursement duties and optional insurance choices have their own requirements. The limited skill is to classify the requested departure accurately. “Lower cushion,” “different estimated bill,” “different initial deposit,” and “cancel the account” are four different requests governed by different conditions.

**Activity:** Write a two-line explanation comparing the impermissible $1,800 required cushion with the expressly supported $2,700 total initial deposit. Then identify what further evidence would be needed if the $1,500 base amount were only a label instead of the result of an actual aggregate analysis.

## 4. G03-U04 — A signature does not legalize every financing term

### Case E: financing an optional credit-life premium

Pinehaven's closed-end consumer mortgage is secured by the consumer's dwelling. The consumer asks for an optional credit-life insurance product. The proposal would add its $1,900 single premium to the mortgage principal at closing. The branch manager signs an “exception” because the consumer understands the cost and prefers no out-of-pocket payment.

Section 1026.36(i) prohibits direct or indirect financing of premiums or fees for the defined credit insurance in covered dwelling-secured consumer transactions. Its interpretation specifically identifies adding a single premium to the amount owed at closing as prohibited. Labeling the premium optional or obtaining consumer consent does not supply an exception. [S4: §1026.36(i)(1), comment 36(i)-1]

This is a rule about financing a defined product in a covered transaction, not a declaration that all insurance is forbidden. The definition includes specified credit-life, disability, unemployment and property coverages and certain debt-cancellation/suspension arrangements, with a particular credit-unemployment exclusion subject to its stated conditions. Homeowners insurance and other products cannot be classified solely by the presence of the word “insurance.” Determine the actual product and legal definition; do not disguise a covered premium as a generic closing cost. [S4: §1026.36(i)(2)(i)]

A lawful alternative, if any, requires actual review of the product, payment arrangement and all applicable law. The originator should not promise eligibility for coverage, give an unlicensed insurance recommendation, or decide that a separate invoice automatically avoids indirect financing. The question is whether the consumer is given a right to defer payment of the covered premium beyond the relevant monthly period, not which department prints the invoice. [S4: §1026.36(i)(2)(ii)]

### Monthly means both calculated and paid monthly

The financing prohibition does not apply to credit insurance whose premiums or fees are calculated and paid in full monthly as specified by the rule. For this provision, calculated monthly means a rate is multiplied by the actual monthly outstanding balance. Calling a product “monthly” because one annual premium is divided into twelve installments does not establish that definition. [S4: §1026.36(i)(1), (2)(iii)]

Consider a stipulated compliant-rate method of 0.00025 times the actual monthly outstanding balance. With a $280,000 actual balance, the current month's premium is $70. With a $279,400 actual balance the following month, it is $69.85. These are arithmetic examples, not a marketed premium, an approved insurance product or an actuarial endorsement. The rule's payment condition also must be satisfied: the monthly premium must actually be paid in full within the applicable monthly arrangement rather than capitalized as unpaid debt beyond its due monthly period.

If the first month's $70 is added to the amount owed after the monthly period closes instead of being paid, the label “monthly-pay insurance” does not cure the financing problem. The interpretation expressly addresses that circumstance. Separately, compliance with this limited financing rule does not establish all insurance-law, compensation, disclosure or consumer-protection compliance. [S4: comment 36(i)-1]

**Activity:** Draft a decision for the $1,900 single-premium proposal. A sufficient answer identifies the covered product and transaction, the proposed financing mechanism, the failed legal boundary and the need for a genuinely permissible alternative. Do not tell the consumer that signing a better disclosure will make the same financed premium lawful.

### Case F: a closing-document rights waiver

For a separate covered closed-end dwelling-secured consumer loan, a manager wants to add a clause requiring arbitration of every future dispute arising from the transaction. The consumer is offered a smaller lender fee for signing. There is no existing dispute. The request is described as a voluntary “documentation exception.”

Section 1026.36(h)(1) prohibits terms in the covered contract or other agreement that require arbitration or another nonjudicial procedure to resolve a controversy or claim arising out of the transaction. The offer of a discount does not make a mandatory term permissible. Section 1026.36(h)(2) also prohibits applying or interpreting the covered agreement to bar a consumer from bringing a court claim concerning an alleged federal-law violation. The rule preserves the ability, after a dispute or claim arises, to agree to settle or use arbitration or another nonjudicial procedure for that dispute. [S5]

The timing and nature of the agreement matter. A post-dispute settlement provision is not the same as forcing all future disputes out of court at origination. Equally, the existence of a post-dispute possibility does not let staff prelabel every loan as already disputed. The institution's legal reviewer should review the actual language and facts; the course does not draft enforceable settlement terms or settle a consumer's claim.

A note saying “borrower was sophisticated and signed voluntarily” does not answer the contractual prohibition. The governance question is whether the institution has authority to require the proposed term at all. A signature can be evidence of agreement; it is not universal permission to impose a prohibited agreement.

**Activity:** Compare a mandatory arbitration clause proposed before any dispute with a genuinely later proposed agreement addressing an existing dispute. State what §1026.36(h) distinguishes without concluding that every post-dispute agreement is automatically enforceable under all laws.

## 5. G03-U05 — Produce a decision that another reviewer can challenge

Consider this queue received by Pinehaven's exception committee:

| Request | Stated basis | Correct initial classification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Remy's $1,000 shortfall under RP-3 | Express internal delegation, eligible evidence still subject to review | Potentially within delegated policy authority; not automatically granted |
| Tessa's no-escrow origination | Consumer accepts payment risk; covered rule has no exemption on the facts | Cannot approve this departure from the stipulated legal requirement |
| Ellis's escrow cancellation at exactly 80% | More than five years elapsed; no delinquency | Another condition passes, but strict balance condition fails |
| Required $1,800 cushion on $7,200 annual disbursements | Consumer prefers more protection | Exceeds the stated cushion ceiling; do not require it |
| Financing the $1,900 credit-life premium | Signed consumer consent | Consent does not authorize the prohibited financing |
| Mandatory future-dispute arbitration at origination | Smaller fee in exchange for signature | Discount does not authorize the prohibited contractual term |

This table is not a collection of interchangeable risk scores. An excellent reserve position cannot compensate for a prohibited financed premium. A small policy shortfall cannot be used to demand waiver of legal rights. A passed five-year condition cannot offset a failed balance condition. Some requirements call for every condition to be true, not a favorable average.

### Write the actual outcome

Under the course's fictional EG-1 control, a decision identifies which requested act is authorized, refused or unresolved. “Approve RP-3 reserve departure of $1,000 on the supported current figures, subject to the named policy evaluation and remaining loan conditions” is narrower than “loan approved.” “Cannot authorize no escrow on the stipulated covered transaction” is narrower than “customer ineligible for all credit.” Precision protects both the consumer and the next employee from a false inference.

For a factually incomplete request, say what is missing rather than forcing a yes/no answer. If the escrow cancellation file has no verified original value or does not resolve which subordinate liens must be counted under comment 35(b)(3)-3, do not assume the favorable ratio. Request relevant evidence using the permitted routes, including the qualified written-certification route described above, and leave an unsupported determination unresolved. An unresolved request is not permission to proceed and is not necessarily a credit denial. Any applicable notice obligation must still be evaluated separately; the committee's internal status does not suspend legal deadlines.

### An independent check must test more than the approver's name

The second reviewer should be able to challenge four things. Was the cited requirement current and applicable? Did the named approver have authority for this exact departure? Do the supplied facts satisfy the actual conditions, including strict boundaries? Was the decision implemented without changing a different term or concealing an unresolved duty?

For Ellis, the reviewer recomputes the original-value denominator, counts subordinate liens of which the creditor/servicer has reason to know subject to the interpretation's permitted certification reliance, and checks less-than rather than less-than-or-equal. For Case D, the reviewer distinguishes the cushion from the base deposit and verifies that the base is supported by an aggregate analysis. For Case E, the reviewer looks at actual financing, not just consent language or a product nickname. These are substantive tests that can produce a refusal; a checkbox verifying the presence of a manager signature cannot perform them.

### Compare decisions without manufacturing uniform outcomes

The committee should review both granted and refused policy requests, not just the loans that closed. Two different outcomes can be justified by different facts or authority, while identical request labels can conceal different legal problems. This is an original governance recommendation, not a claim that federal law prescribes the course's committee or sampling method. E01's nondiscrimination requirements continue to apply; no amount of exception documentation licenses a prohibited basis.

A useful test is to change one fact and ask whether the result changes for the right reason. Increase Remy's shortfall beyond the delegation, and authority changes. Reduce Ellis's verified counted balance below eighty percent, and one legal condition changes. Obtain a consumer's signature on the same prohibited financing plan, and the legal result does not change. This counterfactual method helps distinguish a real condition from a convenient story.

**Final exercise:** Draft three short decisions: a conditionally permissible internal-policy request; a request requiring more evidence; and a prohibited proposal that cannot be cured by managerial consent. Each must name the requested action, controlling source, actual facts, authority, conclusion and remaining duties. Do not use the same generic “approved with exception” stamp on all three.

### G03-Q11 — Known charge
