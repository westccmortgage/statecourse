## 1. Why this belongs in annual ethics

The federal minimum for annual renewal education includes two hours of ethics covering fraud, consumer protection, and fair lending. NMLS also publishes a non-year-specific approved CE topic list covering ethics, fraud, and consumer protection. This provider-selected module emphasizes fair treatment; E02 supplies a distinct fraud/conflict/consumer-protection investigation. These particular titles, cases, and minute allocations are not mandates in the 2026 annual topic list. The mandatory 2026 Federal Law findings are addressed in F01–F03 and must not be counted again as additional Ethics time simply because this lesson mentions a shared rule. [S1, S2]

The practical question is not, “Can I recite a protected-class list?” It is, “Can another competent reviewer reconstruct why two applicants received the same or different help, information, documentation requirements, terms, and decisions?” A professional may sincerely believe that an extra condition protects a customer and still impose it because of an impermissible assumption. Conversely, consistent treatment does not require ignoring an actual difference in repayment evidence. The task is to distinguish a relevant credit fact from a prohibited shortcut and document that distinction without reconstructing a convenient story after a complaint.

By the end, the learner should be able to:

1. Identify less favorable treatment in an origination workflow, including before a final decision.
2. Apply the current Regulation B discouragement rule to a concrete communication, without substituting an outdated formulation.
3. Evaluate protected income individually and distinguish actual evidence from assumptions about age, family, or income source.
4. Distinguish lawful information collection, prohibited use, and voluntary reliance on support income.
5. Separate a joint application or necessary collateral signature from an impermissibly demanded spouse's credit obligation.
6. Compare matched files, identify a legitimate difference or an unsupported exception, and propose a correction that can be tested.
7. Communicate a concern factually without promising approval, erasing records, or declaring a legal violation from statistics alone.

## 2. Establish the legal frame before judging a file

Regulation B prohibits discrimination on a prohibited basis in any aspect of a credit transaction. Its scope is wider than a final approve/deny decision: it reaches the information offered, application process, investigation, evaluation, terms, account administration, and collection. The official interpretation specifically addresses unequal product explanations, extra documentation, and exceptions applied differently to similarly situated applicants. A legitimate, nondiscriminatory factual reason matters; a convenient label that masks the real reason does not. [S3: §1002.4(a), comments 4(a)-1 and -2; S4: §1002.2(m)]

Regulation B's prohibited bases include race, color, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age when the applicant has capacity to contract, receipt of public-assistance income, and good-faith exercise of protected credit-law rights identified by the rule. The rule also addresses associations with other people; it does not permit a lender to make a race-based decision by describing it as a judgment about the applicant's neighbors. Read the defined scope, not a shortened memory aid. [S4: §1002.2(z), comments 2(z)-1 through -3]

The Fair Housing Act separately prohibits discrimination in making available or setting terms of residential real-estate-related transactions because of race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, or disability (the statute uses the term “handicap”). These lists are not identical: marital status is not familial status, and public-assistance receipt is not the same legal category as disability. Mortgage lending can implicate both statutes, and state or local protections may be broader. This lesson does not establish that an unlisted characteristic is lawful to use. [S8: 42 U.S.C. §3605(a)–(b); HUD fair-lending explanation]

For Regulation B's general discrimination and discouragement provisions, “creditor” includes certain regular referral and creditor-selection activities. An originator cannot safely reason that the final lender alone has obligations because “I only routed the customer.” The exact responsibility for another creditor's conduct depends on the definition and the actual involvement and knowledge; do not automatically attribute every participant's violation to everyone in the transaction. [S4: §1002.2(l), comments 2(l)-1 and -2]

### Important September 2026 currency boundary

The April 22, 2026 Regulation B final rule became effective July 21, 2026. It changed discouragement, disparate-impact provisions, and special-purpose credit program provisions. This manuscript uses the current §1002.4(b) and §1002.6(a), not a prior training deck. Current §1002.6(a) states that ECOA does not provide an effects test; its interpretation separately addresses prohibited intentional proxy use. Accordingly, an outcome disparity alone is not taught here as a completed ECOA violation. Investigate why it occurred and assess applicable law. This statement does not resolve Fair Housing Act theories, state-law claims, judicial challenges, or the validity of a particular special-purpose credit program. Those are separate legal-review matters. [S3, S5, S9]

The cases below test disparate treatment and explicit assumptions, not a disputed statistical-liability theory. Legal reviewers must recheck amendments, controlling court orders, and affected jurisdictions immediately before submission and release. “Current agency webpage” is evidence of published agency text, not a representation that every litigation issue has been researched.

## 3. First contact can change access to credit

### Worked case A — A retirement shortcut

Elliot calls Harborline Mortgage about a 30-year purchase loan. He says that he is 67 and retired. Before asking about income, assets, debts, or the requested loan, the originator replies: “At your age, a 30-year application is pointless. We won't approve someone who probably won't work again. Ask your daughter to apply instead.” The firm uses a judgmental underwriting process for this product. No incapacity issue exists.

The problem is not that the originator lacks a completed application or has yet to quote a rate. The statement is directed to a prospective applicant and expressly links likely denial to age and retirement rather than an individualized credit analysis. Current §1002.4(b) prohibits directed oral or written statements that the creditor knows or should know would cause a reasonable person to believe an application would be denied or receive less favorable terms because of a prohibited-basis characteristic. The current commentary itself includes a retirement-related example. [S3: §1002.4(b), comment 4(b)-1.i.A]

Age is not an absolute “never inquire, never consider” category. Regulation B contains limited rules for demonstrably and statistically sound scoring, pertinent elements of creditworthiness in judgmental systems, and favorable treatment of elderly applicants. Those exceptions do not authorize this blanket prediction. Pension and retirement income also cannot be discarded merely because they are retirement benefits. Amount and probable continuance may be considered under the applicable rule using the individual's facts. [S5: §1002.6(b)(2), (5), comments 6(b)(2)-1 through -6 and 6(b)(5)-1]

An appropriate response keeps access open without promising an outcome: “We can review the income and assets you want considered, your obligations, and the proposed transaction. Employment wages are not the only possible source of repayment. I cannot determine eligibility from age alone.” If a product has a legitimate eligibility restriction, staff must confirm its legal basis and applicability; they should not improvise one from a borrower's appearance or assume a relative should become the borrower.

**Learner activity — change the evidence, not the person:** Rewrite the response if Elliot voluntarily reports that his only current benefit ends next month and he has no other income or assets he wishes to rely upon. Identify what can now be investigated. The changed fact permits examination of actual amount and continuance; it does not justify a blanket rule against retirees. The correct response still should not declare that the course has performed a complete underwriting decision.

### Discouragement is not a rule against all targeted encouragement

Current §1002.4(b) and its interpretation distinguish a discriminatory exclusionary message from encouragement directed at a group to apply. A workshop invitation encouraging first-time applicants is not automatically evidence that other people will be refused. This distinction does not create an exception for an advertised discriminatory loan policy or authorize an otherwise unlawful program. For this course, evaluate the actual statement, whom it addresses, the prohibited-basis link, and the reasonable interpretation; do not infer a violation solely because one audience was addressed. [S3: §1002.4(b), comment 4(b)-1]

**Operational recommendation:** Preserve the actual version of a script or message that produced the concern and have the authorized compliance reviewer correct it. Do not merely delete the troublesome sentence and report that the earlier interaction never occurred. Script versioning and a matched review of actual contacts are suggested controls, not a federal mandate to buy a particular software system.

## 4. Same total income does not justify different assumptions

### Worked case B — The unexplained income haircut

Assume these two otherwise matched applicants are applying for the same covered purchase program. The lender considers verified income, the stated amounts meet its lawful evidence and continuance requirements, and no other income, expense, adjustment, or eligibility issue distinguishes the files. The numerical exercise is a comparison tool, not the program's complete approval test.

| Item | Casey | Morgan |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Verified wage income per month | $6,400 | $4,600 |
| Verified retirement benefit per month | $0 | $1,800 |
| Total supported income | $6,400 | $6,400 |
| Stipulated total monthly debt used in comparison | $2,560 | $2,560 |
| Staff's proposed countable income | $6,400 | $4,600 |

The employee says, “We do not count pensions for customers in Morgan's age group; they are not paychecks.” Using the stated debt numerator, Casey's comparison ratio is $2,560 ÷ $6,400 = 40%. Morgan's correctly supported comparison is also 40%. Excluding the benefit solely for that reason gives $2,560 ÷ $4,600 ≈ 55.65%. The manufactured difference could change whether the applicant receives a full product explanation, is asked for another borrower, or is routed away before a fair review.

Section 1002.6(b)(5) prohibits automatic discounting or exclusion because income is part-time, an annuity, a pension, another retirement benefit, or connected to a prohibited basis. The creditor may evaluate amount and probable continuance; it must assess the individual facts. The regulation does not require the use of unsupported income or guarantee approval. Here, however, sufficient evidence and continuance are expressly stipulated, so the staff member's categorical exclusion is not supported. [S5: §1002.6(b)(5), comments 6(b)(5)-1 and -3]

**Why the calculation matters:** A mathematical error can implement an ethical failure. A perfectly calculated 55.65% ratio would still be misleading if its denominator discarded income on an impermissible basis. An audit that checks only the spreadsheet formula would pass the wrong decision. Investigate source selection as well as arithmetic.

**Learner activity:** Write a three-part finding: the specific observed difference, the controlling rule, and the missing or contradicted justification. Then change the facts so that Morgan's benefit is verified to stop in two months without replacement. Explain why the earlier matched comparison no longer resolves treatment, and why an individualized continuance analysis is necessary. Do not change Morgan's age or demand that a family member join the loan as an automatic solution.

### Public assistance, part-time work, and support payments

Public-assistance income cannot be rejected merely because of its source. In an appropriate evaluation, actual eligibility duration and other pertinent factors may matter. Part-time work is not inherently unreliable; equally, the fact that a payment is protected does not make every stated amount verified or permanent. Keep two distinct questions visible: “What information is required and actually available?” and “Why is this amount being accepted, reduced, or excluded?” [S5: §1002.6(b)(2), (5)]

Suppose Arden chooses to rely on child support. Actual receipt history and whether payments are likely to be consistently made may be relevant; a belief that “support payments never last” is not an individualized analysis. A creditor asking whether income comes from alimony, child support, or separate maintenance must give the required disclosure that it need not be revealed if the applicant does not want it considered. Choosing not to disclose such income is not a reason to invent it, compel reliance upon it, or penalize the applicant for that choice. Income the applicant does not ask the creditor to consider need not be added to qualifying income; evaluate the other income and evidence actually relied upon. Necessary questions about obligations to pay support are a different inquiry. [S6: §1002.5(d)(2), comments -1 through -3; S5: §1002.6(b)(5), comment -2]

### Family plans are not underwriting facts

Regulation B prohibits questions about birth-control practices, intentions concerning bearing or rearing children, or capacity to bear children. It allows appropriately neutral inquiries about dependents and dependent-related obligations or expenses. The distinction matters when an employee asks a pregnant applicant to sign a promise that she will return to work, without a factual income issue and without an equivalent process for others. The lender cannot assume that a category of people will have diminished or interrupted future income because they may bear or rear children. Actual, voluntarily supplied changes in income can require individual analysis; a categorical prediction cannot replace that evidence. [S6: §1002.5(d)(3); S5: §1002.6(b)(3)]

**Communication exercise:** Replace “Will you be having any more children during the loan term?” with a permissible inquiry addressing the loan's actual repayment facts. A useful answer describes current and reasonably supported future income and actual obligations without requesting family-planning information. The law does not prescribe this course's exact alternative wording.

### National origin is not an immigration-status finding

An accent or surname does not establish immigration status. Regulation B permits inquiry about permanent residency and immigration status and permits consideration of status and information needed to ascertain repayment rights and remedies. That permission is not a general authorization to use national origin or to infer legal status from appearance. Use the verified facts and the actually applicable rule; route uncertain eligibility, sanctions, or jurisdiction-specific questions to competent review. This lesson does not promise approval to every noncitizen or teach that every immigration-related eligibility distinction is automatically unlawful. [S6: §1002.5(e); S5: §1002.6(b)(7); S4: comment 2(z)-2]

## 5. A required signature needs a defined purpose

### Worked case C — “All married borrowers need a spouse on the note”

Jordan applies individually and independently qualifies for the requested amount and terms. Jordan's spouse, Taylor, owns an interest in the proposed collateral but is not a joint applicant. A documented state-law review establishes that Taylor must sign the security instrument to make the collateral available on default, but Taylor does not need to sign the note or incur personal liability. The employee's checklist nevertheless says “spouse: must co-borrow.”

The individual qualification finding is the starting point. Under §1002.7(d)(1), the creditor generally cannot require a qualifying individual applicant to obtain another person's signature on a credit instrument, subject to the specific exceptions. For secured credit, §1002.7(d)(4) permits signatures necessary, or reasonably believed necessary under applicable state law, to make the offered property available. Its interpretation distinguishes a needed lien signature from unnecessary personal liability. On the stated facts, Taylor's security signature has a legal purpose; the demanded note signature does not. [S7: §1002.7(d)(1), (4), comments 7(d)-1 and 7(d)(4)-1 through -3]

The corrective action is not “never ask a spouse to sign.” It is “identify which instrument, which legal purpose, and which support for necessity.” A state can have different property and signature rules. An integrated document combining note and security agreement requires particular care so that a needed lien signature does not silently create an unnecessary credit obligation. This fictional case stipulates the legal review; it is not a substitute for obtaining that review on an actual property.

### Joint application is a choice, not a retrospective label

A joint financial statement does not itself establish intent to apply jointly. Regulation B's interpretation requires evidence of joint intent at application and distinguishes it from a signature merely verifying information. A signature later placed on a promissory note cannot be used to establish the earlier joint application intent. Do not repair the file by changing Taylor's role to “joint applicant” after the fact solely because the software insists on a second borrower. [S7: §1002.7(d)(1), comments -2 and -3]

If Jordan did not qualify individually and another party's personal liability were needed under the lawful standards, the lender might request a qualified cosigner, guarantor, or similar party. It could not insist that this person be Jordan's spouse. A spouse may voluntarily serve, but marriage is not the prescribed remedy. [S7: §1002.7(d)(5), comments -1 and -2]

**Learner activity:** Prepare a signature-purpose log with four fields: requested signature, applicant's role/intent, the identified legal or credit purpose, and evidence supporting necessity. Complete it for Taylor's security instrument and proposed note. A passing answer permits the supported security signature and rejects the unsupported note requirement; it does not erase Taylor's property interest or fabricate joint intent.

## 6. Integrated matched-file investigation

### Evidence packet — Larkspur Mortgage review LM-26-208

Larkspur reviews two applicants, A and B. Both seek the same $320,000 purchase product on the same pricing day. The files stipulate identical relevant credit scores, supported income, debts, property type, loan-to-value ratio, lock period, documentation completeness, and authorized product eligibility. Both request the full set of options they qualify for. Their race differs and is known to the assigned employee. No special-purpose credit program applies.

The firm's lawful checklist requires one recent statement for the asset account shown. Both provide it. An audit trail shows:

1. A receives the full eligible product comparison and the ordinary checklist.
2. B receives only the highest-cost eligible option and a demand for three additional statements, despite no discrepancy or missing item in B's statement.
3. The employee writes privately: “People from B's group need extra proof; they usually don't understand the better option.”
4. When B asks why the conditions differ, the supervisor replies internally, “Delete that note and call it an investor requirement.” No such investor requirement exists.
5. No final credit decision has been made. The team can still correct the handling, but earlier records and contacts already exist.

### Learner work — complete before reviewing the guide

1. Identify the exact adverse differences. State the evidence of their basis rather than concluding that any two different outcomes are discrimination.
2. Explain why “no final denial” does not resolve the concern.
3. Separate actions to correct B's present treatment from actions to preserve and investigate the earlier handling.
4. Draft a factual customer response that does not guarantee an approval, deny the concern without investigation, or falsely attribute the conditions to an investor.
5. Choose a comparison sample and an independently testable prevention control. Include both files that closed and those that stopped before underwriting.

### Private review guide

**Rule application:** B received a narrower explanation and greater documentation requirements based on an explicit racial assumption, despite stipulated matching legitimate factors. These are forms of treatment identified in comment 4(a)-2. They concern access, information, and investigation even without a final denial. The employee's statement and absent investor rule support the analysis; a mere difference in an aggregate approval rate would not by itself establish these facts. [S3: §1002.4(a), comments -1 and -2; S4: §1002.2(m); S5: §1002.6(a)]

**Immediate action:** Route the concern to the firm's authorized compliance reviewer. Correct the unsupported requirements and provide an accurate opportunity to receive the available comparison under the same lawful standards. Do not issue an automatic approval or waive legitimate requirements as an improvised remedy. Determine and deliver required notices through the established process; this module does not restart or suspend F01's disclosure deadlines by labeling a matter an ethics investigation.

**Evidence:** Preserve the original checklist, product/pricing version, applicant requests, record of what was offered, extra conditions, employee note, supervisor instruction, and corrective actions. Make an accurate new entry identifying the correction; do not substitute it for the original. Follow the applicable record-retention and legal-hold requirements. The specific comparison worksheet and escalation route are recommended controls, not prescribed form names.

**Example response:** “You asked why we requested additional records and showed a narrower set of options. We are reviewing those differences against the applicable requirements. We will explain the verified conditions and the options for which your information supports consideration. We cannot promise an approval, but we will not describe an internal preference as an investor requirement.” This is a fictional communication exercise, not mandatory legal notice text.

**Comparison and prevention:** Review similarly situated applicants served by the same team, including inquiries that did not become completed applications. Compare the information and assistance offered, documentation demands, exceptions, and reasons. Ask whether the control could detect a staff member using a legitimate-sounding field to conceal the same prohibited basis. A control that checks only whether a reason box is filled cannot do this. A pattern warrants further investigation; it is not a substitute for verifying underlying facts and the legal theory.

### A legitimate difference changes the analysis

Change the evidence: B's statement contains a $35,000 deposit whose source is needed under the same lawful policy applied to everyone, while A's statement does not. A request proportionate to resolving that deposit can have a legitimate explanation. It does not authorize a racial remark, unnecessary unrelated conditions, or concealing eligible options. Record the actual difference, the policy's applicability, and the response. Neither automatic identical treatment nor automatic suspicion is the objective; supported, nondiscriminatory treatment is.
