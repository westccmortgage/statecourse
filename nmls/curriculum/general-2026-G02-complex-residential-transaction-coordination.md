## Purpose and boundaries

A purchase with two loans is not simply a first mortgage with another line on a spreadsheet. There can be two credit contracts, two sets of conditions, distinct payment obligations, different disclosure duties and a legal priority relationship between the liens. A refinance can replace one obligation while leaving another untouched. The transaction coordinator must recognize these connections without claiming authority to change another creditor's agreement.

This lesson develops that skill through new purchase and refinance cases. The work is structural: reconcile sources and uses, distinguish assistance from forgiveness, identify whose terms changed, determine which obligation is replaced, and separate creditor approvals from the legal conditions for disbursement. It does not repeat F01's application clocks, F02's fee-tolerance exercises, F03's settlement-charge review, G01's source-document handoffs, or N02's repayment-ratio case. Those obligations remain applicable; their earlier instructional time is not counted again here.

The cases involve consumer-purpose residential credit. Unless a variation says otherwise, a new closed-end first mortgage is subject to applicable Regulation Z and TRID requirements. A subordinate assistance loan is analyzed on its own facts; the retained HELOC in the refinance case is an existing open-end obligation, not a newly originated closed-end loan. Business-purpose financing, reverse mortgages, construction draws, state assistance programs and specialized investor eligibility require separate analysis. No government program is represented as offering the fictional assistance described here.

NMLS recognizes a CE-elective category. That category does not approve this particular content, make these internal blocks freely interchangeable, or establish that every licensee needs three general-elective hours. G02 is intended only for the eventual approved architecture and agency-specific hour allocation. [S1]

### Learning objectives and proposed sequence

The learner will distinguish debts from grants; reconcile a multi-creditor funding change without inventing money; test a claimed partial disclosure exemption; identify payoff, retention and lien-priority decisions in a refinance; and construct a release sequence that respects each applicable legal condition.

## 1. G02-U01 — One purchase, two promises to repay

### Case P: Maren and Devon's acquisition

Maren Sato and Devon Price are purchasing a $500,000 home that will be their principal dwelling. Birchline Lending proposes a $440,000 first mortgage. Oakbridge Assistance proposes a $35,000 subordinate loan. The assistance contract requires no interest or periodic installment, but the principal becomes payable when the property is sold or ceases to be their principal dwelling. It does not forgive the principal. The couple will contribute $40,000 of their own funds. Stipulated transaction costs, including all fees, prepaids and reserves relevant to this exercise, total $15,000. There are no other credits, adjustments or financing sources.

| Uses across the transaction | Amount | Sources across the transaction | Amount |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| Purchase price | $500,000 | First mortgage | $440,000 |
| Stipulated transaction costs | $15,000 | Subordinate assistance loan | $35,000 |
| — | — | Borrower funds, including deposit already paid | $40,000 |
| **Total uses** | **$515,000** | **Total sources** | **$515,000** |

This is a coordination worksheet, not a completed Closing Disclosure. The $40,000 includes a $10,000 earnest-money deposit already paid and properly credited. Thus the remaining borrower contribution, on these limited facts, is $30,000; it is not $40,000 plus the deposit. The settlement statement still must correctly show the actual cash flows and applicable disclosures. The exercise supplies a complete simplified ledger so that no learner must guess at omitted prorations.

Now separate three questions that the balanced total cannot answer. First, have the sources actually been committed on the stated terms? Second, are the obligations and financing structure acceptable under each applicable creditor and program requirement? Third, are the funds and documents available for a lawful closing? Equal arithmetic proves none of those conclusions.

The assistance is debt. No installment today does not mean no repayment obligation tomorrow. Calling it a gift could cause the borrower to misunderstand a future sale, and could cause the first creditor to evaluate an incomplete structure. For a covered first mortgage, the applicable repayment analysis considers specified obligations, including a known simultaneous loan, under the relevant rules. This lesson does not assign a universal monthly payment to a deferred obligation; the actual terms, applicable rule and product criteria must be evaluated. [S2: §1026.43(b)(12), (c)(2)(iv), (c)(6)]

### A loan reduction cannot be solved by relabeling a source

Before closing, Birchline changes its approved first-mortgage amount to $430,000. For this variation, all other stated uses and sources stay the same. Available sources now total $505,000 against $515,000 of uses: a **$10,000 gap**. The coordinator cannot enter a $10,000 additional assistance amount simply because Oakbridge has not yet funded.

There are possible structures to investigate, not automatic solutions: additional verified borrower resources, a separately authorized increase in assistance, a permissible change to transaction costs or price, or a different approved financing arrangement. Each has its own evidence and consequences. A seller credit is not unrestricted cash and cannot be assumed to cover any category or exceed an applicable limit. An undisclosed personal loan is a new obligation, not borrower savings. An increase in the second loan changes that creditor's contract and potentially the first creditor's evaluation.

The professional update identifies both the missing amount and the decision still required: “The first-loan reduction leaves $10,000 unfunded under the current transaction budget. No increased assistance commitment is in hand. We are evaluating permitted alternatives with the relevant creditors and the consumers.” It should not say “the second lender will handle it” or promise closing from an unapproved proposal.

**Activity:** Reconcile the case if Oakbridge later formally approves $45,000 and Birchline accepts that exact revised structure after its required review. Sources again total $515,000, but list three matters that arithmetic still cannot clear: the revised assistance contract/disclosures, the legal lien arrangement, and the funding/release requirements. Your answer must not treat the coordinator's worksheet as either creditor's authorization.

## 2. G02-U02 — An assistance label is not a disclosure exemption

Oakbridge's administrator says, “This is an assistance program, so there are no federal forms.” That statement is too broad. Regulation Z §1026.3(h) provides a **partial** exemption for certain qualifying transactions. Its conditions must all be satisfied. It does not exempt an entire organization or every product the organization offers. [S3]

The required inquiry is more useful than a yes/no checkbox:

1. Is the transaction secured by a subordinate lien?
2. Is its purpose within the listed assistance purposes: home-buyer assistance, rehabilitation, energy efficiency, or foreclosure avoidance/prevention?
3. Does the credit contract require no interest?
4. Does the contract provide one of the specified forgiveness or deferral arrangements? These include qualifying forgiveness subject to specified ownership/occupancy conditions, deferral for at least twenty years, or deferral until sale or until the property ceases to be the consumer's principal dwelling.
5. Are the consumer's costs at consummation confined to the permitted types, with application and housing-counseling fees together **less than one percent** of the amount of credit extended?
6. Are the appropriate compliant disclosures supplied under one of the permitted alternatives?

This list summarizes the operative conditions, not every interpretive detail. Interest and qualifying forgiveness/deferral terms must be in the credit contract, not merely in a brochure. The permitted cost types are recording fees, transfer taxes, a bona fide and reasonable application fee, and a bona fide and reasonable housing-counseling fee. The less-than-one-percent calculation applies to the latter two fee types, not to the recording fees and transfer taxes. Evidence of compliance must be retained under the applicable record rule. [S3: §1026.3(h)(1)–(6), comments 3(h)-1 and -2]

### Separate exercise H: changing the loan amount changes the test

Consider a different fictional subordinate assistance transaction, H-28. It finances $28,000 of home-buyer assistance. Its contract has no interest and defers principal until sale or until the property ceases to be the principal dwelling; all nonfee conditions for §1026.3(h) are stipulated satisfied. At consummation, the consumer pays only these charges:

| Charge | Amount | Included in the paragraph (h)(5)(ii) percentage numerator? |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Recording | $90 | No |
| Transfer tax | $120 | No |
| Bona fide, reasonable application fee | $200 | Yes |
| Bona fide, reasonable housing-counseling fee | $60 | Yes |

The capped-fee subtotal is $260. One percent of $28,000 is $280, and $260 ÷ $28,000 ≈ 0.9286%. The stated fee condition is met because $260 is less than $280. The $470 total consumer cost is not the numerator used by that particular condition. This calculation does not independently prove all the other exemption requirements; those were stipulated only for the exercise.

Now the approved amount falls to $25,000 while those same fees remain. One percent is $250; $260 is 1.04% and fails the condition. A note that “fees were approved last week” does not preserve an exemption whose current facts no longer satisfy the rule. Exactly one percent would also fail, because the rule says less than, not at most.

Change the original $28,000 example another way: add a $50 title-search charge payable by the consumer at consummation. Its modest size does not make it an allowed cost under this partial exemption. The enumerated-fee condition fails even if the application/counseling subtotal remains below one percent. This is not a statement that title work is unnecessary or can never be paid for; it is a condition on the transaction using this particular exemption.

### Choose the actual disclosure path

For a qualifying transaction, §1026.3(h) exempts the §1026.19(g) special-information-booklet requirement and permits the relevant disclosure alternative described in the rule. The creditor may provide compliant §1026.18 disclosures instead of the Loan Estimate/Closing Disclosure route, or choose compliant §1026.19(e) and (f) disclosures. Choosing the latter does not permit incomplete TRID compliance. Other applicable Regulation Z provisions remain, including rescission where applicable. [S3: opening text, paragraph (6), comment 3(h)-1]

Consequently, the closer might legitimately receive a first loan on the LE/CD path and qualifying assistance on a different disclosure path. The correct question is whether the two transactions have each been handled under their actual requirements, not why their forms are not identical. A combined purchase budget does not merge two creditors' legal obligations into a single approved loan.

If H-28 fails the exemption conditions after a change, refer it for the ordinary applicable disclosure analysis; do not conclude that the loan is permanently illegal or that another exemption necessarily applies. Determine the complete coverage and timing consequences before proceeding. The student is not authorized to fix a failed exemption merely by reducing a fee without the relevant creditor's actual decision and accurate documentation.

**Activity:** Write a short exemption finding for the $25,000 variation. A complete answer identifies the changed denominator, the $260 numerator, the $250 strict threshold, the failed condition, and the authorized reviewer responsible for choosing a compliant response. Do not call the transaction “NMLS exempt”; this is a Regulation Z issue unrelated to whether a school has CE approval.

## 3. G02-U03 — A refinance can replace one loan and leave another alive

### Case R: Aisha's refinance with a retained line

Aisha Rowe and Corin Vale co-own their existing principal dwelling. Aisha proposes a new $306,000 closed-end loan from Maple Reach Credit. The current first-mortgage creditor is a different institution, with no merger or other successor relationship to Maple Reach. The current first-loan payoff for the stipulated date is $276,000. Costs total $6,000 and the remaining $24,000 is intended for the consumers. An existing HELOC from another creditor has a $55,000 limit and an $18,000 drawn balance. The proposed refinance does not pay it off or close it.

The uses are $276,000 + $6,000 + $24,000 = $306,000. That reconciliation is not proof of first-lien priority, correct disclosure, repayment eligibility, permissible cash-out treatment, or permission to disburse. The HELOC remains a separate obligation with its existing terms unless its creditor and the consumers actually agree to a change. A zero balance, if later achieved, would not by itself establish that the account or lien had been terminated.

Map four separate events: satisfaction of the old first obligation; creation of the new obligation; the retained HELOC's position relative to the new lien; and the permitted release of funds. Which events occur at signing, funding or recording depends on actual agreements and applicable law. Do not assume those words identify the same moment in every state. Regulation Z's consummation concept is tied to the consumer becoming contractually obligated under applicable state law. [S4: §1026.2(a)(13), comment -1]

### Use the payoff, not a balance guess

A periodic statement's principal balance is not automatically the amount needed to pay the obligation in full on a specified date. The payoff request must reach the correct responsible party and identify the date and authorized requester. Under §1026.36(c)(3), the applicable creditor, assignee or servicer must provide an accurate statement within a reasonable time, generally no more than seven business days after receiving the written request. Specified circumstances such as bankruptcy, foreclosure, reverse/shared-appreciation mortgages and natural disasters can require a reasonable time beyond seven business days. Reasonable identity, authorization and request-channel requirements also matter. [S5: §1026.36(c)(3), comments -1 and -2]

This is not an instruction to assume every payoff has a seven-day processing delay or to send consumer records to an unverified address. It is a federal response standard with conditions, not a guarantee that a proposed settlement date is feasible. If the release date changes, obtain a payoff that supports the actual date or a valid calculation permitted by the responsible party's instructions. Do not extend a good-through date by editing the creditor's statement.

### Retaining the HELOC requires an actual priority solution

The new creditor may require its intended lien priority. Whether a retained lien needs an executed subordination agreement, what must be recorded, and whether a statute preserves priority are not resolved by the word “second” on an old application. Obtain the actual title/legal analysis and applicable creditor requirements. The coordinator can identify the issue and request the evidence; an originator's assurance does not alter a lien.

**Investor-specific illustration:** For loans subject to Fannie Mae's cited Selling Guide provision, retained subordinate financing generally requires an executed and recorded resubordination agreement. The guide recognizes a state-law path preserving the subordinate position when the statute's criteria are met; title insurance alone does not replace compliance. This is Fannie Mae delivery policy, not a universal federal rule or a complete account of state priority law. [S6: B2-1.2-04, resubordination section]

Case R does not stipulate a Fannie Mae delivery or an automatic-priority state. Therefore the correct case conclusion is that the priority question needs documented resolution, not that a particular form is always required. An electronic message “HELOC approved” is incomplete unless it identifies what was approved: continued availability, changed terms, subordination, closure, or something else.

### Ordinary language and a technical definition can differ

Consumers commonly call Case R a refinance. Under §1026.20(a), however, the technical refinancing provision concerns replacement by the original creditor or a holder or servicer of the original obligation; replacement credit from another person is a new transaction under Regulation Z. This does not exempt a new creditor from disclosure requirements. Separately, a mere modification of an existing obligation is not automatically a refinancing: the contract and applicable law determine whether the old obligation is satisfied and replaced, subject to the provision's exceptions. [S7: §1026.20(a), comments -1, -2 and -5]

**Activity:** A colleague writes, “New lender, therefore not a §1026.20(a) refinance, therefore no new disclosures.” Identify the false second inference. Then identify the different evidence needed to decide whether a same-creditor rate/payment change is a technical refinancing. Do not apply a marketing label in place of the legal test.

## 4. G02-U04 — Rescission follows the transaction, not the appointment label

Case P finances acquisition of the consumers' principal dwelling and is secured by that acquired dwelling. On those facts, the residential-mortgage-transaction exemption from rescission applies. Junior lien status does not itself defeat that acquisition exemption. Conversely, taking a loan secured by an already-owned current principal dwelling to help buy another home is not automatically acquisition-exempt: the official commentary specifically addresses bridge financing secured by the current home. Always identify what dwelling is encumbered and what the transaction finances. [S4: §1026.2(a)(24); S8: §1026.23(f)(1), comments 23(f)-1/-2 and 23(a)(1)-4]

Case R is different. It places a new security interest in an existing principal dwelling in a new-creditor transaction; no rescission exemption is stipulated. The special original-creditor refinance exemption is not available merely because the transaction pays off an old mortgage. The original-creditor rule also has a new-advance boundary: even a qualifying original-creditor refinance can be rescindable to the extent of a new advance. This lesson does not calculate that specialized amount from a loan's face amount alone. [S8: §1026.23(a)(1), (f)(2), comment 23(f)-4]

### Identify the people who hold the right

For the exercise, Aisha signs the credit agreement and Corin does not, but both have an ownership interest in the principal dwelling subject to the new security interest and both are entitled to rescind. Corin's absence from the repayment promise does not eliminate the separate rescission right. Each entitled consumer must receive the required material disclosures and two copies of the rescission notice; the rule permits one notice copy for each consumer when properly delivered electronically under E-Sign. [S8: §1026.23(a)(1), (b)(1), comments 23(a)(1)-2 and 23(b)(1)-1]

Do not use that instruction to demand that Corin become a co-borrower. The ownership/security analysis and the personal-credit-liability analysis are distinct. Likewise, a contractual signature does not by itself identify everyone entitled to rescind. A legal review of the actual interests is necessary where the facts are uncertain.

### Work from the latest trigger for each entitled consumer

Assume all applicable pre-consummation disclosure obligations have already been satisfied; this is not another CD waiting-period exercise. Case R is consummated Tuesday, September 15, 2026. Both consumers receive all required material disclosures that day. Aisha receives a correct rescission notice that day. Corin does not receive the required notice until Thursday, September 17. No federal legal holiday falls in this exercise, and no waiver or other complication applies.

The ordinary rescission period runs until midnight of the third business day following the last of consummation, delivery of the required notice, and delivery of the material disclosures. For this purpose business days include Saturdays and exclude Sundays and federal legal public holidays specified by the rule, irrespective of whether the lender normally opens on Saturdays. [S8: §1026.23(a)(3), comment -1; S4: §1026.2(a)(6)]

For Aisha, the counted days are Wednesday September 16, Thursday September 17 and Friday September 18: expiration at midnight Friday. For Corin, whose notice is later, the counted days are Friday September 18, Saturday September 19 and Monday September 21: expiration at midnight Monday. The transaction cannot be released on Friday merely because the borrowing owner's period is nearly finished. When more than one consumer holds the right, one consumer's exercise is effective for all. [S8: §1026.23(a)(4)]

### An expired period and authority to release are not identical

Section 1026.23(c) delays performance until the period has expired and the creditor is reasonably satisfied that the consumer has not rescinded. It permits a valid escrow arrangement, but not simply handing funds to the consumer under an “escrow agent” label. Its interpretation permits specified preparatory actions that fall short of actual performance, subject to other law. [S8: §1026.23(c), comments -1 through -4]

For Case R, Tuesday September 22 is the earliest day after both stated periods expire, **not a promised funding date**. The creditor still needs the required reasonable satisfaction about nonexercise and all other release conditions. Assurance from only Aisha cannot establish that Corin did not exercise an independent right. Notice of rescission can be effective when mailed within the permitted period; simply seeing no uploaded portal notice is not a universal substitute for the required determination. [S8: §1026.23(a)(2), comment -1; comment 23(c)-4]

The consumer-waiver provision is narrow. A bona fide personal financial emergency, a dated written statement describing it and specifically waiving/modifying the right, and signatures of all entitled consumers are required; printed waiver forms are prohibited. A desired moving date or the closer's convenience is not, without more, a finding of a qualifying emergency. The existence of a signed waiver does not automatically insulate the creditor from liability. [S8: §1026.23(e), comments -1/-2]

**Activity:** Explain to the closing team why Corin's later notice changes the release analysis without blaming Corin or saying the refinance has been declined. Identify the two distinct unresolved tasks: the actual rescission/nonexercise determination and the still-unresolved retained-lien priority. Clearing one must not silently clear the other.

## 5. G02-U05 — Make the transaction decision explicit

Prepare a release discussion for each case using four questions: What credit obligations will exist afterward? Which cash flows rely on which commitment? Which legal/contract conditions remain? What can the consumer reasonably be told now?

For Case P after the first-loan reduction, the discussion should not lead with “we have enough combined equity.” It should identify the $10,000 funding gap, the absence or presence of an authorized revised second loan, the consumer's future repayment obligation and the separate disclosure/closing path for each loan. Equity is not cash at settlement, and a subordinate position is not forgiveness.

For Case R, the discussion should not lead with “the first loan is approved.” It should identify the payoff supported for the actual release date, the HELOC that remains, the resolved or unresolved lien-priority conditions and each entitled consumer's rescission timing. The consumers must not be told that the retained HELOC vanished because its proceeds do not appear in the new-loan uses.

An accurate consumer explanation avoids both a legal lecture and an unsupported guarantee: “The new loan would replace the existing first loan, while your existing line would remain under its own agreement. We must complete the required priority and release checks before confirming when proceeds can be paid. We will identify any proposed change to the line rather than assume you agreed to it.” This is an original communication exercise, not mandated notice language.

**Final written exercise:** A closer proposes paying the old first mortgage immediately into the consumer's personal account “to hold in escrow,” proceeding without the retained lender's required subordination decision, and using an earlier payoff amount after the expected date changes. Identify each defect separately. A good response rejects consumer-held pseudo-escrow under the stated rescission rule, requires an actual priority solution, and requests a payoff calculation supporting the correct date. It does not announce that federal law always requires one named subordination form or that a seven-business-day response standard means the payoff must be exactly seven days old.
