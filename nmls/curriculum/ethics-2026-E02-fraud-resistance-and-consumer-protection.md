## 1. The professional decision: protect the file without prejudging the person

Annual CE must include two hours of ethics encompassing fraud, consumer protection, and fair lending. That category requirement does not mean NMLS mandates this lesson title, these cases, or a particular fraud-detection system. This module applies current federal obligations to situations an experienced MLO can encounter; E01 supplies the complementary fair-lending treatment. [S1, S2]

Fraud resistance is not the ability to call a document fake quickly. It is the discipline to recognize an inconsistency, understand what decision depends on it, obtain appropriate verification, preserve a truthful record, and keep pressure from turning unresolved information into a convenient fact. There are two foreseeable failures. One is knowingly advancing unreliable information because a loan is close to closing. The other is treating every mismatch as proof of dishonesty and making an unsupported accusation against a customer who may be an identity-theft victim or the subject of a data-entry error.

Consumer protection also reaches conduct initiated inside the mortgage business. An accurate pay statement does not cure a referral kickback. A properly disclosed rate does not excuse prohibited steering. A signed borrower acknowledgment does not make coercion of an appraiser permissible. An ethical workflow examines the originator's incentives and conduct, not just the applicant's records.

### Learning objectives

By the end of the proposed instruction, a learner should be able to:

1. Separate an observed discrepancy, a supported finding, and an allegation of intentional fraud.
2. Apply the reasonably reliable third-party verification standard to income that a creditor relies on, without inventing a rule that borrower-delivered records are always unacceptable.
3. Preserve and escalate material evidence through a factual, proportionate process without using protected characteristics as substitutes for evidence.
4. Identify a thing of value, an agreement or understanding, and a settlement-service referral in a RESPA scenario, while recognizing actual-service and affiliated-business boundaries.
5. Distinguish a permissible request to correct valuation facts from prohibited pressure to reach a desired value.
6. Correct a misleading payment presentation and recognize compensation-driven steering without treating one disclosure as a universal safe harbor.
7. Write a defensible release-or-hold recommendation identifying the unresolved fact, responsible reviewer, and consumer communication, rather than manufacturing a clearance.

## 2. A red flag is a reason to investigate, not a verdict

Use this five-part working method: **observation → relevance → verification → decision → record**. This is a recommended operational method, not wording prescribed by a federal regulation.

- **Observation:** identify exactly what differs. “Two payroll summaries for the same period show different gross base pay” is an observation. “Borrower is a fraudster” is a conclusion not established by that observation.
- **Relevance:** identify which pending representation or decision depends on it. Does the higher amount support repayment qualification? Does a name mismatch affect which consumer's credit report is being used? Is a payment actually compensation for a referral?
- **Verification:** identify a source capable of resolving the fact. Obtain authorized information through the institution's approved process. A manager's desire to close is not a source of income or identity evidence.
- **Decision:** the authorized reviewer evaluates the resolved facts under applicable law and program requirements. The MLO should not quietly substitute a preferred conclusion for that reviewer.
- **Record:** retain the original evidence, the explanation, the verification result, and the actual decision in the authorized record system. A correction should explain an earlier entry, not falsely suggest the earlier entry never existed.

This method is deliberately different from collecting more documentation from every applicant whose name, accent, neighborhood, family, or income source feels unfamiliar. Reg B prohibits treatment on a prohibited basis; E01 explains its current scope and exceptions. An actual mismatch may justify a proportionate inquiry, but a stereotype is not an identity-control exception. [S3]

Do not assume an internal “fraud review” label pauses every disclosure, notice, or contractual deadline. Application status, adverse-action notices, and other obligations must be evaluated on their own rules. F01 addresses those clocks. This module does not award duplicate Federal Law time for repeating them. Likewise, reporting obligations, SAR decisions and confidentiality, criminal-law elements, breach notifications, and product-specific government or investor reporting require the appropriate specialist; this lesson does not invent a universal “report every discrepancy” rule.

## 3. Worked case A — Two income records, one closing target

### Evidence packet: Harbor Lane file HL-26-411

Assume a closed-end consumer purchase loan secured by the consumer's dwelling is a covered transaction for the general ability-to-repay rule. No exemption, special refinance provision, or alternative compliance path is being invoked in this exercise. Creditor underwriting relies on Rowan's base salary, not collateral value, a hoped-for commission, or household averages.

The file contains:

| Record | What it says | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Employer-issued monthly payroll summary A, uploaded Monday | Gross base salary: $7,400 for August 2026 | Whether an additional raise became effective later |
| Purported replacement payroll summary B, uploaded Tuesday | Gross base salary: $9,600 for the same August period | Why the same historical period changed |
| MLO note | “Need at least $9,500 base under this lender's stated conditions” | That the applicant actually earns that amount; the amount is a case-specific lender condition, not a federal threshold |
| Applicant message | “My recruiter said the new role pays more. I sent what I received” | Who created B, whether the change is approved, or whether the applicant intended deception |
| Supervisor message | “Use the new one, remove A, and mark salary verified; we cannot lose Friday” | Reliable verification or authority to falsify the review record |

The difference is $2,200 for the same monthly period. It is approximately 29.73% of the original $7,400: $2,200 ÷ $7,400 × 100. That percentage is a descriptive comparison, not a regulatory fraud threshold. It helps identify the material inconsistency. It proves neither which document is accurate nor who created the conflict.

For this covered transaction, the creditor must verify the income or assets it relies on using third-party records that provide reasonably reliable evidence. Individualized records matter: the employer's typical pay range or a website reporting average wages is not verification of Rowan's salary. The rule permits various sources, including payroll statements, financial-institution records, and records from the employer. A tax-return transcript is a possible source, not a universally mandated document. [S4: §1026.43(c)(3)–(4), comments 43(c)(3)-1 and -2, 43(c)(4)-1 and -3]

**The upload channel does not settle authenticity.** The official interpretation expressly permits obtaining third-party records directly from the consumer when they are reasonably reliable and specific to the individual. A borrower-uploaded employer statement is not automatically disqualified. Conversely, receipt through a familiar portal does not make contradictory information reliable. The question is the quality and substance of the evidence, not whether a preferred label appears beside the file. [S4: comment 43(c)(3)-2]

### Learner activity: write the next four actions

Before reading the guide, write four actions that address the inconsistency. For each, identify whether it is a legal verification requirement or a recommended method of satisfying and documenting that requirement. Also write one sentence that should **not** be sent to Rowan.

### Private review guide

1. Preserve A, B, the upload history, and the relevant communications within authorized access controls. The specific preservation workflow is an operational recommendation; it keeps a real discrepancy visible for the legal verification and any separate retention obligations.
2. Do not mark the higher salary verified on the present evidence. A supervisor's instruction does not satisfy the reasonably reliable evidence requirement for income being relied upon.
3. Obtain appropriate authorized verification of actual pay and any raise through the institution's established process. Confirming contact information through an independently established channel is a recommended anti-impersonation control; this lesson does not claim that §1026.43 requires a particular telephone directory, vendor, or communication technology.
4. Give the authorized underwriting/compliance reviewer the conflicting records and verified explanation. Separate whether a prospective raise may be considered under the applicable rule and program from whether a historical document accurately described August pay. Record the resulting decision truthfully. The MLO does not cure the historical inconsistency by editing a pay statement.

Do not write, “We know you forged your income.” The current evidence does not support that claim. A factual alternative is: “We received two records for the same pay period with different base-pay amounts. We need to resolve which amount is accurate through our verification process before the creditor can rely on it.” Do not promise approval or a particular closing date while the material fact remains unresolved.

### Changed fact: a real raise, an inaccurate historical document

The approved verification process establishes that August base salary was $7,400. A signed employer record confirms a raise to $9,600 beginning October 1, subject to conditions stated in that record. This new evidence does not make B an accurate August payroll record. It changes the factual discussion: there is a verified historical amount and separate prospective employment information.

Whether and how the creditor can rely on the prospective amount is an underwriting and legal-applicability question, not permission to relabel future income as already earned. This ethics case does not prescribe a universal waiting period or a rule that every future raise must be ignored. Escalation must preserve both the actual verification and the remaining program question. A legitimate route forward can exist without a fabricated past.

### A bank deposit is not automatically earnings

Suppose the file also shows a new $12,000 bank deposit with no source description. “Money arrived” does not establish that it is income. The official interpretation gives an unidentified deposit as a case requiring a basis to conclude that funds are the consumer's income rather than, for example, loan proceeds. Do not turn an unexplained balance into qualifying salary merely because it fills the gap. [S4: comment 43(c)(4)-4]

## 4. Worked case B — An identity mismatch without a stereotype

The application identifies Cameron Vale III. A credit report displays Cameron Vale II with a different birth date. One uploaded document bears a shortened first name; that shortening alone might be ordinary. The birth-date and suffix differences could reflect a mixed file, entry error, family-member confusion, identity theft, or other facts not yet known.

The proper initial statement is narrow: **the available records do not yet establish that the report belongs to the applicant**. It is not, “This name sounds foreign,” “This customer speaks with an accent,” or “People from this neighborhood often have problems.” Those are not identity evidence. [S3]

Apply the organization's approved identity-resolution and credit-report procedures, using appropriate access and authorization. Route the mismatch to the responsible specialist; confirm the factual identifiers through allowed channels and determine whether the report or application needs correction. This is a recommended process, not a claim that every MLO has the same federally mandated identity tool or must personally investigate a suspected crime. Avoid disclosing full identifiers in an unrestricted email chain or asking a customer to send sensitive records to an unapproved location.

For ability-to-repay purposes, the official interpretation recognizes that a creditor may know or have reason to know that a report is inaccurate, including information contradicting it or certain alerts or disputes. It discusses disregarding inaccurate or disputed information and does not impose an automatic requirement to obtain additional third-party records in every such case. That treatment is not permission to knowingly use somebody else's credit history to support the applicant's approval. Product rules, credit-report duties, identity controls, and notice obligations still require their own analysis. [S4: comment 43(c)(3)-3]

### Learner activity: observation versus accusation

Classify each statement as an observation, a reasonable issue to resolve, or an unsupported conclusion:

1. “The report birth date differs from the application birth date.”
2. “We need to establish whether the report is associated with this applicant before relying on it.”
3. “The customer has an unusual surname, so the documents must be false.”
4. “The applicant is definitely the person who altered the report.”

Review guide: 1 is an observation; 2 is a reasonable issue to resolve; 3 and 4 are unsupported conclusions. Statement 3 also substitutes a potentially prohibited assumption for relevant evidence. A competent file review can take an inconsistency seriously while leaving intent and responsibility undecided.

## 5. Worked case C — A “quality fee” that follows referrals

### Evidence packet: referral arrangement RL-26-52

Summit Quay Title pays MLO Jules, who is not its employee, $175 for every referred federally related mortgage loan that closes using Summit's settlement services. They agree to call the payment a “quality-review fee.” Jules does no review or other service for Summit. The ledger shows twelve closed referrals and a payment of $2,100. Borrowers pay Summit's ordinary price, with no separately identified surcharge for Jules's payment.

The title does not determine the legal analysis. Section 1024.14(b) prohibits giving or accepting a fee, kickback, or thing of value pursuant to an agreement or understanding that settlement-service business involving a federally related mortgage loan will be referred. A thing of value can extend beyond cash. An agreement need not be written; a practice or pattern can establish the understanding. A referral can be an action influencing selection of a settlement-service provider. [S5: §1024.14(b), (d), (e), (f)]

Apply the facts, not a generic slogan:

| Element | Evidence in this case |
| --- | --- |
| Thing of value | $175 per closed referred transaction; twelve × $175 = $2,100 |
| Agreement or understanding | Express agreement to pay when a referred loan closes with Summit |
| Referral of covered settlement-service business | Jules directs the borrower to Summit for the stipulated federally related mortgage transaction |
| Claimed services | No actual quality-review work; the invoice label is unsupported |

The actual-services provision does not protect a payment when the supposedly compensated services did not occur. The fact that the borrower did not pay an added charge does not convert the referral payment into a permitted one. The regulation also makes clear that a high price alone is not proof of a violation; the issue is not resolved simply by comparing consumer prices. [S5: §1024.14(g)(1)(iv), (g)(2)]

### What this does not mean

Not every payment involving a settlement-service business is a prohibited referral payment. The regulation recognizes specified payments and arrangements, including bona fide compensation for goods, facilities, or actual services, qualifying promotional or educational activities, and an employer's payment to its own employees for referral activities. Each has its own conditions. Jules is expressly not Summit's employee, and no real service exists in the stipulated case. Do not expand the own-employee provision to a payment from an outside title business merely because the MLO is somebody's employee. [S5: §1024.14(g)(1)]

For promotional or educational activities, “educational” is not sufficient by itself: the activity must not be conditioned on referrals and must not defray expenses that the person in a position to refer would otherwise incur. For actual-services arrangements, reasonable market value does not include the value of expected referrals. Where someone is paid for multiple services, the relevant services must be actual, necessary, and distinct from the person's primary services. [S5: §1024.14(g)(1)(vi), (g)(2)–(3)]

An agreement called a marketing-services agreement is therefore neither automatically unlawful nor automatically safe. The reviewer needs evidence of what is actually delivered, who receives value, how payment is determined, whether the work is real and distinct, and whether referrals are being purchased. A signed invoice stating “fair market value” is not independent proof of those facts.

This case concerns a referral payment under §1024.14(b). It does **not** teach that every disputed fee retained entirely by one provider automatically establishes a fee-splitting violation under RESPA section 8(b). That separate provision, its elements, and controlling judicial interpretation require a distinct analysis; do not collapse every pricing concern into the same violation.

### Affiliated business: disclosure is one condition, not the whole exception

Change the facts. Jules's employer owns an interest in a title business and refers consumers to it. The affiliated-business exception has multiple conditions, including the required relationship and estimated-charge disclosure at the applicable time, no required use except specified exceptions, and limits on things of value received, including permissible ownership returns. It is not a general permission to pay a per-file referral bonus when a disclosure has been signed. Returns labeled “dividends” are not protected merely by that label if they are really calculated on actual or expected referrals. [S6: §1024.15(b)(1)–(3), (c)]

The default disclosure timing is no later than the referral; §1024.15(b)(1) specifies exceptions, including particular lender situations. This module does not replace those conditions with a universal “three days after application” instruction. Nor does it teach an absolute rule that no provider may ever be required: the regulation contains particular lender-related exceptions. Use the actual arrangement and controlling text before deciding whether the exception applies.

### Learner activity: revise the recommendation

Draft recommendation: “Approve the $175 payment because the borrower did not pay extra and the parties will sign an affiliated-business disclosure.”

Replace it with a recommendation grounded in the original facts. Review guide: do not approve a fee for nonexistent services linked to covered referrals. Identify the agreement, payment, and referrals; preserve the ledger and communications; route for corrective-action review. Neither unchanged borrower pricing nor a new label or disclosure establishes the missing exception. Determine any required restitution, reporting, discipline, or wider review through authorized legal/compliance processes; do not promise a particular remedy without that analysis.

## 6. Worked case D — Correcting a valuation versus buying the answer

### Evidence packet: valuation VI-26-79

For a consumer loan secured by the consumer's principal dwelling, an appraiser's written valuation is $398,000. The contract price is $420,000. The MLO writes: “Get this to $420,000 by tomorrow or you will not get another assignment. We will hold your fee until it closes.” Separately, a property record appears to identify a factual error in the reported lot size.

The $22,000 difference between contract price and appraised value is not itself evidence of appraiser misconduct. A contract price is not an instruction to deliver that value. The scope of §1026.42 matters: it addresses consumer credit secured by the principal dwelling and covered persons, including persons providing settlement services. Its valuation definition and commentary should be consulted for the actual service; do not assume every property-price opinion in every transaction has the same coverage. [S7: §1026.42(a)–(b)]

The regulation prohibits coercion and attempted coercion designed to make a valuation depend on something other than the valuer's independent judgment. Threatening future assignments, withholding payment to influence value, and conditioning compensation on consummation are among the expressly addressed forms of pressure. An attempt can be prohibited even if the appraiser refuses and leaves the number unchanged. [S7: §1026.42(c)(1), (c)(1)(i)(B)–(E); comments 42(c)(1)-2 and -4]

It also prohibits covered persons from falsifying a valuation and, subject to its terms, materially altering a valuation. The originator should not change the final value in the file to make a ratio work. A bona fide error is distinguished from misrepresentation; correcting a genuine fact through the proper process is not the same as manufacturing an outcome. [S7: §1026.42(c)(2)]

### A permissible request is factual and outcome-neutral

Section 1026.42(c)(3) permits requests to consider additional appropriate property information, provide further explanation, or correct errors. A rewritten request could say: “The attached property record appears to show a different lot size from page 2 of the report. Please review whether the reported fact is accurate and explain any resulting revision under your independent judgment.” The request supplies information and asks for analysis; it neither commands a target value nor links payment or future work to reaching one.

That correction request does not erase the earlier threat. If a creditor knows before consummation of a violation covered by §1026.42(e), it may not extend credit based on the valuation unless it documents reasonable diligence establishing that the valuation does not materially misstate or misrepresent the dwelling's value. Obtaining a second valuation is not automatically required in every case, and obtaining one does not retroactively make coercion proper. Route the incident to the responsible reviewer and preserve the evidence. [S7: §1026.42(e), comment 42(e)-1]

### Reporting threshold: do not punish independence

The reporting provision addresses a reasonable belief that an appraiser materially failed to comply with applicable USPAP or ethical/professional legal requirements. Under §1026.42(g)(1), the failure is material if it is likely to significantly affect the value assigned to the principal dwelling. The rule calls for reporting to the appraisal-certifying and licensing agency for the state where that dwelling is located, within a reasonable period after determining there is a reasonable basis for the report. It does not say “report an appraiser whenever a value is below contract” or impose a universal forty-eight-hour deadline. On the present facts, the appraiser's refusal to increase the value is not itself that material failure. [S7: §1026.42(g)(1)–(3)]

### Learner activity: classify the request

Classify each as a permitted type of request, prohibited pressure on these stipulated facts, or an issue requiring further evidence:

1. “Here is a recent comparable sale we believe may be relevant; please consider it independently.”
2. “Your next five assignments depend on reaching the contract price.”
3. “Please explain the treatment of the recorded easement.”
4. “The report has a one-digit typographical error; therefore the appraiser must immediately be reported for a material violation.”

Guide: 1 and 3 are permitted types under §1026.42(c)(3); 2 is prohibited pressure; 4 overstates what the identified fact establishes. A factually small typographical error is not automatically a material professional failure; determine what it affects before applying the reporting standard. Permissible subject matter does not protect a request delivered with an accompanying threat.

## 7. Consumer protection: a correct number can still be an incomplete presentation

### Worked case E — The payment headline

A lender's ordinary website product page—not a banner linked to an application, a pop-up, television, radio, or an envelope—advertises a closed-end first-lien consumer mortgage secured by a dwelling as “Your entire monthly home payment: $1,850.” The $1,850 is principal and interest only. For the fictional example, taxes are estimated at $360 per month and homeowners insurance at $140 per month. There is no mortgage insurance or association charge in this exercise. The page provides no explanation that the advertised payment excludes taxes and insurance.

The known illustrative outlay is $1,850 + $360 + $140 = **$2,350**. The difference is **$500 per month**, or **$6,000 over twelve months** at those unchanged estimates. These figures do not guarantee future tax or insurance amounts and do not establish that every customer's costs are identical.

For an advertisement within §1026.24(f)'s scope that states a payment amount, the rule requires the applicable payment amounts and their periods. For a first-lien dwelling loan, it also requires disclosure, where applicable, that payments exclude taxes and insurance and that the actual payment obligation will be greater. The amounts and periods have an equal-prominence and close-proximity requirement; the tax-and-insurance statement must have prominence and close proximity. These are not identical wording requirements. A distant footnote is not the model supplied by the official interpretation. [S8: §1026.24(f)(1), (3), comment 24(f)-2]

Do not fix the page solely by changing the headline to $2,350 and assume the advertisement is compliant. The example must accurately identify what its numbers include, what is estimated, applicable payment periods and other disclosures; an advertised payment also triggers the applicable additional-terms requirements in §1026.24(d). Interest-rate and APR presentations have separate rules. Any actual revised advertisement needs a complete review of its facts, medium, disclosures, and governing rules. This case isolates one failure rather than delivering preapproved advertising copy. [S8: §1026.24(c), (d), (f)]

### Worked case F — Steering for greater compensation

Two creditors with which a loan-originator organization regularly does business offer options for which the consumer is likely to qualify. The case stipulates that the options have identical loan amount, payment schedule, rate, fixed-rate period, prepayment provisions, service, and consumer-relevant features. Option A charges $750 less in upfront consumer costs. Option B produces greater creditor-paid compensation for the originator. There is no offsetting consumer benefit to B. The originator hides A, directs the consumer to B solely for the greater compensation, and the consumer consummates B.

For the transaction within §1026.36(e)'s scope, this is the conduct the steering prohibition addresses: directing a consumer to consummate a transaction because of greater creditor-paid compensation unless the consummated transaction is in the consumer's interest. The official interpretation states that this provision requires consummation for a violation. Do not describe an unconsummated suggestion as already satisfying every element of §1026.36(e)(1), although it warrants correction before harm and may raise other issues. [S9: §1026.36(e)(1), comments 36(e)(1)-1 through -3]

The rule is not a mandate to select the option paying the least compensation in every case, nor a command to search the entire mortgage market. Consumer interest can depend on actual costs and features, and the interpretation addresses creditors with which the originator regularly does business. What is impermissible in this stipulated case is suppressing the otherwise better available option for the compensation advantage without a consumer-interest justification. A signature saying “I selected B” does not establish that hidden A was fairly presented or that greater compensation was not the reason for steering.

This lesson is not the complete loan-originator compensation rule. Term-based compensation, dual compensation, product coverage, and the detailed non-steering safe-harbor conditions require their own review. Do not convert “showed three choices” into a universal safe harbor without satisfying the actual conditions.

## 8. Integrated release decision — The file is attractive, the evidence is not ready

The original Harbor Lane file returns for final preparation. Its status screen shows green checks for income, title selection, valuation, and consumer communication. The detailed record shows:

- Higher income was marked verified without resolving the two historical records.
- A title ledger contains the per-referral “quality fee” from case C.
- The valuation reviewer received the threat from case D, but nobody assessed its effect under §1026.42(e).
- The consumer's written comparison omits the lower-cost otherwise identical option from case F.
- A supervisor asks the MLO to delete the four supporting messages “because the summary is what the auditor reads.”

The green summary is evidence of a system status, not evidence that the underlying legal and factual conditions have been met. Each issue has a different legal basis. Do not combine them into a generic “fraud cleared” checkbox that conceals which fact was verified, which conduct occurred, and which legal review remains.

### Learner assignment

Prepare a short recommendation with five fields:

1. The specific unresolved fact or conduct.
2. The proposed release restriction and who can decide it.
3. The evidence that must be preserved or obtained.
4. The explanation to the consumer that is accurate without accusation or an unsupported promise.
5. The condition that would support the next decision, distinguishing a corrected fact from an earlier violation that still needs remediation.

### Private review guide

The recommendation should not clear the file on these facts. For relied-on income, the missing reasonably reliable verification must be resolved under §1026.43. For the stipulated referral payment, an invoice rename does not supply real services or undo the agreement. For valuation, the creditor's pre-consummation knowledge triggers the applicable §1026.42(e) restriction and diligence analysis. For steering, correct the presentation and consumer-interest problem before consummation rather than collecting a broad waiver. Record and route the supervisor's deletion request; do not comply by manufacturing a clean history. [S4, S5, S7, S9]

An appropriate consumer message might say: “We have identified information and process issues that need review before the loan can move forward. We will identify any additional information we need from you and explain the available options once that review is complete. We cannot confirm the proposed closing date yet.” Tailor actual communications to the facts and applicable disclosure/notice duties. Do not use this suggested wording to conceal a decision already made or delay a legally required notice.

No single clearance automatically cures the others. Verified income does not legalize a kickback; a new appraisal does not erase a threat; a corrected comparison does not establish that a previous consummated transaction was compliant. The decision record must explain both the prospective release criteria and any retrospective incident review.

### E02-Q01 — Two pay records

For a covered transaction under the general ATR rule, the creditor relies on salary. Two purported employer payroll summaries show different base salaries for the same period. The higher amount would satisfy a case-specific underwriting condition. What is the best initial conclusion?

A. Treat the difference as an approved pay change because the replacement satisfies the lender's stated condition.
B. Verify the conflicting pay amounts before relying on the higher salary; intent remains undetermined.
C. Use the lower summary as verified income because choosing the conservative amount resolves the conflict.
D. Accept the newer summary after obtaining the applicant's written statement that the increase is genuine.

### E02-Q02 — What belongs in the escalation packet?

In Harbor Lane's file, the source and intent of the conflicting payroll summary are still unknown. Compliance is ready to review the incident. The supervisor asks the MLO to send the two summaries but omit the message directing staff to remove the earlier record and mark salary verified. Which recommended escalation packet best separates evidence from allegation?

A. Send the record comparison alone and keep the supervisor's instruction outside the referral unless compliance specifically requests it.
B. Submit the higher summary as confirmed applicant fraud and identify its uploader as the person who created it.
C. Resolve the payroll discrepancy first, then decide whether the internal instruction warrants any referral to the incident reviewer.
D. Include both summaries and the instruction; distinguish documented conduct from unresolved authorship and intent.

### E02-Q03 — A verified raise is not a historical correction

Authorized employer verification establishes Rowan's August base pay at $7,400 and a conditional raise to $9,600 beginning October 1. An MLO drafts: “Employer verified August pay of $9,600; discrepancy closed.” Which replacement accurately records the finding without deciding prospective-income eligibility?

A. August pay: $7,400. The October raise to $9,600 is conditional; send future-income use for underwriting review.
B. Employer confirmed $9,600; treat the replacement August summary as verified because it matches the upcoming raise.
C. Both figures are employer-supported; choose $9,600 for August because the creditor needs at least $9,500.
D. Record $7,400 as permanent salary and close the raise review because only previously earned income is usable.

### E02-Q04 — A mixed credit file

For a covered general ATR determination, a report has a different birth date from the application, and its suffix may identify a family member. The creditor has not established whose history the report describes. What is the best recommended response?

A. Suspend reliance on that report and route the factual mismatch through authorized identity and credit-report procedures.
B. Use the score but remove the disputed account; the remaining report is sufficient to resolve whose history it describes.
C. Use the application identifiers to amend the report locally, then continue underwriting with its existing accounts and score.
D. Request a new report using the original identifiers and accept its result if the same high score reappears.

### E02-Q05 — A fee with no service

A title company agrees to pay a nonemployee MLO $175 for each closed settlement-service referral involving a federally related mortgage loan. The MLO performs no service. Which review of the proposed “quality-review fee” is supported?

A. The MLO is employed in mortgage lending, so the title company's payment fits the own-employee referral exception.
B. The title company bills ordinary prices, so the payment can be approved as compensation that does not burden borrowers.
C. The agreed payment buys covered referrals, and the stipulated absence of work defeats the claimed actual-services exception.
D. The per-file rate appears reasonable, so the actual-services exception applies without separate evidence of delivered services.

### E02-Q06 — Record the arrangement, not only the price

Under Q05's arrangement, twelve referrals have closed and three remain pending. The agreement pays $175 only for a closed referral. Borrowers pay the title company's ordinary prices. Which incident entry correctly identifies the compensation due and its treatment?

A. Record $2,625 as due; include pending referrals because the agreement began when each customer was referred.
B. Record $2,100 for the twelve closed referrals; unchanged consumer pricing does not create an exception for the agreed referral payments.
C. Record $2,100 for closed referrals; classify it as permissible if the title company's ordinary charges cover the expense.
D. Record $2,100 for closed referrals; defer the referral-prohibition analysis until a separate consumer-paid fee split is established.

### E02-Q07 — A purported educational benefit

B. Removing the referral quota supplies the exception even if the provider continues paying ordinary office rent.
C. Disclosure supplies the exception if the borrower remains free to choose a different settlement provider.
D. The referral quota and payment of ordinary office rent independently defeat the claimed education exception.

### E02-Q08 — An affiliated-business disclosure

### E02-Q09 — Attempted valuation pressure

For a transaction covered by §1026.42, the MLO threatens to withhold future assignments unless the appraiser reaches the contract price. The appraiser refuses and retains the independently determined value. Which conclusion follows?

### E02-Q10 — A permitted factual request

### E02-Q11 — Knowledge before consummation

### E02-Q12 — The incomplete payment headline

An ordinary website page advertises a first-lien dwelling mortgage payment of $1,850 that is only principal and interest. Estimated taxes are $360 and homeowners insurance $140 monthly. ### E02-Q13 — Compensation and consummation

An originator hides an otherwise identical, lower-cost likely-qualifying option and directs the consumer solely for greater creditor-paid compensation to the more costly option, with no consumer-interest justification. D. The violation follows from the originator receiving more compensation; the same result applies even when the chosen transaction demonstrably benefits the consumer.

### E02-Q14 — Close the fact, not the incident

## 11. Production and review boundaries

- **No approval or credit claim.** Proposed timing, word count, correct generated answers, and source links do not constitute NMLS approval, representative learner validation, delivery approval, or a final examination.
- **Human source and instructional review remain required.** A qualified reviewer must test applicability, factual realism, distractor fairness, accessibility, and all source-dependent conclusions before submission or delivery. Review the operative authorities and any applicable judicial orders again at release.
- **Fraud does not equal an accusation or an automatic filing.** No case determines criminal intent or supplies a universal SAR, police-report, or regulator-reporting instruction. AML/reporting curriculum must separately verify institutional coverage, thresholds, timing, and confidentiality. Do not double-count this Ethics time as 2027 mandatory Federal Law AML instruction.
- **Law and program conditions differ.** The income threshold in case A is stipulated lender policy, not a federal number. Record-preservation and independent-contact methods are recommended controls, not a claim that one workflow or software is required in every institution.
- **Scope is deliberately bounded.** ATR examples assume the stated general-rule coverage. Valuation examples concern the covered principal-dwelling transactions. RESPA examples stipulate federally related mortgage transactions and relevant settlement services. The advertising example is an ordinary web page, not an excluded medium or linked banner. The steering case stipulates §1026.36(e) coverage and consummation.
- **Not comprehensive specialist training.** This is not a complete Reg N, UDAAP, FCRA, data-security, criminal-fraud, loan-originator-compensation, appraisal, affiliated-business, or government-program course. Any such claims require separate current-source coverage. State law, licensing duties, investor rules, and institutional obligations may add requirements.
- **Distinct credit allocation.** F01–F03 cover the separate 2026 federal mandatory topics. E01 covers consistent treatment; E02 evaluates evidence integrity, incentives, and consumer harm with distinct cases. N01/N02 will address nontraditional-product features and underwriting analysis. Repeating a cross-reference is not additional category time.
- **Private assessment control.** Keep worked-case review guides, questions, and keys in controlled authoring assets until the delivery design determines learner access. Do not expose private answer keys through static public files or claim these items are an approved final assessment.
