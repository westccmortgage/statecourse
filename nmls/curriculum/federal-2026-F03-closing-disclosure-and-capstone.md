## Scope and learning outcomes

This lesson addresses ordinary consumer-purpose, closed-end real-property mortgage transactions covered by Regulation Z's Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure requirements. Reverse mortgages, HELOCs, timeshares, exempt transactions, and a complete treatment of rescission are outside the cases. Whether a transaction is rescindable must be determined from its actual facts and applicable law; a refinance label alone does not decide that question.

The 2026 NMLS topic list determines the required subjects. The regulation and official interpretations determine the operative legal tests. **Topic 10's recipient is the person ultimately receiving payment for a settlement service. It does not mean the consumer receiving the CD.** This lesson teaches payee identification as the required topic, then separately addresses consumer delivery because a correctly completed form still has to reach the correct people. [S1, S3, S4]

By the end, the learner should be able to:

1. Reconcile a consumer charge with the service provider's actual receipt, not merely the last estimate.
2. Distinguish the actual-charge ceiling from Loan Estimate tolerance analysis.
3. Test a claimed average-charge program against every condition and exclusion.
4. Identify and substantiate the ultimate payee of a CD service line.
5. Distinguish a CD's service section from the facts governing tolerance classification.
6. Select required consumer recipients and calculate the ordinary CD receipt/waiting sequence.
7. Prepare a corrective-action record that preserves the original evidence and can be independently re-tested.

## 1. Required topic 9 — The estimate is not permission to keep the difference

The annual examination finding describes an appraisal charge on a final CD exceeding the appraisal cost supported by the file. It directs attention to §1026.19(f)(3)(i). Although NMLS places this finding under a zero-tolerance heading, the operative issue here is a distinct actual-charge rule. Do not stop after comparing the CD with the LE. [S1, S2]

Under §1026.19(f)(3)(i), the amount imposed on the consumer for a settlement service cannot exceed what the settlement service provider actually receives for that service, unless the qualified average-charge exception applies. An invoice is important evidence of the service and amount due. The legal test is the amount actually received; reconciliation may also need the final disbursement record, adjustments, refunds, or other reliable payment evidence. An invoice marked paid is stronger evidence than an unverified draft quotation. [S2]

A practical review uses three different comparisons:

| Question | Compare | What a passing result does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Was the estimate in good faith? | Consumer's final charge against the applicable LE baseline and tolerance category | That the provider actually received that amount |
| Is this an excessive service charge? | Consumer's charge against the provider's actual receipt, subject to a lawful averaging program | That the LE tolerance or revision rules were satisfied |
| Is the CD accurately completed? | Service, amount, payee, payer, and payment timing against supporting records | That the disclosure reached the required consumers on time |

These are an operational review aid, not three interchangeable legal exemptions. The first test is principally §1026.19(e)(3)–(4); the second is §1026.19(f)(3); the third includes §1026.38. [S2, S3]

### Worked example: the fee passed the wrong test

Maple Harbor Lending initially discloses a $690 appraisal fee. It does not permit shopping, and there is no permissible revision of the tolerance baseline. The final CD charges $690. The independent appraisal company's final invoice and payment record both establish $645 for the entire service. There is no average-charge program, no other appraisal-related service, and no additional provider payment.

The LE-to-CD comparison has no increase. Nevertheless, the consumer is being charged $45 more than the provider receives. Leaving the fee at $690 because “the borrower already accepted it” does not satisfy the actual-charge rule. If this is found before collection and consummation, correct the proposed charge and disclosure rather than deliberately collecting the overage. If discovered after collection, escalate for the applicable correction and reimbursement analysis with the original records preserved. Do not invent a universal cure deadline for every legal defect. [S2]

Now change the facts. The original LE is $620 and the provider actually receives $645. Charging the consumer $645 would meet the actual-charge ceiling, but the $25 increase still raises the separate zero-tolerance issue. An actual invoice does not itself supply a permissible reason to reset the LE baseline. F02's revision analysis remains necessary. [S2: §1026.19(e)(3)(i), (iv), and (e)(4)]

**Decision pause:** Which evidence would establish whether a $30 difference is an improper retained amount or payment for an additional real service? Identify the service order, final itemized invoice, payment evidence, and appropriate disclosure/tolerance treatment. Merely renaming the difference “handling” does not establish a service or make it lawful. This exercise does not approve a separate handling fee or decide every RESPA, compensation, or state-law issue.

### The average-charge exception is a program, not an excuse

Section 1026.19(f)(3)(ii) allows a creditor or settlement service provider to use an average charge when its conditions are satisfied. Consequently, a particular consumer's permitted average charge can exceed that transaction's individual service cost. That is not a license to keep an unexplained spread or choose averaging only on the files where it is profitable. [S2]

Test the proposed program in this order:

1. **Eligible service.** Averaging cannot be used for any type of insurance, any charge based on loan amount or property value, or where otherwise prohibited by law. A uniform title-insurance premium is not saved by a large sample. A percentage-based transfer tax does not become eligible merely because its average can be calculated. State and local restrictions still matter.
2. **Defined comparable class.** The class must use an appropriate period, geographic area, and loan type. The official interpretation permits a sampling period of at least 30 days and no more than six months, with a sample large enough for reasonable precision. A period within that range is necessary, not sufficient. Geographic area and loan type must not pool dissimilar populations.
3. **Supported average.** The charge cannot exceed the permitted average amount paid for the service across the class. Use supported service costs and the defined methodology, not a desired revenue figure. The interpretation generally relates the charge to the preceding defined period, with reasonable implementation lag permitted when subsequent periods are scheduled consistently.
4. **Uniform application.** Use the same average for the service throughout the defined class. Do not charge the average on inexpensive files and the higher actual amount on expensive files. Uniformity also does not permit charging for a service that is not required or is not obtained in the individual transaction.
5. **Continuing reconciliation.** Retrospective review and adjustment must keep the program from collecting more for the corresponding services than is paid to providers over time. The interpretation describes permissible methods, including appropriate refunds or adjustment of future averages. It also describes a six-month calculation period with rolling monthly reevaluation as a compliant method; that is not a requirement to use only that one design.
6. **Reconstructible evidence.** Retain the source records, class definition, calculation, receipts, ledger, and enough method documentation for an examiner to reproduce the result. Comment 19(f)(3)(ii)-9 specifies at least three years after any settlement for which the average was used. This is not permission to destroy CD-related records subject to the longer rule discussed below.

Authority: §1026.19(f)(3)(ii)(A)–(D), comments 19(f)(3)(ii)-1 through -9. These are source-based conditions; an employer's particular approval workflow is an implementation choice. [S2]

### Worked example: one average, two different costs

Assume a documented, otherwise lawful tax-certification averaging program for a properly defined comparable class. Its adequately supported prior-period cost is $4,800 for 48 services, producing a $100 average. Its methodology, exclusions, state-law review, uniform use, and retrospective controls all satisfy the rule. No other TRID tolerance issue is present.

In the next period, one eligible transaction's provider receives $84 and another's receives $118. Charging $100 for each is not automatically a violation simply because the first charge exceeds $84. That is the function of the qualified exception. Charging $100 on the first and $118 on the second would defeat uniform use. Charging $100 on a third file where that service is not required or obtained would not be justified by its membership in the class. [S2: comments -3 and -5]

Change one fact: the service is title insurance. Stop. The insurance exclusion applies before testing whether the arithmetic is accurate. Change another fact: the program has a properly calculated average but permits permanent retention of aggregate overcollections. Stop again. A correct initial calculation does not excuse failure to reconcile the program over time. [S2: comments -5 and -7]

### Consequences and prevention

The immediate consumer harm is payment of an unsupported amount. Operational consequences can include correction, reimbursement analysis, broader file review, and findings that earlier controls failed. Do not teach a fixed penalty or assert that one refund extinguishes all possible liability. The annual list directs providers to discuss consequences; it does not supply a universal fine for this scenario. [S1]

**Recommended control:** require a fee-level reconciliation before release, followed by a check of actual disbursements. An averaging exception must point to the authorized program and eligible class, not free text reading “average.” An independent reviewer should sample both sides of the average and files with no service. A review that only samples transactions where the fee was below the average cannot test uniformity or detect all excess collections.

## 2. Required topic 10 — “To whom?” must have an answer

The 2026 finding identifies CDs that used placeholders for the ultimate recipients of mortgage-insurance and appraisal-completion payments. The controlling citation is §1026.38(f)(2), not the rule governing which borrower receives a disclosure. [S1, S3]

For services disclosed under **Services Borrower Did Not Shop For**, §1026.38(f)(2) calls for an itemized service and cost, the name of the person ultimately receiving each payment, the correct payment column, and the appropriate borrower-paid total. The scope is creditor-required settlement services supplied by persons other than the creditor or mortgage broker. Other CD sections have their own rules; do not put every fee into this section or assume this one provision decides every insurance entry. [S3]

### Worked example: the amount is right, but the destination is missing

A draft CD lists an appraisal-completion inspection at $165, borrower-paid at closing, with payee “TBD.” The lender required the service and did not permit shopping. A final invoice and verified payment instructions identify Granite Field Review LLC as the provider and ultimate recipient. The settlement agent will transmit the payment but will not retain it for its own service.

The amount and payment column may be correct; the payee field is not. Replace the placeholder with the supported ultimate recipient. Substituting the settlement agent's name only because the money first passes through its account does not identify the stated ultimate destination. Preserve the evidence linking the service, entity, amount, and disbursement. If records disagree, resolve the discrepancy rather than guess a payee. [S3]

This lesson's examples deliberately avoid complex appraisal-management-company, subcontractor, and split-title-premium arrangements. In a real multi-party arrangement, determine the actual service and payment structure before applying the disclosure rule; do not automatically substitute the individual who visited the property or the bank holding the wire account for the correct recipient.

### The section heading is not the tolerance category

Suppose a creditor permits shopping, provides the required written provider list, and a consumer selects a listed, unaffiliated provider for a required service. The charge was initially shown in the LE's services-you-can-shop-for section. On the CD, that service belongs under §1026.38(f)(2), **Services Borrower Did Not Shop For**, because the selected provider was on the written list. Assuming the other requirements are met, the charge can remain in the ten-percent aggregate tolerance category. The CD section name alone does not convert it into a zero-tolerance charge. [S3: §1026.38(f)(2)–(3); S2: §1026.19(e)(3)(ii)]

Conversely, §1026.38(f)(3) addresses the qualifying required service where the consumer shops and selects a provider outside that written list. It also requires the ultimate recipient's name. Moving a fee into a different section does not eliminate payee identification. [S3]

### What if information is not yet known?

The creditor must exercise due diligence and use the best information reasonably available when an actual term is unknown. A verified term sitting in the settlement agent's records is not unavailable merely because the originator has not requested it. Permitted estimation and later correction rules are not a blanket authorization to release a knowingly unresolved placeholder as the final answer. Obtain the actual information and provide corrections as required. The creditor remains responsible for ensuring compliant delivery even when the settlement agent provides the CD. [S2: comments 19(f)(1)(i)-2 and §1026.19(f)(1)(v)]

**Recommended release check:** flag empty payees and placeholder language, then have a reviewer compare nonempty entries with source documents. A field containing “Granite” passes a nonempty-string check even if the real payee is unrelated; therefore the automated check alone cannot establish accuracy. A changed recipient also merits payment-security verification under the firm's approved procedures. This is a recommendation for reliable implementation, not a claim that Regulation Z requires a particular software product.

## 3. Adjacent safeguard — The consumer receiving the CD

Keep the two meanings of recipient separate: the payee receives a service payment; the consumer receives the disclosure. A file can pass one test and fail the other.

### Multiple consumers

| Transaction facts | Federal CD delivery rule relevant here |
| --- | --- |
| Nonrescindable; two consumers each primarily liable | Delivery may be made to either primarily liable consumer |
| Nonrescindable; one principal debtor and a mere guarantor | Delivery to the guarantor alone does not satisfy delivery to a primarily liable consumer |
| Rescindable under §1026.23; more than one consumer has a right to rescind | Give the CD separately to each consumer who has that right |

Authority: §1026.17(d), comment 17(d)-2. A lender can adopt a more protective policy of delivery to all borrowers, but that policy should not be presented as the nonrescindable federal minimum. [S4]

A consumer with a right to rescind need not be a note signer. A natural person whose ownership interest in their principal dwelling is encumbered may have the right, subject to §1026.23's coverage and exemptions. Check title and occupancy as well as the note. Do not announce that all nonborrowing spouses have rescission rights or that every refinance is rescindable. [S5: §1026.23(a)(1), comment 23(a)(1)-2; S6: §1026.2(a)(11)]

### Receipt and the ordinary pre-consummation waiting period

For the covered ordinary transaction, the consumer must receive the initial CD at least three business days before consummation. Here business days are all calendar days except Sundays and the specified legal public holidays, whether or not the lender opens on Saturday. Consummation is when the consumer becomes contractually obligated on the credit transaction under applicable law, not automatically the funding or recording date. [S2: §1026.19(f)(1)(ii)(A); S6: §1026.2(a)(6), (13)]

If not given in person, receipt is presumed three business days after delivery or mailing; the creditor may instead rely on evidence of earlier actual receipt. Electronic delivery must satisfy the applicable E-Sign consent and other requirements. An email's sent timestamp alone is not evidence that the consumer received the disclosure that same day. [S2: §1026.19(f)(1)(iii), comments -1 and -2; S3: §1026.38(t)(3)(iii)]

Use two separate calendar calculations when relying on presumed receipt: first delivery-to-receipt; then receipt-to-consummation. Do not collapse them into “send three days before closing.”

**Calendar example — no intervening legal public holidays:**

- In-person receipt Tuesday, September 22, 2026: Wednesday 23 is day one, Thursday 24 day two, and Friday 25 day three. The ordinary CD waiting rule permits consummation Friday 25, assuming every other condition is satisfied.
- Mailing Tuesday, September 22, with no evidence of earlier receipt: presumed receipt Friday 25. Saturday 26, Monday 28, and Tuesday 29 are the next three business days. The ordinary rule permits consummation Tuesday 29, not Friday 25.
- In a rescindable transaction requiring separate CDs, one required consumer receives a CD Tuesday 22 and the other Wednesday 23. The later consumer's ordinary waiting period reaches day three Saturday 26. Friday 25 is too early. Saturday being counted does not require a settlement office to open that day.

The pre-consummation CD waiting period is not the separate rescission period. For a rescindable transaction, the usual rescission period runs from the last applicable triggering event, including consummation, receipt of the required rescission notice, and receipt of material disclosures. This lesson does not authorize funding merely because the CD wait has elapsed. [S5: §1026.23(a)(3), (c)]

### Corrections: not every change restarts three days

Before consummation, a new three-business-day waiting period is required when the APR becomes inaccurate under §1026.22, the disclosed loan product changes as specified in the rule, or a prepayment penalty is added. Other changes generally require corrected disclosures received at or before consummation, not an automatic new three-day wait. The consumer must also be allowed to inspect the known items during the business day immediately preceding consummation under §1026.19(f)(2)(i). For this inspection right, use the general creditor-business-function definition of business day; do not import the special CD receipt/waiting-period definition. [S2: §1026.19(f)(2)(i)–(ii); S6: §1026.2(a)(6)]

Do not teach “any APR change restarts the clock.” Accuracy must be evaluated under the applicable APR rule. Nor should a person assert that a fee change can never trigger a restart: its effect on APR or other specified terms must be evaluated. The exercises below expressly state whether a restart event exists instead of asking the learner to infer it from incomplete loan mathematics.

A waiver is not an ordinary scheduling tool. Section 1026.19(f)(1)(iv) requires a bona fide personal financial emergency, receipt of the disclosures first, and a dated written statement describing the emergency and specifically modifying or waiving the period, signed by all consumers primarily liable on the obligation. Printed forms for that purpose are prohibited. The required waiver signers are described by that provision; do not silently replace them with a different recipient test. No case here involves a waiver. [S2]

## 4. Integrated capstone — The green dashboard, the red file

**Proposed 15-minute instructional exercise, not validated time.** Work from the fictional evidence, not the dashboard's green label. This is a pre-consummation review, with no fee yet collected. The exercise uses the ordinary rules and has no emergency waiver. All Monday–Friday dates below are business days for the initial-LE deadline; the precise calendar-day definition applies to CD timing. There are no intervening legal public holidays.

### Evidence packet: Cedar Bridge Lending file CB-26-104

1. On Monday, September 14, 2026, the originator receives all six TRID application items for Evelyn's covered consumer-purpose refinance in a recorded application. The application remains pending and is neither denied nor withdrawn. The initial LE is first delivered on Friday, September 18. The LOS was created Wednesday, September 16 and measures the initial deadline from that date. Any separate initial-LE pre-consummation waiting requirement is satisfied by the scheduled consummation date.
2. The applicable LE discloses $750 for a required appraisal from an unaffiliated provider. Shopping was not permitted. There is no valid revised baseline, no changed circumstance, and no average-charge program.
3. Clear Ridge Valuation LLC completes the entire appraisal service. Its final invoice and verified payment instructions establish a total of $710 to that company, with no additional service, split, or payment. The proposed consumer charge and pending disbursement must be brought into agreement before consummation, then actual receipt must be reconciled afterward.
4. The CD says appraisal $780, borrower-paid at closing, payee “TBD.” No part of that charge has yet been paid by the consumer. No other fee or credit changes are present in this exercise.
5. Evelyn signs the note. Noah does not. Both own and occupy the principal dwelling, both ownership interests secure the credit, and the compliance determination establishes that this transaction is rescindable and both have a right to rescind. No exemption applies.
6. Evelyn receives her CD in person Tuesday, September 22. Noah has not received a CD. Staff plan consummation Friday, September 25. On Wednesday, September 23, the reviewer identifies the missing recipient and arranges in-person delivery of an accurate CD to Noah that day and the corrected CD to Evelyn. Assume the fee/payee correction leaves the APR accurate under §1026.22, does not change the loan product, and does not add a prepayment penalty.
7. The file contains the invoice but no linked review of receipt evidence, no support for “TBD,” and no record explaining the initial-LE trigger. The dashboard says “ready.”

### Learner tasks — answer before opening the reviewer key

1. Identify the initial-LE due date and the cause of the misleading green status. Can the later accurate CD erase that historical timing failure?
2. State the LE tolerance issue and the actual-charge issue separately. Determine the necessary reduction of the proposed $780 consumer charge on these facts. Explain why adding the two identified overages would double-count part of the correction.
3. Identify the proper payee and the supporting evidence. Does a “TBD” label become acceptable because the dollar amount is corrected?
4. Identify required consumer recipients. Calculate the earliest consummation allowed by the ordinary CD waiting rule after the stated Wednesday delivery. Explain whether the corrected fee alone creates a separate new wait for Evelyn.
5. Write a concise, factual explanation to the consumer without claiming the file was always compliant or pressuring a waiver.
6. Specify the retained evidence and one prevention test that can fail on this actual pattern.

### Private reviewer key and evaluation criteria

**1. Initial disclosure:** Thursday, September 17. The trigger was Monday receipt of the six items, not Wednesday LOS creation. Friday delivery is late on the stated assumptions. A later CD does not retroactively make the initial LE timely. Preserve the actual dates and investigate the control failure; do not backdate the receipt or create a fictional earlier delivery. [S2: §1026.19(e)(1)(iii)(A); S6: §1026.2(a)(3)(ii)]

**2. Separate ceilings:** The proposed charge exceeds the unrevised $750 zero-tolerance baseline by $30. It also exceeds the supported $710 entire service amount by $70, with no averaging exception. Reduce the proposed consumer charge to $710, a $70 correction. That single reduction removes both indicated excesses; $30 plus $70 would double-count $30. This is a correction before collection, not a claim that a $70 refund has already occurred. If actual provider receipt later differs, reconcile it rather than treating the invoice as the last word. [S2: §1026.19(e)(3)(i), (f)(3)(i)]

**3. Payee:** Clear Ridge Valuation LLC, supported by the final invoice and verified service/payment records. Resolve any disagreement before release. A correct dollar amount does not cure missing payee identification. [S3: §1026.38(f)(2)]

**4. People and timing:** Both Evelyn and Noah need separate CDs because both have a right to rescind. Noah's absence from the note does not remove that right on the stipulated facts. His Wednesday September 23 receipt makes Thursday 24 day one, Friday 25 day two, and Saturday 26 day three. Saturday September 26 is the earliest date under this CD waiting rule; Friday is not permissible. Other conditions may require later consummation or funding. The stipulated ordinary fee/payee correction does not independently restart Evelyn's wait, because none of the three restart events occurs. Preserve delivery evidence for each person and every pertinent version. [S4; S5; S2: §1026.19(f)(1)(ii)–(iii), (f)(2)(i)–(ii)]

**5. Example communication:** “Our review identified an appraisal-charge correction and an additional required disclosure delivery. We are correcting the appraisal charge to $710 and identifying the service provider. Each required recipient will receive the appropriate disclosure. We also need to adjust the scheduled consummation date to satisfy the disclosure waiting period. We will confirm the revised timing after the remaining closing requirements are checked.” This communication is an original example, not a regulator-prescribed notice or an admission template for every situation. The historical LE defect should be handled factually through the firm's approved compliance process, not concealed.

**6. Evidence and prevention:** Preserve the original intake record, all LE/CD versions and delivery evidence, tolerance baseline/revision determination, final invoice, verified payee and disbursement instructions, ownership/rescission determination, each required recipient's receipt record, correction approval, actual payment reconciliation, and any remediation/legal-hold record. A useful independent test compares original intake timestamps against LE delivery across channels and checks title-derived required recipients against person-specific CD receipts. Include cases with a non-note-signing owner. A test comparing only LOS-created timestamps or only borrowers listed on the note would miss these defects. [S2, S4, S5, S6, S7]

Evaluation is qualitative and formative: the learner must distinguish the two monetary tests, identify both required consumers, reject the incorrect Friday date, and preserve historical evidence. Do not treat a memorized dollar figure as demonstrated competence. Ask for a corrected explanation and new application where a misconception remains. Final-assessment scoring, retries, randomization, and independence require a separate approved design.

## 7. Evidence retention and release notes

For §1026.19(e)/(f) compliance evidence generally, §1026.25(c)(1)(i) specifies three years after the later of consummation, the required disclosure date, or the required action date. The more specific CD provision requires each completed required CD and all related documents for five years after consummation. When a creditor disposes of the loan and does not service it, copies must accompany the loan-file transfer to the owner or servicer, which retains them for the balance of that five-year period. The creditor's own five-year rule remains. Do not replace those requirements with F01's 25-month Regulation B period or apply the shortest period to the whole file. [S7]
