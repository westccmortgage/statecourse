## Learning objectives and route map

- 5A: Apply TILA and TRID coverage, application and disclosure triggers to a dated file; distinguish APR, rescission and consummation
- 5B: Apply RESPA referral and settlement-charge restrictions and the Loan Estimate revision/tolerance and Closing Disclosure rules to a bounded case
- 5C: Select appropriate application handling and notices under ECOA and FCRA; explain the role of HMDA and fair-housing protections
- 5D: Recognize when ATR/QM, HOEPA/HPML, compensation, advertising, privacy and BSA/AML issues require a particular rule check

Use four questions throughout: What rule covers this transaction? Which event starts the obligation? What evidence establishes that event? What action is due, by whom and when? The questions are a training method, not a statutory checklist.

## Unit 5A TILA and the disclosure timeline

Proposed future OIL allocation, 45 minutes: instruction 20; legal case 13; peer/instructor dialogue and revision 8; formative checks 4.

### Start with coverage

The Truth in Lending Act is implemented principally through Regulation Z. It promotes informed use of consumer credit and includes substantive mortgage protections as well as disclosure duties. Personal, family or household purpose matters. Calling a loan an investment or having a borrower sign a business-purpose statement does not replace analysis of its actual purpose and the relevant exemptions. [P5-01]

The integrated Loan Estimate (LE) and Closing Disclosure (CD) rules generally cover closed-end consumer credit secured by real property or a cooperative unit. They do not apply to every loan bearing the word mortgage. HELOCs and reverse mortgages have different disclosure frameworks; a loan secured only by a manufactured home that is personal property requires separate analysis. A construction purpose does not automatically exclude a covered real-property loan. The correct question is what this provision covers, not whether another product has a similar name. [P5-02]

RESPA and Regulation X have their own coverage rules for federally related mortgage loans and exclusions. ECOA has broader credit coverage than TILA. A loan outside TRID can still be subject to other federal duties. Use one coverage entry per law rather than one global exempt box. [P5-03, P5-12]

### Interest rate, finance charge and APR

The note rate determines interest under the loan terms. The finance charge expresses credit cost in dollars under a defined inclusion/exclusion test; it is not automatically the sum of every settlement expense. The annual percentage rate expresses the cost of credit as a yearly rate using the legally prescribed method. Certain bona fide and reasonable real-estate-related charges can be excluded from the finance charge under stated conditions. A fee's label alone does not decide inclusion. [P5-04, P5-05]

Suppose two fictional fixed-rate loans have the same principal, note rate, payment schedule and term. One charges more prepaid finance charges. Their note-rate payments may match while their APRs differ. That teaches why rate alone is incomplete. It does not justify adding a percentage of fees to the note rate as a compliant APR calculation. Use a validated calculation and the applicable accuracy rules. For comparisons, also consider cash required, the consumer's expected holding period and risks that an APR does not summarize.

A learner can explain an APR without promising that the lowest APR is always the best individual choice. Likewise, an APR change is not automatically an inaccurate APR. Later CD restart analysis uses the accuracy standard in Regulation Z, not simply whether the displayed digits changed. [P5-05]

### An application can arrive before a completed file

For the covered TRID transaction, six submitted items establish the application: the consumer's name, income, Social Security number to obtain a credit report, property address, estimated property value, and mortgage loan amount sought. If a consumer lacks a Social Security number, the official interpretation permits another unique identifier used to obtain the credit report. Verification documents, a signed purchase contract and underwriting approval are not extra prerequisites. Record when the last of the six arrived, including through an authorized intake channel. Do not replace that event with the date someone created the loan in software. [P5-06]

The initial LE must ordinarily be delivered or mailed no later than the third business day after application. That deadline uses days on which the creditor's offices are open to the public for substantially all business functions. Delivery or mailing must also occur at least seven business days before consummation; that waiting period uses all calendar days except Sundays and the specified legal public holidays. The two definitions cannot be interchanged. A timely denial or withdrawal within the initial period can implicate an exception; it does not erase independent ECOA duties. [P5-02, P5-06]

Before the consumer receives the LE and indicates intent to proceed, fees generally cannot be imposed, except a bona fide and reasonable credit-report fee. Acknowledging receipt does not itself necessarily express intent to proceed. A creditor may ask for documents, but cannot require verification documents before providing the LE. Record intent through a permitted process; do not treat silence as consent. [P5-02]

### Worked calendar: one file, three separate events

Harbor Pine Lending is open for substantially all business functions Monday through Friday. The case dates have no intervening legal public holiday. On Monday, September 14, 2026, it receives all six TRID elements. A processor enters the file on Wednesday 16. Pay stubs arrive Friday 18. The LE is delivered in person Thursday 17. An accurate CD is received in person Tuesday 22. There are no restart events.

The initial LE deadline is Thursday 17: Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday are the three creditor business days after application. Wednesday's software entry does not move the trigger. For the seven-day LE wait beginning with Thursday delivery, count Friday 18, Saturday 19, Monday 21, Tuesday 22, Wednesday 23, Thursday 24, Friday 25. For the CD wait, count Wednesday 23, Thursday 24, Friday 25. On these stipulated facts both waiting periods permit consummation Friday 25, assuming all other requirements are met. Saturday counts in the waiting periods even though the lender is closed. [P5-02, P5-06]

Now replace in-person CD receipt with mailing on Tuesday 22 and no evidence of earlier actual receipt. Presumed receipt is Friday 25. Count Saturday 26, Monday 28, Tuesday 29 for the CD wait. Mailing Tuesday does not permit Friday consummation under those assumptions. Electronic delivery must satisfy applicable E-Sign requirements; an email sent timestamp alone does not establish actual same-day receipt. [P5-02]

### Consummation is not automatically funding

Consummation occurs when the consumer becomes contractually obligated on the credit transaction under applicable state law. Signing, funding, recording and delivery of possession can occur at different times. Establish the legally relevant event rather than moving a disclosure deadline by relabeling the closing. A settlement appointment is a proposed schedule, not evidence that a legal waiting period is complete. [P5-06]

### Rescission is a separate right and clock

Certain consumer transactions secured by a consumer's principal dwelling carry a right to rescind. A residential mortgage transaction used to acquire or initially construct that dwelling is generally exempt from this rescission right. A same-creditor refinancing has a specific exemption with a new-advance qualification. Do not reduce these rules to every refinance has three days or every purchase has a cancellation period. A person whose ownership interest is encumbered may have rescission rights without signing the note. [P5-07]

For an ordinary covered rescindable transaction, the consumer may rescind until midnight of the third business day after the last of consummation, delivery of the required rescission notice, or delivery of the material disclosures. Here business day excludes Sundays and the specified legal public holidays. If all three events occur Friday, September 25, the ordinary period ends midnight Tuesday 29: Saturday is day one, Monday day two, Tuesday day three. This is after consummation, unlike the pre-consummation CD wait. Disbursement ordinarily waits for expiration and the creditor's reasonable satisfaction that the consumer has not rescinded. Defects and exceptions require review; the example is not a funding authorization. [P5-07]

### Your timeline case

For a new file, all six items arrive Monday, September 21, 2026. The lender's business schedule is Monday-Friday. There are no relevant holidays. The LE is delivered in person Thursday 24. The CD is received in person Monday 28. No exception, waiver or restart event applies.

Record four entries: the application event, initial LE deadline, earliest consummation under the LE waiting rule, and earliest consummation under the CD waiting rule. Then identify the later controlling date. Change the CD fact to mailing Monday 28, with no earlier receipt evidence, and recompute only the affected part. Keep delivery, presumed receipt and consummation in separate rows. For optional self-review, show the count and the definition used for each row rather than recording a date alone.

For optional self-review, explain whether any calculated date changes on these facts if the lender also opens Saturday for substantially all business functions. Identify which definition depends on that schedule and whether a Saturday falls within the relevant count. Check your draft for an unsupported definition or assumed receipt event, then revise in two or three sentences; no long essay is required. You may discuss your reasoning offline if you choose. This practice does not supply an instructor or peer exchange.

## Unit 5B Settlement charges, revisions and referrals

Proposed future OIL allocation, 40 minutes: instruction 18; legal case 10; peer/instructor dialogue and revision 8; formative checks 4.

### Follow the service and the money

RESPA section 8 and Regulation X prohibit giving or accepting a thing of value under an agreement or understanding for referrals of covered settlement-service business. They separately prohibit splitting a settlement charge other than for services actually performed. An agreement can be inferred from conduct; a referral arrangement need not be written. A gift, paid marketing expense or discounted service can carry value. Disclosing a prohibited referral payment does not make it lawful. [P5-08]

Payments for actual services and other specified permitted arrangements require their own conditions. Calling a payment advertising does not establish that the service exists or that compensation reflects reasonable market value. The value of referrals cannot be built into the valuation. An affiliated-business arrangement requires analysis of disclosure, required use and permitted returns; a common owner is neither an automatic ban nor an automatic exemption. [P5-08, P5-09]

Worked example: a title firm offers an unrelated mortgage lender's originator $90 for every covered loan referred to it. The originator is not the title firm's employee; there is no separate service or applicable exception. A borrower disclosure and an invoice labeled outreach do not change what is being bought. The originator should decline and escalate the arrangement through the firm's process. Contrast a proposed fixed-price advertisement with identified deliverables: you still need evidence of actual services, reasonable value and no payment for referrals before concluding that the arrangement is permissible. Do not infer compliance merely because compensation is flat rather than per loan.

### Classify a fee before calculating a tolerance

The LE good-faith comparison starts with the legally usable estimate. Typical categories are:

- General, commonly called zero tolerance: creditor or broker fees, affiliate service fees, required third-party services for which shopping is not permitted, and transfer taxes
- Ten-percent aggregate: recording fees and qualifying required unaffiliated services where shopping is permitted and the consumer selects a provider on the required written list
- Permitted variations: specified prepaid interest, property insurance, escrow and property-tax charges, plus qualifying outside-list selections and services not required by the creditor

These are abbreviated navigation labels, not substitutes for the rule. Paragraph (e)(3)(iii) has specific bona-fide-charge provisions, including for listed charges paid to an affiliate; therefore anything involving an affiliate is always zero tolerance is overbroad. Shopping must be real, and the provider list must meet the rule. A missing list creates a compliance issue but does not always turn an unaffiliated shoppable service into zero tolerance. An estimate without a percentage cap still requires good-faith diligence. [P5-02]

### Worked charge ledger

Assume all three services below are correctly in the ten-percent aggregate, all were performed, amounts are exact dollars, no valid revised baseline exists, and all other requirements are satisfied.

| Charge | Valid estimate | Final consumer charge |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Recording fees | $250 | $265 |
| Required unaffiliated title service, chosen from list | $800 | $920 |
| Required unaffiliated survey, chosen from list | $250 | $245 |
| Total | $1,300 | $1,430 |

The aggregate ceiling is $1,300 multiplied by 1.10, or $1,430. The title line rose 15%, but the applicable aggregate has not exceeded its ceiling. If the title charge instead becomes $960, the final aggregate becomes $1,470 and the excess is $40. Do not refund 10% of every fee, or treat each line as its own ten-percent bucket. Do not add an estimated service that was never performed to create extra cushion. [P5-02]

Now consider a separate zero-tolerance origination fee of $1,200 estimated and $1,260 charged, with no permissible revised baseline. A $60 decrease in an unrelated zero-tolerance fee does not net away this $60 increase. Keep each relevant test separate.

A tolerance comparison also does not prove a settlement charge is permissible. Suppose an appraisal is estimated at $700, the consumer is charged $700, and the provider actually receives $650 for the entire service. No lawful average-charge program or other service exists. The unchanged LE-to-CD number does not justify retaining the extra $50. Regulation Z's actual-charge provision is a distinct test. A genuine average-charge program has specific eligibility, uniformity, calculation and record conditions; it cannot be invented retrospectively for a convenient file. [P5-02]

### A changed fact is not a blanket reset

Permitted revision paths include qualifying changed circumstances affecting charges or eligibility, consumer-requested changes, rate-lock events for rate-dependent terms, and other specified situations. Determine the reason, affected charge and amount, when sufficient information arrived, and applicable timing. A clerical error or an invoice reflecting facts already available is not automatically a changed circumstance. A revision justified for one fee does not reprice every unrelated fee. For an aggregate charge, an event below the applicable aggregate threshold does not automatically reset the comparison baseline. [P5-02]

A revised LE used for the permitted comparison generally must be delivered or mailed within three creditor business days after receiving sufficient information to establish the reason. The consumer must receive it no later than four precise business days before consummation. A revised LE cannot be provided on or after the date the CD is provided. When a permitted event occurs too late for a revised LE, the CD route has its own requirements. These are separate clocks, not one three-day rule. [P5-02]

Worked decision: a lender initially omits its known $75 processing charge, then finds the omission in its own checklist. A colleague proposes issuing a new LE and using the higher amount because the borrower has not objected. The missing fact is not the borrower's objection. It is a legally permitted revision reason linked to that increase. Preserve the error and obtain the authorized correction decision; do not manufacture a changed-circumstance explanation.

### CD correction is not always a new wait

A corrected CD must be handled under the applicable provision. Before consummation, three specified changes require a new three-business-day waiting period: an APR that becomes inaccurate under the relevant accuracy rule, a specified change in loan product, or addition of a prepayment penalty. Other changes generally require corrected disclosure at or before consummation without automatically restarting the wait. A changed fee can affect APR; therefore fee changes never restart is also wrong. [P5-02, P5-05]

If the consumer pays amounts exceeding the permitted good-faith tolerance, Regulation Z provides a refund and corrected-CD process within 60 days after consummation. Do not describe that as a universal deadline for every legal defect or permission to knowingly overcharge at closing. The proper CD also needs supported amounts, payees and payment columns. An accurate subtotal does not cure a placeholder payee or failure to deliver the form to a required consumer. [P5-02, P5-10]

### Your settlement case

A proposed closing has a ten-percent aggregate valid estimate of $900 and final qualifying charges of $1,015. All services were performed; no permitted revision exists. Separately, the lender's own zero-tolerance fee rises from $800 to $845. An appraisal appears unchanged at $610, but the provider receives $585; there is no lawful averaging program or other service. Nothing has yet been collected.

Record the aggregate ceiling and excess, the independent lender-fee increase, and the separate appraisal issue. Explain why the three questions cannot be answered solely by comparing total cash to close. Identify the records and decision maker needed before release. Do not add a guessed remedy for a defect not covered by the facts.

For optional self-review, defend the baseline you used and test whether the source of an increase matters. As a separate hypothetical, consider a genuinely new borrower-requested change affecting one charge: identify the facts and timing evidence you would need before revising that charge, without inventing an amount or assuming a permissible reset. Leave unrelated analyses intact. A supported alternative is welcome; invented facts are not. You may compare your reasoning with the lesson or discuss it offline if you choose; no instructor response is supplied here.

## Unit 5C Fair application handling and notices

Proposed future OIL allocation, 35 minutes: instruction 15; legal case 10; peer/instructor dialogue and revision 7; formative checks 3.

### An application has more than one legal definition

Under Regulation B, an application is a request for credit made in accordance with the creditor's procedures. Actual practices matter. A completed application contains the information the creditor regularly obtains and considers for the decision; the creditor must exercise reasonable diligence to obtain it. This is different from TRID's six-item application. A missing property address can prevent the TRID trigger while an evaluated and declined request raises ECOA notice duties. [P5-12]

An employee who evaluates a consumer's information, decides the creditor will not approve, and communicates that decision cannot avoid the consequence by calling the conversation prequalification. Conversely, a general explanation of potential terms and the application process does not necessarily make every inquiry a denied application. Record what actually happened. [P5-12, P5-13]

### Use the notice that matches the decision

For the ordinary consumer-credit cases here, assume a creditor that received more than 150 applications in the preceding calendar year, and no special exception. A completed application generally requires notification of approval, counteroffer or adverse action within 30 days. Incompleteness involving information the applicant can provide requires timely action/notice or a compliant written incompleteness notice under the rule. That notice identifies the needed information, a reasonable response period and the consequence of not responding. Silence is not an express withdrawal. [P5-13]

If sufficient information supports a denial on actual credit grounds, do not use missing documents as a false reason. Adverse-action reasons must reflect the real principal factors. The notice must contain the required action, creditor, ECOA/enforcement-agency and reasons or right-to-reasons components. Generic references to internal policy are not enough. Use an approved notice process rather than improvising a legal form. [P5-13]

A notice is not a cure for a faulty underlying decision. If a debt was counted twice, ask the authorized reviewer to correct the analysis as well as the communication. Preserve both the original event and correction. Regulation B generally has a 25-month retention rule for the consumer application records addressed here, with different anchors and extended retention in specified circumstances. TRID has separate retention duties; do not use one law's period as a universal destruction instruction. [P5-14, P5-15]

### FCRA adds a different notice and access test

The Fair Credit Reporting Act regulates access to and use of consumer reports. A permissible purpose is required; knowing a consumer or having technical access does not establish one. Follow the firm's authorized consumer-report workflow and any applicable consent requirements. A report obtained for a credit decision is not available for unrelated curiosity or marketing. [P5-16]

When adverse action is based in whole or in part on a consumer report, FCRA notice requirements can apply alongside ECOA. The FCRA information includes the reporting agency's contact information, its lack of responsibility for the credit decision, and the consumer's rights to obtain a free report within 60 days and dispute accuracy. When a credit score is used, additional score disclosures apply. The reporting agency's score factors are not necessarily the creditor's actual ECOA denial reasons. A properly designed combined notice can address both laws, but simply sending a score does not do so. [P5-16, P5-17]

Risk-based pricing and mortgage credit-score disclosures have separate rules and exceptions. Approval at a less favorable price does not mean no consumer-report disclosure can be required. Identify the transaction and current rule rather than treating the adverse-action form as the only FCRA document. [P5-17, P5-18]

### Equal treatment and the purpose of monitoring

ECOA prohibits discrimination on specified bases, including race, color, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age when the applicant can contract, receipt of public-assistance income, and good-faith exercise of rights under the Consumer Credit Protection Act. Evaluate permissible evidence consistently. Reg B's current discouragement provision addresses statements directed at applicants or prospective applicants that the creditor knows or should know would cause a reasonable person to believe credit would be denied or offered on less favorable terms because of a prohibited characteristic. [P5-19]

The Fair Housing Act separately prohibits discrimination in covered residential real-estate-related transactions based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status or disability. The lists and scopes are not identical. Do not assume a state's additional protections disappear because they are absent from a federal list. PE06 practices these ethical and fair-treatment decisions in greater depth. [P5-20]

HMDA and Regulation C require covered institutions to collect, report and disclose specified mortgage information for public-policy purposes, including identifying possible discriminatory lending patterns. HMDA is not a loan-approval standard. Coverage, transaction exclusions and data instructions require current checks. Required demographic collection is not permission to price or deny on a prohibited basis. Preserve the difference between a consumer's response and a permitted observation-based entry under the applicable instructions; never fabricate an answer to make a field look complete. [P5-21]

For a covered first-lien dwelling application, Regulation B also requires notice of the right to receive appraisal/valuation copies and timely copies, generally promptly upon completion or three business days before consummation, whichever is earlier, subject to the rule's waiver provisions. A waiver of timing is not a waiver of the copy itself. Denial does not automatically erase the copy obligation. [P5-22]

### Your notice selector

File A: A consumer has asked for financing but has not identified a property. The creditor evaluates the submitted information, decides it will not lend because of documented recent serious delinquency, and communicates the decision. The decision relied in part on a consumer report.

File B: An ordinary application is missing an applicant-provided asset explanation. There is insufficient information for a credit decision. A colleague asks you to mark withdrawn because the consumer has not answered one call.

For each file, identify the application/notice rules to check, the triggering evidence, and the next responsible action. For File A, distinguish the ECOA decision reason from FCRA reporting-agency information. For File B, distinguish a proper incompleteness path from invented withdrawal. You are selecting and routing a notice process, not composing a legally sufficient notice for a real consumer.

For optional self-review, test the claim that no address means no application of any kind. Use one fact and one source to explain your conclusion. Consider separately whether changing the employee's response to a general explanation changes the result, and identify the evidence needed to support that distinction. Save a revised explanation for your own review or optional offline discussion; this is not instructor assessment.

## Unit 5D Recognizing specialized rule checks

Proposed future OIL allocation, 30 minutes: instruction 12; legal case 7; peer/instructor dialogue and revision 7; formative checks 4.

### Ability to repay and Qualified Mortgages

For a covered transaction, the creditor must make a reasonable, good-faith ability-to-repay determination. The general rule requires consideration of income/assets, employment if relied upon, the covered-loan payment, simultaneous-loan payments, mortgage-related obligations, other debts/alimony/child support, DTI or residual income, and credit history, with required verification. A large down payment or likely future refinance is not a substitute. [P5-23]

Qualified Mortgage categories provide specified routes to a presumption of compliance, with different conditions and protections. General QM is not synonymous with conventional, conforming or a universal 43% DTI ceiling. A non-QM loan is not automatically illegal or exempt from applicable ATR duties. Conversely, a product label does not prove the legal conditions were met. Use the current category, pricing, points-and-fees and underwriting requirements and applicable effective dates. [P5-23]

Worked screen: a fictional consumer can afford only an introductory payment. The originator says rising home value will allow refinancing before the increase. Record the future-payment issue and request the required repayment analysis. Do not solve a verification gap by obtaining a statement that the consumer accepts the risk. Disclosure and repayment analysis are distinct obligations.

### HOEPA and HPML are different screens

HOEPA high-cost coverage can arise from APR, points-and-fees or prepayment-penalty tests for covered principal-dwelling credit. It brings additional disclosures, counseling and substantive restrictions. HPML status uses a different APR-to-average-prime-offer-rate comparison for covered principal-dwelling closed-end credit and can bring escrow and appraisal requirements, each with its own exceptions. A loan can need one analysis without satisfying the other's definition. [P5-24, P5-25, P5-26]

For HPML classification, the current basic spreads are at least 1.5 percentage points for a qualifying nonjumbo first lien, 2.5 for a jumbo first lien under the specified Freddie Mac-limit test, and 3.5 for a subordinate lien. Use comparable-transaction APOR in effect when the rate is set, correct lien/loan facts, and the scope of the requirement being tested. Escrow and appraisal provisions contain additional rules; passing one exemption does not exempt every duty. [P5-25]

Example: stipulated covered nonjumbo first lien, applicable APOR 6.00%, APR 7.60%. The spread is 1.60 percentage points, so the stated basic HPML definition is met. That alone neither establishes HOEPA status nor tells you whether every escrow/appraisal requirement applies. These rates are fictional, not current market data. Annual dollar thresholds and points-and-fees tiers should be refreshed for the applicable year, not memorized from this example. [P5-24, P5-25]

### Compensation and advertisements

For covered loan-originator compensation, Regulation Z restricts compensation based on transaction terms or their proxies and addresses dual compensation and steering. A lender-paid label does not remove the rule. The fact that a borrower signs a disclosure does not authorize prohibited compensation. A proposal that pays more for placing a consumer in a costlier product requires review before it is offered or paid. Specific exceptions and permissible structures must be analyzed rather than generalized. [P5-27]

Regulation Z advertising rules require actually available terms, appropriate rate/APR treatment and additional disclosures when stated trigger terms are used. Advertising a particular payment or down payment can trigger repayment disclosures. A teaser payment must not conceal the later obligation or omit required context. A social-media post is not outside the rules merely because it is short. PE06 includes an honest-payment-message exercise; compliance review still must check the actual advertisement and applicable format. [P5-28]

### Privacy, security and suspicious activity

GLBA privacy duties and Regulation P address notices and sharing of nonpublic personal information, including applicable opt-out rights and exceptions. There is no general rule that borrower information may be shared freely once an application exists. Conversely, not every necessary service-provider disclosure requires the same opt-out process. Identify the recipient, information, purpose and applicable provision. Security safeguards are a separate obligation; a privacy notice does not secure an inbox. [P5-29, P5-30]

For financial institutions under its jurisdiction, the FTC Safeguards Rule requires an appropriate information-security program. Mortgage lenders and brokers can be covered. An employee should use approved channels, limit access, verify recipients and escalate an incident promptly under the firm's plan. The firm's compliance/security team must evaluate regulator and consumer notices; a learner should not invent a universal breach deadline or suppress an incident because no loss is yet proven. [P5-30]

FinCEN's rules require covered nonbank residential mortgage lenders and originators to maintain AML programs and make required suspicious-activity reports. Program elements include risk-based controls, a compliance officer, ongoing training and independent testing. A discrepancy is a reason to gather facts and escalate, not automatically proof of crime. Under the applicable SAR rule, reportability includes thresholds and suspicious-transaction conditions; the designated team evaluates them. SARs and information revealing their existence are confidential subject to specified exceptions. Do not alert a borrower that a SAR was filed or promise that one will not be filed. [P5-31, P5-32]

### Your rule-navigation case

A processor flags three issues on a fictional file: the proposed originator payment increases with the interest rate; a salesperson advertises a temporary payment as the permanent payment; and a bank-statement account number differs from the previously verified record. The consumer has not been accused of wrongdoing.

Create three short rows: relevant rule family, evidence still needed, and responsible reviewer. Keep compensation, advertising and identity/security issues separate. Explain why sending a general disclosure does not clear all three. For optional self-review, test one proposed action for unnecessary disclosure of private data. Revise any response that exposes documents in a discussion forum or announces suspected crime as established fact. Use only fictional facts and public process criteria; no instructor review or peer participation is supplied by this practice.

## Practical glossary

- Application trigger: the event satisfying the particular provision's application definition
- APR: credit cost expressed as a yearly rate under the prescribed calculation, distinct from note rate
- APOR: a benchmark derived from low-risk mortgage pricing; the applicable comparison depends on the rule
- Consummation: when the consumer becomes contractually obligated on the credit transaction under applicable law
- Good faith estimate: an estimate subject to the applicable disclosure, diligence, variation and revision rules
- Adverse action: a covered unfavorable credit action as defined by the applicable law, not simply any disappointing conversation
- QM, HPML and HOEPA: separate legal classifications; none is established by a marketing label
- SAR: suspicious activity report; confidential reporting rules do not replace factual internal escalation
