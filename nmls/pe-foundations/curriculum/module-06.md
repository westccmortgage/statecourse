## Unit 6A Apply consistent standards to individual facts

### Begin with the decision you control

A new originator may think ethics belongs only to the underwriter who approves or denies a loan. In practice, the originator decides what to explain, which questions to ask, whether to record an applicant's request, and what information to pass to others. A customer can lose a meaningful opportunity before an underwriter sees the file. A helpful tone does not repair an unsupported restriction hidden inside a friendly conversation.

Separate three kinds of guidance. A **legal rule** has an identified scope and authority. A **program or lender requirement** applies when that program or lender actually requires it and must itself be lawful. A **recommended procedure** is a way to perform the work reliably. For example, this module recommends a comparison note that identifies the relevant difference between two files. No cited federal rule requires a worksheet with that exact name. Conversely, a manager cannot waive a legal prohibition by approving an internal exception.

Ask four questions before imposing a different requirement: What specifically differs? Why is that difference relevant to this decision? Which applicable requirement supports the response? Would we respond the same way to another applicant with that fact? The questions organize your work; they do not themselves supply legal clearance. An answer such as “investor preference” needs an identifiable requirement rather than an assumption attached to a familiar label.

### Understand the overlapping protections

Regulation B implements the Equal Credit Opportunity Act. Its prohibited bases include race, color, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age when the applicant has capacity to contract, public assistance income, and good faith exercise of rights under the Consumer Credit Protection Act. Its definitions also address associations with other people. Certain regular referral and creditor selection activities fall within the creditor definition for the general discrimination and discouragement rules. [P6-01]

The general discrimination rule reaches any aspect of a credit transaction. Its interpretation includes unequal product explanations, additional documentation, and differently applied exceptions for similarly situated applicants. A final approval does not erase earlier prohibited treatment. A stated neutral reason can also be a pretext rather than the actual reason. [P6-02]

The Fair Housing Act separately covers residential real estate related lending. HUD identifies race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, and disability as protected bases. Familial status is not simply another term for marital status. The two federal frameworks overlap but are not identical; state and local protections can add requirements. This module does not treat absence from one list as permission to disadvantage a consumer. [P6-03]

**September 2026 source boundary.** The April 22 2026 Regulation B final rule became effective July 21 2026 and changed provisions on discouragement, disparate impact, and special purpose credit programs. Current section 1002.6(a) does not provide an ECOA effects test and its interpretation addresses intentional use of proxies. A statistical difference can warrant investigation; it is not taught here as a completed ECOA violation by itself. Other laws, applicable court orders, and actual facts require separate review. These exercises focus on concrete treatment and documented assumptions. [P6-04, P6-05]

### First contact must not replace evidence with a stereotype

Under the current discouragement provision, the issue includes a directed oral or written statement the creditor knows or should know would lead a reasonable person to expect denial or worse terms because of a prohibited characteristic. Do not substitute an older training slogan for that text. The current interpretation also distinguishes encouraging one group to apply from discouraging people who were not the intended recipients. [P6-02]

Consider a consumer who asks whether the company offers loans with a long repayment term. “We can explain the available terms and review your repayment resources” preserves a meaningful inquiry. “People your age should not bother applying here” substitutes a characteristic for individual facts. The appropriate correction does not promise approval. It gives accurate information and routes any actual eligibility issue to the authorized reviewer.

A small change in facts can change the needed review. If the consumer reports that the income they intend to use will end soon, the timing is relevant. Record the actual report and identify the evidence needed. Do not convert that specific fact into a policy against everyone of the same age or income category. An honest question about an identified payment stream is different from guessing what someone will earn based on appearance.

### Income and family questions need a defined purpose

Regulation B permits consideration of income amount and probable continuance, but prohibits automatic discounting because income is part time, pension, annuity, other retirement income, or connected to a prohibited basis. Evaluation must address the individual's circumstances. That protection does not make every reported amount verified, guarantee future receipt, or require approval. [P6-05]

Before an inquiry that may elicit alimony, child support, or separate maintenance income, provide the required explanation that the applicant need not reveal it if they do not want it considered. Questions about an obligation to pay support serve a different purpose. Questions about intentions to bear or rear children are prohibited; appropriately neutral inquiries about dependents and related financial obligations are distinguished by the rule. [P6-06]

Use this distinction when reviewing a script. “What income would you like considered?” requires the appropriate support income disclosure when applicable. “Are you planning a baby?” is not a substitute for asking about actual repayment resources. If an applicant voluntarily reports a forthcoming income change, preserve what they actually said without adding a prediction about their family or motivation.

An individually qualified applicant generally cannot be required to add a spouse's credit obligation merely because they are married. A signature needed under applicable state law to make collateral available can be a different matter. The instrument, purpose, and necessity must be established. Joint financial information alone does not establish joint application intent. Do not call a spouse a coapplicant after the fact just to satisfy a software field. [P6-07]

### Worked example W6A The extra condition

Fictional applicants Tessa and Grant seek the same product on the same day. Their relevant verified income, debts, credit, loan amount, property type, LTV, and documentation status are stipulated identical. Each applies individually and qualifies individually under the stated lawful criteria. Neither file has a collateral signature issue. Tessa is told to add a spouse because “women usually need someone else on the obligation.” Grant receives the ordinary process without that demand.

The observed difference is the additional personal liability requirement imposed on Tessa. The employee's recorded explanation supplies a prohibited basis for it. There is no unresolved credit distinction in the facts that supports the demand. The appropriate response is to preserve the instruction, stop using the unsupported condition, and route correction and incident review through the authorized compliance process. It is not to add the same unlawful condition to Grant's file in the name of consistency.

Now change one fact: a competent state law review establishes that a nonapplicant owner must sign a security instrument to make their property interest available. That can justify a properly scoped collateral signature. It does not establish that the owner must assume personal liability on the note. Ask what the signature does before deciding that any difference in signatures is discriminatory. [P6-07]

### Your case C6A The Juniper comparison

Lina and Owen request the same fixed rate purchase product. The supplied record stipulates the same pricing day, credit score, supported monthly income of $7,200, debts, LTV, property type, and eligible options. Their documentation is complete. Lina is a woman; Owen is a man. No other relevant difference or special purpose program is supplied.

Owen receives an explanation of two eligible options and the standard checklist. Lina receives only the higher upfront cost option and a request for two extra months of bank statements. The originator writes, “Women with young families will probably stop working; give her the safer choice.” Nothing in Lina's file reports a planned income change. Both files remain under review.

Write a five line decision record: the two treatment differences; the evidence of their basis; the unsupported assumption; the immediate corrective review needed; and one sentence to Lina that acknowledges the actual issue without promising approval. Do not infer how many children Lina has from the employee's remark or invent a regulatory remedy.

For a separate second round, use this supplied changed fact: Lina's statement, unlike Owen's, contains an unexplained transfer whose source is required under the same lawful funds policy. Explain which proportionate inquiry that fact supports and which earlier conduct it does not justify. A different fact can support a different request without making every other difference legitimate.

### Optional self-review D6A

Write the condition you would correct first and the specific evidence supporting that choice. Identify one fact that would strengthen or weaken your conclusion, then test the factual comparison and the scope of the proposed correction. Revise one line of your decision record, or explain why it remains supported. Compare against the lesson and public process criteria; a conclusion without reasoning is not a useful self-check. You may discuss your draft offline if you choose; instructor feedback and peer participation are not supplied here.

## Unit 6B Investigate discrepancies without manufacturing certainty

### Separate a warning sign from a finding

A discrepancy is a difference that needs explanation. A red flag is a fact or pattern that warrants attention. Neither automatically identifies who created a document or proves intentional fraud. Fraud can involve deliberate material misrepresentation or concealment, but its legal elements depend on the applicable law. This course does not make criminal findings about fictional applicants, much less real ones.

It is equally unsafe to ignore a material conflict until someone proves a crime. The pending decision may require reliable evidence now. You can say “the figures remain unresolved” and withhold a verification claim without saying “the customer committed fraud.” That middle position is active work: preserve, ask the right question, obtain authorized verification, and record the review result.

For this module, use a five part note: observation, affected decision, evidence needed, responsible reviewer, and present status. This is a recommended work method. Use the institution's actual approved process in practice. If a supervisor is implicated, use the designated alternative escalation route. Do not merely document an instruction to falsify a fact and then follow it.

Keep originals and identify later corrections in the authorized system. Preservation must follow applicable retention requirements and legal holds; it is not permission to keep unnecessary copies indefinitely. Regulation B has ordinary retention rules and circumstances requiring longer preservation. A correction is a dated, attributable change, not a backdated replacement that makes an earlier instruction disappear. The exact note format here is recommended rather than federally prescribed. [P6-23]

### Income records must describe the right person and period

For transactions covered by the general ability to repay rule used here, relied on income or assets require reasonably reliable third party records. They must support the individual consumer rather than an occupational average. A consumer can deliver an employer produced record; the delivery channel alone does not decide reliability. A conflicting upload needs review even when it arrived through an approved portal. [P6-08]

Start with the period, source, and type of figure. Monthly gross salary, net pay, a bonus, and a year to date total describe different things. Two numbers can differ without either being false. A new payroll cycle may also cross a raise date. Conversely, matching totals do not verify an employer or explain an undocumented alteration. State what you compared before deciding what the difference means.

### Worked example W6B A raise in the wrong period

A fictional file contains an employer record showing $3,100 gross per semimonthly pay period for August. A replacement record shows $3,600 for the same period. The application uses $7,200 monthly income. The customer says a promotion begins in October. A manager writes, “The annual amount is going up anyway; use the replacement as August proof.” Assume the creditor intends to rely on salary under the general ATR rule.

The initial annualization of the first amount is $3,100 × 24 = $74,400, or $6,200 monthly. The replacement annualizes to $86,400, or $7,200 monthly. The $1,000 monthly difference is significant to the record, but no percentage in this calculation is a federal fraud threshold. Neither the promotion report nor the manager's instruction establishes August earnings.

Preserve both records and the communications. Through the approved process, obtain reliable verification of August pay and the effective date and conditions of any change. Suppose that verification confirms $6,200 monthly for August and an October salary of $7,200. Record the historical and prospective facts separately. The underwriter must determine whether the prospective amount is usable under applicable requirements. A possible lawful use of future income does not turn it into pay already earned. [P6-08]

A suitable request says: “The two records for the same August period show different base pay. We need to resolve the historical amount and separately verify the reported October change.” It identifies the task without accusing the customer or claiming the higher amount is acceptable.

### Occupancy is a factual representation

Principal residence, second home, and investment property are not boxes to select according to which gives the best price. Identify the applicant's intended use, timing, property configuration, and the program's actual requirements. Fannie Mae's guide, for example, distinguishes occupancy categories and contains exceptions. It is program guidance, not a universal federal definition for all mortgage transactions. [P6-09]

A lease is a reason to read further. Does it cover the whole house, one unit of a duplex, a past period, or a tenancy that will end before the proposed occupancy? A tax return reports a historical period; it does not by itself settle future intent. A long commute might have an ordinary explanation such as remote work. None permits ignoring an actual contradiction, but none supports an automatic accusation without examining the relevant facts.

Consider an applicant who plans to live in Unit A of a duplex while Unit B remains rented. Rental income and an owner occupancy representation can coexist. Compare that with a current lease giving a tenant exclusive use of both units throughout the proposed move in period. The latter creates a different unresolved question. In either case, preserve the documents and route the program interpretation; never shorten a lease in the file or coach the applicant to report a fictitious plan.

### Resolve identity using identifiers and authorized evidence

A report may belong to the wrong person because of data entry, a mixed credit file, family name similarities, or identity theft. A customer whose information has been misused may be a victim. Compare the specific mismatched identifiers using approved procedures and restricted access. A surname, accent, disability, or unfamiliar household arrangement is not proof of identity misuse.

For example, the application names Devin Ellis Jr., while the report shows Devin Ellis Sr. and a different birth year. The supported conclusion is that association of the report with the applicant needs resolution. It is not that the younger person owns the older person's accounts. Reordering a report with the same unverified inputs may reproduce the same problem. Route the mismatch before relying on the disputed association; do not edit the report locally to make it appear to match. This is a recommended identity control, not a universal requirement to buy a new report in every dispute. [P6-08]

### Protect independent valuation

For covered consumer credit secured by a principal dwelling, Regulation Z prohibits coercion and attempted coercion aimed at substituting another factor for the valuer's independent judgment. It permits appropriate additional information, explanation requests, and factual error correction. Threats about payment or future assignments are not acceptable ways to obtain a desired value. The rule has separate conditions for creditor reliance after known violations and for reporting material appraisal failures. [P6-10]

A report states three bedrooms while the supplied floor plan appears to show four. Send the evidence through the approved review channel and ask for an independent explanation. Do not require an increase equal to a financing gap. An appraiser can correct a room count while leaving value unchanged. A contract price above the opinion is not itself proof of misconduct, and an appraiser's refusal to meet a target is not a reason to manufacture a complaint.

### Keep verification from becoming a privacy incident

Mortgage files contain information that can harm consumers if exposed. Privacy governs disclosure and information sharing; security governs protection against unauthorized access and use. GLBA addresses both. Regulation P's sharing limits have defined exceptions, so neither “we can share anything after a privacy notice” nor “every transfer requires an opt out” is accurate. Use the approved purpose, recipient, channel, and applicable notice or exception analysis. [P6-11, P6-12]

The FTC Safeguards Rule applies to covered financial institutions under its jurisdiction, including examples such as mortgage lenders and brokers. It requires a written information security program and includes access controls and encryption requirements with specified alternative control provisions. Coverage and limited small institution exemptions matter. This is not a claim that every mortgage participant is regulated by the FTC in the same way. [P6-13]

For daily work, verify the recipient before sharing, limit access to the task, use approved systems, and keep personal email and unapproved public tools out of the file workflow. A vendor logo or a coworker's request does not establish authorization. If you accidentally send records to the wrong recipient, promptly use the incident response process and preserve facts about what happened. Do not promise that deleting the sent message removes the exposure. Specialists determine applicable containment and notification duties; do not wait to escalate until a consumer reports harm.

Changed closing wire instructions need independently established contact verification. Do not rely only on a phone number supplied in the message containing the change, even if the message sits in a familiar email thread. If funds have already gone to a suspected fraudulent destination, urgent contact with the bank or transfer company is important; recovery is not guaranteed. [P6-14]

### Escalation and suspicious activity reporting are different steps

FinCEN has AML program and suspicious activity reporting requirements for covered nonbank residential mortgage lenders and originators. Their application is an institutional compliance question. Give the responsible compliance function a factual escalation; do not invent a universal rule to file a SAR for every typo or personally decide that a reported concern proves a crime. [P6-15]

A SAR and information revealing its existence are confidential subject to the governing exceptions. Do not tell an applicant that a SAR has been filed or use that possibility as leverage to obtain a signature. The underlying transaction facts and documents have a distinct treatment; confidentiality is not a reason to conceal ordinary factual questions from authorized reviewers. Follow the designated compliance process. [P6-16]

### Your case C6B The Willow investigation

A proposed purchase involves a two unit property. The application says the borrower will occupy Unit A. A lease gives a tenant occupancy of Unit B. A separate email from the sales partner says, “The tenant wants the whole property for another year.” The employer's September record shows $6,800 monthly salary; a replacement labeled September shows $8,100. No reason for the change is supplied. The credit report bears the applicant's name but a different birth date. The appraisal lists one unit. A supervisor asks you to copy the preferred facts into a clean summary and discard the conflicting versions.

Prepare four short entries, one each for income, occupancy, identity, and appraisal. Each must name the exact conflict or ambiguity, the affected decision, the evidence needed, and the responsible review function. Add one sentence declining the supervisor's proposed record handling. Do not decide who lied, select a final occupancy classification, or supply a target appraised value.

Then write a two sentence borrower update that identifies the next requested information and the actual review status. Preserve ordinary notice and disclosure obligations through the appropriate process; an internal investigation label does not suspend all deadlines. The objective is a truthful handoff that protects the consumer and the integrity of the decision.

### Optional self-review D6B

For optional self-review, consider a different first priority from the one you selected. Explain one risk of waiting on that item and one conclusion the available evidence does not yet support. Identify what a verified clarification for one issue would need to establish while the other issues remain unresolved; do not invent that clarification as an actual case fact. Explain which status you could then update and why resolving one discrepancy would or would not permit the entire file to proceed. Compare your reasoning with the lesson, or discuss it offline if you choose; no peer reply or instructor clarification is supplied here.

## Unit 6C Explain choices and resist conflicts

### Identify who benefits from the recommendation

A consumer depends on an originator to explain the options actually available through that business. Your employer may have limited programs, referral relationships, and compensation arrangements. Those facts do not justify inventing a marketwide claim such as “this is the cheapest loan anywhere.” Ask what the borrower wants to achieve, what costs and risks differ, and what evidence supports the recommendation.

Under Regulation Z's steering provision, an originator cannot direct a consumer to consummate a covered transaction because it produces greater creditor paid compensation unless the transaction is in the consumer's interest. The interpretation considers likely qualifying alternatives available through creditors with which the originator regularly does business. It does not require searching every lender in the market or always selecting the lowest compensation option. The specified violation includes consummation; a problematic recommendation should nevertheless be corrected before that point. [P6-17]

For a fictional comparison, two available likely qualifying loans have the same amount, rate, term, payment schedule, and relevant features. One has $900 less in upfront consumer costs. The other pays the originator more, with no offsetting consumer advantage stipulated. Suppressing the lower cost choice solely for that compensation advantage is a problem to correct immediately. A borrower signature on the remaining choice does not establish that the omitted option was explained.

Avoid an equally careless shortcut in the other direction. If one option has lower upfront costs but higher later payments, a consumer's needs and holding period matter. Document the real tradeoff. “Best” needs a stated comparison basis. An educational side by side summary should use common assumptions and identify differences rather than compare an introductory payment in one column with a later payment in the other.

### A referral label does not establish a lawful payment

For covered federally related mortgage settlement business, RESPA's referral prohibition concerns a thing of value exchanged pursuant to an agreement or understanding for referrals. Value can be noncash; the understanding need not be written. The regulation recognizes specified permissible payments and activities, including actual services, under their conditions. It does not authorize an outside provider to purchase referrals merely by renaming the payment. [P6-18]

Suppose a title provider offers an independent mortgage broker a $60 grocery card each time a referred consumer closes with it. There is an express referral agreement, no performed service, and no applicable exception in the case. Calling the card a thank you does not remove the referral arrangement. Six completed referrals would produce $360 in benefits under that agreement. Unchanged consumer pricing does not supply an exception.

Affiliated businesses require a separate conditions review. A relationship disclosure alone does not establish compliance: applicable required use limits and limits on things of value also matter. Do not assume every ownership distribution is a disguised referral fee, or that every payment labeled a dividend is protected. The actual arrangement controls. Route proposed benefits for review before accepting them; do not tell the consumer that their signature legalizes the exchange. [P6-19]

### Explain what a payment includes and when it changes

Principal and interest is not necessarily the total housing expense. Taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance, and association charges can be additional. Some are collected with a mortgage payment; others may be paid separately. A cost does not disappear because it is outside escrow. Estimates can change, and a fixed note rate does not fix taxes or insurance for the life of the loan.

For covered closed end dwelling advertisements, Regulation Z imposes additional disclosures when payment terms are stated. Its first lien provisions address excluded taxes and insurance and the greater actual obligation; prominence and proximity requirements differ for payment amounts and periods versus that exclusion statement. Other triggered advertising disclosures still require review. This lesson's arithmetic is not preapproved advertising copy. [P6-20]

Regulation N prohibits material misrepresentations in covered mortgage commercial communications, including about costs, variability, government association, approval, and the likelihood of future refinancing. Saying the borrower can certainly refinance later is not an explanation of today's repayment obligation. A future application depends on facts and conditions then. [P6-21]

### Worked example W6C The incomplete payment

An originator's draft says, “Your full monthly home cost is $1,640.” The stipulated figures are $1,640 principal and interest, $325 estimated taxes, $105 estimated homeowners insurance, $80 mortgage insurance, and a $65 association charge paid separately. Assume all figures are monthly and no other charges are included in this exercise.

The total of stated components is $2,215: $1,640 + $325 + $105 + $80 + $65. The difference from the headline is $575 monthly. If the first four items are collected together as stipulated, that collection is $2,150 and the separate association charge is $65. An originator should not describe either the $1,640 P&I amount or the $2,150 collection as the full $2,215 outlay.

A better educational explanation identifies each component and says which numbers are estimates and which are paid separately. It also checks whether any payment, mortgage insurance, tax, or other component can change. CFPB's disclosure explainer helps consumers distinguish projected payments and charges outside escrow. The actual loan terms and disclosures remain controlling. [P6-22]

Ask a comprehension question: “Which of these amounts would you pay outside your mortgage payment, and which could change?” That is more useful than asking whether the borrower understands everything. A mistaken answer identifies what to explain again. This is a recommended communication method, not a prescribed legal script or a claim that a teach back can replace required disclosures.

### Pressure does not create informed agreement

A legitimate deadline can be explained with its source and consequences. A fabricated deadline or a threat of losing a benefit that has not been verified is a different matter. Do not say a rate is locked, an approval is final, or a fee cannot change unless the actual evidence supports that status. A lock expiration may require prompt action, but it does not authorize altering documents or skipping the applicable process.

When a consumer asks for time or clarification, explain the verified options and any real timing constraint. Do not pressure them to sign blank or inaccurate forms or discourage them from obtaining advice. If you discover that an earlier statement was wrong, route a timely, clear correction to the affected recipient through the approved process and preserve the original. Whether new disclosures, a timing change, or other remediation is required needs its own review.

Your role is not to choose for the consumer or promise that an attractive projection will occur. It is to make the material facts usable and keep the decision process honest. A recommendation can acknowledge uncertainty: the customer may value lower upfront cash, but that preference does not make an omitted later increase irrelevant.

### Your case C6C Rewrite the payment message

The marketing team proposes this ordinary webpage message for a closed end first lien dwelling mortgage: “Only $1,820 a month, your all in payment, fixed forever. No need to compare. Sign today and we guarantee a lower rate refinance next year.” It is not a radio or television ad, envelope, linked banner, or pop up. You are reviewing the draft before publication.

The illustration states $1,820 monthly principal and interest for the full loan term. Estimated monthly taxes are $390, homeowners insurance $120, mortgage insurance $95, and association charges $75 paid separately. There is no evidence supporting a refinance guarantee or today's claimed signing deadline. A settlement provider also offers the outside mortgage broker a $100 gift card for each consumer sent to its settlement service. The broker is not employed by the provider, no work would be performed for it, and no applicable exception is stipulated. The arrangement is expressly conditioned on referrals in covered federally related mortgage transactions.

Calculate the total stated monthly outlay and the difference from the headline. Write a four sentence consumer explanation that identifies the components, distinguishes estimates and the separate charge, removes the unsupported promises, and names a useful next step. Identify the advertisement for a full required disclosure and placement review rather than declaring your rewrite ready to publish.

In a separate two line internal decision, address the proposed gift card and the unsupported deadline. State what evidence and review are needed. Do not tell a consumer to accept a product merely to protect a referral relationship, and do not offer a future financial outcome as compensation for present uncertainty.

### Optional self-review D6C

For optional self-review, identify the most likely remaining misunderstanding in your rewrite. Test a specific phrase against the supplied figures or a missing qualification. Defend the recommended next step from the consumer's perspective, then revise the wording or explain, with evidence, why your original choice is adequate. Keep the draft for your own review or discuss it offline if you choose. This exercise does not send or publish a message and supplies no instructor response or peer exchange.

## Before moving on

Review your three decision records. Each should identify a concrete fact, explain its relevance, acknowledge uncertainty, and name an authorized next step. An ethical response can protect access to credit while refusing unreliable evidence. It can disclose a useful option while recognizing that the consumer decides. The goal is not a reassuring green status indicator; it is a decision trail that accurately shows what happened and why.

Use the 16 public formative questions with this module when available, review feedback for every option you considered, and retry when useful. These are learning opportunities, not an official final or proof of participation time. Keep optional self-review notes with your case work if useful. ## Key terms

**Prohibited basis:** a characteristic or protected circumstance identified by the applicable discrimination law; the lists and exceptions differ by law.

**Similarly situated applicants:** applicants comparable on the legitimate facts relevant to the treatment being examined.

**Pretext:** an asserted explanation concealing a different actual reason.

**Discouragement:** the covered directed statements addressed by the current Regulation B provision; apply its exact scope rather than using the term for every disappointing answer.

**Discrepancy:** information that conflicts or does not yet reconcile; it requires an appropriate inquiry, not an automatic accusation.

**Material fact:** a fact important to the relevant decision; its precise legal meaning depends on context.

**Verification:** obtaining and evaluating evidence appropriate to the fact and governing requirement; an upload or a manager's preference alone is not verification.

**Occupancy:** the use of the property evaluated under applicable law and program requirements.

**Valuation independence:** protection of the valuer's independent judgment from prohibited influence, while permitting appropriate factual review.

**Thing of value:** a broad RESPA concept that can include cash, gifts, benefits, and paid expenses; the arrangement and applicable exception matter.

**Steering:** influencing a transaction choice; the specific Regulation Z compensation prohibition has defined coverage, consumer interest, and consummation elements.

**Nonpublic personal information:** a defined financial privacy term; identifying mortgage information must be handled under the applicable privacy and security framework.

**SAR:** suspicious activity report under the applicable BSA rules; its existence and contents are subject to confidentiality requirements.

**Teach back:** a recommended request that the consumer explain a material point in their own words so a misunderstanding can be corrected.
