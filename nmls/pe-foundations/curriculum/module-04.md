## What you should be able to do

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

- Distinguish conventional, FHA insured, VA backed, and USDA guaranteed financing without promising eligibility
- Compare actual borrower obligations rather than relying on a program label or advertised rate
- Explain what processing, underwriting, settlement, and servicing teams do
- Reconcile a simplified cash to close calculation without counting a deposit or credit twice
- Recognize a servicing transfer, escrow change, or payoff issue and identify the evidence needed
- Respond to payment difficulty without promising relief or encouraging missed payments

## Unit 4A Comparing mortgage programs

Proposed future OIL allocation, 50 minutes: instruction and worked example 25; applied practice 10; peer and instructor dialogue with revision 10; formative checks and feedback 5. Allocation includes active work and must be tested with learners.

### A program name is the start of a comparison

A mortgage program describes a financing channel and its requirements. It does not describe every feature of an individual loan. Two loans in the same program can have different rates, costs, permissible structures, and lender requirements. A borrower who asks for the lowest payment may actually need a manageable total housing expense, enough remaining savings, and protection from a payment increase. Those concerns require more than a label.

Begin by recording the purpose, property, expected occupancy, available funds, income documentation, existing obligations, and the borrower's plans. Then investigate plausible options using current sources. Eligibility information supports that investigation; it is not a substitute for an application, verification, or underwriting. A program can be available in principle while the proposed borrower, property, or transaction fails a particular requirement.

Do not import a colleague's remembered minimum credit score, maximum debt ratio, annual fee, or county limit into the discussion as a universal rule. Identify whether a requirement comes from law, a government program, an investor, or a lender. Record the version and relevant effective date. A lender may impose additional requirements within applicable law; an institutional requirement should not be described as a federal minimum.

### Conventional financing

Conventional mortgages are not insured or guaranteed under a government mortgage program such as FHA, VA, or USDA. They can be conforming or nonconforming. Conforming financing must satisfy applicable Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac requirements, including the relevant loan limit; being below that limit alone does not establish eligibility. A loan above the applicable conforming limit is commonly called jumbo. Other loans may be nonconforming for reasons besides size. [S1, S2]

Neither “portfolio” nor “nonconforming” means that consumer protection requirements disappear. A portfolio lender's decision to retain a loan does not itself determine its legal classification. Likewise, conforming status and Qualified Mortgage status answer different questions; do not treat the labels as synonyms.

Loan limits depend on the applicable year, location, and number of units. Use FHFA's current county resources rather than a national number copied from an older consumer summary. Ask whether mortgage insurance applies to the proposed conventional loan and how its cost is reflected. Do not promise that every conventional loan requires a large down payment or that every borrower can cancel insurance on demand. [S2, S3]

### FHA insured financing

FHA is part of HUD and provides mortgage insurance on loans made by FHA approved lenders. That insurance protects the lender against covered loss; it does not mean the government has approved a particular consumer's application or will make the consumer's monthly payments. The mortgage must meet applicable insurance requirements. Borrowers pay mortgage insurance premiums through lenders. [S4]

For an actual comparison, verify the proposed FHA transaction's current borrower, property, documentation, financing, and mortgage insurance requirements. Do not assume every FHA loan has identical costs or that FHA is exclusively a first time buyer program. The current Handbook 4000.1 index is the starting point for policy verification, but a newly published handbook can contain provisions with later effective dates. Check the provision that governs the actual transaction. [S5]

A useful borrower explanation is: “FHA insurance may make this financing option available through an approved lender. We still need to evaluate your application and property under the applicable requirements, and include mortgage insurance in the cost comparison.” This explains the benefit without describing insurance as guaranteed approval.

### VA backed financing

A VA Certificate of Eligibility, or COE, documents eligibility for the home loan benefit. A VA backed purchase loan also requires satisfaction of VA and lender credit, income, and other requirements, along with the applicable occupancy requirement. A COE alone does not approve the mortgage. VA backed purchase financing does not require PMI or FHA MIP. A one time funding fee may apply, with exemptions for qualifying borrowers. Other closing costs can still exist. [S6, S7]

Avoid the shortcut “VA means no cash needed.” A no down payment possibility does not erase closing costs, price and appraisal issues, entitlement questions, or lender review. Determine which benefits and restrictions apply to this borrower and proposed transaction. Funding fee exemption, if relevant, is a fact to verify rather than a status to assume from appearance or an informal statement.

### USDA guaranteed financing

The USDA Section 502 Guaranteed Loan Program works through approved lenders for eligible households purchasing or otherwise financing qualifying primary residences in eligible rural areas. Property location and household income eligibility matter. An address that looks rural is not sufficient evidence; use USDA's official eligibility resources and the applicable review process. Household income eligibility is distinct from deciding what income can support repayment. [S8, S29]

USDA also has a separate direct loan program. Do not interchange the direct and guaranteed programs' eligibility rules, application channels, or terms. Guaranteed financing may offer no down payment for qualifying borrowers, but that does not make the transaction cost free or remove credit and repayment review. Program guarantees reduce a lender's risk; they do not excuse the borrower's repayment obligation. [S8, S9]

### Compare the same transaction

Gather written terms for the same purpose, property, loan amount, term, and rate structure wherever possible. If a difference is intentional, explain it. Compare principal and interest, mortgage insurance or applicable program fees, estimated taxes and insurance, cash needed at closing, remaining borrower resources, and features that change later. State which amounts are estimates and which assumptions could change. Total monthly payment can differ from principal and interest alone. [S3, S10]

A low initial payment might reflect a smaller loan, financed costs, a temporary feature, or expenses omitted from the quote. Investigate the cause before calling one option better. A larger loan caused by financed charges is still debt. A permitted gift or assistance source also needs its actual terms reviewed; an obligation with deferred repayment is not automatically a grant.

### Worked example 4A Comparing a cash and payment tradeoff

Fictional borrower Mira receives two illustrative quotes for the same $285,000, 30 year fixed rate loan. The comparison assumes identical nonloan expenses and no financed fees. Offer Alder requires $8,400 of net upfront loan costs and a $1,925 monthly principal and interest payment. Offer Birch requires $6,400 of net upfront loan costs and a $1,975 monthly principal and interest payment. These are supplied exercise figures, not market quotes or program minimums.

Birch saves $2,000 initially but costs $50 more in each stipulated monthly payment. Dividing $2,000 by $50 gives 40 monthly payments. This is a simplified nominal payment difference comparison, not a full economic break even analysis. It omits time value, tax effects, the balance remaining when the loan ends, and costs not stipulated equal. It cannot establish suitability.

If Mira expects to keep the loan for only 18 months, the stipulated payment difference is $900; at 60 months it is $3,000. Those calculations help frame questions about liquidity and holding period. They do not authorize predicting a refinance, disregarding other costs, or selecting a program before eligibility is reviewed.

### Learner case 4A Eligibility evidence

Fictional borrowers Alex and Nia want a principal residence and are considering two properties. Alex has a COE. One property is described by its seller as “USDA eligible,” but no address check or household income evaluation has been completed. Their loan officer says, “Choose either government program and your approval is assured.”

In four short bullets, identify the unsupported promise, one VA verification still needed, two USDA eligibility facts to establish, and one cost comparison that remains useful. Then write one sentence replacing the promise without discouraging either option prematurely. Do not invent a credit score, debt ratio, fee, or loan limit.

### Optional self-review 4A Explaining the guarantee

Write a two or three sentence answer to the fictional borrower question: “If the government guarantees it, why do you still need my documents?” Explain whose risk the protection addresses and why verification remains necessary. Add why no down payment does not necessarily mean no cash to close. Check your wording for an unsupported approval promise, then revise it using the lesson. An under one minute spoken explanation or a discussion with someone you choose offline is optional. No peer or instructor response is provided by this practice.

## Unit 4B Processing and closing

Proposed future OIL allocation, 50 minutes: instruction and worked example 20; applied practice 20; peer and instructor dialogue with revision 5; formative checks and feedback 5.

### From information to an authorized decision

Processing organizes and verifies the information needed for the lending decision. Underwriting evaluates the borrower, property, and proposed financing against applicable requirements. Requests for additional evidence can arise because a document is incomplete, a fact has changed, or the existing information does not resolve a condition. Sending a document is not the same as having the condition accepted. [S11, S12]

Track the actual stage: requested, received, reviewed, accepted, or still unresolved. These are practical workflow descriptions rather than mandatory federal software labels. For each unresolved item, identify the issue, responsible person, evidence needed, and next follow up. A useful record might say, “Updated statement received; source of recent deposit remains under review.” “Bank statement done” hides the remaining question.

Prequalification and preapproval are not universally standardized promises. Lenders use these terms differently, and letters may contain conditions or expiration dates. Read what was evaluated and what the letter actually commits to. Do not describe a preliminary letter as a guaranteed loan offer merely because its title sounds strong. [S13]

### The people have different responsibilities

The loan officer explains options and coordinates the application. Processing staff assemble and verify supporting material. The lender's underwriting function evaluates credit and property information and makes the applicable decision through its authorized process. An automated recommendation does not justify ignoring inaccurate inputs, required documentation, or outstanding conditions. [S11, S12]

Title and settlement work addresses ownership, title matters, documents, funds, and the closing under the applicable local structure. The person conducting closing may be a settlement agent, escrow agent, or attorney. Practices and legal requirements vary by state. Title work and credit underwriting are related but separate: a title issue can block an otherwise acceptable credit file, and a clean title report cannot approve the borrower's income. [S14, S15]

A coordinator should not promise a legal title conclusion or instruct someone to ignore a recorded lien. Route title and lien questions to the designated title professional or counsel, and credit conditions to underwriting. Make the handoff concrete by stating what document or fact needs review. This is more useful than sending an unexplained message marked “urgent.”

### Closing readiness has several parts

Before representing a file as ready, distinguish underwriting conditions, disclosure requirements, settlement conditions, and authorization to disburse. A scheduled appointment does not prove these are satisfied. Signing, consummation, funding, recording, and possession can be separate events. Which event creates a legal obligation or permits a particular release depends on applicable law and transaction facts.

For transactions subject to the Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure rules, compare the latest accurate documents and resolve unexplained changes. The Closing Disclosure generally must be received at least three business days before consummation; coverage, counting, corrections, and exceptions belong to Module 5. The instruction here is to flag timing questions to the designated compliance reviewer, not to announce that a calendar date or every fee revision automatically restarts a waiting period. [S16]

Certain credit transactions secured by a consumer's principal dwelling have a federal right of rescission. A purchase money residential mortgage transaction is generally exempt. Not every refinance is rescindable: the same creditor exception and new advance rules, among other coverage details, matter. A second home is not automatically a principal dwelling. Do not promise same day refinance disbursement or a cancellation right merely from the word “refinance.” Route the actual transaction for the Module 5 analysis. [S17]

### Closing costs and cash to close answer different questions

Closing costs are charges associated with obtaining the mortgage and completing the transaction. Cash to Close is the amount the consumer must bring to closing, or in applicable transactions receive, after the relevant loan proceeds, deposits, credits, payoffs, and adjustments are accounted for. The down payment is not itself a closing cost. Money already deposited should not be collected again without the correct reconciliation. [S18]

Loan related charges, prepaids, and initial escrow deposits also serve different purposes. Prepaid interest covers the applicable initial interest period. An initial escrow deposit establishes funds to pay identified future bills. Calling an escrow deposit a lender fee obscures its purpose. Conversely, an initial deposit does not guarantee that future taxes or insurance costs will stay unchanged. [S18]

Use a sources and uses worksheet to test completeness, then reconcile it to the actual disclosures and settlement documents. Do not assume a seller credit can pay every category, exceed program limits, or become unrestricted cash back. Verify the credit's agreed amount, permissible use, and correct placement. If a disclosed total already includes a lender credit, subtracting that credit again produces a false result.

### Worked example 4B Reconciling the remaining contribution

Fictional borrower Luca purchases a home for $360,000 using a $324,000 loan. Gross borrower closing charges before credits are $10,800. This exercise expressly includes all relevant loan charges, prepaids, and initial escrow funding. A $9,000 earnest money deposit is already paid and credited. A $3,600 seller credit and $1,200 lender credit are stipulated permissible, approved, and fully usable. There are no other prorations, payoffs, financed charges, or adjustments.

The difference between price and loan is $36,000. Add $10,800 of gross closing charges to obtain $46,800. Subtract the $9,000 deposit and $4,800 total credits. Remaining cash to close is $33,000.

Check the other side: price plus gross charges is $370,800. Sources are the $324,000 loan, $9,000 deposit, $33,000 remaining borrower contribution, and $4,800 credits, totaling $370,800. The figures balance. That establishes the exercise arithmetic; it does not establish actual underwriting approval, lawful credit use, or permission to release funds.

Suppose the lender credit falls to $700, with every other stipulated fact unchanged. The remaining contribution increases by $500 to $33,500. The coordinator should reconcile the changed terms with the creditor and settlement team and route any disclosure consequences for review. Do not tell Luca the amount is unchanged because “closing costs were already approved.”

### Protect the handoff of funds

Borrowers should confirm the form of acceptable closing payment with the closing agent. “Cash to close” usually means a verified transfer or cashier's check rather than currency brought to the table. Verify wire instructions through an independently established trusted contact channel, especially when an unexpected message changes the account. A familiar name in an email is not verification. [S11, S19]

Provide an accurate status update when a condition remains. “The credit decision is conditional on the updated evidence, and settlement has a separate title question” is informative. “Everything is approved; sign now and we will fix it later” creates an expectation that the known facts do not support.

### Learner case 4B Two unresolved decisions

Fictional borrower Elena has a conditional approval for a $250,000 loan. The underwriter still needs acceptable evidence explaining a recent deposit. The settlement agent also reports an unreleased prior lien. Elena's closing appointment is tomorrow. An assistant has marked both items complete because the borrower emailed a bank statement and said the old lien was paid.

Prepare a five line status note: describe each unresolved issue, name its decision owner, identify why the received information is not itself acceptance, and state what cannot yet be promised. Add a one sentence response to an email changing the wire destination and asking for immediate payment. Do not decide the title question or compute a disclosure deadline from incomplete facts.

### Optional self-review 4B Answering a closing question

In two or three sentences, answer the fictional question, “My costs are $10,800, so why am I bringing $33,000?” Use Luca's figures to explain the down payment, deposit, and credits. Check that each amount is counted once. Explain why a balanced worksheet does not guarantee funding, and revise any wording that confuses arithmetic with condition clearance. Keep the response for your own review or discuss it offline if you choose; instructor feedback and peer participation are not supplied here.

## Unit 4C Servicing after closing

Proposed future OIL allocation, 50 minutes: instruction and worked example 25; applied practice 10; peer and instructor dialogue with revision 10; formative checks and feedback 5.

### Find the right company and the right record

The originator helps arrange the mortgage. The owner holds the loan interest. The servicer administers the account, including payments and applicable escrow activity. These roles can be held by different organizations. A borrower may reasonably return to the original loan officer with a servicing question, but that does not give the officer authority to alter the account. [S30, S31]

Start with the statement, transfer notice, escrow analysis, or payoff quote relevant to the question. Identify the claimed discrepancy and its dates. Keep payment receipts and correspondence. Do not tell a borrower a balance is wrong simply because it differs from a number remembered at origination. Equally, do not dismiss a mismatch without identifying its components.

### A servicing transfer does not suspend payment duties

For covered loans, servicing transfer notices identify the effective date, the new servicer, and when payments move. Regulation X generally requires notice by the old servicer at least 15 days before transfer and by the new servicer within 15 days afterward, subject to specified exceptions and combined notice rules. The transfer itself does not alter mortgage terms other than terms directly related to servicing. [S21]

The important payment protection is precise. During the 60 days beginning on the effective transfer date, a payment the old servicer receives by the applicable due date, including any contractual grace period, cannot be treated as late for any purpose. This protects a timely payment sent to the wrong servicer. It is not a 60 day payment holiday, and mailing late is not made timely merely because transfer occurred. [S21]

Follow the verified notice for future payments, check automatic payment arrangements, and examine subsequent statements. If an otherwise protected payment is marked late, preserve proof of receipt and route the error through the servicer's designated process. Avoid promising how quickly a credit record will change or assuming autopay transferred automatically. [S20]

### Fixed rate does not freeze every housing expense

A standard fully amortizing fixed rate loan can have unchanged scheduled principal and interest while its total payment changes. Escrow generally collects money for expenses such as property taxes and homeowners insurance. Different tax bills, insurance premiums, or permitted adjustments after account analysis can change the required escrow collection. Costs paid separately, such as some association charges, remain part of the household budget even though they are absent from the mortgage payment. [S10]

A shortage means the balance is below the required target; a deficiency means the account is negative. These are different account conditions. Escrow rules govern analysis, collection, statements, and the treatment of surpluses or shortages. Do not prescribe an arbitrary cushion, promise an automatic refund, or say every borrower can demand an escrow waiver. Actual account facts, applicable law, program requirements, and the agreement matter. [S22]

### Worked example 4C Explaining a changed payment

Fictional borrower Rowan's scheduled principal and interest is $1,700 monthly. The prior escrow collection was $400, making the stipulated payment $2,100. The new account analysis identifies $450 monthly for ongoing taxes and insurance and an allowed $600 shortage repayment spread over 12 months. Assume the analysis and repayment arrangement have been independently verified for this exercise; no mortgage insurance or other payment component applies.

Shortage recovery is $600 divided by 12, or $50 monthly. The new payment is $1,700 plus $450 plus $50, totaling $2,200. The $100 increase has two parts: $50 for higher ongoing escrow needs and $50 for shortage recovery. None of these figures indicates a changed note rate.

After the stipulated shortage is repaid, do not guarantee that the total becomes $2,150 forever. That would require ongoing escrow needs to remain $450 and no other adjustment to apply. Explain the current analysis, identify the temporary component, and direct questions about projected bills to the servicer. This exercise is arithmetic using supplied facts, not a rule prescribing every shortage repayment schedule.

### Payoff requires a date and a reliable quote

A statement balance is not necessarily the amount needed to satisfy the loan on a future date. A payoff includes applicable interest through the intended payoff date and can include other unpaid amounts. A permitted prepayment charge, if applicable, also matters. Request the current payoff from the lender or servicer, identify its good through date and instructions, and reconcile it with the contemplated closing. [S23]

For example, if a fictional statement shows $180,000 principal and the verified payoff supplies $420 accrued interest plus $80 of applicable unpaid charges, the stipulated payoff is $180,500. Do not substitute the principal figure or assume a remembered escrow balance can be netted against it. If payment will arrive later than the quoted date, obtain the appropriate updated amount or verified per diem instruction. Satisfaction of the debt and the required release of its lien must both be handled through the proper process; recording requirements vary by state.

### Respond early to payment difficulty

A borrower who expects to miss a payment should contact the servicer promptly and can seek a HUD approved housing counselor. Loss mitigation can involve different arrangements depending on the account and available programs. A forbearance may pause or reduce payments temporarily, but does not itself forgive the missed amount. A modification changes loan terms through an approved arrangement; it should not be promised before evaluation. [S24, S25]

Federal servicing protections have coverage rules and exceptions. Regulation X does not require a servicer to offer a particular loss mitigation option merely because a borrower requests it. Foreclosure rights and procedures also depend on applicable state law. An originator should recognize urgency, provide verified contact channels, and route legal questions rather than predict a sale date or tell the borrower that a submitted application automatically stops every proceeding. [S26, S27]

Be alert to a person demanding upfront fees, guaranteed relief, title transfer, or payments redirected away from the verified servicer. Preserve the message and help the borrower identify legitimate assistance. Never recommend intentionally missing payments as a shortcut to approval. [S28]

### Learner case 4C Transfer confusion

Fictional borrower Sam's covered loan transfers servicing on October 1. The payment is due October 1 and the contract allows a grace period through October 15. The old servicer receives the full payment October 8. The new statement shows a late fee for that payment. Sam also expects difficulty paying November's installment and says, “The transfer gives me two months off anyway.”

Give four short responses: explain the October payment protection using the supplied receipt date, correct the proposed payment holiday, identify evidence and the appropriate servicing contact, and explain the next step for the separate November hardship. Do not promise a modification or treat an unverified phone statement as a changed payment agreement.

### Optional self-review 4C Explaining the next step

In two or three sentences, answer the fictional question, “My rate is fixed, and you said my payment was $2,100. Why is it $2,200?” Separate Rowan's unchanged principal and interest from ongoing escrow and shortage recovery. Identify the record needed before asserting an error, and check that your response does not promise a permanent future payment. Revise against the lesson and public process criteria. An offline discussion is optional; this self-review does not provide or replace the genuine exchanges required by the proposed future OIL design.

## Working glossary

Conventional mortgage: a mortgage outside government insurance or guaranty programs. Conforming mortgage: financing meeting applicable enterprise purchase requirements. COE: VA evidence of eligibility for the home loan benefit. Underwriting: evaluation of borrower, collateral, and transaction eligibility. Condition: an unresolved requirement attached to a decision or closing step. Settlement agent: the professional coordinating the closing within the applicable local structure. Cash to close: the reconciled amount due from or to the consumer at closing. Escrow analysis: review of account funding and projected disbursements. Servicer: the company administering the mortgage account. Payoff: the amount necessary to satisfy the debt as of a specified date. Forbearance: an agreed temporary payment pause or reduction with repayment obligations remaining. Loss mitigation: processes and options intended to address delinquency and potential foreclosure.
