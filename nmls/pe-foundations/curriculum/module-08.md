## Unit 8A Build the file before choosing an outcome

### How one change can affect several decisions

A mortgage file is a collection of connected decisions. Income affects a qualification calculation. A debt can affect both that calculation and the amount available to close. A change in value can change the proposed loan amount, payment, borrower contribution and disclosures. A property concern may remain unresolved even after the borrower passes a numerical screen.

Begin with the consumer's request and the transaction's coverage. Then identify the particular rule that applies. A company checklist does not replace that analysis. In this lesson, use three labels:

| Label | Meaning | Example in this file |
|---|---|---|
| Law | An applicable statute, regulation or other controlling legal authority | Covered ability-to-repay and disclosure obligations |
| Program | A requirement of the intended loan program or investor | Fannie Mae treatment of personal unsecured loan proceeds |
| Company assumption | A fictional internal rule or assigned authority supplied for this exercise | The lender's 45.00% DTI screening limit |

The company cannot waive law by approving an exception. An internal policy decision also does not waive a binding program condition. If a source or delegation is missing, record that gap. Do not convert a familiar industry practice into a nationwide legal requirement.

Use four evidence states: **reported**, **received**, **reviewed**, and **accepted for a stated purpose**. Receipt of a PDF establishes that a file arrived. Acceptance needs an actual decision by the authorized reviewer. A record accepted for one issue may not answer another issue. For example, a bank record may prove a deposit occurred without proving its source is eligible.

### The Linden purchase file

Casey Linden applies alone to fictional Northbank Home Lending to purchase 14 Alder Court in a fictional U.S. locality. No real address or state is supplied. Casey intends to occupy the one-unit detached home as a principal residence. For this exercise, ordinary consumer-purpose, closed-end, first-lien purchase coverage under TRID, the general ability-to-repay rule, ECOA and the applicable RESPA provisions is stipulated. It is not a HELOC, reverse mortgage, construction loan or business-purpose loan. State contract, title and consummation questions remain assigned to competent review; the examples do not supply state-law answers. [P8-01, P8-02, P8-03, P8-04]

Northbank intends a conventional loan for possible Fannie Mae delivery. That intention is not a finding that every delivery requirement is met. The exercise uses selected current Fannie Mae requirements where identified. It does not claim FHA, VA or USDA eligibility, impose those programs' rules, or constitute a complete underwriting guide.

The following authority assignments are fictional company facts. Mina, the originator, collects and explains information. Pavel, the processor, checks completeness and routes evidence. Jules, the underwriter, decides the specified income, debt, funds and collateral conditions. Ren, the disclosure specialist, performs the identified disclosure and charge review. A job title at another company may carry different authority.

### Worked reconciliation W8A Income and the first payment

Convert the base salary before doing any ratio:

$3,300 × 24 ÷ 12 = **$6,600 monthly base pay**.

Add the accepted pension: $6,600 + $1,400 = **$8,000 accepted monthly income**. The application total of $9,000 is $1,000 higher because it includes the unsupported bonus. Keep the reported amount and the accepted amount as separate dated facts. Do not alter an employer record to match an application. Fannie Mae's income guidance requires the applicable history, stability and continuance analysis; the case's accepted amounts are supplied after that review. [P8-12]

For $360,000 at a fixed 6.00% annual note rate, 360 end-of-month payments and monthly compounding, P&I is $2,158.38. The standard formula is P × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)^−n], where P = $360,000, r = 0.06 ÷ 12 = 0.005 and n = 360. This is payment arithmetic, not an APR calculation or a loan offer.

Housing expense = $2,158.38 + $450 + $125 + $100 = **$2,833.38**. Existing other included payments = $420 + $90 + $240 = **$750**. Total DTI = ($2,833.38 + $750) ÷ $8,000 × 100 = **44.79%**. Use dollar inputs rounded to cents and round the final percentage to two decimals. At this stage mortgage insurance is assumed zero for the stated 80% structure; a different structure requires a fresh applicable review.

Northbank's fictional screen is a maximum 45.00% total DTI. This is a company assumption for the exercise, not a federal maximum, a universal Fannie Mae threshold, or proof of QM status. Passing it does not decide the general ability-to-repay determination or the full credit file. General ATR requires a reasonable, good-faith determination using the applicable factors and verification, not a ratio alone. [P8-03]

### A fair income review uses individual facts

An assistant writes, “Remove the pension because people receiving pensions should not be taking new mortgages.” That instruction is unsupported. Regulation B permits analysis of amount and probable continuance but does not permit automatic discounting of pension or other retirement income. The appropriate response preserves the actual record and sends the unsupported instruction for correction and compliance review. It does not promise Casey approval. [P8-05]

As of this lesson's September 30, 2026 source check, the Regulation B amendments effective July 21, 2026 changed discouragement, disparate-impact and special-purpose-credit-program provisions. The current discouragement rule addresses directed statements the creditor knows or should know would make a reasonable person expect denial or worse terms because of a prohibited characteristic. Current section 1002.6(a) does not establish ECOA disparate-impact liability. This lesson evaluates documented treatment and reasons; a statistical difference by itself is not its finding of an ECOA violation. Fair Housing Act protections, other applicable law and relevant court orders require separate analysis. [P8-06, P8-07, P8-05, P8-23]

A lawful review can still exclude Casey's unsupported bonus while accepting the documented pension. That distinction rests on the individual evidence and applicable requirements. Equal treatment does not mean counting every asserted income source, nor does it mean applying the same unlawful shortcut to everyone.

## Unit 8B Reassess the file when facts change

### Exhibit B The value and product decision

On Monday September 21, the completed appraisal and Jules's collateral review establish an accepted value of **$440,000**. The contract price remains **$450,000**. The appraisal copy is delivered in person that day, and the required valuation-rights notice was provided with the initial disclosure process. The example assumes the applicable copy requirements are satisfied; copies and valuation independence are separate from the underwriting decision. [P8-15]

Fannie Mae's ordinary purchase LTV method uses the lower of price and current appraised value, subject to its stated exceptions. No exception is stipulated here. The old $360,000 loan gives $360,000 ÷ $440,000 × 100 = 81.8181818…%, or **81.82% as a classroom mathematical display rounded to two decimal places**, rather than 80.00%. This is not Fannie Mae's delivery ratio. The guide requires truncation to two decimal places and then rounding up to the nearest whole percent: 81.8181818…% becomes 81.81%, then **82% for delivery**. Apply the actual guide method when evaluating the delivery ratio; do not substitute the classroom display. A contract price is not a direction to an appraiser to reach that number. A documented factual correction request can be appropriate; pressure for a target value is not. [P8-08, P8-14]

Casey requests a new **$352,000** loan to keep the mathematical LTV at exactly 80.00%, which is also **80% for Fannie Mae delivery**: $440,000 × 0.80 = $352,000. Price minus loan rises from $90,000 to **$98,000**, an $8,000 increase. That is a borrower contribution calculation, not a conclusion that Casey has the funds or must accept the arrangement. Actual contract rights are outside the supplied facts.

The September 22 comparison uses the same $352,000 principal and 30-year term. All prices and rates are invented. No discount points or prepayment penalty are assumed. The fixed loan is the requested path after the comparison; the ARM is a teaching alternative and has not been found eligible for this borrower.

| Feature | Fixed path | Illustrative ARM path |
|---|---:|---:|
| Initial annual note rate | 6.00% for the term | 5.25% for 60 payments |
| Initial monthly P&I | $2,110.42 | $1,943.76 |
| Rate after initial period | Unchanged | Adjusts annually after the first 60 payments |
| Stated ARM terms | Not applicable | Margin 2.75%; first/subsequent adjustment caps 2 percentage points; lifetime increase cap 5 points; floor 2.75% |
| Adverse scenario | Fixed P&I remains $2,110.42 | At first adjustment, illustrative index 4.50% + margin 2.75% = 7.25%; within the first-adjustment cap |

An ARM's index, margin and caps govern its adjustments; its initial payment does not describe every future payment. Taxes, insurance and dues can also change independently of either note rate. A projected refinance is not assured. The CFPB ARM booklet is a consumer reference for these mechanics. [P8-24]

**Worked payment path.** Using full precision for the theoretical ARM amortization, the balance after 60 payments is $324,366.20. The balance formula is P(1+r)^k − M[(1+r)^k − 1]/r, with P = 352,000, r = 0.0525/12, k = 60 and unrounded M = 1,943.75703153948. Reamortizing that unrounded balance over 300 payments at 7.25% gives **$2,344.54 P&I**. The displayed increase is **$400.78**, approximately **20.62%**, compared with the initial ARM P&I. Keeping the example's $675 housing add-ons produces $3,019.54 total housing expense at that reset.

This is a scenario, not a forecast, maximum lifetime payment or regulatory qualifying-payment calculation. Rounding an actual loan's scheduled payments each month can create a small balance difference. The creditor must use the actual product and the applicable ATR/program payment method; a teaser payment or this illustration cannot replace it. [P8-03]

Explain a tradeoff without choosing for Casey: “The ARM begins $166.66 below the fixed loan's monthly principal and interest. Under the stated first-reset scenario, its P&I becomes $2,344.54. We still need the actual loan terms, costs and applicable repayment review, and taxes and insurance are additional.” Compensation-driven steering restrictions remain relevant; a lower displayed starting payment is not a reason to conceal another available option. [P8-20]

### Recalculate the chosen fixed path

The revised fixed P&I is $352,000 × 0.005 ÷ [1 − 1.005^−360] = **$2,110.42**. Housing = $2,110.42 + $675 = **$2,785.42**. With the accepted $8,000 income and existing $750 obligations, total DTI = $3,535.42 ÷ $8,000 = **44.19%**. The monthly numerator fell $47.96 while the needed price contribution rose $8,000. A smaller payment does not necessarily mean less cash is needed now.

On Thursday September 24 Casey receives the initial Closing Disclosure in person for the revised fixed loan. The ordinary CD wait counts Friday September 25, Saturday September 26 and Monday September 28. Thus the later of the initial LE and CD waiting-period results is **September 28**. The appointment is Tuesday September 29. This timing comparison does not clear underwriting or title. Consummation means becoming contractually obligated under applicable state law; it is not automatically identical to recording or funding. [P8-01, P8-02]

### Exhibit C The late deposit explanation

On Friday September 25, B2 arrives: a signed third-party personal loan agreement identifies the $18,000 transfer as an unsecured installment loan with a required **$380 monthly payment** and more than ten payments remaining. Casey had described the deposit as a “gift from savings” in a later message. Neither description should be silently deleted. The agreement and the message conflict and require a factual clarification. There is no finding of intentional fraud in this exercise.

Fannie Mae does not accept personal unsecured loan proceeds for down payment, closing costs or reserves. Its liability guidance also requires analysis of relevant obligations and a recalculation when additional liabilities are discovered through closing. The fact that the loan did not appear on the earlier credit report does not erase it. [P8-09, P8-10, P8-13]

Use two calculations because the deposit has two effects:

1. **Funds before payoff:** $122,000 ending balance − $18,000 unacceptable source = **$104,000 otherwise supported funds**, assuming the remaining money has been verified and is eligible
2. **DTI while the loan remains payable:** ($2,785.42 + $750 + $380) ÷ $8,000 × 100 = **48.94%**

The ratio now exceeds Northbank's fictional 45.00% screen. The correct recommendation is to hold the affected release decision for re-underwriting. It is not an automatic ECOA denial notice, and it is not an instruction to increase income or hide debt. The authorized decision and notice process must determine the actual disposition. [P8-21]

Casey asks whether repayment could change the result. Staff can explain the need for review without promising it. Paying off debt for qualification must be evaluated under the applicable program and full file. An intention to repay is not evidence of payoff. Where a payoff changes usable assets, update both sides; do not subtract a debt payment without also accounting for the money spent. [P8-11]

### Worked revision W8B Payoff evidence arrives

On Monday September 28, Casey elects to repay the personal loan. On Tuesday September 29 at 09:00, Jules receives and accepts reliable third-party evidence that the debt is fully paid, no monthly payment remains, and the verified account balance is now **$104,000**. The exercise stipulates no payoff charge or other intervening cash movement. Jules documents acceptance of excluding the $380 payment for this file after the required review.

DTI returns to **44.19%**. The real account balance now reflects the $18,000 outflow. Do **not** subtract the $18,000 again from $104,000; it is no longer in that balance. Equally, do not use the old $122,000 balance when calculating remaining funds. The same dollars cannot be spent on payoff and retained as closing money.

The revised analysis establishes the supplied ratio and funds facts. It does not erase the September 25 discovery or establish that every credit condition is complete. Preserve the earlier result and date the new decision. Preserve records under the applicable retention requirements and any legal hold, not an invented deletion date. [P8-27] If an automated underwriting result predates material corrected inputs, route the required reassessment. Fannie Mae's DU findings do not relieve the lender of responsibility for accurate data and applicable requirements. [P8-22]

Write separate September 25 and September 29 records. Each needs the relevant document, income denominator, included debts, DTI, usable asset amount and responsible reviewer. For September 25, state the next evidence needed. For September 29, identify exactly what new evidence supports changing the record.

A revised LE cannot replace the CD after the CD has been provided. If one of those three findings changes, review the waiting period again. A manager's urgency does not supply an emergency waiver. [P8-02]

## A later refinance variation

This is a separate hypothetical continuation using law checked September 30, 2026, not a representation about future rule changes or the main file's actual completion. Assume Casey later owns and occupies the home and requests a consumer-purpose refinance from a **different creditor**. Stipulate that the transaction is subject to the ordinary rescission rule, no exemption or waiver applies, and the required consumers and disclosures have been correctly identified. Do not infer rescission coverage from the word refinance alone.

For a calendar exercise, consummation occurs Monday October 5, 2026, and every consumer entitled to rescind receives the required notices and all material disclosures that day. The three counted business days are October 6, 7 and 8. The ordinary period ends **midnight Thursday October 8**. The creditor may not disburse the funds described by the rule until the period has expired and it is reasonably satisfied that no consumer has rescinded. Under these assumptions the earliest possible release is **Friday October 9**, subject to the remaining requirements. [P8-16]

This differs from the original purchase: a covered residential mortgage transaction financing acquisition is exempt from that rescission right. It also differs from the CD wait, which precedes consummation. In the variation, assume all applicable pre-consummation disclosure waits were separately satisfied. They do not replace the post-consummation rescission review. The triggering event for rescission is the last of consummation, delivery of the required rescission notice and delivery of the material disclosures, not necessarily signing alone. [P8-16]

### Refinance funds and the date on the payoff

The variation's proposed new principal is $360,000. The old servicer's payoff quote is **$352,250 good through October 9**, while a recent statement shows **$351,000 principal**. The payoff quote, not principal alone, supplies the exercise payoff. Assume $5,600 closing charges and $1,650 prepaids/escrow are paid from proceeds; no other uses, credits or cash adjustment apply. Then $360,000 − $352,250 − $5,600 − $1,650 = **$500 remaining proceeds**. This arithmetic does not classify a Fannie Mae refinance or determine permissible cash back.

Now change only the payoff-receipt date. Assume the quote expressly adds **$58.60 for each calendar day after October 9 through the date the payoff is received** and there are no other changes. Receipt on October 13 adds four calendar days: October 10, 11, 12 and 13. Added interest = 4 × $58.60 = **$234.40**. Updated payoff = **$352,484.40**, leaving **$265.60** under the same simplified uses. The quote's daily accrual convention is a supplied contract assumption; it is not the rescission business-day definition. The authorized team must obtain or confirm a current usable quote and reconcile the actual documents. [P8-26]

A borrower may later receive an escrow refund from the old servicer. Do not count an expected future refund as money already available for this settlement. Existing escrow handling has its own rules and actual record. The consumer needs to know which amounts are due now, which are estimates and which may arrive later. [P8-30]

## A reusable decision checklist

Before giving a mortgage-file status, work through these questions:

1. What action is actually being considered, and what transaction coverage is established?
2. Which facts are reported, received, reviewed or accepted, and by whom?
3. Which source controls: law, this program, this company policy or a supplied exercise assumption?
4. Which number changed, and what other calculations or documents use it?
5. Have income, liabilities, LTV, monthly housing expense and remaining funds been reconciled without double counting?
6. Are the application, delivery, receipt, consummation, payoff and release events separately recorded?
7. Does the actual evidence support consistent individual treatment and an accurate consumer explanation?
8. Who can make the outstanding decision, and what evidence still needs to reach that person?
9. Does the latest status describe the current record without manufacturing approval, denial, funding or completion?

For a useful optional final self-review, revisit your first response. Name one changed fact, identify the line of your decision that changed because of it and explain why. If no revision is needed, defend the original line with its source. Repeating a correct answer can reinforce learning, but no practice attempt creates regulatory credit.

## Glossary

**Accepted fact:** Evidence an authorized reviewer has found adequate for a particular decision; not a promise that every condition is cleared.

**APR:** A regulated cost-of-credit measure distinct from the note interest rate. The payment examples here do not calculate disclosure APR. [P8-29]

**Cash to close:** The remaining amount the borrower must provide for the transaction after the relevant loan proceeds, deposit, credits and adjustments.

**Condition:** A stated requirement that still needs an appropriate disposition before an approval or release can be relied upon.

**Consummation:** The point when the consumer becomes contractually obligated on the credit transaction under applicable state law.

**DTI:** Included monthly debt obligations divided by accepted monthly income. The included obligations and accepted income must fit the governing method.

**Escrow account:** An account used for specified property charges, such as taxes and insurance, under the applicable agreement and rules.

**LTV:** Loan amount divided by the value determined under the applicable program's method. Price and value are not interchangeable when the method distinguishes them.

**Overlay:** An additional lender or investor requirement beyond a referenced program baseline; it does not override law.

**P&I:** Principal and interest. It excludes the taxes, insurance, dues and other expenses outside that calculation.

**Payoff quote:** A dated amount required to satisfy an obligation under the stated terms; it can differ from unpaid principal.

**Rescission:** A right to cancel certain covered transactions within the applicable period; it is not a universal right for every mortgage.

**Re-underwriting:** Review of the credit analysis after relevant new or corrected facts, using the actual applicable requirements.

**Versioned decision:** A dated record showing the evidence used, decision maker, scope and later changes without erasing the earlier record.
