# PE07 Nontraditional Mortgage Products

StateCourse | Public self-guided noncredit lesson | Sources checked September 30 2026

A small first payment can hide a large future obligation. This module teaches you to trace what happens to the rate, payment, balance, and final due date, then connect those mechanics to repayment evidence and an honest consumer explanation. You will calculate two payment paths, identify a missing underwriting fact, and practice explaining the adverse scenario without predicting the market.

## Unit 7A Follow the contractual obligation

### Three classifications answer different questions

First identify the program or funding family. A conventional mortgage is not federally insured or guaranteed. A conforming conventional mortgage must satisfy the applicable Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac purchase requirements; being conventional does not prove it is conforming. An FHA mortgage is government-insured, while a VA-backed mortgage carries a government guaranty. Government backing does not establish the applicant's credit decision. A VA Certificate of Eligibility establishes eligibility for the benefit, while credit, income, occupancy, and lender requirements remain. [P7-01, P7-02, P7-22]

Next identify the payment structure: fixed or adjustable rate; amortizing, interest-only, or potentially negatively amortizing payments; and a maturity that may include a balloon. These features can overlap. A conventional loan can be an ARM. A fixed-rate loan can have an interest-only period. An ARM need not have either interest-only payments or negative amortization. Fannie Mae has specific ARM requirements, which would be pointless if every ARM were outside the conventional agency market. [P7-03]

Finally identify the applicable legal classification and coverage. Qualified Mortgage is a defined legal classification, with requirements that depend on the QM route. Non-QM is not a synonym for nontraditional, unlawful, business-purpose, or exempt. Neither jumbo size nor alternative documentation alone answers every QM question. A portfolio loan describes where a loan is held; it does not decide the legal rules or its payment behavior. For a covered consumer dwelling-secured loan, the non-QM label does not remove the required ability-to-repay analysis. General QM criteria restrict payment features such as interest-only periods and negative amortization. Specific balloon QM routes have their own conditions. Do not turn these distinctions into a claim that every non-QM loan has risky payment features or that every balloon is unlawful. [P7-04]

The 2006 interagency nontraditional-product guidance focuses on principal or interest deferral, notably interest-only loans and payment-option ARMs. Its scope is narrower than the educational subjects in NMLS's PE topic guidance, which includes ARM features and qualified/non-qualified programs. The older supervisory document is useful for understanding risk layering and communication; it is not a substitute for current law or evidence of an NMLS allocation approval. Its preamble expressly says the guidance does not establish a securities-style suitability standard. HELOCs and reverse mortgages are outside that guidance’s principal product scope; a simultaneous second-lien HELOC can still be relevant to its risk analysis. In this course, evaluating fit means examining the consumer's stated needs, factual repayment resources, material risks, and applicable duties without promising that a product is suitable for a real applicant. [P7-05, P7-06]

### Read the ARM terms before calculating

An ARM note identifies the index, margin, adjustment dates, applicable lookback and rounding rules, and any caps or floor. The index is the variable reference. The margin is the contract's addition to that index. A first-adjustment cap can differ from a later periodic cap, while a lifetime ceiling limits the rate over the loan's life. These are different limits. An interest-rate cap limits a rate change; a payment cap limits a payment change and may not prevent unpaid interest or a later recast. Read the actual obligation rather than infer details from a marketing label such as 5/6. [P7-07, P7-08]

Use a four-step worksheet: identify the reset date and contractual index observation; add the margin; apply the actual caps, floor, rounding, and any other contract provisions; then calculate the payment using the appropriate outstanding balance and remaining term. A fully indexed rate used in an origination qualification rule is not necessarily the rate that will be observed years later. Do not substitute a forecast for either calculation.

An adjustment interval also matters. Two contracts may use the same index and margin but observe that index on different dates or adjust at different intervals. A cap of two percentage points means a move from 6% to 8%, not a 2% relative increase to 6.12%. A rate floor may keep the rate from falling as far as index-plus-margin alone suggests. None of those facts tells you the dollar payment without the balance and remaining term. [P7-07, P7-08]

### Worked example W7A A fully amortizing ARM

Assume $240,000 borrowed, a 30-year term, 6.00% annually for the first 60 monthly payments, and monthly accrual at annual rate divided by 12. Payments fully amortize the loan. There are no extra principal payments, fees added to principal, or missed payments. At the first reset, the contract adds a 2.50 percentage-point margin to the observed index; the first increase is capped at 2 percentage points. For this simplified exercise, there is no additional rounding or carryover, and the floor and lifetime cap do not further restrict the calculated first reset.

For a balance B, monthly rate r, and n payments, Payment = B × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)^(-n)]. At 6.00%, r = 0.005 and n = 360. Initial P&I is $1,438.92. Keeping full precision internally, balance after 60 payments = B × (1 + r)^60 − Payment × [(1 + r)^60 − 1] ÷ r = $223,330.46. The reset payment uses that balance and 300 remaining months, not the original $240,000 and another 360 months.

| First-reset scenario | Contract rate calculation | New monthly P&I |
|---|---|---:|
| Observed index 5.00% | 5.00% + 2.50% = 7.50%, below the 8.00% first-reset ceiling | $1,650.39 |
| Adverse index 6.50% | 6.50% + 2.50% = 9.00%, limited to 8.00% at this reset | $1,723.70 |

The adverse first-reset payment rises by $284.78, or 19.79%, from the initial P&I. It is a scenario, not a rate forecast, lifetime maximum payment, regulatory qualifying payment, or complete household cost. Taxes, insurance, dues, and any other required payments are outside this arithmetic. The balance has fallen despite the ARM label. For a reasonableness check, the first month’s interest is $240,000 × 0.005 = $1,200; the initial payment therefore includes about $238.92 of principal. At reset, using another 360 payments would artificially extend the term and understate the stipulated 300-month payment. [P7-07; original W7A assumptions and calculation]

### Interest-only and balloon are different obligations

During an interest-only period, a payment that covers all accrued interest does not reduce principal. Provided there are no new advances, capitalized amounts, or voluntary principal payments, the principal stays unchanged. Later amortization must repay it over a shorter remaining term. That creates a possible payment increase even at an unchanged rate. A balloon instead leaves a substantial amount due at maturity. A payment calculated on a 30-year amortization schedule does not create a 30-year legal term when the note matures sooner. [P7-19, P7-10]

A balloon borrower needs to understand the actual maturity amount and the uncertainty of obtaining cash, selling, or refinancing. Those future options depend on facts not guaranteed today. An amortizing payment before maturity does not remove the balloon. For an interest-only balloon, the principal may remain entirely due at maturity; for a partly amortizing balloon, calculate the remaining balance under the stipulated schedule. [P7-08, P7-10]

### Worked example W7A2 Principal deferral and rate shock

Consider a separate $240,000 loan with a 30-year total term and a five-year interest-only period. The fixed rate is 6.00%. All 60 interest-only payments are timely, and no principal is paid or added. Interest = $240,000 × 0.06 ÷ 12 = $1,200 monthly. After 60 payments the principal is still $240,000; $72,000 of interest paid is not principal reduction.

From payment 61, amortizing $240,000 over the remaining 300 months at 6.00% requires $1,546.32 P&I. The increase is $346.32, or 28.86%, with no rate increase. In a separately stipulated adjustable version whose lawful contractual reset is 8.00% at that transition, the 300-month payment would be $1,852.36, a 54.36% increase over $1,200. The 8.00% is a sensitivity assumption, not a prediction or an assertion of a contractual maximum. [P7-04, P7-09; original W7A2 arithmetic]

### A balance can grow despite timely payments

Negative amortization occurs when the required payment is below accrued interest and the unpaid interest is added to principal. A $100,000 illustrative balance accruing at 7.20% annually, or 0.60% monthly, with a $400 monthly payment would develop as follows. Assume capitalization each month and no fees, advances, or other changes. Carry full precision and round only the displayed values.

| Month | Opening balance | Interest | Payment | Closing balance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $100,000.00 | $600.00 | $400.00 | $100,200.00 |
| 2 | $100,200.00 | $601.20 | $400.00 | $100,401.20 |
| 3 | $100,401.20 | $602.41 | $400.00 | $100,603.61 |

The recurrence is new balance = old balance × 1.006 − $400. Under those unchanged assumptions, month 12 ends at $102,480.81. This is a mechanical illustration, not a currently available product or a permitted underwriting method. A balance cap, payment cap, rate cap, and recast provision address different things. When amortization begins, a larger balance and fewer remaining payments can combine with a higher rate to raise the payment. [P7-11]

### Worked comparison W7A3 An amortizing balloon

Use W7A’s initial $240,000, 6% rate, and 30-year amortization calculation, but change the legal maturity to immediately after the 60th regular monthly payment. Assume a fixed rate throughout this separate five-year loan and no fees or other payoff amounts. The regular payment remains $1,438.92, yet $223,330.46 of principal remains after payment 60. That remaining principal is then due under this exercise’s balloon terms. If the contract instead bundles the final regular installment with the balloon, the displayed final payment must reflect both; a payoff quote may also contain accrued interest and other lawful amounts. The balance formula and the note’s due-date convention must agree. [P7-10; original W7A3 assumptions]

A five-year balloon and five years of interest-only payments can both end with a large obligation, but their balances differ. The amortizing balloon has reduced principal by $16,669.54; the interest-only example has reduced it by zero. Asking only for the lowest payment misses this difference.

### Your case C7A Two payment paths

Use a new $180,000 loan, a 30-year total term, 6.00% fixed annual rate, and 60 initial interest-only payments. Use the same monthly-accrual convention and no principal additions or reductions. Calculate the initial interest payment and balance after payment 60. Calculate the fully amortizing payment for the remaining 300 months at (a) 6.00% and (b) a separately stipulated 8.00% reset. Show each dollar increase and percentage increase from the interest-only payment. Explain one reason the lower initial payment is not the complete comparison.

For a separate five-year interest-only balloon with the same $180,000 balance and no principal payments, state the principal due at maturity. Do not assume refinancing will be available. Write a small calculation grid and two explanatory sentences. Use full precision for intermediate calculations, then round displayed currency to cents and percentages to two decimal places.

### C7A Public solution and written self-review

The initial interest payment is $180,000 × 0.06 ÷ 12 = **$900**. Sixty payments total $54,000 of interest; the principal remains **$180,000**. With 300 payments remaining, the amortization formula gives **$1,159.74** at 6% and **$1,389.27** at 8%. Relative to $900, the increases are **$259.74 / 28.86%** and **$489.27 / 54.36%**. The unchanged-rate path isolates principal repayment and the shorter amortization period; the 8% path adds rate shock. The separate interest-only balloon requires **$180,000 of principal at maturity**, in addition to any final interest or other amounts actually due under its terms.

Review your work: Did you use 300 rather than 360 payments? Did you subtract $900 before finding the percentage increase? Did you avoid claiming that interest paid reduces principal? Rewrite one sentence that could be mistaken for a promise of refinancing. If you choose to discuss this offline, ask someone to identify which input causes each change; participation is optional.

### Your case C7A2 A minimum payment that grows the debt

Assume a separate illustrative $85,000 loan starts with 12 monthly payments of $340, while interest accrues at a fixed 7.20% annual rate, or 0.006 monthly. Unpaid interest is added to principal each month. There are no new advances, fees, missed payments, caps, or voluntary principal payments. Immediately after payment 12, the contract requires amortization over the remaining 348 months of a 360-month term at the unchanged 7.20% rate. These artificial terms are for mechanics only, not a product offer or underwriting method.

Calculate months 1 and 2, the balance after month 12, the recast payment, and its dollar increase over $340. Explain why a record of timely minimum payments does not prove that principal fell. Then identify which additional contract provisions a real review would need before predicting the actual recast date.

**Public solution.** Month 1 interest is $510 and the ending balance is $85,170. Month 2 interest is $511.02 and the ending balance is $85,341.02. Use B(k) = $85,000 × 1.006^k − $340 × (1.006^k − 1) ÷ 0.006. B(12) = **$87,108.68**, a **$2,108.68** balance increase after $4,080 of payments. Amortizing the unrounded balance over 348 months at 0.006 per month gives **$597.12**, up **$257.12**. Rate increases are unnecessary for this result: capitalized interest and fewer repayment months are enough. A real analysis also needs rate adjustments, minimum-payment changes, a negative-amortization limit, and the actual scheduled or earlier recast provisions. [P7-11, P7-20]

**Written self-review.** Explain the distinction between interest-only and negative amortization in two sentences using these figures. A sufficient response says that a $510 first payment would cover this month’s interest but reduce no principal, whereas the $340 payment leaves $170 to be added to the balance. Neither comparison establishes real-loan eligibility or a qualifying payment.

## Unit 7B Connect product risk to repayment evidence

### Establish coverage before selecting a method

Start with the transaction’s primary purpose, collateral, open-end or closed-end structure, and the particular rule being tested. Section 1026.43 generally covers consumer credit secured by a dwelling, with stated exclusions. A HELOC subject to §1026.40 is excluded from §1026.43 under paragraph (a)(1). A reverse mortgage subject to §1026.33 and a qualifying temporary or bridge loan of 12 months or less are excluded from paragraphs (c) through (f) under paragraph (a)(3); that is a scoped exclusion, not a statement that all of Regulation Z disappears. Read the listed conditions rather than rely on “bridge” in a product name. [P7-04]

Business-purpose analysis comes from the facts and §1026.3’s rules and interpretations. A self-employed person buying a personal home is not automatically obtaining business credit. Conversely, the commentary has specific treatment for credit to acquire, improve, or maintain non-owner-occupied rental property. A DSCR label, an LLC name, or a rental figure is no substitute for identifying the applicable facts and rule. Other laws and program conditions can still apply when one federal provision does not. [P7-17]

### Three payments can be different

The advertised or initial payment is not necessarily the qualifying payment, and neither necessarily equals the household's full monthly cost. On a covered interest-only loan using the general ATR method, the rule calls for the greater of the fully indexed or introductory rate and substantially equal monthly P&I over the term remaining at recast. Other special features have other methods; do not extend the interest-only formula automatically to balloons or negative amortization. The legal analysis belongs with the qualified compliance and underwriting process. [P7-04, §1026.43(c)(5); P7-09]

Take W7A2's fixed-rate interest-only loan as a bounded illustration of that method: $1,546.32 is the relevant amortizing P&I, rather than $1,200 of initial interest. Add applicable taxes, insurance, other mortgage-related obligations, simultaneous loans, and other included debts through the correct method. An affordable first month does not establish the required repayment finding at origination.

For a covered negative-amortization loan under the general ATR method, the rule uses the defined maximum loan amount and the remaining term at recast; it does not simply reuse the original balance. Balloon-payment methods depend in part on whether the transaction is a higher-priced covered transaction. That classification must be established before applying its special payment rule. These legal methods must not be replaced with a casual adverse-rate forecast. The two ARM reset scenarios in W7A describe later contractual cash flow, not origination qualification. [P7-04, §1026.43(b)(7), (c)(5)(ii)]

### Alternative documentation must still support the fact

A bank statement can be reasonably reliable evidence of transactions while still not proving that every deposit is personal income. Separate customer receipts, transfers between owned accounts, loan proceeds, refunds, and other inflows. Business receipts precede business expenses and any ownership allocation. A product name such as bank-statement loan does not tell you which periods, expense method, ownership evidence, trend review, or adjustments are permitted. Obtain the written requirements and an authorized analysis. For covered transactions, relied-upon income or assets must meet the applicable verification standard; a consumer's signed estimate alone is not a universal alternative. [P7-04, §1026.43(c)(3)–(4)]

Original practice extract: Sam's twelve-month business statements contain $120,000 of deposits. Reliable supporting records identify $18,000 as transfers from another Sam-owned account and $6,000 as borrowed money. The exercise reviewer accepts the remaining $96,000 as business receipts, accepts $60,000 of expenses for this arithmetic only, and stipulates 100% ownership with no other adjustments. The result is ($120,000 − $18,000 − $6,000 − $60,000) ÷ 12 = $3,000 monthly. Simply dividing deposits by 12 would produce $10,000 and misclassify cash movements as repayment income.

Even the $3,000 result is not an approval. The illustration stipulates what a real file would have to establish. If the latest months show a serious decline, or an expense category is missing, route the discrepancy instead of treating the old average as verified continuing income. Do not describe self-employment as business-purpose credit when the loan actually finances a personal residence. [P7-04]

### Evaluate the risks together

Risk layering means several vulnerabilities interact. An interest-only transition, limited verified reserves, a simultaneous second payment, variable earnings, and dependence on a sale can stress the same household at once. Record which risks exist and which mitigation is actually evidenced. Stated savings, a hoped-for raise, or rising property values are not interchangeable with verified resources. A larger down payment can reduce leverage but does not by itself prove the required monthly repayment capacity. [P7-05]

Use a simple record with four fields: exposure, supporting fact, evidence gap, and next owner. For example: payment rises when amortization begins; the note provides a five-year interest-only period; qualifying income has not been accepted; underwriter reviews evidence and the applicable qualifying method. The record should allow a hold rather than force every case into an offer.

### Your case C7B The incomplete alternative file

Riley seeks a consumer-purpose, closed-end loan secured by the home Riley will occupy. Assume no ATR exemption. The proposed product is described as non-QM and has a three-year interest-only period. The file has six months of business statements but the supplied fictional program requires twelve months and a supported expense analysis. It also shows a $500 monthly simultaneous second-loan payment. The sales note says to use the low first payment and ignore the second because both loans share the same house.

Choose three actions and justify each in one sentence: (1) the missing evidence request, (2) the payment or obligation review to correct, and (3) the current file status and decision owner. Identify the legal requirement separately from the invented twelve-month program requirement. Do not calculate a DTI or approve the loan from facts the case does not supply.

### C7B Public solution and written self-review

Request the remaining six months and the supported expense analysis required by this fictional program. Identify transfers, borrowing, ownership, and unexplained changes before accepting a repayment-income figure. The legal verification duty and the invented twelve-month policy are separate; this lesson does not claim that every non-QM program or federal rule requires twelve business statements.

Correct the payment analysis through the authorized underwriting process: test the covered interest-only loan using the applicable method and consider the known simultaneous second-loan obligation. Sharing collateral does not erase the second payment. Confirm the appropriate calculation for that second loan rather than silently assuming its first advertised amount is the qualifying amount. Record the file as incomplete and not cleared for an approval recommendation. The underwriter reviews repayment evidence and product eligibility; compliance resolves uncertain legal coverage or required notices. This working status does not suspend actual application or notice deadlines. [P7-04]

A signed acknowledgment can document receipt or a stated understanding, but it cannot create the missing evidence. For a written self-review, answer: “Which newly verified fact could change this record, and who must accept it?” A supported expense analysis and complete records could change the income analysis; a promise to refinance or a borrower’s willingness to accept risk does not supply them. Do not invent a DTI when income and the appropriate first-loan payment are not established.

Return separately to Sam’s fully stipulated ledger. Excluding $18,000 of transfers and $6,000 of borrowed money leaves $96,000 of receipts. Subtracting $60,000 of accepted expenses leaves $36,000 annually or **$3,000 monthly**. Treating every deposit as income would overstate this exercise’s monthly figure by **$7,000**, or 233.33% of the supported $3,000 result. This shows why classifying evidence comes before division. It does not turn Sam’s figures into Riley’s missing evidence.

## Unit 7C Explain the tradeoff and recognize the boundary

### Explain what changes and what remains unknown

A useful explanation names the first payment, what it includes, how long it lasts, the trigger for a change, principal behavior, a meaningful adverse scenario, and the uncertainty of any exit plan. Compare costs over consistent assumptions and ask the consumer to explain the transition back in their own words. A teach-back question tests understanding; a signature alone cannot demonstrate it.

For W7A2, a concise explanation is: The first $1,200 covers interest only, so the $240,000 principal does not fall under these assumptions. After five years, paying that principal over the remaining 25 years raises P&I to about $1,546 even if the rate stays at 6%. Taxes and insurance are additional, and their costs can change. We must evaluate the actual terms and repayment evidence; refinancing before that transition is not guaranteed.

This explanation is a teaching example, not an advertisement, compliant disclosure form, or personal loan recommendation. A required disclosure and an understandable conversation serve related but different functions. For covered transactions, the Loan Estimate identifies relevant product features, including interest-only, balloon, and negative-amortization features, through prescribed content. ARM information and subsequent adjustment notices have their own applicable requirements. Route the actual document and timing review rather than improvising a substitute chart. [P7-12, P7-13, P7-16]

### Some products require another analysis

| Product or feature | Question to resolve | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| HELOC | How do draw and repayment periods, variable rates, minimums, and any final balance work? | Open-end home-equity plans have a separate Regulation Z framework; a closed-end Loan Estimate analysis is not automatically transferable |
| Reverse mortgage including FHA HECM | What can become due, and which occupancy, property-charge, upkeep, counseling, and program conditions apply? | Not a promise of a home without bills; use specialized product and legal review |
| Short-term bridge financing | What supports the exit and what happens if the sale is delayed? | The actual transaction may have scoped exceptions, but the label does not establish every exemption |
| Investment-property or DSCR-branded loan | What is the actual primary credit purpose and what do the contract and program require? | Property type and rental calculations do not alone decide legal coverage |

HELOC rules appear in §1026.40. Reverse-mortgage provisions in §1026.33 and the scoped exclusions in §1026.43 require separate analysis; HUD’s HECM information directs consumers to lender and counselor resources. The course does not teach a complete HELOC, reverse, bridge, or business-purpose origination method. A specialist referral is a competent action when the unresolved issue exceeds the originator's authority. [P7-04, P7-14, P7-15, P7-18]

### Worked example W7C A HELOC transition

A fictional HELOC has a $40,000 drawn balance at the end of its draw period. The final draw-period payment covers interest only. For this simplified illustration, use an unchanged 9% annual rate, monthly accrual at 9% ÷ 12, no further advances, and a repayment phase of 120 equal monthly payments. The interest-only payment is $40,000 × 0.09 ÷ 12 = **$300**. Amortizing $40,000 over 120 months requires **$506.70**, a **$206.70** or **68.90%** increase. Variable rates or different repayment terms would change the result. This is a mathematical comparison, not the required disclosure example or a universal HELOC contract. [P7-14; original W7C assumptions]

A credit limit and a drawn balance are different. If this plan’s limit were $60,000 but only $40,000 were drawn, this example calculates interest on $40,000. An unused $20,000 limit is not income and is not a guarantee that funds will remain available. Read the conditions that govern later draws, suspension, termination, and repayment. Section 1026.40 has detailed limits; neither the learner nor an originator should improvise them. [P7-14]

### Reverse mortgages require precise language

A HECM is an FHA-insured reverse mortgage program, not the name for every reverse mortgage. Under the usual reverse-mortgage structure, interest and fees can be added to the balance, so the debt can grow while the borrower makes no monthly mortgage payment. The absence of that payment does not eliminate property taxes, insurance, maintenance, occupancy conditions, or other program duties. A borrower who does not meet required conditions can face the loan becoming due. Death, transfer, and leaving the principal residence also raise repayment and possible deferral questions that require the actual legal and program rules. Avoid promising a particular outcome for a spouse or heir. [P7-15, P7-18, P7-21]

Refer HECM eligibility, counseling, financial assessment, property charges, and any non-borrowing-spouse issue to current HUD guidance and an appropriately qualified specialist. The public lesson does not quote a current principal limit, rate, fee cap, or universal age rule for every reverse mortgage. Its purpose is to recognize the boundary and explain the next reliable source. [P7-15, P7-21]

### Your case C7C Repair the explanation

An originator says: This fixed-rate interest-only loan has a permanent $900 payment, and you can always refinance before the principal is due. The case terms are the $180,000 loan in C7A; taxes, insurance, and fees were omitted from that statement.

Rewrite the explanation in no more than five sentences. Include principal behavior, the transition, omitted costs, and refinancing uncertainty. Add one question that checks understanding without pressuring the consumer. Then identify what specialist review would be needed if the consumer instead asks about a HECM.

### C7C Public solution and written self-review

One five-sentence revision is: “The first $900 monthly payment covers interest for five years and does not reduce the $180,000 principal under these assumptions. After those five years, repayment over the remaining 25 years raises monthly principal and interest to about $1,159.74 even though the rate stays at 6%. Taxes, insurance, and applicable fees or other property costs are additional, and some can change. Refinancing is uncertain and must not be treated as a guaranteed way to avoid the higher payment. In your own words, what happens to the balance during the first five years, and what changes after that?”

This explanation concerns C7A’s 30-year loan with a five-year interest-only period; it does not describe C7A’s separate five-year balloon. For the balloon, replace the transition sentence with the actual principal amount due at maturity. A correct figure attached to the wrong legal term is still a misleading explanation.

If the consumer asks about a HECM instead, identify the product change, use HUD’s counselor and lender resources, and seek specialized review of eligibility, property obligations, occupancy, repayment triggers, and relevant household protections. Do not convert the conventional loan’s payment illustration into a HECM quote. For the HELOC variation in W7C, record **$300 → $506.70** and explain the changed repayment period before discussing any separate rate change.

For written self-review, underline each statement about balance, change trigger, future cost, and uncertainty. Replace any “always,” “permanent,” or implied approval that is not supported by the case. An optional offline discussion can test whether your explanation is clear; no instructor or peer response is provided by this public lesson. Revisit the public practice questions as often as useful. Correct answers and public case solutions support learning; they do not grant credit or satisfy a future approved offering’s participation or completion requirements.

## Bring the parts together

Before calling an explanation complete, check the same obligation through three views. First, the cash-flow worksheet must follow the actual dates, rates, balance, amortization period, and maturity. Second, the evidence record must apply the correct coverage and repayment method and make gaps visible. Third, the consumer explanation must describe both the immediate obligation and the later consequence without supplying a guarantee that the documents do not contain. If one view conflicts with another, preserve the conflict and route it to the responsible reviewer.

For example, a truthful “$900 interest-only payment” can still be incomplete if the consumer is left to believe it lasts for the full 30 years. A correct $1,159.74 transition payment can still be insufficient for an underwriting conclusion if the relied-upon income is unsupported. A complete income file can still be paired with a misleading assurance of refinancing. The skill is to connect the facts, not to use one correct answer as clearance for every other part of the file.
