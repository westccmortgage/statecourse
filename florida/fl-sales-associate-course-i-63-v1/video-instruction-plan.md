# FREC Course I — video instruction plan

**Planning only.** Nothing here is scripted, recorded, rendered or active. Every candidate is
`PENDING / NOT ACTIVE`. The machine-checkable version — production rules, activation gate, and the
full field set for each candidate — is `config/florida/video-instruction-plan.mjs`, which
`tests/florida-foundation.test.mjs` asserts against.

---

## What Florida says about video, which is less than you would expect

Both rules that once governed it are **repealed**:

- Rule 61J2-3.016, *Video Tape Quality Standards* — repealed 4 May 2008
- Rule 61J2-3.017, *Video Tape Classroom Viewing Conditions* — repealed 27 April 2008

So **no Florida media standard survives.** Both still appear in the Department of State's chapter
index carrying "(Repealed)" in their titles — exactly the listing that gets cited by someone who
read the title and not the status.

What governs video instead is Rule 61J2-3.008(4)(b)3, which says nothing about production and a
great deal about time, interaction and evidence. Three sub-items bear on video:

- **Hours are hours.** Distance study runs "on a classroom hour per classroom hour basis", and a
  50-minute distance hour equals a 50-minute classroom hour.
- **Stated hours must be provable.** Sub-item 3.e requires an objective study that stated hours
  match actual hours.
- **The course must be interactive.** Video is not interchange. It can sit inside a course that
  provides interchange; it cannot be what provides it.

## The tension, and how it resolves

Optional-and-non-blocking sits awkwardly beside hour-per-hour. If a video is optional, a student who
skips it has spent less time than the timeline claims; if it counts toward the 3,000 instructional
minutes, skipping it makes the stated hours untrue for that student.

**Video sits outside the instructional-hour budget.** The 3,000 minutes are carried entirely by
material every student receives. Three consequences, each a constraint on later work:

1. A video may never be the only place a required point is taught.
2. The objective time study measures the course **without** video; run time is reported separately
   and claimed as nothing.
3. Section hour allocations are computed from written and interactive material alone, so adding or
   removing a video never disturbs the hour architecture or invalidates the time study.

The alternative — counting video toward the hours — would force it to be mandatory, blocking and
tracked to the second, contradicting three product rules to buy nothing Florida asked for.

## Production rules

- Optional; **never blocks progression**.
- **No video minutes count toward required Florida instructional time** unless primary authority
  *and* the approved time model expressly support it. Neither does.
- **No quiz answer at the end of a video.** The mastery check appears on the **next screen**.
- Real documents shown as **static frames**. Crops and highlights produced **deterministically in
  post-production**.
- **No AI-recreated government forms.** No automatic zooms. No generated labels over document text.
- **Captions may not obscure the field being discussed.**
- A video stays `PENDING / NOT ACTIVE` until **all seven** exist: MP4, VTT, transcript, poster,
  placement, accessibility, registry.

## Confidentiality — video leaks differently

A page can be checked by reading it. A video has a script, a rendered audio track, a caption file, a
transcript, a poster frame, a thumbnail, chapter markers, and a filename — eight separate artifacts,
most of which a reviewer who watched the video has not checked. No examination item, distractor,
answer or page reference may appear in any of them.

## Rejected candidates — 8

A plan that lists only what was chosen looks as though everything was considered. These were
considered and rejected.

| Section | Subject | Why not video | Better instrument |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Course Overview: The Real Estate Business | Orientation prose with no mechanism to show. A video here would be a welcome message, which the production rules exclude. | text |
| 2 | Real Estate License Law And Qualifications For Licensure | Rule-dense and citation-heavy. Video makes this material harder to verify against the statute and much harder to update: when an hour requirement or a fee changes, a page is edited and a video is re-recorded. Putting volatile regulated detail into video is a maintenance trap. | text with a citation table |
| 3 | Real Estate License Law And Commission Rules | Same as Section 2. Commission composition, powers and duties are enumerated facts, and enumerated facts belong in a table a student can scan and an author can update. | text with a table |
| 6 | Violations Of License Law, Penalties And Procedures | The penalty structure is tabular data — violation, category, range. A table is searchable, comparable and correctable; a video is none of those. The complaint procedure is a sequence, but a numbered list carries it without a production cost. | table plus a numbered procedure |
| 8 | Property Rights: Estates And Tenancies | The estates and tenancies material is a set of definitional distinctions best held side by side. A comparison table lets a student see all of them at once, which is exactly what a linear video prevents. | comparison table |
| 15 | The Real Estate Markets And Analysis | Supply, demand and market indicators are relationships between variables. An interactive that lets a student move one variable and see the others respond teaches the relationship; a video of someone else moving it does not. | interactive exercise |
| 16 | Real Estate Appraisal — the three approaches to value, conceptually | Sixteen objectives, most of them conceptual distinctions among types and principles of value. Video would be a lecture. The three approaches ARE worth a worked calculation, but as an interactive exercise the student performs rather than watches. | interactive worked exercise |
| 19 | Planning, Zoning And Environmental Hazards | Largely definitional and jurisdictionally variable. Text keeps it correctable. | text |

## Sequence

Nothing here is authorised to proceed. When curriculum authoring is authorised:

1. Curriculum written and paginated, section by section, in syllabus order.
2. Section hour allocations computed from that material alone.
3. Objective time study run against the course **without** video.
4. Video candidates re-tested against the finished sections — several of the seven will probably not
   survive contact with the written material, which is the point of testing them afterwards.
5. Missing sources acquired (FL-V06 and FL-V07 are blocked on this).
6. Scripts written, captioned, and checked against the confidentiality rules across all eight
   artifact surfaces.

No rendering, no production and no scripting happens before step 4.
