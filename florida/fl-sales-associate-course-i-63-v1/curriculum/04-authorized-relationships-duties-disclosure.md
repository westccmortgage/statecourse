## Core lesson

A brokerage relationship identifies whose interests a licensee serves and which duties apply. It is not determined merely by who pays compensation, who first called the office, or whether a particular associate conducted the showing. Florida permits single agency and transaction brokerage and allows a customer to choose no brokerage relationship. It prohibits a licensee from acting as a disclosed or undisclosed dual agent in the statutory fiduciary sense.

Keep three questions separate. First, what brokerage relationship exists? Second, what duties follow? Third, does this transaction or encounter require a particular written disclosure, and when? An exception to a disclosure requirement does not erase the permitted-relationship rules or authorize dishonesty. A signature acknowledging a disclosure likewise does not answer every question about the employment agreement, compensation or actual authority.

## Objective-level application pages

### Objective 4.1 — curriculum page 30

**Teachable proposition.** Section 475.274 applies the authorized relationships in sections 475.2755 and 475.278 throughout brokerage activities defined in section 475.01(1)(a). The disclosure requirements of section 475.278 apply only to residential sales as defined in subsection (5)(a). Do not convert “this notice is not required in this encounter” into “no duties apply.”

Section 475.278(5)(b) describes transaction and encounter limitations. The listed transactions include nonresidential work; rentals or leases, subject to the stated residential purchase-option exception; auctions; appraisals; and business-enterprise/opportunity dispositions subject to their residential-property exception. The statute also addresses a known existing representative, qualifying owner-built new residential sales settings, a genuinely limited open house or model showing, unanticipated casual conversations, general factual responses about advertised property, and limited information about the licensee or firm. Every condition matters.

An open house ceases to fit the narrow encounter description when it involves eliciting confidential information, executing an offer or representation agreement, or negotiating price, terms or conditions. The event's sign outside the door does not preserve the exception after the conversation changes.

**Worked example.** A visitor asks whether an advertised house has three bedrooms and where the kitchen is. The facts concern a general inquiry. The visitor then says, “The divorce must finish quickly; I can pay $40,000 more than my offer. Will you negotiate that offer for me?” The licensee must reassess the encounter and relationship instead of relying indefinitely on the open-house label. Sensitive information, representation and negotiation have become material.

**Guided source task.** Read subsection (5)(b) in full. Create two columns: transaction-based limitations and encounter-based limitations. For each, record the condition that could remove it. Include both the rental purchase-option language and the limits on open-house conversations.

**Independent application.** Classify: a simple apartment lease; a lease with an option to purchase a duplex; an appraisal assignment; a sale of a restaurant business including a residential duplex; and a model-home encounter that becomes negotiation. For each explain what further facts are needed and why notice coverage does not itself establish a fiduciary relationship.

### Objective 4.2 — curriculum page 31

**Teachable proposition.** The statutory residential-sale definition has three branches: improved residential property with four units or fewer; unimproved residential property intended for four units or fewer; and agricultural property of ten acres or fewer. Price is not the test. A buyer's intended use and the property classification can matter; “vacant” or “expensive” is not a complete classification.

**Worked classification.** A four-unit residential building fits the improved-property branch. A five-unit building does not fit that branch merely because one unit is vacant. A vacant parcel intended for a duplex points to the unimproved-residential branch. A ten-acre agricultural parcel fits the agricultural acreage branch; an eleven-acre agricultural parcel does not fit that branch merely because ten acres are actively planted. These examples apply the definition, not a zoning entitlement or a conclusion about every mixed-use property.

**Guided source task.** Read section 475.278(5)(a). Prepare a three-row definition card retaining “sale,” property type, unit/intended-unit count and acreage. Explain which facts an advertisement may fail to supply.

**Independent application.** A parcel is marketed as “agricultural investment,” contains an occupied house, and may be subdivided. The acreage is disputed between a brochure and a survey. Identify the missing classification and acreage evidence. Do not choose the most convenient statutory branch or assume a proposed subdivision has been approved.

### Objective 4.3 — curriculum page 32

**Teachable proposition.** A single agent is a fiduciary for the buyer or seller engaged as principal. A transaction broker provides limited representation to a buyer, seller or both without representing either as a fiduciary or single agent. No brokerage relationship means the licensee is not representing that customer in an authorized brokerage relationship, while still owing the specified baseline duties.

Florida's section 475.278(1)(b) presumes transaction brokerage unless single agency or no brokerage relationship is established in writing with the customer. This Florida rule must not be imported into Colorado, whose written-relationship law changed in August 2026. A course must teach the jurisdiction actually involved.

The statutory prohibition on dual agency means the same licensee cannot solve a buyer/seller fiduciary conflict merely by obtaining both parties' general consent to dual agency. A lawful transition or the separate qualifying designated-sales-associate provision must be analyzed on its own terms. A broker's internal staffing preference cannot replace the customer's required consent.

**Worked example.** A broker has a seller as single-agent principal and wants to assist an interested buyer in the same transaction. The associate says both parties agreed verbally that the office can be “loyal to everyone.” That does not authorize prohibited dual agency or establish a valid transition. Identify the current roles and required lawful arrangement before proceeding.

**Guided source task.** Read subsections (1), (2), (3)(a) and (4)(a). Build a role chart with represented party, fiduciary status, statutory duties, writing/disclosure issue and prohibited inference. Keep the transaction-broker presumption distinct from the single-agent notice deadline.

**Independent application.** In three files, a customer signs single-agency terms, signs no-brokerage terms, or has neither established in writing. Identify the Florida relationship issue in each. Then explain why compensation from a seller or another broker is not, by itself, a complete relationship analysis.

### Objective 4.4 — curriculum page 33

**Teachable proposition.** The duty lists must be applied to actual conduct, not memorized only as acronyms.

| Role | Statutory duty structure |
| --- | --- |
| No brokerage relationship | Honest and fair dealing; disclosure of known facts materially affecting residential value that are not readily observable to the buyer; accounting for funds entrusted to the licensee |
| Transaction broker | Honest and fair dealing; accounting for all funds; skill, care and diligence; the specified residential material-fact disclosure; timely offers/counteroffers unless otherwise directed in writing; limited confidentiality unless waived in writing; additional mutually agreed duties |
| Single agent | Honest and fair dealing; loyalty; confidentiality; obedience; full disclosure; accounting for all funds; skill, care and diligence; timely offers/counteroffers unless otherwise directed in writing; the specified residential material-fact disclosure |

A no-brokerage customer is still entitled to honest handling of money and the stated defect disclosures. A transaction broker's limited confidentiality covers the listed price/offer information, motivations, alternative financing terms and other information a party requests remain confidential, subject to the statutory framework. It is not permission to conceal a known material property fact that must be disclosed.

**Worked example.** A seller privately tells the transaction broker that $20,000 below the asking price would be acceptable and that the roof leaked during the last storm. The price concession and roof condition are different categories. Do not disclose the confidential price merely to accelerate a deal; do not use limited confidentiality to suppress a known non-readily-observable material residential defect. Determine materiality and actual knowledge from facts rather than treating every imperfection as automatically material.

**Guided source task.** Read each complete duty list in section 475.278. Mark the duties shared by all three roles and those added for a particular role. For offer presentation, preserve the statute's written-direction qualification; do not replace it with an oral instruction or an assumption that an existing contract ends the duty.

**Independent application.** A licensee receives an earnest deposit from a no-brokerage customer, receives a second offer after a first contract, and learns a confidential relocation motive. For each event identify the relationship, precise duty and fact that could change the response. Explain which written instruction or waiver would have to exist before relying on it.

### Objective 4.5 — curriculum page 34

**Teachable proposition.** Single agency adds a fiduciary obligation to promote the principal's interests through loyalty, confidentiality, lawful obedience and full disclosure. Transaction brokerage supplies limited representation and the duties expressly described in subsection (2). The word “limited” modifies the kind of representation; it does not excuse carelessness, unfairness or mishandled funds.

Loyalty to a principal is not an instruction to violate law or conceal a fact that must be disclosed. Confidentiality is not interchangeable with the duty to disclose known material residential property facts. Obedience requires examining the instruction's lawfulness and scope, rather than treating every demand as binding. Full disclosure to a principal should not be reduced to sharing only the information that helps close a transaction.

**Worked comparison.** A buyer asks whether the seller might accept a lower amount. A buyer's single agent analyzes lawful available information in the buyer-principal's interest while respecting duties and protected information. A transaction broker cannot offer to advocate as the buyer's exclusive fiduciary while simultaneously claiming limited representation to both sides. In neither role may the licensee invent an offer, disclose protected confidences without authority, or misstate a known material fact.

**Guided source task.** Compare subsections (2)(f) and (3)(a). In your own words explain the difference between the transaction broker's limited confidentiality list and the single agent's confidentiality duty. Then identify the property-disclosure duty common to both.

**Independent application.** Rewrite these inaccurate statements: “A transaction broker has no confidentiality”; “A single agent must obey a seller's instruction to hide a known leak”; and “Both sides paying the broker makes dual agency lawful.” Cite the controlling part of the duty structure for each correction.

### Objective 4.6 — curriculum page 35

**Teachable proposition.** Build a timeline before deciding a notice is timely. For a required single-agent disclosure, the writing is due before or at entry into the listing/representation agreement or before showing property, whichever occurs first. For a required no-brokerage disclosure, give it before showing property. For a transition from single agency, first obtain the principal's written consent to the change.

Do not invent a separate statutory transaction-broker notice requirement from an old form or from another state. The current statute describes the transaction-broker presumption and duties and separately specifies notices for single agency, no brokerage and transition. The scope and exceptions still must be checked.

**Worked timeline.** On Monday the licensee agrees to act as single agent for a residential seller and enters the listing agreement. A showing is planned Wednesday. Thursday notice delivery would be late; the earlier listing/representation event already controlled. In a separate no-brokerage residential file, a licensee cannot wait until the purchase offer to supply the required notice if property was shown earlier.

**Guided source task.** Locate the timing language in subsections (3)(b)1, (3)(b)2 and (4)(b). Draw the agreement, showing and proposed relationship-change events on one line for each role. Record the notice and consent evidence that must precede the controlling event.

**Independent application.** An associate emailed an unsigned transition form after beginning to provide transaction brokerage to both parties. Identify what the email proves, what it does not prove, and the sequencing defect. Preserve the actual record; do not backdate a signature to make the timeline appear compliant.

### Objective 4.7 — curriculum page 36

**Teachable proposition.** Notice content, presentation and timing are separate checks. Section 475.278 supplies the Single Agent Notice, Consent to Transition to Transaction Broker and No Brokerage Relationship Notice wording. Read the complete applicable text, including duties, name insertion, acknowledgment or consent and date/signature provisions. A provider-created summary is teaching material, not a substitute statutory notice.

When the specified notices are incorporated into another document, the statute requires type at least as large as the other provisions, conspicuous placement, and the specified first sentence in uppercase bold type. Do not substitute an unsupported universal twelve-point rule for the actual relative-size requirement. A heading alone does not cure missing required text or consent.

**Worked audit.** A listing document buries a single-agent notice in eight-point text while the rest is eleven-point, prints the statutory first sentence in ordinary sentence case, and omits one duty. Even if the consumer signs the document, the content and format questions remain. Correct all three; a signature is not proof that every statutory presentation requirement was met.

**Guided source task.** Open the official statutory forms in subsection (3)(c)1–2 and (4)(c). Build a checklist for title, first sentence, complete duty text, correct entity/associate identification, acknowledgment/consent and date/signature, together with the incorporated-document requirements in (3)(b) and (4)(b). Keep the exact source text beside the checklist.

**Independent application.** Inspect a fictional notice assembled from the source: one field names only the associate, the first sentence is bold but not uppercase, and the copy is made after a proposed transition has already occurred. List each separate issue and the evidence needed to resolve it. The exercise does not authorize modifying the statute's required language.

### Objective 4.8 — curriculum page 37

**Teachable proposition.** Transition changes the representation owed to the principal. A single agent may transition to transaction brokerage only after the principal gives written consent to the change and the appropriate duties are disclosed. The customer must understand the relinquishment of the single-agent relationship; staff cannot make that choice unilaterally for convenience.

The sequence is current role, proposed role, required explanation/disclosure, principal's written consent, then the changed relationship. If the principal declines, no completed transition has been shown. Do not represent the refusal as consent merely because the principal still wants to sell or because another party wants to use the same brokerage.

**Worked example.** A seller hired the office as single agent. A buyer now wants the office's help. Before choosing an operating arrangement, explain the existing single-agent duties and the proposed limited representation, use the required transition disclosure and obtain the seller's written consent if that path is selected. The buyer's preference cannot waive the seller-principal's consent. A signed original listing is not automatically evidence of a later transition unless the actual valid document and consent cover it.

**Guided source task.** Read subsections (1)(a), (3)(b)2 and (3)(c)2 together. Identify exactly which written evidence authorizes the role change and which notice describes the resulting duties. Compare the timing with your objective 4.6 timeline.

**Independent application.** A principal agrees on a telephone call but refuses to sign the supplied consent. A second principal signs after the office changes its conduct. Explain why neither fact pattern should be logged as a properly completed prior-written-consent transition. State the appropriate escalation and preserve what actually occurred.

### Objective 4.9 — curriculum page 38

**Teachable proposition.** Section 475.2755 provides a distinct designated-sales-associate arrangement for a transaction other than a residential sale as defined in section 475.278(5)(a), where buyer and seller each meet the $1 million asset threshold and request the arrangement. The broker may designate associates to act as single agents for different customers. This is not a blanket residential designated-agency alternative or permission for ordinary dual agency.

Both customers sign disclosures stating that their assets meet the threshold and requesting designated-sales-associate representation. The designated associates owe the single-agent duties and the applicable disclosures. The statute supplies additional confidentiality language: information may be shared with the broker or specified persons for advice/assistance for the represented customer's benefit, and the broker must hold it confidential and may not use it to the other party's detriment. Read the exact text rather than promising that the broker can never receive it.

**Worked example.** Two qualifying customers in a nonresidential transaction request separate designated associates and supply the required signed disclosures. That directs the analysis to section 475.2755. If the same parties instead buy and sell a four-unit residential building, their wealth alone does not remove the residential-sale exclusion. If one party has assets below $1 million, the threshold is not met by adding the parties' assets together.

**Guided source task.** Read both subsections of section 475.2755. Construct an eligibility checklist, required customer signatures, associate duty list and broker-confidentiality controls. Note that its buyer/seller definitions include the stated transferee/lessee and transferor/lessor roles; do not rely only on everyday sale terminology.

**Independent application.** A commercial buyer reports $1.2 million in assets; the seller reports $850,000. Another office proposes assigning two associates to a residential duplex sale between wealthy parties. Identify the failed element in each. Do not cure a failed element by renaming the relationship or omitting the required disclosure.

### Objective 4.10 — curriculum page 39

**Teachable proposition.** Separate ending the brokerage authority, ending an employment/representation agreement, ending a purchase contract and disposing of held funds. They are different legal events. A closing may accomplish the agreed brokerage purpose; an agreement may reach its expiration; the parties may mutually release the engagement; a principal may revoke authority or a broker may renounce it, with potential contract consequences; death, incapacity, destruction of the subject property, loss of required licensure or another legal event may affect authority or performance. The particular agreement, applicable law and any exception must be examined before choosing the result.

Revocation is not proof that every fee or damage claim disappears. A terminated listing does not automatically terminate a buyer's executed purchase contract. It does not authorize unilateral release of disputed earnest money, destruction of records or disclosure of confidences. Termination and surviving obligations need their own review rather than a single “closed” button.

**Worked example.** A seller emails, “Cancel everything,” while a purchase agreement and earnest deposit remain outstanding. Record the request and escalate the engagement/contract issues. Identify each separate agreement and obligation. Do not assume the email releases the buyer, resolves broker compensation, allocates the escrow or destroys the duty to account.

**Guided source task.** Use the actual fictional engagement supplied by the instructor and identify purpose, term, termination, compensation and survival clauses. Connect the analysis to the FREC syllabus's termination topic and to the statutory fund-accounting and confidentiality duties already read. Any Florida-specific automatic-termination rule, exception or remedy beyond the held sources requires additional current authority; do not infer it from a generic list.

**Independent application.** Compare completion of the agreed sale, expiration before any sale, a written mutual release, unilateral cancellation during an unexpired engagement and a broker's loss of licensure. For each identify the event, agreement affected, unresolved rights and required fund/record actions. State where counsel or the employing broker must resolve an issue rather than guessing at damages or an automatic release.

## Integrated application lab

Create a file for a residential four-unit sale. The seller initially requests single agency. An open-house visitor first asks general factual questions, then shares a confidential negotiating ceiling and requests representation. The seller later considers transition but has not signed consent. A new non-readily-observable water issue is reported, and a second offer arrives. Finally, the seller emails a cancellation demand while a purchase contract and deposit remain outstanding.

Submit: transaction classification; a role-and-duty table at every stage; the scope/encounter analysis; a notice/consent timeline; a statutory-format checklist; separate confidential-information and required-property-disclosure decisions; offer-handling instructions and any necessary written direction; and a closing/cancellation issue list separating the engagement, purchase contract, money and records. Identify all missing facts instead of supplying convenient assumptions.

The reviewer uses the objective-specific criteria above. Critical errors require correction: prohibited dual agency; treating a disclosure exception as no duties; suppressing a required known material fact; revealing protected information without authority; missing the prior consent sequence; aggregating assets to evade the designated-associate threshold; or treating termination as automatic escrow disbursement. Use a changed fact pattern for remediation and retain both attempts. This original formative lab is not a secure end-course examination.
