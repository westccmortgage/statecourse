## Regulatory framework and method

Florida's license law protects the public by regulating people who perform defined services. Section 475.001, Florida Statutes, states the public-welfare purpose. Chapter 475 supplies the real-estate-specific definitions, qualifications, exemptions, education rules, and enforcement provisions. Chapter 455 supplies general professional-regulation provisions where they apply. The Florida Administrative Code implements authority delegated by statute. Department applications, checklists, and candidate instructions administer that law but must not be converted into unstated statutory rules.

This hierarchy matters when sources appear to differ. Read the current statute first, then the effective rule, then the current form or operational instruction. Record the edition, effective date, and access date. Do not rely on a remembered fee, form number, mutual-recognition state, or online workflow. Those operational details can change without changing the syllabus objective.

For licensing problems, use this sequence:

1. Identify the person and the act actually performed or proposed.
2. Apply the broker and sales-associate definitions in section 475.01.
3. Test every element of any claimed exemption in section 475.011.
4. Determine the person's license category, status, and registered relationship.
5. Separate initial qualification, application completeness, examination eligibility, license issuance, and activation. Passing one gate does not satisfy the others.

“Caveat emptor,” or “let the buyer beware,” is not a substitute for regulation or professional duty. The license law reflects a public-protection judgment: consumers often cannot efficiently verify a practitioner's competence, authority, handling of funds, or disciplinary history on their own. Regulation does not guarantee a transaction's success, and a license does not establish expertise in every adjacent field.

## Objective-level application pages

### Objective 2.1 — curriculum page 10

**Teachable proposition.** Section 475.17(1)(a) sets the core qualifications for a natural-person applicant. The applicant must be at least eighteen; hold a high school diploma or its equivalent; be honest, truthful, trustworthy, and of good character; and have a good reputation for fair dealing. An applicant for an active broker's license or a sales associate's license must also be competent and qualified to conduct transactions and negotiations with safety to investors and persons who may place trust and confidence in the applicant.

Those words are standards, not a promise that checking age and education boxes produces a license. Prior denial, suspension, revocation, disbarment, or conduct that would have supported discipline under Chapter 475 may require Commission evaluation. Section 475.17(1)(a) also permits consideration of lapse of time, subsequent good conduct and reputation, and other reasons the Commission deems sufficient in deciding whether the public and investors are likely to be endangered. A school should teach the standard without predicting how the Commission will decide an individual's record.

United States citizenship is not one of the section 475.17 qualifications. Section 455.10 says a person may not be disqualified from a state-regulated occupation solely because the person is not a United States citizen. That does not eliminate identity, lawful-process, Social Security number, or other current application requirements. Read the current application and governing law rather than replacing one requirement with another.

**Worked Florida example.** Priya is nineteen, has a GED, and is not a United States citizen. The age and education facts satisfy those two threshold elements, and citizenship alone cannot disqualify her under section 455.10. Nothing in those facts establishes honesty, character, competence, application completeness, exam eligibility, or authority to practice. The correct conclusion is limited: two qualifications are supported, one prohibited basis for sole disqualification is identified, and every remaining gate is still open.

**Guided primary-source task.** Read section 475.17(1)(a) alongside section 455.10. Build a checklist containing every stated natural-person qualification. Mark which standards are objective facts and which call for judgment. Cite the exact provision that addresses prior discipline and subsequent conduct. Then explain why “not a citizen” and “automatically qualified” are both incomplete conclusions.

**Independent new-fact application.** Mateo is thirty, holds an equivalent diploma, and had an occupational license suspended in another jurisdiction for dishonest billing six years ago. Identify the facts that meet threshold requirements, the fact requiring Commission review, and the additional evidence relevant under section 475.17(1)(a). Do not predict approval or denial.

### Objective 2.2 — curriculum page 11

**Teachable proposition.** Application, education, examination, issuance, and activation are separate gates. A typical initial path requires the applicant to use the current Department application, provide the required identity and background information, submit the required fee and digital fingerprints, establish education eligibility, obtain examination authorization, pass the applicable state examination, receive a license, and establish the registered relationship needed for active practice. The governing source—not a course summary—controls each operational step.

Section 475.175(1)(a) ties examination entitlement to the appropriate signed or electronically authenticated application, digital fingerprint data, and fee. Paragraph (b) identifies course-completion proof, the examination admissions authorization letter, and proof of identification at examination. Current electronic reporting or vendor instructions may affect how the applicant demonstrates those items operationally, so the candidate must follow the current notice rather than assume a paper process.

Section 475.181(2) contains two independent two-year limits: an application expires two years after receipt if the applicant does not pass the appropriate examination, and successful course completion becomes invalid for licensure if the licensing examination is not passed within two years after course completion. A learner should place both dates on a timeline because the dates need not be the same.

Nonresidents remain subject to Florida law. Section 475.180(2)(b) requires nonresident applicants and licensees to comply with Commission rules and Part I. A resident licensee who becomes a nonresident must notify the Commission and comply with nonresident requirements within sixty days under section 475.180(2)(a). Mutual recognition, when available, changes selected education or examination requirements; it does not erase application, qualification, fingerprint, or Florida-law obligations.

**Worked Florida example.** Ava completes Course I on March 1 and DBPR receives her application on June 15. She postpones the state examination. The course-completion clock and application clock began on different dates. “I have two years” is therefore not a sufficient calendar plan. Ava must calculate both statutory deadlines, obey current authorization instructions, and pass before the earlier applicable limit invalidates a necessary component. Completing the course did not issue or activate a license.

**Guided primary-source task.** Read sections 475.175(1) and 475.181(1)–(2). Draw a timeline from application through issuance, assigning each required act to the applicant, school, Commission, or Department. Add the two expiration rules on separate lines. Then inspect the current DBPR initial-application checklist and candidate bulletin, record their revision or access dates, and list any operational instruction that is not stated in the statute.

**Independent new-fact application.** A nonresident applicant submits fingerprints and an application but has only an expired course certificate and assumes an out-of-state license eliminates the Florida exam. Identify the separate issues, the sources that decide each, and the facts needed to determine whether mutual recognition is even available.

### Objective 2.3 — curriculum page 12

**Teachable proposition.** Background questions must be answered according to the exact words of the current application. “Convicted” is not the only possible trigger. A form may separately address findings of guilt, guilty or nolo contendere/no-contest pleas, withheld adjudication, pending investigations, prior application denials, professional discipline, aliases, or records believed sealed or expunged. The applicant should not silently substitute an everyday meaning for the form's defined scope.

Section 455.213 authorizes the Department to require personal-background, criminal-history, complaints, investigations, discipline, and related information. It also requires an application to be supplemented when a material circumstance changes between filing and final grant or denial and the change might affect the decision. Accuracy is therefore a continuing obligation, not a snapshot taken on submission day.

Full disclosure does not mean that every disclosed event necessarily requires denial. Section 475.17(1)(a) calls for a qualification analysis, including relevant later conduct and public risk. But a false answer or concealment can create a separate problem: section 475.25(1)(m) authorizes discipline when a license was obtained by fraud, misrepresentation, or concealment. The applicant should obtain the actual record and qualified legal advice when uncertain, not guess that an event is too old, minor, withheld, sealed, or expunged to report.

**Worked Florida example.** An application asks whether the applicant has ever entered a guilty or no-contest plea regardless of adjudication. Jordan pleaded no contest five years ago and adjudication was withheld. Jordan cannot answer “no conviction, so no” because that response ignores the separate plea language and adjudication qualifier. Jordan should read the entire current question and instructions, obtain the disposition, disclose or seek legal advice as the form requires, and preserve a copy of the submission.

**Guided primary-source task.** Read section 455.213's application and supplementation language, section 475.17(1)(a), and section 475.25(1)(m). Then examine the current DBPR Sales Associate Initial Application—not a cached example—and create a trigger list using the form's exact categories without copying sensitive personal data. For one category, explain why disclosure and substantive qualification are different decisions.

**Independent new-fact application.** After filing, an applicant learns that another state's licensing board has opened an investigation. The applicant has not been charged and believes the matter will close. Apply the material-change rule, identify the current form or Department instruction that must be consulted, and describe a document-preservation plan without predicting the licensing outcome.

### Objective 2.4 — curriculum page 13

**Teachable proposition.** Section 475.175(1)(a) supplies the core fingerprint chain. The applicant submits digital fingerprint data. The fingerprints go to the Florida Department of Law Enforcement's Division of Criminal Justice Information Systems for criminal-history processing and to the Federal Bureau of Investigation for criminal-history processing. The resulting information goes to DBPR so the agency can determine whether the applicant is statutorily qualified for examination.

The fingerprints are evidence, not the final legal conclusion. Agency staff compare the returned information, the application, supporting documents, and qualification standards. When a record requires Commission consideration, administrative materials may present the matter to the Commission, including through the Summary of Applicants process referenced by the FREC syllabus. A summary or agenda placement is not itself a finding of guilt, automatic denial, or approval. The Commission must act under the governing law and record.

A name, date, or record mismatch should be addressed through the official process. The applicant should retain the transaction control number or receipt, respond to a lawful deficiency request by its deadline, and submit accurate certified dispositions where required. A school cannot promise processing time or tell a learner that silence means clearance.

**Worked Florida example.** Sam discloses a prior case and submits fingerprints. The fingerprint return reflects a similar name and a disposition different from Sam's certified court record. The sound response is to preserve both records, follow the Department's correction or deficiency instructions, and provide reliable identifying and disposition evidence. It is not sound to resubmit a changed answer merely to match the database or to assume staff will resolve the discrepancy without evidence.

**Guided primary-source task.** Diagram section 475.175(1)(a) as five labeled nodes: applicant, FDLE division, FBI, DBPR, and statutory qualification decision. Under each arrow, state what moves between nodes. Then inspect the current DBPR fingerprint instructions and record the approved submission method and access date without hard-coding a vendor price into your permanent notes.

**Independent new-fact application.** An applicant submits fingerprints through an unauthorized service, receives no tracking information, and schedules travel for an exam based on a course certificate alone. Identify every unsupported assumption, the current agency records the applicant should obtain, and the difference between course completion and exam authorization.

### Objective 2.5 — curriculum page 14

**Teachable proposition.** Prelicense, postlicense, continuing, and reactivation education solve different regulatory problems and are not interchangeable.

| Education stage | Core requirement | Timing and consequence | Primary authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales-associate prelicense | Course I; 63 fifty-minute hours inclusive of examination; satisfactory course completion | Before initial licensure path proceeds; course completion alone is not a license | s. 475.17(2); Rule 61J2-3.008 |
| Sales-associate postlicense | Commission-prescribed or approved course work totaling at least 45 fifty-minute hours within the statutory ceiling | Before first renewal following initial licensure; failure makes the sales-associate license null and void and requires requalification | s. 475.17(3); Rule 61J2-3.020 |
| Continuing education | At least 14 fifty-minute hours during each biennium for renewal of an active license, subject to current rule content | Recurring renewal after the initial-license treatment; cannot substitute a remembered course mix for the effective rule | s. 475.182(1); Rule 61J2-3.009 |
| Reactivation education | 14 hours when involuntarily inactive twelve months or less; 28 hours when more than twelve but fewer than twenty-four months | Required to reactivate within those windows; more than two years involuntarily inactive leads to expiration and null-and-void status | s. 475.183(2); Rule 61J2-3.010 |

Rule 61J2-3.008 makes a score of seventy percent or higher on the prescribed end-of-course examination satisfactory Course I completion and imposes the course's delivery and assessment conditions. That school examination is distinct from the state license examination. Passing either one does not by itself authorize practice.

Education exemptions must be read narrowly. Rule 61J2-3.008(8) exempts an active member in good standing of The Florida Bar who is otherwise qualified from the prescribed prerequisite education course for sales-associate licensure. It does not say that Bar membership supplies a real estate license, removes the state examination, or excuses later education. Section 475.17(6) addresses an applicant or licensee with a four-year or higher degree in real estate from an accredited institution and exempts the initial and postlicense course requirements stated there. Rule 61J2-3.012 governs equivalency evidence. The current law and applicant's actual transcript control.

**Worked Florida example.** Riley passes Course I and the state exam, receives a sales-associate license, and assumes the same 14-hour continuing-education requirement applies before the first renewal. The categories are being collapsed. The first-renewal postlicense requirement is governed by section 475.17(3), and noncompletion has a null-and-void consequence. The recurring 14-hour requirement under section 475.182 should not be used as a substitute.

**Guided primary-source task.** Read sections 475.17(2), (3), (5), and (6), 475.182(1), and 475.183(2), plus Rules 61J2-3.008, .009, .010, .012, and .020. Complete the table above from the sources, adding the controlling paragraph for examination, hardship, and exemption. Flag Rule 61J2-3.013's limited wording: its hardship definitions expressly address postlicense and reactivation education, so do not invent a prelicense hardship definition from that text.

**Independent new-fact application.** A newly licensed sales associate with a general business bachelor's degree completes fourteen hours of continuing education before first renewal but no postlicense course. Determine which facts matter, whether the degree described fits the statutory degree exemption, and the consequence stated by section 475.17(3)(c). Do not assume a hardship extension without an application and Commission action.

### Objective 2.6 — curriculum page 15

**Teachable proposition.** The three principal individual categories in this part of the syllabus are broker, sales associate, and broker associate.

- A **broker** performs broker-defined acts for another for compensation or expected consideration and may operate a brokerage in a legally permitted form. A broker who employs associates owes statutory supervision duties.
- A **sales associate** performs acts in the broker definition under the direction, control, or management of another person. The associate's license does not authorize an independent brokerage.
- A **broker associate** is qualified to be issued a broker license but operates as a sales associate in the employ of another. Broker-level qualification does not change the person's operating role while registered as a broker associate.

Section 475.161 permits a broker associate or sales associate to be licensed as an individual or, with Department of State authorization, in specified professional-entity form, but it does not permit that person to register or be licensed as a general partner, member, manager, officer, or director of a brokerage firm under section 475.15. Do not confuse a professional designation attached to an individual's legal name with a separately registered brokerage.

License category and specialty are different. A sales associate may specialize in commercial property, and a broker may specialize in residential leasing; specialty does not alter the category. Likewise, REALTOR® membership is neither a license category nor evidence of active status.

**Worked Florida example.** Morgan passed the broker examination and qualifies for a broker license but chooses to work under Broker Chen as a broker associate. Morgan may have broker-level qualifications, yet operates in the associate role under Chen. Morgan may not open an independent branch simply by printing “broker” on a card; the registered category and relationship control.

**Guided primary-source task.** Read section 475.01(1)(a), (b), and (j), then section 475.161. For each category, write who directs the person's licensed activity and whether the person may independently operate a brokerage. Identify the sentence that prevents specified associate-owned professional entities from becoming brokerage firms merely by entity designation.

**Independent new-fact application.** A sales associate forms a professional limited liability company, receives Department of State authorization, and announces that the company is now an independent brokerage. Explain what section 475.161 permits, what it expressly does not permit, and which brokerage-registration provision must be checked.

### Objective 2.7 — curriculum page 16

**Teachable proposition.** Section 475.01(1)(a) defines covered real estate services broadly. For another and for direct or indirect compensation, promised compensation, expected compensation, or intent to receive it, the definition reaches appraising within applicable limits, auctioning, selling, exchanging, buying, renting, and offering, attempting, or agreeing to do those acts. It also reaches public advertising or holding out, procuring sellers, purchasers, lessors, or lessees, directing or assisting in procurement, negotiating, assisting negotiation or closing, and advertising rental-property information or lists.

The analyst should not reduce the definition to “signing a contract” or “getting paid at closing.” An attempt can qualify; promised or expected value can qualify; and procuring a prospect can qualify. Section 475.01(3) says one covered act is sufficient to bring a person within the operative meaning, excluding statutory exceptions, and each prohibited act can be a separate offense.

The usual elements are a useful starting point, not a substitute for the full text:

1. a covered act or holding out;
2. performed for another;
3. involving covered real property, interests, business enterprises, or opportunities;
4. with the compensation or expectation described by statute; and
5. no applicable exemption.

Section 475.17(1)(b) adds a qualification warning: specified unlicensed acts during the year before application or while an application is pending may support disapproval, and the paragraph says the conduct can matter regardless of compensation. Therefore “I did it for free while waiting for my license” is not a safe workaround.

**Worked Florida example.** Nico agrees to introduce an investor to off-market homeowners and participate in price negotiations. Payment is a “marketing bonus” due only if a purchase closes. Procurement, negotiation assistance, action for another, and expected transaction-based value all appear. The bonus label does not remove the compensation fact, and one transaction is enough to require the exemption and licensure analysis.

**Guided primary-source task.** Parse section 475.01(1)(a) into verbs, object or property, relationship, and compensation. Then read subsection (3) and section 475.17(1)(b). Write a rule statement that preserves the ordinary compensation element while accurately explaining why uncompensated preapplication conduct can still affect qualification under the separate paragraph.

**Independent new-fact application.** An unlicensed assistant schedules showings from a script, then answers a buyer's price objection and suggests an offer amount while the licensee is unavailable. Separate the ministerial and negotiation facts, identify which verb in section 475.01 is implicated, and state what office policy should prevent the boundary crossing.

### Objective 2.8 — curriculum page 17

**Teachable proposition.** Unlicensed activity includes performing a covered act without the required license, holding oneself out as entitled to perform it, or acting while a license or registration does not authorize practice. Common danger points include negotiating price or lease terms, procuring parties for transaction-based compensation, independently showing or marketing another's property, receiving a referral payment for licensed services outside a statutory exception, practicing after a license ceases to be in force, and treating course or examination completion as a license.

Section 475.41 makes a contract for commission or compensation for covered acts invalid unless the broker or sales associate was licensed and registered under the chapter when the service was performed. Section 475.42 contains prohibited acts and criminal consequences that must be read in their current form. Administrative enforcement under Chapter 455 can also be relevant. A learner should identify the conduct and refer to the actual provision rather than use “unlicensed” as a vague criticism.

Supervision does not convert every act by an unlicensed employee into a licensed act. An assistant may carry out genuinely administrative functions within office policy, but cannot cross into procurement, negotiation, or other regulated conduct simply because a broker is somewhere in the organization. Likewise, an inactive license is not permission to practice.

**Worked Florida example.** Taylor completes Course I on Friday, tells a seller that the license is “basically issued,” and negotiates a listing on Saturday. Taylor passes the state examination the following week. The later exam result does not retroactively authorize Saturday's holding out and negotiation. Analyze the conduct at the time it occurred and keep the course certificate, examination result, license issuance, and active registration separate.

**Guided primary-source task.** Read sections 475.01(3), 475.17(1)(b), 475.41, and 475.42(1). Identify one consequence directed at compensation agreements, one qualification consequence, and the single-act rule. Then find the current DBPR method for verifying a license's category and status; record the search date and fields reviewed.

**Independent new-fact application.** A licensed but voluntarily inactive sales associate offers to negotiate a cousin's commercial lease for no fee and advertises online that the associate is “available for Florida deals again.” Identify the status, holding-out, act, compensation, and relationship issues separately. Explain why the absence of a fee does not end every part of the analysis.

### Objective 2.9 — curriculum page 18

**Teachable proposition.** Section 475.011 exemptions are specific fact patterns, not a general “no license needed” list. The person claiming an exemption should prove every limiting fact. The current statute contains the following categories, summarized here for issue spotting:

| Subsection | Exempt person or activity | Limiting fact that must not be dropped |
|---:|---|---|
| (1) | Attorney in fact for executing contracts or conveyances; attorney at law or CPA within professional duties; specified court- or will-appointed fiduciaries; specified charitable or family-purpose trustee | Scope and source of authority control; a professional title does not exempt unrelated brokerage |
| (2) | An individual or entity selling, exchanging, or leasing its own real property | Not available to the extent a commission- or transactionally compensated agent, employee, or contractor makes ordinary-course sales, exchanges, or leases to the public |
| (3) | Employee of a public utility, rural electric cooperative, railroad, or state or local government acting for the employer | Must act within employment and receive no compensation in addition to salary for the transaction activity |
| (4) | Salaried employee of an owner, or the owner's registered broker, working in an onsite rental office of an apartment community | Employer, salary, onsite location, apartment community, and leasing capacity all matter |
| (5) | Salaried manager of a condominium or cooperative apartment complex handling rentals of individual units | Arranged rentals may not exceed one year |
| (6) | Person or entity handling specified FCC-regulated radio, television, or cable enterprise transactions | A Chapter 475 licensee must handle the part involving land, buildings, fixtures, and improvements |
| (7) | Full-time graduate student in a Commission-approved Florida appraisal degree program | Direct supervision, program-related appraisal activity, and report issued in supervisor's name are required |
| (8) | Owner reselling the owner's own timeshare periods; qualifying exchange company activities | Own-use and statutory Chapter 721 limits control |
| (9) | Person registered, licensed, or certified by DBPR under Part II as an appraiser or trainee appraiser | Must perform appraisals in accordance with Part II |
| (10) | Person using the unit-rule method to appraise a railroad or terminal company for ad valorem taxation | Limited method, property, and tax purpose |
| (11) | Person or entity renting or advertising transient occupancy in a Chapter 509 public lodging establishment | Transient occupancy and licensed-establishment conditions matter |
| (12) | Specified securities dealer or federally insured depository institution and related entity handling a business-enterprise transaction with an accredited investor | Does not exempt unrelated land, building, fixture, or improvement transactions |
| (13) | Apartment owner or property-management firm paying an unlicensed tenant a finder or referral benefit | Value may not exceed $50 per transaction, and the unlicensed person may not advertise or promote procurement services |

Do not memorize only the profession or property type. For example, “owner” is incomplete without the own-property and compensation limitations; “attorney” is incomplete without scope of legal duties; and “tenant referral” is incomplete without value and conduct limits.

**Worked Florida example.** A developer owns a subdivision through an LLC and hires an unlicensed independent contractor for $2,000 per closed lot to meet buyers and negotiate terms. The developer points to the own-property exemption. Section 475.011(2) expressly withdraws that exemption to the extent a commission- or transactionally compensated agent, employee, or independent contractor makes sales, exchanges, or leases to the public in the ordinary course. Entity ownership does not erase the compensation and conduct facts.

**Guided primary-source task.** Read all thirteen subsections of section 475.011. Select four exemptions from different rows above and create an element checklist in the statute's words. For subsection (2), underline the exception to the exemption. For subsection (13), distinguish a permitted introduction from prohibited advertising or promotion of procurement services.

**Independent new-fact application.** An unlicensed tenant posts weekly advertisements offering to find renters for the apartment community and receives a $45 rent credit for each signed lease. Analyze subsection 475.011(13) one element at a time. Explain why satisfying the dollar limit alone does not establish the exemption.

### Objective 2.10 — curriculum page 19

**Teachable proposition.** “Licensure” and “registration” can overlap in general regulatory vocabulary, but Chapter 475 uses them to answer different operational questions. Section 455.01 defines “license” broadly for Chapter 455 to include a permit, registration, certificate, or license. Within Chapter 475, an individual broker, broker associate, or sales associate is licensed based on qualification, while brokerage entities and relevant partners, members, officers, and directors are registered under section 475.15. The Department's records also connect an associate to the employer under whose direction the associate operates.

A useful working distinction is:

- **Licensure** addresses whether the individual has been found qualified for a category and issued the state credential.
- **Registration** identifies an entity or legally material relationship through which brokerage is operated.

That distinction is not permission to ignore the exact statute's vocabulary. Both must be current and effective where required. An individual sales associate can possess a license yet lack authority to operate because the license is inactive or the necessary employer relationship is not in force. A brokerage entity's registration can be canceled automatically during a period when at least one active broker member's license or registration is not in force under section 475.15.

**Worked Florida example.** Elise holds a current sales-associate license but leaves Broker A on Monday and expects to join Broker B next week. The individual credential did not vanish, but the prior employer relationship no longer authorizes work. Section 475.23 says a sales associate's license ceases to be in force upon changing employer and requires notice. Elise must complete the current Department process and verify the resulting active registration before performing licensed services for Broker B.

**Guided primary-source task.** Compare sections 455.01(4), 475.15, 475.161, 475.181, and 475.23. Prepare two examples where “license” is the precise term and two where “registration” is the precise term. Then explain why a database record reading “current” is insufficient unless category, status, employer, and effective relationship are also confirmed.

**Independent new-fact application.** A corporation is registered as a brokerage, but its sole active broker dies unexpectedly. A licensed sales associate tells clients that the corporation's registration alone allows business to continue. Identify the entity and individual issues, the automatic-cancellation language to review, and the emergency-broker rule the firm must follow before resuming operations.

### Objective 2.11 — curriculum page 20

**Teachable proposition.** Mutual recognition is a written arrangement under which Florida and another jurisdiction recognize specified education, experience, or examination qualifications for eligible nonresident licensees. It is not automatic interstate practice, a transfer of the foreign license, or a general promise that each state accepts every license from the other. “Reciprocity” is often used loosely; the FREC syllabus requires learners to distinguish it from Florida's agreement-specific mutual-recognition route.

Section 475.180(1) authorizes the Commission, in its discretion, to enter written agreements designed to provide comparable nonresident opportunities for Florida licensees. Subsection (2) keeps nonresident applicants and licensees subject to Florida rules and Part I. The actual agreement and current DBPR instructions decide eligibility.

DBPR's official page, checked September 1, 2026, listed ten participating states: Alabama, Arkansas, Connecticut, Georgia, Illinois, Kentucky, Mississippi, Nebraska, Rhode Island, and West Virginia. That roster is an operational snapshot, not a permanent course promise. The same page stated core conditions including non-Florida residency at application, a valid current active license in good standing from the participating jurisdiction, original qualification there through its education and examination rather than reciprocity, and passage of a Florida-specific law examination. It described that examination as forty one-point questions with thirty points required. Every one of those administrative facts must be rechecked on DBPR's current page and the particular agreement when a learner applies.

Mutual recognition does not waive accurate application responses, fingerprinting, fees, license issuance, Florida status and employer requirements, or postlicense and renewal duties after issuance. A Florida resident cannot manufacture eligibility merely by obtaining an out-of-state license.

**Worked Florida example.** Devon lives in Florida and holds an active Georgia sales license obtained when Georgia accepted a third state's license by reciprocity.
