## Authority map

The Legislature enacts statutes. The Florida Real Estate Commission (FREC or Commission) is the seven-member body created within the Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR) to exercise the powers assigned by law. The Division of Real Estate (DRE) supplies the Chapter 475 services identified by statute. The Commission adopts rules within delegated authority and decides matters placed before it; the Department and Division perform administrative, licensing, examination, legal, recordkeeping, and investigative work.

These bodies collaborate, but they are not interchangeable:

| Source or actor | Function in this section | Limit to remember |
|---|---|---|
| Florida Legislature | Enacts Chapters 455 and 475 and delegates authority | An agency cannot amend a statute with a form or informal statement |
| FREC | Exercises assigned rulemaking, education, qualification, and adjudicative responsibilities | Must act within statutory authority and effective procedural law |
| DBPR | Umbrella department administering professional regulation | Department powers do not erase powers reserved to the Commission |
| DRE | Provides the Chapter 475 services listed in section 475.021 | Administration and investigation are not the same as final Commission action |
| Florida Administrative Code | Publishes effective agency rules, including Division 61J2 | A proposed rule, repealed rule, or old course summary is not current law |
| Form, checklist, portal, or FAQ | Communicates an operational process | Verify currency and do not treat guidance as higher authority than statute or rule |

When answering a status or authority question, name the decision maker, cite the controlling provision, identify the operative facts, and then verify the current administrative step. Avoid statements such as “DBPR approved it” when the record shows only receipt, or “FREC requires it” when the source is merely a provider policy.

## Objective-level application pages

### Objective 3.1 — curriculum page 22

**Teachable proposition.** Section 475.02 creates FREC within DBPR and specifies a seven-member Commission:

- four members must be licensed brokers who each held an active license for the five years before appointment;
- one member must be a licensed broker or licensed sales associate who held an active license for the two years before appointment;
- two members must be persons who are not and have never been brokers or sales associates; and
- at least one of all seven members must be sixty years of age or older.

The Governor appoints the members, subject to Senate confirmation, for four-year terms. The category requirements and age overlay must all be satisfied. The sixty-or-older member need not occupy a particular professional or consumer seat unless another controlling appointment record says so.

Section 455.207 supplies general board-operation rules. The Commission annually elects a chairperson and vice chairperson from its members, must meet at least annually, and may meet more often as necessary. A quorum is required for official business. Unless another law controls, at least fifty-one percent of appointed members form a quorum, and a majority of the quorum is needed for official action. Meeting attendance, quorum, and voting should be verified from the actual public record rather than assumed from a full roster.

Section 475.02(3) also permits a member who is a licensed broker or sales associate and holds an active real estate school permit, instructor permit, or both to offer, conduct, or teach an authorized course to the extent the permit authorizes it. Commission membership alone is not a teaching credential.

**Worked Florida example.** A proposed roster has four brokers with six active years each, one sales associate with eighteen active months, and two members who never held a broker or sales-associate license. One consumer member is sixty-two. The roster fails the two-year requirement for the fifth professional member even though it satisfies the four broker seats, two consumer seats, and age overlay. Counting seven people is not enough; each seat must be tested.

**Guided primary-source task.** Read section 475.02(1)–(3) and section 455.207(2)–(4). Draw seven seats and label the credential and lookback requirement for each. Apply the age overlay without assigning it to an invented seat. Then calculate the ordinary quorum for seven appointed members and explain why a vacant seat or special statute requires checking the actual appointment record.

**Independent new-fact application.** A nominee was an active sales associate for three years, became voluntarily inactive six months before appointment, and is proposed for the fifth professional seat. Identify the exact lookback wording, apply the inactive period to the two years immediately preceding appointment, and explain why an earlier three-year active period does not satisfy that seat's stated requirement. Keep statutory qualification separate from gubernatorial appointment and Senate confirmation.

### Objective 3.2 — curriculum page 23

**Teachable proposition.** FREC's responsibilities are commonly organized as executive, quasi-legislative, and quasi-judicial functions. Those labels describe the nature of an act; they do not create authority beyond the statute.

- **Executive or administrative responsibility.** The Commission administers assigned provisions, evaluates qualifications, directs educational policy, and works through the Department and DRE. Section 475.03 permits delegation by resolution of duties and powers except disciplinary powers and rulemaking; the Commission may not delegate those reserved functions through that provision. Section 475.021 separately permits majority-vote delegation to an appropriate division and rescission of the delegation.
- **Quasi-legislative responsibility.** Under section 475.05, the Commission may enact bylaws for its government and adopt rules under Chapter 120 to implement laws conferring powers or duties. It may decide questions of practice arising in proceedings, with regard to Chapter 475 and rules then in force. A rule must implement delegated law; it cannot contradict or enlarge the statute merely because policy would be convenient.
- **Quasi-judicial responsibility.** The Commission applies law to records in matters such as qualifications and discipline, issues final agency action where authorized, and imposes only sanctions the law permits. Section 475.25 lists potential Commission action including denial, probation, suspension, revocation, fines, and reprimand when supported by a statutory ground and proper process.

Education is an express duty. Section 475.04 directs the Commission to foster education of brokers, broker associates, sales associates, and instructors in ethical, legal, and business principles, and authorizes prescribed or approved courses and publications. Regulatory action on a filing is not promotional support for the school or course.

Legal-process boundaries protect impartiality. Under section 475.03, Commission disciplinary and rulemaking powers are not delegated by that provision. Independent legal counsel for a specific matter requires prior Attorney General approval, and an attorney may not both prosecute a matter and advise the Commission on that same matter.

**Worked Florida example.** FREC directs DRE staff to evaluate whether a submitted application is complete under established criteria. That is administrative implementation. FREC later considers the record and decides a contested qualification question; that is quasi-judicial application of law. FREC proposes a generally applicable rule within delegated authority and follows Chapter 120; that is quasi-legislative. Calling all three “licensing” would hide different procedures and decision makers.

**Guided primary-source task.** Read sections 475.03, 475.04, 475.05, and the opening sanctions language of section 475.25(1). Assign each statutory verb to executive, quasi-legislative, quasi-judicial, or education functions and justify the choice. Identify the two powers section 475.03 does not permit the Commission to delegate.

**Independent new-fact application.** Commission staff posts a new website checklist adding a qualification that does not appear in statute or effective rule, then treats the checklist as binding in every case. Identify the authority-level problem, the sources to compare, and the procedure needed before a generally applicable requirement can be enforced. Do not assume the checklist is invalid without examining the actual delegated authority.

### Objective 3.3 — curriculum page 24

**Teachable proposition.** DBPR is the department named in section 475.01(1)(e) and in the general definitions of section 455.01. It supports multiple regulated professions through department-wide functions. Section 455.203 assigns responsibilities that include renewal procedure, board executive-director appointments subject to board approval, board-member training, rulemaking to implement Chapter 455, recording proceedings, investigator selection, and approval of professional-license applications that meet all statutory and rule requirements.

DRE is the specialized operating division for Chapter 475. Section 475.021(1) says all services concerning the chapter—including recordkeeping, examination, legal, and investigative services, plus Chapter 455 services necessary to perform Chapter 475 duties—are provided by DRE. The list is illustrative rather than exhaustive. Under subsection (2), DRE is funded by Commission fees and assessments, and Commission-collected funds are used only for real estate regulation.

FREC remains the statutory Commission. DRE's investigation does not itself prove a violation; DBPR's receipt does not itself establish completeness; and staff processing does not substitute for a Commission decision where law reserves the decision. Conversely, FREC does not personally perform every clerical, examination, or investigative step.

**Worked Florida example.** A consumer files a complaint through DBPR. DRE staff receives records and investigates. If the matter proceeds, the legally authorized panel or Commission acts at later stages. The statement “the Commission investigated me and found a violation when the complaint was uploaded” compresses intake, investigation, probable cause, adjudication, and final action into one event. The record should name the actual stage and actor.

**Guided primary-source task.** Read section 475.021 sentence by sentence and section 455.203(1)–(10). Make a responsibility chart for Legislature, FREC, DBPR, and DRE. For examination, recordkeeping, investigation, application approval, rulemaking, and discipline, cite the provision and mark whether the responsibility is administrative, delegated, or reserved.

**Independent new-fact application.** An applicant receives an email from DRE requesting a missing document and tells an employer, “FREC denied my license.” Identify what the email actually proves, what decision has not been shown, and which records would establish a deficiency closure, Commission decision, or Department issuance.

### Objective 3.4 — curriculum page 25

**Teachable proposition.** License records use terms that answer different questions. A careful status review asks whether the credential is current, whether it is active, whether an employer or business registration is in force, and whether a disciplinary or statutory event limits authority.

| Term | Meaning or effect in this course | Controlling source to check |
|---|---|---|
| Current | The credential remains within its effective licensing period or record category; “current” alone does not establish active authority | Current DBPR record plus Chapters 455 and 475 |
| Active | The license and required registered relationship authorize the category's licensed activity, subject to any order or condition | Ch. 475 and current DBPR record |
| Voluntarily inactive | Results when a licensee applies to DBPR for inactive status and pays the prescribed fee | s. 475.01(1)(m) |
| Involuntarily inactive | Results when a license is not renewed at the end of the prescribed license period | ss. 475.01(1)(g), 475.182(3) |
| Ceases to be in force | Section 475.23 consequence when a broker or school changes business address or an associate or instructor changes employer; notice is due within ten days | s. 475.23 and current change form |
| Probation | A Commission disciplinary disposition governed by its final order; it is not a synonym for inactivity | s. 475.25(1), (4) |
| Suspended | Authority is removed for the ordered period; section 475.25 permits suspension up to ten years where a ground is proven | s. 475.25 and final order |
| Revoked | The license, registration, or permit is withdrawn by authorized final action | s. 475.25 and final order |
| Null and void | No operative credential remains; examples include specified postlicense failure or more than two years of involuntary inactivity | ss. 475.17(3)(c), 475.183(2)(b) |
| Canceled | Can describe automatic cancellation of a brokerage entity registration without an active broker member, or revocation/cancellation of a license issued by mistake | ss. 475.15, 475.25(2) |
| Authority voided or not in force | A consequence description, not a shortcut status. Identify the event and governing provision | The particular statute, rule, or final order |

Section 475.31 creates a further derivative consequence: an order suspending or revoking a broker automatically makes the licenses of associates registered with that broker, and specified entity participants, involuntarily inactive while the broker's license is inoperative or until a new employment or connection is secured. The associates are not automatically found guilty of the broker's misconduct; their operating relationship has lost its active broker.

**Worked Florida example.** A database shows a sales-associate license as “current, inactive.” The holder argues that “current” permits one transaction. The record answers two axes: the credential has not become null and void, but inactive status does not authorize licensed activity. The associate must satisfy the applicable activation requirements and verify an effective employer relationship before acting.

**Guided primary-source task.** Read sections 475.01(1)(g) and (m), 475.15, 475.17(3)(c), 475.23, 475.25(1)–(2), 475.31, and 475.183(2). For every row in the table, supply a statutory phrase and write what fact the status does **not** prove. Use a current DBPR lookup only as evidence of the displayed record, not as a substitute for the law.

**Independent new-fact application.** A broker's license is suspended, and an innocent sales associate registered with that broker receives a lead the next day. Identify the broker's status, the associate's derivative status, the difference between discipline and inactivity, and the lawful step the associate must complete before providing licensed service.

### Objective 3.5 — curriculum page 26

**Teachable proposition.** Active and inactive are authority-to-practice classifications. A current active status, together with the required registration and absence of a limiting order, allows licensed activity in the person's category. Voluntarily inactive status preserves a current credential at the licensee's request but does not permit licensed activity. Involuntarily inactive status follows nonrenewal and also does not permit practice.

The distinction matters because an inactive person may still truthfully possess a Florida license while being prohibited from operating. “Licensed” is therefore not a complete response to a consumer or employer asking whether the person may perform a transaction today. Verify category, current/expired condition, active/inactive status, employer or entity relationship, and discipline.

For a sales associate or broker associate, activation also depends on an authorized registered employer. Section 475.215(2) limits an associate to one registered employer at a time. Section 475.23 addresses the effect of changing employer. A broker's active license and the brokerage's effective registration present related but distinct checks.

**Worked Florida example.** Quinn voluntarily places a broker license inactive while taking a job outside real estate. Six months later, a former client offers a referral fee if Quinn negotiates a purchase. The license may remain current, but voluntarily inactive status does not authorize brokerage. Quinn must use the current reactivation process, satisfy education and fee requirements, and verify active status before performing the act; future activation cannot retroactively authorize it.

**Guided primary-source task.** Read section 475.01(1)(g) and (m), sections 475.182(3) and 475.183(1)–(3), and current Rules 61J2-1.014 and 61J2-3.009. Create a decision tree starting with “Is the license active?” Include voluntary choice, failure to renew, education, fees, employer relationship, and status verification. Keep voluntary and involuntary inactivity on separate branches.

**Independent new-fact application.** A sales associate's portal record says “current, active,” but names a former employer. The associate has accepted a new broker's offer but no effective change appears. Explain why the status label alone is incomplete, which statute governs the employer change, and what verification is needed before the first showing.

### Objective 3.6 — curriculum page 27

**Teachable proposition.** Involuntarily inactive status is caused by failure to renew at the end of the prescribed license period. Section 475.182(3) makes the reversion automatic. Section 475.183(2) then creates three time bands:

1. **Twelve months or less:** reactivation requires satisfactory completion of at least fourteen hours of Commission-prescribed continuing education.
2. **More than twelve months but fewer than twenty-four months:** reactivation requires satisfactory completion of twenty-eight hours of Commission-prescribed education.
3. **More than two years:** the license automatically expires and becomes null and void without further Commission or Department action.

The boundary language matters. Exactly twelve months remains in the first band. More than twelve and fewer than twenty-four belongs in the second. “More than two years” triggers the third statutory consequence. The text does not expressly assign the exact twenty-four-month instant to either phrase, so do not invent an answer for that instant; verify the Department's effective dates and current administration rather than estimate from a calendar year.

The statute directs DBPR to provide notice ninety days before expiration, prescribes current renewal fees for each missed period plus an applicable late fee, and allows the Commission by rule to set the late fee within a statutory cap. Do not memorize a fee from this course. Section 475.183(4) gives the Commission discretionary reinstatement authority for an individual whose license became void through illness or economic hardship, but the person must apply within six months after voiding, meet education requirements, pay appropriate fees, and otherwise remain eligible. That is a limited possible remedy, not automatic extra time.

Rule 61J2-3.010 controls the current reactivation course content. Rule 61J2-3.013 defines illness or economic hardship for postlicense and reactivation contexts and requires a written request; its words should not be expanded to an unrelated context.

**Worked Florida example.** Harper has been involuntarily inactive for sixteen months and completes fourteen hours of continuing education. The time falls in the more-than-twelve/fewer-than-twenty-four band, so fourteen hours is insufficient under section 475.183(2)(a). Harper must satisfy the twenty-eight-hour reactivation requirement and all current fee and application steps before DBPR shows active authority.

**Guided primary-source task.** Read sections 475.182(3)–(4) and 475.183(2)–(4), then Rules 61J2-3.010 and .013. Build a number line with day or month boundaries at twelve and twenty-four months. Add education, notice, fee categories, expiration, and the six-month discretionary reinstatement application window. Identify every use of “may” and “shall” or equivalent mandatory language.

**Independent new-fact application.** A license became null and void five months ago after extended involuntary inactivity. The former licensee experienced a documented illness but has completed no education. Identify the possible statutory route, every unmet condition, the deadline, and why the Commission's authority does not guarantee reinstatement.

### Objective 3.7 — curriculum page 28

**Teachable proposition.** Multiple licenses and group licenses solve different structural problems.

| Feature | Multiple broker licenses | Group license |
|---|---|---|
| Holder | Licensed broker only | Sales associate or broker associate working for an owner-developer structure |
| Purpose | Additional broker licenses shown necessary for conducting brokerage business | One owner-developer controls multiple connected, subsidiary, interlocking, or affiliated property-owning entities |
| Core authority | s. 475.215(1), Fla. Stat. | Rule 61J2-6.006, F.A.C., read with s. 475.011(2) |
| Required showing | Necessity and no likely prejudice or harm; qualification remains reviewable | Substantially common ownership or control and required entity certificates |
| Important limit | Not issued to the broker as a sales associate or broker associate; discipline reaches primary and multiple licenses | Does not authorize service for unrelated owners and is not a real estate advertising “team” |

Section 475.215(2) states the ordinary rule that a sales associate or broker associate has no more than one registered employer at a time. Rule 61J2-6.006 addresses a group-license situation in which an owner-developer proves that properties titled in different entities are sufficiently connected through common ownership or control. Each entity executes the certificate attached to the request. The group license does not turn the associate into an independent broker and does not extend to unrelated client property.

A multiple license is also not a branch-office registration. It is an additional license issued to a broker after the required showing. A group license is not “team or group advertising” under Rule 61J2-10.026. Shared words do not create shared legal functions.

**Worked Florida example.** Broker Alvarez wants to be the active broker for two unrelated brokerage corporations. That request raises section 475.215's multiple-license standard. By contrast, Sales Associate Brooks works for one owner-developer that holds three projects in affiliated LLCs under substantially common control. That request raises Rule 61J2-6.006's group-license standard. Giving Brooks a multiple broker license is impossible because Brooks is not the holder described by subsection 475.215(1).

**Guided primary-source task.** Read section 475.215 and current Rule 61J2-6.006. Create an element checklist for each route. Underline “broker, but not as a sales associate or as a broker associate” in the statute and the common-control facts in the rule. Then inspect DBPR's current group-license instruction and identify the entity certification required without assuming a form version.

**Independent new-fact application.** One sales associate is asked to sell units for four LLCs. Two share the same controlling owner, one has a minority investment from that owner but independent control, and one is unrelated. Determine which facts could support a group-license request, which entities require more evidence, and why the unrelated entity cannot be added merely for administrative convenience.

## Integrated application lab

Prepare an authority-and-status memorandum for this new fact pattern:

> Sunline Brokerage LLC's only active broker is suspended by final order. A broker associate says the LLC remains “current,” plans to act under a second company without changing registration, and describes the arrangement as both a multiple license and a group license. A DRE investigator has requested records, but FREC has not taken action against the associate.

Identify the status and registration consequences for the broker, entity, and associate; distinguish investigation from final action; apply the one-employer rule; and explain why neither multiple nor group licensing fits merely because two company names appear. Cite every conclusion and list facts or current agency records still needed. Do not describe any person or entity as approved, reinstated, or authorized without an effective record.
