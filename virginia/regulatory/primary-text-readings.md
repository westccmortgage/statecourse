### 18VAC135-20-330, verbatim

> Any unlawful act or violation of any of the provisions of Chapter 21 (§ 54.1-2100 et seq.) of
> Title 54.1 or of Chapter 5.1 (§ 36-96.1 et seq.) of Title 36 of the Code of Virginia or of the
> regulations of the board by any real estate salesperson, employee, partner or affiliate of a
> principal broker, supervising broker, or both, may not be cause for disciplinary action against
> the principal broker, supervising broker, or both, unless it appears to the satisfaction of the
> board that the principal broker, supervising broker, or both, knew or should have known of the
> unlawful act or violation and failed to take reasonable action under the circumstances to
> remedy the situation.

**This is a knew-or-should-have-known standard, not vicarious liability.** A course that teaches
"the broker is always responsible for the acts of the salesperson" is teaching the opposite of
what 330 says.

### 18VAC135-20-220 A, verbatim in substance

A licensee must disclose whom the licensee represents to an actual or prospective buyer or seller
who is **not** the client of the licensee, who is **not** represented by another licensee, and
with whom the licensee has **substantive discussions about a specific property**, under
§ 54.1-2138 A. The disclosure must be in writing at the earliest practical time, **but in no
event later than the time specific real estate assistance is first provided**. A licensee acting
as a dual or designated agent or representative must obtain the **written consent of all clients**
at the earliest practical time; consent is presumed from a client who signs a disclosure
complying with §§ 54.1-2139, 54.1-2139.01 and 54.1-2139.1. That disclosure must reach the buyer
**no later than the time an offer to purchase is presented** to the licensee who will present it
to the listing agent or seller.

### 18VAC135-20-300, first five subdivisions in substance

Misrepresentation or omission includes: bait-and-switch advertising; failing, as a licensee
representing a seller or landlord as a standard agent, to disclose in a timely manner to a
prospective purchaser or tenant **all material adverse facts pertaining to the physical condition
of the property that are actually known by the licensee**; failing to tender promptly every
written offer, counteroffer and rejection; failing to disclose in a timely manner to the
licensee's client all material facts related to the property or the transaction where the failure
would be a failure to exercise ordinary care; and failing to provide required written notices to
all principals in a timely manner.

**"Actually known" is the standard in subdivision 2.** It is not a duty to investigate. A course
that teaches a Virginia licensee must discover defects is overstating the rule.

## Operative statutory text, read 2026-08-20

The sections listed as "not yet read" above were read the same day. What follows is what the
text actually says, in the words that matter for instruction. These are the passages a Virginia
course most often gets wrong.

### § 54.1-2131 — Licensees engaged by sellers

**A** — seven duties: (1) perform in accordance with the brokerage agreement; (2) promote the
seller's interests by (a) marketing per the agreement, seeking a sale at the agreed price and
terms, **without being obliged to seek additional offers while the property is under contract**
unless agreed; (b) assisting in drafting and negotiating offers, counteroffers, amendments and
addenda **pursuant to § 54.1-2101.1**; (c) receiving and presenting written offers and
counteroffers in a timely manner **even when the property is already under contract**; (d)
providing reasonable assistance to satisfy contract obligations and facilitate settlement;
(3) maintain confidentiality of all personal and financial information and anything the client
asked to be kept confidential; (4) **exercise ordinary care**; (5) account in a timely manner for
all money and property; (6) **disclose material facts related to the property or the transaction
of which the licensee has actual knowledge**; (7) comply with the article, fair housing, and
other law not in conflict.

**B** — treat all prospective buyers honestly; do not knowingly give false information; disclose
**all material adverse facts pertaining to the physical condition of the property that are
actually known by the licensee**, in writing. Defective drywall as defined in § 36-156.1 is named
specifically. And the statute defines the boundary of "physical condition of the property": it
means the land and improvements on it, and **does not** refer to (i) matters outside the
boundaries of the land or relating to adjacent or nearby properties, (ii) matters relating to
governmental land use regulations, or (iii) matters relating to highways or public streets.

**C** — ministerial acts for a buyer do not breach the seller agreement and do not form a
brokerage relationship with the buyer.

### § 54.1-2132 — Licensees engaged by buyers

**A 1 is the rule most often stated wrongly:** a licensee engaged by a buyer shall
**enter into a brokerage agreement with the prospective buyer prior to showing property to that
prospective buyer.** In Virginia the agreement comes before the showing. The remaining duties
mirror § 54.1-2131: perform per the agreement; promote the buyer's interests; confidentiality;
ordinary care; timely accounting; disclose material facts of actual knowledge; comply with the
article and fair housing.

**B** — treat prospective sellers honestly; disclose actual knowledge of defective drywall; and,
in a residential transaction, **disclose to the seller whether the buyer intends to occupy the
property as a principal residence.** The buyer's statement of intent in the contract satisfies
this, and no cause of action arises from the disclosure, an inaccuracy in it, or its absence.

### § 54.1-2136 — Preconditions to brokerage relationship

Before entering any brokerage relationship the licensee shall advise the prospective client of
**(i) the type of brokerage relationship proposed by the broker** and **(ii) the broker's
compensation and whether the broker will share it with another broker who may have a brokerage
relationship with another party.**

### § 54.1-2137 — Commencement, termination, and what a brokerage agreement must contain

**A** — the relationship commences when the client engages the licensee and continues until
completion of performance, or the earlier of an agreed expiration date, mutual termination,
default, or termination under § 54.1-2139 G.

**B** — except as otherwise provided, the licensee shall enter into a brokerage agreement
**before providing brokerage services**. Preparing property-specific materials with the intent to
obtain a relationship does not require an agreement first.

**C** — brokerage agreements shall be in writing and shall: (1) **have a definite termination
date; if none is specified the agreement terminates 90 days after its date**; (2) state the amount
of the brokerage fees and how and when they are paid; (3) state the services to be rendered;
(4) include the other agreed terms; and (5) where entered with consent to dual representation,
carry the § 54.1-2139 A disclosures.

**D** — after termination, expiration, or completion, the licensee owes **no further duties**
except to (i) account for all money and property and (ii) keep confidential the client's personal
and financial information and anything the client asked be kept confidential.

### § 54.1-2138 — Disclosure of brokerage relationship, residential

**A** — on having a **substantive discussion about a specific property** with an actual or
prospective buyer or seller who is neither the licensee's client nor represented by another
licensee, disclose any brokerage relationship with another party. In writing, at the earliest
practical time, **and in no event later than when specific real estate assistance is first
provided.** If combined with other disclosures the disclosure must be **conspicuous — bold, all
capitals, underlined, or in a separate box.** The statute prints a model form.

**B** — for landlords and tenants the disclosure goes in the lease application or in the lease,
whichever comes first, and **does not apply to lease terms of less than two months.**

**C** — if the licensee's relationship changes, disclose that in writing to everyone already
involved.

**D** — keep copies of disclosures relating to fully executed purchase contracts for
**three years**, acknowledged or not.

### § 54.1-2138.1 — Limited service agent

Permitted **only pursuant to a written brokerage agreement** which (i) discloses the licensee is
acting as a limited service agent, (ii) lists the specific services the licensee will provide,
and (iii) lists the specific standard-agent duties from the applicable duty section that the
licensee **will not** provide. Conspicuous, bold or all capitals, underlined or boxed. The statute
prints the consent language, whose substance is that neither the other party nor the other party's
licensee has any legal obligation to help the client with the duties the limited service agent is
not performing.

### § 54.1-2139.1 — Designated agency

A principal or supervising broker may assign different affiliated licensees to different clients
in the same residential transaction. That is **not** dual agency so long as no designated agent
represents more than one client — but **the supervising broker is a dual agent or representative**.
Designated agents may not disclose client confidences except to their broker.

### § 54.1-2140 and § 54.1-2144

Payment or the promise of payment **does not create a brokerage relationship**. And the common law
of agency relative to brokerage relationships is **expressly abrogated to the extent inconsistent
with the article**.

### § 54.1-2110.1 — Duties of supervising broker

Every place of business, branch office, **and real estate team** must be supervised by a
supervising broker. "Reasonable and adequate supervision" is defined and includes being available
at reasonable times to review and approve documents; ensuring training and **written office
policies covering eight named areas** — escrow handling, fair housing, advertising and marketing,
negotiating and drafting, oversight of unlicensed assistants, agency and independent-contractor
relationships, distribution of new or amended law, and disclosure of information about the
physical condition of property; ensuring competent brokerage services; steps to ensure licensees
are currently licensed; oversight of affiliated teams and business entities; ensuring brokerage
agreements carry the supervising broker's name and contact information; and **keeping these
records for three years**. A supervising broker residing more than **50 miles** from a supervised
branch office must certify supervision in writing quarterly.

### § 36-96.3 — Virginia's protected classes

The Virginia Fair Housing Law list, as it appears in the statute:
**race, color, religion, national origin, sex, elderliness, source of funds, familial status,
sexual orientation, gender identity, military status** — and **disability**, which appears in
subdivisions 3 through 9. Virginia protects **more classes than the federal Fair Housing Act**,
and elderliness, source of funds, sexual orientation, gender identity and military status are the
additions a licensee is most likely to get wrong.

Two drafting rules come straight from subdivision 3: the use of **words or symbols associated
with a particular religion, national origin, sex, or race is prima facie evidence of an illegal
preference, and a general disclaimer does not overcome it**; but **reference alone to places of
worship** — churches, synagogues, temples, mosques — **is not** prima facie evidence.

### § 55.1-703 — the Virginia Residential Property Disclosure Act is buyer-beware

The section is headed *Required disclosures for buyer to beware; buyer to exercise necessary due
diligence*. The owner furnishes a **residential property disclosure statement provided by the Real
Estate Board on its website**, and the statement's content is a series of statements that
**the owner makes no representations** — as to the condition of the property, lot lines or the
ability to expand, adjacent parcels, historic district ordinances, Chesapeake Bay Act resource
protection areas, and further matters — coupled with advice that the purchaser exercise whatever
due diligence that purchaser deems necessary.

**Virginia is not a seller-disclosure state in the way most states are.** A course that teaches a
Virginia seller must complete a condition checklist is teaching another state's law. The seller's
statutory instrument tells the buyer to investigate.

**The version read carries the heading "(Effective until January 1, 2027)."** A successor version
exists and has not been read. Any statement in the curriculum about the disclosure statement's
contents must be rechecked before the course is delivered after that date.
