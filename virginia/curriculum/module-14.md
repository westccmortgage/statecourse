## Hour 53 — Virginia agency and license-law case lab

## 53.1 The method

Every Virginia relationship problem is solved the same way, and the method is worth more than any
individual answer:

**One — who is engaged by whom?** That selects the duty section: § 54.1-2131 seller, § 54.1-2132
buyer, § 54.1-2133 landlord, § 54.1-2134 tenant, § 54.1-2135 management.

**Two — does a written brokerage agreement exist, and does it satisfy § 54.1-2137 C?** Definite
termination date or 90 days; fee and how and when paid; services; the supervising broker's name and
contact information under § 54.1-2110.1 B 6. On the buyer side, § 54.1-2132 A 1 requires it **before
showing property**.

**Three — were the § 54.1-2136 preconditions given?** The type of relationship proposed, and the
compensation and whether it is shared.

**Four — is anyone in the conversation neither a client nor represented?** If so, § 54.1-2138 A
requires written disclosure of any relationship with another party on a substantive discussion about
a specific property, no later than when specific real estate assistance is first provided,
conspicuously.

**Five — is the firm on both sides?** Then it is dual under § 54.1-2139, or designated under
§ 54.1-2139.1 with the supervising broker as dual — and written consent from **all** clients is
required, timed by 18VAC135-20-220 A 3, failing which 18VAC135-20-270 2 makes it a conflict of
interest.

**Six — what is the standard for the duty in question?** Ordinary care; actual knowledge; timely; in
writing.

**Seven — what must be retained, and for how long?** Three years, under 18VAC135-20-185 C 1,
§ 54.1-2138 D, and § 54.1-2110.1 B 7.

**Eight — remember § 54.1-2144.** The common law of agency is abrogated to the extent inconsistent.
Do not answer from national doctrine.

## 53.2 Case A — the buyer who was shown a house first

*Facts.* At an open house on Saturday, Licensee M meets a couple, talks with them about the property
for twenty minutes, and then drives them to two other listings that afternoon. On Sunday they sign a
buyer brokerage agreement dated Sunday, with no termination date. On Monday they offer on one of
Saturday's houses, which is listed by Licensee M's own firm.

*Resolution.*

**§ 54.1-2132 A 1 was breached on Saturday.** A licensee engaged by a buyer shall enter into a
brokerage agreement with the prospective buyer **prior to showing property**. Signing on Sunday does
not cure Saturday.

**§ 54.1-2136 must have preceded the relationship** — the type of relationship proposed and the
compensation and whether shared. Nothing in the facts shows it was given.

**§ 54.1-2138 A applied at the open house.** The couple were neither M's clients nor represented, and
M had a substantive discussion about a specific property; if M's firm had a relationship with the
seller, written disclosure was required no later than when specific real estate assistance was first
provided — which was, at the latest, when M drove them to other properties.

**The Sunday agreement terminates 90 days from its date** under § 54.1-2137 C 1, since it states none.

**Monday makes the firm dual or designated.** Written consent from all clients under § 54.1-2139 or
§ 54.1-2139.1, timed by 18VAC135-20-220 A 3, or it is a conflict of interest under 18VAC135-20-270 2.

## 53.3 Case B — the offer that was not presented

*Facts.* A listing licensee receives a written offer $40,000 below list from a buyer whose lender
letter is a pre-qualification. Believing it a waste of the seller's time, the licensee emails the
buyer's agent "seller isn't entertaining anything under list" and does not tell the seller. Three
weeks later the seller learns of it.

*Resolution.*

**18VAC135-20-300 3** — failure to tender promptly to the client every written offer, counteroffer and
rejection — is a misrepresentation or omission. **§ 54.1-2131 A 2 c** requires receiving and presenting
written offers in a timely manner, and it does so even when the property is already under contract,
which is a fortiori here.

**§ 54.1-2131 A 4**, ordinary care, and **A 1**, performance in accordance with the brokerage
agreement, are also engaged.

**The pre-qualification is not a ground for withholding.** It is a fact to present **with** the offer,
so the seller can weigh it. The licensee substituted their judgment for the client's, which is the
error the rule exists to prevent.

**Broker exposure** is decided by **18VAC135-20-330** — knew or should have known, and failed to take
reasonable action to remedy. A firm whose written policies under § 54.1-2110.1 B 2 d and whose
document review under B 1 would have caught it is in a very different position from one with neither.

## 53.4 Case C — the confidence after closing

*Facts.* Eighteen months after a sale closes, the former seller's licensee is representing a buyer.
The buyer is considering a property whose owner is that former seller. The licensee knows, from the
earlier engagement, the former seller's financial circumstances and the lowest figure they said they
would take.

*Resolution.*

**§ 54.1-2137 D.** After termination, expiration or completion of performance, the licensee owes no
further duties **except** to account and to **keep confidential** the client's personal and financial
information and anything the client asked be kept confidential — unless the law provides otherwise or
the client consents **in writing**.

So the confidentiality duty **survives**, and the licensee may not use or disclose the information for
the new client's benefit. This creates a genuine conflict with the duty owed to the current buyer
under § 54.1-2132 A 3 to promote their interests, and the resolution is not to split the difference:
it is to escalate to the principal broker and, if the conflict cannot be managed, to decline the
engagement as to this property. **§ 54.1-2138 C** requires written disclosure to all clients and
customers already involved if the licensee's relationship changes.

## 53.5 Case D — the licensee's own purchase

*Facts.* A licensee, personally, offers to buy a listed property from an elderly seller represented by
another firm, without mentioning their licence, and later resells it.

*Resolution.*

**§ 54.1-2138.2** requires a licensee to disclose an ownership interest in specific real property.
**18VAC135-20-155 B** makes clear the Board reaches conduct in a **personal capacity**: a licensee
failing to comply with Chapter 21 or the regulations in performing acts covered by § 54.1-2100 may be
charged **regardless of whether the acts are in a personal or professional capacity**. And
**§ 36-96.2 A**'s owner exemption from fair housing **does not inure to the benefit of any Real Estate
Board licensee, regardless of capacity** — so buying and selling personally does not step outside
either regime.

### Hour 53 engagement check

**Question.** Resolve, with citations: a firm's Licensee P lists a house on 2 February with a written
agreement stating no termination date. On 20 May Licensee Q of the same firm brings a buyer with whom
Q signed a buyer agreement before showing anything. The seller accepts. No consent forms were signed
by anyone.

**Expected answer.** **The listing expired.** § 54.1-2137 C 1 — no definite termination date means the
agreement terminated **90 days after 2 February**, in early May, before 20 May. Unless it was extended
in writing, P had no brokerage agreement on 20 May, and § 54.1-2137 B requires an agreement before
providing brokerage services. **Q's side is compliant** as to timing — § 54.1-2132 A 1 requires the
agreement before showing property, and Q did that — though Q's agreement is subject to the same
§ 54.1-2137 C requirements. **The firm is on both sides**, so this is designated agency under
§ 54.1-2139.1 with the **supervising broker as dual agent**, or dual agency under § 54.1-2139;
**written consent from all clients was required**, timed under **18VAC135-20-220 A 3**, and its
absence makes this a **conflict of interest under 18VAC135-20-270 2**. Retention of any consents, once
obtained, is three years from execution under **18VAC135-20-185 C 1**.

## Hour 54 — Fair housing and marketing case lab

## 54.1 The audit method

**One — is any statement about people rather than property?** Advertising, conversation, screening
notes, or an internal email.

**Two — does it touch a protected basis?** Virginia's list from § 36-96.3: race, colour, religion,
national origin, sex, elderliness, source of funds, familial status, sexual orientation, gender
identity, military status, and disability. **Five of those are not federal.**

**Three — which subdivision does the conduct fall under?** Refusal (A 1); terms and conditions (A 2);
advertising (A 3); representing unavailability (A 4); access to services (A 5); covenants (A 6);
blockbusting (A 7); disability refusal (A 8); disability terms (A 9).

**Four — is a disability accommodation or modification in play?** § 36-96.3 B (i) and (ii).

**Five — does the advertising comply with 18VAC135-20-190?**

**Six — what is the correction, and who has to be told?**

## 54.2 Case A — the neighbourhood question

*Facts.* A relocating buyer asks, "What are the families like in Ashford Green? Is it a good area for
someone like us?"

*Resolution.* Answering with any description of who lives there — by race, national origin, religion,
family composition, age — is **steering**, and a statement about the composition of an area falls
within § 36-96.3 A 1 and A 2 if it influences availability or terms, and A 4 if it makes a property
seem unavailable.

The compliant answer names **objective sources the buyer consults for themselves**: school division
data and attendance zones, crime statistics published by the locality's police department, census
data, tax rates, commute times, and the association's documents. And it offers to show every property
that meets the buyer's stated criteria.

Record the buyer's criteria **in the buyer's own words**. It is the record that shows the search was
driven by the buyer.

## 54.3 Case B — the screening exception

*Facts.* A landlord's published criteria require income of three times the rent and no evictions in
five years. An applicant with a housing choice voucher has income of 1.6 times the rent; the voucher
pays the balance. The manager rejects them on income. Separately, the manager waives the income
criterion for a different applicant "who seemed like a good fit".

*Resolution.* Two violations.

**Source of funds.** Applying a whole-rent income multiple to an applicant whose subsidy pays most of
the rent screens out voucher holders as a class. The criterion must be applied to the **tenant's
portion**, and the Virginia protected class is **source of funds** under § 36-96.3 A 1 and A 2.

**Inconsistent application.** Waiving a published criterion for one applicant and not another is the
classic evidentiary pattern in a disparate-treatment case, whatever the reason given. Published
criteria are a defence only while they are applied uniformly.

The correction: rewrite the criterion to apply to the tenant's portion where a subsidy exists; apply
published criteria to every applicant identically; document each application; and remove the
discretion that produced the "good fit" waiver. The firm's written fair housing policy under
**§ 54.1-2110.1 B 2 b** is where the corrected criterion belongs.

## 54.4 Case C — the accommodation refused by policy

*Facts.* An applicant with a disability requests an exception to a no-pets policy for an assistance
animal and asks for a reserved parking space near the entrance. The manager replies that the building
has a no-pets policy, that pet rent is $45 a month if an exception is made, and that parking is
first-come.

*Resolution.* Both requests are **reasonable accommodations** under **§ 36-96.3 B (ii)** — changes to
rules, practices, policies or services necessary to afford equal opportunity to use and enjoy a
dwelling.

**A no-pets policy is not an answer**, because an assistance animal is not a pet. **Pet rent and pet
deposits may not be charged** for it, for the same reason — though the tenant remains responsible for
damage the animal causes and for its behaviour. **The parking request is an accommodation to a
first-come practice**, and the ordinary cost of making it falls on the provider.

Where the disability or the need is not readily apparent, a provider may seek reliable information
confirming the disability-related need, without demanding details of the diagnosis. **Virginia has
enacted specific provisions on assistance animals in rental housing and on the verification a provider
may request; this course does not state that procedure**, because those provisions have not been read
from primary text for this project. Read them, or refer — and do **not** refuse the request while the
question is open.

## 54.5 Case D — the advertisement, audited line by line

*Facts.* A post reads: *"Rare find in the Hollow! Charming 2BR cottage, walk to First Baptist, quiet
street — ideal for a mature couple or single professional. Owner prefers no children. Recently
renovated. Text Dana 555-0100. #JustListed"* — and the property has been under contract for six days.

*Audit.*

**"walk to First Baptist"** — under § 36-96.3 A 3, **reference alone to a place of worship is not
prima facie evidence** of an illegal preference. This line, on its own, is permissible. It is included
in the exercise precisely because students over-correct here.

**"ideal for a mature couple or single professional"** — preference on **elderliness** and **familial
status**, § 36-96.3 A 3.

**"Owner prefers no children"** — an express statement of preference on **familial status**, and a
statement of an intention to discriminate. This is the plainest violation on the page, and note that
even an exempt owner may not advertise this way: every § 36-96.2 exemption is expressed "except for
subdivision A 3 of § 36-96.3".

**"#JustListed" on a property under contract for six days** — property information inconsistent with
the property's current contract status, **18VAC135-20-190 B 3**, and a failure to update in a timely
manner after a material change to listing status, **B 4**. If the intent is to harvest buyer leads
rather than sell at those terms, **18VAC135-20-300 1**, bait and switch.

**No firm name, no advertising disclosure** — **18VAC135-20-190 A** requires advertising in the name
of the firm with the firm's licensed name clearly and legibly displayed, and **B 1** requires a clear,
legible and conspicuous advertising disclosure with the firm's name and office contact information as
the firm's written policy specifies.

**Compliant rewrite.** *"Two-bedroom cottage in the Hollow, recently renovated, on a quiet street.
[Firm's licensed name], [office contact information per firm policy]. Contact Dana, 555-0100."* —
posted only while the listing status is accurately represented.

### Hour 54 engagement check

**Question.** A licensee's internal note on a rental applicant reads: "Nice guy, National Guard, gets
deployed — probably won't be around, look for someone more stable." The applicant is rejected. What is
wrong, and what does the firm need to change?

**Expected answer.** **Military status is a Virginia protected class** under **§ 36-96.3**, and
rejecting an applicant on the basis of it violates **A 1** (refusal to rent) and **A 2** (terms and
conditions). The written note is direct evidence of the reason, which is what makes this case simple
and dangerous. The firm needs published, objective, written screening criteria applied uniformly and
documented for every applicant, with the deployment-related speculation removed entirely from the
process — and that criterion belongs in the firm's written fair housing policy, which
**§ 54.1-2110.1 B 2 b** requires a firm doing residential leasing to have. Note also that a fair
housing violation by an affiliated licensee can reach the supervising broker under
**18VAC135-20-330**, which names Chapter 5.1 of Title 36 expressly.

## Hour 55 — Leasing and property management case lab

## 55.1 The method

**One — which chapter of Title 55.1 governs?** Chapter 12 VRLTA for residential; Chapter 13
manufactured home lots; Chapter 14 nonresidential; Chapter 16 leases generally.

**Two — read the operative section and the sections around it**, at law.lis.virginia.gov, in its
current text. Do not answer from memory, and do not answer from a summary.

**Three — read the rental agreement**, which may give the tenant more than the statute requires but
not less where the Act sets a floor.

**Four — apply the licensing duties.** § 54.1-2133 landlord, § 54.1-2134 tenant, § 54.1-2135
management; § 54.1-2138 B disclosure in the lease application or the lease, whichever comes first, and
not applicable to terms under two months.

**Five — apply fair housing**, § 36-96.3, with source of funds and accommodation requests the two most
common.

**Six — apply the trust accounting rules.** 18VAC135-20-181 A 1 and A 2; 18VAC135-20-185 A.

**Seven — refer the legal conclusions.** Whether a notice was valid, whether a breach is material, and
what remedy is available are counsel's.

## 55.2 Case A — the maintenance request that became a habitability dispute

*Facts.* A tenant reports a failing heating system by text on 4 January. The manager sees the text,
schedules a vendor for 11 January, the vendor does not attend, and nothing further happens. On 22
January the tenant emails saying they will withhold rent and are consulting a lawyer.

*Resolution.*

**The record is the case.** What was reported, when, how, what was done, and when. A text seen and not
logged is the failure that decides this dispute, and it is a **§ 54.1-2135** timely-performance and
ordinary-care problem before it is anything else.

**Which chapter:** Chapter 12 of Title 55.1, the VRLTA, governs the landlord's obligation to maintain
fit premises and the tenant's remedies, including whether and how rent may be withheld or escrowed.
**This course states none of those provisions**, because Chapter 12 has not been read from primary
text for this project. They must be read now, in current text, before any position is taken.

**What the manager does immediately:** get the heat working; log everything retrospectively with
dates and sources; write to the owner; escalate to the principal broker; and route the withholding
question to counsel. **What the manager must not do** is tell the tenant whether they may lawfully
withhold, or tell the owner to treat non-payment as a default.

**What should have existed:** a request-to-completion tracking system with defined response times for
emergency and routine work, vendor accountability, and an escalation trigger when a scheduled visit
does not occur.

## 55.3 Case B — the deposit at move-out

*Facts.* A tenant vacates 30 April. The manager deducts carpet replacement in full for carpet
installed six years earlier, plus a $150 "administrative fee" not mentioned in the lease, and returns
the balance on 20 June with a one-line note.

*Resolution.*

**Three separate problems.**

**The carpet.** Ordinary wear and tear is not chargeable, and a six-year-old carpet has consumed most
of its useful life; charging full replacement transfers the landlord's capital cost to the tenant. The
defensible approach is depreciation over useful life, supported by the move-in and move-out condition
record — which is why that record is taken.

**The administrative fee.** A charge not provided for in the rental agreement has no basis. Whether it
is also prohibited is a Chapter 12 question.

**The timing and the itemisation.** Both the deadline for returning a deposit and the itemisation
required are set by the VRLTA. **This course states no deadline**, for the reason given above. Read
Chapter 12.

**The trust dimension.** The deposit is escrow money under **18VAC135-20-181 A 2**, its receipt and
disposition must appear in the records **18VAC135-20-185 A** requires, and the accounting duty is
**§ 54.1-2135**. A deposit that was never in escrow is a violation independent of everything above.

## 55.4 Case C — the accommodation, the vendor, and the money

*Facts.* A tenant with a disability requests a grab bar in the bathroom and asks the manager to
arrange it. The manager has it installed, pays the vendor $380 from the escrow account, and charges it
to the owner's ledger.

*Resolution.*

**Classification first.** A grab bar is a physical change to the premises, so it is a **reasonable
modification** under **§ 36-96.3 B (i)** — permission may not be refused, and it is **at the disabled
person's expense**. It is **not** an accommodation, which is a change to a rule. Getting this wrong in
either direction causes a problem: refusing it is a violation, and paying for it charges the owner for
something the statute places on the tenant.

**The restoration condition.** In a rental the landlord may, **where reasonable**, condition permission
on the tenant agreeing to restore the interior to its prior condition, reasonable wear and tear
excepted.

**The money.** Paying a vendor from escrow against an owner's ledger is permissible only within the
authority the management agreement gives and only if the owner's ledger carries the funds. If it does
not, this is the Hour 33 conversion problem: **18VAC135-20-181 A 1 e** requires the balance to be
sufficient **at all times** to account for all funds held.

**What the manager should have done:** classify the request correctly; confirm in writing who bears
the cost and on what statutory basis; obtain the owner's authority for any expenditure above the
management agreement's threshold; and record the whole exchange.

## 55.5 The decision log

Every case above is won or lost on documentation. A decision log entry records: **date and time; who
said what, by what channel; what was decided; on what authority — the statute, the lease, the
management agreement, or the owner's written instruction; who was notified; and what the next step and
its date are.**

A log kept contemporaneously is evidence. A log reconstructed after a complaint is an admission that
none was kept.

### Hour 55 engagement check

**Question.** A landlord client instructs you: "Change the locks Friday — they're two months behind
and I want them out." Write your response and identify every issue.

**Expected answer.** I decline the instruction, in writing. **Self-help eviction — changing locks,
removing possessions, or shutting off utilities — is unlawful**, and possession is recovered through
the statutory judicial process. Under **§ 54.1-2133** my duties include compliance with the article
and all applicable law, and a client's instruction cannot authorise a violation; carrying it out would
expose the owner to liability and me to Board discipline. What I will do is: confirm the ledger and
the exact arrears; identify the notice the **VRLTA, Chapter 12 of Title 55.1**, requires and its
period — read from the current text rather than recalled, because that is where landlords lose cases —
confirm what notice has actually been given and how; refer the owner to counsel to commence the
process; and log the instruction, my refusal, and the referral. I also notify my **principal broker**.

## Hour 56 — Real estate mathematics mastery

## 56.1 How to correct an error by method

A wrong answer has a cause, and there are only a handful. Before re-attempting, classify:

**Direction error** — adjusting the subject instead of the comparable, or adjusting up when the
comparable is superior. Fix: restate the rule aloud — adjust the comparable; comparable better,
subtract.

**Base error** — taking a percentage of the wrong number. LTV on the price when the appraisal is
lower; cash-on-cash on the down payment when closing costs were also invested; commission on the list
price rather than the sale price.

**Period error** — annual when the question wants monthly, or a 360-day year when the contract says
365.

**Inclusion error** — putting debt service or a capital expenditure into operating expenses; leaving
the replacement reserve out; prorating a security deposit.

**Side error** — debiting the party who should be credited.

**Rounding error** — rounding a daily rate before multiplying rather than after.

## 56.2 Area and volume

Rectangle: length × width. Triangle: ½ × base × height. Irregular shape: divide into rectangles and
triangles and sum.

**1 acre = 43,560 square feet.** A commercial square foot is measured differently from a residential
one, and land is frequently priced per acre or per square foot.

*A lot measures 210 ft by 290 ft. Acres?* 210 × 290 = 60,900 sq ft; ÷ 43,560 = **1.398 acres**.
*At $78,000 per acre:* 1.398 × 78,000 = **$109,044**.

## 56.3 Commission

*Sale $624,000; total fee 5.5%; split 50/50 between firms; listing salesperson on a 70/30 split with
their firm.*
Total: 624,000 × 0.055 = **$34,320**. Listing firm: **$17,160**. Salesperson: 17,160 × 0.70 =
**$12,012**.
*Reverse:* a salesperson netted $12,012 on a 70% split of half a 5.5% fee → firm half 12,012 ÷ 0.70 =
17,160 → total 34,320 → price 34,320 ÷ 0.055 = **$624,000**.

Fees are negotiable between client and broker, and **§ 54.1-2136 (ii)** requires the compensation and
whether it will be shared to be disclosed **before the relationship begins**.

## 56.4 Taxes and prorations

*Assessed $476,000; rate $1.14 per $100.* 476,000 ÷ 100 = 4,760; × 1.14 = **$5,426.40**.

*Prorate at settlement 8 August, paid in arrears, 365-day year, settlement day to the buyer.*
Daily: 5,426.40 ÷ 365 = **$14.8668**. Seller's days 1 Jan–7 Aug = 31+28+31+30+31+30+31+7 = **219**.
219 × 14.8668 = **$3,255.83 — debit seller, credit buyer.**

## 56.5 Finance

*Price $580,000; 15% down.* Down payment **$87,000**; loan **$493,000**; LTV **85%**.
*Two discount points:* 493,000 × 0.02 = **$9,860**.
*First month's interest at 6.5%:* 493,000 × 0.065 ÷ 12 = **$2,670.42**.
*Payment factor 6.32 per $1,000:* 493 × 6.32 = **$3,115.76** P&I; principal in month one =
3,115.76 − 2,670.42 = **$445.34**.
*Ratios with gross monthly income $12,500, PITI $3,940, other debts $780:* front-end 3,940 ÷ 12,500 =
**31.5%**; back-end 4,720 ÷ 12,500 = **37.8%**. Thresholds are the lender's and are not stated here.

## 56.6 Appraisal and adjustment

*Comparable sold at $455,000. It has a 3-car garage; the subject has 2. Garage bay contributes
$9,000. It has 1,850 sq ft; the subject has 1,975, at $70 per sq ft. It sold 4 months ago in a market
rising 0.3% per month.*

Garage: comparable **superior** by one bay → **−$9,000**.
Area: comparable **inferior** by 125 sq ft → 125 × 70 = **+$8,750**.
Market conditions: 4 × 0.3% = 1.2% → 455,000 × 0.012 = **+$5,460**.
Adjusted: 455,000 − 9,000 + 8,750 + 5,460 = **$460,210**.

## 56.7 Income property

*18 units at $1,540; other income $6,200; vacancy 6.5%; operating expenses $153,400 including a
$9,000 reserve; cap rate 6.25%; monthly gross rent for GRM $27,720.*

PGI = 18 × 1,540 × 12 + 6,200 = 332,640 + 6,200 = **$338,840**.
Vacancy 6.5% = 22,024.60. **EGI = $316,815.40.**
**NOI = 316,815.40 − 153,400 = $163,415.40.**
**Value = 163,415.40 ÷ 0.0625 = $2,614,646.40.**
*GRM at 13.9:* 13.9 × 27,720 = **$385,308** — a figure for a small residential income property, and
note that it uses gross rent and therefore ignores vacancy and every operating expense.

### Hour 56 engagement check

**Question.** A student computes a tax proration as follows: annual tax $4,380, settlement 12
September, "so nine months have passed, 9 ÷ 12 × 4,380 = $3,285, debit buyer." Identify every error by
category and give the correct entry using a 365-day year with the settlement day charged to the buyer.

**Expected answer.** Three errors. A **period error** — nine full months have not passed on 12
September; the seller's period is 1 January to 11 September, which is **254 days**, not 9/12 of a
year. A **rounding or method error** — using whole months rather than the 365-day daily rate the
convention requires. And a **side error** — taxes paid in arrears mean the **buyer** will pay the
whole bill, so the seller owes the buyer: it is a **debit to the seller and a credit to the buyer**,
not a debit to the buyer. Correct: 4,380 ÷ 365 = $12.00 daily; 254 × 12.00 = **$3,048.00, debit
seller, credit buyer.**
