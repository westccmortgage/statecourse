# Hour 49 — Property, flood, and transaction insurance

## 49.1 The four coverages a residential transaction touches

**Hazard or property coverage** — physical loss to the dwelling and other structures from covered
perils.
**Liability coverage** — bodily injury and property damage to others for which the insured is
responsible.
**Flood insurance** — a separate policy, because flood is **excluded** from standard homeowners
coverage.
**Title insurance** — Hour 12, and the only one of the four that looks **backward**.

A buyer who believes "the insurance covers it" usually has no idea which policy they mean, and part
of a licensee's job is to make the four visible.

## 49.2 Homeowners coverage, in parts

A homeowners policy is conventionally organised as: **Coverage A** dwelling; **Coverage B** other
structures; **Coverage C** personal property; **Coverage D** loss of use, which pays additional
living expenses while the home is uninhabitable; **Coverage E** personal liability; **Coverage F**
medical payments to others.

**Named peril versus open peril.** A named-peril form covers only the perils it lists; an open-peril
or all-risk form covers everything except what it excludes. Reading the exclusions is therefore how
one reads an open-peril policy.

**Replacement cost versus actual cash value.** Replacement cost pays to replace without deduction for
depreciation; actual cash value deducts depreciation. The difference on a fifteen-year-old roof is
large. **Extended or guaranteed replacement cost** endorsements address the case where rebuilding
costs exceed the policy limit.

**Coinsurance.** Where a policy requires the insured to carry a stated percentage of replacement
value — commonly 80% — a loss is reduced proportionally if they carry less. It is the provision that
turns under-insurance into a partial recovery on a partial loss.

**Deductibles**, including separate percentage deductibles for wind and hail in coastal areas — which
matters in eastern Virginia.

**Common exclusions**: flood, earth movement, ordinary wear and neglect, mould in many forms,
intentional acts, and business use. **Ordinance or law** coverage is a common endorsement addressing
the cost of rebuilding to current code, which a standard policy may not cover.

**Condominium and cooperative units** need an **HO-6** unit-owners policy, and its scope depends
entirely on where the **association's master policy** stops. That boundary is set by the declaration,
which Hour 29 said must be read.

**Landlords** need a landlord or dwelling-fire policy, not a homeowners policy, and **tenants** need
renters coverage, because the landlord's policy covers the building and not the tenant's belongings
or liability.

## 49.3 Flood

**Flood is excluded from standard homeowners policies.** Coverage comes from the **National Flood
Insurance Program**, administered by FEMA and sold through participating insurers, or from the
private flood market, which has grown and sometimes offers higher limits.

**Flood zones** are mapped on **Flood Insurance Rate Maps**. Properties in Special Flood Hazard Areas
face a **lender requirement** to carry flood insurance on a federally regulated or insured loan.

Three points a licensee must be able to state:

**Maps change.** A property outside a mapped hazard area today may be inside one after a map revision,
and the lender requirement follows the map.

**Being outside a mapped hazard area is not the same as not flooding.** A substantial share of flood
claims arise outside Special Flood Hazard Areas.

**NFIP coverage has limits and its own waiting period.** A policy bought the week before a storm may
not be in force for it.

Virginia's coastal and riverine geography makes this a live question in many localities, and the
answer for any specific property comes from FEMA's mapping, the locality, and an insurance
professional — not from the licensee.

## 49.4 Other coverages around the transaction

**Title insurance** — Hour 12: owner's and lender's policies, one-time premium, backward-looking,
covering defects existing at policy date and not excepted. It does **not** cover physical condition.

**Home warranty or service contract** — a contract, not insurance, covering repair or replacement of
specified systems and appliances, with exclusions, caps, service fees, and pre-existing-condition
provisions. Frequently offered in a transaction, and frequently misunderstood as covering everything.

**Errors and omissions insurance** — the licensee's own professional liability coverage. It is not
required by the Board's regulations that this course has read, but it is standard practice and a firm
requirement in most firms, and a licensee should know their policy's limits, deductible, and
reporting obligations.

## 49.5 What none of them covers

Assemble the gaps and give them to the buyer, because this is where surprises live:

Flood, unless a separate flood policy exists. Earth movement, generally. Ordinary maintenance and
wear. Pre-existing physical defects — not covered by title insurance, and often excluded from a home
warranty. Matters **excepted** on Schedule B-II of the title commitment, including rights of parties
in possession and anything an accurate survey would show. Business use of the property. And anything
above the policy limits.

## Hour 49 engagement check

**Question.** A buyer under contract on a 1998 house says: "I'm getting title insurance and a home
warranty, so I don't need the inspection." Identify precisely what each product would and would not
respond to, and state your reply.

**Expected answer.** **Title insurance** is backward-looking indemnity against **defects in title**
existing at the policy date and not excepted — forged instruments, undisclosed heirs, recording
errors, undisclosed liens — and it covers **no physical condition whatsoever**; it also excepts
matters an accurate survey would show and the rights of parties in possession. A **home warranty** is
a **contract**, not insurance, covering specified systems and appliances subject to exclusions, caps,
service fees, and pre-existing-condition provisions — so the very defect an inspection would find is
the one most likely to be excluded. Neither responds to a failing roof, a structural problem, a septic
failure, or an encroachment. My reply is that the inspection is the only one of the three that looks
at the property, that § 55.1-703 itself names a home inspection as diligence the purchaser is advised
to exercise, and that I will confirm the contingency deadline in writing.

# Hour 50 — Risk management, data protection, and regulatory compliance

## 50.1 Wire fraud — the loss that actually happens

Real estate wire fraud follows a consistent pattern. An attacker compromises an email account
belonging to someone in the transaction — a licensee, a settlement agent, a lender, or a party.
They read quietly, learn the settlement date and the amount, and then send the buyer a message that
appears to come from a trusted party carrying **changed wire instructions**. The buyer wires the
money. It is gone within hours, frequently within minutes.

The operating rule, which should be taught as an absolute:

> **Any change to wire instructions is fraudulent until verified. Verify by telephoning a number you
> obtained independently, never a number in the email, and never a number in an attachment.**

Supporting practices: give the buyer written wire-fraud warnings at ratification and again before
settlement, in a document they sign; establish the settlement agent's verification protocol in
advance and tell the buyer what it is; discourage wiring altogether where the settlement agent
accepts another method; and instruct the buyer to call the bank **immediately** on any suspicion,
because a recall attempted within hours occasionally succeeds and one attempted the next day almost
never does.

The professional obligation is not merely to avoid causing the loss. Under **§ 54.1-2131 A 4** and
**§ 54.1-2132 A 5** the standard is ordinary care, and a licensee who knows this pattern exists and
never warns the client has an ordinary-care problem.

## 50.2 The firm's written policies

**§ 54.1-2110.1 B 2** requires the supervising broker to ensure the office has **written procedures
and policies giving clear guidance** in eight areas, and a licensee should be able to name them,
because they are the map of where risk actually sits:

(a) handling of escrow deposits in compliance with law and regulation; (b) complying with federal and
state fair housing laws and regulations, if the firm does residential brokerage, leasing or
management; (c) advertising and marketing of the firm and any affiliated teams or business entities;
(d) negotiating and drafting of contracts, leases and brokerage agreements; (e) exercising
appropriate oversight and limitations on the use of unlicensed assistants; (f) creating agency or
independent contractor relationships and their elements; (g) distributing information on new or
amended laws or regulations; and (h) disclosing required information relating to the physical
condition of real property.

**A firm without these policies has a supervision failure**, and under **18VAC135-20-330** their
absence makes "should have known" much easier to establish against the broker.

## 50.3 Legal update control — (g), specifically

Subdivision (g) — **distributing information on new or amended laws or regulations** — is the duty
that keeps a course and a practice current, and this course has two live examples in its own text:
**§ 55.1-703 was read in a version effective until 1 January 2027**, and **§ 36-96.2 in a version
effective until 1 July 2027.** Both are recorded for recheck in
`virginia/compliance/legal-update-sop.md`.

For a practising licensee the equivalent discipline is: know that the Code and 18VAC135-20 are
published free and current at law.lis.virginia.gov, read the section rather than a summary of it, and
treat any remembered figure as suspect.

## 50.4 Data protection

**What a firm holds** is unusually sensitive: names, addresses, dates of birth, social security
numbers on some documents, bank details, income and asset documentation, and complete transaction
histories.

Baseline controls: multi-factor authentication on every email account, which defeats most account
compromise; unique passwords in a manager; encrypted transmission of documents rather than plain
email attachments; access limited to those who need it; secure disposal of paper and devices; a
retention schedule that keeps what the rules require and disposes of the rest; and a written
incident-response plan naming who is called first.

**Retention is a legal obligation, not a preference.** **18VAC135-20-185 C 1** — brokerage agreements
and dual and designated consents, **three years from execution**. **§ 54.1-2138 D** —
brokerage-relationship disclosures relating to fully executed purchase contracts, **three years**.
**§ 54.1-2110.1 B 7** — the supervising broker's supervision records, **three years**, furnished to
the Board's agent on request. **§ 54.1-2146** — licensee maintenance of records. And
**18VAC135-20-240** requires provision of information to the Board, so records that cannot be
produced are themselves a problem.

## 50.5 Identity, integrity, and unlicensed activity

**Seller impersonation fraud** — a person purporting to be the owner of an unencumbered, often vacant
or non-owner-occupied property, listing it and attempting to close remotely — has become a recognised
pattern. Warning signs: an owner who will only communicate by email, refuses video or in-person
identification, is unusually remote, pushes for a fast cash closing, and gives payoff or proceeds
instructions inconsistent with the record. The response is to insist on identity verification through
the settlement agent, who is regulated under **Chapter 10 of Title 55.1**, and to escalate to the
principal broker.

**Unlicensed activity** is a risk the firm creates for itself. **18VAC135-20-335** sets what licensees
must perform and what non-licensees may do; **18VAC135-20-165 2 and 3** require the supervising broker
to take reasonable steps to ensure only licensees undertake activities requiring a licence and to
supervise unlicensed individuals adequately; **§ 54.1-2105.2** authorises the Board to issue cease and
desist orders and civil penalties for unlicensed activity.

## 50.6 Complaints, escalation, and E&O

When something goes wrong: notify the **principal broker immediately**; preserve every record and
change nothing; do not contact a complainant to argue; notify the errors and omissions carrier within
the policy's reporting period, because late notice can void coverage; respond to the Board within the
time given, since **18VAC135-20-240** makes failure to provide information a violation; and obtain
counsel.

**§ 54.1-2111.1** provides a **voluntary compliance program** for real estate brokers, and
**§§ 54.1-2112 through 54.1-2120** establish the **Transaction Recovery Fund**, from which a member of
the public with an unsatisfied judgment against a licensee may recover — with **§ 54.1-2119**
providing that the licensee's licence is **revoked** on payment from the fund on their account.

## Hour 50 engagement check

**Question.** Two days before settlement your buyer forwards you an email, apparently from the
settlement agent's paralegal, with the subject "Updated wire instructions — bank change", from the
correct display name and with the firm's logo. The buyer asks if it looks right. What do you do, in
order?

**Expected answer.** Treat it as **fraudulent until verified**. Tell the buyer **in writing, at once,
not to send anything.** Then telephone the settlement agent on a number obtained **independently** —
from the ratified contract, the firm's own file, or the settlement agent's published listing — and
**never** a number in the email, its signature block, or an attachment. Confirm with a named person
whether any instructions changed. Notify the **principal broker** and the lender. If money has already
moved, instruct the buyer to call their bank **immediately** and request a recall, and to report to
the FBI's IC3 and to law enforcement — hours matter. Preserve the email and its full headers, and
change nothing. Whatever the outcome, document the whole sequence in the file.

# Hour 51 — Integrated seller transaction: listing through contract

## 51.1 Before the appointment

**§ 54.1-2136** governs what is said first: before entering into any brokerage relationship, advise
the prospective client of **(i) the type of brokerage relationship proposed** and **(ii) the broker's
compensation and whether it will be shared** with another broker who may have a brokerage relationship
with another party.

Then verify who the seller actually is. Ownership from the land records; every person on title; a
trust, estate, or entity and who has authority to sign; a spouse whose interest must be released;
whether anyone is deceased and whether the estate has been administered. **Seller impersonation
fraud** — Hour 50.5 — is checked here, at intake, not at settlement.

## 51.2 The brokerage agreement

**§ 54.1-2137 B and C**: in writing, before providing brokerage services, with **a definite
termination date failing which it ends 90 days after its date**; the **amount of the brokerage fees
and how and when they are paid**; the **services to be rendered**; the other agreed terms; and, where
entered with consent to dual representation, the **§ 54.1-2139 A disclosures**. Under
**§ 54.1-2110.1 B 6** it must carry the **supervising broker's name and contact information**.

If the licensee is acting as a **limited service agent**, **§ 54.1-2138.1 A** requires the written
agreement to disclose that status, list the specific services that **will** be provided, and list the
specific standard-agent duties from § 54.1-2131 A 2 that **will not** be — conspicuously, in bold or
all capitals, underlined or boxed, with the statutory consent language.

Retention: **18VAC135-20-185 C 1**, three years from execution.

## 51.3 Property facts and disclosure

**Fixtures** — Hour 4: walk the property, record what conveys and what does not, and resolve the
chandelier before the photographs, not after an offer.

**Owner's disclosure** — **§ 55.1-703**: the owner furnishes the **Real Estate Board's residential
property disclosure statement**, whose content is that the owner makes **no representations** and
which advises the purchaser to exercise whatever due diligence they deem necessary. The licensee's
job is to ensure the statement is provided as the Act requires, not to help the seller describe the
property's condition on it.

**The licensee's own duty is separate.** Under **§ 54.1-2131 B**, disclose to prospective buyers, **in
writing**, all material adverse facts pertaining to the **physical condition** of the property that
the licensee **actually knows** — and actual knowledge of **defective drywall** under § 36-156.1 —
remembering the statutory exclusions for matters outside the boundaries, land use regulations, and
highways and public streets.

**Lead-based paint** — for target housing built before 1978, the federal disclosure, the pamphlet, the
records, the contract warning and acknowledgments, and the **ten-day** assessment opportunity for
sales.

**Common-interest community** — if the property is in one, § 55.1-2308 requires the **contract** to
carry five specific disclosures, and § 55.1-2309 requires the **seller or the seller's agent to obtain
the resale certificate from the association and provide it**, an obligation which **shall not be
waived or changed by agreement**. Request it in writing at listing, not at ratification: the 14-day
clock in § 55.1-2309 B runs from a written request.

## 51.4 Pricing evidence and marketing

Pricing is Hour 3 and Hour 43: define the market area, screen for arm's-length closed sales, adjust
the comparables toward the subject, reconcile to a **range**, and present it as market evidence rather
than as a value. Never call it an appraisal.

Marketing is Hours 26 through 28: describe the property and never the desired occupants; observe
**18VAC135-20-190** — in the firm's name, under the broker's supervision, with the firm's licensed
name clearly and legibly displayed, carrying the advertising disclosure the firm's written policy
specifies, with property information **consistent with the property's condition and current contract
status** and **updated in a timely manner**, and with a **timely written request** for changes where a
third party controls the advertising.

## 51.5 Offers

**Present every written offer.** **§ 54.1-2131 A 2 c** requires receiving and presenting written
offers and counteroffers in a timely manner **even when the property is already subject to a contract
of sale**, and **18VAC135-20-300 3** makes failure to tender promptly **every** written offer,
counteroffer and rejection a misrepresentation or omission.

**Disclose the relationship.** **§ 54.1-2138 A**: on a substantive discussion about a specific
property with an actual or prospective buyer who is neither the licensee's client nor represented,
disclose any brokerage relationship with another party — in writing, at the earliest practical time
and **no later than when specific real estate assistance is first provided**, conspicuously.

**Confidentiality governs what is said about competing offers.** § 54.1-2131 A 3 protects the client's
personal and financial information and anything the client asked be kept confidential; the client
decides what is disclosed about their own transaction, and the licensee follows lawful instructions.

**Where the firm will be on both sides**, obtain the § 54.1-2139 or § 54.1-2139.1 disclosures and
**written consent from all clients** — under **18VAC135-20-220 A 3**, from the buyer no later than
when the offer is presented to the licensee who will present it, and from the seller no later than
when the offer is presented to the seller. Absent that consent, **18VAC135-20-270 2** makes it a
**conflict of interest**.

## 51.6 The file audit

At ratification the seller file should contain, and a licensee should be able to check without
prompting: the written brokerage agreement with a definite termination date, the fee, the services,
and the supervising broker's name and contact information; the limited-service disclosure if
applicable; evidence of the § 54.1-2136 precondition disclosures; the owner's § 55.1-703 disclosure
statement as furnished; the licensee's written material-adverse-fact disclosures; the lead-based paint
package where the house predates 1978; the common-interest community contract disclosures and the
written resale certificate request; every written offer received with the date and time and evidence
of presentation; all brokerage-relationship disclosures signed and dated; any dual or designated
consents; the ratified contract with every addendum referenced and attached; the deposit receipt and
evidence of deposit to escrow; and the advertising record.

## Hour 51 engagement check

**Question.** A seller signs a listing on 4 March. The agreement states a fee and the services but no
termination date. On 20 June the seller accepts an offer from a buyer represented by another licensee
in the same firm. What is the status of the listing, and what documents must exist before the contract
is ratified?

**Expected answer.** Under **§ 54.1-2137 C 1**, a brokerage agreement with **no definite termination
date terminates 90 days after its date** — 4 March plus 90 days ends the agreement in early June, so
**the listing had already expired before 20 June** unless it was extended in writing. That must be
resolved before anything else. Assuming a valid written extension: because two licensees of the same
firm represent the two sides, this is **designated agency under § 54.1-2139.1**, in which the
**supervising broker is a dual agent or representative**. The **§ 54.1-2139.1 B disclosure** and
**written consent from all clients** must exist — under **18VAC135-20-220 A 3**, from the buyer no
later than when the offer is presented to the licensee who will present it and from the seller no
later than when it is presented to the seller — and without that written consent
**18VAC135-20-270 2** makes it a conflict of interest. The consents are retained three years from
execution under **18VAC135-20-185 C 1**.

# Hour 52 — Integrated buyer transaction: financing through closing

## 52.1 Before showing anything

**§ 54.1-2136** first: the type of brokerage relationship proposed, and the broker's compensation and
whether it is shared.

**Then § 54.1-2132 A 1: enter into a brokerage agreement with the prospective buyer *prior to showing
property*.** In writing, satisfying § 54.1-2137 C — definite termination date or 90 days, the fee and
how and when paid, the services — and carrying the supervising broker's name and contact information
under § 54.1-2110.1 B 6.

And send the buyer to a lender **before** the search. A **pre-approval** rests on a submitted
application and verified documentation; a **pre-qualification** does not; and letting one be presented
as the other misrepresents the buyer's position to a seller.

## 52.2 Search and offer

Search criteria come from the buyer, recorded in the buyer's own words. Fair housing governs the
process: show everything meeting the stated criteria and let the buyer narrow; answer neighbourhood
questions with objective sources rather than demographics; never let a preference become steering.

On offer: the **§ 54.1-2138 A** disclosure where the licensee has a substantive discussion about a
specific property with an unrepresented seller; and, in a residential transaction, **§ 54.1-2132 B**
requires disclosure to the seller of **whether or not the buyer intends to occupy the property as a
principal residence** — satisfied by the buyer's statement of intent in the contract.

Where the property is in a **common interest community**, confirm the contract carries the five
**§ 55.1-2308** disclosures, because their absence gives the buyer a right to cancel before settlement
and makes cancellation the buyer's sole remedy.

## 52.3 The milestone map

Built from **ratification** as Hour 18 described, after reading the contract's day-counting rule and
its notice provision:

**Deposit** delivered by the contract's deadline, to the holder the contract names, and into escrow
under **18VAC135-20-181 A 1 and A 2**.
**Loan application** within the contract's period; **Loan Estimate** follows.
**Resale certificate** — written request immediately; the **14-day** clock in § 55.1-2309 B; then the
**§ 55.1-2312** cancellation period, which is the agreed period or, absent one, **three days from
ratification** if delivery preceded ratification, **three days from receipt** if it followed, and
**open until settlement** if nothing was ever delivered.
**Inspections** within the inspection period, with the notice the contract requires — and remember
that a period allowed to lapse without notice may be deemed satisfied or waived.
**Lead-based paint** ten-day assessment opportunity for pre-1978 target housing, unless the parties
agreed otherwise in writing.
**Appraisal**, ordered early enough that a reconsideration of value is possible.
**Title examination and objection**, then the seller's cure period.
**Loan conditions**, chased weekly in writing.
**Loan commitment** by the financing contingency deadline.
**Closing Disclosure** received by the buyer for the required period — and a late change to the loan
product **re-starts** it.
**Final walk-through**, then **settlement, funding, recording, possession.**

Every date gets an owner, a required document, and a stated consequence if it passes. Internal
reminders sit several days ahead of each.

## 52.4 Resolving conflicting deadlines

Three conflicts recur, and a licensee should have an answer ready.

**A financing contingency expiring after settlement.** Incoherent, as Hour 17 showed. Raise it in
writing at once and get a written amendment; do not decide which date controls.

**A resale certificate that arrives late, extending the cancellation window past a planned
settlement.** § 55.1-2312 gives the purchaser a cancellation right that does not bend to the
settlement calendar; the settlement date moves, by written amendment, or the buyer waives by
proceeding to settlement — and § 55.1-2308(v) says the right is **waived conclusively if not exercised
before settlement**.

**An appraisal gap discovered close to the deadline.** The gap must be closed by the buyer, by a
price reduction, by reconsideration, or by termination under the contract's terms. The licensee
computes the gap, states the options, and refers the legal question of what the contract permits.

## 52.5 Before the wire, and at settlement

Hour 50's protocol applies at exactly this point: **any change to wire instructions is fraudulent
until verified by telephone to an independently obtained number.** Give the buyer the warning in
writing at ratification and again before settlement.

At settlement, Hour 40's checklist: title requirements satisfied; clear to close; Closing Disclosure
received for the required period; repairs completed and documented; walk-through done; association
matters resolved; insurance bound effective the settlement date; funds delivered.

Afterwards: deliver the complete file to the buyer; confirm the deed is recorded and returned; and
retain the firm's records for the periods **18VAC135-20-185 C 1**, **§ 54.1-2138 D** and
**§ 54.1-2110.1 B 7** require.

## 52.6 What survives the closing

**§ 54.1-2137 D**: after termination, expiration, or completion of performance, the licensee owes no
further duties **except** to (i) account for all money and property relating to the relationship and
(ii) keep confidential the client's personal and financial information and anything the client asked
be kept confidential — unless the law provides otherwise or the client consents in writing.

The confidentiality duty does not end with the transaction. It is the one that surfaces years later,
when a former client's circumstances become interesting to someone else.

## Hour 52 engagement check

**Question.** Contract ratified 1 May on a condominium; settlement set for 5 June. The resale
certificate is delivered 30 May. The contract specifies no cancellation period. On 2 June the buyer,
having read the minutes, wants to cancel. The seller says the settlement date has passed the point of
no return. Who is right, and what must the buyer do?

**Expected answer.** The buyer. Delivery occurred **after ratification**, so **§ 55.1-2312 B**
applies: with no period specified in the contract, the purchaser has **three days from receipt** of
the resale certificate to cancel. Received 30 May, the buyer is within the period on 2 June.
Cancellation is **without penalty** under subsection F and the deposit must be **returned promptly**.
The buyer must give **written notice of cancellation in accordance with the terms of the contract**,
and under subsection D the **buyer bears the burden of demonstrating delivery** of that notice — so it
goes by a method that produces proof, and the proof is retained. The settlement date is irrelevant to
the calculation, except that under § 55.1-2308(v) the right would be **waived conclusively if not
exercised before settlement**.

**Insurance in Hour 49 is stated as general product structure, not as Virginia insurance law.**
Coverage forms, coinsurance percentages, deductible structures and NFIP terms are matters of the

routes every specific to an insurance professional and to FEMA's mapping.

**Errors and omissions insurance is described as standard practice, not as a Board requirement.** No
requirement to carry it appears in the regulations read for this project, and Hour 49.4 says so.

**Wire fraud response in Hour 50** names IC3 and law enforcement as reporting routes; it states no
recovery rate and no time limit, because none is guaranteed. The rule the hour teaches — verify by
telephone to an independently obtained number — is a practice standard, and Hour 50.1 grounds the
professional obligation in the ordinary-care duty at § 54.1-2131 A 4 and § 54.1-2132 A 5 rather than
claiming a specific Virginia rule that does not exist.

Federal lead-based paint requirements and TRID timing are federal law and are stated at the level of
the well-settled requirement; **no TRID waiting period is given in days.**
