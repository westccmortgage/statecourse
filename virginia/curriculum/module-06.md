## Hour 21 — Virginia duties to sellers, buyers, landlords, and tenants

## 21.1 Five duty sections, one architecture

The article assigns duties by **who engaged the licensee**:

- **§ 54.1-2131** — licensees engaged by sellers
- **§ 54.1-2132** — licensees engaged by buyers
- **§ 54.1-2133** — licensees engaged by landlords to lease property
- **§ 54.1-2134** — licensees engaged by tenants
- **§ 54.1-2135** — licensees engaged to manage real estate

The four transactional sections share an architecture. Learn § 54.1-2131 properly and the others
follow, with the differences noted below.

## 21.2 The seller's licensee — § 54.1-2131 A

Seven duties:

**1. Perform in accordance with the terms of the brokerage agreement.** The agreement is the first
source of duty, not an afterthought.

**2. Promote the interests of the seller by** four named activities:
 (a) **conducting marketing activities** per the agreement, seeking a sale at the agreed price and
 terms or at a price and terms acceptable to the seller — and note the express carve-out: the
 licensee is **not obligated to seek additional offers while the property is subject to a contract
 of sale**, unless the brokerage agreement or the contract of sale so provides;
 (b) **assisting in the drafting and negotiating** of offers, counteroffers, amendments and addenda
 **pursuant to § 54.1-2101.1**, and establishing strategies for the seller's objectives;
 (c) **receiving and presenting in a timely manner** written offers and counteroffers to and from
 the seller and purchasers — **even when the property is already subject to a contract of sale**;
 (d) providing reasonable assistance to satisfy the seller's contract obligations and to facilitate
 settlement.

**3. Maintain confidentiality** of all personal and financial information received from the client
during the relationship, and any other information the client asked be kept confidential, unless
the law provides otherwise or the seller consents **in writing** to release.

**4. Exercise ordinary care.** Not "utmost care", not a fiduciary standard imported from the common
law — **ordinary care**, because § 54.1-2144 abrogated the common law to the extent inconsistent.

**5. Account in a timely manner** for all money and property received in which the seller has or may
have an interest.

**6. Disclose to the seller material facts related to the property or concerning the transaction of
which the licensee has actual knowledge.**

**7. Comply** with the article, with all fair housing statutes and regulations applicable to
residential transactions, and with all other applicable law not in conflict with the article.

## 21.3 The seller's licensee toward the other side — § 54.1-2131 B

Treat all prospective buyers **honestly**; do not **knowingly** give false information. Disclose
**all material adverse facts pertaining to the physical condition of the property that are actually
known** by the licensee, **in writing**. Disclose actual knowledge of **defective drywall** as
defined in § 36-156.1.

And the statutory boundary, which Hour 19 introduced and which must be memorised: "physical
condition of the property" refers to the physical condition of the land and any improvements on it,
and **does not** refer to (i) matters outside the boundaries of the land or relating to adjacent or
other nearby properties, (ii) matters relating to governmental land use regulations, or (iii)
matters relating to highways or public streets.

The section closes with two protections and one preservation: **no cause of action arises against a
licensee for revealing information as required** by the article or applicable law; the section does
not limit the **Virginia Residential Property Disclosure Act**; and ministerial acts for a buyer,
under subsection C, neither breach the seller agreement nor create a relationship with the buyer.

Subsection D adds that a seller's licensee does **not** breach any duty by showing alternative
properties to prospective buyers.

## 21.4 The buyer's licensee — § 54.1-2132

The same architecture, with three Virginia-specific differences a student must be able to state:

**A 1 — enter into a brokerage agreement with the prospective buyer *prior to showing property* to
that prospective buyer.** Virginia puts the agreement before the showing. This is the single most
distinctive rule in the article.

**A 3 a — the licensee is not obligated to seek or show other properties while the buyer is a party
to a contract to purchase**, unless the relationship provides otherwise. The mirror of the seller
carve-out.

**B — in a residential transaction the buyer's licensee shall disclose to the seller whether or not
the buyer intends to occupy the property as a principal residence.** The buyer's expression of that
intent in the contract satisfies the requirement, and no cause of action arises from the
disclosure, from an inaccuracy in it, or from its absence.

The rest tracks § 54.1-2131: perform per the agreement; promote the buyer's interests by seeking
suitable property, assisting in drafting and negotiating under § 54.1-2101.1, presenting written
offers and counteroffers in a timely manner even when the buyer is already under contract, and
assisting to settlement; confidentiality; **ordinary care**; timely accounting; disclosure of
material facts of actual knowledge; compliance with the article and fair housing.

Subsection D confirms that a buyer's licensee does not breach any duty by showing properties the
buyer is interested in to other prospective buyers, by representing other buyers, or by representing
sellers as to other properties.

## 21.5 Landlord, tenant, and management

**§ 54.1-2133** and **§ 54.1-2134** carry the same duty architecture into leasing — perform per the
agreement, promote the client's interests, confidentiality, **ordinary care**, timely accounting,
disclosure of material facts of **actual knowledge**, compliance with the article and fair housing.
**But they are not identical, and the difference decides what a licensee does first.**

**§ 54.1-2134 A 1 requires a licensee engaged by a tenant to *enter into a brokerage agreement with
the prospective tenant prior to showing property to such prospective tenant*.** That is the same
requirement § 54.1-2132 A 1 imposes on buyer representation. **§ 54.1-2133 imposes no such
requirement on a licensee engaged by a landlord**, and neither does § 54.1-2135 on a licensee engaged
to manage. So the rule to carry out of this hour is: **on the tenant side and the buyer side the
agreement comes before the showing; on the landlord side and the owner side it does not.**

One further leasing duty has no counterpart in the sale sections. **§ 54.1-2133 B** requires a
licensee engaged by a landlord to disclose to prospective tenants all material adverse facts about
the physical condition of the property **that are actually known**, and adds a specific item: *"if a
licensee has actual knowledge of the existence of **any pipe, pipe or plumbing fitting, fixture,
solder, or flux that does not meet the federal Safe Drinking Water Act definition of 'lead free'
pursuant to 42 U.S.C. § 300g-6** in a residential property, the licensee shall disclose the same to
the prospective tenant."* **The disclosure must be in writing.** The same subsection carries the
three exclusions from "physical condition of the property" that Hour 21 has already met on the sale
side — matters outside the boundaries of the land or relating to adjacent properties, matters
relating to governmental land use regulations, and matters relating to highways or public streets —
and preserves the prospective tenant's own right to inspect.

**§ 54.1-2135** covers licensees engaged to manage real estate, where the accounting duty does the
heaviest work because the licensee handles other people's money continuously rather than once. Note
that its duty list is the **shortest** of the four: it contains **no "promote the interests of the
client" subdivision at all**, because the management relationship is defined by the management
agreement. **§ 54.1-2135 F** limits the section to residential — *"the provisions of this section
shall not apply to licensees engaged in commercial real estate transactions."* Hours 33 and 55
develop management practice.

## 21.6 Reading the standards, not the vocabulary

Four standards appear in these sections and a student should attach each to the right duty:

- **Ordinary care** — the general standard of performance.
- **Actual knowledge** — the trigger for both the client disclosure duty and the physical-condition
  duty toward the other side. There is no duty to investigate in these sections.
- **Timely** — accounting, presentation of offers, and disclosures.
- **In writing** — the physical-condition disclosure under § 54.1-2131 B, consent to release
  confidential information, and the brokerage agreement itself.

### Hour 21 engagement check

**Question.** A listing licensee learns from the seller that the county has approved a plan to widen
the road in front of the property, and separately observes active water staining on a bedroom
ceiling. What must the licensee disclose to a prospective unrepresented buyer, and on what basis?

**Expected answer.** The **water staining** is a material adverse fact pertaining to the physical
condition of the property, actually known, and must be disclosed **in writing** under
§ 54.1-2131 B. The **road widening** is a matter relating to highways or public streets, which the
statute expressly places outside the definition of "physical condition of the property", so the
§ 54.1-2131 B physical-condition duty does not reach it. That is not permission to mislead: the
licensee must still treat the buyer honestly and must not knowingly give false information, and the
professional course is to state what is known and direct the buyer to the locality and to VDOT.
Toward the **seller**, who is the client, the road plan is a material fact concerning the property
or the transaction of which the licensee has actual knowledge, and § 54.1-2131 A 6 requires it be
disclosed to the seller.

## Hour 22 — Virginia brokerage disclosures: dual, designated, limited service

## 22.1 Disclosure of brokerage relationship — § 54.1-2138

**The trigger** is a **substantive discussion about a specific property or properties** in a
residential transaction with an actual or prospective buyer or seller who is **not** the licensee's
client and **not** represented by another licensee. On that trigger, the licensee must disclose any
brokerage relationship the licensee has with another party to the transaction.

**The timing** is *in writing, at the earliest practical time, but in no event later than the time
when specific real estate assistance is first provided.*

**The form.** The disclosure may be combined with other disclosures or given with other information
— but if it is, it must be **conspicuous: printed in bold lettering, all capitals, underlined, or
within a separate box.** The statute prints a model form, and a disclosure complying substantially
in effect with it is deemed compliant.

**Leasing — subsection B.** For an actual or prospective landlord or tenant who is not the client
and not represented, the disclosure must be in writing and **included in all applications for lease
or in the lease itself, whichever occurs first**; if the lease terms do not provide for it, no later
than the signing of the lease. **It does not apply to lease terms of less than two months.**

**Change of relationship — subsection C.** If a licensee's relationship to a client or customer
changes, the licensee shall disclose that **in writing to all clients and customers already involved
in the specific contemplated transaction**.

**Retention — subsection D.** Copies of disclosures relating to **fully executed purchase contracts**
must be kept **three years**, whether or not the disclosure was acknowledged in writing.

The Board's regulation at **18VAC135-20-220 A** restates the requirement and adds the consent timing
for dual and designated relationships: written consent from all clients at the earliest practical
time, given to and obtained from the **buyer not later than the time an offer to purchase is
presented** to the licensee who will present it, and from the seller not later than the time the
offer is presented to the seller.

## 22.2 Limited service agent — § 54.1-2138.1

A licensee may act as a **limited service agent** in a residential transaction **only pursuant to a
written brokerage agreement** in which the licensee:

**(i)** discloses that the licensee is acting as a limited service agent;
**(ii)** provides a **list of the specific services** the licensee will provide to the client; and
**(iii)** provides a **list of the specific standard-agent duties** — from § 54.1-2131 A 2,
§ 54.1-2132 A 3, § 54.1-2133 A 2 or § 54.1-2134 A 3 as applicable — that the licensee **will not**
provide.

The disclosure must be **conspicuous and printed either in bold lettering or all capitals, and
underlined or in a separate box**. The statute prints consent language whose substance is that
**neither the other party nor any licensee representing the other party is under any legal
obligation to assist the client with duties the limited service agent is not performing.**

A limited service agent must still disclose dual agency under § 54.1-2139.

**Subsection B** protects the licensee on the other side. A licensee engaged by one client who deals
with an unrepresented party, or with a party represented by a limited service agent, and who
**without additional compensation** gives that party information or helps them secure a contract or
meet its obligations, **does not incur liability except for gross negligence or wilful misconduct**,
and **does not create a brokerage relationship** by doing so. If that licensee instead charges a fee
for services or information, no brokerage relationship is created — but the licensee is held to the
**ordinary standard of care** in providing them.

**§ 54.1-2138.1 also reaches advertising**: 18VAC135-20-190 A requires advertising to comply with
the § 54.1-2138.1 disclosure when applicable.

## 22.3 Dual agency and dual representation — § 54.1-2139

A licensee acting for **both** clients in the same residential transaction is a **dual agent or dual
representative**, and Virginia permits it only **as disclosed dual agency with written consent**.
The subsection A disclosures must be given, and where a brokerage agreement is entered in
conjunction with consent to dual representation, **§ 54.1-2137 C 5** requires those disclosures to
be part of the brokerage agreement itself.

Subsection G provides for termination, and § 54.1-2137 A (ii)(d) makes a § 54.1-2139 G termination
one of the ways a brokerage relationship ends.

**§ 54.1-2139.01** authorises disclosed dual agency and dual representation in **commercial**
transactions on its own terms.

The professional reality is worth stating plainly: a dual agent cannot advocate for either side on
price or terms, because advocating for one is acting against the other. What the dual agent can do
is facilitate — and clients frequently do not understand the difference until it matters. Explaining
it before consent is signed is the work.

## 22.4 Designated agency — § 54.1-2139.1

A principal or supervising broker may **assign different affiliated licensees** as designated agents
or representatives to represent different clients **in the same residential transaction**, to the
exclusion of all other licensees in the firm.

Two rules follow, and the second is the one licensees forget:

**Using designated agents is not dual agency** so long as no designated agent represents more than
one client in the transaction.

**The principal or supervising broker who is supervising the transaction is a dual agent or
representative.** The firm is on both sides; only the individual licensees are not.

Designated agents **may not disclose**, except to their own broker, personal or financial
information received from their client, or anything the client asked be kept confidential, unless
the law provides otherwise or the client consents in writing.

Disclosure is required, in the same conspicuous form, and the statute prints a model that names the
firm, identifies the clients, records the acknowledgment that the dual agent may not pass
confidences between the sides, and names each designated agent and the capacity in which they act —
**standard agent, limited service agent, or independent contractor**.

## 22.5 Two rules that prevent accidental relationships

**§ 54.1-2140** — the payment or promise of compensation **does not create a brokerage
relationship**.

**§ 54.1-2141** — a brokerage relationship is **not created by using a common source information
company**.

And **§ 54.1-2138.2** requires a licensee to **disclose an ownership interest in specific real
property**. Buying or selling for one's own account is permitted; concealing it is not.

### Hour 22 engagement check

**Question.** A firm's supervising broker assigns Licensee A to the seller and Licensee B to the
buyer in the same residential transaction. The buyer asks Licensee B whether the seller would take
less. Licensee B does not know, but Licensee A does, and the supervising broker knows too. Who may
tell whom what, and what is the supervising broker's status?

**Expected answer.** This is **designated agency** under § 54.1-2139.1. Licensee A may not disclose
the seller's confidential information to Licensee B; designated agents may disclose client
confidences **only to their own broker**. The **supervising broker is a dual agent or
representative** in this transaction and therefore may not pass confidential information from one
side to the other, as the statutory disclosure form itself acknowledges. So the answer to the buyer
is that Licensee B does not know and cannot obtain it, and the required disclosures and written
consents from **both** clients must be in place — under 18VAC135-20-220 A 3, from the buyer no later
than when the offer is presented to the licensee who will present it, and from the seller no later
than when the offer is presented to the seller.

## Hour 23 — Virginia licensing structure and regulatory authority

## 23.1 The Board and the Department

The **Real Estate Board** is established under **§ 54.1-2104** and its general powers are at
**§ 54.1-2105**, which includes the power to promulgate regulations and to set educational and
experience requirements for licensure. **§ 54.1-2105.1** carries other powers and duties, and
**§ 54.1-2105.2** authorises cease and desist orders and civil penalties for unlicensed activity.

The Board sits within the **Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation**. In practice
the Board makes regulatory and disciplinary decisions and DPOR administers licensing, receives
complaints, and conducts investigations.

Board regulations are published at **18VAC135-20**, the *Virginia Real Estate Board Licensing
Regulations*, and **§ 54.1-2143** requires the Board's regulations to be consistent with the
brokerage-relationships article.

## 23.2 Licence categories

**§ 54.1-2106.1** requires licences. The categories a student must be able to distinguish:

**Salesperson** — an individual licensed to provide real estate brokerage services **on behalf of
and under the supervision of** a broker. A salesperson never holds a licence that stands alone.

**Broker** — an individual qualified to operate independently and to hold a firm licence.

**Firm** — the business entity licence. **18VAC135-20-20** sets its requirements.

**Principal broker** — the broker responsible for the firm, including responsibility for the firm's
escrow accounts, with signatory authority, under 18VAC135-20-181 A 1 b.

**Supervising broker** — the broker responsible for supervision of a place of business, a branch
office, **or a real estate team**, under § 54.1-2110.1 A.

**Branch office** — an additional place of business, each of which must be supervised.

**Real estate team** — expressly named in § 54.1-2110.1 A as requiring a supervising broker, and in
B 5 as something the supervising broker must ensure operates in accordance with the chapter and the
regulations. A team is not a firm and does not have its own independent existence.

**§ 54.1-2103** sets out exemptions from the chapter — the categories of person who may do things
that would otherwise require a licence.

## 23.3 Entry, activation, and renewal

**18VAC135-20-30 and -35** set the qualifications for individual licensure; **-45** covers a
salesperson or broker licence held as a business entity; **-50** concurrent licences; **-65**
universal licence recognition; **-70** activation or transfer of licence.

A licence must be **active** and held with a firm to be used. An inactive licensee may not provide
brokerage services.

**Post-licence education** is separately required. **§ 54.1-2105.01** requires educational
requirements for all salespersons **within one year of licensure**, and **18VAC135-20-95** carries
the post-licence education requirements. **§ 54.1-2105.03** and **18VAC135-20-101** govern
continuing education and qualification for renewal; **-110** the procedures for renewal;
**§ 54.1-2105.04** and **-140 through -150** reinstatement after failure to renew.

**This course does not state the hour counts, deadlines or fees** for pre-licence, post-licence or
continuing education, or the licence fees at 18VAC135-20-13 and -15, because those figures have not
been read from the primary text for this project and they change. A student's own obligations must
be read from the Board's current regulation and fee schedule.

## 23.4 Supervision — § 54.1-2110.1

Every place of business, branch office, and real estate team must be supervised by a supervising
broker, who must exercise **reasonable and adequate supervision**. The broker may designate another
broker to assist, **but the designation does not relieve the supervising broker of responsibility**.

Subsection B defines reasonable and adequate supervision, and a salesperson should know what their
broker is required to provide:

1. **Being available at reasonable times** to review and approve all documents — leases, contracts,
   brokerage agreements, and advertising — affecting the firm's clients and business.
2. Ensuring training opportunities and that the office has **written procedures and policies**
   giving clear guidance in eight areas: (a) escrow deposit handling; (b) federal and state fair
   housing compliance, if the firm does residential brokerage, leasing or management; (c)
   advertising and marketing of the firm and affiliated teams or entities; (d) negotiating and
   drafting contracts, leases and brokerage agreements; (e) oversight and limits on the use of
   unlicensed assistants; (f) creating agency or independent contractor relationships; (g)
   distributing information on new or amended laws and regulations; and (h) disclosing required
   information relating to the physical condition of real property.
3. Ensuring brokerage services are carried out competently and per the chapter.
4. Reasonable steps to ensure compliance by all licensees assigned to a branch office, **including
   ensuring licensees hold a current licence**.
5. Ensuring affiliated teams and business entities operate per the chapter and the regulations.
6. Ensuring **brokerage agreements include the supervising broker's name and contact information**.
7. **Maintaining these records for three years**, furnished to the Board's agent on request.

Subsection C adds that a supervising broker residing **more than 50 miles** from a supervised branch
office with licensees regularly conducting business there must certify supervision **in writing
quarterly**.

**18VAC135-20-165** adds four further duties: supervise brokerage services in a timely manner; take
reasonable steps to ensure only licensees undertake activities requiring a licence, per
18VAC135-20-335 A; provide adequate supervision over unlicensed individuals and entities, per
18VAC135-20-335 B; and act with reasonable diligence and promptness in responding to inquiries from
cooperating brokers.

**18VAC135-20-335** is the section that tells an unlicensed assistant what they may and may not do.
It is the everyday application of duties 2 and 3, and a salesperson working with an assistant should
read it directly.

## 23.5 Place of business and records

**§ 54.1-2110** requires a resident broker to maintain a place of business in Virginia;
**18VAC135-20-160** carries the regulation. **§ 54.1-2146** requires licensee maintenance of
records, and **18VAC135-20-185** sets out what the principal broker must keep. **18VAC135-20-225**
provides for audits. **§ 54.1-2109** deals with the death or disability of a broker;
**§ 54.1-2106.2** with certification of audit on renewal.

## 23.6 The Transaction Recovery Fund

**§§ 54.1-2112 through 54.1-2120** establish the **Virginia Real Estate Transaction Recovery Fund**:
a fund maintained by assessments on licensees from which a member of the public who obtains an
unsatisfied judgment against a licensee for improper or dishonest conduct may recover, subject to
limitations. **§ 54.1-2119** provides that a licensee's licence is **revoked** upon payment from the
fund on that licensee's account.

The Fund is a consumer-protection mechanism and, for the licensee, a reminder that the profession
collectively pays for individual misconduct.

### Hour 23 engagement check

**Question.** A newly licensed salesperson joins a firm whose supervising broker lives 70 miles from
the branch office where the salesperson works. The salesperson is given an unlicensed assistant and
asks the broker for the office's written policies. What is the broker required to do in each
respect, and where is each requirement found?

**Expected answer.** Because the supervising broker resides **more than 50 miles** from a branch
office with licensees regularly conducting business there, the broker must **certify supervision in
writing quarterly** — § 54.1-2110.1 C. The broker must have **written procedures and policies** in
the eight areas listed at § 54.1-2110.1 B 2, including oversight and limits on the use of unlicensed
assistants at B 2 e, and must provide adequate supervision over unlicensed individuals under
18VAC135-20-165 3 with reference to 18VAC135-20-335 B. The broker must also be available at
reasonable times to review and approve documents (B 1), ensure the salesperson holds a current
licence (B 4), and retain the supervision records for **three years** (B 7).

## Hour 24 — Virginia standards of conduct and discipline

## 24.1 Where the disciplinary power comes from

**18VAC135-20-155 A** gives the Board power to impose a monetary penalty and to suspend or revoke a
licence or certificate where the licensee has violated, or cooperated with others in violating, any
provision of Chapters 1, 2, 3 and 21 of Title 54.1, **Chapter 10 of Title 55.1** — the Consumer Real
Estate Settlement Protection Act — or any Board regulation. It operates through the **Administrative
Process Act**, § 2.2-4000 et seq.

**Subsection B matters to conduct outside work.** A licensee failing to comply with Chapter 21 or
the Board's regulations in performing acts covered by § 54.1-2100 **may be charged with a violation
regardless of whether those acts are in the licensee's personal capacity or in the licensee's
capacity as a licensee.** Selling one's own house badly is within reach of the Board.

## 24.2 Prohibited acts — 18VAC135-20-260

The section lists prohibited acts. Among them: obtaining or attempting to obtain a licence by false
or fraudulent representation; holding more than one broker or salesperson licence in Virginia except
as the chapter permits; signing an experience verification form without direct supervision or actual
knowledge of the applicant's activities, **or unreasonably refusing to sign one**; conviction or a
finding of guilt, however adjudicated, in any US jurisdiction of a misdemeanour involving fraudulent
or dishonest acts, a sexual offence, non-marijuana drug distribution, or battery, or **any** felony,
with review subject to § 54.1-204; **failing to inform the Board in writing within 30 days** of such
a conviction; having a professional or occupational licence suspended, revoked, or surrendered in
connection with a disciplinary action in any jurisdiction; and **failing to inform the Board in
writing within 30 days** of such a disciplinary action.

Two 30-day clocks. Both are self-reporting obligations, and both are frequently missed by licensees
who assume the Board will learn of the matter another way.

## 24.3 Conflict of interest — 18VAC135-20-270

Verbatim, the whole section:

> Actions constituting a conflict of interest include:
> 1. Being active with a real estate broker other than the licensee's principal broker, without the
> written consent of the principal broker; and
> 2. Acting for more than one client in a transaction governed by the provisions of §§ 54.1-2139,
> 54.1-2139.01, and 54.1-2139.1 of the Code of Virginia without first obtaining the written consent
> of all clients.

Subdivision 2 is the enforcement side of Hour 22: **dual and designated arrangements without written
consent from all clients are a conflict of interest**, not merely a paperwork lapse.

## 24.4 Misrepresentation or omission — 18VAC135-20-300

The section lists actions constituting misrepresentation or omission. The first five:

1. **Bait and switch** — advertising or offering property for sale or rent with the intent not to
   sell or rent at the advertised price or terms, unless the advertisement clearly states a specific
   limited quantity or period and the licensee in fact had that quantity at those terms at the time,
   including listing status and condition of property.
2. Failing, as a licensee representing a seller or landlord **as a standard agent**, to disclose in
   a timely manner to a prospective purchaser or tenant **all material adverse facts pertaining to
   the physical condition of the property that are actually known by the licensee**.
3. Failing **to tender promptly to the client or unrepresented party every written offer, every
   written counteroffer, and every written rejection** to purchase, option, or lease.
4. Failing to disclose in a timely manner **to the licensee's client all material facts related to
   the property or concerning the transaction** where the failure would be a failure to exercise
   ordinary care.
5. Failing to provide in a timely manner to all principals to the transaction the required written
   notices.

Subdivision 3 is worth stating twice, because it is the rule licensees most often break out of
misplaced judgment: **every** written offer goes to the client. Not the ones the licensee thinks are
worth presenting.

## 24.5 The other conduct sections

**18VAC135-20-280 — improper financial transactions and dealings.**
**18VAC135-20-310 — improper delivery of instruments.**
**18VAC135-20-240 — provision of information to the Board.**
**18VAC135-20-340 and -345 — the effect of disciplinary action on subordinate licensees and on
certificates, approvals and concurrent licences.** A firm licence problem does not stay inside the
firm.

## 24.6 When is the broker responsible?

**18VAC135-20-330**, which must be read exactly, because it is commonly taught backwards:

> Any unlawful act or violation ... by any real estate salesperson, employee, partner or affiliate
> of a principal broker, supervising broker, or both, **may not be cause for disciplinary action
> against the principal broker, supervising broker, or both, unless it appears to the satisfaction
> of the board that the principal broker, supervising broker, or both, knew or should have known of
> the unlawful act or violation and failed to take reasonable action under the circumstances to
> remedy the situation.**

This is a **knew-or-should-have-known** standard coupled with a **failure to remedy**. It is not
vicarious liability. A broker who did not know, could not reasonably have known, or who knew and
took reasonable action, is not disciplined for the salesperson's act.

Note what makes "should have known" bite: the supervision duties in § 54.1-2110.1 B. A broker
without written policies, without document review, and without oversight of unlicensed assistants
has a much harder time saying they could not reasonably have known.

## 24.7 The process, and what a licensee does when a complaint arrives

A complaint is filed with DPOR; an investigation follows; the licensee is given notice and an
opportunity to respond; the matter may be resolved by consent order, by an informal fact-finding
conference, or by a formal hearing under the Administrative Process Act; and sanctions range from a
monetary penalty through suspension to revocation. **§ 54.1-2111.1** provides for a **voluntary
compliance program** for real estate brokers.

The professional response to a complaint is: tell the principal broker immediately; do not contact
the complainant to argue; preserve every record, and change nothing; respond within the time given;
and obtain counsel. **18VAC135-20-240** requires provision of information to the Board — failing to
respond is itself a violation.

### Hour 24 engagement check

**Question.** A salesperson receives three written offers and presents only two, believing the third
to be too low to be worth the seller's time. The supervising broker had written policies, reviewed
documents weekly, and learned of the third offer a month later. Identify the violation and state
whether the broker is subject to discipline.

**Expected answer.** The salesperson violated **18VAC135-20-300 3** — failure to tender promptly to
the client every written offer — and, in substance, **§ 54.1-2131 A 2 c**, which requires receiving
and presenting written offers to and from the seller in a timely manner. As to the broker,
**18VAC135-20-330** applies: the broker is subject to discipline only if the broker **knew or should
have known** and **failed to take reasonable action to remedy**. On these facts the broker had
written policies and a document-review practice and learned only later, so the knew-or-should-have-known
element is not established on what is stated — and the answer turns on whether the broker took
reasonable action once they did learn.
