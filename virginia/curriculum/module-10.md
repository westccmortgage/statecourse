# Hour 37 — Alternative financing and consumer protection

## 37.1 Structures other than the level-payment fixed loan

**Adjustable-rate mortgage.** The interest rate changes periodically according to an **index** plus a
**margin**. The margin is fixed for the life of the loan; the index moves. **Caps** limit movement:
an initial cap at the first adjustment, a periodic cap at each subsequent adjustment, and a lifetime
cap. An **initial fixed period** — the "5" in a 5/6 ARM — precedes the first adjustment. The
borrower carries the interest-rate risk in exchange for a lower initial rate, and the question that
matters is not the start rate but the payment at the caps.

**Balloon loan.** Payments are calculated on a long amortisation but the entire remaining balance
falls due on a much earlier maturity date. The borrower must refinance, sell, or pay, and none of
those is guaranteed to be available.

**Interest-only.** Payments cover interest only for a period; no principal is retired, so no equity
is built by amortisation.

**Buydown.** Points are paid at closing to reduce the rate — permanently, or temporarily on a
declining schedule such as a 2-1 buydown. Frequently paid by a seller or builder as a concession, and
the concession must appear in the contract and in the settlement documents.

**Construction loan.** Short-term, disbursed in **draws** against inspected progress, interest paid
on the drawn balance, and converted or replaced by permanent financing at completion.

**Bridge loan.** Short-term financing against existing equity to buy before selling. Expensive, and
it depends on the existing property actually selling.

**Reverse mortgage.** For older homeowners, converting equity into payments with repayment deferred
until the borrower dies, sells, or ceases to occupy. Occupancy, tax, insurance and maintenance
obligations continue and their breach can accelerate the loan. **Home Equity Conversion Mortgages**
are FHA-insured and require counselling. A licensee's role here is referral, always.

**Home equity loan and line of credit.** A second lien against the equity — a fixed advance, or a
revolving line. Hour 13's subordination problem lives here.

**Seller financing, wraparound, and land contract** were covered at Hour 34.4, including that
Virginia has a **Residential Executory Real Estate Contracts Act** at Chapter 30 of Title 55.1
governing installment contracts, whose provisions this course does not state because they have not
been read from primary text for this project.

## 37.2 The federal consumer-protection layer

**Truth in Lending Act (TILA)** and **Regulation Z** require disclosure of the cost of credit,
principally the **annual percentage rate**, which expresses interest plus specified costs as a yearly
rate so that offers can be compared. TILA also governs advertising of credit terms: state one
triggering term and the rest must follow.

**Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (RESPA)** and **Regulation X** govern the settlement process
for federally related mortgage loans. Two prohibitions matter to a licensee personally:

**Section 8(a)** prohibits giving or accepting **any fee, kickback or thing of value pursuant to an
agreement or understanding for the referral of settlement service business.**

**Section 8(b)** prohibits **unearned fees** — splitting a charge for settlement services where no
service was actually performed.

**Affiliated business arrangements** are permitted where the relationship is disclosed to the
consumer in the prescribed form, the referral is not required, and the only thing of value received
is a return on ownership interest.

Points for licensees to hold on to: a "marketing agreement" that is in substance payment for
referrals is a violation whatever it is called; and gifts, event sponsorship, and desk rentals
between settlement service providers and licensees are exactly where RESPA enforcement concentrates.

**TILA-RESPA Integrated Disclosure (TRID)** merged the two disclosure regimes into the **Loan
Estimate**, delivered shortly after application, and the **Closing Disclosure**, which must be
received by the borrower a defined period before consummation. The timing rules mean that certain
late changes **re-start** a waiting period, which is why a change to the loan two days before
settlement can move the settlement date. A licensee who does not know this will make promises about
dates that the process cannot keep.

**Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA)** and **Regulation B** prohibit discrimination in credit
transactions on the basis of race, colour, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age
(provided the applicant has capacity to contract), receipt of public assistance income, and the good
faith exercise of rights under consumer credit protection law.

**Home Mortgage Disclosure Act** requires lender reporting used to detect lending patterns.
**Fair Credit Reporting Act** governs credit reports and adverse action notices.
**The SAFE Act** requires licensing of mortgage loan originators.
And the **Dodd-Frank Act** brought the **ability-to-repay** requirement and the **qualified
mortgage** standard, which restrict several of the structures above for owner-occupied residential
loans.

## 37.3 Predatory lending, and what a licensee does

Predatory patterns to recognise: loans made without regard to the borrower's ability to repay;
**equity stripping**; **loan flipping** — repeated refinancing that generates fees without benefit;
packing loans with unnecessary products; steering a qualified borrower to a higher-priced product;
undisclosed balloon payments; and pressure to sign without reading.

The licensee's role is not to diagnose. It is to notice, to say what has been noticed, and to route
the client to a **HUD-approved housing counselling agency**, to counsel, or to another lender for a
comparison — and to keep out of any arrangement in which the licensee's own benefit depends on the
borrower choosing a particular lender.

## Hour 37 engagement check

**Question.** A title company offers a licensee's firm $1,200 a month for a "marketing services
agreement" under which the firm displays the title company's brochures. The firm's actual referrals
to that company are expected to rise. Analyse.

**Expected answer.** The question is whether the payment is compensation for **services actually
performed at market value** or is in substance a payment **for the referral of settlement service
business**. If it is the latter, it violates **RESPA section 8(a)**, and if the fee exceeds the value
of any service actually rendered, the excess is an **unearned fee** under **section 8(b)** — and
labelling the arrangement a marketing agreement does not change its substance. The fact that
referrals are expected to rise as a result of the payment is precisely the evidence enforcement looks
for. The correct step is to route the proposal to the principal broker and to counsel before any
money changes hands, and § 54.1-2110.1 B 2 requires the firm to have written policies in this area.

# Hour 38 — Finance mathematics

## 38.1 Loan-to-value and down payment

**LTV = loan amount ÷ the lesser of sale price or appraised value.**

*Purchase price $385,000, loan $327,250.* LTV = 327,250 ÷ 385,000 = **85%**. Down payment
= 385,000 − 327,250 = **$57,750**, which is 15%.

*Purchase price $400,000, appraisal $385,000, lender lends 80% LTV.* The lender lends against the
**lower** figure: 0.80 × 385,000 = **$308,000**. The buyer must bring 400,000 − 308,000 =
**$92,000** — the $80,000 they planned plus the **$15,000 appraisal gap**. This calculation is the
whole reason the appraisal contingency exists.

## 38.2 Discount points and origination

**One point = 1% of the loan amount**, paid at closing. Discount points buy down the interest rate;
an origination fee compensates the lender for making the loan.

*Loan $327,250, 1.5 discount points.* 327,250 × 0.015 = **$4,908.75**.

The break-even question is the one a buyer actually needs: monthly saving from the lower rate divided
into the cost of the points gives the number of months to recover it. Whether it is worth doing
depends on how long the borrower will hold the loan — which the borrower knows and the licensee does
not.

## 38.3 Interest

**Simple interest: I = P × R × T**, with T in years.

*$327,250 at 6.25% for one month.* 327,250 × 0.0625 = $20,453.13 per year; ÷ 12 = **$1,704.43** for
the month. That is the interest portion of the first payment; everything above it retires principal.

**Per diem interest** at closing: annual interest ÷ 365 × days. *Closing on 22 June with the first
payment 1 August:* interest is collected for 22–30 June, nine days. 20,453.13 ÷ 365 = $56.04 per day;
× 9 = **$504.36**.

## 38.4 Amortisation and the payment factor

An amortising payment is constant; its composition changes. Each payment covers the interest accrued
and applies the remainder to principal, so the interest portion falls and the principal portion rises
over the term.

A **payment factor** is the monthly payment per $1,000 of loan at a given rate and term.

*Factor 6.16 per $1,000, loan $327,250.* 327,250 ÷ 1,000 = 327.25; × 6.16 = **$2,015.86** monthly
principal and interest.

Then check the first month: interest $1,704.43 (above), so principal = 2,015.86 − 1,704.43 =
**$311.43**, and the balance after one payment is 327,250 − 311.43 = **$326,938.57**.

**PITI** adds taxes and insurance: principal, interest, taxes, insurance — plus mortgage insurance
and any association dues the lender includes in qualifying.

## 38.5 Qualifying ratios

**Front-end (housing) ratio = monthly housing expense ÷ gross monthly income.**
**Back-end (total debt) ratio = total monthly obligations ÷ gross monthly income.**

*Gross monthly income $9,200. PITI $2,640. Other monthly obligations $610.*
Front-end: 2,640 ÷ 9,200 = **28.7%**.
Back-end: (2,640 + 610) ÷ 9,200 = 3,250 ÷ 9,200 = **35.3%**.

**The thresholds are the lender's and the programme's, and they change.** This course states none.
A licensee computes the ratio to help a buyer think; the lender decides.

## 38.6 Cash to close

Cash to close = down payment + closing costs + prepaid items + escrow reserves − earnest money
already paid − seller credits − lender credits.

*Worked:* purchase $385,000; down payment $57,750; closing costs $6,400; prepaid interest $504.36;
first-year hazard premium $1,850; escrow reserves $1,420; earnest money already paid $5,000; seller
credit $3,000.

57,750 + 6,400 + 504.36 + 1,850 + 1,420 = 67,924.36
less 5,000 and 3,000 = **$59,924.36**.

**This is an estimate a licensee prepares to help a buyer plan.** The controlling figure is the
lender's **Closing Disclosure** and the settlement agent's statement, and every estimate a licensee
gives should say so in writing.

## 38.7 Commission arithmetic

*Sale price $385,000, total brokerage fee 5%, split evenly between listing and selling firms, and the
listing firm splits 60/40 with its salesperson.*
Total fee: 385,000 × 0.05 = **$19,250**. Listing firm: **$9,625**. Salesperson: 9,625 × 0.60 =
**$5,775**.

Reverse it: *a salesperson received $5,775 on a 60% split of a firm's half of a 5% fee.* Firm's half
= 5,775 ÷ 0.60 = 9,625; total fee = 19,250; sale price = 19,250 ÷ 0.05 = **$385,000**.

Brokerage fees are negotiable between the client and the broker, and **§ 54.1-2136 (ii)** requires
the licensee to advise a prospective client of the broker's compensation and whether it will be
shared with a broker representing another party **before the relationship begins**.

## Hour 38 engagement check

**Question.** Purchase price $460,000. The buyer plans 10% down. The appraisal returns $448,000 and
the lender will lend 90% LTV. Compute the loan, the required cash for the down payment, and the
appraisal gap. Then compute one discount point.

**Expected answer.** The lender lends against the **lower** of price or appraised value: 0.90 ×
448,000 = **$403,200**. The buyer must bring 460,000 − 403,200 = **$56,800**, against the $46,000
they planned — an **appraisal gap of $10,800**. One discount point on the loan is 403,200 × 0.01 =
**$4,032**. The professional consequence is that the appraisal contingency now controls: the gap must
be closed by the buyer, by a price reduction, by a successful reconsideration of value, or by
termination under the contract's terms.

# Hour 39 — The loan process and financing a transaction

## 39.1 The stages

**Pre-qualification** — an informal estimate from information the borrower states and nobody
verifies. It is a conversation.

**Pre-approval** — a submitted application, verified income, assets and credit, and usually a
conditional underwriting decision subject to a property. A seller evaluating offers is entitled to
know which of the two is in front of them, and a buyer's licensee who lets a pre-qualification be
presented as a pre-approval has misrepresented their buyer's position.

**Application.** The formal application starts the TRID clock; the **Loan Estimate** must follow
within the required period.

**Processing.** Documentation is gathered: income, employment, assets, credit explanations, the
purchase contract and any amendments, and — where applicable — condominium or association documents.

**Appraisal and title.** Ordered by the lender and the settlement agent respectively. Both can stop a
closing, and both are outside the buyer's control.

**Underwriting.** The decision. Automated underwriting first in most cases, with manual underwriting
where the file requires it. The output is an approval **with conditions**.

**Conditions.** The list of items required before the loan can close. Prior-to-document conditions
must clear before the closing package is issued; prior-to-funding conditions before money moves.
This is the stage that most often breaks a settlement date, because a condition raised on Tuesday for
a Thursday settlement is a condition that cannot be met in time.

**Clear to close, Closing Disclosure, consummation, funding, recording.**

**Servicing.** The loan is typically sold or its servicing transferred. Borrowers must be notified,
and the terms of the note do not change.

## 39.2 What underwriting actually examines

**Capacity** — income, its stability, and the ratios of Hour 38.
**Credit** — history and score, and the explanation for anything derogatory.
**Capital** — assets, reserves, and the **source** of funds. Large recent deposits must be
documented; an undocumented deposit is a condition, and a cash gift needs a gift letter and a paper
trail.
**Collateral** — the appraisal and the property's condition and eligibility for the programme.

## 39.3 What buyers do that breaks their own loan

A buyer's licensee should give this list in writing at ratification, because every item on it happens:

Do not change or leave a job. Do not open credit accounts or finance furniture, a car, or
appliances. Do not close accounts or pay off collections without asking the loan officer first. Do
not make large deposits that cannot be documented. Do not move money between accounts unnecessarily.
Do not co-sign for anyone. Do not miss a payment on anything.

Underwriting is frequently re-verified immediately before closing, so a purchase made a week before
settlement can undo an approval given a month earlier.

## 39.4 The financing contingency, managed

Read the contract's financing contingency and extract four things: the **loan type and amount** it
protects; the **deadline** for the commitment; **what notice** must be given and to whom; and **what
happens** if the deadline passes without action — which, under many forms, is that the protection is
lost.

Then work backwards. Application within the contract's period. Documentation to the lender
immediately. Appraisal ordered early enough for a reconsideration to be possible. Conditions chased
weekly, in writing, with a written record of each request and response. Escalate to the principal
broker the moment a deadline is at risk.

## 39.5 The licensee's boundary in financing conversations

The licensee **may**: explain what the categories are; explain the difference between
pre-qualification and pre-approval; explain the mechanics of the contingency and the deadlines;
compute an estimate and say it is an estimate; and refer to more than one lender.

The licensee **may not**: state that a buyer will qualify; state a rate; state what a programme
requires as though it were settled; complete or advise on the loan application; or receive anything
of value for a referral, which is Hour 37's RESPA section 8 problem.

## Hour 39 engagement check

**Question.** A buyer's file is approved with conditions on 3 October for a 20 October settlement. On
15 October the lender adds a condition: documentation of a $9,000 deposit made in September. The
buyer says it was a gift from a parent. What happens, and what does the licensee do?

**Expected answer.** An undocumented large deposit is a **capital** condition: underwriting requires
the **source** of funds to be documented, and a gift requires a **gift letter and a paper trail**
showing the donor's withdrawal and the borrower's deposit. Until it clears, the loan cannot proceed
to clear-to-close, so the 20 October settlement is at risk. The licensee's job is to get the
requirement to the buyer in writing the same day with a plain description of exactly what the lender
needs, to confirm in writing what date the lender needs it by, to notify the other side promptly that
a date may move, and to check the contract's **financing contingency deadline** and whether an
extension by written amendment is needed. The licensee does **not** advise on how to characterise the
deposit and does not contact the donor.

# Hour 40 — Settlement, closing, and title transfer

## 40.1 Who is at the table

**Settlement agent.** In Virginia, settlement agents are regulated under the **Consumer Real Estate
Settlement Protection Act**, Chapter 10 of Title 55.1 (§ 55.1-1000 et seq.). The Act is referenced
directly in the licensing scheme: **§ 54.1-2132 F** refers to settlement services under Chapter 10,
and **18VAC135-20-155 A** makes a violation of Chapter 10 a ground for Real Estate Board discipline.
A Virginia licensee should know that the settlement side of the transaction has its own statutory
regime and that violating it can reach their own licence.

**Attorney** — for one or more parties, and in many Virginia transactions conducting settlement.
**Lender** and its closing department. **Title insurer**. **Surveyor**, if a survey was obtained.
**Real estate licensees** for each side. And the **parties**.

## 40.2 What must be true before money moves

**Title.** The examination is complete, the Schedule B-I requirements are satisfied, payoffs are
obtained and good through the settlement date, and any curative instruments are in hand.

**Loan.** Clear to close; the closing package is issued; the **Closing Disclosure** has been received
by the borrower for the required period; funds are wired.

**Property.** Contract conditions are met — agreed repairs completed with documentation, the
**final walk-through** conducted, and any lender-required repairs completed and re-inspected.

**Association.** For a common-interest community, the **resale certificate** obtained and delivered
under § 55.1-2309, the cancellation period under § 55.1-2312 expired or waived by settlement, and any
association fees and outstanding assessments accounted for.

**Insurance.** The buyer's policy is bound effective the settlement date, and evidence is with the
lender.

**Funds.** Buyer funds delivered by the method and deadline the settlement agent requires — and by a
method verified as described in Hour 50.

## 40.3 The settlement statement

The **Closing Disclosure** is the borrower's statement in a federally related mortgage loan; the
settlement agent produces a settlement statement covering both sides.

The logic is **debit and credit**. A **debit** is a charge to a party; a **credit** is an amount in
their favour. Each side's statement balances: the buyer's debits total what they owe and their
credits total what they bring or receive, and the difference is cash to close.

The buyer's principal debits: purchase price; loan-related charges; title and settlement charges;
recording and transfer charges allocated to them; prepaid interest, insurance and escrow reserves;
and their share of prorated items paid in advance by the seller. Principal credits: the loan amount;
earnest money; seller and lender credits; and their share of prorated items the seller owes.

The seller's principal debits: existing loan payoffs; the brokerage fee; the seller's share of
prorations; and the charges allocated to them. Principal credit: the purchase price.

**Prorated items** appear on both statements in opposite directions, and Hour 41 does the arithmetic.

## 40.4 Signing, funding, recording, possession

Documents are signed and notarised; the deed is delivered; funds are disbursed; the deed and deed of
trust are **recorded** in the land records of the circuit court of the city or county. Recording is
the step that gives the world constructive notice — Hour 11 — and until it happens the buyer's
interest is vulnerable.

**Possession** transfers when the contract says, which is usually at recording and funding but is not
necessarily so. A post-settlement occupancy by the seller is a separate written agreement, and it
raises insurance and liability questions that neither party usually thinks about.

## 40.5 What stops a closing, and what the licensee does about it

The recurring stop conditions: a title requirement discovered late; a payoff figure that arrives
short or expires; an undisclosed judgment or lien against a seller with a common name; a loan
condition raised at the last moment; a Closing Disclosure change that re-starts the waiting period; a
failed walk-through; a resale certificate never delivered; an unresolved survey matter; and a wire
that has not arrived.

The licensee's contribution is the same in every case: **know the deadline, know who owns the item,
put the request in writing, and escalate early.** A settlement date is met by work done three weeks
earlier, not by urgency on the day.

## 40.6 After closing

Deliver the buyer their complete file. Ensure the **certificate of satisfaction** releasing the
seller's paid-off deed of trust is recorded — the seller's problem, but a foreseeable one. Confirm
the recorded deed is returned. And retain the transaction records: the firm's obligations under
**18VAC135-20-185 C 1** — brokerage agreements and dual and designated consents for **three years
from execution** — under **§ 54.1-2138 D** — brokerage-relationship disclosures relating to fully
executed purchase contracts for **three years** — and under **§ 54.1-2146**, which requires licensee
maintenance of records.

## Hour 40 engagement check

**Question.** Two days before settlement the lender issues a revised Closing Disclosure changing the
loan product. The buyer's licensee has promised the seller possession on the settlement date, and the
seller has a moving van booked. What has happened and what must the licensee do?

**Expected answer.** Certain changes to the Closing Disclosure — including a change in loan product —
**re-start the mandatory waiting period** before consummation under the TRID rules, so settlement
cannot occur on the scheduled date. The licensee must notify their client and the other side **in
writing immediately**, obtain from the lender the earliest date consummation may occur, and get a
**written amendment** moving the settlement date and any dependent dates, including possession, the
rate lock expiry, and any post-settlement occupancy. The licensee must not promise a date the lender
has not confirmed, and must not tell the parties what the rule requires as though it were settled —
the lender and the settlement agent state the date.

## Instructor source note for Module 10

Virginia statements rest on text read directly from law.lis.virginia.gov on 2026-08-20:
**§ 54.1-2132 F** and **18VAC135-20-155 A**, both of which reference **Chapter 10 of Title 55.1**,
the Consumer Real Estate Settlement Protection Act; **§§ 54.1-2136, 54.1-2138 D, 54.1-2146,
54.1-2110.1 B 2**; **18VAC135-20-185 C 1**; and **§§ 55.1-2309 and 55.1-2312** for the resale
certificate and cancellation conditions in Hour 40.2. The Title 55.1 chapter list identifying
Chapters 10 and 30 was read the same day.

**Federal statements** — TILA and Regulation Z, RESPA and Regulation X including sections 8(a) and
8(b) and affiliated business arrangements, TRID's Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure and its
re-start rule, ECOA and Regulation B, HMDA, FCRA, the SAFE Act, and Dodd-Frank's ability-to-repay and
qualified mortgage standards — are federal law and are stated at the level of the well-settled
requirement. **No TRID waiting period is given in days**, no qualifying-ratio threshold is given, and
no PMI cancellation threshold is given: each is stated as a rule whose figure the lender and the
settlement agent supply. Hour 40's engagement check is written so that the correct student answer is
"the lender states the date".

**Chapter 10 of Title 55.1 has not been read section by section for this project.** Hour 40.1 states
only that settlement agents are regulated under it and that a violation of it is a Board disciplinary
ground under 18VAC135-20-155 A — both of which are supported by the text actually read — and states
no requirement from within the chapter.

All arithmetic in Hour 38 is worked in full so a reviewer can verify each figure. The rates, factors
and prices are illustrative.
