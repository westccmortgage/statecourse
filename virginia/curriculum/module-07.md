## Hour 25 — Virginia escrow, records, advertising, and supervision

## 25.1 Escrow — the account itself

**18VAC135-20-181 A 1.** If money is to be held in escrow, each firm or sole proprietorship must
maintain one or more **federally insured** escrow accounts into which **all funds received in
connection with a real estate transaction must be deposited**. Then five requirements:

**a.** The account must be **in the name by which the firm or sole proprietorship is licensed.**
**b.** The **principal broker will be held responsible** for these accounts, **including having
signatory authority** on them.
**c.** The **supervising broker and any other licensee with signatory authority may be held
responsible.**
**d.** All such **accounts, checks and bank statements must be labelled "escrow"**, and the accounts
must be **designated as escrow accounts with the financial institution**.
**e.** The **balance must be sufficient at all times** to account for all funds designated to be
held by the firm.

Read (b) and (c) together: responsibility runs to the principal broker automatically and to anyone
else with signatory authority potentially. A salesperson given signing authority on an escrow
account has accepted exposure.

## 25.2 Escrow — the deposits

**18VAC135-20-181 A 2** requires that **all** of the following be deposited into escrow: down
payments, earnest money deposits, money received on final settlement, application deposits, rental
payments, rental security deposits, money advanced by a buyer or seller for the payment of expenses
in connection with closing, and money advanced by the broker's client or others.

The statutory layer sits above it: **§ 54.1-2108** protects escrow funds held by a broker;
**§ 54.1-2108.1** addresses protection in the event of **foreclosure** of the real property; and
**§ 54.1-2108.2** addresses protection in the event of **termination of a real estate purchase
contract** — the disputed-deposit case.

**Commingling** — mixing escrow money with the firm's operating funds — and **conversion** — using
escrow money for any purpose other than the one it was entrusted for — are the two failures the
whole scheme exists to prevent. They are not accounting errors. A firm that pays its rent from the
escrow account has converted client funds.

**18VAC135-20-225** provides for **audits**, and **§ 54.1-2106.2** requires certification of audit on
renewal of a firm or sole proprietorship licence.

## 25.3 Records

**18VAC135-20-185 A.** The principal broker must maintain financial records for any transaction
under the authority of the broker's licence, **readily accessible from the broker's place of
business**, containing: from whom money was received; the date of receipt; the place of deposit; the
date of deposit; the final disposition of the funds; and anything else the chapter requires. Where
the principal broker's office is outside Virginia and the firm has a Virginia branch, **a copy must
be readily accessible in the Virginia office.**

**Subsection B** requires a bookkeeping or recordkeeping system that accurately and clearly
discloses full compliance, in sufficient detail to determine it.

**Subsection C 1** sets the retention period a licensee must know: **each brokerage agreement must
be retained for three years from the date of execution**, and **each disclosure and consent to dual
agency or dual representation, and to designated agency or designated representation, must be
retained for three years from the date of execution.**

Three other three-year periods sit alongside it and are easy to confuse — they are three separate
obligations that happen to share a number:

- **§ 54.1-2138 D** — copies of brokerage-relationship disclosures relating to **fully executed
  purchase contracts**, three years.
- **§ 54.1-2110.1 B 7** — the **supervising broker's supervision records**, three years, furnished
  to the Board's agent on request.
- **18VAC135-20-185 C 1** — brokerage agreements and dual/designated consents, three years from
  execution.

**§ 54.1-2146** is the statutory requirement of licensee maintenance of records.

## 25.4 Advertising — 18VAC135-20-190

**Subsection A**, in substance: **all advertising must be under the direct supervision of the
principal broker or supervising broker, in the name of the firm** and, where applicable, must comply
with the § 54.1-2138.1 limited-service-agent disclosure. **The firm's licensed name must be clearly
and legibly displayed on all advertising.**

**Subsection B 1:** all advertising by a firm or affiliated licensee must contain a **clear, legible
and conspicuous advertising disclosure** including **(i) the firm's name and (ii) the office contact
information**. And then the delegation students miss: **the office contact information and any
additional required information will be specified by the principal or supervising broker in the
firm's written policies.**

That means the Board sets the requirement and the **firm** sets the format. A course cannot state a
required layout, and a licensee must read their own firm's written policy — which § 54.1-2110.1 B 2
c requires the firm to have for advertising and marketing.

**B 2:** all advertising by an affiliated licensee must be done under the policies of a principal or
supervising broker.

**B 3:** any property information provided by and in the licensee's control **must be consistent
with the property condition and the property's current contract status**. If a third party controls
the advertising, the licensee must make **a timely written request** for changes.

**B 4:** all advertising must be **updated in a timely manner** whenever there is a material change
to listing status or property description, with the same written-request rule where a third party
controls it.

Subsections B 3 and B 4 are why a sold listing left live with old photographs is a regulatory
problem and not merely untidy.

## 25.5 Place of business, and teams

**§ 54.1-2110** requires a resident broker to maintain a place of business in Virginia;
**18VAC135-20-160** carries the detail. **Real estate teams** must be supervised by a supervising
broker under § 54.1-2110.1 A, and the supervising broker must ensure affiliated teams operate per
the chapter and the regulations under B 5, and must have written policies covering **advertising and
marketing of the brokerage firm and any affiliated real estate teams or business entities** under
B 2 c. A team's branding is the firm's advertising.

### Hour 25 engagement check

**Question.** A firm receives a $10,000 earnest money deposit on Friday. The office manager, who is
unlicensed, deposits it Monday into the firm's operating account "because the escrow account was
short this month", and the firm transfers it to escrow on Wednesday. Identify every failure and its
source.

**Expected answer.** Depositing an earnest money deposit into the operating account is
**commingling and conversion**: 18VAC135-20-181 A 1 requires all funds received in connection with a
real estate transaction to be deposited into a designated escrow account, and A 2 names earnest
money specifically. That the escrow account was "short" is itself a violation of **A 1 e**, which
requires the balance to be sufficient **at all times** to account for all funds held. The
**principal broker is responsible** for the accounts under A 1 b, and any licensee with signatory
authority may be under A 1 c. The supervising broker's failure engages § 54.1-2110.1 B 2 a, which
requires written policies giving clear guidance on handling escrow deposits in compliance with law
and regulation, and 18VAC135-20-165 3, which requires adequate supervision over unlicensed
individuals. Whether the broker is disciplined for the manager's act is decided by
**18VAC135-20-330** — knew or should have known, and failed to remedy.

## Hour 26 — Federal and Virginia fair housing

## 26.1 The federal floor

The **federal Fair Housing Act** — Title VIII of the Civil Rights Act of 1968, as amended in 1974
and 1988 — prohibits discrimination in the sale, rental, and financing of dwellings on the basis of
**race, colour, religion, national origin, sex, familial status, and disability (handicap)**. Seven
classes. The 1974 amendment added sex; the 1988 amendment added familial status and disability.

**The Civil Rights Act of 1866** is older and narrower and, in one respect, stronger: it prohibits
**racial** discrimination in the sale or lease of all real and personal property, and — as the
Supreme Court held in *Jones v. Alfred H. Mayer Co.* (1968) — **it carries no exemptions**. A
transaction exempt from the Fair Housing Act is not exempt from the 1866 Act if the discrimination
is racial.

**Prohibited practices** under federal law include refusing to sell or rent; discriminating in terms
or conditions; discriminatory advertising; representing that a dwelling is unavailable when it is
not; **blockbusting** — inducing sales by representations about the entry of protected-class persons
into a neighbourhood; **steering** — directing prospects toward or away from areas on a protected
basis; and **redlining** — denying loans or insurance based on the composition of an area rather
than the applicant or property.

**Enforcement** runs through HUD complaint, administrative proceedings, and federal civil actions,
with the Department of Justice bringing pattern-or-practice cases.

## 26.2 Virginia goes further — § 36-96.3

The **Virginia Fair Housing Law**, Chapter 5.1 of Title 36, is broader. Reading § 36-96.3 A 1, the
protected bases are:

**race, colour, religion, national origin, sex, elderliness, source of funds, familial status,
sexual orientation, gender identity, military status** — and **disability**, which appears throughout
subdivisions 3 through 9.

Five of those are not in the federal list. A licensee working from a national text will be working
from an incomplete list, and the five additions — **elderliness, source of funds, sexual orientation,
gender identity, military status** — are exactly the ones most likely to appear in a real complaint.

**Source of funds** deserves particular attention because it is the newest habit to break. Refusing
to consider a housing choice voucher, or advertising "no vouchers", is discrimination on the basis
of source of funds in Virginia.

## 26.3 The nine unlawful practices, in the statute's own order

§ 36-96.3 A makes it unlawful to:

1. **Refuse to sell or rent** after a bona fide offer, refuse to negotiate, or otherwise make
   unavailable or deny a dwelling, because of a protected basis.
2. **Discriminate in the terms, conditions, or privileges** of sale or rental, or in the provision of
   services or facilities.
3. **Make, print, or publish** any notice, statement, or advertisement indicating a preference,
   limitation, or discrimination, or an intention to make one. Two drafting rules live here:
   **the use of words or symbols associated with a particular religion, national origin, sex, or race
   is prima facie evidence of an illegal preference, and a general disclaimer does not overcome it**;
   but **reference alone to places of worship — churches, synagogues, temples, mosques — is not**
   prima facie evidence.
4. **Represent that a dwelling is not available** for inspection, sale or rental when it is.
5. **Deny access to or membership in** a multiple listing service, brokers' organisation, or similar
   facility, or discriminate in the terms of access.
6. **Include a discriminatory restrictive covenant** in any transfer, sale, rental or lease, **or
   honour or exercise, or attempt to honour or exercise, any such covenant.**
7. **Induce or attempt to induce a sale or rental by representations regarding the entry or
   prospective entry** into the neighbourhood of persons of a protected class — blockbusting, in the
   statute's own words.
8. **Refuse to sell or rent, refuse to negotiate, or otherwise make unavailable or deny a dwelling
   because of a disability** of (i) the buyer or renter, (ii) a person residing or intending to
   reside there after the transaction, or (iii) **any person associated with the buyer or renter**.
9. **Discriminate in terms, conditions, privileges, services or facilities because of a disability**
   of the same three categories of person.

Subdivisions 8 and 9 reach **association**: discriminating against a buyer because of the disability
of someone associated with them is itself unlawful.

**Subsection C** extends the prohibition to **political jurisdictions**, their employees and
appointed commissions, in the application of local land use ordinances and in the permitting of
housing developments — including discrimination because a development contains affordable housing
units for households at or below 80 percent of area median income.

## 26.4 The exemption that does not help a licensee

**§ 36-96.2 A** exempts, in narrow circumstances, a single-family house sold or rented by a private
individual owner who owns no more than three at a time, subject to conditions — including that the
sale or rental occurs **without the use in any manner of the sales or rental services or facilities
of any real estate broker, agent or salesperson**, and without discriminatory advertising, which
remains prohibited by § 36-96.3 A 3 regardless.

Then the sentence every Virginia licensee must know:

> **This exemption shall not apply to or inure to the benefit of any licensee of the Real Estate
> Board or regulant of the Fair Housing Board, regardless of whether the licensee is acting in his
> personal or professional capacity.**

A licensee cannot use the owner exemption. Not when helping an exempt owner — the exemption is lost
the moment a licensee is used — and **not when selling their own house**.

**§ 36-96.2 B** covers rooms or units in an owner-occupied dwelling with living quarters for no more
than four families. **§ 36-96.2 C** permits a religious organisation to limit or prefer as to
dwellings it owns and operates for a non-commercial purpose, unless membership is restricted on a
protected basis, and covers certain private membership clubs. **Advertising remains prohibited under
every one of these exemptions**, because each is expressed as an exception "except for subdivision
A 3 of § 36-96.3".

## 26.5 Practice rules that follow

**Use objective, uniform, written criteria** for qualification, and apply them to everyone.
**Never answer a "what kind of neighbourhood is it?" question with demographics** — answer with
objective sources the buyer can consult for themselves: school ratings, crime statistics published
by the locality, census data. **Show every buyer everything that meets their stated criteria**, and
let the buyer narrow. **Record what the client asked for in their own words.**

And under **§ 54.1-2131 A 7** and **§ 54.1-2132 A 8**, compliance with all fair housing statutes and
regulations applicable to residential transactions is itself a statutory duty owed to the client —
so a fair housing violation is simultaneously a Real Estate Board matter.

### Hour 26 engagement check

**Question.** A licensee is asked by a landlord client to "screen out the voucher people" and to
"keep the building quiet — no kids". A second client, selling their own home privately, asks the
licensee for informal help but says the licensee will not be paid. Address all three requests.

**Expected answer.** Refusing housing choice vouchers is discrimination on the basis of **source of
funds**, a Virginia protected class under § 36-96.3 A 1 and 2. Excluding children is discrimination
on the basis of **familial status**. The licensee must refuse both instructions and say so in
writing; § 54.1-2131 A 7 and § 54.1-2133 make fair housing compliance a duty owed to the client, and
a client's instruction cannot authorise a violation. As to the third request: the § 36-96.2 A owner
exemption **is lost as soon as a licensee's services are used in any manner**, and in any case the
exemption **does not inure to the benefit of any Real Estate Board licensee regardless of capacity**
— so the licensee has no exemption to rely on and must comply fully.

## Hour 27 — Disability, accessibility, assistance animals, and housing legislation

## 27.1 The two things a disabled person may ask for

**§ 36-96.3 B** defines discrimination to include a refusal in three situations, and the first two
are the ones a licensee meets constantly.

**Reasonable modification — B (i).** A refusal to permit, **at the expense of the disabled person**,
reasonable modifications of existing premises occupied or to be occupied, if the modifications may
be necessary to afford full enjoyment of the premises. In a **rental**, the landlord may, **where it
is reasonable to do so**, condition permission on the renter agreeing to **restore the interior** to
its prior condition, reasonable wear and tear excepted.

**Reasonable accommodation — B (ii).** A refusal to make reasonable accommodations in **rules,
practices, policies, or services** when necessary to afford equal opportunity to use and enjoy a
dwelling.

The distinction, in one line: **a modification is a physical change and the tenant pays; an
accommodation is a change to a rule and the housing provider bears the ordinary cost of making it.**
A grab bar is a modification. Waiving a no-pets rule for an assistance animal is an accommodation.
Assigning a parking space nearer the door is an accommodation.

## 27.2 Design and construction — B (iii)

For **covered multi-family dwellings** designed and constructed for first occupancy **after 13 March
1991**, failure to design and construct so that:

1. **public and common use areas are readily accessible** to and usable by disabled persons;
2. **all doors designed to allow passage into and within the premises are wide enough** for
   wheelchair passage; and
3. premises within covered units contain **an accessible route into and through the dwelling**;
   **light switches, electrical outlets, thermostats and other environmental controls in accessible
   locations**; **reinforcements in bathroom walls to allow later installation of grab bars**; and
   **usable kitchens and bathrooms** in which a person in a wheelchair can manoeuvre.

**"Covered multi-family dwellings"** means buildings of **four or more units** where the building has
**one or more elevators**, and **ground floor units** in other buildings of four or more units.

**§ 36-96.3 D** provides that compliance with **ANSI A117.1**, or with other standards adopted in
HUD regulations, is deemed to satisfy the design requirement.

## 27.3 Assistance animals

An assistance animal is not a pet, and the request to keep one is a **reasonable accommodation**
request under § 36-96.3 B (ii). The practical consequences a licensee must understand:

- A **no-pets policy does not defeat the request.**
- **Pet fees and pet deposits do not apply** to an assistance animal, because it is not a pet.
- The tenant remains responsible for **damage the animal causes** and for the animal's behaviour.
- A housing provider may seek **reliable information** confirming the disability-related need where
  the disability or the need is not readily apparent, but may not demand details of the diagnosis.

Virginia has enacted specific provisions on assistance animals in rental housing, and the
verification a provider may request is regulated. **This course does not state that procedure**,
because the relevant sections of Title 36 and Title 55.1 have not been read from primary text for
this project. A licensee handling such a request must read the current provisions or route the
question to counsel — and must not refuse the request in the meantime.

**Service animals under the Americans with Disabilities Act are a different question.** The ADA
governs places of public accommodation and commercial facilities; **housing is governed by fair
housing law**, not the ADA. A leasing office open to the public may be a place of public
accommodation, and the two regimes can apply to different parts of the same property. Do not import
ADA service-animal rules into a housing accommodation analysis.

## 27.4 Familial status, elderliness, and housing for older persons

**Familial status** protects households with children under 18, a person who is pregnant, and a
person in the process of securing legal custody. **Elderliness** is a separate Virginia protected
class.

Federal law and Virginia law both permit qualified **housing for older persons** to operate as such
without violating the familial status prohibition, on conditions that must be met and documented.
**This course states no threshold**, because the qualifying conditions have not been read from
primary text for this project. A licensee marketing a community described as age-restricted must
obtain the community's own documentation of its qualification, in writing, and must not describe it
as exempt on the strength of its marketing.

## 27.5 Where fair housing meets the licensing statute

Two connections close the hour.

**18VAC135-20-330** names **Chapter 5.1 (§ 36-96.1 et seq.) of Title 36** alongside Chapter 21 as the
law whose violation by a salesperson can, on the knew-or-should-have-known standard, reach the
supervising broker. Fair housing is expressly inside the Board's supervisory scheme.

**§ 54.1-2110.1 B 2 b** requires a firm engaged in residential brokerage, leasing or management to
have **written procedures and policies giving clear guidance on complying with federal and state
fair housing laws and regulations.** A licensee should have read their firm's policy, and if the
firm does not have one, that is itself a supervision failure.

### Hour 27 engagement check

**Question.** A tenant with a mobility impairment asks a landlord for (a) permission to install a
ramp at the front entrance and grab bars in the bathroom, and (b) an assigned parking space nearer
the door in a lot with unassigned parking. The landlord asks who pays for what and whether the
tenant must undo anything.

**Expected answer.** (a) is a **reasonable modification** under § 36-96.3 B (i): permission may not
be refused, and the modification is **at the tenant's expense**. Because this is a rental, the
landlord may — **where it is reasonable to do so** — condition permission on the tenant agreeing to
**restore the interior** to its prior condition, reasonable wear and tear excepted; the interior
grab bars are the natural candidate, and a front-entrance ramp is an exterior change to which the
restoration condition on its face does not extend. (b) is a **reasonable accommodation** under
§ 36-96.3 B (ii) — a change to a rule or practice — and the housing provider bears the ordinary cost
of making it; no fee may be charged for the accommodation itself.

## Hour 28 — Real estate marketing and advertising compliance

## 28.1 Marketing the property, not the people

The organising principle for compliant marketing is short: **describe the property, never the
occupants or the desired occupants.**

"Four bedrooms, two baths, fenced rear yard, walking distance to the elementary school" describes
the property. "Perfect for a young family" describes who should live there, and is an expression of
preference on the basis of familial status. "Quiet building, ideal for mature professionals"
expresses preference on elderliness and familial status. "Walk to St. Anne's" is, on § 36-96.3 A 3's
own terms, **reference alone to a place of worship and is not prima facie evidence** — but
"a wonderful Christian community" uses words associated with a particular religion and **is** prima
facie evidence of an illegal preference that a disclaimer will not cure.

The same rule applies to photographs, to the models used in imagery, and to the language of a
property's own website.

## 28.2 The Board's advertising rules, applied

From Hour 25, applied to a real advertisement:

**Is it in the name of the firm, under the direct supervision of the principal or supervising
broker, with the firm's licensed name clearly and legibly displayed?** (18VAC135-20-190 A)

**Does it carry a clear, legible and conspicuous advertising disclosure with the firm's name and the
office contact information as the firm's written policy specifies?** (B 1)

**Is it done under the policies of a principal or supervising broker?** (B 2)

**Is the property information consistent with the property's condition and current contract
status?** (B 3)

**Has it been updated in a timely manner after any material change to listing status or property
description?** (B 4)

**Where a third party controls it, has a timely written request for changes been made?** (B 3 and
B 4)

**Where the licensee is a limited service agent, does it comply with the § 54.1-2138.1 disclosure?**
(A)

## 28.3 Digital, social, and third-party platforms

Everything above applies to a social post, a video, a syndicated listing feed, and a paid
advertisement, because the rules speak of "all advertising" without qualification.

Three digital-specific problems:

**Third-party control.** Syndication sends listing data to platforms the firm does not control.
18VAC135-20-190 B 3 and B 4 anticipate exactly this and prescribe the remedy: **a timely written
request for changes.** Keep the request; it is the evidence that the duty was met.

**Audience targeting.** Choosing who is shown a housing advertisement can itself make a statement of
preference. Restricting a housing ad's audience by age, by family composition, by ZIP code chosen
for its demography, or by proxies for a protected class implicates § 36-96.3 A 3 as surely as
printed words would. Platforms have built housing-specific advertising categories for this reason;
using them is not optional politeness.

**Persistence.** A post from last year advertising a property at a price that no longer applies is
still an advertisement, and B 4 requires timely updating.

## 28.4 Teams, personal branding, and the firm's name

A **real estate team** must be supervised by a supervising broker (§ 54.1-2110.1 A), and the
supervising broker must have written policies covering the advertising and marketing of the firm and
any affiliated teams or business entities (B 2 c) and must ensure teams operate per the chapter and
regulations (B 5). Combined with 18VAC135-20-190 A's requirement that all advertising be **in the
name of the firm** with the firm's licensed name clearly and legibly displayed, the rule for team
branding is: the team name may appear, but it may not displace or obscure the firm.

## 28.5 Truthfulness, and the specific trap of bait and switch

**18VAC135-20-300 1** makes it misrepresentation to advertise or offer property for sale or rent
**with the intent not to sell or rent at the price or terms advertised** — unless the advertisement
clearly states that the property is limited in specific quantity or to a specified time period **and
the licensee in fact had at least that quantity at those terms at the time of advertising**,
including as to listing status and condition.

Two everyday versions: advertising a property already under contract in order to generate buyer
leads, and advertising a rent that is available only under conditions the advertisement does not
state.

## 28.6 Data, images, and consent

Photographs, floor plans, drone footage, and video are **copyrighted works**, usually owned by the
photographer unless the agreement says otherwise. A licensee who reuses another firm's listing
photographs after a listing changes hands is infringing, not economising. Written licences,
retained, are the answer.

Client data collected through a website is subject to privacy obligations and to the firm's own
policy; and lead information, once collected, is a record. Hour 50 covers data protection and wire
fraud, which is where a marketing website becomes a security question.

### Hour 28 engagement check

**Question.** Audit this social post and list every problem with its source: *"JUST LISTED by the
Carter Team! Charming 3BR in a quiet, established neighborhood — perfect for empty nesters. No
Section 8. Call Dana at 555-0100."* The property went under contract four days ago.

**Expected answer.** **"Perfect for empty nesters"** expresses a preference on the basis of
**elderliness and familial status** — § 36-96.3 A 3. **"No Section 8"** is discrimination on the
basis of **source of funds**, a Virginia protected class — § 36-96.3 A 1, 2 and 3. **"JUST LISTED"**
on a property under contract for four days is property information inconsistent with the property's
current contract status — **18VAC135-20-190 B 3** — and a failure to update after a material change
to listing status — **B 4** — and, if the intent is to generate leads rather than sell at those
terms, bait and switch under **18VAC135-20-300 1**. The post carries **the team name and an
individual's phone number but not the firm's licensed name**, contrary to **18VAC135-20-190 A**, and
carries no **advertising disclosure with the firm's name and office contact information** as
required by **B 1** and specified in the firm's written policy under § 54.1-2110.1 B 2 c.
