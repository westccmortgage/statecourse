## Hour 46 — Investment and Tax Calculations

**Required subject mapping:** T12, T13 — Investment; taxes

### Learning objectives

- Calculate cash-on-cash return and basic ROI measures.
- Explain equity buildup, basis, gain, and depreciation as concepts.
- State the limits of licensee tax and investment advice.

### Instructional content

Cash-on-cash return equals annual before-tax cash flow divided by initial cash invested, using consistently defined inputs. Return on investment may use gain plus income relative to cost or invested equity; because definitions vary, the analyst must show the formula. Equity can change through principal reduction, capital investment, and market value movement.

Basis generally begins with acquisition cost and is adjusted by qualified capital improvements, depreciation, and other tax rules. Gain is not simply sale price minus original down payment. Depreciation for tax purposes is a noncash deduction governed by current tax law and differs from appraisal depreciation.

Students distinguish property-level analysis, financing analysis, and after-tax analysis. A licensee can calculate a transparent educational metric from client-provided data but does not represent a property as tax-free, guarantee deductions, or recommend an entity/exchange without qualified legal and tax advice.

### 50-minute instructional sequence

- **0–5:** Retrieval prompt, objectives, and prior-topic connection.
- **5–20:** Direct instruction/review segment A with worked evidence.
- **20–35:** Direct instruction/review segment B with misconceptions and source control.
- **35–45:** Applied exercise — Students calculate cash-on-cash return under two financing structures and reconcile why the higher percentage also carries higher risk.
- **45–50:** Scored engagement check, immediate feedback, and completion gate.

### Scored engagement check

**Question:** A property produces $18,000 annual before-tax cash flow on $225,000 initial cash invested. What is cash-on-cash return?

**Expected answer and rationale:** 8%: $18,000 ÷ $225,000.

## Hour 47 — Feasibility, Market Analysis, and Investment Decision

**Required subject mapping:** T02, T12 — Market analysis; investment

### Learning objectives

- Build a market and feasibility evidence chain.
- Create base, downside, and upside scenarios.
- Identify assumptions that most influence the decision.

### Instructional content

Feasibility asks whether a proposed use is supportable physically, legally, financially, and by the market. The study defines the product, market area, target users, competitive supply, pipeline, rents/prices, vacancy, absorption, operating costs, development or renovation cost, financing, timing, and exit.

Evidence should be dated, sourced, and comparable. Asking rent is not achieved rent; announced supply is not completed supply; average vacancy can hide segment differences. Assumptions are separated from observed facts. Sensitivity analysis changes one or more key variables to show the range of outcomes.

A go/no-go decision includes thresholds and stop conditions rather than a persuasive narrative. Licensees stay within competence and recommend appraisal, engineering, environmental, zoning, construction, finance, legal, and tax specialists as required.

### 50-minute instructional sequence

- **0–5:** Retrieval prompt, objectives, and prior-topic connection.
- **5–20:** Direct instruction/review segment A with worked evidence.
- **20–35:** Direct instruction/review segment B with misconceptions and source control.
- **35–45:** Applied exercise — Students run base/downside/upside changes in rent, vacancy, expenses, and exit cap rate, then identify the two most sensitive variables.
- **45–50:** Scored engagement check, immediate feedback, and completion gate.

### Scored engagement check

**Question:** Why is a sensitivity analysis more useful than one forecast?

**Expected answer and rationale:** It shows how results change when uncertain assumptions change and exposes which assumptions drive risk.

## Hour 48 — Residential Construction, Property Condition, and Development Risk

**Required subject mapping:** T10, T20, T21 — Home ownership; appraisal; development

### Learning objectives

- Identify major site and building systems.
- Recognize physical, functional, and external depreciation.
- Use inspect/investigate/refer boundaries.

### Instructional content

Residential improvements include site work, foundation, structure, exterior envelope, roof, drainage, plumbing, electrical, HVAC, insulation, interior finishes, life-safety components, and utility/septic/well systems. New construction adds plans, specifications, permits, inspections, change orders, draws, lien waivers, warranties, and completion/occupancy evidence.

Physical deterioration is wear or damage; functional obsolescence is loss from design or utility; external obsolescence arises outside the property. A defect may affect safety, insurability, lending, use, marketability, cost, or value. Cosmetic appearance does not prove system condition.

Licensees observe and disclose as required but do not diagnose engineering, environmental, code, mold, roof, structural, or system conditions beyond competence. The operational rule is: document the observed fact, avoid causal conclusions, recommend appropriate inspection, track the contract right/deadline, and preserve reports.

## Hour 49 — Property, Flood, and Transaction Insurance

**Required subject mapping:** T11, T23 — Title/insurance; housing legislation

### Learning objectives

- Identify core property-insurance coverage concepts and exclusions.
- Explain flood-risk verification and NFIP concepts.
- Coordinate property and title insurance without conflating them.

### Instructional content

Property insurance may address dwelling/building, other structures, personal property, loss of use/business income, and liability subject to limits, deductibles, exclusions, valuation basis, conditions, endorsements, and named perils or open-peril structure. Replacement cost and actual cash value are not interchangeable.

Flood is commonly excluded from standard property policies. A flood-zone determination for lending does not describe every physical risk or guarantee coverage cost. The National Flood Insurance Program and private flood markets have changing maps, rules, limits, and premiums. Buyers should obtain property-specific quotes early and investigate prior loss and elevation information where relevant.

Title insurance covers specified title risks, not physical casualty. Home warranty/service contracts are also different. Insurance availability can be a financing and contingency issue; the licensee tracks evidence but the licensed insurance professional explains coverage.

### Scored engagement check

## Hour 50 — Risk Management, Data Protection, and Regulatory Compliance

**Required subject mapping:** T22, T25 — Regulatory statutes; Board regulations

### Learning objectives

- Build a transaction risk-control system.
- Apply wire-fraud, identity, privacy, and cybersecurity safeguards.
- Use supervision, documentation, and complaint escalation.

### Instructional content

Risk management starts before a problem: clear scope, competent service, written agreements, verified facts, approved forms, realistic timelines, documented delivery, broker review, secure records, and timely specialist referral. Errors and omissions insurance transfers some risk but does not legalize misconduct or replace controls.

Wire fraud prevention uses independently verified phone numbers, dual confirmation, no trust in last-minute email changes, client warnings, role-based access, multifactor authentication, strong unique credentials, patched devices, restricted downloads, and incident response. Sensitive data is collected only when needed and transmitted through approved secure channels.

Complaints and suspected violations are preserved, acknowledged, escalated, investigated under policy, and corrected without retaliation or record destruction. A legal-update register connects each Virginia statutory/regulatory source to affected lessons, forms, ads, workflows, and release date.

## Hour 52 — Integrated Buyer Transaction: Financing Through Closing

**Required subject mapping:** T06, T07, T14, T19 — Security instruments; mortgage types; financing; closing

### Learning objectives

- Sequence buyer representation through possession.
- Coordinate financing, inspection, appraisal, title, insurance, and settlement deadlines.
- Resolve conflicts without unauthorized contract or lending advice.

### Instructional content

The buyer case starts with brokerage agreement, lawful criteria, funds/financing status, property type, occupancy, timing, and risk tolerance. Property search avoids steering. Before offer, the buyer considers disclosures, common-interest documents, title/land-use questions, condition, insurance, financing program, and cash/reserve needs.

After ratification, the licensee opens a deadline map for deposit, loan application, lender documents, inspection, appraisal, title, association/resale package, insurance, settlement, and possession. Each event has owner, evidence, status, and escalation. A lender’s condition is not the same as a contract contingency right.

Closing preparation reconciles final terms, credits, repairs, walk-through, loan/settlement figures, vesting, wire verification, signing, funding, recording, keys, and post-closing records. The licensee never tells the buyer to ignore a lender discrepancy or sign a document known to be inaccurate.

## Hour 54 — Fair Housing and Marketing Case Lab

**Required subject mapping:** T16, T24, T25 — Marketing; fair housing; Board regulations

### Learning objectives

- Detect discriminatory language, targeting, steering, and inconsistent service.
- Process source-of-funds and disability requests lawfully.
- Design corrective action and records.

### Instructional content

The case includes an owner instruction against vouchers, an ad targeting a preferred age/lifestyle, an applicant requesting an assistance animal, and a licensee who gives different neighborhood information based on perceived national origin. Students compare each act with current Virginia protected classes and prohibited practices.

Source of funds is a Virginia protected class. Qualification criteria must be lawful, relevant, and consistently applied; income calculations and documentation cannot be designed to exclude lawful assistance. Disability requests use the accommodation process and current assistance-animal guidance rather than pet rules or purchased certificates.

Corrective action stops the conduct, preserves evidence, alerts the broker, fixes content/process, responds to affected persons, trains staff, and audits lead/application outcomes. The file records objective criteria and response times without collecting unnecessary sensitive data.

## Hour 56 — Real Estate Mathematics Mastery

### Learning objectives

- Solve mixed area, commission, tax, proration, loan, and valuation problems.
- Choose the correct formula and units.

### Instructional content

Math performance depends on translating facts into a labeled equation. Students identify the unknown, list known values with units, select the formula, convert percentages and rate formats, calculate, test reasonableness, and round only at the stated step.

The mixed set includes square footage/acreage, commission allocation, assessed tax, daily proration, LTV, down payment, points, simple interest, cash to close, PGI/EGI/NOI, cap rate, GRM, and comparable adjustments. Each problem states assumptions; actual transactions use official figures and documents.

Error categories are wrong formula, wrong base, percentage conversion, time conversion, debit/credit direction, unit mismatch, premature rounding, or reading error. Remediation assigns a new item from the same error family until the learner explains and corrects the method.

## Hour 57 — National Principles Cumulative Review

**Required subject mapping:** T03, T04, T05, T06, T09, T11, T18, T19 — Integrated national principles

### Learning objectives

- Retrieve and connect property, contract, title, lien, finance, and closing concepts.

### Instructional content

The review uses a property-to-closing chain: classify the property and rights; identify ownership and encumbrances; form the contract; investigate title, survey, and condition; obtain financing; satisfy contingencies; reconcile closing; deliver/record transfer; and preserve records.

High-risk distinctions include real estate versus real property; fixture versus personal property; easement versus license; deed versus title; note versus security instrument; lender versus owner title policy; price versus value; assignment versus sublease; client versus customer; and signing versus closing.

Retrieval practice is closed-note first, followed by source-supported correction. Students explain why a distractor fails and then change one fact to see whether the result changes. The goal is durable reasoning, not recognition of memorized wording.

## Hour 58 — Virginia Law and Regulations Cumulative Review

**Required subject mapping:** T15, T22, T24, T25 — Agency; statutes; fair housing; Board regulations

### Learning objectives

- Navigate controlling Virginia sources quickly.
- Apply brokerage, supervision, escrow/records/advertising, and fair-housing rules.
- Separate verified mandates from provider or firm policy.

### Instructional content

The Virginia review is source-based. Students navigate Title 54.1, Chapter 21; 18VAC135-20; the Virginia Fair Housing Office/Law; the Residential Property Disclosure Act; landlord-tenant law; and common-interest statutes. They locate the current rule before applying it.

Core scenarios cover written brokerage agreements, termination, client duties, relationship disclosure, dual/designated/limited service, ownership interest, compensation versus relationship, material facts, supervision, deposits, records, advertising, protected classes, accommodations, and complaint response.

Students label each conclusion as statute, regulation, official guidance, contract, broker policy, or StateCourse academic policy. This prevents a private policy from being taught as Virginia law and supports future legal updates.

## Hour 60 — Final Instructional Review and Readiness Verification

**Required subject mapping:** T01–T25 — All required subjects

### Learning objectives

- Build a complete concept and source map.
- Demonstrate readiness on legal distinctions and calculations.
- Understand final-exam integrity, monitoring, and remediation procedures.

### Instructional content

The final instructional hour is not the monitored final exam. It completes the 3,000 instructional minutes through retrieval, explanation, and targeted practice. Students build a one-page map linking all 25 required topics to the transaction lifecycle and official Virginia sources.

Readiness verification samples definitions, applications, calculations, and Virginia source navigation. Critical errors include discrimination, mishandling funds, false statements, missing relationship consent, unauthorized legal advice, wire fraud response, and skipped title/contract deadlines. The instructor explains the separate monitored written final: identity check, permitted materials, environment rules, time, item count, passing policy, incident process, result, remediation, alternate-form retake, affidavit, records, and completion certificate.
