## 1. Why Georgia is next

Georgia combines a large addressable market with an unusually short personal broker route.

- 2025 population estimate: **11,302,748**.
- GREC FY2025: **118,387 total license records** and **3,452 new licenses**.

The 3,452 figure is not 3,452 course buyers. It includes resident and nonresident records and more than one license category. It supports market scale, not a revenue forecast. No official current figure was found for unique 75-hour students, exam attempts, applications, or statewide course purchases.

## 2. Owner broker route

GREC's reciprocity guidance says a person licensed in another state or jurisdiction, other than a Florida resident, may seek the comparable Georgia license when the source license is current, in good standing, and was obtained by examination. The current reciprocal application charges **$170** and issues the license for **four years**.

The first filing position is **inactive Georgia Broker**. This keeps the personal license application independent from a later choice among:

1. opening/registering a Georgia firm;
2. becoming qualifying broker of an existing Georgia firm; or
3. affiliating as an associate broker with a Georgia-licensed firm.

Rule 520-1-.04(3)(g) is broader than a California-only history: the filing must contain an original certification issued within 12 months from **each real-estate jurisdiction that has licensed the applicant**. Each certification must establish the comparable examination, satisfaction of that jurisdiction's prelicense and continuing-education requirements, current good standing, and no disciplinary action. GREC may accept certified electronic data in its discretion.

The package also needs a law-enforcement criminal-history report from the state of legal residence that is no more than 60 days old, notarized lawful-presence verification, an acceptable identity document, disclosures/supporting records where applicable, and the fee. If the residence-state report identifies a record in another jurisdiction, or the applicant cannot obtain the required equivalent report, the rule requires the fingerprints/fees/authorization needed for GREC to obtain an FBI report.

### Residence fork

The exception depends on **legal residence**, not on holding a Florida MLO license or eventually holding a Florida real-estate license. A Florida resident cannot use the ordinary reciprocal application and must pass the Georgia Law and Practice supplement examination through PSI.

The GREC form says that examination costs $121; a newer 2026 PSI bulletin located in Phase 0 says $175. The package deliberately stores no current fee until the live amount is confirmed immediately before payment.

### Filing-order inference

Submitting Georgia while California is the only real-estate licensing jurisdiction in the record would reduce the number of required certifications. This is timing logic, not a waiver: every jurisdiction that has licensed the applicant must still be disclosed and certified.

### If the owner later activates or opens a firm

Inactive status cannot be used to conduct Georgia brokerage. The later routes are materially different:

1. affiliate as an associate broker under an active Georgia broker, who must complete and sign Section C of the reciprocal application and certify a written affiliation agreement;
2. become qualifying broker of an already licensed Georgia firm through the Change of Qualifying Broker filing, while holding the form's required entity-specific role—LLC member/manager, corporate officer, partner, or sole owner—and authority to bind the firm in a contested case; or
3. open a Georgia firm and serve as its qualifying broker.

For a foreign LLC, the firm route includes Georgia Secretary of State authority to transact business, a Georgia registered agent/office, the GREC Open Firm package, and the qualifying-broker role. The Secretary of State currently publishes **$235** for foreign-entity registration. GREC's 01/2021 Open Firm form says **$275**, while current Rule 520-1-.04 arithmetic is **$200** ($75 activation plus $125 renewal). These are unresolved conflicts; no firm fee should be paid until GREC confirms the current form and amount.

## 3. Instructor route

A Georgia broker license does **not** automatically make the owner an approved prelicense instructor. GREC separately approves instructors.

Within the year before the instructor application, the published route requires completion of:

- a Commission-approved 75-hour Salesperson Prelicense Course; and
- a Commission-approved Instructor Development Workshop.

The application also calls for a background report and a fully supported record of education, brokerage transactions/experience, teaching, and course development. The separate June 2024 worksheet identifies **65 points** as the favorable-review threshold. Its section maxima are 20 for real-estate licensure, 40 for teaching, 5 for formal general education, and 35 for real-estate experience. Transactions earn 0.3 point each, capped at 30 (100 transactions), and auxiliary-field work earns 1 point per year, capped at 5.

The owner's provisional **53–55** is reproducible only from unverified assumptions: 10 points for ten active-license years, 5 for holding a broker license, 30 for 100 documented brokerage transactions, 5 for five auxiliary-field years, and 3–5 for a qualifying bachelor's/graduate degree. That leaves an estimated **10–12-point evidence gap**. The required instructor workshop and salesperson course are prerequisites, not point categories in the current workbook. None of the inputs is treated as verified until documentary evidence is matched to the live worksheet, and the total is not a GREC score or approval.

Rule 520-2-.04(12)(d) provides an avenue for a corresponding prelicense course approved by another jurisdiction when its hours are similar and its delivery is consistent with Georgia standards. The decisive issue is whether GREC will credit the owner's particular prior course toward the one-year prerequisite and precisely which Instructor Development Workshop qualifies. Obtain that answer before buying a course.

The rule also permits GREC to approve a nonresident whom another jurisdiction's real-estate regulator approved as an instructor and who proves at least five years actively teaching prelicense courses as an approved instructor. The repository does not hold evidence that this alternative applies.

For later renewal planning, the current rule carries **60 hours of instruction plus 36 hours of instructor education**, with at least **12 of the 36 hours from GREC's annual meetings for approved schools and instructors**. The balance may come from qualifying courses the instructor teaches or courses GREC approves for instructor education. An older renewal form says 24 hours; the current rule is the planning control.

## 4. School and GNPEC are separate gates

GREC school approval requires a complete institutional package. In addition to the school/owner/director/coordinator, records, policies, curriculum, instructor, vendor, and reporting materials, the located form requires a school-director law-enforcement criminal-history report issued no more than 60 days before filing, disclosure support where applicable, notarized lawful-presence verification, and a secure/verifiable identity document. The package must address required media/equipment availability and the learning environment, explicitly stating how requirements apply to an online-only school rather than silently omitting them. Once complete, Rule 520-2-.02 gives GREC 60 days to approve, disapprove, or request more information. A failure to answer a request within 120 days can abandon the application.

The school form located in Phase 0 says $175 but is revision 10/2015 and contains stale references. Current Rule 520-1-.04 arithmetic is $200 ($75 activation plus $125 renewal). The initial instructor form has the same $175-versus-$200 conflict. The school renewal form says $125 paper/$100 online, while the current rule's online-discount sentence does not expressly name schools. Form, amount, and filing channel must be confirmed before payment.

There is also a parallel Georgia Nonpublic Postsecondary Education Commission question. O.C.G.A. § 20-3-250.3(a)(4) covers institutions regulated or licensed by another Georgia agency as determined by GNPEC, and GNPEC publicly identifies real-estate schools regulated through the Secretary of State as exempt. But the determination is not self-executing for this out-of-state provider. StateCourse will request a written **Other State Agency Oversight** determination. GNPEC's published process mentions an institutional proposal/EDvera workflow and lists $200 for the category; applicability must be confirmed.

Every approved school must also offer a qualifying continuing-education course every calendar year in addition to prelicense/postlicense offerings, report specified changes, and preserve the required five-year records. No advertising, offering, enrollment, or payment from Georgia residents begins until both regulators' required determinations are documented.

## 5. Course architecture that must be cleared before authoring

The planned course is 75 distance-credit hours, each defined as 60 minutes. The controlling rules require GREC authorization of the self-paced methodology before course development, so Phase 0 does not author curriculum.

Before instructional design begins, the fixed salesperson curriculum must account for all 15 required areas: contracts; instruments/conveyances; RESPA closing procedures; agency/disclosure; pricing; financing; residential mortgage fraud; community-association and property-management activities; environmental law; taxation; city/urban development; fair housing; antitrust; safety; and Georgia license law. Ethics must be integrated throughout. Every unit must include out-of-class reading and extensive graded written work with the prescribed personal-completion certification, and the design must demonstrate applied proficiency in contracts, pricing, and financing.

The planned method must also demonstrate:

- genuine interactivity rather than primarily screen text, audio, or video;
- measurable objectives, regular diagnostics, mastery, individualized remediation, and locked progression;
- credible time validation, personal module completion, certification statements, identity oversight, and instructor availability;
- graded work/examinations consuming no more than 10% of required course time;
- a nine-person pilot with at least three unlicensed participants, at least three licensed salespersons or community association managers, at least six non-educators, and documented variation in computer and real-estate skill levels; and
- five-year retention of versions, module completion, dates, grades, examinations, answer keys, and final scores.

Only three format elements form the **safe harbor from submitting the final examination**: multiple-choice format, at least four choices per question, and at least 100 questions. That is not a mandatory format; failure of any of those three conditions requires submission and GREC approval before use. Independently of that safe harbor, the salesperson final must include at least five questions in each of 13 fixed buckets—brokerage engagements; legal descriptions/contracts; payment and earnest money; special stipulations/sales contracts; leases/fair housing; licensees as principals; antitrust; basic finance; loan types; pricing; seller costs; qualifying purchasers/purchaser costs; and closing—plus any additional matters GREC later requires or authorizes. It also needs two secure forms and proctoring, and remains subject to separate scheduling, passing-score, security, and retake requirements. Current official text reviewed in Phase 0 does not settle the numeric passing score, an acceptable remote-proctor model, or how the separate-exam-day rule operates in a self-paced course. These remain written-confirmation gates.
