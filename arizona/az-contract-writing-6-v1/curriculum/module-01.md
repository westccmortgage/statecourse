# Arizona Real Estate Contract Law and Contract Writing — Module CW-01

## Contract law, and drafting a contract to purchase real property

**Course:** Real Estate Contract Law and Contract Writing · **Instructional hours: 3.0** of 6.0

>
> **2. It is proved at application.** The certification goes to the Department **at the time of
> application**, not at examination.
>
> **3. It is not a lecture.** The statute requires **participation by the applicant in drafting**, and names
> **three document types**: **contracts to purchase real property**, **listing agreements**, and **lease
> agreements**. **A course that reads contract law aloud for six hours has not satisfied § 32-2124(L).**
> Every hour of this course therefore ends in the student drafting, and the drafting is the assessed work.

## Learning objectives

1. State the elements a contract requires and apply them to a real estate transaction.
2. Distinguish an offer from an invitation to treat, and a counter-offer from an acceptance.
3. State what the statute of frauds requires of a contract for the sale of an interest in land.
4. Distinguish contingencies, conditions and covenants, and draft each correctly.
5. Draft a contract to purchase real property containing the terms Arizona rule requires on its face.
6. State the Arizona rules that govern what a purchase contract must say and when it must be delivered.

## A. The elements, applied to real estate

Contract law is general content, and it is delivered here as the working foundation for drafting rather
than as theory.

| Element | What it means in a purchase contract |
|---|---|
| **Offer** | A definite proposal, communicated, showing intent to be bound on stated terms |
| **Acceptance** | Unqualified assent to those exact terms, communicated to the offeror |
| **Consideration** | Something of legal value each way — the price and the promise to convey |
| **Capacity** | Legal ability of each party to contract |
| **Legality of object** | A lawful purpose |
| **Mutual assent** | A genuine meeting of the minds on the same terms |
| **Writing** | Required for an interest in land — see Part C |

### A.1 Offer, and what is not an offer

A **listing** is not an offer to sell; it is an employment agreement between owner and broker. An
**advertisement** is ordinarily an invitation to make an offer. **The buyer's written purchase contract is
usually the first offer in the transaction**, and the salesperson who prepares it is preparing the offer
itself — which is why drafting error at this stage is expensive.

### A.2 Acceptance must be unqualified

Any change to a term is a **counter-offer**. A counter-offer **rejects** the original offer, and the
original cannot then be accepted unless it is renewed. Students draft counter-offers wrongly more often than
they draft offers wrongly, because a counter-offer written as if it were an acceptance leaves the parties
disagreeing about which document controls.

**Communication matters.** An acceptance signed and left in a drawer has not been communicated. Drafting
practice: state in the document **how and by when** acceptance must be communicated.

### A.3 Capacity and legality

Minors, persons adjudicated incompetent, and entities acting outside their authority raise capacity
questions. **Who signs for an entity, a trust or an estate is a legal question**, and this course states no
Arizona rule about it — **no Arizona statute on capacity, entity authority, or trust or probate signature
authority was read by this project.** The drafting instruction is: **identify the parties exactly as they
hold title, and route any doubt to the designated broker and to counsel.**

## B. Contingencies, conditions and covenants

Students conflate these three, and the conflation produces contracts that do not do what the parties
intended.

| Term | What it is | Effect if not satisfied |
|---|---|---|
| **Condition precedent** | An event that must occur **before** a duty to perform arises | The duty never arises |
| **Contingency** | A stated event on which a party's obligation depends, usually with a deadline and a stated remedy | The contract operates as the clause provides — commonly cancellation and return of deposit |
| **Covenant** | A **promise** to do or not do something | Breach — the contract stands, and the remedy is for breach |

**The drafting rule that follows:** a contingency clause is not finished until it states **three** things —
**what event**, **by when**, and **what happens if it does not occur.** A clause naming an event without a
deadline, or a deadline without a consequence, is the single most common defect in student drafting, and it
is checked directly in the Part D exercise.

**Time.** Whether time is of the essence changes the effect of a missed date. **This course does not state
an Arizona rule on it** — none was read — and the drafting instruction is to **state the parties' intention
expressly in the document rather than leave it to be inferred.**

## C. Writing, and the statute of frauds

A contract for the sale of an interest in land must be **in writing** and **signed by the party to be
charged**, identifying the **parties**, the **property**, and the **price or a means of determining it.**

**Arizona's own statute of frauds was NOT read by this project**, and **no Arizona section number, wording,
or exception is stated in this course.** What is stated here is the general common-law requirement, taught
because it drives the drafting standard: **write it down, completely, and have it signed.**

### C.1 What "identify the property" means when drafting

A street address is an identifier, not a legal description. Module AZ-04 of the 90-hour course delivered the
general content on legal descriptions. **The drafting instruction is to use the description that identifies
the property unambiguously, and never to invent or abbreviate one.**

### C.2 Electronic signatures and delivery

Contracts in practice are signed and delivered electronically. **No Arizona electronic transactions statute
was read by this project** and **none is stated here.** What *is* stated, because it was read, is
**A.A.C. R4-28-802(A)**: *"Upon execution of any transaction document a licensee shall **expeditiously
deliver a legible copy of the signed document and final agreement to each party signing the document.**"*
**"Legible"** and **"each party signing"** are drafting-and-delivery requirements, not clerical ones.

## D. Drafting exercise — a contract to purchase real property

**This is the statutory core of the module.** § 32-2124(L) requires **participation by the applicant in
drafting contracts to purchase real property**, and the student drafts one here.

### D.1 What the draft must contain

The student drafts a complete purchase contract for a stated fact pattern. The draft is assessed against a
checklist in two parts: **general drafting requirements**, and **the Arizona rules read by this project.**

**General drafting requirements:**

1. **Parties**, identified exactly as they hold or will hold title.
2. **Property**, unambiguously identified.
3. **Price**, and how it is to be paid.
4. **Earnest money** — amount, and what happens to it in each outcome.
5. **Closing date**, and what occurs on it.
6. **Possession** — when, and by whom.
7. **Each contingency**, stating **event, deadline, and consequence.**
8. **Title and conveyance** — what is to be delivered.
9. **Prorations** — what is prorated and as of when.
10. **Signature and acceptance provisions**, including **how and by when acceptance is communicated.**

**Arizona rules the draft must satisfy — every one transcribed from a rule this project read:**

| Requirement | Rule |
|---|---|
| The contract **indicates where the earnest money or down payment will be deposited**, and **names** the **title company**, the **broker's trust account**, or **other depository** | **A.A.C. R4-28-803(C)** |
| Where money is paid **directly to the seller and not into neutral escrow**: **conspicuous disclosure**, the **purchaser signs or initials the provision**, and the **bold or large-print warning** appears | **A.A.C. R4-28-803(D)** |
| Where a **public report** is required: the legend **in bold or larger print above the signature portion** | **A.A.C. R4-28-803** |
| Where the transaction is an **unimproved subdivided lot or unsubdivided land**: the rescission legend — **midnight of the seventh calendar day**, and the **six-month** inspection right | **A.A.C. R4-28-804(A)** |
| Where the transaction is a **time-share interval**: **midnight of the tenth calendar day** | **A.A.C. R4-28-804(B)** |
| The **seller's complete and current name, address, telephone number and email address**, **conspicuously, on the face** of the agreement, where a rescission right applies | **A.A.C. R4-28-804(C)** |
| Any **conflict** under R4-28-1101(E) disclosed **in writing before the parties enter any binding agreement** | **A.A.C. R4-28-1101(E)** |

### D.2 What the exercise assesses

The student's draft is reviewed against that checklist. **A draft missing any of the ten general items, or
any Arizona item applicable to the stated fact pattern, is returned for correction.** Completion of this
course requires a completed draft, not an attempted one — the statute requires **participation in
drafting**, and a partial draft is not participation in it.

### D.3 The limits of the exercise, stated to the student

**A licensee is not a lawyer.** Module AZ-11 of the 90-hour course covers **A.A.C. R4-28-1101(K)**, which
requires a licensee to **recommend that the client seek appropriate counsel from insurance, legal, tax and
accounting professionals** regarding pre-possession and post-possession risk. **This course teaches drafting
within a licensee's role**, and states **no rule about the boundary between real estate practice and the
unauthorised practice of law in Arizona** — **no Arizona statute, rule or authority on that boundary was
read by this project**, and inventing one would be worse than silence. **That boundary is a question for the
designated broker and for counsel.**

**No form is prescribed here.** **No Department-approved or industry contract form was read by this
project**, and **this course reproduces none.** The student drafts; the brokerage supplies the forms it
requires under **A.A.C. R4-28-1103(A)**, which obliges the designated broker to maintain **written policies
on the use of disclosure forms and contracts.**
