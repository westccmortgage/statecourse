## A. Contract review, applied to employment and to tenancy

Both documents in this module are contracts, so Module CW-01's Parts A through C apply in full: offer,
acceptance, consideration, capacity, legality, mutual assent, and writing. What changes is **what the
contract does.**

| Document | What it is | Parties |
|---|---|---|
| **Purchase contract** (CW-01) | A contract for the **sale of an interest in land** | Buyer and seller |
| **Listing agreement** (this module) | A contract of **employment** — the owner employs the broker | Owner and **broker** |
| **Lease agreement** (this module) | A contract **creating an estate** — a leasehold — and containing covenants | Landlord and tenant |

**The listing is the one students misclassify.** It is **not** an offer to sell and it does **not** convey
anything. It employs a broker. Module AZ-05 of the 90-hour course developed the agency content this rests
on, and **Module AZ-11 delivered A.A.C. R4-28-1101 — the fiduciary duty the listing brings into being.**

**Who the broker party is.** The employment runs to the **broker**, not to the salesperson personally. A
salesperson drafting a listing writes the **employing broker** in as the party. This is the same distinction
**A.A.C. R4-28-502(E)** enforces in advertising, requiring the **employing broker's legal name or a dba on
the licence certificate**, and the same one **R4-28-1103(D)** rests on in making the employing broker
**responsible for the acts of all licensees.**

## B. Drafting exercise — a listing agreement

### B.1 What the draft must settle

The student drafts a complete listing agreement for a stated fact pattern. The draft is assessed against:

1. **Parties** — the **owner** as they hold title, and the **employing broker**.
2. **Property**, unambiguously identified.
3. **Price and terms** the owner will accept.
4. **The broker's authority** — what the broker is employed to do.
5. **Term** — a **definite beginning and a definite end.**
6. **Compensation** — amount or rate, and **what earns it.**
7. **Cooperation and compensation with other brokers**, if any.
8. **Owner's undertakings** — what the owner will do or refrain from doing.
9. **Any limitation the owner places on the broker's authority**, expressly stated.
10. **Signatures**, with the broker party correctly named.

**Item 5 is checked strictly.** A listing without a stated end date has no stated end date, and no rule read
by this project supplies one. **This course states no maximum listing term for Arizona and no rule that a
listing terminates automatically** — **no Arizona statute or rule on listing duration was read by this
project**, so **none is stated.** The drafting instruction is therefore: **state the term expressly.**

### B.2 The Arizona rules the drafter must know are operating

A listing agreement in Arizona operates against rules the drafter does not get to vary. **Each of these was
read by this project and is transcribed in `arizona/regulatory/primary-text-readings.md`.**

| Rule | What it does to a listing |
|---|---|
| **R4-28-1101(A)** | The listing creates a **fiduciary duty** to the client, and a duty to **deal fairly with all other parties** |
| **R4-28-802(B)** | The licensee must **promptly submit all offers** during the term — and **is not released from that duty by the client's acceptance of an offer**, unless the client instructs **in writing** to cease, **or as otherwise provided in the listing agreement** |
| **R4-28-802(B)**, continued | Disclosure of the **existence and terms of additional offers** requires the **seller's or lessor's permission** |
| **R4-28-802(B)**, continued | A licensee **may voluntarily submit offers regardless of limitations in the listing agreement, and after the listing agreement is terminated** |
| **R4-28-1101(E)** | Conflicts — including that the **licensee is acting as a principal** or that the owner is in the **licensee's or designated broker's immediate family** — must be disclosed **in writing before any binding agreement** |
| **R4-28-1101(F)** | Compensation from, or representation of, **more than one party** requires the **prior written consent of all parties** |
| **R4-28-701** | The **compensation-sharing disclosure** — in writing to **all parties**, at least **three calendar days before closing**, naming each employing broker who represents a party and will receive compensation |
| **R4-28-502(B)** | If the licensee owns the property, the advertisement must disclose the licence and carry **"owner/agent"** |

**Two drafting consequences follow directly, and both are assessed.**

**First**, R4-28-802(B) names the listing agreement itself as a place where the continuing offer-submission
duty may be *"otherwise provided."* A drafter who intends the duty to end on acceptance must **say so in the
listing**; a drafter who says nothing leaves the duty running **until closing.** Either is permissible; what
is not permissible is not knowing which the document does.

**Second**, the same subsection permits a licensee to submit offers **despite limitations in the listing
agreement** and **after termination.** A limitation drafted into the listing therefore does not do what an
inexperienced drafter may expect it to do.

## C. Drafting exercise — a lease agreement

### C.1 What a lease creates

A lease conveys a **leasehold estate** — possession for a term — and simultaneously contains **covenants**
between the parties. Module AZ-01 of the 90-hour course delivered the general content on leasehold estates.
The drafting consequence is that a lease must settle both halves: **the estate** (who has possession, of
what, for how long) and **the promises** (who does what during it).

### C.2 What the draft must settle

1. **Parties** — landlord and tenant, identified exactly.
2. **Premises** — what is let, including anything included or excluded.
3. **Term** — beginning and end, and what happens at the end.
4. **Rent** — amount, when due, where and how paid.
5. **Deposits** — amount, purpose, and the conditions of return.
6. **Use** — what the premises may be used for.
7. **Maintenance and repair** — allocated between the parties, item by item.
8. **Utilities and services** — who pays and who arranges.
9. **Assignment and subletting** — permitted, prohibited, or conditional.
10. **Default and remedies** — what constitutes default and what follows.
11. **Access by the landlord** — when, and on what notice.
12. **Signatures.**

### C.3 A boundary this module states plainly

**Arizona's landlord-tenant statutes were NOT read by this project.** Neither residential nor commercial
landlord-tenant law appears in the readings file, and **this course therefore states no Arizona rule on
security deposit limits, deposit return periods, notice periods, entry notice, habitability, late fees,
eviction procedure, retaliation, or any tenant remedy.**

**Nothing in the list at C.2 is an Arizona legal requirement**, and the list does not present itself as
one — it is a **drafting checklist of what a lease must settle**, which is a different thing. **Any figure
or period a student writes into items 3, 5, 10 or 11 must come from the brokerage's forms and from counsel,
not from this course**, and the exercise instruction says so on its face.

**This is a submission gate**, recorded as one in `arizona/regulatory/primary-text-readings.md`. A version
of this course that states Arizona landlord-tenant requirements must first read those statutes.

### C.4 One Arizona rule that does apply, and was read

**A.A.C. R4-28-802(B)** covers offers **to purchase or lease**, and R4-28-802(A) requires expeditious
delivery of a **legible copy of the signed document and final agreement to each party signing** — leases
included. **A.A.C. R4-28-1101(J)** applies with particular force in leasing: a licensee shall not **permit
or facilitate access to or occupancy in a person's real property without prior written authorization from
the owner.** A salesperson handing over keys is inside that rule.
