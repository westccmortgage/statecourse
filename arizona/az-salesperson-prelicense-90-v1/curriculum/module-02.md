## Legal descriptions, encumbrances of record and land use control

## Learning objectives

1. Read the three legal description methods and identify which Arizona uses.
2. Compute area and acreage, and apply R4-28-502(H)'s rule about the word "acre."
3. Distinguish a legal description from an address, a parcel number and a plat reference.
4. Distinguish zoning, variances, conditional uses and nonconforming uses.
5. Identify the land use questions a licensee routes rather than answers.
6. Apply R4-28-1101(B)(3) to a land use problem.

## A. Legal descriptions

An **address** identifies a mailbox. An **assessor's parcel number** identifies a tax record. **Neither
describes land.**

### A.1 The rectangular (government) survey — Arizona's primary system

Arizona is a **public land survey state**, and most of its land is described by **township, range and
section** referenced to the **Gila and Salt River Meridian**.

- A **township** is six miles square and contains **36 sections**.
- A **section** is **one square mile** and contains **640 acres**.
- Sections are numbered **1 through 36**, beginning in the **north-east** corner and running west across
  the top row, east across the second, and so on.

**Worked example.** *The SW ¼ of the NE ¼ of Section 22* contains 640 × ¼ × ¼ = **40 acres.** Read the
description **backwards** — start at the section, take the NE quarter, then the SW quarter of that. Each
"of" divides.

*The N ½ of the SW ¼ of the SE ¼* = 640 × ¼ × ¼ × ½ = **20 acres.**

### A.2 Metes and bounds, and lot-and-block

**Metes and bounds** is used where the rectangular system does not neatly describe a parcel: a **point of
beginning**, **courses** and **distances**, called **monuments**, returning to the point of beginning. **A
description that does not close describes no parcel**, and that is a surveyor's problem.

**Reference to a recorded plat** — *Lot 14, Block 3, of [named] Subdivision, according to the plat recorded
in Book __ of Maps, Page __, records of __ County, Arizona.*

**A fence or a block wall is not a boundary.** It is evidence of where somebody once thought the boundary
was. Where anything sits near a line, the answer is a **current survey.**

### A.3 The Arizona advertising rule that turns on a measurement

**A.A.C. R4-28-502(H):**

> *"A licensee shall **not use the term 'acre,' either alone or modified, unless referring to an area of
> land representing at least 43,560 square feet**."*

**"Either alone or modified."** *"Nearly an acre," "a generous acre," "an acre-plus lot"* — each is the term
**modified**, and each requires **at least 43,560 square feet**. A 38,000-square-foot lot advertised as
*"almost an acre"* has used the term in a way the rule forbids.

**That is a genuinely Arizona-specific rule, and it is the reason a licensee needs the arithmetic.**
38,000 ÷ 43,560 = **0.87 acres** — describe it as **0.87 acres** or as **38,000 square feet**, and the
problem disappears.

## B. Encumbrances of record

A **title search** discloses what the record holds: **deeds of trust and other liens**, **easements**,
**CC&Rs**, **plat notes and dedications**, **judgments**, and **assessments**.

**A preliminary title report lists exceptions** — the matters the policy will **not** cover. **Reading the
exceptions is the point of receiving it.** A licensee who hands a buyer a preliminary report without saying
so has handed over a document the buyer will not open.

**And R4-28-1101(B)(4)** makes **the existence of a lien or encumbrance** a matter to be **disclosed in
writing to all other parties.**

## C. Land use control

### C.1 The instruments

**General plan.** **Zoning ordinance** — use districts plus **bulk and dimensional controls**: setbacks,
height, lot coverage, minimum lot area, frontage. **Subdivision ordinance.** **Building codes**, with
permits and a **certificate of occupancy**. **CC&Rs and community association governance**, which in much
of Arizona are as consequential as zoning.

**Arizona's planning and zoning statutes and every local ordinance were not read for this project. This
course states no setback, height limit, lot size, use category, permit fee or approval deadline.**

### C.2 The four relief mechanisms

| Mechanism | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Variance** | Permission to depart from a requirement, generally on a hardship showing not created by the owner. |
| **Conditional (special) use permit** | A use the ordinance **already contemplates** in that district, allowed once stated conditions are met. |
| **Nonconforming use** | A use that **lawfully predates** the ordinance; its scope and continuation are set by the ordinance. |
| **Rezone** | Amendment of the ordinance — a legislative act. |

**"Grandfathered" is not a legal term and it is not a licensee's conclusion.** Say what is known — *the
owner states the use predates the current zoning* — and route the rest.

## D. Where land use meets the Arizona disclosure duty

**R4-28-1101(B)** requires a licensee to *"**disclose in writing to all other parties any information the
licensee possesses that materially or adversely affects the consideration to be paid by any party**,"*
including **"(3) any known adverse material fact concerning the property or material defect existing in the
property being transferred."**

**And R4-28-1101(I)** goes further than most states:

> *"A licensee shall **exercise reasonable care in ensuring that the licensee OBTAINS information material
> to a client's interests and relevant to the contemplated transaction** and accurately communicates the
> information to the client. A licensee shall **take reasonable steps to assist a client in confirming the
> accuracy of information** relevant to the transaction."*

**Read (B) and (I) together and Arizona's shape appears.** **(B)** is a duty to **disclose what you
possess**, in writing, to **everyone**. **(I)** is an affirmative duty to **obtain** information material to
your **client's** interests and to **help them confirm it.**

**That is a more active standard than several other states set**, and a licensee who treats "I wasn't
required to look into it" as a complete answer has answered a question Arizona did not ask.

**But it is not a duty to become an expert.** **R4-28-1101(H)** supplies the limit: work outside your field
of competence requires **engaging someone competent**, unless the lack of expertise is **disclosed to the
client in writing first** and the client **then** employs you.

**Obtain, communicate, help confirm — and where it is beyond you, bring in someone who can.**
