## Learning objectives

1. State, in the rule's own words, what duty an Arizona licensee owes a client and what duty is owed to
   everyone else.
2. State the four categories of information R4-28-1101(B) requires be disclosed in writing to **all other
   parties**.
3. State the four conflict situations in R4-28-1101(E) and **when** the disclosure must be made.
4. Distinguish **prior written consent** under (F) from **prior written acknowledgement** under (G), and
   state (G)'s single carve-out.
5. State the competence limit in (H) and the two ways a licensee may lawfully proceed outside it.
6. State the affirmative information duty in (I) and explain how it differs from a duty merely to pass on
   what arrives.
7. State the possession prohibitions in (J) and the counsel duty in (K).
8. State the 24-hour rule in R4-28-1102 and how a principal may alter it.

## Part 1 — The first sentence: Arizona is a fiduciary state

Read the opening subsection before anything else, because it settles the character of every duty that
follows.

> **A.A.C. R4-28-1101(A)**: *"A licensee owes a **fiduciary duty** to the client and shall **protect and
> promote the client's interests**. The licensee shall also **deal fairly with all other parties to a
> transaction**."*

Two duties, two audiences, in two sentences.

| To whom | Duty | Rule source |
|---|---|---|
| **The client** | **Fiduciary duty**; protect and promote the client's interests | R4-28-1101(A), first sentence |
| **All other parties** | **Deal fairly** | R4-28-1101(A), second sentence |

### Why this sentence matters more than it looks

A licensee who has studied for a licence in another state may arrive in Arizona with the opposite
instinct, because **several states have deliberately legislated away the fiduciary standard.** This project
has built courses in some of them, and the contrast is exact:

| State | What that state's law says about the standard |
|---|---|
| **Arizona** | *"A licensee owes a **fiduciary duty** to the client"* — R4-28-1101(A) |
| **Washington** | Its statute provides that the duties it lists are **statutory duties and not fiduciary duties** |
| **Oklahoma** | Its statute **abrogates the common-law fiduciary duties** of a broker and substitutes a statutory list |

**Arizona did not do that.** The word is in the rule, in the first sentence, and it is not qualified
anywhere in R4-28-1101. A licensee transferring in from a statutory-duty state who assumes Arizona works
the same way is wrong, and the error is not academic — it changes what a licensee must do when the client's
interest and the licensee's convenience diverge.

**What "fiduciary" imports.** Module AZ-05 developed the general content: loyalty, obedience to lawful
instruction, disclosure to the principal, confidentiality, reasonable care and skill, and accounting.
R4-28-1101(A) adopts that standard by name and then **adds specific requirements on top of it** in
subsections (B) through (K). The rule's specific requirements are a floor, not a ceiling: nothing in the
rule says a licensee who satisfies (B) through (K) has thereby discharged the fiduciary duty in (A).

**"Deal fairly" is a real duty, not a courtesy.** The second sentence is the source of the licensee's
obligation to the party across the table. Subsection (B), immediately following, is its most concrete
expression — and note that **(B) runs to *all other parties*, not to the client.**

## Part 2 — R4-28-1101(B): what must be disclosed, in writing, to everyone else

> **(B)**: *"A licensee participating in a real estate transaction shall **disclose in writing to all other
> parties any information the licensee possesses that materially or adversely affects the consideration to
> be paid by any party** to the transaction, **including**:*
> **1.** *"Any information that the **seller or lessor is or may be unable to perform**;*
> **2.** *"Any information that the **buyer or lessee is, or may be, unable to perform**;*
> **3.** *"Any **known adverse material fact concerning the property or material defect existing in the
> property** being transferred; and*
> **4.** *"The **existence of a lien or encumbrance** on the property being transferred."*

### Read the structure before the list

Three things are built into the opening clause, and each of them is doing work.

**1. "Information the licensee possesses."** The trigger is possession. Subsection (B) does not require a
licensee to go looking — that duty appears separately in **(I)**, and it runs to the **client**, not to
other parties. Keep the two apart: **(B) is a disclosure duty about what you have; (I) is an acquisition
duty about what you should get.**

**2. "Materially or adversely affects the consideration to be paid by any party."** This is the standard.
The four numbered items are stated as **examples of it** — the rule says *"including"* — so a licensee who
works down the list as a checklist and stops has misread the rule. Information that materially affects what
someone will pay is disclosable whether or not it fits one of the four.

**3. "In writing to all other parties."** Not orally. Not to the client only. **In writing, to everyone
else in the transaction.**

### Items 1 and 2 — inability to perform, on either side

This is the pair a licensee from another state is least likely to expect, and it is symmetrical:

- **Item 1** — information that **the seller or lessor** is or may be unable to perform.
- **Item 2** — information that **the buyer or lessee** is, or may be, unable to perform.

Note the phrasing: **"is or may be"**. The rule does not wait for certainty.

**What this means in practice.** A licensee who learns that the buyer's financing has collapsed, or that the
seller cannot deliver clear title on the agreed date, holds information the rule treats as **materially
affecting the consideration to be paid** — and must **disclose it in writing to the other parties.** A
licensee's instinct to protect a client by keeping quiet about the client's own difficulty runs directly
into subsection (B).

**This is where (A)'s two sentences meet.** The fiduciary duty to the client does not license concealment
from the other side of something the rule names as disclosable. Where a licensee genuinely cannot see how to
satisfy both, the rule's answer is in **(C)**: notify the designated broker. The rule does **not** provide
that the client's interest overrides (B).

### Item 3 — known adverse material fact or material defect

Two phrases: **"known adverse material fact concerning the property"** and **"material defect existing in
the property."** Again the trigger is **known**.

**A boundary the licensee must know and this course does not cross.** Arizona has a separate statutory
scheme on residential disclosure obligations and on facts a licensee **is not** obliged to disclose.
**Those statutes were NOT read by this project**, and **no such rule, exception, or list of
non-disclosable facts is stated anywhere in this course.** Module AZ-13 records the same gap for the
disclosure documents it covers. **A licensee's question about whether a particular fact must be disclosed
under Arizona statute is a question for the designated broker and for counsel**, and this module states
only what R4-28-1101(B) itself says.

### Item 4 — the existence of a lien or encumbrance

Stated flatly. **Existence**, disclosed in writing to all other parties. Module AZ-03 built the general
content on liens and encumbrances; this is the rule that requires the licensee to say so.

## Part 3 — (C) and (D): performance, delay, and controversy between licensees

> **(C)**: *"A licensee shall **expeditiously perform all acts required by the holding of a license**. A
> licensee **shall not delay performance, either intentionally or through neglect**. If a licensee is unable
> to perform any act as required by rule or statute, **they must expeditiously notify their designated
> broker of their inability to perform**."*

Three separate requirements in one subsection:

1. **Perform expeditiously.**
2. **Do not delay — intentionally *or through neglect*.** The rule reaches negligent delay, not only
   deliberate stalling. A licensee who simply forgot has still breached (C).
3. **If unable, tell the designated broker — expeditiously.** This is the rule's own escape route, and it
   runs **upward**, to the broker. Module AZ-12 develops what the designated broker must then do under
   **R4-28-1103**.

> **(D)**: *"A licensee **shall not allow a controversy with another licensee to jeopardize, delay, or
> interfere with the initiation, processing, or finalizing of a transaction on behalf of a client**."*

**What (D) does not do.** The rule adds that it **does not oblige a licensee to alter compensation terms or
to relinquish the right to maintain an action.** So a commission dispute may be pursued — it simply may not
be pursued **through the transaction.** The client's transaction is not leverage.

**The pattern in (C) and (D) together:** the client's transaction moves forward, and the licensee's own
difficulties — inability, neglect, a fight with another licensee — are handled somewhere other than by
letting the transaction stall.

## Part 4 — R4-28-1101(E): four conflicts, in writing, before any binding agreement

> **(E)**: *"A licensee shall not act directly or indirectly in a transaction **without informing the other
> parties in the transaction, in writing and before the parties enter any binding agreement**, of a present
> or prospective interest or conflict in the transaction, **including that the**:*
> **1.** *"**Licensee has a license and is acting as a principal**;*
> **2.** *"**Purchaser or seller is a member of licensee's or designated broker's immediate family**;*
> **3.** *"Purchaser or seller **has ownership or is the employee of the licensee's employing broker**; or*
> **4.** *"**Licensee, or a member of the licensee's immediate family, has a financial interest in the
> transaction** other than the licensee's receipt of compensation for the real estate services."*

### The timing requirement is the operative part

**"In writing and before the parties enter any binding agreement."** A disclosure made at closing, or when
the conflict becomes awkward, or when someone asks, is **late**, and lateness is the breach. The rule fixes
the deadline at a specific event — **before the parties enter any binding agreement** — and a licensee who
knows of the conflict earlier has no reason to wait.

**"Directly or indirectly."** A licensee cannot cure a conflict by routing the transaction through someone
else.

**"Present or prospective."** A conflict that has not yet materialised but is in prospect is disclosable.

**"Including."** As in (B), the four numbered items are examples of *"a present or prospective interest or
conflict"*, not an exhaustive list.

### The four named situations

| # | Situation | The point of it |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Licensee is licensed and is acting as a principal** | Buying or selling for your own account. The other side is entitled to know in writing that the person across the table holds a licence. |
| **2** | **Purchaser or seller is in the licensee's — or the designated broker's — immediate family** | Note it reaches the **designated broker's** family, not just the licensee's. |
| **3** | **Purchaser or seller has ownership in, or is an employee of, the licensee's employing broker** | The conflict is at the firm level, and the licensee must disclose it even though it is not the licensee's own interest. |
| **4** | **Licensee or immediate family has a financial interest in the transaction** other than the licensee's compensation | The carve-out matters: ordinary commission is **not** the conflict. Anything else financial is. |

**Item 1 is the classic.** A licensee selling their own house is not doing anything prohibited — they are
required to say, in writing, before any binding agreement, that they hold a licence and are acting as a
principal.

## Part 5 — (F) and (G): consent, acknowledgement, and the subsection licensees breach without noticing

Two subsections, two different words, and the difference is examinable.

> **(F)**: *"A licensee shall **not accept compensation from or represent more than one party to a
> transaction without the PRIOR WRITTEN CONSENT of all parties**."*
>
> **(G)**: *"A licensee shall **not accept any compensation, including rebate or other consideration,
> directly or indirectly, for any goods or services provided to a person if the goods or services are
> related to or result from a real estate transaction, without that person's PRIOR WRITTEN ACKNOWLEDGEMENT
> of the compensation**. **This prohibition does not apply to compensation paid to a broker by a broker who
> represents a party in the transaction.**"*

### Comparing them

| | **(F)** | **(G)** |
|---|---|---|
| **What it governs** | Compensation from **more than one party**, or **representing** more than one party | Compensation — **including rebate or other consideration** — for **goods or services** related to or resulting from a transaction |
| **What is required** | **Prior written CONSENT** | **Prior written ACKNOWLEDGEMENT** |
| **Whose** | **All parties** | **That person's** — the person to whom the goods or services were provided |
| **Timing** | **Prior** | **Prior** |
| **Exception** | None stated in (F) | **Broker-to-broker**: does not apply to compensation paid to a broker by a broker who represents a party in the transaction |

**Consent is permission. Acknowledgement is receipt.** The rule chose different words for the two
subsections and a licensee should not treat them as interchangeable. Under **(F)** the parties must
**agree**; under **(G)** the person must have **acknowledged the compensation** in writing beforehand.

### Why (G) is the one that catches people

Read how wide it is: **any** compensation, **including rebate or other consideration**, **directly or
indirectly**, for **any** goods or services, if those goods or services are **related to or result from** a
real estate transaction.

That reaches referral arrangements, marketing arrangements, and consideration flowing from vendors — in each
case requiring the **prior written acknowledgement of the person to whom the goods or services were
provided.** A licensee who receives something of value because a client was sent somewhere is inside (G).

**The single carve-out is narrow and specific:** it applies **only** to compensation **paid to a broker by a
broker who represents a party in the transaction.** Ordinary co-brokerage is not caught. Nothing else is
excluded by the rule's text.

**A boundary this module does not cross.** Federal law also governs referral arrangements and settlement
services, and Module AZ-08 covers the general federal content. **This module states only what R4-28-1101(G)
requires**, and does **not** state how (G) interacts with any federal requirement — that comparison was not
worked from primary federal text for Arizona purposes and is **not stated here.**

## Part 6 — (H) and (I): competence, and the affirmative duty to obtain information

> **(H)**: *"The services that a licensee provides to a client shall **conform to the standards of practice
> and competence recognized in the professional community** for the specific real estate discipline ... A
> licensee **shall not undertake to provide professional services concerning a type of property or service
> that is outside the licensee's field of competence without engaging the assistance of a person who is
> competent to provide those services, unless the licensee's lack of expertise is first disclosed to the
> client in writing and the client subsequently employs the licensee**."*

**(H) has two lawful routes out of an incompetence problem, and no third:**

1. **Engage the assistance of a competent person**; or
2. **Disclose the lack of expertise to the client in writing first**, *and* the client **subsequently
   employs the licensee.**

Note the sequence in route 2: **disclosure first, employment after.** A licensee who takes the engagement
and discloses afterwards has not satisfied (H). And the disclosure alone is not enough — the rule requires
that the client **then** employ the licensee, which is to say the client must have chosen with the
disclosure in hand.

**"The specific real estate discipline."** The standard is measured against the discipline, not against real
estate at large. Competence in residential resale is not competence in the commercial, land, or property
management disciplines.

> **(I)**: *"A licensee shall **exercise reasonable care in ensuring that the licensee obtains information
> material to a client's interests and relevant to the contemplated transaction and accurately communicates
> the information to the client**. A licensee shall **take reasonable steps to assist a client in confirming
> the accuracy of information relevant to the transaction**."*

### (I) is an acquisition duty, and that is the point

Three obligations:

1. **Obtain** information material to the client's interests and relevant to the transaction.
2. **Accurately communicate** it to the client.
3. **Assist the client in confirming its accuracy.**

**Obligation 1 is affirmative.** The licensee is not merely a conduit for information that happens to
arrive. Compare, again, a state this project has built for: **Washington's statute expressly provides that
its licensees have no duty to investigate.** **Arizona's rule runs the other way** — (I) requires reasonable
care in **obtaining** material information. **A course that treated the two states alike would misstate
both**, and this module states Arizona's rule as Arizona wrote it.

**Obligation 3 is the one licensees forget.** The rule does not merely require accurate transmission; it
requires **reasonable steps to assist the client in confirming accuracy.** Directing a client to the source,
to an inspection, or to the professional who can verify a figure is the substance of it.

**The standard throughout is "reasonable care" / "reasonable steps"** — not a guarantee, and not perfection.
The rule does not define what is reasonable, and **this module does not invent a definition.**

## Part 7 — (J) and (K): access, possession, and the duty to send the client to counsel

> **(J)**: *"A licensee shall not: **1.** Permit or facilitate **access to or occupancy in a person's real
> property without prior written authorization from the owner**; or **2.** **Deliver possession prior to
> closing unless expressly instructed to do so by the owner** of the property or property interest being
> transferred."*

| | Prohibited | Cured only by |
|---|---|---|
| **(J)(1)** | Permitting or facilitating **access to or occupancy in** a person's real property | **Prior written authorization from the owner** |
| **(J)(2)** | **Delivering possession prior to closing** | **Express instruction from the owner** of the property or property interest being transferred |

**(J)(1) reaches "permit or facilitate."** A licensee who does not personally open the door but arranges for
someone else to is inside the prohibition. **"Access"** is broader than occupancy — a showing is access.
The required authorization is **prior** and **written**.

**(J)(2) is the early-possession problem.** Buyers ask to move in before closing; sellers ask to stay after.
The rule addresses the first directly: **no delivery of possession before closing** unless the **owner
expressly instructs it.**

> **(K)**: *"A licensee shall **recommend to a client that the client seek appropriate counsel from
> insurance, legal, tax, and accounting professionals regarding the risks of pre-possession or
> post-possession of a property**."*

**(K) is an affirmative duty and it names four professions:** **insurance, legal, tax, and accounting.** It
attaches to **pre-possession or post-possession** arrangements — exactly the situations (J)(2) contemplates.

**Note what (K) does not do.** It does not authorise the licensee to advise on those risks. It requires the
licensee to **recommend that the client get that advice from the professionals named.** Module AZ-12
develops the general boundary between real estate practice and the unauthorised practice of law; (K) is one
of the places Arizona's rule builds the referral into the licensee's own duty.

## Part 8 — R4-28-1102: contacting another licensee's principal

> **(A)**: *"Real estate licensees **may not contact a principal represented by another licensee** unless
> the principal's **Designated Broker, broker representative with delegated authority, and the licensee are
> unavailable for 24 hours**. A principal may **waive or alter this requirement by issuing written
> instructions**."*
>
> **(B)**: *"For a buyer and unless the buyer waives this requirement in writing, **negotiation must be
> conducted exclusively through the principal's broker or the broker's representative** unless the
> designated broker, a broker with delegated authority, and the buyer's licensee with an agency relationship
> are **unavailable for 24 hours**."*

### The 24-hour rule

The prohibition is on **contacting a principal represented by another licensee.** It lifts only when
**all three** are unavailable for **24 hours**:

1. the principal's **designated broker**;
2. a **broker representative with delegated authority**; and
3. the **licensee**.

**All three, and for 24 hours.** One unreturned call does not open the door.

**The principal controls the rule, not the licensee.** *"A principal may waive or alter this requirement by
issuing written instructions."* The waiver belongs to the represented party — the licensee who wants to make
contact cannot supply it.

**(B) applies the same structure to buyer-side negotiation**, requiring that negotiation be conducted
**exclusively through the principal's broker or the broker's representative** unless the buyer waives in
writing or the same three people are unavailable for 24 hours.

**Where this sits in practice.** Module AZ-06 covers the general content on presenting and negotiating
offers. **R4-28-1102 is the Arizona rule that decides *whom you may talk to* while doing it**, and it is a
rule licensees breach out of impatience rather than intent.

## Part 9 — Working the rule as one instrument

The eleven subsections are not eleven separate rules. Work a single ordinary transaction through them and
the shape appears.

**A licensee lists a house. The seller is the licensee's brother-in-law. The buyer, found by the same
licensee, asks to move in two weeks before closing. A title search turns up a lien. The buyer's lender has
gone quiet.**

| Fact | Subsection | What the rule requires |
|---|---|---|
| Seller is immediate family | **(E)(2)** | Written disclosure to the other parties **before any binding agreement** |
| Licensee represents or is compensated by both sides | **(F)** | **Prior written consent of all parties** |
| Lien discovered | **(B)(4)** | **Written disclosure to all other parties** |
| Lender has gone quiet — buyer may be unable to perform | **(B)(2)** | **Written disclosure to all other parties**; *"is, or may be"* |
| Buyer asks for early possession | **(J)(2)** | **No delivery of possession before closing** unless the **owner expressly instructs** |
| Early possession is being discussed at all | **(K)** | **Recommend counsel** — insurance, legal, tax, accounting |
| Licensee has never handled early possession | **(H)** | Engage competent assistance, **or** disclose the lack of expertise in writing **first** and be employed after |
| The licensee cannot get the paperwork out in time | **(C)** | **Expeditiously notify the designated broker** |
| The co-op licensee is fighting about commission | **(D)** | The **dispute may not delay the transaction**; compensation terms need not be altered |
| Licensee wants to call the buyer's agent's client directly | **R4-28-1102(A)** | **Not unless** the designated broker, a delegated broker representative, **and** the licensee are **unavailable for 24 hours** — or the principal has waived in writing |

Nine subsections and a second rule, in one transaction, none of them exotic. **That is why this module is
7.0 hours and why the 27-hour Arizona-specific block cannot be waived for an out-of-state licensee** — the
duties are Arizona's own, and several of them run opposite to the state that licensee came from.
