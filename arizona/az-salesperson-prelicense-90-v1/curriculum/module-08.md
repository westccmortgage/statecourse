## Learning objectives

1. State the structure of fair housing law — federal, state and local — and which controls.
2. Identify the prohibited practices by conduct rather than by label.
3. Recognise steering, and the requests that invite it.
4. Distinguish a reasonable accommodation from a reasonable modification.
5. Apply R4-28-1101(B) to a property condition problem.
6. Identify the environmental conditions common in Arizona and who answers each.

## Part 1 — Fair housing

### A. Three layers

**Federal law sets a floor. Arizona's own law adds to it. Local ordinances in some Arizona jurisdictions add
further.** **Where the layers differ, the MOST PROTECTIVE applies.**

> **What this course does not state.** **No protected class list from any of the three was read for this
> project, and none is stated here.** This is deliberate: a fair housing list that is out of date or
> incomplete is worse than no list, because a licensee will rely on it.
>
> **The safest operating posture, and the one this course teaches: do not screen, sort, steer, describe or
> decide on ANY personal characteristic of a person.** A licensee who never uses a characteristic never
> needs the list.

### B. The prohibited practices, taught as conduct

**Refusal to deal** — including **failing to return calls**, **not sending listings**, and **being
unavailable**. A refusal does not have to be spoken.

**Different terms or conditions** — a different deposit, fee, showing hours or level of service.

**Discriminatory advertising** — any statement, in any medium, indicating a preference, limitation or
discrimination. **This reaches photographs, captions and the description of a neighbourhood's character.**

**Steering** — directing prospects toward or away from areas on the basis of a characteristic, **including
by what is shown, what is described, and what is left out.** Steering by omission is still steering.

**Blockbusting. Redlining. Discrimination in services and facilities. Retaliation or interference.**

### C. Steering, in the form a licensee actually meets it

- *"What kind of people live there?"*
- *"Is this a good neighbourhood for a family like ours?"*
- *"Where would you live if you were us?"*
- *"Which schools are the good ones?"*

**Answering any of them from your own characterisation is steering, whoever asked.**

**The reliable response has three parts:** say what you can and cannot do, plainly; **route the objective
questions to objective sources** — school data, crime statistics, commute times are all published; and
**ask what they actually need in terms of the property.**

**And notice that the fourth question is also an opinion presented as fact**, which **R4-28-502(C)** reaches
independently.

### D. Disability

A **reasonable accommodation** is a change in **rules, policies, practices or services**. A **reasonable
modification** is a **physical change to the premises**. **The distinction is what is changed: a RULE or the
PREMISES.**

**An assistance animal is not a pet.** **The verification a housing provider may lawfully request is
governed by rules this project did not read** — the question goes to counsel **before** a refusal, not
after. **R4-28-1101(H)** requires engaging someone competent outside your field of competence.

### E. Advertising

**Describe the property, not the people.** *"Three bedrooms, north-facing patio, cul-de-sac"* describes
property. *"Perfect for a young family"* describes people.

**And the advertisement is more than the words** — photographs, captions, hashtags, the account it is posted
from, and **the audience it is targeted to.** **R4-28-502(J)** expressly brings electronic media within
Arizona's advertising rules, naming *"the **Internet, Artificial Intelligence**, or web site technology"* —
Module AZ-12 develops it.

## Part 2 — Property condition and the Arizona disclosure duty

### F. R4-28-1101(B) — the rule that governs

> *"A licensee participating in a real estate transaction shall **disclose in writing to all other parties
> any information the licensee possesses that materially or adversely affects the consideration to be paid
> by any party** to the transaction, **including**:*
> **1.** *"Any information that the **seller or lessor is or may be unable to perform**;*
> **2.** *"Any information that the **buyer or lessee is, or may be, unable to perform**;*
> **3.** *"Any **known adverse material fact concerning the property or material defect existing in the
> property** being transferred; and*
> **4.** *"The **existence of a lien or encumbrance** on the property being transferred."*

**Four features.**

**"In writing."** Not orally, not by implication.

**"To all other parties."** Not merely to the client — **to everyone in the transaction.**

**"Any information the licensee POSSESSES."** The trigger is possession, not investigation.

**And the list is introduced by "including" — it is illustrative, not exhaustive.** The test is whether the
information **materially or adversely affects the consideration to be paid.**

### G. And R4-28-1101(I) — the duty that goes further

> *"A licensee shall **exercise reasonable care in ensuring that the licensee OBTAINS information material
> to a client's interests and relevant to the contemplated transaction** and accurately communicates the
> information to the client. A licensee shall **take reasonable steps to assist a client in confirming the
> accuracy of information** relevant to the transaction."*

**Read (B) and (I) together.** **(B)** requires **disclosure of what you possess**, in writing, to
**everyone**. **(I)** requires **obtaining** what is material to your **client's** interests and **helping
them confirm it.**

**Arizona expects a licensee to be active, not merely honest.** A licensee who says "nobody asked me to look
into it" has answered a question Arizona did not ask.

**The limit is R4-28-1101(H):** outside your field of competence, **engage someone competent** — unless the
lack of expertise is **disclosed to the client in writing first** and the client then employs you.

### H. Conditions a licensee routes rather than opines on

For each: **what it is, and who answers it.** The second half is the licensee's job.

**Lead-based paint** — the federal disclosure obligation attaches to **target housing**, generally
pre-1978 dwellings, and reaches **known** lead-based paint and known hazards. **Painting over it does not
remove it.** **The federal regulation was not read for this project**; no notice period or retention
requirement is stated. → **A certified inspector or risk assessor.**

**Radon.** → **A current test.** An old result is not a current reading.

**Asbestos**, common in older construction. → **A qualified inspector; a licensed abatement contractor
before any renovation.**

**Mould and moisture** — the useful question is almost never "is there mould" but **"where is the water
coming from."** → **A qualified inspector; a contractor.**

**Soils.** In parts of Arizona, **expansive soils, subsidence and earth fissures** are real and
consequential. → **A geotechnical engineer; published hazard mapping; the jurisdiction.**

**Water — supply, quality and the right to use it.** Whether a rural parcel has an adequate or assured
supply, and what must be disclosed about it, are governed by **Arizona statutes this project did not read**,
including the **Affidavit of Disclosure** required for certain unsubdivided land sales. → **The Arizona
Department of Water Resources, a well professional, a testing laboratory, and counsel.**

**On-site septic and alternative wastewater systems.** → **The county environmental or health department for
the permit; a qualified inspector for the condition.**

**Flood and stormwater**, including washes and floodplain designation. → **Published mapping; the
jurisdiction; an insurer.**

**Termites and other wood-destroying organisms.** → **A licensed inspector.**

**Roof, HVAC and pool systems** — in a climate that is hard on all three. → **Qualified inspectors.**

> **This course states no action level, threshold, testing protocol, remediation standard, buffer, hazard
> designation, water rule, disclosure requirement or insurance term for ANY of the above**, because no
> federal regulation, Arizona environmental or water statute, county rule or hazard map was read for this
> project. **Every one is routed.**

### I. The habit

Separate three things and say which is which, every time:

1. **What you know** — you saw it, measured it, or hold the document.
2. **What you were told** — attributed to whoever told you.
3. **What you are guessing** — say nothing, and route it.

**Every disclosure failure in this course traces back to one of the three being presented as another.**

**And never advise a seller to remain ignorant.** *"Don't test — what you don't know you don't have to
disclose"* is bad advice legally and bad advice practically. **R4-28-1101(I)** points the other way
entirely.
