## Learning objectives

1. State the essential elements of a valid contract.
2. Distinguish void, voidable, unenforceable and valid.
3. Apply offer, acceptance, counteroffer and the mirror image rule.
4. Identify the defects in reality of consent.
5. State what the Statute of Frauds requires.
6. Distinguish the remedies for breach and explain why specific performance is available.
7. Apply R4-28-802(B) — the duty to submit all offers, and what does and does not end it.

## A. Essential elements

**Competent parties. Mutual assent. Consideration. Lawful object.** And **a writing**, where the Statute of
Frauds requires one — which it does for contracts conveying an interest in real property.

**Recording and notarisation are not elements of a contract.** A contract can be valid, fully enforceable
and never recorded.

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Valid** | Meets all elements; enforceable by either party. |
| **Void** | Never had legal effect. |
| **Voidable** | Valid **until the party holding the power to avoid it elects to do so**. |
| **Unenforceable** | Valid in substance; a court will not enforce it — commonly for want of a writing. |

**The difference between void and voidable is who holds the power.** A contract with a minor is voidable
**at the minor's election** — the adult cannot escape it. A contract induced by fraud is voidable at the
**defrauded** party's election.

**Bilateral** (promise for promise) versus **unilateral** (promise for an act). **Executory** versus
**executed**. **A purchase contract is executory from acceptance until closing** — and that period, with
deadlines falling and money in escrow, is where a licensee does most of the work.

## B. Offer and acceptance

An **offer** may be **revoked at any time before acceptance**, unless consideration was given to hold it
open — which is what an **option** is.

An offer **terminates** by **revocation**, **rejection**, **lapse**, **death or incapacity of either
party**, **destruction of the subject matter**, or **counteroffer**.

### B.1 The mirror image rule

Acceptance must be **unqualified** and **mirror** the offer. **A purported acceptance that changes any term
is a counteroffer** — which **rejects and terminates the original offer** and reverses the roles.

Work it through. A buyer offers Monday. The seller signs Tuesday but strikes the close-of-escrow date and
writes a new one. **That is not an acceptance.** The buyer's offer is **gone** and cannot be revived by the
buyer signing the original on Wednesday. What exists is the seller's counteroffer.

**Acceptance must also be communicated.** A signed contract sitting in a drawer has accepted nothing.

### B.2 The Arizona rule that governs what you do with offers

**A.A.C. R4-28-802(B):**

> *"During the term of a listing agreement, a licensee shall **promptly submit to the licensee's client all
> offers** to purchase or lease the listed property. **Upon receiving permission from the seller or
> lessor**, the licensee ... **may disclose to all offerors or their agents the existence and terms of all
> additional offers** on the listed property. **The licensee shall submit to the client all offers made
> prior to closing and IS NOT RELEASED FROM THIS DUTY BY THE CLIENT'S ACCEPTANCE OF AN OFFER unless the
> client instructs the licensee IN WRITING to cease submitting offers** or unless otherwise provided in the
> listing agreement, lease, or purchase contract. The licensee **may voluntarily submit offers ...
> regardless of any limitations contained in the listing agreement and may submit offers after the listing
> agreement is terminated**."*

**Four things a licensee must hold.**

**Submit ALL offers, promptly.** The licensee does not filter by their own judgement of merit.

**Acceptance does NOT end the duty.** The subsection says so expressly. Offers made **prior to closing**
still go to the client.

**Only three things end it:** a **written** instruction from the client to cease; a provision in the
**listing agreement**; or a provision in the **lease or purchase contract.** An oral "don't bother me with
any more" is not one of them.

**Disclosing other offers requires the seller's permission.** The rule permits it *"upon receiving
permission from the seller or lessor"* — so it is the **seller's** decision, not the licensee's, and not
the other buyers'.

**And the closing sentences run the other way**: a licensee **may** submit offers **regardless of
limitations in the listing agreement**, and **may** submit them **after the listing agreement is
terminated.** The rule protects the flow of offers to the owner in both directions.

## C. Reality of consent

Assent must be real. It is defective where there is **fraud**, **misrepresentation** (innocent or
negligent), **duress**, **undue influence**, or **mistake**. In each case the contract is **voidable at the
election of the injured party.**

**Puffing** is opinion a reasonable person would not rely on as fact. **Misrepresentation** is a false
statement of fact — and the line moves toward misrepresentation as the statement becomes **more specific and
more verifiable.**

**A.A.C. R4-28-502(C)** states Arizona's advertising counterpart:

> *"A licensee shall ensure that all advertising contains **accurate claims and representations**, and
> **fully states factual material** relating to the information advertised. A salesperson or broker shall
> **not misrepresent the facts or create misleading or ambiguous impressions**."*

**"Create misleading or ambiguous impressions"** is broader than "make a false statement," and it reaches
what an advertisement implies as well as what it says.

## D. Writings

Contracts for the sale of an interest in real property must generally be **in writing and signed by the
party to be charged** to be enforceable. **Arizona's Statute of Frauds was not read for this project**, and
this course **states no Arizona lease-term threshold and no Arizona exception.**

**What was read is a set of Arizona rules requiring writings of their own**, which are licensing
requirements rather than Statute of Frauds requirements:

- **R4-28-1101(B)** — material adverse information, **in writing**, to **all other parties**.
- **R4-28-1101(E)** — conflicts, **in writing and before the parties enter any binding agreement.**
- **R4-28-1101(F)** — **prior written consent of all parties** before accepting compensation from, or
  representing, more than one party.
- **R4-28-1101(G)** — **prior written acknowledgement** before accepting compensation for related goods or
  services.
- **R4-28-1101(H)** — a lack of expertise **disclosed to the client in writing** before proceeding outside
  your competence.
- **R4-28-1101(J)(1)** — **prior written authorization from the owner** before permitting access.
- **R4-28-701** — the compensation sharing disclosure, **in writing at least three calendar days before
  closing.**
- **R4-28-802(B)** — a **written** instruction to cease submitting offers.
- **R4-28-502(I)** — the **written consent of the property owner** before placing a sign.

**Nine writings, and none of them comes from the Statute of Frauds.** An Arizona licensee who asks "does the
Statute of Frauds require this?" has often asked the wrong question.

**The parol evidence rule** bars evidence of prior or contemporaneous oral agreements offered to vary the
terms of a complete written contract. Combined with the list above, the working rule is: **every change goes
on paper, signed.**

## E. Performance, breach and remedies

A contract discharges by **performance**, **mutual agreement**, **operation of law**, or **breach**.

**Assignment** transfers rights and generally **leaves the assignor liable**. **Novation** substitutes a new
party and **releases** the original — which is why it needs everyone's agreement.

**Remedies:** **damages**; **liquidated damages**; **rescission**; and **specific performance**.

**Specific performance is available in real estate because each parcel is unique**, so damages are often
inadequate.

### E.1 Two Arizona rescission rights that are NOT contract-law rescission

**A.A.C. R4-28-804** creates statutory rescission rights that have nothing to do with breach, and a licensee
must not confuse the two periods:

- **Unimproved subdivided lot, or any unsubdivided land** — rescission **without cause or reason of any
  kind**, by written notice, **by midnight of the SEVENTH calendar day** following execution. **And** if the
  purchaser did **not inspect the lot before execution**, they have **SIX MONTHS to inspect** and, **at the
  time of inspection**, the right to **unilaterally rescind.**
- **Time-share interval** — rescission **without cause or reason of any kind**, by written notice, **by
  midnight of the TENTH calendar day** following execution.

**Seven days for land; ten days for a time-share.** Module AZ-13 develops both, together with **R4-28-804(C)**,
which requires the seller's **complete and current name, address, telephone number and email address on the
FACE of all agreements** so the right can actually be exercised.

### E.2 Where the money sits

**R4-28-803(C)** requires the contract to **indicate where the earnest money or down payment will be
deposited**, naming **the title company, the broker's trust account, or other depository.**

**And R4-28-803(D)** requires a **bold or large-print warning**, signed or initialled by the purchaser, where
money is paid **directly to the seller and not placed in a neutral escrow depository.** Module AZ-13
transcribes it.

**Arizona's trust account provisions — A.R.S. §§ 32-2151 and 32-2151.01 — were not read for this project.**
**This course states no deposit deadline, account requirement or reconciliation interval.**
