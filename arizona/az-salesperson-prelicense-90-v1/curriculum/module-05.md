# Arizona Real Estate Salesperson Prelicense — Module AZ-05

## Agency principles

**Course:** Real Estate Salesperson Prelicense · **Instructional hours: 7.0** of 90.0
**Arizona-specific:** no — general content; Arizona's licensee duties are Module AZ-11
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No Arizona prelicensure credit is available.

> **Read this module knowing where Arizona lands.** **A.A.C. R4-28-1101(A)** opens: *"**A licensee owes a
> FIDUCIARY DUTY to the client and shall protect and promote the client's interests. The licensee shall also
> DEAL FAIRLY with all other parties to a transaction.**"* **Arizona is a fiduciary state.** Some states
> abolished common-law fiduciary duties by statute and replaced them with a closed list; Arizona did not.
> **Module AZ-11 delivers Arizona's own rule in full.** This module supplies the agency framework it rests
> on.

## Learning objectives

1. Define agency and identify the parties.
2. State the common-law fiduciary duties and what each requires.
3. Distinguish the ways an agency relationship is created and terminated.
4. Distinguish single agency, dual agency and subagency.
5. Distinguish an agent's duties to a principal from duties owed to third parties.
6. State what A.R.S. § 32-2124(E)(3) requires an applicant to understand.

---

## A. Agency, and the parties

An **agency relationship** exists where one person, the **agent**, is authorised to act on behalf of
another, the **principal**, in dealings with **third parties**.

**A.R.S. § 32-2124(E)(3)** requires the state examination to establish that an applicant has:

> *"A **thorough understanding of the obligations between principal and agent**, the **principles of real
> estate and business opportunity practice**, the **applicable canons of business ethics**, **this chapter
> and rules adopted pursuant to this chapter**."*

**"Thorough," not "general."** Compare § 32-2124(F) and (G), which require only a **"general
understanding"** of the principal-agent obligations for cemetery and membership camping applicants. **The
real estate salesperson standard is the higher one**, and this module and Module AZ-11 are written to it.

---

## B. The fiduciary duties

The common-law duties an agent owes a principal, commonly remembered as **OLD CAR**:

- **Obedience** — to follow the principal's **lawful** instructions.
- **Loyalty** — to place the principal's interests above the agent's own.
- **Disclosure** — to tell the principal what is material to their interests.
- **Confidentiality** — to protect the principal's confidences.
- **Accounting** — to account for money and property.
- **Reasonable care and diligence** — to bring competence and effort to the work.

**Obedience covers LAWFUL instructions.** An instruction to conceal a known material defect is not one, and
**R4-28-1101(B)** — disclosure in writing to **all other parties** — makes that concrete in Arizona.

**Loyalty is what an agency relationship adds.** Honesty and fair dealing are owed to everyone;
**loyalty** is owed to the client alone. **R4-28-1101(A)** states both halves in one sentence: a fiduciary
duty **to the client**, and **fair dealing with all other parties.**

---

## C. Creation and termination

**Created by:** **express agreement** — the ordinary and correct route; **implied agreement**, from conduct;
**ratification**, where a principal adopts an unauthorised act; or **estoppel**, where a principal's conduct
led a third party reasonably to believe an agency existed.

**Terminated by:** **performance**; **expiration** of the agreed term; **mutual agreement**; **revocation or
renunciation**; or **operation of law** — death or incapacity of either party, destruction of the property,
or bankruptcy.

**The POWER to terminate and the RIGHT to terminate without consequence are different questions**, and the
second is answered in the employment agreement.

---

## D. Types of representation

**Single agency** — representing one side.

**Dual agency** — representing both. **In Arizona this is governed by R4-28-1101(F)**, and the rule is
stated as a compensation-and-representation prohibition with a consent exception:

> *"A licensee **shall not accept compensation from or represent more than one party to a transaction
> without the PRIOR WRITTEN CONSENT of all parties**."*

**Prior. Written. All parties.** Module AZ-11 develops it.

**Subagency** — a broker cooperating on behalf of another broker's principal. **And a caution that belongs
here:** cooperation and compensation are not representation, and **R4-28-701** requires a separate
**compensation sharing disclosure** to **all parties, in writing, at least three calendar days before
closing.**

---

## E. Duties to third parties

An agent owes the principal the fiduciary duties. **To everyone else the agent owes honesty and fair
dealing** — and in Arizona, considerably more than that, because **R4-28-1101(B)** requires written
disclosure **to all other parties** of information materially or adversely affecting the consideration to be
paid, **including a party's inability to perform on either side.**

**That is unusually broad**, and it is the single most important structural fact about Arizona agency
practice. Module AZ-11 transcribes it in full.

---

## F. Where an agent's authority stops

**An agent does not have authority to do what the principal has not authorised**, and an agent who exceeds
their authority may bind nobody and be liable themselves.

**Three Arizona rules mark the boundary in practice:**

- **R4-28-1101(J)(1)** — no permitting or facilitating **access to or occupancy in** a person's real
  property **without prior written authorization from the owner.**
- **R4-28-1101(J)(2)** — no **delivering possession prior to closing** unless **expressly instructed by the
  owner.**
- **R4-28-502(I)** — **written consent of the property owner** before placing a sign or publishing to an
  electronic medium, and **prompt removal on the owner's request.**

**Each is an authority rule wearing different clothes**: the licensee acts on the owner's property only with
the owner's authorisation, and the rule requires that authorisation **in writing.**
