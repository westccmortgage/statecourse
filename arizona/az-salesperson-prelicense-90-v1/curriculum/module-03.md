# Arizona Real Estate Salesperson Prelicense — Module AZ-03

## Transfer of title, deeds and recording

**Course:** Real Estate Salesperson Prelicense · **Instructional hours: 7.0** of 90.0

**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish voluntary from involuntary transfer.
2. State the essential elements of a valid deed and distinguish the deed types.
3. Explain what recording accomplishes and what it does not.
4. Distinguish owner's from lender's title insurance.
5. Describe an Arizona escrow closing and the licensee's role in it.
6. Apply R4-28-802(A) — the delivery duty — and R4-28-1101(J) — the possession rules.

---

## A. Transfer

**Voluntary:** by **deed** during life; by **will** at death — the maker a **testator**, the gift of real
property a **devise**; and by **dedication**.

**Involuntary:** **intestate succession** (**Arizona's intestacy statutes were not read for this project**,
and no share or priority is stated — note that **community property** bears on this directly); **escheat**;
**eminent domain**; **trustee's sale** under a deed of trust (**A.R.S. Title 33's deed of trust provisions
were not read; no notice period, timeline, reinstatement right or deficiency rule is stated**); **tax
lien foreclosure**; **adverse possession** (**Arizona's statutory period was not read and is not stated**);
and **accretion, erosion, avulsion and reliction.**

**What matters to a licensee is recognising the trigger.** A long-standing wall, a neighbour's driveway, an
outbuilding across a line — all go to a surveyor and counsel.

## B. The deed

**Essential elements:** a **grantor** with capacity; a **grantee** identified with certainty; **words of
conveyance**; **consideration** recited; a **legal description**; the **grantor's signature**; and
**delivery and acceptance.**

**A deed takes effect on delivery, not on signing and not on recording.** The grantee does not sign a deed.
**Acknowledgement before a notary is required for recording.**

| Deed | What it warrants |
|---|---|
| **General warranty deed** | The fullest covenants, reaching back through the entire chain of title. |
| **Special warranty deed** | Covenants only against defects arising during the grantor's ownership — common in Arizona practice. |
| **Bargain and sale deed** | Implies the grantor holds title; covenants vary by form. |
| **Quitclaim deed** | Conveys whatever interest the grantor has — **possibly none** — with **no warranty**. |
| **Beneficiary deed** | An Arizona instrument by which an owner may designate who takes on death. **A.R.S. Title 33's provisions governing it were NOT read for this project**, and no requirement, revocation rule or effect is stated here. |

**A quitclaim deed is not a lesser-quality conveyance of good title. It conveys whatever the grantor happens
to hold, warranting nothing** — which is why it is the right instrument for clearing a cloud and the wrong
one for a purchase. **Recording does not improve it.**

**A.R.S. § 32-2124(E)(2)** names **deeds, mortgages, deeds of trust, security agreements, bills of sale and
land contracts of sale** among the forms an applicant must understand the **general purpose and legal effect
of.** That is the standard this module is written to — **purpose and legal effect**, not drafting.

## C. Recording

Recording with the **county recorder** where the property lies gives **constructive notice** to the world.

- **Actual notice** — what a person in fact knows.
- **Constructive notice** — what the record imputes.
- **Inquiry notice** — what a person would have learned had they investigated a fact that should have
  prompted investigation. **Someone else in possession** is the classic trigger.

**What recording does NOT do:** it does not **validate an invalid instrument** — a forged deed is void
whether recorded or not; it does not **improve the estate conveyed**; and it does not **establish that the
grantor had good title.**

**Arizona's recording statutes were not read for this project.** This course **states no Arizona recording
priority rule and no recording deadline.** The practical consequence is universal: an unrecorded interest is
an exposure.

## D. Title assurance

**Chain of title. Title search and examination. Cloud on title** — an unreleased deed of trust, a gap in
the chain, an unsatisfied judgment, an heir who never joined in a deed. **Marketable title. Quiet title
action.**

### D.1 Two policies, two beneficiaries

- **Lender's policy** — protects the **lender**, in the amount of the loan, decreasing as the loan is paid.
- **Owner's policy** — protects the **owner**, in the amount of the purchase price.

**A buyer who pays for a lender's policy has bought the lender protection, not themselves.** That is the
single most useful sentence a licensee can say at an Arizona closing table.

**Every policy has exceptions** — often including matters a **survey** would disclose. **No policy form or
rate manual was read for this project**, and no term, exception, endorsement or premium is stated here.

## E. Closing in Arizona, and two rules that bite there

Arizona closes through **escrow**, conducted by a title or escrow company as a **neutral depository**.

### E.1 The delivery duty — R4-28-802(A)

> *"**Upon execution of any transaction document a licensee shall expeditiously deliver a legible copy of
> the signed document and final agreement to each party signing the document.**"*

**Three words carry it: expeditiously, legible, each.** Not "when convenient," not a partial scan, and not
only to your own client — **to each party signing the document.**

**And R4-28-1101(C)** stands behind it: *"A licensee shall **expeditiously perform all acts required by the
holding of a license**. A licensee **shall not delay performance, either intentionally or through
neglect**."*

### E.2 Possession — R4-28-1101(J) and (K)

> **(J)** *"A licensee shall not: **1.** Permit or facilitate **access to or occupancy in a person's real
> property without prior written authorization from the owner** of the real property; or **2.** **Deliver
> possession prior to closing unless expressly instructed to do so by the owner** of the property or
> property interest being transferred."*
>
> **(K)** *"A licensee shall **recommend to a client that the client seek appropriate counsel from
> insurance, legal, tax, and accounting professionals regarding the risks of pre-possession or
> post-possession** of a property."*

**These are the rules behind the early-move-in request**, which arises in most markets and which Arizona
addresses directly.

**(J)(1) reaches ACCESS, not only occupancy**, and it requires **prior WRITTEN authorization from the
owner.** Letting a buyer's contractor in to measure, before closing, without the seller's written
authorisation, is within the prohibition.

**(J)(2) requires the owner's EXPRESS instruction** to deliver possession before closing.

**(K) then requires the licensee to send the client to insurance, legal, tax and accounting professionals**
about the risks — because pre-possession raises questions about who insures the property, who is liable for
an injury, and what happens if the transaction does not close.

### E.3 Prorations, and where a licensee stops

Items paid in advance or in arrears are **prorated** as of closing — property taxes, association
assessments, rents and deposits, interest.

**A proration is always a double entry.** Whatever is credited to one party is debited to the other. **A
prorated item appearing once has been done wrong.**

**Which party owns the day of closing, and whether a 360-day or 365-day convention applies, are matters of
contract and local practice. This course states neither as an Arizona rule**, because the statutes and rules
read for this project state neither. Practice problems specify the convention inside the problem.

**And the licensee does not conduct the escrow, prepare the deed, or opine on title.** **R4-28-1101(H)**
requires engaging someone competent outside your field, and **(K)** requires recommending counsel.
