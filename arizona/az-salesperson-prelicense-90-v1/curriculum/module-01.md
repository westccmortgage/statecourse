## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish real from personal property and apply the fixture tests.
2. Identify the bundle of rights and the four government powers that limit it.
3. Distinguish freehold from leasehold estates.
4. Distinguish the forms of concurrent ownership, including Arizona's community property character.
5. Distinguish liens from non-lien encumbrances.
6. Connect each to R4-28-1101(H) and (K) — the competence limit and the duty to recommend counsel.

## A. Real and personal property

**Land** is the surface, what lies beneath, and the airspace above. **Real property** is land plus what is
permanently attached plus the rights running with it. **Personal property** is everything else.

The distinction decides what conveys, and **A.R.S. § 32-2124(E)(2)** names **bills of sale** among the
forms an applicant must understand — the instrument by which personal property moves.

### A.1 Fixtures

Personal property that has become real property by attachment. The tests: **method of annexation**;
**adaptation** to this property; the **relationship of the parties**; and **intention**, usually
controlling and read from the objective circumstances.

**The practical answer is not the doctrine — it is the paperwork.** Name in writing what stays and what
goes, item by item, before anyone signs. Every fixture dispute a licensee ever sees was preventable by a
sentence.

**Trade fixtures** are articles a **tenant** attaches for a **business**; they remain the tenant's and are
removable **before the term ends**, after which they generally pass to the landlord by **accession**.

## B. The bundle of rights, and what limits it

**Possession. Use. Exclusion. Disposition. Encumbrance.** The sticks can be sold separately: a landowner
who grants a utility easement has sold one; one who leases has transferred possession and kept the rest;
one who grants a deed of trust has pledged, not conveyed.

### B.1 The four government powers

- **Police power** — regulation for public health, safety and welfare: zoning, building codes, subdivision
  regulation. **No compensation**, because nothing is taken.
- **Eminent domain** — taking for public use, **with just compensation**, through **condemnation**.
- **Taxation.**
- **Escheat.**

**The compensation line is the distinction to hold.**

### B.2 Appurtenant rights, and the Arizona question a licensee must never guess at

Rights running with the land: **air rights**, **subsurface and mineral rights**, **water rights**, and
**appurtenant easements**.

**Water deserves its own paragraph in Arizona.** Whether a parcel has an assured or adequate water supply,
whether it sits inside an Active Management Area, and what a buyer of unsubdivided land must be told about
water are questions governed by **Arizona statutes this project did not read** — including the groundwater
and water adequacy provisions and the **Affidavit of Disclosure** required for certain unsubdivided land
sales.

**This course states no water rule, no disclosure requirement and no exemption from any of them.** A
licensee who tells a buyer a rural parcel "has water" has made a representation they cannot support — and
**R4-28-1101(H)** is the rule that governs it:

> *"A licensee **shall not undertake to provide professional services concerning a type of property or
> service that is outside the licensee's field of competence without engaging the assistance of a person
> who is competent** to provide those services, **unless the licensee's lack of expertise is first disclosed
> to the client in writing and the client subsequently employs the licensee**."*

**Read the exception carefully.** A licensee may proceed outside their competence **only** if the lack of
expertise is **disclosed to the client in writing first** and the client **then** employs them. Otherwise
the rule requires **engaging someone competent.**

## C. Estates in land

### C.1 Freehold

**Fee simple absolute** — perpetual, inheritable, freely transferable, subject to the government powers and
to whatever encumbers it.

**Fee simple defeasible** — a fee that can end on a stated condition. **Determinable** ends
**automatically** (language of duration: *so long as*, *while*, *during*), with a possibility of reverter.
**Subject to a condition subsequent** gives the grantor a **right of re-entry** that must be exercised
(language of condition: *but if*, *provided that*, *on condition that*).

**A licensee's job is to notice the language and route it**, not to construe it.

**Life estate** — measured by a life; the life tenant may possess and use and must avoid **waste**; the
estate then passes to a **remainderman** or **reverts**. A life estate **pur autre vie** is measured by
another's life.

### C.2 Leasehold

| Estate | How it ends |
|---|---|
| **Estate for years** | Definite beginning, definite end. **Terminates on its own terms; no notice required.** |
| **Periodic tenancy** | Renews period to period **until proper notice**. |
| **Tenancy at will** | Continues at the pleasure of both parties. |
| **Tenancy at sufferance** | A holdover the landlord never agreed to. |

**Arizona's notice periods are in the Arizona Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, A.R.S. Title 33, which
this project did not read. No notice period is stated in this course.**

**A lease is two things at once** — a **conveyance** of a possessory interest for a term **and a contract**
of covenants. That is why a lease dispute can be framed either way, and why the **covenant of quiet
enjoyment** matters as much as any written clause.

**Assignment** transfers the tenant's **entire** remaining interest; a **sublease** transfers **less than
all** of it. **In both cases the original tenant generally remains liable unless expressly released.**

## D. Concurrent ownership — and Arizona's own character

**Tenancy in common.** Undivided interests that may be **unequal**, **no survivorship**, each co-tenant may
generally transfer or encumber their own share and seek **partition**.

**Joint tenancy.** The four unities — **possession, interest, time, title** — with a **right of
survivorship**. Severing a unity converts the severed share to a tenancy in common.

### D.1 Arizona is a community property state

**Property acquired during marriage is presumptively community property in Arizona.** That single fact
changes several questions a licensee meets weekly:

- **Who must sign** a listing, a contract, or a deed.
- **What the title report shows**, and whether a separate-property characterisation is documented.
- **How the interest passes** on death, and what a will can and cannot do with it.

> **What this course does NOT state.** **Arizona's community property statutes were not read for this
> project.** This module states **no rule about what is community property, what is separate property, how
> either is characterised, how it may be conveyed, or how it passes at death.** It states **that Arizona is
> a community property state** — the fact a licensee needs in order to ask the right question — and routes
> the answer to **the title company and to counsel.**
>
> **A licensee who guesses at community property characterisation has guessed at whether the seller can
> convey.**

**Ownership in trust, by entity, and in common-interest communities** — condominium and planned community
forms — are further structures governed by **A.R.S. Title 33, which was not read for this project.**

### D.2 And the rule that tells you what to do about it

**R4-28-1101(K):**

> *"A licensee shall **recommend to a client that the client seek appropriate counsel from insurance,
> legal, tax, and accounting professionals** regarding the risks of pre-possession or post-possession of a
> property."*

**Read alongside (H)**, the pattern is clear: **Arizona expects a licensee to know the boundary of their own
competence and to bring in someone who has the answer.**

## E. Encumbrances

### E.1 Liens

**Deed of trust** — Arizona's dominant security instrument, and a **three-party** arrangement (trustor,
trustee, beneficiary) permitting **trustee's sale** rather than judicial foreclosure. **A.R.S. Title 33's
deed of trust provisions were not read for this project**, and **no notice period, timeline, reinstatement
right or deficiency rule is stated in this course.**

**Property tax lien** — statutory, involuntary, generally superior in priority. **Mechanics' and
materialmen's lien** — **Arizona's lien statute was not read; no deadline, threshold or priority rule is
stated.** **Judgment lien.** **Special assessments.**

**R4-28-1101(B)(4)** makes one of these a disclosure item in its own right:

> *"**The existence of a lien or encumbrance on the property being transferred.**"*

**Disclosed in writing, to all other parties.** Not merely to the client.

### E.2 Non-lien encumbrances

**Easement** — an interest in land; **appurtenant** easements run with the land, easements **in gross**
benefit a person or entity. **Licence** — revocable permission, **not** an interest in land.
**Encroachment** — found by a **survey**, not by a fence or a wall. **Restrictive covenant** — a private
limitation running with the land, commonly through a **declaration of covenants, conditions and
restrictions** in a planned community.

**A restrictive covenant is private and enforced privately; zoning is public and enforced by the
jurisdiction. Where the two differ, the more restrictive controls what the owner may actually do** — and a
licensee who checked only the zoning has checked half of it.
