# Arizona Real Estate Salesperson Prelicense — Module AZ-12

## Arizona broker supervision, employment and advertising

**Course:** Real Estate Salesperson Prelicense · **Instructional hours: 7.0** of 90.0
**Arizona-specific:** ★ **YES** — this module is part of the **27-hour Arizona-specific course** that
**A.R.S. § 32-2124(B)** and **A.A.C. R4-28-401(A)(2)** provide **may not be waived.**
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No Arizona prelicensure credit is available.

> ### Why a salesperson studies the broker's rules
>
> Module AZ-11 delivered the duties **you** owe. This module delivers the rules that decide **who is
> answerable for what you do** — and they matter to a salesperson for a blunt reason stated in the rule
> itself: **A.A.C. R4-28-1103(D)** provides that *"an employing broker is responsible for the acts of all
> licensees and other employees acting"* in the brokerage.
>
> A salesperson works **inside** a supervision system the designated broker is required to build. Two
> practical consequences follow. **First**, the brokerage's written policies are not office preference — the
> rule **requires** them, and the broker's failure to enforce them is itself a violation. **Second**, the
> single activity a salesperson is most likely to conduct alone — **advertising** — is one the rule assigns
> to the designated broker's responsibility: **R4-28-502(G)**, *"the designated broker is responsible for
> the advertising of all real estate activity."*
>
> **A.A.C. R4-28-1103, R4-28-502 and R4-28-503** were read in the **2026 ADRE Law Book** on **2026-08-20**.
> Every quoted passage below is transcribed from that reading.

## Learning objectives

1. State the standard R4-28-1103(A) imposes on an employing and a designated broker.
2. List the areas the rule says written policies, procedures and systems must cover.
3. State what R4-28-1103(B) requires a designated broker's compliance-monitoring system to include, and
   what happens if it is not enforced.
4. State the rule on responsibility for the acts of licensees and employees.
5. State the advertising requirements in R4-28-502 that a salesperson performs personally.
6. State the "owner/agent" rule and the rule on the word "acre."
7. State what R4-28-502(J) provides about electronic media, artificial intelligence and scrolling.
8. State the prohibitions in R4-28-503 on premiums, prizes and games of chance.

## A. Supervision: A.A.C. R4-28-1103

### A.1 The standard — subsection (A)

> **(A)**: An employing broker and a designated broker shall exercise **reasonable supervision and control**,
> which **includes the establishment and enforcement of written policies, procedures, and systems** to
> review and manage —

| The rule names | Which in practice means |
|---|---|
| **Transactions of all licensees** | Every file, not the difficult ones only |
| **Use of disclosure forms and contracts** | Which forms the brokerage uses and how they are completed |
| **Filing, storing and maintaining documents** | Where the file lives and for how long |
| **Handling of trust funds** | Money that is not the brokerage's |
| **Use of unlicensed assistants** | What an unlicensed person may and may not do |
| **Advertising** | Everything in Part B of this module |

— and, further, to **oversee delegation of authority**; to **familiarize licensees with federal, state and
local law**; and to **review and inspect** documents that may have a material effect on a party's rights or
obligations, **and advertising and marketing by licensees in the broker's employ.**

**Read two words carefully.** The rule says **"establishment and enforcement."** A written policy that
exists and is not enforced does not satisfy (A) — establishment is half of the requirement.

And the policies must be **written.** The rule does not treat an understood office practice as a policy.

**"Familiarize licensees with federal, state and local law"** is a supervision duty owed to *you*. It does
not relieve you of the duties in Module AZ-11 — R4-28-1101 runs to the licensee — but it tells you
something about how a properly run Arizona brokerage is supposed to operate, and gives you a legitimate
expectation to raise when it does not.

### A.2 The monitoring system and the progressive disciplinary policy — subsection (B)

> **(B)**: A designated broker shall establish a system for monitoring compliance that ***"must include a
> progressive disciplinary policy for managing violations of the employing broker's policies which would
> also represent a violation of any statutory requirement"*** — and **failure to enforce it is itself a
> violation.**

Three separate requirements, and licensees routinely notice only the first:

1. **A system for monitoring compliance** must exist.
2. That system **must include a progressive disciplinary policy** — aimed specifically at violations of the
   brokerage's own policies **that would also be statutory violations.**
3. **Failure to enforce it is itself a violation.** The rule closes the obvious gap: a brokerage cannot
   satisfy (B) by writing a disciplinary policy it never applies.

**"Progressive."** The rule uses the word and does not define it, and **this module does not supply a
definition, a number of steps, or a sequence of sanctions** — none is stated in the rule text read.

### A.3 Responsibility for the acts of others — subsection (D)

> **(D)**: *"An employing broker is **responsible for the acts of all licensees and other employees
> acting**"* in the brokerage.

**"And other employees."** The responsibility is not limited to licensees. Unlicensed staff are inside it,
which is why (A) requires a written policy on the **use of unlicensed assistants.**

**What this does not mean.** Broker responsibility under (D) does not remove the salesperson's own duties.
R4-28-1101 imposes them on the **licensee**, and Module AZ-11 works through them. **(D) adds a second
person who answers; it does not subtract the first.** A salesperson who assumes that a broker's
responsibility is a personal shield has misread the relationship between the two rules.

**Where (C) of R4-28-1101 fits.** Recall that when a licensee cannot perform an act required by rule or
statute, R4-28-1101(C) requires the licensee to **expeditiously notify the designated broker.** That duty
and this supervision system are the same machinery seen from the two ends.

## B. Advertising: A.A.C. R4-28-502

This is the part of the module a salesperson uses weekly, and the part where a licensee acting alone can
breach a rule in a single post.

### B.1 The two rules about who is behind the offer

> **(A)**: A licensee **shall not advertise property in a manner that implies that no salesperson or broker
> is taking part** in the offer.

The classic breach is the advertisement written to look like a private sale. **If a licensee is involved,
the advertisement may not imply otherwise.**

> **(B)**: A licensee advertising **their own or another licensee's property** shall **disclose they are
> licensed** and, **as the property owner, place the words "owner/agent" in the advertisement.**

**Two obligations here, and they are different.** Advertising your own property **or another licensee's**
requires disclosure that you are licensed. Where you are the **property owner**, the rule prescribes the
words themselves: **"owner/agent."** The rule states that term, and this module states no substitute for it.

**Note how this pairs with Module AZ-11.** R4-28-1101(E)(1) requires written disclosure to the other
parties, before any binding agreement, that the **licensee has a license and is acting as a principal.**
R4-28-502(B) requires the disclosure **in the advertisement**, before there are any parties at all. **Two
rules, two moments, both mandatory.**

### B.2 Accuracy — subsection (C)

> **(C)**: All advertising shall contain **accurate claims and representations** and **fully state factual
> material**; a licensee **shall not misrepresent the facts or create misleading or ambiguous impressions.**

Three standards stacked: **accurate**, **fully stated**, and **not misleading or ambiguous.** The third is
the widest — an advertisement in which every individual statement is true can still create a misleading
impression, and the rule reaches that.

### B.3 Identifying the employing broker — subsection (E)

> **(E)**: All advertising shall **identify in a clear and prominent manner the employing broker's legal
> name or the dba name(s)** on the employing broker's licence certificate.

**"Clear and prominent."** Not present somewhere. And the name used must be the **legal name or a dba
appearing on the employing broker's licence certificate** — not an informal team or personal brand name
that appears on no certificate.

### B.4 The designated broker is responsible — subsection (G)

> **(G)**: *"**The designated broker is responsible for the advertising of all real estate activity.**"*

This is the sentence that connects Part B back to Part A. It is also why **R4-28-1103(A)** requires the
broker's written policies to cover advertising, and requires the broker to **review and inspect advertising
and marketing by licensees in the broker's employ.** A salesperson who publishes without the brokerage's
process has put the designated broker in breach of a rule the salesperson does not personally hold.

### B.5 The word "acre" — subsection (H)

> **(H)**: *"A licensee shall **not use the term 'acre,' either alone or modified, unless referring to an
> area of land representing at least 43,560 square feet.**"*

**"Either alone or modified"** closes the obvious workaround — "nearly an acre," "acre-plus," "a full acre"
are all inside the rule. **43,560 square feet** is the figure the rule states, and it is the figure Module
AZ-04 uses in the general land-measurement content.

### B.6 Signs and electronic publication — subsection (I)

> **(I)**: Before placing a sign or publishing to an electronic medium, a licensee shall **secure the
> written consent of the property owner**, and the sign or publication **shall be promptly removed upon
> request of the property owner.**

**Written consent before**, and **prompt removal on request.** The removal duty has no stated condition —
the owner's request is enough.

### B.7 Electronic media, artificial intelligence, and the no-scroll rule — subsection (J)

This subsection is the one most likely to be new even to an experienced licensee, and it is transcribed in
full because each clause does distinct work.

> **(J)**: *"The use of an **electronic medium, such as the Internet, artificial intelligence, or web site
> technology**, that **targets residents of this State** with the offering of a property interest or real
> estate brokerage services **constitutes the dissemination of advertising** as defined in A.R.S. §
> 32-2101(2). All advertising using an electronic medium must comply with Subsection E ... and **if
> advertising online, all requirements of advertising set forth above must be satisfied on the
> advertisement without the need to scroll.**"*

| Clause | What it settles |
|---|---|
| *"electronic medium, such as the Internet, **artificial intelligence**, or web site technology"* | The rule **names artificial intelligence** as an electronic medium. Output generated by such a medium is not outside the advertising rules. |
| *"**targets residents of this State**"* | The trigger is targeting Arizona residents — not where the licensee, the server, or the platform sits. |
| *"**constitutes the dissemination of advertising**"* | It is advertising. Every rule in R4-28-502 applies to it. |
| *"must comply with **Subsection E**"* | The employing broker's legal or certificate dba name, **clear and prominent**. |
| *"**without the need to scroll**"* | The requirements must be satisfied **on the advertisement itself**, visible without scrolling. |

**The no-scroll rule is a design requirement, not a disclosure preference.** A post that carries the
brokerage name only in a linked profile, a comment, a bio, or below the fold does not satisfy (J) as it is
written. **This is the rule short-form social posts break most often.**

## C. Promotional activities: A.A.C. R4-28-503

> A licensee **shall not** describe a premium offered at no cost or reduced cost as an ***"award"*** or
> ***"prize"*** or a similar term, and shall **clearly disclose in writing the terms, costs, conditions,
> restrictions, and expiration date** of a premium offer **before the person participates.** Unless
> otherwise provided by law, a person **shall not solicit, sell, or offer to sell an interest in a
> development by conducting a lottery contest, drawing, or game of chance.**

Three rules:

1. **Vocabulary.** A premium offered at no cost or reduced cost may not be called an **"award," a "prize,"
   or any similar term.** The rule regulates the *word*, because the word is what misleads.
2. **Written disclosure, before participation.** Five things must be disclosed **in writing** and **before
   the person participates**: **terms, costs, conditions, restrictions, and expiration date.** Missing one
   is a breach; disclosing all five afterwards is a breach.
3. **No lottery, drawing or game of chance** may be used to solicit, sell or offer to sell an interest in a
   development, **unless otherwise provided by law.**

**The exception clause is real and this module does not fill it in.** *"Unless otherwise provided by law"*
points somewhere outside the rule text read, and **no such other law was read by this project.** A licensee
proposing any promotion of this kind takes it to the designated broker and to counsel.

## D. Working the three rules together

**A salesperson wants to run a social campaign on a listing, using an image-generation tool, offering a gift
card drawing to anyone who books a showing, and identifying the listing as "just under an acre."**

| Element | Rule | Outcome under the rule as written |
|---|---|---|
| Social post targeting Arizona residents | **R4-28-502(J)** | It **is advertising**; all of R4-28-502 applies |
| Generated by an AI tool | **R4-28-502(J)** | The rule **names artificial intelligence** as an electronic medium — no exemption |
| Brokerage name in the linked bio only | **R4-28-502(E) and (J)** | Fails: the legal or certificate dba name must be **clear and prominent** and satisfied **without scrolling** |
| "Just under an acre" | **R4-28-502(H)** | Prohibited — *"either alone or modified"*, unless at least **43,560 square feet** |
| Gift card drawing | **R4-28-503** | A **drawing or game of chance** may not be used to solicit or offer to sell an interest in a development, unless otherwise provided by law |
| Calling the gift card a "prize" | **R4-28-503** | Prohibited vocabulary for a premium at no or reduced cost |
| Sign to go with the campaign | **R4-28-502(I)** | Requires the **owner's written consent first**; **prompt removal on request** |
| Nobody at the brokerage reviewed it | **R4-28-502(G)**, **R4-28-1103(A)** | The **designated broker is responsible for the advertising**, and the broker's written policies must cover advertising and require review and inspection |

**Seven rule references in one campaign.** Nothing in it is unusual, and that is the point of putting
supervision and advertising in the same module: **the salesperson generates the exposure and the broker
carries it.**
