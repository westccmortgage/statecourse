## Learning objectives

## A. Vocabulary, with the arithmetic attached

**Principal and interest. Amortization. Debt service. PITI. Equity. Loan-to-value ratio. Points. Yield.**

**Loan value is customarily the LOWER of contract price and appraised value.** That convention is why an
appraisal below contract price is a problem for the buyer: **the lender lends against the lower figure and
the difference must come from somewhere.**

**A discount point is one percent of the loan amount.**

### A.1 Worked examples, all figures given inside the problem

**Points.** A $438,000 loan with 1.25 points: 438,000 × 0.0125 = **$5,475** at closing.

**First month's split.** A $438,000 balance at 6.375 percent with a monthly payment of $2,733:
interest = 438,000 × 0.06375 ÷ 12 = **$2,326.88**; principal reduction = 2,733 − 2,326.88 = **$406.12**;
new balance = **$437,593.88.**

**Interest is always computed on the PREVIOUS balance**, and only the remainder touches principal.

**Loan-to-value.** Contract price $565,000, appraised value $548,000, loan $438,400. The lender recognises
**$548,000** — the lower — so LTV = 438,400 ÷ 548,000 = **80 percent**, and the buyer covers the **$17,000**
gap from their own funds.

## B. The note and the security instrument

**The promissory note** is the borrower's **promise to repay** — a contract, to which everything in Module
AZ-04 applies. **The security instrument pledges the property.** **Hypothecation** is the idea that ties
them: the borrower pledges the property **while keeping possession of it.**

### B.1 Deed of trust — Arizona's dominant instrument

| | **Mortgage** | **Deed of trust — Arizona's usual instrument** |
|---|---|---|
| Parties | **2** | **3** |
| Who they are | Mortgagor, Mortgagee | **Trustor, Trustee, Beneficiary** |
| Enforcement | **Judicial foreclosure** | **Trustee's sale** — non-judicial |

**The third party is what makes the shortcut possible.** A **trustee** holds the security for the
beneficiary's benefit and can conduct a **trustee's sale** without a lawsuit.

**A.R.S. Title 33's deed of trust provisions were NOT read for this project.** **This course states no
notice period, no timeline, no reinstatement right, no redemption right and no deficiency rule.** A licensee
asked about a trustee's sale timeline routes the question — to counsel, and to the trustee's own notices.

**A.R.S. § 32-2124(E)(2)** names **mortgages, deeds of trust and security agreements** among the forms an
applicant must understand the **general purpose and legal effect of.** That is the standard here.

### B.2 Special note provisions

- **Acceleration clause** — on default the **whole balance** becomes due at once.
- **Prepayment penalty clause.**
- **Due-on-sale (alienation) clause** — the balance becomes due if the property is sold. **This is why
  assumption is rare.**

### B.3 Priority and release

**First**, **second** and further liens rank by **recording**, subject to statutory priorities such as
property tax liens. **Subordination** is the deliberate exception.

**And the release matters.** A deed of trust paid but **never released of record is still a cloud on
title** — Module AZ-03 met the same idea from the title side, and **R4-28-1101(B)(4)** makes **the existence
of a lien or encumbrance** a written disclosure item to **all other parties.**

## C. Loan programmes and repayment types

**Conventional** — not government insured or guaranteed; **conforming** loans meet secondary-market
standards; **private mortgage insurance (PMI)** typically appears at higher loan-to-value.

**FHA-insured** — FHA **does not build housing and does not lend**; it **insures approved lenders against
loss**; borrowers pay a **mortgage insurance premium (MIP)**.

**VA-guaranteed** — the VA **guarantees** approved lenders for eligible veterans; a **funding fee** applies;
the defining feature is the possibility of **no down payment.** **A VA release of liability does not by
itself release the original borrower from liability to the lender.**

**USDA Rural Development** — direct and guaranteed loans in eligible areas.

**Arizona also has state and local down-payment assistance and bond programmes.** **No programme document
was read for this project**, and **no eligibility limit, income cap, purchase price limit or assistance
amount is stated in this course.** They are named so a licensee knows to ask a lender.

**By repayment:** **fixed-rate level payment (fully amortized)**; **adjustable rate (ARM)** with an
**index**, a **margin**, a **periodic cap** and a **lifetime cap** — and note that **a cap on the PAYMENT is
not a cap on the INTEREST**, so a payment cap can produce **negative amortization**; **interest-only /
term**; **balloon**; **graduated payment**; **buydown**. **By purpose:** **construction**, **blanket** (with
a **partial release** clause), **package**, **open-end**, **bridge**.

**Seller financing** — a **purchase money deed of trust** or an **agreement for sale.** **A.R.S. § 32-2124(E)(2)**
names **land contracts of sale** among the forms an applicant must understand. **Arizona's law on agreements
for sale was not read for this project**, and a licensee whose transaction involves one **routes it to
counsel, without exception** — **R4-28-1101(H)** requires engaging someone competent outside your field.

## D. Primary and secondary markets, and federal regulation in outline

The **primary market** is where a loan is **made**; the **secondary market** is where a loan already made is
**bought** — **Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac and Ginnie Mae** — through **loan pooling** and **mortgage-backed
securities.** **The link is conforming standards**: a lender writes to them so the loan can be sold, and
selling it returns capital to lend again.

**TILA and Regulation Z** govern disclosure of the cost of consumer credit and the **advertising** of credit
terms. The **APR** folds points and certain fees into a single annualised rate — **it is not the interest
rate**, and it is normally higher.

**RESPA and Regulation X** govern settlement services and prohibit **kickbacks and referral fees** among
settlement service providers. **TRID** consolidated the two sets of disclosures into the **Loan Estimate**
and the **Closing Disclosure**, each with timing requirements.

> **None of those regulations was read for this project.** **This course states no disclosure deadline, no
> tolerance category, no trigger term list and no penalty.** Every live deadline goes to the lender.
>
> **What a licensee should carry away about advertising is the safe practice: publish no numbers other than
> the price.** A listing that says *"$2,850/month!"* has entered territory Regulation Z governs. And
> **R4-28-502(C)** independently requires **accurate claims**, **full statement of factual material**, and no
> **misleading or ambiguous impressions.**

### D.1 And an Arizona compensation rule that sits beside RESPA

**R4-28-1101(G):**

> *"A licensee shall **not accept any compensation, including rebate or other consideration, directly or
> indirectly, for any goods or services provided to a person if the goods or services are related to or
> result from a real estate transaction, without that person's PRIOR WRITTEN ACKNOWLEDGEMENT** of the
> compensation. **This prohibition does not apply to compensation paid to a broker by a broker who
> represents a party in the transaction.**"*

**Two bodies of law, two questions.** RESPA asks whether a payment among settlement service providers is
lawful. **R4-28-1101(G) asks whether the person got prior written acknowledgement** — and it reaches
**rebates and other consideration**, **direct or indirect**, for **any** related goods or services. The only
carve-out is **broker-to-broker.**

## E. What a licensee may and may not say

**"You'll qualify." "That'll appraise." "Rates are coming down before your lock expires."** Each is a
**representation.**

**R4-28-1101(H)** and **(I)** together set the standard: **obtain information material to the client's
interests, communicate it accurately, help the client confirm it** — and **do not undertake services outside
your field of competence** without engaging someone competent or disclosing the lack of expertise **in
writing** first.

**The reliable formulation is three words: ask your lender.**
