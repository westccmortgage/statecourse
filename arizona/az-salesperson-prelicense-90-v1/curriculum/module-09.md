## A. Leasehold estates and lease types

| Estate | How it ends |
|---|---|
| **Estate for years** | Definite beginning and end. **Terminates on its own terms; no notice required.** |
| **Periodic tenancy** | Renews period to period **until proper notice**. |
| **Tenancy at will** | Continues at the pleasure of both parties. |
| **Tenancy at sufferance** | A holdover the landlord never agreed to. |

**Arizona's notice periods are in A.R.S. Title 33 and are not stated in this course.** A manager who guesses
at one has guessed at whether a tenancy ended.

**Lease types by rent structure:** **gross** (landlord pays operating expenses); **net** (tenant pays rent
plus some or all of taxes, insurance and maintenance — *single, double, triple net*); **percentage** (a
share of gross sales above a breakpoint); **ground** (land leased for the tenant to build on); **index or
graduated.**

**Worked example — percentage lease.** *Base rent $5,100 monthly; 4 percent of gross sales above a $95,000
monthly breakpoint. In a month with $164,000 of sales:* overage = 164,000 − 95,000 = **$69,000**; percentage
rent = 69,000 × 0.04 = **$2,760**; total = 5,100 + 2,760 = **$7,860.**

## B. Property management

**Functions:** market and screen (**under fair housing law, with written criteria applied identically to
every applicant, and documented** — Module AZ-08); collect rent and enforce the lease; maintain; account to
the owner; keep records; handle deposits; serve notices correctly and on time.

**The management agreement should settle:** the parties and property; **the scope of authority**; the term;
compensation; **the owner's approval thresholds for expenditure**; reporting and accounting; the handling of
tenant funds and deposits; insurance; indemnity; and termination.

**An unwritten scope of authority is how a manager ends up having spent an owner's money on something the
owner did not authorise.** Set the threshold in dollars, in the agreement.

**And two Arizona rules bear directly on management.** **R4-28-1101(J)(1)** — no permitting or facilitating
**access to or occupancy in** a person's real property **without prior written authorization from the
owner.** **R4-28-1103(A)(1)(d)** — the employing and designated broker's written policies must cover **the
handling of trust funds.**

### B.1 The operating statement

**Potential gross income**, less **vacancy and collection loss**, plus **other income** = **effective gross
income**; less **operating expenses** = **net operating income.**

**Debt service is not an operating expense.** And **omitting a replacement reserve overstates NOI and
therefore overstates value.**

**Worked example, all figures given.** *A 36-unit building; market rent $1,425 per unit per month; vacancy
and collection loss 7 percent; other income $2,100 per month; operating expenses $268,000 annually;
management fee 6 percent of effective gross income; annual debt service $341,000.*

Potential gross income = 36 × 1,425 × 12 = **$615,600.**
Vacancy and collection loss = 615,600 × 0.07 = **$43,092.**
Other income = 2,100 × 12 = **$25,200.**
Effective gross income = 615,600 − 43,092 + 25,200 = **$597,708.**
Management fee = 597,708 × 0.06 = **$35,862.48** *(charged on EGI, not on potential gross — a manager paid
on collections is paid on what came in).*
Net operating income = 597,708 − 268,000 = **$329,708.**
Before-tax cash flow = 329,708 − 341,000 = **−$11,292** *(a negative, and a real one).*

*(**This course states no management fee rate**, because compensation is negotiated and no course sets it.)*

## C. Real estate mathematics

### C.1 Area and the Arizona "acre" rule

**1 acre = 43,560 square feet. 1 section = 640 acres = 1 square mile. 1 township = 36 sections. 1 square
yard = 9 square feet.**

**Rectangle = base × height. Triangle = ½ × base × height. Trapezoid = ½ × (base₁ + base₂) × height.** An
irregular parcel with straight sides is **decomposed**, each piece computed and added.

**Worked example.** *A lot is a rectangle 95 ft × 160 ft with a right-triangular extension 95 ft × 48 ft.*
Rectangle = **15,200 sq ft.** Triangle = ½ × 95 × 48 = **2,280 sq ft.** Total = **17,480 sq ft** =
17,480 ÷ 43,560 = **0.40 acres.**

**And R4-28-502(H) applies to how you describe it:** *"A licensee shall **not use the term 'acre,' either
alone or modified, unless referring to an area of land representing at least 43,560 square feet**."* **A
17,480-square-foot lot may not be advertised as "almost half an acre" using the word "acre" — describe it as
0.40 acres, which is accurate, or in square feet.**

### C.2 Commissions and net to seller

**This course states no commission rate, split or minimum.** Every problem supplies its rate.

*Sale price $712,000; the seller's agreement provides 5.5 percent to the listing brokerage, shared evenly
with the selling brokerage; the licensee's split with the employing broker is 65/35 in the licensee's
favour.*
Total = 712,000 × 0.055 = **$39,160.** Each brokerage = **$19,580.** The licensee = 19,580 × 0.65 =
**$12,727.**

**Price needed to NET a required sum** — algebra, not arithmetic, because the commission is a percentage
**of the unknown price.**

*A seller must net $340,000 after a 5.5 percent commission and $6,200 of other costs, with no loan payoff.*
**P − 0.055P − 6,200 = 340,000**, so **0.945P = 346,200**, and **P = $366,349.21.**
**Check:** 366,349.21 × 0.055 = 20,149.21; 366,349.21 − 20,149.21 − 6,200 = **$340,000.** ✓

**The classic error is to add 5.5 percent to $346,200**, which gives $365,241 — short by more than $1,100.

### C.3 Prorations

**Two conventions, and every problem must say which it uses: 365-day** (actual days) and **360-day /
30-day-month.** **Two rules that never change: a proration is always a DOUBLE ENTRY**, and **whichever party
owns the day of closing must be stated.** **This course states neither convention nor a settlement-date rule
as Arizona law**, because the statutes and rules read for this project state neither.

**Worked example — taxes paid in advance, 360-day method, seller owns the day of closing.** *Annual property
tax $4,320, paid in full by the seller for the year. Closing 21 August.*
Daily = 4,320 ÷ 360 = **$12.** Monthly = **$360.**
Seller's period = 1 January through 21 August = 7 months + 21 days = 2,520 + 252 = **$2,772.**
Buyer's share = 4,320 − 2,772 = **$1,548.**
**The seller prepaid the whole year, so the buyer reimburses: credit the seller $1,548 and debit the buyer
$1,548.** Double entry. ✓

### C.4 The settlement statement

A **debit** is what a party **owes**; a **credit** is what a party **has coming.**

For the **buyer**: the **purchase price is a debit**; the **earnest money** and the **loan amount** are
**credits.**
For the **seller**: the **purchase price is a credit**; the **loan payoff** and the **commission** are
**debits.**

**Cash to close (buyer) = buyer's debits − buyer's credits.**
**Net to seller = seller's credits − seller's debits.**

**Worked example.** *Price $565,000; earnest money $15,000 in escrow; new loan $452,000; buyer's lender and
escrow charges $9,400; seller's commission 5.5 percent; seller's loan payoff $288,600; seller's escrow and
other charges $2,150; the tax proration above credits the seller $1,548 and debits the buyer $1,548.*

**Buyer:** debits 565,000 + 9,400 + 1,548 = **$575,948**; credits 15,000 + 452,000 = **$467,000**;
**cash to close = $108,948.**
**Seller:** credits 565,000 + 1,548 = **$566,548**; debits (565,000 × 0.055) 31,075 + 288,600 + 2,150 =
**$321,825**; **net to seller = $244,723.**

**Check the double entry:** the tax proration appears **once on each side**, both $1,548. ✓
