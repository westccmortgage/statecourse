**Courses:** "Principles of real estate" (30 hours) and "Kansas practice course" (30 hours)
**Control date:** August 19, 2026
**Source policy:** The Kansas Statutes Annotated, the Kansas Administrative Regulations, and current Kansas Real Estate Commission forms control. Federal material uses official federal sources. Commercial exam-prep material and competitor outlines establish nothing.
**Status:** Research complete on the rules reachable from this session. **The curriculum cannot be written yet.** See "The blocker" below.

## The blocker, stated first because it governs everything else

**K.A.R. 86-1-11(a):** "Each school offering a course approved by the commission under K.S.A. 58-3046a(a), and amendments thereto, **shall use a course outline provided by the commission** and shall use the title **'principles of real estate.'**"

**K.A.R. 86-1-11(c):** the same, for the course under K.S.A. 58-3046a(c), titled **"Kansas practice course."**

Kansas does not invite a provider to propose an outline and defend it. It does not publish a topic-and-hour table in the regulation the way Alaska does at 12 AAC 64.063(c). **It hands the school an outline document and requires the school to use it** — and that document is not part of the regulation, is not in the Kansas Register, and is not reachable from this session. The Commission's own site returned HTTP 403 to every request from this environment on 2026-08-19.

**Consequence: no Kansas curriculum can be written until the Commission's outlines for both courses are in hand.** Writing 60 hours of instruction against a guessed outline would manufacture a structure the rule does not authorise, and would very likely be discarded. That is the opposite of how this project works, and it is not done here.

**What must be obtained, and from whom.** The Commission's education contact is published as kreceducation@ks.gov. Three documents, and one answer:

1. **The commission-provided course outline for "principles of real estate"** — K.A.R. 86-1-11(a).
2. **The commission-provided course outline for the "Kansas practice course"** — K.A.R. 86-1-11(c).
3. **The commission's approved certificate-of-completion form** — K.A.R. 86-1-17(b)(1) requires schools to use "certificate forms approved by the commission", and the roster format under 86-1-17(c)(1) is likewise "a format approved by the commission".
4. **An answer on the school-eligibility route** — see "The second regulator" below.

Until item 1 and item 2 arrive, the Kansas build is research and engineering, not authorship.

## What is settled, from primary text

### The education requirement — K.S.A. 58-3046a

Read from the Kansas Office of Revisor of Statutes on 2026-08-19.

**Salesperson, two courses, 60 hours:**

| Subsection | Course | Hours | Recency |
|---|---|---:|---|
| (a) | A **principles of real estate** course | not less than 30 | completed within the **12 months** immediately preceding the commission's receipt of the application — or, if evidence goes to the testing service, within the 12 months before the **examination** |
| (c) | A **Kansas real estate practice** course | not less than 30 | completed within the **six months** immediately preceding the commission's receipt of the application |

The two windows are different lengths and run to different events. A student who takes both courses on the same schedule can satisfy (a) and miss (c).

**Broker, for completeness:** (b) a Kansas real estate fundamentals course, not less than 30 and **no more than 45** hours, within 12 months; (d) a Kansas real estate management course, not less than 30 and no more than 45 hours, within six months, and additional to the hours used for (b), (c), (e) or (f).

**K.S.A. 58-3046a(i):** the commission **may** approve distance education courses consisting solely or primarily of instruction provided online or in other computer-assisted formats, or by correspondence, audiotape, videotape or other media. And: **"attendance of one hour of instruction shall mean 50 minutes of classroom instruction or the equivalent thereof in distance education study as determined by the commission."**

**K.S.A. 58-3046a(h)** is the delegation behind K.A.R. 86-1-11: the commission shall adopt regulations to prescribe **minimum curricula and standards** for all courses, designate a course of study to fulfil any specific requirement (**which may include a testing requirement**), prescribe minimum instructor qualifications, and establish standards and procedures for approval, monitoring, advertising, registration, records, and withdrawal of approval.

**K.S.A. 58-3046a(j):** courses may be approved **either before or after** completion.
**K.S.A. 58-3046a(k):** the commission publishes a list of approved courses.
**K.S.A. 58-3046a(l):** no license issues or renews unless the requirements are met **within the time prescribed**.

### The second regulator — K.S.A. 58-3046a(g)

Courses required by the section must be approved by the commission **and offered by** one of six kinds of entity:

1. an institution **accredited** by a USDE-recognised accrediting agency in the department's database;
2. a **technical college** as defined by K.S.A. 74-32,407;
3. **a private or out-of-state postsecondary educational institution that has been issued a certificate of approval pursuant to the Kansas private and out-of-state postsecondary educational institution act**;
4. any **agency of the State of Kansas**;
5. a **similar institution, approved by the commission, in another state**; or
6. **an entity, approved by the commission, to provide continuing education.**

Route (3) is the second regulator the screening pass flagged, now confirmed at statute rather than inferred: it requires a certificate of approval under the Kansas private and out-of-state postsecondary educational institution act, administered outside the Real Estate Commission.

**Route (6) is the open question, and it is the cheap one.** On its face, an entity the commission approves to provide continuing education is eligible to offer the courses "required by this section" — which includes the prelicense courses — without the postsecondary certificate. **Whether the commission reads (g)(6) that way is the question to put to KREC before anything else**, because the answer decides whether Kansas costs one approval or two.

Route (5) is also worth asking about, since StateCourse is an out-of-state provider: "a similar institution, approved by the commission, in another state" may become available once StateCourse holds an approval elsewhere.

### Course approval procedure — K.A.R. 86-1-10

Read from the **Kansas Register, vol. 43 no. 50 (12 December 2024)**, which carries the operative amended text effective 27 December 2024. This is newer than third-party mirrors and controls.

**Definitions — 86-1-10(a):**
- **"Asynchronous distance education course"** — a course for which the school provides **self-paced** instruction by mail or electronic transmission to students **physically separated from the instructor** for all or a portion of the course.
- **"Synchronous distance education course"** — **live instruction in real time** by electronic transmission to students physically separated from the instructor for all of the course.
- **"In-person education course"** — students not physically separated from the instructor.
- **"Monitoring"** — review of approved courses by commission staff to ensure that the attendance, presentation platform, instruction time, outline, and materials meet the commission's requirements.
- **"School"** — an entity eligible under K.S.A. 58-3046a(g).
- **"Coordinator"** — the individual who is the school's primary contact and is responsible for complying with the regulation.

Asynchronous is a defined, separately approvable delivery type. Nothing has to be argued into existence.

**Every course — 86-1-10(b).** Submitted to the commission **at least 45 days before the first scheduled class session**: the completed application; **a copy of all course materials, including textbooks, student workbooks, and examinations with answers**; the total number of sessions, sections, or modules; the duration of each; the total hours requested; the course objectives and a detailed course outline; and the fee.

Note the second item. Kansas requires **the examinations with their answers** to be filed. That is a disclosure to the regulator, not to students, and it does not disturb the rule that answer keys never reach a student payload — but it does mean the bank must exist in a form that can be handed over.

**Asynchronous courses additionally — 86-1-10(c)(1):**

- (A) the **means to access** the course as it will be offered to students;
- (B) evidence of **sufficient information technology support** to enable students to complete it;
- (C) documentation of how the course will require **active participation by each student and substantial interaction between the students and the instructor, other students, or a computer program**; and
- (D) **evidence that the system used for testing students will scramble questions and items for any quizzes or examinations to ensure a random presentation.**

**86-1-10(c)(2):** an asynchronous course **certified by ARELLO is presumed** to meet (c)(1). A presumption, not a requirement — which distinguishes Kansas from Idaho, Mississippi and Montana.

**86-1-10(c)(4):** a prelicense course under (a), (b), (c) or (d) shall require each student to answer **at least 50 quiz or examination questions**.

**86-1-10(c)(5):** the school **shall issue a certificate of completion** to each student **"who has answered at least 90 percent of the quiz or examination questions correctly during the distance education course."**

**86-1-10(f) — changes.** A **new application** is required for any significant change to course content, outline, objectives, or presentation platform. A new application is **not** required for a change of coordinator, school location, course title, or schedule — but the commission must be notified **at least five days before** such a change, and **at least 15 days before** discontinuing a course or closing the school.

**86-1-10(g) — term.** Course registration **expires 31 January each year**. A renewal application received after expiry requires a **new** approval application.

**86-1-10(e) — instructors.** The school coordinator is responsible for ensuring instructors have the specialized preparation, training, and experience in the subject matter taught.

### School responsibilities — K.A.R. 86-1-17

- **(a)** The school must have each registrant verify their registration or licence number and use their name exactly as it appears on file with the commission.
- **(b)(1)** Within **five calendar days** of course completion, issue a certificate of completion, in person, electronically, or by mail, **using certificate forms approved by the commission**.
- **(b)(2)** No certificate to a student **absent for more than 10 percent** of the scheduled classroom hours for a course registered under the K.A.R. 86-1-11 titles — including "principles of real estate" and "Kansas practice course". For any **other** commission-approved course, no certificate to a student absent **during any portion** of the scheduled hours.
- **(c)(1)** Submit a **list of individuals who completed** each course, in a commission-approved format, **within five calendar days** after the completion date — or by the expiration date, where completion falls less than five days before an expiry under K.S.A. 58-3045 or K.A.R. 86-1-3.
- **(c)(2)** A roster with incorrect or incomplete information may be returned, and **no credit is entered until it is corrected**.
- **(d)** Advertising: no advertising a course as meeting the licence act's education requirements **before approval**; no advertising under a school name other than the one on the approval form; **no guaranteeing that completion will result in passing the licensing examination**; the mandatory/elective hour statement on continuing-education advertising and registration forms; and no false, misleading, or inconsistent information.
- **(e)** Records: maintain a record of each student who successfully completed an approved course **for at least three years**, and keep attendance records **current and available for inspection** by commission representatives on request.

### Monitoring — K.A.R. 86-1-12

- **(a)** Every approved course is subject to **monitoring by the commission, with or without prior notice**.
- **(b)** Approval may be **withdrawn** for falsification of attendance records or failure to comply with K.A.R. 86-1-10, 86-1-11, or 86-1-17.
- **(c)** Withdrawal during sessions in process does not affect credit for students then attending, and approval may be reinstated on satisfactory evidence that deficiencies were corrected.

### Fees — K.A.R. 86-1-5

- **(e)** Course approval under K.S.A. 58-3046a: **$75** per course provider seeking approval.
- **(f)** A licensee seeking approval of a course of instruction under 58-3046a(j): $20.

## Kansas substantive law identified for the eventual curriculum

Read and cached, so that when the Commission's outlines arrive the material can be written against primary text rather than researched from scratch.

- **K.S.A. 58-3035** — definitions, including the definition of the regulated activity at (f).
- **K.S.A. 58-3036** — licensure required.
- **K.S.A. 58-3037** — eleven exemptions, including the owner's own property, powers of attorney without compensation, attorneys, fiduciaries and court appointments, government officers, wholly-owned MLSs, nonprofit referral organisations, regulated utilities, the **5 percent ownership** employee exemption, new-home sales by the builder, and **the lease of real estate for agricultural purposes**.
- **K.S.A. 58-3039 to 58-3045** — examination, application, issuance, and expiry.
- **K.S.A. 58-3050** — renewal.
- **K.S.A. 58-3061 — trust accounts.** A separate designated trust account in Kansas, or in an adjoining state **with written permission**; all down payments, earnest money, advance listing fees and other trust funds deposited there unless all interested parties agree otherwise **in writing**; no retaining accrued interest without the **written consent of all parties**; notification to the commission by **consent-to-audit form**; full access and examination at the commission's direction; no payments out except earned commissions or distributions to beneficiaries; and **no entitlement to any part of the earnest money as commission until the transaction is consummated or terminated** unless otherwise agreed in writing. **(g):** once an offer is accepted and earnest money deposited, disbursement only on **written authorisation of buyer and seller**, a **court order**, or **closing according to the parties' agreement**.
  **(h) is distinctly Kansas**: the statute sets out, verbatim, an optional contract clause under which a party's failure to respond in writing to the broker's **certified letter within seven days**, or failure to make written demand within **30 days** of notice of cancellation, **constitutes consent** to the distribution proposed. It is a drafting answer to the deadlock (g) creates, and it belongs in the contracts material.
  **(i):** the commission may direct a broker to remit trust money held **five or more years** in a disputed or unauthorised state to the real estate recovery revolving fund.
- **K.S.A. 58-3062** — grounds for discipline.
- **K.S.A. 58-3066 to 58-3071 — the real estate recovery revolving fund.** Assessed at **$10 per broker and $5 per salesperson** whenever the balance falls below **$100,000**, with licence suspension for non-payment after 30 days and a $15 reinstatement fee. Payments are **only pursuant to a court order**; capped at **$15,000 in the aggregate for claims arising out of the same transaction**, irrespective of the number of claimants or parcels; **$30,000 within any calendar year** against any one licensee; and **$50,000 in the aggregate** against any one licensee ever. Unsatisfied claims are paid in the order made and accrue interest at **4 percent**.
- **K.S.A. 58-30,101 to 58-30,113 — the Brokerage Relationships in Real Estate Transactions Act (BRRETA).** Kansas's agency scheme, and the centrepiece of any Kansas practice course. Its definitions at 58-30,102 establish **agency**, **agency agreement**, **affiliated licensee**, **client** against **customer**, **confidential information**, **designated agent**, **exclusive agency agreement** against **exclusive right to sell agreement**, and the transaction-broker relationship. **58-30,101(b)** makes failure to comply with 58-30,103 or 58-30,110 a matter the act itself addresses.

## What Kansas requires of the engine that no earlier state did

Three of these are new, and two of them cannot be satisfied by the platform as it stands today.

**1. The passing mark is the regulator's, not the provider's.** K.A.R. 86-1-10(c)(5) sets **90 percent** as the condition for issuing the certificate. Every earlier state left the figure to the school, and StateCourse documents accordingly label 80 percent as an instructional standard. **Kansas documents must state 90 percent as the Commission's requirement and must not carry the StateCourse 80 percent language.** The catalogue needs a field that distinguishes a regulator-prescribed pass mark from a provider one, so a document generator cannot mislabel it.

**2. Questions and items must both be scrambled.** K.A.R. 86-1-10(c)(1)(D) requires evidence that the testing system **"will scramble questions and items for any quizzes or examinations to ensure a random presentation."** The platform today permutes **choices** deterministically per item id and delivers **items** in a fixed order. That is half the requirement.

The engineering constraint is that item-order randomisation must not break the property the whole audit trail rests on: **a recorded `selected_choice` must keep its meaning permanently.** It does so today because choice order is a pure function of the item id. The answer is to make presentation order a function of a **recorded per-attempt seed**, so any attempt's exact presentation can be reconstructed from the record years later. Positional answers stay meaningful because the seed that produced the positions is stored beside them.

**3. At least 50 questions.** K.A.R. 86-1-10(c)(4) sets a floor of 50 quiz or examination questions **per prelicense course**, not per assessment. A 30-hour course therefore needs a bank sized to that floor with margin, and the course configuration should assert it rather than leave it to be counted by hand.

**And one that is a filing obligation rather than an engine change:** 86-1-10(b)(2) requires **all course materials including examinations with answers** to be submitted to the commission. The answer key leaves the building — to the regulator, under a filing, and never to a student. The build guard that proves no key reaches `dist` is unaffected.

## Open questions that must be answered before filing

1. **The Commission's course outlines** for "principles of real estate" and the "Kansas practice course" — K.A.R. 86-1-11(a) and (c). **Blocking. Nothing can be written without them.**
2. **School eligibility** — whether K.S.A. 58-3046a(g)(6) admits a commission-approved continuing education entity to offer the prelicense courses, or whether the (g)(3) certificate under the Kansas private and out-of-state postsecondary educational institution act is required. **Blocking on cost, not on authorship.**
3. **The commission-approved certificate form** — K.A.R. 86-1-17(b)(1).
4. **The commission-approved roster format** — K.A.R. 86-1-17(c)(1).
5. **How the 10 percent absence rule in K.A.R. 86-1-17(b)(2) is measured for an asynchronous course.** The rule speaks of "classroom hours scheduled", which a self-paced course does not have. The platform's completion evidence is the natural analogue, but the Commission's reading should be confirmed rather than assumed.
6. **What "the equivalent thereof in distance education study as determined by the commission" means** for the 50-minute hour under K.S.A. 58-3046a(i) — whether the Commission accepts recorded active study time, and against what floor.
7. **Whether the 50-question floor in 86-1-10(c)(4) counts distinct items or answered items** on repeat attempts.

None of these is assumed anywhere in any StateCourse document.

## Change-control rule

1. Recheck K.S.A. 58-3046a and K.A.R. 86-1-5, 86-1-10, 86-1-11, 86-1-12, and 86-1-17 before submission and before every production release.
2. Recheck the **commission-provided outlines**, which can change without a regulation changing — this is Kansas's particular exposure, and it is why the outline version has to be recorded with the course.
3. Record the review date and reviewer.
4. Treat any significant change to content, outline, objectives, or presentation platform as requiring a **new application** under K.A.R. 86-1-10(f)(1), not an amendment.
5. Calendar the **31 January** registration expiry on approval, not on first offering.
