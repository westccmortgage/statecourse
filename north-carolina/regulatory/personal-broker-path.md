# Anatoliy Kanevsky — North Carolina broker and instructor path

**Prepared:** 2026-09-04  
**Known facts:** California resident since 1994; California real estate Broker license 01385024,
first licensed in 2009 and reported current through 2029; no real estate license in another state.
Current/good-standing status must still be documented in the NC application.

## Best path for the stated goal

Choose the **21 NCAC 58A .0511(b)(1) State-section route**, not the no-exam provisional route.

Rule .0511 permits an applicant whose out-of-state salesperson or broker license is current and was
active in good standing within the preceding three years to choose either:

1. pass the North Carolina **State** section and receive a North Carolina broker license on a status
   NCREC finds comparable to the qualifying out-of-state license category; or
2. take a North Carolina license on **provisional** status without that exam, then comply with the
   90-hour postlicensing requirement.

The second route defeats the immediate instructor objective because 21 NCAC 58H .0302(b)(1) requires
an instructor applicant to hold an NC broker license **not on provisional status**. The first route is
therefore the correct strategic election. It is not yet established that NCREC will map a California
Broker to a nonprovisional NC broker: the phrase “status comparable” leaves that adjudication to
NCREC. Obtain it in writing before relying on the route for instructor eligibility.

## Personal application sequence

1. Ask California DRE for a current certified license history showing license category, original
   date, current/active/good-standing status, and discipline history.
2. Obtain written NCREC confirmation that CA Broker + Rule .0511(b)(1) + a passed NC State section
   will be issued as nonprovisional NC Broker status, or identify exactly what additional proof is
   required.
3. Create the [NCREC broker application account](https://eservices.ncrec.gov/ncrecapp/Account/Logon?ReturnUrl=%2Fncrecapp%2FApplication%2FIndexApp).
4. Assemble Rule .0301 data: legal/contact identity and SSN/DOB; residence history for seven years;
   employment history for three years; all real estate/occupational license history and any
   discipline; criminal matters; liens or unpaid judgments; RELINC acknowledgment; declaration and
   signature.
5. Order the criminal records report only through the NCREC-designated reporting service and close
   enough to filing that it remains within six months. The reviewed broker-application rule requires
   this report; it does **not** identify a fingerprint submission.
6. Submit the application and **$105** fee. Select the other-jurisdiction/State-section election only
   if the live form describes it consistently with Rule .0511.
7. When NCREC accepts the application, it ordinarily emails an exam-eligibility notice. The April
   2025 official handbook describes a 180-calendar-day window and 2-3-day notice timing; treat those
   as workflow guidance and recheck the current notice.
8. Book only the NC State section with Pearson VUE. The April 2025 official outline has 60 scored
   State items plus five pretest items. Recheck the live single-section fee; the handbook's $50 figure
   was not independently frozen for September 2026.
9. Pass and wait for NCREC's status determination. Do not represent nonprovisional status until it
   appears in the Commission record.
10. Keep the NC license current. Renewal is $50 under the rule effective April 1, 2026. Active status
    also requires the applicable NC CE; out-of-state licensure does not replace NC CE.

at least one year in North Carolina. The user's known California experience cannot supply the NC year.
Possible alternatives are three years of secondary/postsecondary teaching, pre/post instructor
approval in another jurisdiction, or qualifications NCREC finds equivalent. Evidence for none of
those alternatives is presently in the repository.

## Firm/BIC separation

Education-provider certification is not an NC brokerage firm license. StateCourse does not need a
brokerage firm/BIC merely to be an education provider unless it will itself engage in brokerage.
If a separate brokerage firm is later opened, it needs a qualifying broker and ordinarily a BIC for
each office. BIC status requires an active nonprovisional license, qualifying recent experience,
BIC eligibility, and the 12-hour BIC course timing under Rule 58A .0110. Do not mix that later
brokerage filing with the education-provider filing.
