# North Carolina Broker Prelicensing Course — Module NC-01

## Section I · North Carolina Real Estate Licensure

**Commission syllabus section:** I — NC Real Estate Licensure
**Instructional Level:** **1**. At this level, students should be able to recall, define, classify,
describe, and explain the basic licensing facts and procedures.
**Licence examination weight:** **3 items** on the scored State-section distribution in the Commission's published
prelicensing syllabus.
**Recommended allocation:** **2.0 instructional hours.** This is the Commission's curriculum
allocation, not a claim that the present text by itself supplies two hours. Student-visible words,
media, activities, and assessments must be measured in the final unit timing ledger.
**Course version:** 1.1 development draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No North Carolina education credit is available.

> North Carolina prescribes the subjects, depth, activities, and completion standards for this
> course. StateCourse authors the explanations and practice. It does not use or attempt to obtain
> actual license-examination questions.

## Learning objectives

After completing this module, a student should be able to:

1. distinguish the standard prelicensing route from the route for a person already licensed in
   another jurisdiction;
2. identify the threshold qualifications for an individual broker license;
3. sequence education, application, criminal-record review, examination, license issuance, and
   activation without treating any one step as permission to practice;
4. explain the three-year education clock, six-month criminal-report clock, and 180-day examination
   eligibility period;
5. describe the National and State examination sections, current published fees, results, and
   refiling rule;
6. distinguish inactive, active, provisional, nonprovisional, and broker-in-charge status; and
7. state the basic postlicensing, annual renewal, and continuing-education obligations that follow
   initial licensure.

## 1. One license title, several status questions

North Carolina licenses an individual as a **broker**. A new licensee is usually a **provisional
broker**, often abbreviated **PB**. “Provisional” is a status attached to the broker license; it is not
a separate salesperson license.

Every student should ask two independent questions before performing brokerage:

- **Is the license current or expired?** North Carolina licenses expire annually unless renewed.
- **Is the license active or inactive?** A current license can still be inactive. An inactive license
  does not authorize brokerage activity.

A provisional broker has an additional constraint: the license must be active **and** the provisional
broker must work under a broker-in-charge's supervision. Passing an examination does not activate a
license. Receiving an inactive license does not authorize practice. Affiliation and activation are
separate steps after issuance.

### Status map

| Term | What it tells you | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| applicant | the person has asked the Commission for a license | approval, examination success, or authority to practice |
| provisional broker | the individual holds the entry-level broker status | that the license is active |
| nonprovisional or “full” broker | provisional status has been removed or comparable status was issued | that the license is active or that the broker is BIC Eligible |
| active | the current license is eligible for brokerage activity, subject to all other limits | independent-practice or BIC authority |
| inactive | the person may hold a current license but may not perform brokerage | that the license has expired |
| broker-in-charge | a properly qualified and designated broker responsible for an office | that every nonprovisional broker is automatically a BIC |

The practical discipline is simple: **never infer authority from a title alone.** Verify the current
Commission record, including license status and any affiliation or designation required for the work.

## 2. Threshold qualifications

[G.S. 93A-4](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_93A/GS_93A-4.html)
and the Commission's April 2026 *Real Estate Licensing in North Carolina* guide (**RELINC**) establish
the basic individual-license framework. An applicant must:

- be at least **18 years old**;
- have the citizenship, lawful-presence, or federal work-authorization status described in the
  current application instructions;
- satisfy the education or permitted equivalent-qualification route;
- submit a complete application and current criminal record report;
- pass the examination section or sections required for that applicant, unless a specific exception
  applies; and
- satisfy the Commission as to competency, honesty, truthfulness, integrity, good character, and
  general fitness.

The statute does not turn course completion into an automatic entitlement to a license. Education is
one qualification. The application, examination when required, and character determination remain
independent Commission decisions.

### Character is not a last-minute checkbox

Every applicant must make complete and accurate disclosures. A criminal conviction is not the only
possible character issue. RELINC also directs applicants to disclose matters such as occupational
license discipline, unpaid judgments or liens, and other items asked on the current application.

An individual with a criminal history may use the Commission's **Petition for Pre-Determination**
process to ask whether that history is likely to disqualify the person. A predetermination is not a
license and does not eliminate the later application. It is a planning tool that can be used before
paying for the prelicensing course.

## 3. Standard prelicensing route

For most first-time applicants, the process is:

1. **Complete the 75-hour North Carolina Broker Prelicensing Course** with a Commission-certified
   Education Provider and pass its mandatory end-of-course examination.
2. **Wait for the provider's electronic completion report.** A student may start the online
   application, but the standard-course applicant cannot finalize it until the Commission has the
   provider's passing-completion report.
3. **Order the Commission-designated criminal record report.** The completed report is linked to the
   application.
4. **Submit the complete application and application fee.** The current published application fee is
   **$105**.
5. **Receive the Notice of Exam Eligibility** after the Commission processes a qualifying complete
   application.
6. **Schedule and pass the required National and State examination sections** through Pearson VUE.
7. **Wait for the Commission to issue the license.** Pearson and the test center do not issue it.
8. **Activate and affiliate the license before performing brokerage.** A new provisional broker and
   broker-in-charge use the Commission's License Activation/Affiliation process.

The order protects the applicant from a common mistake: treating a course certificate, eligibility
notice, or passing result as a license. None of those documents authorizes brokerage.

### The education clock

For the standard route, the Commission-approved North Carolina Broker Prelicensing Course must have
been completed within the **three years preceding the license application**. The syllabus describes
the measuring point as the course-completion date shown on the certificate issued after the student
passes the end-of-course examination.

That clock is not an exam-appointment deadline and it is not the license's renewal period. It answers
one question only: whether the course can still be used to support the application.

### The criminal record report clock

The criminal record report must be prepared by the Commission-designated reporting service within
the **six months before the application is filed**. The search must address the places and names
required by the current instructions. Multi-state, international, or multiple-name searches can take
longer and cost more.

Plan the report so it is recent enough to remain valid but early enough not to hold up filing. If the
report expires before a required new application, a new report is needed. Use only the service linked
from the current Commission application workflow; a consumer background report obtained elsewhere is
not a substitute.

## 4. Applicants with an existing license in another jurisdiction

An applicant who holds a current real estate salesperson or broker license in another qualifying
jurisdiction, and whose license was active within the preceding three years, may have a different
education and examination route under
[21 NCAC 58A .0511](https://reports.oah.state.nc.us/ncac/title%2021%20-%20occupational%20licensing%20boards%20and%20commissions/chapter%2058%20-%20real%20estate%20commission/subchapter%20a/21%20ncac%2058a%20.0511.pdf).
The applicant supplies an official certification of licensure meeting the Commission's current
requirements and elects one of two materially different paths:

| Election | Examination | Possible initial NC status |
|---|---|---|
| take the NC State section | National section is not repeated; State section must be passed | status the Commission determines comparable to the qualifying out-of-jurisdiction license |
| waive the NC State section | no NC license examination under this option | provisional broker status, with North Carolina postlicensing obligations |

“Comparable” is a Commission determination, not a promise a provider or applicant can make. An
out-of-state title that contains the word “broker” does not automatically establish what North
Carolina status will issue. The applicant must read the issued record.

The two elections also have different downstream consequences. RELINC states that a provisional
broker who used the no-State-exam option is not eligible for the out-of-state experience/education
postlicensing waiver under Rule 58A .1905. A candidate choosing speed at the application stage may
therefore accept a significant postlicensing obligation.

This special route should never be presented as the standard path for an unlicensed student. It
depends on a qualifying existing license and official documentation.

## 5. License examination

The North Carolina examination has separately timed and separately scored **National** and **State**
sections. Most standard-route applicants must pass both in the same eligibility period.

| Section | Scored items | Unscored pretest items | Time allowed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| National | 80 | 5 | 2.5 hours |
| State | 60 | 5 | 2.0 hours |

The five pretest items in each section are mixed among the scored items. Candidates cannot identify
them, and they do not count toward the score. The published passing score is **75 for each required
section**, calculated separately.

The Commission's syllabus distributes the 140 scored items by subject so a student can allocate
study effort. That distribution is not permission to ignore a “low-weight” topic, and it does not
reveal actual examination questions.

### Eligibility and fees

The official April 2026 RELINC guide publishes:

- **$105** to the Commission for each license-application submission;
- **$63** to Pearson for an attempt containing both sections; and
- **$53** to Pearson for an attempt containing one section.

These are the current published figures captured for this course. Fees and vendor terms can change;
the candidate must confirm the amount in the live Commission and Pearson workflows before paying.

The Notice of Exam Eligibility creates a **180-calendar-day examination eligibility period**. All
sections required for that candidate must be passed within the same period. A section passed in an
expired eligibility period does not carry into a new one.

### Results, failure, and refiling

Each application submission and application fee cover **one examination attempt**. A candidate who
fails a required section must return to the Commission application portal, verify or update the
application, refile, and pay another application fee before scheduling a retake. Pearson also charges
the applicable examination fee for the new appointment.

If the 180-day period expires before all required sections are passed, the applicant needs a new
eligibility period, a new application fee, and a new criminal record report. There is no published
limit on the number of times an otherwise qualified person may refile, but repeated attempts do not
extend the original 180-day clock.

The test center does not issue a license. Pearson reports the result to the Commission. The
Commission issues a license only after the required examination sections are timely passed and the
character requirement is satisfied. A person may not perform brokerage until the issued license has
been placed on the required active status.

### Examination integrity

The syllabus quotes Commission Rule 58H .0304(b): instructors must not obtain, use, or attempt to
obtain or use North Carolina license-examination questions in any manner or form. StateCourse
questions are independently authored from the prescribed curriculum and public official sources.
They are study checks, not recalled or reconstructed state-examination items.

## 6. After the license is issued

### Initial inactive provisional status

Most applicants licensed by examination receive an **inactive provisional broker license**. Before
performing brokerage, the provisional broker must affiliate with and be supervised by a
broker-in-charge and complete the Commission's License Activation/Affiliation process. Brokerage is
not lawful merely because an email reports a passing exam result.

An applicant who qualified through licensure in another jurisdiction and passed the State section
may instead be issued a comparable nonprovisional status. Because the Commission decides
comparability, the applicant must verify the status actually issued before relying on it.

### Postlicensing

A provisional broker must complete **90 instructional hours of North Carolina Postlicensing
education within 18 months after initial licensure** to remain eligible for active status. The program
contains three 30-hour courses:

- Post 301 — Broker Relationships and Responsibilities;
- Post 302 — Contracts and Closing; and
- Post 303 — NC Law, Rules, and Legal Concepts.

After all three are reported, the Commission removes provisional status. If the deadline is missed,
the license is placed on inactive status until the education deficiency and reactivation requirements
are satisfied. Postlicensing is a one-time transition requirement; it is not the same as annual
continuing education.

### Renewal and continuing education

North Carolina real estate licenses expire at midnight on **June 30** unless renewed. The current
published renewal window is **May 15 through June 30**, and the current annual renewal fee is **$50**.
A recently issued license can still expire on the next June 30, so the licensee must check the actual
record rather than assume a full year from issuance.

For active-status eligibility, the ordinary annual continuing-education requirement is **8 hours**:
a 4-hour Update Course and a 4-hour Commission-approved elective. CE is due by **June 10** during the
applicable license period. RELINC identifies a limited initial-year exception: a broker newly licensed
by examination or reinstatement has no CE requirement during the initial license year and must meet
the annual requirement before the second renewal to renew on active status.

Renewal and CE answer different questions. Paying the renewal fee keeps the license current; timely
education is part of maintaining active-status eligibility. A current but inactive broker may not
perform brokerage.

### No authority to practice law

G.S. 93A-4 states that the broker license does not authorize the practice of law. Later modules teach
the operational boundary between completing permitted preprinted forms and composing legal language.
When a transaction requires legal advice or drafting, the broker refers the parties to a qualified
attorney.

## 7. Worked decisions

### Decision 1 — “I passed today, so I can show property tonight.”

No. A passing result is transmitted to the Commission; it is not the issued and activated license.
The candidate must wait for issuance and then satisfy the activation/affiliation requirements that
apply to the issued status.

### Decision 2 — “My course certificate is two years and eleven months old.”

The certificate may still satisfy the three-year education window if the complete application is
filed before the period ends and every other requirement is met. The student should not schedule the
plan so tightly that an incomplete application or expired criminal report pushes filing beyond the
deadline.

### Decision 3 — “I am licensed elsewhere, so North Carolina must issue a full broker license.”

No. A qualifying out-of-jurisdiction license can open the Rule .0511 route. If the applicant elects
and passes the State section, the Commission determines comparable status. If the applicant waives
the State section, the rule provides provisional status. The final issued Commission record controls.

### Decision 4 — “I failed one section; I will just book Pearson again.”

Not yet. The candidate must first return to the Commission portal, refile the application for another
attempt, and pay the application fee. Only after a new eligibility notice authorizes the remaining
section may the candidate pay Pearson and schedule it. The remaining section must still be passed
within the same 180-day eligibility period.

### Decision 5 — “I renewed online, so my license is active.”

Not necessarily. Renewal prevents expiration, but active-status eligibility also depends on the
education and affiliation requirements applicable to the licensee. Verify the Commission record
before performing brokerage.

## StateCourse application-clock activity

This is a StateCourse practice activity, not a Commission-prescribed Required Activity for Section I.
Complete it before the module assessment.

For each scenario, write the document or date you would verify next and explain why:

1. A student's course certificate will reach three years of age in 35 days, but the criminal record
   report has not been ordered.
2. An applicant has passed the National section, has 20 days left in the examination eligibility
   period, and has not passed the State section.
3. A newly issued provisional broker has an inactive status and an offer to conduct an open house.
4. A broker pays the renewal fee on June 25 but has not satisfied the CE requirement that applies to
   that license period.
5. An out-of-state broker wants to waive the State section but also expects immediate
   nonprovisional status.

An acceptable response identifies the controlling clock or status and does not invent an extension.
The purpose is to connect dates to decisions: three years governs education use; six months governs
the criminal report; 180 days governs required exam sections; June 10 is the ordinary CE deadline;
June 30 is the license-expiration and renewal deadline; and issuance is separate from activation.

## Module NC-01 assessment

The module contains one applied scenario, five engagement checks, and five remediation checks. The
applied item must be correct; the engagement and remediation sets use an 80% StateCourse practice
threshold.

That 80% threshold is not the Commission's end-of-course standard. The separate regulated
end-of-course examination must be comprehensive, closed-book, proctored, and passed at **75% or
higher** under the applicable Commission rule. This development build does not yet administer that
protected final or award credit.
