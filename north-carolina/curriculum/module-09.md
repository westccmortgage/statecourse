# North Carolina Broker Prelicensing Course — Module NC-09

## Section IX · Real Estate Practice

**Commission Syllabus section:** IX — Real Estate Practice
**Instructional Level:** **2** except as noted. *Assigned by the Commission; providers and instructors
are **REQUIRED to comply**.* **Fair Housing is Level 3.** **Typical property management duties, the
design and construction requirement, fair housing enforcement and penalties, the ADA, and the whole of
Risk Management are Level 1.**
**Licence examination weight:** **National Exam Category — 14 items**, the second-heaviest national
section.
**Instructional hours:** **4.0**, incorporating the Commission's recommended 1.5 hours for Fair
Housing/Anti-discrimination.
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No North Carolina prelicensing credit is available.

## Learning objectives

1. State the three limits on broker responsibility and apply the rule on soliciting another firm's
   active clients.
2. Distinguish the listing contract types, including limited service and protection agreements.
3. Name the eleven typical full service listing contract provisions.
4. Name the ways a brokerage agreement terminates.
5. Describe the types of brokerage compensation and state the position on net listings.
6. **Recite the seven federal protected classes and the prohibited practices.** *(Level 3)*
7. **Distinguish blockbusting, steering and redlining and give examples of each.** *(Level 3)*
8. State the fair housing enforcement routes and their filing periods. *(Level 1)*
9. State what the Civil Rights Act of 1866 covers and how it is enforced.
10. Describe the ADA's application to a real estate office. *(Level 1)*
11. Apply the Do Not Call rules to expired listings and FSBOs. *(Level 1)*
12. Explain vicarious liability and antitrust exposure. *(Level 1)*
13. Distinguish fraud from misrepresentation and describe E&O and general liability cover. *(Level 1)*

## A. Broker Responsibility

### A.1 Practise within the scope of your experience

### A.2 Avoid the unauthorized practice of law

Module NC-10 sets out the North Carolina prohibition in full. The principle here: **completing
preprinted forms is permitted; drafting is not.**

### A.3 Soliciting the active clients of other firms

- **May constitute tortious interference with contractual relations.**
- **Violates the Code of Ethics (Article 16) of the National Association of REALTORS®**, which governs
  members of that Association.

**And the Commission's rule for the situation that actually arises, transcribed:**

> If a seller **whose property is currently listed with another company** initiates a conversation with
> a broker about the broker becoming their new listing agent, the broker:
>
> - **"May discuss the terms of the possible listing agreement that would begin AFTER the current
>   listing ends"**; but
> - **"Should NOT suggest that the seller terminate the current listing agreement early."**

**The seller may raise it; you may discuss a future listing; you may not encourage them to break the
present one.**

## B. Creating Brokerage Agreements

### B.1 Seller — the listing agreement

**General concepts:** it **usually creates special agency**; it **must usually be in writing** — and in
North Carolina, per Module NC-08, **agency contracts with property owners must be in writing from the
outset and oral listings are unlawful**; and the **agency contract is owned by the brokerage company**,
not by the individual broker.

**Types of listing contracts:**

**Full service listing contracts:**

- **Open or nonexclusive listing.**
- **Exclusive agency listing.**
- **Exclusive right to sell listing.**

**Limited service listing contracts:**

- **Brokerage compensation is frequently a fixed or flat fee** rather than a percentage of gross sales
  price.
- **"May limit services, but generally should NOT limit legal duties dictated by statute or state
  regulations."**
- **Instructor Note:** *"Consultation with a real estate attorney is recommended prior to creating such
  an agreement."*

**That distinction — services may be limited, statutory duties may not — is the whole of limited
service brokerage in one line.**

**Protection agreements:**

- **An agreement between a buyer agent and a seller for a single transaction**, usually when dealing
  with a **For Sale By Owner (FSBO)** situation.
- **The agent should provide and review the Working with Real Estate Agents Disclosure with the
  seller.**

**Multiple or Cooperative Listing Services (MLS).** **Instructor Note:** *"Mandatory training on MLS use
is standard among the various MLS organizations."*

### B.2 Typical full service listing contract provisions — eleven

**Term of agreement. Property description**, including fixtures and personal property. **Listing price
and terms of sale. Broker/firm's compensation. Cooperation with and compensation to other brokerages.
The brokerage company's duties. Marketing. Earnest money. Seller's representations and duties.
Dual/designated agency authorization. Names and signatures of parties.**

*(Recall Module NC-08 E.2: a North Carolina written agency agreement must additionally have a **definite
expiration date**, the **prescribed anti-discriminatory language** in a clear conspicuous manner,
**signatures of all parties**, and the **listing broker's individual licence number.**)*

### B.3 Buyer — the buyer representation agreement

**Nonexclusive buyer agency** and **exclusive buyer agency**, and their typical provisions.

### B.4 Landlord — the property management agreement

**Instructor Note:** *"Per Commission Rule 58A .0104(a), **all property management agreements must be in
writing from inception of the relationship**."*

Typical provisions: **property description; contract period; agent's management fee; agent's authority;
responsibilities of agent and owner.**

**Typical property management duties** *(Level 1)*: establishing a rental schedule; preparing an
operating budget; marketing the property; qualifying and selecting tenants; executing leases for the
owner; collecting rents and security deposits; **handling collected monies according to laws/rules**;
maintaining the property; preparing and enforcing rules and regulations; maintaining property
insurance; maintaining accurate records; **preparing income/expense reports and accounting to the
owner**; **performing the landlord's legal duties**; **instituting legal actions such as tenant
eviction**; and **complying with the Americans with Disabilities Act and Fair Housing laws.**

> **Required Activity — prescribed by the Commission:** *"Discuss factors that may/may not be considered
> due to **Fair Housing Act criteria (e.g., credit reports, criminal records)**."*

### B.4a Required Activity evidence — neutral tenant-screening matrix

Apply the same written screening standard to every applicant. A criterion is not made lawful merely by
omitting a protected class from the application, and it is not made unlawful merely because it is not
listed in the Fair Housing Act. The decision turns on **what criterion is used, why it is used, whether
it is applied consistently, and whether disability accommodation or another governing law changes the
analysis.**

| Proposed factor | Federal fair-housing classification | Required broker response |
|---|---|---|
| race, color, religion, national origin, sex, familial status or disability | protected statutory basis | never use to refuse, rank, steer, alter terms, or decide availability |
| credit history or documented ability to meet rent | not itself one of the seven statutory bases | use only a written, transaction-related standard applied consistently; comply separately with consumer-reporting law |
| conviction record | not itself a statutory protected class | do not treat withdrawn HUD criminal-record guidance as current law; apply the owner's lawful written standard consistently and never as a pretext for protected-class discrimination |
| arrest without a disposition or an inaccurate vendor match | not an automatic fair-housing answer | pause the decision, verify the record and governing policy, and obtain BIC/counsel review rather than improvise a rule |
| source of income | not a federal Fair Housing Act class | check North Carolina, local and housing-program law before using it; do not convert it into a proxy for a protected class |
| disability-related request to change a rule, policy, practice or service | possible reasonable accommodation | route through the accommodation process; do not treat the request itself as a negative screening factor |
| an assistance animal under a no-pets policy | possible reasonable accommodation | evaluate under the accommodation rules, not the pet rules; a pet deposit or fee is not charged merely because the animal is present |

**Current-source warning.** HUD withdrew its 2022 criminal-record guidance on September 17, 2025 and
states on its current fair-housing page that earlier guidance discouraging criminal background checks
is no longer its enforcement policy. This course therefore does **not** teach the withdrawn guidance as
binding. The statutory prohibition on intentional discrimination because of a protected class still
controls every screening decision.

### B.5 Termination of agency agreements

**Expiration. Completion/full performance. Termination by law. Destruction of property or death of
principal. Mutual agreement. Breach of contract.**

### B.6 Broker/firm's compensation

> **Required Activity — prescribed by the Commission:** *"Discuss various ways brokerage fees are
> determined."*

**Types:** **percentage of gross sale price; fixed or flat fee; fee for services for named services;
protection period; net listing.**

**Instructor Note on net listings, transcribed:** *"Use of net listing arrangements is **either illegal
or strongly discouraged in most states** due to the great potential for abuse and fraud based on
inherent conflict of interest."*

**Co-listing by more than one broker/company.**

### B.6a Required Activity evidence — compensation-method lab

The numbers below are hypothetical arithmetic inputs, not customary rates. For each method, identify
what the parties must negotiate and where a conflict can arise.

| Method | Worked example | Compliance point |
|---|---|---|
| percentage of sale price | At a hypothetical negotiated 2.4 percent on $410,000, the fee is $9,840. | The parties negotiate the percentage; never describe it as standard or customary. |
| fixed fee | The agreement states $4,500 for the defined package of services. | State the included services and do not contract away statutory or regulatory duties. |
| fee for named service | Photography is $600 and contract coordination is $1,200. | Identify each service and charge rather than implying full service. |
| protection-period fee | A protected buyer identified during the listing closes after expiration. | The written agreement controls the defined period, identified prospects and fee trigger. |
| net listing | The seller is promised a stated net and the broker keeps the excess. | The structure creates an acute conflict and abuse risk; do not use it without current BIC and legal approval. |
| co-listing | Two firms agree how a negotiated fee will be divided. | Division does not authorize competitors to set market-wide prices or exchange future pricing plans. |

## C. Fair Housing *(Level 3)*

> **Required Activity — prescribed by the Commission:** *"**Provide examples of each type of
> discrimination.**"*
>
> **This activity must be completed.** Work each prohibited practice below and produce a concrete
> example — including at least one that a licensee could commit without intending to.

### C.1 The Federal Fair Housing Act — Title VIII of the 1968 Civil Rights Act

**Purpose: equal opportunity in housing.**

**Protected classes — the Commission's own list and its own order:**

1. **Familial status**
2. **Race**
3. **Sex**
4. **Handicapping condition/disability**
5. **Color**
6. **Religion**
7. **National Origin**

### C.2 Prohibited practices in the sale or rental of housing

- **Blockbusting (or panic peddling).**
- **Steering (or channeling).**
- **Refusing to sell, rent, or negotiate based on membership or PERCEIVED membership in a protected
  class.**
- **Representing that a dwelling is not available for sale or lease when it is.**
- **Discriminatory practices in terms, conditions, or privileges; in the FINANCING of housing** —
  e.g. **redlining** and related practices — **and in ADVERTISING.**

**Instructor Note on advertising, transcribed:** *"**Includes any Internet or social media posting.**"*

**Note "perceived membership".** A refusal based on what a licensee **assumed** about someone is a
violation whether or not the assumption was correct.

**And the practical discipline, which the Required Activity should surface:** steering is most often
committed by helpful-sounding remarks — *"you'd be more comfortable over there," "that's really more of
a family neighbourhood"* — and by **omission**, simply not showing or mentioning areas that meet the
buyer's own stated criteria. **Never characterise who lives anywhere.** Current HUD guidance does not
require silence about school quality or crime information: a broker may provide reliable information
when it is supplied **equally and consistently** and the broker is not intentionally steering because
of a protected characteristic.

### C.2a Required Activity evidence — discrimination examples

Classify each fact pattern before reading the answer. Intent is not required for the learner to spot
the dangerous act; the exercise is designed to prevent the act before a court or agency must classify
the evidence.

| Type | Concrete example | Correct action |
|---|---|---|
| refusal to sell, rent or negotiate | a landlord rejects an otherwise qualified applicant after learning the applicant is Muslim | stop the decision; religion may not be used |
| false availability | a broker tells a Black prospect “nothing is open” while offering the same unit to a White tester | provide truthful, equal availability information |
| unequal terms or services | families with children are charged a higher deposit than otherwise identical households | use the same lawful written terms |
| discriminatory advertising | a social post says “ideal for Christians” or “no children” | remove the preference; internet and social advertising are covered |
| steering/channeling | a broker omits qualifying homes in one area because of the buyer's race | show options based on the buyer's stated property criteria, not a protected class |
| blockbusting/panic peddling | a broker solicits listings by claiming that families of a named national origin are moving in and values will fall | do not induce a sale through protected-class representations |
| redlining/financing discrimination | a lender changes price or availability because the collateral is in a predominantly Black neighborhood | apply lawful underwriting without protected-class geography as the reason |
| disability accommodation | a housing provider rejects a necessary exception to a no-pets rule without evaluating an assistance-animal request | evaluate the requested policy change and the statute's defenses |
| disability modification | a landlord categorically refuses a tenant-paid grab bar needed for use of the unit | evaluate and permit a reasonable modification subject to lawful conditions |
| design and construction | an elevator building first occupied after March 13, 1991 has inaccessible common areas and doorways | covered multifamily design requirements reach all units in that elevator building |
| retaliation or interference | a manager threatens a tenant for filing a fair-housing complaint | do not coerce, threaten, intimidate or interfere with protected activity |

### C.2b Current HUD neighborhood-information lab

HUD's April 24, 2026 letter says the Fair Housing Act does not itself prohibit a real-estate
professional from sharing neighborhood crime-rate or school-quality information. It also states that
unlawful steering requires intentional discrimination based on a protected characteristic. Apply that
distinction:

1. A buyer asks for the school district's published performance data. Provide the same reliable source
   and explanation you would provide every similarly situated buyer.
2. A buyer asks for “the right kind of people.” Decline to classify residents or neighborhoods by
   protected characteristics.
3. A broker sends crime data only to buyers of one race to discourage them from an area. The data does
   not cure the intentionally discriminatory use; stop the steering.
4. A brokerage elects a written policy of linking every consumer to the same official school and crime
   sources. Apply it consistently and keep the source/date in the file.

### C.3 Exemptions

The exemptions are narrow and must be read separately from the advertising prohibition:

- Under 42 U.S.C. §3603(b)(1), a qualifying private owner of no more than three single-family houses
  may fall outside specified §3604 prohibitions only if the detailed ownership/transaction conditions
  are met, **no real-estate broker or agent is used**, and no discriminatory advertisement is used.
- Under §3603(b)(2), rooms or units in a dwelling for no more than four independent families may fall
  within an exemption when the owner actually occupies one unit. Section 3604(c)'s advertising rule is
  not part of that exemption.
- Section 3607 contains narrow noncommercial religious-organization and private-club rules and the
  housing-for-older-persons provisions. The latter limits familial-status coverage; it is not a general
  license to discriminate on another protected basis.
- Section 1982's race-based equal-property right does not inherit these Fair Housing Act exemptions.
- North Carolina or local law may be more restrictive. Module NC-15 applies that separate state layer.

### C.4 Design and construction of multi-family housing *(Level 1)*

The Syllabus shorthand says all buildings with four or more residential units constructed after March
13, 1991. The current statute is more precise: the requirement concerns **covered multifamily dwellings
designed and constructed for first occupancy after March 13, 1991**. It reaches **all units in a
building with four or more units and one or more elevators**, but only **ground-floor units** in another
building with four or more units. Public/common areas, routes, doors and adaptive-design features carry
the requirements in 42 U.S.C. §3604(f)(3)(C) and (f)(7).

### C.5 Enforcement and penalties *(Level 1)*

- **Conciliation** — an attempt to resolve the complaint through **informal negotiations**.
- **HUD administrative complaint:** file with the **Office of Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity
  (FHEO)** no later than **one year after the alleged discriminatory practice occurred or terminated**.
- **Private civil action:** file in an appropriate state or federal court no later than **two years
  after occurrence or termination**; time while a qualifying administrative proceeding is pending is
  excluded from that calculation.
- **Section 3617** makes coercion, intimidation, threats or interference with protected fair-housing
  activity unlawful. Do not turn that civil prohibition into an automatic criminal-penalty statement:
  the separate criminal statute, 42 U.S.C. §3631, requires its own elements, including force or threat
  of force and specified intent.

**One year administratively; two years by private suit.** Both figures are the Commission's.

### C.6 The Federal Civil Rights Act of 1866

- **42 U.S.C. §1982 protects the same right as White citizens to inherit, purchase, lease, sell, hold
  and convey real and personal property.** The Commission teaches this as the 1866 Act's race rule with
  no Fair Housing Act exemptions.
- Enforce the right through civil litigation. This course does not repeat the Syllabus's unsupported
  “federal court only” shortcut; forum, parties and remedies are legal questions.

### C.7 The Americans with Disabilities Act *(Level 1)*

**Title III addresses places of public accommodation and commercial facilities.** A customer-facing
real-estate or rental office is a sales/rental or service establishment and is covered; an isolated
employees-only administrative office may be only a commercial facility. Obligations include equal
access to services, effective communication, accessible new construction/alterations, and removal of
existing architectural barriers where readily achievable. Do not treat the ADA and the Fair Housing
Act as interchangeable: the office and customer service are the ADA problem; the dwelling transaction
and reasonable accommodation/modification are the Fair Housing Act problem.

## D. Risk Management *(Level 1)*

### D.1 Supervision

The basic agency relationships are as Module NC-07 sets out. The client engages the **firm**, and an
affiliated broker may act within that firm's express representation. That is not the same as saying
that every affiliated broker automatically becomes a common-law subagent, and it does not create
outside-firm seller subagency. An outside firm needs the seller's written authorization and the
required disclosure. The Commission adds one further rule here:

> **"When an affiliated broker leaves the brokerage, they CANNOT take the contracts/clients with
> them."**

Because the agency contract is **owned by the brokerage company**, not by the individual.

**Responsibilities of the employing broker — firm or BIC:**

- **Supervising all actions of affiliated licensees and employees.**
- **Providing training for affiliated licensees.**

### D.1a Required Activity evidence — client versus third-party duties

| Situation | Client duty | Duty to customer or other third party |
|---|---|---|
| seller wants a known roof leak concealed | obedience reaches only lawful instructions; loyalty does not permit concealment | disclose the material fact honestly and fairly |
| buyer asks for negotiating advice | provide loyal advocacy, lawful obedience, confidentiality, accounting, disclosure, skill and care | do not misrepresent the buyer's position to the seller |
| unrepresented buyer asks whom the listing broker represents | protect seller confidences | disclose agency status and deal honestly; do not imply representation of the buyer |
| competing offer exists | follow the seller's lawful disclosure instruction and protect confidential strategy | do not invent, exaggerate or reveal offer information without authority |

The evidence rule is simple: **client status adds fiduciary/agency duties; customer status never removes
honesty, fairness and material-fact disclosure.**

### D.2 Do Not Call laws and rules

- **The National Do Not Call Registry is maintained by the Federal Trade Commission.** A live sales
  call to a listed number requires an applicable basis such as the seller's own established business
  relationship or the consumer's clear written permission; an entity-specific do-not-call request
  overrides an established relationship.
- The established-business-relationship periods in the FTC rule are **18 months after a qualifying
  transaction** and **three months after the consumer's inquiry or application**. The relationship is
  with the seller, not every competitor or affiliate.
- A compliance process uses Registry data downloaded no more than **31 days** before the call, written
  procedures, training, an entity-specific suppression list, monitoring and records. The ordinary FTC
  calling window is **8 a.m. to 9 p.m. local time** absent prior consent otherwise.
- Do not collapse live calls, prerecorded calls and texts into one rule. Prerecorded sales calls and
  automated calls/texts have separate written-consent and opt-out requirements under the TSR/TCPA;
  an established relationship does not by itself authorize a prerecorded sales call.

**The two North Carolina practice traps the Commission spells out:**

- **Calls to homeowners with EXPIRED listings with another broker/company are prohibited — "the
  'established business relationship' exemption only applies to the expired listing broker."** So the
  broker whose listing expired may call; **you may not.**
- **Calls to solicit a listing from a FSBO on the Registry are prohibited.** **But the Instructor Note
  adds the exception:** *"**Broker MAY call a registered FSBO on behalf of a prospective buyer-client
  who is interested in buying the FSBO property.**"*

**No exception survives the consumer's entity-specific request not to be called.** Penalty amounts and
FCC consent rules can change; verify them at the campaign date rather than encoding an old syllabus
figure.

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"Brokers should stay up-to-date on any restrictions on **electronic
communication (e.g., email, texts, social media)** that might affect their solicitation of business in
that manner. **Mass electronic newsletters or 'just listed/sold' emails would be subject to such
restrictions.**"*

### D.2a Do Not Call decision drill

1. You did not hold the expired listing. The seller's prior relationship with the former listing firm
   is not your relationship. If the number is registered and there is no other valid basis, do not call.
2. An FSBO number is registered and your purpose is to solicit the listing. Do not call.
3. A buyer-client asks you to contact that FSBO about the identified property. The Commission treats
   the property inquiry differently from listing solicitation; identify the buyer purpose and do not
   turn the call into a listing pitch.
4. A past client tells your firm “do not call again.” Stop live and automated solicitation even if an
   18-month transaction period otherwise remains.
5. Before any campaign, document the call type, consent or exception, Registry scrub date, local time,
   caller identity and opt-out handling. Escalate automated calls, texts and mass electronic campaigns
   for current TCPA/FCC review.

### D.3 Vicarious liability

- **Liability for another's actions because of the relationship between the parties.**
- A firm and BIC have regulatory supervision responsibilities for affiliated brokers. Civil vicarious
  liability is a separate, fact-specific agency question: scope of work, control and the act at issue
  matter. Do not tell a student that every act by every affiliate automatically creates civil liability.
- A genuine subagency relationship can create responsibility through agency principles; Module NC-07
  explains why outside-firm seller subagency is not automatic in North Carolina.

### D.4 The Sherman Antitrust Act

Section 1 prohibits agreements that unreasonably restrain trade. Agreements among competitors to fix
prices, allocate customers or markets, or rig bids are criminal violations; group boycotts and trade-
association restraints may also violate the Act. Current DOJ enforcement specifically treats real-
estate brokerage competition and commission rules as antitrust issues.

**And the Commission's own instruction, transcribed:**

> **"NEVER imply or state that the quoted fee is 'customary' or 'the going rate' for the industry or a
> local area."**
>
> **Instructor Note:** *"Brokers should **only discuss their company's fee** and **not answer questions
> about how this fee compares to fees of other companies**."*

**That is a stricter practice rule than "don't collude."** It forbids characterising your own fee as
customary, and it forbids answering a comparison question at all.

### D.5 Fraud and misrepresentation

- **The employing broker must discourage all fraud/misrepresentation by affiliates.**
- **Fraud requires intentional deception; negligent misrepresentation is a different theory.** Do not
  teach “negligent fraud” as though intent and negligence were interchangeable.
- North Carolina licensing discipline separately reaches willful **or negligent** material
  misrepresentation or omission under G.S. 93A-6(a)(1). That regulatory wording does not redefine the
  elements of common-law fraud.

### D.6 Types of liability insurance

- **Errors and Omissions (E&O)** — **"covers most mistakes made in listing and selling activities."**
- **General liability** — **"covers the risk assumed when the public enters the brokerage building."**

The Syllabus warns that criminal acts, fraud, civil-rights violations and antitrust conduct may fall
outside coverage. Treat that as a prompt to read the actual policy, not as a universal coverage ruling:
definitions, exclusions, defense obligations and endorsements vary. Intentional misconduct cannot be
made safe by assuming E&O will defend it.
