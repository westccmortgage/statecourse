# North Carolina Broker Prelicensing Course — Module NC-16

## Section XVI · Real Estate Math Calculations

**Commission Syllabus section:** XVI — Real Estate Math Calculations
**Instructional Level:** **3** for the entire section — **analysis and evaluation**, the highest level the
Commission assigns anywhere in this course, and the only section assigned Level 3 throughout. *Assigned
by the Commission; providers and instructors are **REQUIRED to comply**.*
**Licence examination weight:** **National Exam Category — 7 items.**
**Instructional hours:** **0.0 as a standalone block — this section is delivered inside the other fifteen
modules.** See the Commission's instruction below.
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No North Carolina prelicensing credit is available.

> ### The Commission's instruction on how mathematics must be taught — transcribed in full
>
> *"**Instruction on relevant mathematical functions is to be provided under the appropriate subject areas
> rather than as a separate arithmetic course section.** To assist instructors, however, **all required
> real estate mathematics instruction is listed in Section XVI of the Outline.** **Consideration was given
> to the amount of mathematics involved in each subject area when establishing the recommended number of
> instructional hours for subject areas.** This **integrated approach is recommended because it helps
> assure that students understand how various mathematical functions relate to the various areas of real
> estate practice.** **Instruction in basic mathematics concepts such as decimals, fractions, percentages,
> multiplication, division, etc. is not included in this course syllabus.** Students **deficient in basic
> math skills should be provided special assistance outside of class or directed to take a math refresher
> course.**"*

### What that instruction means for this course, stated plainly

**Section XVI carries no instructional hours of its own, and it is not a module a student sits through in
sequence.** Three consequences follow, and all three are the Commission's, not this project's:

1. **The mathematics is taught where the subject lives.** Excise tax with property taxes; discount points
   with financing; adjustments with the sales comparison approach; prorations with settlement.
2. **The hours are already allocated.** The Commission states it took the mathematics into account when it
   set the recommended hours for each subject area. Adding separate hours for Section XVI would
   **double-count them** against the 72.0 instructional hours the Commission's own table produces.
3. **Basic arithmetic is out of scope.** Decimals, fractions, percentages, multiplication and division are
   **not part of this course**, and a student who cannot do them is to be **helped outside class or sent to
   a refresher course.** An instructor who spends course time on long division is spending prelicensing
   hours on something the Commission has excluded.

**This module is therefore the course's mathematics register**, not a lesson. It exists so that an
instructor, a reviewer and the Commission can see **every required calculation in one place**, together
with **the module in which it is actually delivered.** Its engagement items are the course's integrated
mathematics practice, drawn across all fifteen subject modules.

## Learning objectives *(all Level 3, all delivered in the modules named)*

The student can **perform, and evaluate the result of**, every calculation listed in Parts A through G
below, in the context of the transaction that produces it.

## A. Brokerage commissions — sales and property management transactions

Commission calculations on both sales transactions and property management transactions. Delivered with
the compensation material in Module NC-09 and with property management contracts in Module NC-14.

> **Required Activity, transcribed:** *"**Calculate various sales commissions and property management fees
> based on typical ways they are determined.**"*
>
> **Delivered.** *(Source note: this Required Activity is printed at a page boundary in the Syllabus,
> adjacent to a second activity concerning mechanics' lien priority. The fee calculation activity is
> carried here because Part A is its subject; the lien timeline is carried in Module NC-15 with the same
> note.)*
>
> Note the Commission's phrase — ***"typical ways they are determined."*** Module NC-09 records the
> Commission's own Required Activity to *"discuss various ways brokerage fees are determined,"* and
> Modules NC-08 and NC-09 record that **compensation is negotiable and is never set by the Commission, by
> any association, or by any course.** **No commission rate, split, management fee percentage or minimum
> is stated anywhere in this build.** Every practice problem supplies its own rate inside the problem, as
> a given.

## B. Investment calculations

1. **Profit/loss (appreciation/depreciation) on sale of real estate, including percentages.**
2. **Return on investment (NOI).**
3. **Tax implications on investment.**

*(B.2 uses the NOI derivation taught in Module NC-13 — potential gross income, less vacancy and rental
loss, less operating expenses, and **debt service excluded.**)*

> **Required Activity, transcribed:** *"**Calculate excise tax for multiple properties: include scenarios
> for property that is located inside city/town limits, located only in the county, have odd purchase
> prices, or include seller concessions or financing information.**"*
>
> **Delivered in Module NC-04**, where the current statutory rate, payer, base and four scenarios are
> taught from G.S. 105-228.30.
>
> Four scenario types are prescribed and all four must appear: **inside city/town limits**; **county
> only**; **odd purchase prices**; and **seller concessions or financing information.** The last two are
> there to defeat pattern-matching — a student who has only ever seen round numbers, and who has never had
> to decide whether a concession changes the taxable figure, has not learned the calculation.
>
> G.S. 105-228.30 was read from current official text on 2026-09-07. It requires the transferor to pay
> the register of deeds before recording, at $1 for each $500 or fractional part of the consideration or
> value conveyed. Module NC-04 supplies all four prescribed scenario types.

## C. Area and related calculations

1. **Conversion of acreage to square footage and vice-versa.**
2. **Square footage of variously-shaped buildings and parcels of land with straight line sides, including
   irregular shaped parcels/buildings.** The Commission names the formulas required: *"squares,
   rectangles, **trapezoids** and **right triangles**."*
3. **Square yards.**
4. **Cost/price per square foot / acre / front foot.**

**Trapezoids and right triangles are named explicitly**, which means an irregular parcel must be
decomposed rather than estimated. That is exactly the Level 3 demand: break the shape into shapes you can
compute, compute each, and add.

*(Module NC-15 applies the complete retained **2026 NCREC Residential Square Footage Guidelines**,
including current exterior/interior measurement, above/below-grade, heat, finish, access, ceiling and
record-retention criteria.)*

## D. Finance calculations — all delivered in Module NC-12

1. **Simple interest** — *"How to compute monthly interest, and amount of interest paid over life of a
   fixed-rate loan."*
2. **Monthly principal and interest payment** — *"using factors, if given the loan amount, interest rate,
   term and factor."*
3. **Mortgage debt reduction** — *"How to compute principal balance after one monthly payment, given
   previous principal balance, monthly P & I payment and interest rate, **with and without using
   amortization tables**."*
4. **Loan origination fees and discount points.**
5. **Loan value** — *"applying loan-to-value ratios."*
6. **Basic buyer qualification using given income and debt ratios.**
7. **Equity, including percentages.**
8. **Yield** — *"on mortgage loans involving discount points using **'rule of thumb' value**."*

Four of these deserve a note.

**D.2 says "using factors."** The student is **given** the factor. This is not an amortisation-formula
exercise; it is a use-the-table-correctly exercise, and the examinable skill is knowing what the factor
is per and multiplying accordingly.

**D.3 requires both methods** — with and without an amortisation table. The manual method is the one that
proves understanding: interest for the month is computed on the **previous** balance, the remainder of the
payment reduces principal, and the new balance follows.

**D.6 says "using given income and debt ratios."** The ratios are supplied in the problem. Module NC-12
transcribes the Commission's own figures — **28/36 conventional, 31/43 FHA, 43 VA** — and the rule that
**a buyer must qualify on both ratios** inside that Syllabus model, which is where most calculation errors
occur. Those figures do not announce live loan eligibility; a real creditor applies current program and
underwriting rules.

**D.8's "rule of thumb"** is the relationship Module NC-12 transcribes from the Syllabus: **1 point = 1/8
percent yield for the investor.** It is an examination shortcut, not a guaranteed market-price result.

## E. Settlement and closing costs

1. **Estimating net to seller after brokerage fee and other closing expenses are paid** — *"when setting
   listing price or considering an offer."* The Commission adds: *"**Includes sale price needed to net $X
   to seller.**"*
2. **Excise tax on property transfers.**
3. **Calculation of individual amounts to be entered on the settlement statement when the licensee has the
   data necessary** — *"e.g., prorations (real estate taxes, rent, HOA dues); interim interest; escrow
   deposits; etc."*
4. **Calculation of bottom-line figures given all relevant needed information:** *"Total settlement charges
   to buyer-borrower and seller; net due seller (cash to seller); cash from buyer-borrower."*

**E.1's second half is the harder problem and it is stated separately for that reason.** Computing a
seller's net from a known price is arithmetic. Computing **the price needed to produce a required net** is
algebra, because the commission is a percentage *of the unknown*. A student who solves it by subtracting
the commission from the desired net has made the classic error.

**E.3 and E.4 sit exactly where Module NC-12's Part J note draws the line.** The Commission states there
that a licence candidate **will not be asked to complete a settlement statement**, but **will be asked to
perform calculations for individual entries and to know how they appear — debit or credit, and to whom.**
E.4's bottom-line figures are computed from given data, not built up from a blank form.

> **Required Activity, transcribed:** *"**Explain and demonstrate proration math calculations for handling
> annual homeowners' association dues, monthly rent, and annual real estate taxes. Tax problems should
> include closing dates early and late in the calendar year and when the seller has and has not already
> paid the annual tax bill. Students must be given multiple practice problems on various proration
> scenarios.**"*
>
> **Delivered in Module NC-12.**
>
> The prescription is specific and every element is required: **three prorated items** (HOA dues, rent,
> taxes); **closing dates early and late in the year**; **the tax bill both paid and unpaid**; and
> **multiple practice problems**, not one. That is four variables, and the Commission wants them crossed.
>
> Module NC-12 records the three rules that govern every one of these problems, all transcribed from the
> Syllabus: **both the 365-day and the 360-day/30-day methods must be taught**; the **360-day/30-day
> method is the one used on the end-of-course examination and the licence examination**; and **in North
> Carolina the seller "owns" the date of settlement.** Also: **always a double entry.**

> **Required Activity, transcribed:** *"**Perform multiple math calculations to determine annual property
> tax liability. Include property tax situations that do and do not require calculation of both county and
> city taxes.**"*
>
> **Delivered in Module NC-04.**
>
> The county-only versus county-and-city distinction is the same one the excise tax activity prescribes,
> and it reflects a real North Carolina fact: a property inside municipal limits is taxed by **two**
> jurisdictions, and a student who computes only one has under-stated the liability. **No North Carolina
> tax rate, city rate or county rate is stated anywhere in this build**; every problem supplies its rates
> as givens.

## G. Property valuation (appraisal) calculations — delivered in Module NC-13

**1. Assessed value and ad valorem property taxes.** The Commission states the requirement precisely:

> *"amount of real property tax using a given tax rate and assessed value; **students should be able to
> perform calculations using either a tax rate in dollars per $100 of assessed value [NC method] or a mill
> rate [National method]**"*

**Both methods, and the Commission labels which is which.** **Dollars per $100 of assessed value is the
North Carolina method**; the **mill rate is the national method.** The state section of the licence
examination and the national section will not ask the same way, and a student who knows only one will lose
items on the other. *(Also delivered in Module NC-04, where property taxes are taught.)*

**2. Sales comparison approach and comparative market analysis.**

> **Required Activity, transcribed:** *"**Have students to prepare a CMA that includes superior and
> inferior comps to arrive at a range for probable sales price.**"*
>
> **Delivered in Module NC-13.** Note the deliverable: **a range**, not a single number — which matches
> the Syllabus's statement that adjusted comp prices *"should provide a tight range of value"* and its
> prohibition on **simple averaging.** And note that **both directions of adjustment must appear**:
> superior comps adjusted **down**, inferior comps adjusted **up**, and **never the subject.**

**3. Cost approach.**

> **Required Activity, transcribed:** *"**Provide examples of estimating replacement cost using the square
> foot method. Have students determine a single estimate of value of at least one property by the cost
> approach using the replacement cost method with the square foot method and age/life depreciation for
> estimating the value of the building.**"*
>
> **Delivered in Module NC-13.**
>
> The Commission specifies the exact combination to be worked: **replacement cost** (not reproduction),
> by the **square foot method** (not quantity survey or unit-in-place), with **age/life depreciation**
> (not market abstraction or breakdown) — and it wants **a single estimate of value for at least one whole
> property**, which means the land component must be estimated separately and added back, and **land is
> never depreciated.**

**4. Income capitalization approach.**

> **Required Activity, transcribed:** *"**Provide an example of estimating value using the Direct
> Capitalization Formula. Have students determine a single estimate of value of at least one property by
> the direct capitalization approach. Provide the CAP rate. There will be no calculations using a GRM.**"*
>
> **Delivered in Module NC-13.**
>
> Three instructions, all binding. **The CAP rate is provided** — students do not derive it. **At least one
> whole-property estimate** must be produced by direct capitalization: **Value = NOI ÷ CAP rate**, with
> **debt service excluded from NOI.** And — stated by the Commission in its own words —
> ***"There will be no calculations using a GRM."***
>
> That last sentence also resolves the formula discrepancy flagged in Module NC-13. The Syllabus's two GRM
> lines are not algebraically consistent with each other, and **the Commission excludes GRM from the
> required calculations entirely.** GRM remains examinable as a **concept** — what it relates, and which
> property class it serves — and **no item in this course requires a GRM computation.**

## Module NC-16 engagement check

Eleven items, delivered as in Module NC-01, and **every one of them is a calculation.** This is the
course's integrated mathematics assessment: items are drawn across Parts A through G and each is set in
the transaction context that produces it, because the Commission's stated reason for the integrated
approach is that it *"helps assure that students understand how various mathematical functions relate to
the various areas of real estate practice."*

The 80 percent engagement threshold and the 100 percent applied threshold are **StateCourse standards**;
the **75 percent** end-of-course standard is the Commission's, under Commission Rule 58H .0207 as the
Syllabus quotes it.

**Every variable local property-tax rate, factor, CAP rate, commission rate and amortisation figure used
in an item is supplied inside the item as a given.** North Carolina's statutory excise-tax rate is taught
from current G.S. 105-228.30. **No item requires a
GRM calculation**, because the Commission states there will be none. **No item asks a student to complete
a settlement statement**, because the Commission states the licence examination will not.
