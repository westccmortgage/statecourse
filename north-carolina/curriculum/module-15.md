## A. Ownership of real property in NC

### A.1 Joint tenancy in NC

> **Note:** *"Though **used infrequently in NC**, joint tenancy is **widely used in many other states and
> is also tested on the National exam section**."*

That note is an examination instruction. The student must learn **two** versions: the general rule the
national section tests, and the North Carolina rule the state section tests. Module NC-06 introduced this;
here it is completed.

- ***"Title must intentionally create joint tenancy."***
- **Four required unities/elements** — *"possession, interest, time, title."*
  - **Note:** *"**In NC, percentage of ownership interest can be unequal, if clearly stated in title.**"*
- **Right of survivorship:**
  - ***"In NC, survivorship not automatic."***
  - ***"Attorney must draft exact wording in title required for right of survivorship."***
- **Termination:** *by agreement*; *partition suit.*

Two North Carolina departures from the common form, and both are examinable:

**Unequal interests are permitted** if the title says so — even though "interest" is one of the four
unities. And **survivorship is not automatic**; it must be created by exact language drafted by an
**attorney**. Elsewhere, a joint tenancy carries survivorship by definition. In North Carolina it does
not, and a student who assumes it does will answer the state section wrongly.

Note also who does the drafting: **an attorney.** This is the drafting prohibition from Module NC-09
appearing in the ownership context. A broker does not supply survivorship language.

### A.2 Tenancy by the entirety

- ***"Available only to legally married couples."***
  - ***"In NC, this is default ownership for buyers married at the time of purchase."***
  - *"Buyer should **consult with a real estate attorney** to explore what ownership type is desired."*
  - ***"Other types of ownership do not automatically convert to tenancy by the entirety if property
    owners marry each other later."***
- **Equal undivided interest.**
- **Four required equal unities/elements** — *"possession, interest, time, title."*
- ***"Automatic right of survivorship that cannot be waived; overrides a will."***
- *"Conveyance requires **voluntary signatures of both spouses**."*
  - **Note:** ***"A spouse cannot convey ½ interest."***
- **Termination:** *by agreement*; *divorce*; *death of a spouse.*

Set this against A.1 and the contrast is the examinable structure:

| | **Joint tenancy in NC** | **Tenancy by the entirety in NC** |
|---|---|---|
| Who may hold it | Anyone | **Legally married couples only** |
| Interests | **May be unequal** if stated in title | **Equal undivided interest** |
| Right of survivorship | ***Not automatic*** — attorney-drafted wording required | ***Automatic, cannot be waived, overrides a will*** |
| Default for married buyers | No | ***Yes, if married at the time of purchase*** |

**"Overrides a will"** is worth its own sentence. A spouse who devises entireties property to a third
party devises nothing — survivorship takes it first. And the **conversion rule** runs one way only:
buying as tenants in common and marrying afterwards does **not** convert the ownership.

Module NC-12's spousal signature rule connects here. Because a spouse **cannot convey a half interest**,
both spouses must sign the deed of trust even where only one signed the note.

### A.3 NC common interest community (hybrid) ownership

#### A.3.a Condominium ownership

Governed by **horizontal property acts, such as the North Carolina Condominium Act of 1986 [NCGS Chapter
47C]**, under which the Commission lists:

- **Application**
- **Developer's disclosure requirements**
- **Public offering statement**
- ***"Buyer's 7-day rescission period on purchase of new condo"***
- ***"Resale certificate for resale condos; no right of rescission"***

Ownership structure:

- *"Individual unit owner holds **fee simple 'title to airspace'** for unit."*
- *"Individual unit owners own an **undivided share of common elements** with other unit owners"* —
  including *"land and common areas, such as hallways, elevators, recreational facilities, etc."*
  - ***"Own as tenants in common without right of partition."***
  - *"Periodic fees/assessments charged to unit owners."*
  - *"Possible **exclusive use of limited common elements**."*

Three points a broker uses constantly. **New condo: 7-day rescission. Resale condo: resale certificate,
no rescission.** The buyer of a resale unit does not get the developer's cooling-off period, and a broker
who tells them otherwise has misstated the Act. **Tenants in common *without* right of partition** — the
ordinary tenancy in common carries a partition right; here it is removed, because a co-owner suing to
partition the hallway would end the building.

#### A.3.b Townhouse ownership

- *"Includes **individual ownership of land**, at least the land upon which the unit sits."*
- *"Usually attached by **party walls**."*
- Common elements *"**owned by the HOA**"*, maintenance funded by periodic fees/assessments.
- *"Most townhouse developments are subject to the **NC Planned Community Act [NCGS Chapter 47F]**."*

The line between the two is **who owns what.** A condominium owner owns **airspace** and an **undivided
share** of the common elements. A townhouse owner owns **land**, and the **HOA** owns the common
elements. Different structures, different Acts — **47C** for condominiums, **47F** for planned
communities.

### A.4 Manufactured (factory built) housing *(Level 1 — this subtopic)*

**Manufactured housing (mobile home)** *[NCGS 143-143.9(6)]*:

- *"Built to **federal HUD standards**; **red HUD certification label**."*
- *"**Titled and regulated by NC Dept. of Motor Vehicles (DMV)**."*
- *"**Permanent non-removable steel chassis**."*
- ***"Personal property until converted to real property"*** *[NCGS 47-20.6 and 105-273(13)]* by all three of:
  - *"Attaching to permanent foundation on land owned by owner of home;"*
  - *"Removing wheels, axle, and moving hitch; and"*
  - *"Filing **affidavit of conversion to cancel DMV title**"*

**Modular housing** *[NCGS 105-164.3(143) and 143-139.1]*:

- *"Built to **state building code**."*
- *"**State inspection label with serial number**."*
- *"Assembled by builder on-site."*
- ***"Becomes real property as soon as assembled on building site."***

The pair is examinable as a contrast. **Federal HUD standard, red label, DMV title, chassis, and three
steps to become realty** — manufactured. **State building code, state inspection label, real property on
assembly** — modular. And the practical consequence for a broker: a manufactured home that has **not**
been converted is **personal property**, so it is not conveyed by the deed.

### A.5 Adverse possession

- **General requirements**
- **Statutory time periods for continuous adverse possession:**
  - ***"20 years for regular adverse possession in NC"***
  - ***"7 years if under color of title in NC"***

Both figures are the Commission's, transcribed. **Color of title** — a defective instrument that appears
to convey — cuts the period from twenty years to seven.

### A.6 NC agricultural fixtures

- **Definition**
- *"Per common law, **became part of the real property and could not be removed at the end of the
  lease**."*
- *"**Modern trend is to treat as trade fixtures.**"*
- *"**Lease terms should address right of removal.**"*

Module NC-03 taught fixtures and trade fixtures. This is the North Carolina agricultural wrinkle, and the
practical instruction is the last line: **put it in the lease.** Where a doctrine is in transition, the
document settles it.

### A.7 The UCC and fixtures

*"Effect of **NC Uniform Commercial Code (UCC) [NCGS 25-9-313]** concerning **security interests in
fixtures**."* Named by the Commission as a subtopic; **NCGS 25-9-313 has not been read by this project**
and no rule of priority, filing or perfection is stated from it.

## B. NC Connor Act — NCGS 47-18

> 1. *"Requires **all conveyances of interest in real property be recorded to be effective against claims
>    by third parties**."*
> 2. ***"A pure race statute."***
>    **Note:** *"**Failure to record does not affect the validity of the document between the parties to
>    the instrument.**"*
> 3. *"Documents required to be recorded **in the county where the property is located**."*

**Pure race** is the phrase to understand, not memorise. Under a pure race statute, **the first to record
wins** — full stop. Not the first to record in good faith. Not the first to record without notice of the
earlier deed. **First to record.** A second buyer who knew perfectly well about the first sale and raced
to the register of deeds still takes the property as against the world.

And the Commission's note draws the boundary of that harshness precisely: the unrecorded deed is still
**valid between the parties who signed it.** It fails against **third parties**, which is the only place
the race matters.

This is the reason Module NC-05's recording material exists, and the reason C.2 in Module NC-11 flagged
uncancelled deeds of trust. In a pure race state, the register of deeds is not a convenience — it is
where ownership is decided.

## D. NC Statute of Frauds — NCGS 22-2

> **1. Certain contracts must be in writing to be enforceable:**
> - *"**All contracts to convey interest in real property**"*
> - ***"A lease that exceeds 3 years from the 'making'/signing of the lease agreement"***
> - *"**Contract modifications**"*
>
> **2.** *"**Verbal negotiations and agreements are not enforceable**"* *[see the Parol Evidence Rule in
> Section XI — Module NC-11]*
>
> **3.** *"**Agency agreements are considered employment contracts, not real estate contracts, and are
> excluded from Statute of Frauds; Commission rules require agency agreements to be in writing.**"*

Three examinable points, and the third is the subtle one.

The **3-year lease rule** is measured *"from the making/signing of the lease agreement"* — not from the
commencement of the term. A one-year lease signed today to begin in three years exceeds three years from
signing.

**Contract modifications** are inside the statute. An oral amendment to a written contract to convey is
not enforceable, which is the practical reason every change goes on paper.

And **agency agreements are outside the Statute of Frauds entirely** — they are employment contracts, not
contracts to convey. So what requires them in writing? **Commission rules**, not the statute. That is why
Module NC-08's buyer agency material turned on Rule 58A .0104 rather than on NCGS 22-2, and why Module
NC-14's property management writing requirement was a rule and not a statute. A student who answers "the
Statute of Frauds" to a question about why a listing agreement must be written has given the wrong source.

## E. NC Residential Property Disclosure Act — NCGS 47E; Commission Rules 58A .0114 and .0019

### E.1 Basic requirements

- *"Requirement for **most sellers of 1-4 residential units**."*
- **Major points:**
  - ***"Seller required to provide completed disclosure forms to prospective buyers prior to presentation
    of 1st offer."***
  - *"Seller **not usually required to disclose facts about property condition** (other than presence of
    lead-based paint) or if previous owners have severed mineral, oil, or gas rights."*
  - *"**Seller must keep information current and accurate.**"*
  - *"Seller **may be held civilly liable for misrepresentation** by the courts **if seller knows a
    problem exists and checks 'No' rather than 'No Representation'**, or if seller **does not disclose any
    known latent defects that a diligent buyer cannot access**."*

That fourth point is the heart of the Act. North Carolina does not compel the seller to describe the
property — it compels the seller to be **honest about the choice they make.** **"No Representation"** is
lawful. **"No"** when the seller knows the answer is yes is **misrepresentation.** The liability attaches
to the false answer, not to the silence.

### E.2 The two mandatory forms

**Residential Property and Owners' Association Disclosure Statement (RPOADS)** and the **Mineral and Oil
and Gas Rights Mandatory Disclosure Statement (MOG)**.

> **Note:** *"Forms, **REC 4.22 and REC 4.25**, are available at www.ncrec.gov."*

**Delivery.** *"Method of delivery is not prescribed; frequently attached to MLS entry."*

**Buyer's right to cancel the contract** — the Commission lists **timing of cancellation** and **written
cancellation notice** as subtopics. **NCGS 47E has not been obtained**, and **this module states no
cancellation period.** Instructors supply it from the statute.

**Broker's responsibilities**, transcribed in full because every one is examinable:

> i. *"Advise seller client of disclosure requirements and the need for continued accuracy and timely
>    delivery"*
> ii. *"Provide seller with copies of required disclosure form(s)"*
> iii. *"Assist seller in assessing the property for accurate completion of disclosure"*
>      **Note: *"Broker must not complete the form(s) for the seller."***
> iv. *"Assist with timely delivery of completed forms"*
> v. *"Retain executed disclosures"*
> vi. *"**Must disclose any material fact regarding a listed property about which the broker knows or
>     should reasonably be expected to be aware, even if the seller legally chooses not to disclose such
>     material fact or marks No Representation regarding the matter**"*
>
> **Note:** *"**If the seller demands that the listing agent agree to not disclose a material defect (or
> other material fact) as a condition of granting the listing, the listing should be refused.**"*

Read iii and vi together and the broker's position is exact. **Assist, but do not complete** — the
disclosure is the seller's statement, and a broker who fills it in has made it partly their own. And the
broker's **own** material fact duty is **independent of the seller's choice.** A seller may lawfully mark
"No Representation"; the broker who knows the roof leaks must still disclose it.

The refusal note is the Commission telling brokers to walk away from a listing. It is stated without
qualification: **the listing should be refused.**

### E.3 Common RPOADS exemptions

### E.4 Unique facts about MOG

> i. *"Same points as above for RPOADS with following differences"*
> ii. ***"Seller must disclose if they have severed or will sever MOG rights; No Representation is not an
>     option on this point."***
> iii. *"Common exemptions match those for RPOADS excluding the transfers where MOG is still mandated."*

Point ii is the whole reason MOG is a separate form. On the RPOADS, "No Representation" is available
throughout. On the **severance question**, it is **not.** The seller must answer.

## F. NC Good Funds Act — NCGS 45A *(Level 1 — this subtopic)*

> **1.** *"Prior to any disbursement of funds, Act requires **settlement agent** to:*
> - *"**Verify funds paid by any party to the transaction**"*
> - *"**Record the deed and deed of trust**"*
>
> **2.** ***"No disbursement of funds should happen at settlement meeting."***

This is the statutory basis for the Settlement-versus-Closing distinction taught in Module NC-11. The
deed is **recorded first**, funds are **verified first**, and only then is anything disbursed. A broker
who tells a seller they will walk out of the settlement meeting with a cheque has described a transaction
North Carolina law does not permit.

## G. NC anti-discriminatory regulations

### G.1 NC State Fair Housing Act — NCGS Chapter 41A *(Level 3 — this subtopic)*

This is one of only two Level 3 assignments in the section. The student must **analyse**, not recall.

**a. Unlawful discriminatory housing practices.**

> **Note:** *"**Provisions of the State Act are virtually identical to those of the Federal Act. Thus, a
> violation of either Act is generally a violation of the other Act.**"*

**b. Exemptions.**

> **Note:** *"**Exemptions under the NC State Act are substantially different from those under the
> Federal Act; when there is a conflict between the federal and state acts, the more restrictive law
> applies.**"*

Those two notes are the entire Level 3 problem, and they point in opposite directions on purpose.

**The prohibitions are virtually identical.** **The exemptions are substantially different.** So a
transaction that is *exempt* federally may not be exempt under North Carolina law — and the tie-break the
Commission states is **the more restrictive law applies.** A broker who reasons only from the federal
exemptions taught in Module NC-09 will get North Carolina problems wrong, and will get them wrong in the
direction that produces a violation.

**The North Carolina exemption list is in NCGS Chapter 41A, which this project has not obtained.** This
module states **no exemption**, federal or state, from an unread source, and teaches the **structure** —
identical prohibitions, different exemptions, more restrictive law controls — which is what the Commission
states itself.

**c. Enforcement**, transcribed:

> i. *"A NC-based complaint filed with **HUD will be referred to the NC Human Relations Commission** for
>    investigation because **NC has an equivalent state fair housing law**"*
> ii. *"By administrative procedure through the **NC Human Relations Commission**; **complaints must be
>     filed within 1 year**"*
> iii. *"By **private lawsuit in state court**; **complaints must be filed within 1 year**"*

**The NC Human Relations Commission**, not the Real Estate Commission — a third regulator, after the Real
Estate Commission and the Appraisal Board. **One year** for both routes, and the figure is the
Commission's, transcribed.

## I. Other disclosure considerations in NC *(Level 3 except as noted)*

### I.1 Stigmatized properties — NCGS 39-50; 42-14.2

> a. ***"Stigmatized/psychologically impacted property is not a material fact in NC unless questioned by
>    the buyer."***
>
> b. **Examples not considered material facts:**
> - *"Previous occupant **died or had a serious illness** while occupying the property"*
> - *"**Reputation as a haunted property**"*
> - *"**A convicted sex offender required to register with law enforcement authorities occupies, occupied,
>   or resides near a property**"* *[based on national law nicknamed "Megan's Law"]*

Module NC-10 established the material fact framework. This is its negative boundary, and the third example
is the one brokers most often get wrong. **A registered sex offender living in or near the property is not
a material fact in North Carolina.** The registry is public; the buyer may search it; the broker is not
required to volunteer it.

The qualifier **"unless questioned by the buyer"** governs all three. A stigma is not disclosed on the
broker's initiative — but a **direct question must be answered truthfully.** Module NC-10's honesty duty
does not switch off because the subject is not a material fact.

Set this against Module NC-10's **material** list — EIFS, polybutylene that leaked, a former meth house —
and the organising principle appears: **North Carolina treats physical conditions of the property as
material and reputational or historical associations as not.**

### I.2 Unfair or Deceptive Trade Practice Act — NCGS 75-1.1 *(Level 1 — this subtopic)*

> a. *"Applies to actions of real estate brokers (and some property owners), **including
>    advertising/postings on internet and social media**"*
> b. *"Creates legal cause of action with **the possibility of treble damages**"*

**Treble damages** — three times actual damages — is why this statute matters more than its Level 1
assignment suggests. And its reach expressly includes **internet and social media advertising**, which is
where a broker is most likely to be careless.

### I.3 The doctrine of caveat emptor

> a. *"Latin phrase (**Let the buyer beware**) indicating that **the burden of discovery is on the buyer**
>    to investigate because they are purchasing the property **'as is'**"*
>
> b. *"In NC, **seller is not required to disclose property condition information** (other than known
>    presence of lead-based paint), **but may not commit fraud**."*
>    **Note:** ***"This is the reason for the 'No Representation' option on the RPOADS."***
>
> c. *"Difficult to bring action against the seller, but **seller could be held civilly liable if**:*
> - *"Engages in **fraud or misrepresentation**"*
> - *"**Induces the buyer to forego an inspection**"*
> - *"Does not disclose **seller-known latent defect that a diligent buyer cannot access**"*
> - *"**Does not provide RPOADS & MOG (if required) prior to presentation of 1st offer**"*
>
> **Note:** ***"Broker's disclosure responsibilities under Real Estate License Law are not affected by
> doctrine of caveat emptor; brokers must still volunteer material facts to all clients and customers."***

This part explains the whole architecture of North Carolina disclosure, and it explains why the seller and
the broker stand in completely different positions.

**The seller** operates under caveat emptor. They need not describe the property. They may mark "No
Representation." Their liability begins at **fraud**, at **inducing the buyer to skip an inspection**, at
**concealing a known latent defect**, and at **failing to deliver the forms.**

**The broker** operates under Licence Law, which caveat emptor does not touch. The broker **must
volunteer material facts to clients and customers alike.**

So in the same transaction, on the same fact, the seller may lawfully say nothing and the broker may not.
That is not an inconsistency — it is two different bodies of law addressing two different people, and the
Commission states it plainly three separate times in this Syllabus.

### I.4 Square footage

> a. **Verify and properly report building square footage**
> - ***"Square footage is not a material fact in NC, but must be accurate if reported."***
> - *"**Brokers are expected to possess the expertise necessary to accurately measure, calculate, and
>   report the square footage of most buildings.**"*
> - **"Broker should NOT rely on:"** *"Property owner's personal statement"*; *"Blueprints"*; *"Tax
>   records"*; *"Information from an earlier transaction."*
> - *"A broker working with a buyer **can reasonably rely on the listing agent's reported square footage
>   unless there is an error that would be obvious to a prudent broker**."*
>
> b. **The Residential Square Footage Guidelines**
> - ***"In NC, the square footage of residential living area is calculated on exterior measurements."***
> - **Above grade vs. below grade area**
> - **Heated Living Area criteria**

The four prohibited sources are the examinable list, and notice what they have in common: every one of
them is **somebody else's figure.** The Commission's position is that the **broker measures.** Tax records
are not evidence. Blueprints are not evidence. The last MLS listing is not evidence.

The one permitted reliance is narrow and asymmetric: a **buyer's agent** may rely on the **listing
agent's** figure — unless the error would be **obvious to a prudent broker**. The listing agent has no
such shelter, because the listing agent is the one who measured.

And **"not a material fact, but must be accurate if reported"** is the North Carolina rule stated exactly.
A broker need not report square footage at all. Having reported it, they own it.

### I.5 Basic construction *(Level 1 — this subtopic)*

The Commission's note defines the demand exactly:

> *"The purpose of this sub-section is to provide the student with an **elementary knowledge** of basic
> construction principles, techniques and terminology which will **assist the student in
> listing/showing/selling** such properties. **Study is limited to the wood-frame (including brick veneer)
> construction because of its predominant use in NC.**"*

**Elementary knowledge**, **wood-frame only**. That is the boundary, and it keeps this subtopic
proportionate to its Level 1 assignment inside a 4.5-hour section.

**a. Basic construction types.** **One story (ranch)** — *"generally the most expensive to build per
square foot"*; **1½ story**; **multi-story (two, 2½, or three levels)**; **split-level.**

*(The ranch cost note is counter-intuitive and therefore examinable: a one-storey house spreads the
expensive roof and foundation over the fewest square feet.)*

**b. Foundations.** Components: **footings, slab, foundation wall, piers.** Major types: **concrete slab,
basement, crawl space.** Plus **termite protection** — *"chemical treatment of ground near foundation
walls and piers"* and *"pressure-treated wood"* — **ventilation**, and **waterproofing.**

**c. Framing.** Systems: **platform (most common)**, **balloon**, **post and beam.** Floor components:
**sill (also sill plate or mud sill), floor joists, girders, bridging, subfloor.** Wall components:
**studs, headers, top plate, sole plate.** Roof and ceiling: designs **gable, hip, shed, flat, mansard,
gambrel**; components **ceiling joists, rafters, ridge board, eaves.**

**d. Exterior walls.** **Sheathing**; **tar paper (asphalt treated paper or felt) or house wrap**; siding
in **wood, manufactured wood, fiber cement, brick veneer, vinyl, stucco, synthetic stucco (EIFS).**

*(EIFS appears here as a siding type. Module NC-10 recorded the Commission's separate rule that **EIFS is
a material fact in North Carolina even if fully replaced.** Both are true and both are examinable.)*

**e. Windows and exterior doors.** **Sliding windows:** double-hung, horizontal sliding. **Swinging
windows:** casement, jalousie, hopper, awning. **Fixed windows:** picture, bay, bow. **Components:** sill,
header, jamb, sash, muntins, mullions. **Door types:** flush, panel, sliding glass, French.

*(Muntins divide the panes within a sash; mullions separate whole window units. The two words are
routinely confused and the distinction is examinable.)*

**f. Roofing components.** **Roof sheathing (decking)**; **roofing felt**; outer covering in **asphalt and
fiberglass composition shingles, wood shingles or shakes, tile**; **eaves** — **fascia board, soffit,
frieze board**; **pitch.**

**g. Insulation.** Materials: **fiberglass batts or blankets, loose fill, foam.** **Weatherstripping.**
**Moisture control: vapor barriers and attic ventilation.** And the figures the Commission states:

> **Required insulation under the North Carolina Uniform Residential Building Code:**
> **Walls R-13 · Ceilings R-30 · Floors R-19**
>
> **Note:** *"**Higher R-values are required in the colder, mountainous counties of northwestern North
> Carolina.**"*

**These three R-values are transcribed from the Commission's Syllabus.** **The North Carolina Uniform
Residential Building Code has not been obtained by this project**, and **no county list, alternative
R-value or code section is stated.** Instructors verify current values against the Code before teaching
them.

**h. Heating and air conditioning.** **Power sources:** sun, gas, oil, electricity, coal, wood.
**Measured in British Thermal Units (BTUs).** **Methods:** solar, warm air, hot water, steam, electric
wire. **Types:** central forced warm air (heat pumps or gas furnaces with fan and duct distribution);
gravity warm air; electric baseboard; space heating (wood stoves, gas heaters, kerosene heaters, electric
heaters, radiators, fireplaces); solar. **Air conditioning:** central forced-air; room units.
**Combination: heat pump.**

**i. Government regulation.** **North Carolina Uniform Residential Building Code**; **building permits** —
**certificate of occupancy (CO)**, **HUD minimum standards**; **contractor licensing.**

### I.6 Subdivision approval procedures — NCGS 160D, Article 8

*(Commission's Q&A brochure: **Residential Subdivisions and Planned Communities**.)*

**Subdivision**; **exemptions**; **typical approval procedures:**

- **Preliminary planning phase**
- *"Review and approval phase **usually performed by multiple municipal departments**"*
- *"**Final subdivision plat approved and recorded with register of deeds**"*

**Selling lots in an unapproved NC subdivision** *[NCGS 160D-807]* — and this is the part a broker must
know cold:

> - ***"Illegal to subdivide or sell land prior to recordation of final approval of the subdivision
>   plat."***
> - **"Contracts to sell or lease allowed after approval of preliminary plat, if contract:"**
>   - *"Includes **copy of preliminary plat** and requires owner to **provide buyer a copy of the recorded
>     final plat prior to closing**"*
>   - *"**Clearly notifies buyer/tenant that until the final plat has been approved**: changes to the
>     preliminary plat are possible, **and** the contract **may be terminated without breach by
>     buyer/tenant if final recorded plat differs in any material respect from the preliminary plat**"*
>   - *"**Buyer/tenant has time after delivery of final recorded plat to close**"*

A narrow, conditional window. **Selling before final recordation is illegal**; **contracting after
preliminary approval is permitted only on those three conditions**, and the middle one hands the buyer a
termination right without breach.

### I.7 NC Subdivision Street Disclosure Law — NCGS 136-102.6

*(Commission's Q&A brochure: **Residential Subdivisions and Planned Communities**.)*

- **Developer requirements.**
- ***"Responsibility for street maintenance is a material fact and should be in writing"*** *(e.g., a
  private road maintenance agreement).*

> **Note:** *"**Do not assume that public street designation on the plat equates to governmental
> acceptance of maintenance and repair responsibilities. Regardless of the age of the subdivision, brokers
> should verify private or public status of the streets before making any representation as to their
> status.**"*

Street maintenance responsibility **is** a material fact — unlike square footage, unlike stigma. And the
plat is not evidence of it. *"Regardless of the age of the subdivision"* forecloses the obvious excuse:
an old neighbourhood is not thereby a public-street neighbourhood.

### I.8 Onsite septic systems

> a. *"**Strongly recommend soil suitability (perc) test for unimproved land**"* — for a **seller prior to
>    listing** to determine appropriate listing price, and for a **buyer prior to making an offer** to
>    determine appropriate offer terms.
>    **Note:** ***"It is a material fact if unimproved property will not support a septic system."***
>
> b. *"**Obtain septic permit, if possible, to verify capacity** of septic system and/or any system
>    restrictions for improved property that is not connected to a municipal or community sewage system"*
> - ***"Capacity indicated by number of bedrooms"***
> - ***"Maximum occupancy is 2 persons per permitted bedroom"***
> - ***"Brokers are subject to disciplinary action for misrepresentation of occupancy limits for property
>   served by an onsite septic system"***
>
> c. ***"Straight-piping is illegal and a material fact."***

Module NC-11 recorded the advertising side of this — a broker cannot advertise more bedrooms than the
septic permit allows. Here is the rest of it. **The permit states capacity in bedrooms**; **occupancy is
two persons per permitted bedroom**; **misrepresenting it is a disciplinary matter.**

Two further material facts are declared here and belong on Module NC-10's list: **unimproved land that
will not support a septic system**, and **straight-piping**, which is also **illegal**.

### I.9 Liens against the real property

**a. Mechanics' lien (materialman's lien):**

> - *"On all NC residential or commercial construction projects **where the cost equals or exceeds
>   $40,000**"*
> - *"**A lien agent must be appointed**"*
> - *"**Owner-occupied single family residences are exempt**"*
> - *"All service providers must **file a Notice to Lien Agent**"*
>   - *"Ability to file a mechanics' lien **within 120 days after last day of providing services or
>     materials**"*
>   - ***"Lien priority effective from 1st day of providing services or materials"***

The last line is why this appears in a prelicensing course. **A mechanics' lien takes priority from the
first day work began** — which can be **before** a deed of trust was recorded, and before a buyer ever
saw the property. In a pure race state, this is the one lien that can beat the recording race, because its
priority date is not its filing date.

> **Required Activity, transcribed:** *"**Provide a timeline example of how mechanic's liens may take
> priority over other recorded liens.**"* **Delivered** — worked as a dated sequence in this module, using
> only the **$40,000 threshold**, the **120-day filing window** and the **first-day priority rule** stated
> above, with all other dates supplied inside the example.
>
> *(Source note: this Required Activity is printed at a page boundary in the Syllabus, adjacent to a second
> Required Activity concerning commission and property management fee calculations. The lien timeline is
> placed here because mechanics' liens are this section's subject; the fee calculation activity is carried
> in Module NC-16 with the same note.)*

**b. NC Commercial Real Estate Broker Lien** *(Level 1 — this subtopic).* Named without content. **Not
obtained; no requirement, deadline or procedure is stated here.**
