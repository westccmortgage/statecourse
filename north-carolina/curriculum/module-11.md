## Learning objectives

1. State the general rules for interpreting contracts.
2. State the prohibition on drafting and what a broker may lawfully do with a preprinted form.
3. State where standardized forms come from and which forms the Commission itself authors.
4. State the two provisions Commission Rule 58A .0112(b) prohibits in a broker-provided form.
5. **Explain the North Carolina due diligence model and why it differs from a contingency model.**
   *(Level 3)*
6. Distinguish the Due Diligence Fee from the Earnest Money Deposit.
7. Distinguish Settlement from Closing.
8. **Apply the 3-day offer delivery rule and the offer-disclosure rules.** *(Level 3)*
9. Describe the settlement agent's role and what a broker provides them.
10. Work through the buyer's due diligence process and the pre-closing matters.
11. State the broker's settlement statement responsibility.

## A. Introduction

### A.1 General rules for the interpretation of contracts

Six, transcribed:

- **The parol evidence rule.**
- **Written overrides oral.**
- **Contracts are interpreted as a whole.**
- **Words will be interpreted per their ordinary meaning unless other intent is clearly noted.**
- **Written overrides preprinted language, if there is a conflict.**
- **The writer of ambiguous language will be held liable for the confusion; the court generally rules in
  favour of the party that did NOT draft the confusing language.**

**The last two together explain why a broker must not write into a form.** Handwriting overrides the
preprinted text, and ambiguity is construed against whoever wrote it — so an improvised sentence both
outranks the professionally drafted form and is read against the person who added it.

### A.2 Drafting by brokers is prohibited

NCGS 93A-6(a)(11); Commission Rule 58A .0111; NCGS 84-2.1.

- **Drafting constitutes unauthorized practice of law.**
- **"Brokers are legally permitted to 'fill in the blanks' of preprinted forms."**
- **Instructor Note:** *"Have an attorney draft any unique contract language."*

### A.3 Sources of standardized sales contract forms

- **REALTORS® may obtain and use the contract forms created by NC REALTORS® as part of their membership
  benefits.**
- **Non-REALTOR® brokers may NOT initiate use of forms bearing only the REALTOR® logo.**
- **Non-REALTOR® brokers may obtain and use the sales contract forms and standard addenda that are
  JOINTLY APPROVED by North Carolina REALTORS® and the North Carolina Bar Association (NCBA), bearing
  only the NCBA logo/approval, through an attorney or forms vendor.**

**Instructor Note, transcribed — and it is important for understanding what the Commission does and
does not control:**

> *"**NCREC does not create the standardized sales contract forms used by the majority of NC
> licensees.** The **only** standardized forms authored by the Commission are:*
>
> - ***Working With Real Estate Agents Disclosure***
> - ***Residential Property and Owners' Association Disclosure Statement (RPOADS)***
> - ***Mineral and Oil and Gas Mandatory Disclosure Statement (MOG)***"

**Three Commission forms. Everything else in a North Carolina residential file comes from NC REALTORS®
and the North Carolina Bar Association.** *(None of the three Commission forms was obtained for this
project; each is recorded as a gap.)*

## B. The Offer to Purchase and Contract *(Level 3)*

### B.1 Requirements for preprinted sales contract forms provided by a broker — Rule 58A .0112

- **The form must include 19 items/issues required by rule.** **Instructor Note:** *"Students do NOT
  need to know the actual 19 items for the exam."*
- **Prohibited provisions — Rule 58A .0112(b):**
  1. **Brokerage compensation.**
  2. **Liability disclaimer of the broker.**
- **Prohibition against a broker including prohibited provisions or drafting contract language, EVEN AT
  THE INSTRUCTION OF A CLIENT.**

**The two prohibited provisions are worth understanding rather than memorising.** A broker's
compensation is not the buyer's and seller's business to settle in their purchase contract, and a broker
may not insert a clause disclaiming their own liability into the parties' agreement.

### B.2 Proper use of the OPC form

- **Primarily intended for the sale of EXISTING SINGLE-FAMILY RESIDENCES.**
- **Use standard addenda to tailor it to the needs of the parties.**
- **Do NOT use it for a lease-option agreement, a lease-purchase agreement, or an installment land
  contract.**

*(Module NC-07 F: those three require an attorney, and using a standard sales contract for them is
expressly warned against.)*

### B.3 General guidelines for contract completion

Transcribed from the Syllabus:

- **Type or print legibly.**
- **Fill in ALL the blanks; enter "N/A" or "none" as appropriate.**
- **Be precise; avoid abbreviations, acronyms, or unclear terminology.**
- **BOTH Buyer and Seller should initial and date EVERY change, addition, or deletion.**
- **Rewrite the offer if there are numerous changes; retain copies of all offers and contracts.**
- **Review ALL contract provisions with the parties and RECOMMEND THEY OBTAIN LEGAL ADVICE to answer
  legal questions.**
- **Instructor Note:** *"Be sure to highlight all the **Notes and Warnings** in the form."*

**"Fill in ALL the blanks" is a real discipline.** A blank left empty is an ambiguity, and A.1's last
rule says ambiguity is construed against the person who created it.

### B.4 The structural facts about the North Carolina contract

**This is what a student must understand about how the North Carolina residential contract works. Every
statement here is the Syllabus's own; none is a description of the form's text.**

#### Defined terms

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"Whenever a term that would not normally be capitalized appears in
the body of a contract and begins with a capital letter, it indicates that **the word or phrase is a
defined term** that can be found somewhere earlier in that contract. Most of the defined terms in Form
2-T are defined in Paragraph #1. **Words that appear in parenthesis** … **are optional words to be used
or ignored as appropriate.**"*

The Syllabus names the defined terms in Paragraph 1 as including: **Names of Seller and Buyer; Property
Description; Purchase Price; Earnest Money Deposit (EMD); Escrow Agent; Effective Date; Due Diligence,
comprising the Due Diligence Fee (DDF) and the Due Diligence Period (DDP); Settlement; Closing; and
Special Assessments.**

#### The due diligence model — the single most important structural fact in North Carolina practice

**Instructor Note, transcribed:**

> ***"There are NO FINANCING OR APPRAISAL CONTINGENCIES in this contract.** Buyer should be strongly
> encouraged to meet with a mortgage lender prior to starting a property search. Ideally, DDP should be
> long enough for Buyer to receive the appraisal and to feel confident in their ability to obtain loan
> approval."*

**And from Part E, transcribed:**

> *"**Standard 2-T contract is NOT contingent on loan approval.**"*
> *"**Inability of the buyer to secure financing AFTER the DDP would result in a buyer's BREACH of the
> contract.**"*

**Read those together, because they describe a system fundamentally different from the contingency model
taught in most national materials.**

In a contingency state, a buyer whose loan fails may terminate under the financing contingency and
recover their deposit. **In North Carolina, under the standard form, there is no financing contingency
at all.** Instead:

- The buyer has a **Due Diligence Period** during which they may terminate **for any reason or no
  reason**.
- The buyer pays a **Due Diligence Fee** for that right.
- **After the DDP ends**, the buyer's right to terminate without penalty is gone — and **a failure to
  obtain financing at that point is a breach.**

**Instructor Note on the DDP, transcribed:** *"Buyers should be **strongly advised to perform due
diligence activities as quickly as possible** to allow time for negotiation about any discovered issues
to be resolved **prior to the end of the DDP**. **The Buyer is not limited to the investigative items
listed** … **Seller is not obligated to engage in repair negotiations or extend the DDP.** If the Buyer
does not terminate the contract prior to the end of the DDP, **the Buyer may continue to investigate the
Property after the DDP but has WAIVED THEIR RIGHT TO TERMINATE without penalty unless there is a Seller
breach.**"*

**Three consequences a licensee must be able to state to a buyer:**

1. **The DDP is the buyer's whole protection**, and it runs whether or not anyone is working.
2. **The seller need not extend it and need not negotiate repairs.**
3. **After it ends, financing failure is the buyer's breach.**

**Which is why the Commission tells you to get the buyer to a lender BEFORE the search begins, and to
make the DDP long enough for the appraisal to arrive.**

#### DDF and EMD are different money

The **Due Diligence Fee** and the **Earnest Money Deposit** are separately defined and handled
differently. **The DDF is paid for the due diligence right; the EMD is a deposit held in escrow.**

> **Required Activity — prescribed by the Commission:** *"Use various scenarios to explore how **initial
> and additional types of EMD should be handled and when it should be delivered to the Escrow Agent** at
> various points during the transaction. Pay particular attention to application of **Commission Rule
> 58A .0116(b)(3-4)** regarding following Buyer's instructions for delivery to and the timing of deposit
> by the Escrow Agent."*
>
> **Worked activity:** classify the payee, instrument and offer status before starting the clock.
> Ordinary trust money with no exception is deposited within three **banking** days after receipt.
> Non-currency EMD received with a pending offer is deposited no later than three days after acceptance.
> A check payable to the seller for an option or due diligence fee, or to the designated escrow agent,
> is delivered or returned under the buyer's instructions and may not be retained beyond three business
> days after contract acceptance. A provisional broker delivers money upon receipt to the affiliated
> broker. Record receipt, acceptance, buyer instruction, delivery and deposit separately.

**Disputed EMD** — NCGS 93A-12; Rule 58A .0116(d). Module NC-10.

#### Settlement is not Closing

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"**The settlement meeting and closing do not always happen on the same
day**, especially if settlement occurs late in the day."*

**Two separate defined events**, and a licensee who uses the words interchangeably will mislead a client
about when they get keys. **Instructor Note on possession:** *"If possession date is critical to Buyer,
**Settlement should be scheduled so that there is sufficient time for Closing to occur before desired
possession date**."*

#### Other structural notes the Syllabus states

- **Prorations and adjustments are based on 365 days per year** — taxes on real property, taxes on
  personal property, rents if any, and dues.
- **Buyer representations and Seller representations** are **"informational only and do not rise to the
  level of a contract condition or contingency absent relevant attached addenda"** — though each party
  **"is liable for the accuracy of all representations made"** and the other **"is entitled to rely on
  the information."**
- **The condition of property / risk of loss paragraph SURVIVES expiration of the DDP.**
- **A party may in stated circumstances be entitled to a seven (7) day delay in Settlement.**
- **"Good and workmanlike manner" is a term of art** — **Instructor Note:** *"It means the work has been
  completed to the standard of the industry."*
- On seller breach: **"Buyer may terminate the Contract and receive a refund of any EMD and DDF paid"**,
  and may **"obtain reimbursement from Seller for reasonable costs incurred in conducting the Due
  Diligence"** — with the Commission's caution that **"an attorney should be consulted to determine if a
  breach has occurred."**
- **Instructor Note on addenda:** *"**A NC real estate broker is NOT permitted to suggest or draft any
  addenda for others** (unless they are an actively licensed NC attorney)."*
- **Instructor Note on the Notice Information page:** *"Entry of any Buyer or Seller contact information
  in the Notice Information section **authorizes communication directly to a Party rather than the
  standard practice of communicating through the agents**."*
- The signature page carries a **Wire Fraud Warning**.
- Mechanic's lien protection appears through an **Affidavit and Indemnification Agreement** — NCGS
  44A-8 — and **Designation of Lien Agent** where required by **NCGS 44A-11.1**.

*(Every item above is the Syllabus's statement about the form. **None of the form's own language is
reproduced here**, and the paragraph-level review the Commission requires remains outstanding.)*

### B.5 Commonly used standard addenda

The Syllabus names eight for review: **Additional Provisions Addendum (2A11-T); Back-Up Contract Addendum
(2A1-T); FHA/VA Financing Addendum (2A4-T); Lead-Based Paint or Lead-Based Paint Hazard Addendum
(2A9-T); Short Sale Addendum (2A14-T); Buyer Possession Before Closing Agreement (2A7-T); Seller
Possession After Closing Agreement (2A8-T); Vacation Rental Addendum (2A13-T).**

**Instructor Note on conflicts, transcribed:** *"In an event of a conflict between information in an
addendum and the contract, **the ADDENDUM will control** — **unless** the conflict involves **the
description of the Property or the identity of Buyer or Seller**, in which case **the CONTRACT will
control**."*

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"If a licensee is **not a REALTOR®, an attorney must prepare any
contract-related forms provided by the licensee to the parties if the content of the document will
affect the parties' legal rights.**"*

> **Required Activity — prescribed by the Commission:** *"Discuss the **advantages and disadvantages of
> allowing possession at a time other than Closing**. Be sure to address **insurance issues** and **what
> happens if the transaction does not close**. Remind brokers to **encourage their clients to consult an
> attorney** about risks/implications of possession before/after closing."*
>
> **This activity must be completed.** Module NC-04 E.2 supplies the insurance half: the **Unoccupied
> Building Exclusion**, the need to **keep insurance in place until recordation/closing**, and the risk
> that **failure to have proper coverage may leave the property uninsured for some period.**

## C. Sales Contract Procedures *(Level 3)*

### C.1 Submitting offers

- **All offers — including multiple offers and back-up offers — must be presented within 3 DAYS of the
  broker's receipt of the executed offer** — Rule 58A .0106(a) — **even if the Property is already under
  contract.**

### C.2 Disclosure of an offer and its terms

- Current Commission guidance says **the existence of multiple offers is not a material fact** and need
  not be volunteered. The listing broker follows the seller's direction about announcing their
  existence and communicates fairly, equally and honestly if authorized.
- **Rule 58A .0115 separately** prohibits sharing an offering party's **price or other material offer
  terms** with a competing party without the **express authority of the offering party**.
- Seller consent to announce multiple offers does not authorize disclosure of a competing buyer's
  price, due diligence fee, escalation language, closing date or another material term.

*(Module NC-10 B.5 states the same three rules. They appear in both sections of the Syllabus.)*

### C.3 Counteroffers and contract modifications

- **Retain ALL offers, even if rejected, amended, or replaced** — Rule 58A .0108.

### C.4 Delivery of a contract copy to all contract parties is required — Rule 58A .0106

## D. NC Closing Procedures

### D.1 The settlement agent

- **Selected by the buyer, to represent the buyer and the buyer's lender**; **may provide limited
  services to the seller**, e.g. preparing the deed.
- **The seller may elect to have their own attorney; more common in commercial transactions.**

**Provide information to the settlement agent as soon as possible** — the Syllabus lists eight items:

1. **A copy of the sales contract with all addenda and/or amendments.**
2. **Contact information for all parties.**
3. **Have the parties provide their social security numbers.**
4. **Contact information for the buyer's lender.**
5. **The seller's lender information for loan payoff.**
6. **A copy of any survey.**
7. **Copies of any required inspection reports.**
8. **The property insurance binder.**

### D.2 The buyer's due diligence process

**Buyer's loan** — as B.4 established: **not contingent on loan approval**, and **inability to secure
financing after the DDP is a buyer's breach.**

**Appraisal. Property survey.**

**Property insurance:**

- **The lender will usually require adequate homeowner insurance coverage in effect as of the day of
  closing.**
- **A flood elevation certificate and flood insurance are usually required by the lender if ANY PART of
  the financed property is in a FEMA special flood hazard zone.**
- **Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"It is **extremely important** for the buyer and seller to have
  appropriate insurance coverage **when the closing is delayed, when the buyer will occupy the property
  before closing, or when the seller will occupy the property after closing.** Failure to have proper
  coverage in these situations may result in **the property being uninsured for some period of time**."*

**Property inspections.** **Instructor Note:** *"Buyer should be advised to perform their own inspections
**instead of only relying on the information in the Residential Property and Owners' Association
Disclosure Statement**."*

- **Wood-destroying insect (termite) inspection.**
- **Property inspection usually performed by qualified professionals** — **"as soon as possible to allow
  time for any needed repair negotiations to be completed prior to the end of the DDP."**
- **Physical inspection by the buyer and the agent working with the buyer.** **Instructor Note:** *"A
  broker working with a buyer is **expected to recognize 'red flag' situations and to assist the buyer in
  checking into such situations**."* Examples the Syllabus gives to investigate: **tenants; easements and
  encroachments; recent construction or renovations; violations of zoning or restrictive covenants** —
  and **"Buyer should obtain and read a copy of any subdivision covenants prior to physical
  inspection."**

**Onsite septic system:**

- **Improved property** — **obtain a copy of the septic permit from the county health department, if
  possible.** **Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"**Broker cannot advertise more bedrooms than allowed by
  septic permit regardless of how many rooms are designed as bedrooms.**"*
- **Unimproved property** — **order a soil suitability test** to determine whether the property will
  support a septic system, **previously called a perc test**. **"Even lots within an approved subdivision
  should be evaluated."**

**The septic bedroom rule is a genuine advertising trap** and it is a North Carolina one: the permit
governs what may be advertised, not the floor plan.

**Other common inspections:** **radon; lead-based paint; asbestos; mould; well water.**

**Resolution of repair issues:**

- **Anything can be negotiated during the DDP.**
- **Repairs themselves do not have to be completed prior to the end of the DDP — only prior to
  Settlement.**
- **There are standard forms available to ratify repair agreements as part of the sales contract.**

**Review of covenants and homeowners' association documents** — **"especially important if the buyer is
planning to add improvements, enlarge existing structures, or use the property for business
purposes."**

**Other matters subject to government regulation:** **permitted property use; plans for adjacent
undeveloped property; public or private status of subdivision streets; school assignment.**

### D.3 Other pre-closing matters

- **Title examination by an attorney, including a personal property lien search and a mechanic's lien
  inquiry.**
- **Clearing of title defects by the seller, if needed.**
- **The loan closing package provided to the settlement agent by the buyer's lender.**
- **Brokers perform an advance review of the settlement statement, if available.**
- **Per the TRID rule, the lender must provide the Borrower's Closing Disclosure to the borrower at least
  3 business days before** consummation.

*(And Module NC-10 B.6: **the broker must review the settlement statement for accuracy and notify all
parties of any errors** — NCGS 93A-6(a)(14).)*
