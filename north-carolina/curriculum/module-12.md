# North Carolina Broker Prelicensing Course — Module NC-12

## Section XII · Financing & Settlement

**Commission Syllabus section:** XII — Financing & Settlement
**Instructional Level:** **1** except as noted. *Assigned by the Commission; providers and instructors
are **REQUIRED to comply**.* Within this section the Commission raises specific subtopics to **Level 2**
— Sale of Mortgaged Property, the General Categories of Residential First Mortgage Loans, Fixed-Rate
Level-Payment loans, Mortgage Priorities, Residential Lending Practices under TRID, Truth-in-Lending,
and RESPA — and raises **The Settlement Statement (Part J) to Level 3**. It also drops named subtopics
back to Level 1 inside otherwise Level-2 blocks: *Purchase Subject to Seller's Existing Mortgage*, *other
FHA loan programs*, *USDA Rural Development*, and *Seller Financing*.
**Licence examination weight:** **National Exam Category — 7 items.**
**Instructional hours:** **7.0**
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No North Carolina prelicensing credit is available.

> ### Instructional Levels, as the Commission defines them
>
> **Level 1 — Knowledge and comprehension.** The student can recall and restate.
> **Level 2 — Application.** The student can use the concept in a situation.
> **Level 3 — Analysis and evaluation.** The student can break a problem apart and judge it.
>
> Most of this section is **Level 1**: the student must *know* the vocabulary of finance. The Commission
> lifts only the parts a broker actually operates — how a sale of mortgaged property is handled, which
> loan category fits which buyer, where a mortgage sits in priority, what TRID and RESPA require of the
> timeline — to **Level 2**. It lifts the **settlement statement to Level 3**, and explains why in its own
> note, transcribed below.

## Learning objectives

1. Define the basic finance vocabulary the Commission lists, including the two meanings of a discount point.
2. Distinguish a mortgage from a deed of trust, and state which North Carolina uses.
3. State the foreclosure methods and the North Carolina statutory redemption period.
4. **Handle a sale of mortgaged property — cash sale, assumption, subject-to, and short sale.** *(Level 2)*
5. **Distinguish conventional, FHA, VA and USDA loans by their defining features.** *(Level 2)*
6. Classify loans by repayment term and by purpose.
7. **Apply mortgage priority, recordation and subordination.** *(Level 2)*
8. Describe the Fed's role, the primary market and the secondary market.
9. **Work a borrower and property analysis under the qualifying ratios the Commission lists.** *(Level 2)*
10. **State what TILA/Regulation Z and RESPA require, and the timing rules a broker must know.** *(Level 2)*
11. **Analyse a settlement statement entry — debit or credit, which party, and the proration.** *(Level 3)*

## A. Basic finance concepts, terminology and calculations

### A.1 Basic concepts and terminology

The Commission lists these by name: **principal and interest; amortization; debt service; PITI; equity;
usury; discount points; yield; loan value; loan-to-value ratio.**

On **discount points** the Syllabus states two figures itself, and both are examinable:

> **1 point = 1% of the mortgage loan amount for the borrower**
> **1 point = 1/8 % yield for the investor**

Read them together. A point is a *charge* measured against the loan amount when you look at it from the
borrower's side, and a *yield adjustment* when you look at it from the lender's or investor's side. The
same payment does two different jobs depending on which end of the transaction you stand at. A student
who memorises only the first figure cannot answer a yield question.

**PITI** is the four-part housing payment — principal, interest, taxes, insurance. It matters later, in
qualifying: the ratios in Part G are computed on the *housing expense*, which is PITI, not on principal
and interest alone.

**Equity** is the difference between what the property is worth and what is owed against it. **Loan
value** is the figure the loan is computed on, and the Commission's own note in Part G settles how it is
chosen: *"Loan value will be calculated on the contract sales price or the appraised value, whichever is
lower."* **Loan-to-value ratio** is loan value over that figure.

**Usury** is charging interest above the lawful maximum. **Debt service** is the periodic payment
required to service the debt.

### A.2 Mortgages and deeds of trust

**Basic terminology the Commission lists:** mortgage; promissory note (mortgage note); deed of trust;
hypothecation; lien theory; title theory; default; foreclosure.

**Hypothecation** is the concept that ties the whole section together: the borrower pledges the property
as security *while keeping possession of it*. Nothing about a mortgage loan moves the borrower out.

#### A.2.b The mortgage note

The Commission notes:

> **"A promissory note is a form of contract."**

That is not filler. Everything Module NC-09 taught about contract validity applies to the note. The
Syllabus asks for the **essential elements of a valid note**, and then three **special note provisions**:

- **Acceleration clause** — on default the whole balance becomes due at once, not just the missed payment.
- **Prepayment penalty clause** — a charge for paying the loan off early.
- **Due-on-sale clause (alienation clause)** — the balance becomes due if the property is sold.

Also examinable: **negotiable note vs. nonnegotiable note**, and the Commission's aside that **some
states use a bond in lieu of a promissory note.**

The due-on-sale clause is the reason Part A.3 exists in the form it does. Hold it.

#### A.2.c The mortgage (or deed of trust) instrument

Here the Commission writes a note that is unusually pointed, transcribed in full:

> *"The term **mortgage** is commonly used incorrectly to refer to the actual loan vs. the legal
> instrument that pledges the property for the loan (e.g., 'I am going to get a mortgage' vs. 'I am going
> to give a deed of trust to get a mortgage loan'). **Brokers are expected to know the difference and the
> technical difference between a mortgage and a deed of trust.**"*

The Commission's own comparison:

| | **Mortgage** | **Deed of trust** — *"Commonly used in NC"* |
|---|---|---|
| Parties | **2** | **3** |
| Who they are | Mortgagor, Mortgagee | Grantor/trustor, Trustee, Beneficiary |
| Theory | **Lien theory** | **Title theory** |
| Foreclosure | **Judicial** | **Power of sale (non-judicial)** |

Two columns, four rows, and the practical consequence sits in the last row. Because North Carolina
commonly uses the **deed of trust**, the ordinary North Carolina foreclosure is a **power of sale**
foreclosure conducted by the **trustee** — a third party who holds title for the benefit of the lender —
rather than a lawsuit. The extra party is what makes the shortcut possible.

The Commission adds: **"A mortgage or deed of trust is a form of contract."** It asks for the
**essential elements** and the **obligations of the borrower and the lender.**

**Signature of spouse.** The Commission's note, transcribed:

> *"If husband and wife are buying as tenants by the entirety (joint tenants in many other states) and
> **only one spouse signs the mortgage note, both spouses must still sign the mortgage or deed of
> trust**."*

The two documents do different things and are signed by different people. The **note** is the promise to
repay — one spouse can make it alone. The **deed of trust** pledges the property — and a tenancy by the
entirety cannot be pledged by half of it. Module NC-06 established that North Carolina recognises
tenancy by the entirety; this is one of the places it bites.

#### A.2.d Rights

**Rights of borrowers**, as listed: **right to possession and use; right of defeasance; right (equity)
of redemption.**

**Rights of mortgage lenders:** the **right to transfer (assign) the mortgage**, and the **right to
foreclose.**

**Foreclosure methods:** **judicial; power of sale (nonjudicial); strict.**

**Foreclosure procedures**, as the Commission lists them:

- **Proper notice of foreclosure**
- **Foreclosure sale at public auction**
- **A 10-day statutory redemption period in NC**
- **Disbursement of sale proceeds**
- **Unsatisfied creditors may appeal to court for a deficiency judgment**

And a North Carolina limit, transcribed:

> *"In NC, **seller financing is prohibited from using deficiency judgments** to recover deficit."*

The **10-day statutory redemption period** and the **seller-financing deficiency prohibition** are the
two North Carolina-specific figures in this part, and both come from the Syllabus's own text. This
project has **not** read the North Carolina foreclosure statutes themselves; see the instructor source
note. Nothing beyond what the Syllabus states is asserted here.

### A.3 Sale of mortgaged property *(Level 2 except as noted)*

The Commission opens with a note that frames the whole part:

> *"**Assumptions of mortgages and purchases subject to a mortgage occur very infrequently** because of
> **due on sale clauses** in most modern mortgages."*

That is why the due-on-sale clause was flagged in A.2.b. The clause is what killed assumption as a
routine practice.

Four ways the Commission lists:

**a. Cash sale.** *"Most common in residential transactions."* And the point students most often miss,
in the Commission's own words: *"Includes transactions where buyer obtains a new mortgage loan."* From
the seller's side the transaction is cash — the seller's loan is paid off and the seller is out. That
the buyer borrowed to get the cash does not change the category.

**b. Sale with assumption of seller's existing mortgage.** The buyer takes on the seller's loan.

**c. Purchase subject to seller's existing mortgage.** *(Level 1 — this subtopic.)* The Commission drops
this one back to Level 1: know what it is, do not be asked to work it.

**d. Short sale.** The Commission asks for a **basic overview of procedure** and then the **broker's
role**, which it states in three parts:

> - *"Existence of a possible short sale situation **is a material fact**"*
> - *"**Listing agent** should attempt to identify potential prior to listing and advise seller-client
>   about the process"*
> - *"**Buyer agent** should advise buyer client about the process and use appropriate contract addenda
>   when making an offer; **buyer agent is not expected to discover** possibility of a short sale"*

Two different standards for two different roles, and the difference is deliberate. The **listing agent
must look** — the seller's loan balance is the listing agent's business. The **buyer agent need not
discover** it, but must advise the client once it appears.

And a boundary, transcribed:

> *"**Broker cannot negotiate a 'workout agreement' with the lender on the borrower's behalf** because
> it would be **unauthorized practice of law**."*

Module NC-09 established the drafting prohibition. This is the same principle reaching into finance: the
line is not what the broker knows, it is what the broker *does on someone else's behalf*.

That the possibility of a short sale is a **material fact** places it alongside the items in Module
NC-10. Material facts are disclosed. This one is a material fact about the *transaction*, not about the
property.

## B. General categories of residential first mortgage loans *(Level 2 except as noted)*

### B.1 Conventional

The Commission's note:

> *"Conventional loans are typically seen as **the most secure loans for lenders** because they usually
> have **the lowest loan-to-value ratio**."*

Under it: **conforming vs. nonconforming**; **private mortgage insurance (PMI)**; **home equity loan and
home equity line of credit (HELOC).**

Conforming means the loan meets the purchase standards of the secondary market entities in Part F.
Nonconforming does not, which is why a nonconforming loan is priced differently — the lender may have to
keep it.

### B.2 FHA-insured

- Operated by the **Federal Housing Authority (FHA)** under **HUD**.
- **"FHA does not build housing or lend money for housing purchases."**
- **"FHA insures FHA-approved lenders against loss if borrowers default; similar to PMI."**

The **Title II, Section 203(b) Program** is *"the most widely used FHA program."* It applies to
**residential 1–4 unit property loans, including manufactured housing.** Its general characteristics, as
listed:

- **Fixed-rate loans with up to 30-year terms** made by lenders under FHA guidelines
- **FHA provides mortgage insurance**
- **Mortgage insurance premium (MIP)**
- **Owner (borrower) occupancy required**
- **Maximum loan amount**
- **Interest rates set by lender, not FHA**
- **Appraisal**
- **No prepayment penalty**
- **Assumability**
- **Original borrower may remain secondarily liable for repayment of an assumed loan**

*Other FHA programs* — senior borrowers, rehabilitation, condominium purchases, graduated payment
mortgages, adjustable rate mortgages — are **Level 1**, and the Commission's note limits the demand
exactly: **"It is only necessary to be aware that other FHA programs exist."**

### B.3 VA-guaranteed

- Operated by the **Department of Veterans Administration.**
- **"VA does not build housing and only directly funds loans for housing for Native American veterans
  living on Federal trust land."**
- **"VA guarantees (or backs) VA-approved lenders against loss if borrowers default."**

Applies generally to **residential 1–4 unit property loans under VA guidelines, including manufactured
housing**, and requires a **VA Certificate of Eligibility.**

General characteristics, as listed:

- **"Most significant feature — possibility of no down payment"**
- **Owner (borrower) occupancy required**
- **Property must meet VA property condition standards**
- **"No maximum VA-guaranteed loan amount"**
- **Qualifying requirements**
- **Lender sets interest rate, not the VA**
- **Funding fee**
- **Certificate of reasonable value (CRV)**
- **No prepayment penalty**
- **"All VA loans are assumable for qualified owner-occupants"**

And the trap, transcribed:

> *"**A VA release of liability does not release the original borrower from liability to the lender**;
> lender must provide a **separate release** for original borrower to be fully absolved of all
> responsibility for an assumed VA loan."*

Two releases, two different parties releasing. The VA's release is the VA's; the lender's release is the
lender's; only the second one gets the veteran off the debt. Note how this parallels the FHA line above —
*"original borrower may remain secondarily liable."* Assumption does not automatically release anyone.

Notice also what is *the same* in FHA and VA and worth learning as a pair: **the lender sets the interest
rate, not the agency**; **no prepayment penalty**; **owner occupancy required**; **assumable.** And what
differs: FHA **insures**, VA **guarantees**; FHA has a **maximum loan amount**, VA has **none**; FHA
charges **MIP**, VA charges a **funding fee**.

### B.4 USDA Rural Development *(Level 1 — this subtopic)*

Operated through the **Rural Housing Service of the US Department of Agriculture**, providing **both
direct and guaranteed single-family housing loans.** Characteristics listed: **longer than normal
repayment period (e.g., 33–38 years); 100% financing, no down payment; subsidized interest rates;
qualifying requirements.**

The Commission directs: *"Contact the NC state office at https://www.rd.usda.gov/nc to obtain loan and
area office information."* **This project has not retrieved that office's materials**, and states no
eligibility figure, income limit or area designation from them.

### B.5 Seller financing *(Level 1 — this subtopic)*

Two instruments named: **purchase money mortgage**, and **land contract / contract for deed.**

Carry forward from A.2.d: **in North Carolina seller financing is prohibited from using deficiency
judgments to recover a deficit.**

## C. Types of mortgage loans

### C.1 By repayment terms

**a. Fixed rate — level payment mortgage (fully amortized)** *(Level 2 — this subtopic)*. The Commission
calls it *"the most commonly used residential loan repayment plan"* and asks for **amortization** under it.

**b. Adjustable rate mortgage (ARM).** **Cap rates** — **periodic (or anniversary) cap**, **lifetime (or
aggregate) cap**, **index**. Then **payment cap**, with the consequence the Commission states: it
**"may result in negative amortization."**

That is the concept to hold: a cap on the *payment* is not a cap on the *interest*. If the payment is
held below the interest accruing, the shortfall is added to principal and the balance grows. A rate cap
limits what is charged; a payment cap only limits what is collected.

**c. Graduated payment mortgage (GPM).**

**d. Term mortgage (interest only or straight mortgage).** *"Term period is usually very short (e.g.,
3–5 years) and may be renewable."* *"Frequently used for construction loans or junior mortgages."*

**e. Growing equity mortgage (GEM).**

**f. Buydown mortgage.** *"Interest rate is temporarily or permanently reduced ('bought down')."* *"A
lump sum (usually stated as points) is paid at closing to the lender to offset reduced payments; **may be
paid by buyer, seller, or third party**."*

**g. Balloon mortgage (partially amortized).**

### C.2 By purpose or special feature

**Construction**; **open end** — *"repaid and reused without additional closing costs,"* *"acts as a line
of credit or equity line"*; **blanket**; **package**; **bridge.**

### C.3 Mortgage priorities *(Level 2 — this subtopic)*

- **Types by priority:** **first (or senior)**; **second, third, etc. (junior).**
- **"Effect and importance of recordation of mortgages (deeds of trust) AND the cancellation or release
  of a mortgage/deed of trust."**
- **Subordination of mortgages.**

Recordation sets priority; that is Module NC-05's recording principle applied to liens rather than to
title. **Subordination** is the deliberate exception — a lender agreeing to sit behind a lender who would
otherwise sit behind it. And the Commission's emphasis on **cancellation or release** connects to Module
NC-11: a paid-off deed of trust that was never cancelled of record is still a cloud, which is why the
Syllabus flagged that seller liens are sometimes cancelled 30–60 days post-closing.

## E. The primary mortgage market

**Purpose**, and the **sources of mortgage loans** the Commission lists: **direct lenders (banks, savings

## F. The secondary mortgage market

**General concepts and operation:** **purpose; loan pooling; mortgage-backed securities.**

**The government-sponsored enterprises (GSE):**

- **Federal National Mortgage Association (FNMA — Fannie Mae)**
- **Government National Mortgage Association (GNMA — Ginnie Mae)**
- **Federal Home Loan Mortgage Corporation (FHLMC — Freddie Mac)**

The primary market is where a loan is *made*. The secondary market is where a loan already made is
*bought*. The link between them is conforming standards from B.1: a lender writes to those standards
precisely so the loan can be sold, and selling it returns capital to lend again.

## G. Residential lending practices and procedures in transactions subject to TRID *(Level 2)*

### G.1 Loan application procedures

**Information typically required**, in three groups: **borrower's personal information** (employment,
earnings, assets, outstanding financial obligations); **property information** (legal description,
improvements, title, survey, taxes); **loan information** (purpose, proposed amount, repayment terms,
interest rate).

Then: **lender's loan estimate (LE)**; **rate lock**; and **"lender denial must be in writing."**

### G.2 Loan underwriting practices

**a. Borrower analysis (qualifying a buyer/borrower).** The Commission's note:

> *"**Brokers are encouraged to prequalify prospective buyers early** in the home buying process to
> minimize wasting time and energy on showing and writing offers on properties that cannot be afforded by
> the buyer."*

Factors listed: **credit history and credit score; adequacy of assets to close the transaction; stability
of income; types of acceptable income; occupancy of the property; adequacy of income.**

The ratios, exactly as the Commission states them:

| Loan type | Housing expense to gross income | Recurring debt to gross income |
|---|---|---|
| **Conventional** | **28%** | **36%** |
| **FHA** | **31%** | **43%** |
| **VA** | *considers several factors including an analysis of the borrower's **residual income*** | **43% maximum**; *"exceptions are possible"* |

And the rule that governs their use, transcribed:

> **"A buyer must qualify on both the income and the debt ratios."**

Both, not either. A buyer who passes the housing ratio and fails the debt ratio does not qualify. These
percentages are **the Commission's figures**, transcribed from its Syllabus — this project has not read
any lender guideline, agency handbook or underwriting manual, and states no figure from one.

**b. Property analysis.** **Title opinion and title insurance**; **"appraisal assigned randomly through
an appraisal management company."** The Commission's note:

> **"Loan value will be calculated on the contract sales price or the appraised value, whichever is lower."**

That single sentence is the reason an appraisal below contract price is a problem, and — carried back to
Module NC-11 — the reason it is a problem *for the buyer* in North Carolina, where **there is no appraisal
contingency in the standard contract.**

**c. Loan analysis.** **Yield (interest rate and discount points); loan term; loan-to-value ratio;
mortgage insurance.**

**d. Loan commitment and closing.** **Lender's letter of commitment**; **"loan principal disbursed at
settlement; 'loan closing'."**

## H. Federal financing legislation

The Commission's framing note, transcribed:

> *"Pursuant to the **Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act**, the **Consumer
> Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB)** was assigned the consumer protection responsibilities of **seven
> (7) different federal agencies** (including the Federal Reserve Board, HUD and the FTC). The CFPB is
> responsible for promulgating rules for the **Truth-in-Lending Act, RESPA, the Fair Credit Reporting
> Act, and the Equal Credit Opportunity Act.** One express mandate of the Dodd-Frank Act was that the
> CFPB **consolidate the duplicate lender disclosures under TILA and RESPA, yielding the TRID rules
> (TILA/RESPA Integrated Disclosures)**."*

That paragraph explains why one form now does two statutes' work.

### H.1 Truth-in-Lending *(Level 2 — this subtopic)*

**TILA** is a compilation of the initial Act, the **Truth in Lending Simplification and Reform Act
(TILSRA)** and the **Disclosure Improvement Act**; its rules are known as **Regulation Z**; it is
**administered by the CFPB.**

**Applicability** — it applies to those who advertise or extend loans that **involve a finance charge**
or **are payable in more than 4 installments.** The Commission also asks for **exemptions.**

**Disclosure of credit information to borrowers:** the **loan estimate (LE)**; **delivery of preliminary
and final LE**; and *"significant change will probably require a new LE and a possible closing delay."*

**The four chief disclosures:**

1. **Annual percentage rate (APR)** — and the Commission's note: **"It is not the interest rate."**
2. **Finance charges**
3. **Amount financed**
4. **Total amount of all payments**

The APR note is there because the confusion is universal. The APR folds the cost of credit — points and
certain fees — into a single annualised rate, so it is normally higher than the note rate. Two loans at
the same interest rate can carry different APRs, and that is the whole point of requiring it.

**Advertising.** The Commission states: *"Regulation Z governs **any** advertisement for consumer credit,
**regardless of who places the ad**."* Then **trigger terms**, and the disclosures required for any ad
that includes one:

- **The amount or percentage of down payment**
- **Terms of loan repayment**
- **APR**

And the practical instruction, transcribed:

> *"**Brokers may avoid Regulation Z issues by not including any numbers in their advertising, other than
> the list or lease price.**"*

### H.2 and H.3 Fair Credit Reporting Act and ECOA

The Commission lists **Fair Credit Reporting Act (implemented under Regulation B)** and **Equal Credit
Opportunity Act (ECOA implemented under Regulation V)**, and under ECOA asks for **purpose** and
**protected classes.**

**Instructor note on this pair.** The Syllabus's parenthetical regulation letters are transcribed above
exactly as printed. **This project has not read Regulation B or Regulation V**, and this module therefore
states nothing about either regulation's content beyond the Commission's own listing. **Instructors
should verify the regulation pairing against the current CFPB text before teaching it**, and should note
that the ECOA protected classes are not the same list as the Fair Housing Act protected classes taught in
Module NC-09 — a student who assumes they are identical will answer wrongly.

## I. Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (RESPA) *(Level 2)*

**Applicability**, as stated:

> *"**All mortgage loans for 1-4 family dwelling** (including manufactured housing) involving a
> **federally related mortgage loan made by institutional lenders.**"*
> *"**Does not apply to loans for commercial, business, or agricultural purposes.**"*

**Enforced by the CFPB and implemented by rules referred to as Regulation X.**

**Major requirements and consumer protections:**

- **Special information booklet.**
- **Loan estimate (LE)** — *formerly known as Good Faith Estimate (GFE)*. *"Disclosure of settlement
  charges must be provided by loan originators to borrowers **within 3 business days following loan
  application**."* *"Lender can only collect a fee for a credit report from the applicant prior to
  issuance of the LE."*
- **Possible borrower credit for "Yield Spread Premiums" paid to mortgage brokers.**
- **Buyer Closing Disclosure (BCD)** — *"must be used by settlement agents in all transactions subject to
  TRID."*
- **The kickback prohibition**, transcribed:

> *"RESPA **prohibits the payment of a kickback or referral fee to any settlement service provider for
> referring consumers**."*
>
> *"Real estate agents may **neither pay nor accept any referral fee from a settlement service provider**,
> such as an appraiser, home inspector, attorney, etc. **Real estate brokers are only permitted to pay and
> receive referral fees from each other.** The law/rules also regulate **controlled or affiliated business
> arrangements (CBA)** that provide real estate services (e.g., brokerage, mortgage loan, legal services,
> home inspections, etc.) under a one-stop shopping approach."*

That is a bright line and it is worth stating plainly: **broker to broker, yes; broker to or from any
other settlement service provider, no.** An inspector who sends a gift card for referrals has put the
broker in violation of a federal statute, and the broker's acceptance is the violation.

- **Regulates reserve (impound or escrow) accounts.**
- **Loan servicing information.**

### I.5 The Loan Estimate

*"A federal form with details of proposed mortgage terms including written estimate of settlement charges
the borrower will incur."*

**Tolerance limits for changes to settlement charges** — the Commission lists **three categories**:
**no tolerance**, **10% tolerance**, **unlimited tolerance**. *"Lender must credit borrower for excess
charges over the permissible limits."* And:

> *"If new information requires that a new LE be issued, there is a **mandatory 3-day waiting period
> before closing can occur**."*
>
> *"**Brokers are expected to be aware of timing of the three business day disclosure delivery.**"*

### I.6 The Borrower Closing Disclosure

*"Written detailed statement of final loan terms, prorations, and closing costs that borrower should
compare to information on the sales contract and the LE."* It **includes tolerance limits and the four
chief disclosures required by TILA.** The Commission asks for **delivery requirements** and for
**"changes that require a new BCD and a new 3 business day waiting period before settlement."**

**I.7 Penalties.**

**Instructor note on timing.** Three separate three-day periods appear in this section and in Module
NC-11, and they are not the same rule: the **LE must issue within 3 business days after application**;
a **re-issued LE starts a 3-day wait before closing**; and the **BCD must be delivered at least 3 business
days before consummation**, with certain changes restarting that wait. **This project has not read
Regulation Z or Regulation X**, and every one of these statements is transcribed from the Syllabus. A
broker facing a live deadline reads the current regulation, or asks the lender, and does not rely on a
course summary.

## J. The settlement statement *(Level 3 — this subtopic)*

The Commission explains the Level 3 assignment itself, and it is the clearest statement of purpose in the
Syllabus:

> *"Because a broker will **not normally be tasked with the preparation** of the actual settlement
> statement for closing, **a license candidate will not be expected to complete a settlement statement
> problem on the license examination.** However, **a broker cannot properly review a statement for
> accuracy without a thorough understanding of each party's responsibilities under the contract terms and
> the calculation of each entry.** A license candidate **will be expected to perform some calculations for
> individual entries and to know how the calculations will appear on the settlement statement** (e.g.,
> debits and credits)."*

Read that carefully, because it defines the examination boundary exactly. **Not** a whole statement.
**Yes** individual entries, and **yes** whether each lands as a debit or a credit and to whom.

### J.1 Basic settlement statement concepts

**Settlement statement. Debit. Credit. Prorations.**

**Prorations.** The Commission's note: **"Students should be able to calculate prorations using both
methods listed below."**

- **365-Day Year Method** — *"Most commonly used method in practice."*
- **360-Day Year / 30-Day Month Method** — ***"Method used on Prelicensing course end-of-course
  examination and real estate license examination."***

Both must be taught. One is what you will use in practice; the other is what you will be tested on. That
is the Commission's own division and it is stated in its own words above.

Two further rules:

> *"License exam proration problems **will specify which party is responsible for ('owns') the date of
> settlement**; **in NC, the seller 'owns' the date of settlement**."*
>
> *"**Always a double entry on the settlement statement.**"*

The double-entry rule is the checking mechanism. A prorated item that appears once has been done wrong:
if the seller is credited, someone is debited the same amount.

**Items commonly prorated**, as listed:

- **Real estate taxes**
  - **When taxes have not been paid prior to settlement** — *closing prior to the tax due date in August*;
    *closing after the tax due date in August*
  - **When taxes have been paid prior to settlement**
- **Homeowners' association dues**
- **Rents (on rented properties being sold)**

*Instructor note.* The Syllabus states an **August** tax due date for North Carolina and this module
transcribes it. **This project has not read the North Carolina property tax statutes**, and states no
listing date, lien date, discount date or delinquency date from them. The proration exercises delivered
