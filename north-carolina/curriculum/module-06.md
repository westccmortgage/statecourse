# North Carolina Broker Prelicensing Course — Module NC-06

## Section VI · Forms of Ownership, Transfer & Recording of Title

**Commission Syllabus section:** VI — Forms of Ownership, Transfer & Recording of Title
**Instructional Level:** **2** except as noted. *Assigned by the Commission; providers and instructors
are **REQUIRED to comply**.* **Freehold estates, landlord/tenant definitions, the bundle of rights,
cooperative ownership, trusts, the doctrine of prior appropriation, support rights, writ of attachment
and methods of transferring title are Level 1.** **Leasehold estates are Level 2.** **Percentage leases
are Level 3.**
**Licence examination weight:** **National Exam Category — 8 items.**
**Instructional hours:** **6.0**, matching the Commission's recommended emphasis for Property Ownership
Types and Transfers.
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No North Carolina prelicensing credit is available.

## Learning objectives

1. Classify freehold estates and identify a defeasible fee from its granting language. *(Level 1)*
2. Explain a life estate, estovers, and the remainder/reversion distinction.
3. Recite the five rights in the bundle. *(Level 1)*
4. Classify the four leasehold estates by **how each ends**. *(Level 2)*
5. Distinguish privity of estate from privity of contract. *(Level 1)*
6. Compute rent under a percentage lease. *(Level 3)*
7. Distinguish severalty, tenancy in common, joint tenancy and tenancy by the entirety — and state the
   **North Carolina** rule on survivorship.
8. Distinguish condominium, cooperative and time share ownership.
9. Describe appurtenant rights, including water rights and boundary shifts.
10. Classify liens as specific or general and state the lien priority rules and their exceptions.
11. Describe special assessments and lis pendens.
12. Name the methods of transferring title. *(Level 1)*
13. State the essential elements of a valid deed and explain why recordation is not one of them.
14. Distinguish general warranty, special warranty, quitclaim, bargain-and-sale, and special-purpose
    deeds by the warranties and authority actually shown in the instrument.

## A. Estates in Real Property

### A.1 Freehold estates *(Level 1 — this subtopic)*

**Freehold estates last for an indeterminable period of time.**

**Estates of inheritance** — the owner can determine who inherits:

- **Fee Simple Estate (Fee Simple Absolute).**
- **Defeasible Fee (Qualified) Estates:**
  - **Fee Simple Determinable** — the Syllabus also calls it *Fee Simple to a Condition Precedent*.
  - **Fee Simple Conditional** — *Fee Simple to a Condition Subsequent*.
- **Estate for Life of Another (pur autre vie)** — **Instructor Note:** *"Based on life of named third
  party, the measuring life."*

**Estates not of inheritance — life estates:**

- **Estate for Tenant's Own Life (Conventional Life Estate)**, with:
  - the **life tenant** — their **rights and restrictions**, and **estovers**, the life tenant's right
    to take timber for necessary repairs and fuel; and
  - the **remainderman**.
- **Marital life estates.**
- **Remainder versus reversionary interest** — to a third party, or back to the grantor.
- **Homestead.**

> **Required Activity — prescribed by the Commission:** *"Provide real world examples of each type of
> estate. Make note that **the difference between the different leasehold estates is how they end**."*

### A.2 The bundle of legal rights *(Level 1 — this subtopic)*

The Commission states five, and states them in its own words:

- **Right to possession** — to occupy.
- **Right to control** — of the property and legal profits.
- **Right of enjoyment** — to use in any legal manner **without interference from others claiming a
  superior title**.
- **Right of exclusion** — to restrict access.
- **Right of disposition** — to transfer ownership or interests, **including the right to
  encumber/mortgage**.

### A.3 Estates in landlord/tenant *(Level 1 except as noted)*

**Definitions:** **landlord (lessor)**; **tenant (lessee)**; **lease**; **privity of estate**;
**privity of contract**.

The last two are worth separating now, because assignment and sublease at A.5 turn on them: **privity of
estate** is the relationship arising from the interest in the land; **privity of contract** is the
relationship arising from the promises.

### A.4 Leasehold (nonfreehold) estates *(Level 2 — this subtopic)*

**And the Required Activity above tells you the organising principle: the difference between them is
HOW THEY END.**

- **Tenancy (or estate) for years.** Definite beginning, definite end. **Ends on its own terms — no
  notice required.**
- **Tenancy (or estate) from period to period.** **Instructor Note:** *"Also known as periodic
  tenancies, week-to-week, month-to-month."* **Ends by proper notice.**
- **Tenancy (or estate) at will.** **Ends at the will of either party.**
- **Tenancy (or estate) at sufferance.** **Instructor Note:** *"**Unlike trespassing, this estate began
  legally.**"* A holdover — possession that started lawfully and outlived its right.

**North Carolina periodic-tenancy notice rule.** Current G.S. 42-14 sets the statutory minimum notice
to quit at **one month before the end of the current rental year** for a year-to-year tenancy,
**seven days** for month-to-month, and **two days** for week-to-week. Rental of only a manufactured-home
space has a separate **60-day** minimum before the end of the current rental period. These are default
statutory rules for the tenancies the section names; the lease, another applicable statute, and counsel
still control a particular termination or eviction.

### A.5 Leases

> **Required Activity — prescribed by the Commission:** *"Explain real world use of each type of
> lease."*

**Types of leases:**

- **Fixed rental (flat or gross) lease.**
- **Percentage lease** *(Level 3 — this subtopic)*.
- **Other lease types** *(Level 1)*: **net lease**; **graduated (step-up) lease**; **index lease**;
  **full service lease**; **ground lease**.

**Percentage lease — Level 3, so worked:**

A percentage lease charges a base rent plus a percentage of the tenant's sales above a **breakpoint**.

*Worked.* Base rent **$3,600 per month**. Percentage rent **5% of gross sales over a natural breakpoint**
of **$864,000 per year**. Annual sales are **$1,180,000**.

Base rent: 3,600 × 12 = **$43,200**.
Sales above the breakpoint: 1,180,000 − 864,000 = **$316,000**.
Percentage rent: 316,000 × 0.05 = **$15,800**.
**Total annual rent = 43,200 + 15,800 = $59,000.**

*(Check the natural breakpoint: base rent ÷ percentage = 43,200 ÷ 0.05 = $864,000. A "natural"
breakpoint is the sales figure at which the percentage rent alone would equal the base rent.)*

**Essential lease provisions.** **Instructor Note:** *"Same as for any contract."*

**Other common provisions — the tenant's use of the premises. Instructor Note, transcribed:**

> *"Lessee may make **any legal use** of the property **unless there are use restrictions in the
> lease**. Tenant has **Implied Covenant of Quiet Enjoyment**; **lessor has no right to enter premises
> during lease term unless granted in the lease**."*

The last clause is the Syllabus's general lease instruction, not a complete North Carolina entry code.
Do not convert it into an absolute answer for emergencies, repairs, court process, or another statute.
For an actual property, read the lease and current law and obtain legal advice where the right of entry
is disputed.

**Assignments and subleases:** **assignment**; **sublease**; **assignee versus assignor**;
**novation**; **renewal and termination**. And two stated rules:

- **"Lessee has no implied right to renew the lease."**
- **"Automatic renewal clause can be included in the lease terms."**

## B. Ownership, Interests, and Rights in Real Property

### B.1 Severalty (sole) ownership

**Instructor Note:** *"Sole owner can be an individual person **or a single business entity**."*

Note that "severalty" means sole ownership, despite sounding like the opposite — a reliable examination
item.

### B.2 Concurrent (joint) ownership

**Tenancy in common:**

- **Undivided fractional interest; may be unequal.**
- **Unity of possession.**
- **A co-owner may dispose of their individual interest without the permission of the co-tenants.**
- **Upon death, the undivided interest passes to the heirs; NO right of survivorship.**

**Joint tenancy:**

- **Tenancy in common is the default** when a conveyance leaves two or more people owning property.
- Under current G.S. 41-71, the instrument creates a **joint tenancy with right of survivorship** when
  it expresses that intent. The statute recognizes several phrases, including “joint tenants,” “joint
  tenancy,” and “with right of survivorship,” unless the instrument provides otherwise.
- Under G.S. 41-72, interests are **equal unless the instrument provides otherwise**. North Carolina
  therefore permits an expressly stated unequal percentage; the old “four equal unities” mnemonic is
  not a safe statement of current North Carolina law.
- G.S. 41-73 lists collective and unilateral termination events, including specified conveyances or
  instruments and a partition petition. A unilateral self-conveyance that expresses termination must
  be recorded before the joint tenant's death.

**North Carolina's transaction point:** survivorship is not supplied merely because multiple people
take title. The conveyance must create it under G.S. 41-71. The statute, amended in 2025, now states
which wording expresses that intent; the broker still routes drafting and title advice to the closing
attorney.

**Which is emphatically not a licensee's judgment to make.** How a buyer takes title is a legal and tax
question for counsel. **What a licensee must do is know that the question exists, raise it early, and
route it** — and never assure a buying couple that "joint tenancy means the survivor gets it."

**Tenancy by the entirety:**

- **Available to two people legally married to each other.**
- Under G.S. 41-56, a conveyance to spouses creates the estate **unless the conveyance expresses a
  contrary intention**.
- **Instructor Note:** *"Other types of ownership **do not automatically convert** to tenancy by the
  entirety if property owners marry each other later."* Current G.S. 41-82(c) confirms that result for
  cotenants who later marry unless a separate instrument changes the estate.
- **Equal undivided interest.**
- **Survivorship while the estate remains in place.** At death, G.S. 41-64 gives the survivor title and
  leaves no descendable share in the deceased spouse, subject to the statute's slayer exception.
- **Neither spouse may unilaterally sell, lease, mortgage, transfer, convey, or encumber the estate.**
  G.S. 41-58 generally requires the written joinder of the other spouse, subject to its listed statutory
  exceptions.
- **It can be terminated.** G.S. 41-63 lists joint sale, voluntary partition, specified involuntary
  transfers, conveyance from one spouse to the other, absolute divorce, and forfeiture; death is treated
  separately in G.S. 41-64. “Cannot be waived” must not be taught as though spouses can never end it.

**The practical consequences:** a will cannot transfer the deceased spouse's former share while the
estate survives to death, and one spouse ordinarily cannot convey or encumber the estate alone. A
broker verifies title and signatories with the closing attorney rather than drafting an ownership result.

> **Required Activity — prescribed by the Commission:** *"Provide an assessment to determine if student
> can **differentiate between these types of hybrid ownership**."*

### B.3 Common interest community (hybrid) ownership

**General concepts.** Managed by owner associations — an **HOA** or **POA** — which **enforce rules
regarding operation and use**, **maintain, repair and clean common areas**, **maintain insurance**, and
take on **additional responsibilities by agreement**. **Governed by covenants and general statutes.**
**May be residential, commercial, industrial or mixed-use.**

**Condominium ownership:**

- **Governed in North Carolina by Chapter 47C** for condominiums created under that Act, with older or
  specially situated projects requiring document and counsel review.
- A unit is real estate whose boundaries are fixed by the declaration. “Title to airspace” is a useful
  classroom picture, but it is not a substitute for those recorded boundaries.
- The unit owner also owns the unit's allocated undivided interest in the common elements. Chapter 47C
  generally prevents partition of the common elements while the condominium exists and recognizes
  limited common elements allocated for fewer than all units.
- For a sale requiring a **public offering statement** under G.S. 47C-4-102(c), G.S. 47C-4-108 gives
  the purchaser an absolute **seven-calendar-day** cancellation period following execution and bars
  conveyance during that period. This is not correctly summarized as “every new condo.”
- For an ordinary resale not requiring a public offering statement and not exempt under
  G.S. 47C-4-101(b), G.S. 47C-4-109 requires the unit owner to provide, before conveyance, a statement
  of monthly common expenses and other owner fees. That section supplies **no statutory cancellation
  right**; any contractual right must be read from the actual contract.
- **Instructor Note:** *"Nationally, a **townhouse is generally a multi-story condo**."*

**Cooperative ownership** *(Level 1)*:

- **Title to land and building held by a corporation.**
- **The tenant purchases corporate stock shares — personal property** — with a **proprietary lease**
  and **no individual deed for units**.

**Time share ownership:**

- **Instructor Note:** *"Also called vacation lease, prepaid hotel reservation, club membership,
  limited partnership, vacation bond."*
- **Allows multiple parties to buy an interest in the same real estate.**
- **The interest may be leasehold or deeded ownership.**
- **Buyers receive the right to occupy for a specific time period.**

**Townhouse is a physical description, not an ownership form.** A townhouse may be a condominium,
planned community, or another structure. Chapter 47C or 47F applies from the recorded legal regime and
statutory coverage, not from the building's appearance. *(North Carolina's current Timeshare Act and
Rules 58B .0400-.0600 are applied in Module NC-15.)*

### B.4 Trusts *(Level 1 — this subtopic)*

**Instructor Note, transcribed, and it is a direction rather than a description:**

> *"**Legal advice is strongly recommended before broker handles a transaction involving property owned
> by a trust.**"*

A **fiduciary arrangement**: a **third party trustee holds assets on behalf of a beneficiary**; the
**trust agreement sets the parameters of the trustee's authority**; it **may allow assets to pass
outside of probate**.

**And the operative practice rule, transcribed:** *"**Brokers should deal with the trustee vs. the
beneficiary** regarding disposition of real estate."*

### B.5 Appurtenant rights (appurtenances)

**Subsurface rights. Air rights.**

**Water rights:** **riparian rights**; **littoral rights**; **foreshore**; **boundary shifts due to
accretion, reliction, erosion, or avulsion**; and the **Doctrine of Prior Appropriation** *(Level 1)*.

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"**Water rights cannot be retained when the property title is
transferred.**"*

**Support rights** *(Level 1)*: **the right to lateral support** and **the right to subjacent support**.

**The benefit of any appurtenant easement**, and **the benefit of any protective (restrictive)
covenants** — both of which run to the new owner.

### B.6 Liens

**Specific liens** — attaching to an identified parcel:

- **Mortgages (deeds of trust).**
- **Real estate tax and assessment liens.**
- **Mechanics' liens (materialman's lien).**

**General liens** — attaching to the debtor's property generally:

- **Judgment liens.**
- **Personal property tax liens.**
- **State tax liens.**
- **Federal tax liens.**

**Lien priority:**

- The general rule is **order of recordation**.
- **Exceptions to the "pure race system":**
  - **Real property and special assessment liens hold superior priority.**
  - **A perfected claim of lien on real property relates back to the claimant's first furnishing of
    labor or materials at the site** under G.S. 44A-10 and -11.
  - **Voluntary subordination of a superior lien.**

The relation-back point matters, but it does **not** mean every contractor automatically has an
enforceable first-day lien. The statutory service, filing, and enforcement steps still control.
G.S. 44A-12 permits filing no later than **120 days after the claimant's last furnishing**, and
G.S. 44A-13 sets a separate **180-day** outside limit to commence enforcement. Recent or ongoing
construction is therefore a listing and closing question routed to the closing attorney.

**Writ of attachment** *(Level 1)*.

### B.7 Special assessments

**Examples. Levied by local municipalities. Special priority** — see the priority exceptions above.

### B.8 Lis pendens

**Instructor Note:** *"This is **notice of an active lawsuit that may affect title**."*

## C. Methods of Transferring Title *(Level 1)*

1. **By descent.** **Instructor Note:** *"When owner dies with no will (intestate), state laws
   regarding **Intestate Succession** will rule **[NCGS Chapter 29]**."*
2. **By will (testate).** **Instructor Note:** *"Remember that **a will cannot override the survivorship
   clause in a deed**."*
3. **By voluntary alienation (by deed).**
4. **By involuntary alienation:** **lien foreclosure sale**; **adverse possession**; **escheat**;
   **eminent domain**.

**The note on wills matters and it connects to B.2.** Tenancy by the entirety's survivorship **overrides
a will**, and a survivorship clause in a deed is not defeated by a testamentary gift.

Current Chapter 29 supplies the intestate succession framework. It distinguishes the surviving
spouse's share under G.S. 29-14 from the shares of other heirs under G.S. 29-15 and applies a 120-hour
survival rule through G.S. 29-13. A broker identifies intestacy and routes the actual heir and share
determination to the estate attorney. Foreclosure procedure is outside this module.

## D. Deeds

### D.1 Grantor and grantee

The **grantor** conveys; the **grantee** receives.

### D.2 Essential elements of a valid deed

The Commission's list, transcribed:

- **Named parties (grantor/grantee).**
- **Words of conveyance (granting clause).**
- **Accurate legal description of the property.**
- **Legal capacity of the grantor.**
- **Signature of the grantor(s).**
- **Delivery to and voluntary acceptance of the deed by the grantee** — usually by recordation.
- **The deed must be in writing.**

**Instructor Note, transcribed:**

> *"**Recordation of the deed is not a legal essential element**; but the standard sales contracts in
> many states like North Carolina **require recordation of the deed to complete closing**. Non-essential
> elements frequently included in a deed are **date, acknowledgement, amount of consideration,
> witnesses, seal, and signature of grantee**."*

**Both halves matter.** Recordation is not an element of validity — **and North Carolina's standard
sales contracts require it to complete closing anyway.** So a licensee should never say "recording
doesn't matter"; it does not go to validity and it does go to whether the transaction has closed.

Note too what is **not** essential: **the grantee's signature**, **consideration in any stated amount**,
**a seal**, **witnesses**, and **acknowledgement** — though acknowledgement is what makes an instrument
recordable in practice.

### D.3 Deed types — the missing required-activity instruction

The deed type changes the grantor's promises; recording does not upgrade those promises:

| Deed | What the student must distinguish |
|---|---|
| **General warranty deed** | The grantor conveys the described estate with title covenants reaching beyond the grantor's own period of ownership. It offers the broadest ordinary deed warranty, but still does not replace a title search or policy. |
| **Special warranty deed** | The grantor limits title covenants to claims arising by, through, or under that grantor. It does not warrant the entire earlier chain of title. |
| **Quitclaim deed** | Conveys whatever present interest, if any, the grantor has, without promising that the grantor owns the property or that title is clear. It does not release a mortgage debt or make a concealed “subject-to” transaction safe. |
| **Bargain-and-sale deed** | Indicates a conveyance for value but, without express warranties, does not supply the broad covenants of a general warranty deed. Read the instrument instead of deciding from its caption. |
| **Special-purpose deed** | Trustee's, executor's, administrator's, guardian's, commissioner's, and similar deeds depend on the signer’s legal authority and the governing proceeding. Refer authority and drafting questions to the attorney. |

**Required deed-classification exercise.** For each instrument, identify (1) who signs and under what
authority, (2) the estate described, (3) the warranties actually stated, (4) whether delivery and
acceptance occurred, and (5) whether it has been recorded for third-party priority. A recorded
quitclaim deed is still a quitclaim deed; a general warranty deed does not become valid against a
prior protected interest merely because its caption says “general warranty.”
