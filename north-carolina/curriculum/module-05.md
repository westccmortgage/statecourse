## A. Disclosure of Property Conditions by Real Estate Brokers *(Level 3)*

### A.1 Responsibilities for verification of land use and recognition of potential land use problems

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"Recognizing and taking appropriate action with regard to potential
problems with **zoning and restrictive covenants** is particularly important."*

**When a property is or might be subject to zoning and/or covenants, the broker must advise the
consumer that:**

1. **Ordinances or covenants could affect the consumer's use and enjoyment of the property**;
2. **property owners may be subject to owners' association dues and assessments**; and
3. **careful review of any covenants and owners' association governing documents is recommended prior
   to signing any offer.**

**Read item 3.** *Prior to signing any offer.* Not during due diligence, not before closing —
**before the offer is signed.**

**And the broker must:**

- **Avoid any misrepresentation about a property's zoning or permitted land uses.**
- **Recognize "red flag" situations.**
- **Recognize patent signs of flooding on a property, or when a property's location may make it
  susceptible to flooding.**
- **Disclose planned major highway or public transportation changes in the area as a material fact.**

**That last one is broader than new licensees expect.** A planned highway or transit change **in the
area** — not on the property — is a **material fact** that must be disclosed.

Note also how it pairs with Module NC-03: **the ability to access an abutting public highway or street
is itself a material fact**, and access may be restricted or forbidden by the state or the
municipality.

### A.2 Environmental hazards are material facts

**Transcribed:**

> *"The presence on a property of **any of the environmental hazards covered in this Section is a
> material fact that must be disclosed to all transactional parties**."*

**All of them. To all transactional parties.** Not only to a client, and not only on request.

### A.3 Liability for cleanup

**Transcribed:** *"**Owner of a property with hazardous waste may be liable for cleanup, even if caused
by a previous owner.**"*

That is the principle behind CERCLA at Part B.2, and it is why an environmental site assessment exists
as a buyer's due diligence step rather than a formality.

### A.4 Underground storage tanks

**Transcribed, and this is a duty rather than a suggestion:**

> *"Any broker who **knows of any current or abandoned USTs** on the property **must disclose to all
> prospective purchasers or tenants**."*

> *"**Buyer agent should strongly encourage buyer to hire an expert** to inspect any tanks and conduct
> a **soil contamination test**."*

**Current or abandoned.** An abandoned tank is still a disclosure obligation, and an abandoned tank is
precisely the one nobody has thought about for thirty years.

### A.5 The radon mitigation system

**Transcribed, and it is the most quietly demanding sentence in the section:**

> *"The **mere presence of a radon mitigation system** in a property **elevates the broker's
> responsibility to discover and disclose the reason behind that presence**."*

**Not merely to disclose the system — to DISCOVER the reason for it.** A pipe and a fan on the side of
a house is a fact that raises a question, and the Commission places the obligation to pursue that
question on the broker.

The practical method: ask the seller, in writing; ask for the test results that prompted the
installation and the post-mitigation results if any; and disclose what is learned — and disclose that
the reason could not be established if it cannot be.

### A.6 The "as is" sale

**Transcribed:**

> *"**Broker's disclosure responsibilities under Real Estate License Law not affected by "as is"
> status.**"*

**"As is" changes the seller's repair obligations. It changes nothing about yours.** A licensee who
believes an "as is" listing relieves them of disclosing a known material fact has misunderstood whose
obligation it is.

## B. Environmental Issues in Real Estate

### B.1 Lead *(Level 2 — this subtopic)*

> **Required Activity — prescribed by the Commission:** *"Review the **Lead-Based Paint or Lead-Based
> Paint Hazard Addendum, NCAR Form 2A9-T**."*
>
> **This activity must be completed.** **This project has not obtained NCAR Form 2A9-T, and no document
> in this build reproduces, describes or paraphrases it.** Obtaining the current form and conducting
> this review is a **submission gate** for this module.

**The facts the Syllabus states:**

- **Residential use of lead-based paint banned in 1978; disclosure required by federal law since
  1992.**
- **Sources of lead.**
- **Serious health risk of lead poisoning.**
- **Federal Residential Lead-Based Paint Hazard Reduction Act of 1992**, and updated **HUD and EPA
  rules** — `www.epa.gov/lead`.

**And the broker's own duty, transcribed — this is the tested point:**

> *"**Brokers have legal duty to ensure compliance with the lead paint disclosure requirements**
> — informing seller/lessor of their obligations under these rules; **either ensure that seller/lessor
> has performed all activities required by the rules OR broker must personally ensure compliance**."*

**Read that.** The duty is not to watch someone else comply. It is either to **ensure the seller has
complied**, or **to comply personally**. There is no third option in which the broker is a bystander.

The requirement **applies to target housing**, and **enforcement and penalties** attach.

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"**No federal law requires the owner to test** for the presence of
lead or lead-based paint."*

Disclosure of what is **known**, and the opportunity for the buyer to investigate — **not** a testing
mandate.

### B.2 Asbestos *(Level 1)*

- **Friable** — the operative concept.
- **Serious health risk** for various respiratory diseases and for **lung and stomach cancer**.
- **Remediation:** a **costly removal process requiring a state-licensed asbestos abatement contractor
  and sealed areas**; or **encapsulation**.

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"The **mere presence of asbestos is not a health hazard unless it is
friable**."*

Which is why intact material is often best left undisturbed and why **renovation plans on an older
house are the trigger**, not the material's existence.

### B.3 Radon *(Level 1)*

- **A health risk as one of the leading causes of lung cancer.**
- **Radon presence over the EPA's actionable level of 4.0 picocuries is a material fact.**

**That is a stated figure and it is stated here because the Syllabus states it: 4.0 picocuries.** Above
it, presence is **a material fact**.

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"Do-it-yourself radon tests are available; **professional testing is
generally more accurate**."*

**Remediation through a mitigation system that uses ventilation** — which connects back to A.5: a
mitigation system on a house means somebody once had a result worth mitigating.

### B.4 Toxic mould *(Level 1)*

Characteristics and where found; **allergic reaction, respiratory and sinus problems**; **potential
homeowner's insurance issues**; remediation.

The insurance point matters commercially: a mould claim history can affect insurability, which
Module NC-04 established is a financing question.

### B.5 Miscellaneous potential issues *(Level 1)*

**Groundwater contamination. Underground storage tanks. Waste disposal sites. Brownfields.**

### B.6 The Environmental Protection Agency *(Level 1)*

**CERCLA** — the **Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability Act**:

- **also known as the Superfund**;
- **identifies hazardous waste sites for clean up**;
- **Environmental Site Assessments (Phase I and Phase II studies) and Impact Studies.**

**The Clean Air Act:**

- **establishes and enforces air quality standards**;
- **affects property owners and businesses that own or repair appliances using refrigerants**, such as
  air conditioners and refrigerators.

**The Clean Water Act:**

- **regulates pollutant discharges into water**;
- **sets water quality standards**;
- **protects wetlands.**

## The rule that governs the whole section

**Recognise, disclose, route.** The Commission's own text places three affirmative obligations on the
broker in this section — **discover the reason for a radon mitigation system**, **disclose known current
or abandoned USTs**, and **ensure lead disclosure compliance personally if the seller has not** — and
beyond those, **a licensee assesses no environmental condition.**

Testing is by a qualified tester. Remediation is by a qualified contractor, and for asbestos by a
**state-licensed abatement contractor**. Interpretation of a result is not a licensee's work, and
neither is reassurance about one.
