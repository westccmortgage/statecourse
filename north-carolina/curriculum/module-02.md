## Learning objectives

At Level 3 — justify, judge, weigh, apply to fact situations:

1. Explain what a broker-in-charge is and what supervision of a provisional broker means.
2. State the four legal requirements for a listing agent to establish a claim to compensation.
3. Apply the procuring cause doctrine to a disputed commission.
4. Identify the six situations in which a broker may be entitled to a fee **even though the
   transaction does not close**.
5. Explain how an earned brokerage fee is shared in an in-house sale and in a co-brokered sale.
6. State who may lawfully pay a provisional broker, and who may pay an affiliated full broker.
7. Apply the out-of-state co-brokerage conditions to a referral fact pattern.
8. State the three legal requirements for a buyer agent to establish a claim to compensation.
9. Apply the active-licence rule to a payment made after a licence has lapsed.

## A. Supervision

### A.1 Broker supervision of provisional brokers

A **provisional broker** does not practise on their own account. They practise **under the supervision
of a broker-in-charge**, and the supervision is a real, ongoing obligation on the supervising broker
rather than a formality of registration.

Statutory and rule references the Syllabus names: **NCGS 93A-2(b)** and **Commission Rule 58A .0506**.

The current official text of Rule 58A .0506 makes the control operational:

- the provisional broker may engage in or hold themself out as engaging in licensed activity only
  while the license is active and the broker is supervised by the BIC of the affiliated firm or
  office;
- ordinarily the PB has one supervising BIC; the narrow exception permits no more than two BICs of
  two affiliated firms sharing the same physical location and acting as co-listing or co-selling
  agents, with both carrying full supervision responsibility;
- the PB and BIC submit the Commission's License Activation and Broker Affiliation form;
- upon submission, the PB may work under supervision, but if written Commission acknowledgment is
  not received within **30 days of the date shown on the form**, the PB must stop licensed activity
  pending acknowledgment; and
- when the supervisory relationship ends, both PB and BIC notify the Commission in writing no later
  than **10 days after termination**.

The rule defines supervision by its purpose: the BIC must supervise in a manner that assures the PB
performs licensed acts in accordance with the License Law and Commission rules. It does not create a
universal “BIC must be physically present” test, nor does a promise to be available by telephone by
itself prove adequate supervision. The work, the PB's competence, risk, review, and actual compliance
all matter.

### A.2 Broker-in-charge — *Level 1 for this subtopic*

*The Commission drops this subtopic to Level 1: review and discuss the basic facts; in-depth
instruction is not required.*

A **broker-in-charge (BIC)** is the broker designated as responsible for a real estate office. The BIC
carries the supervisory duties for the provisional brokers and other licensees affiliated with that
office.

Statutory and rule references the Syllabus names: **NCGS 93A-2(a1)** and **93A-4.2**, and **Commission
Rule 58A .0110(g)(1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 10)**.

Current Rule 58A .0110 ordinarily requires one designated BIC for a firm's principal office and one
for each branch. To obtain BIC Eligible status, a broker must be active and meet the rule's experience
route — generally at least two years of brokerage experience equivalent to 40 hours per week within
the previous five years, or the stated North Carolina-attorney alternative — and complete the
12-hour BIC Course in the permitted window: no earlier than one year before application and no later
than 120 days after it.

The designated BIC's duties include assuring affiliated brokers' license/status compliance,
supervising provisional brokers, responsibility for office advertising, trust accounts and
transaction records, and compliance with agency-agreement and disclosure requirements. The precise
current rule controls; a firm cannot reduce these duties by contract.

## B. Earning a Real Estate Brokerage Fee (Sales)

References the Syllabus names: **NCGS 93A-6(a)(9)** and **Commission Rule 58A .0109**.

This is the Level 3 heart of the section, and it is worth understanding precisely, because compensation
disputes are common and they are decided on these elements.

### B.1 Earning compensation as a listing agent

**Four legal requirements for establishing a claim to compensation:**

1. **A current real estate licence on active status.**
2. **A valid written listing contract.**
3. **Procurement of a ready, willing and able buyer.**
4. **The procuring cause doctrine.**

Take them one at a time.

**Current licence on active status.** Not merely held — **active**. B.2 below returns to what happens
when it is not.

**A valid written listing contract.** Written. An oral listing does not support a compensation claim.

**A ready, willing and able buyer.** *Ready* and *willing* to buy on the seller's terms, and *able* —
financially capable of completing. A buyer who wants the property and cannot fund it is not an able
buyer.

**Procuring cause.** The doctrine that asks **whose efforts actually produced the ready, willing and
able buyer** — an unbroken chain from the broker's efforts to the buyer's decision. It is the doctrine
that resolves the argument between two brokers who both had contact with the same buyer, and it is
decided on facts: who introduced the property, who maintained the relationship, who was continuously
involved, and whether anything broke the chain.

### B.2 Entitlement to a fee where the transaction does NOT close

The Syllabus lists six situations in which a broker who has **fulfilled their contract duties** may be
entitled to a fee even though the contract does not close. Transcribed:

- **The seller refuses to close.**
- **The seller's spouse will not sign the contract or deed.**
- **The seller has an uncorrected defect in the title.**
- **The seller is unable to deliver possession within a reasonable time.**
- **The seller insists on sales terms and conditions not stated in the listing contract.**
- **The buyer and seller conspire after the sales contract has been signed and agree to cancel the
  contract.**

**Read what those six have in common.** In every one, **the failure to close is the seller's doing, or
a collusive act, rather than the broker's failure to perform.** The broker produced what the listing
contract asked for; the transaction failed for a reason on the seller's side of the line.

**And read what they are not.** They are not a general rule that a broker is paid when a deal collapses.
A buyer who cannot obtain financing, a property that does not appraise, an inspection that ends the
transaction — none of those is on this list.

**Whether a fee is actually owed in a given case is a legal question**, and a broker with a disputed
claim takes it to their broker-in-charge and to counsel. What a licensee must be able to do is
**recognise which category a failed transaction falls into**, and that is what Level 3 asks for here.

### B.3 Sharing the listing broker's compensation

Before tracing payment through a firm, apply Rule 58A .0109:

- a licensee may not take more than nominal value from a vendor or supplier for an expenditure made
  for the principal without the principal's written consent;
- compensation for a service recommended, procured, or arranged for a party requires full and timely
  disclosure to that party;
- in a sales transaction, compensation from the broker's principal must be provided for in the
  written agency contract;
- compensation from another person requires full and timely disclosure to the principal; an oral
  disclosure must be confirmed in writing before the principal makes or accepts an offer;
- the disclosure identifies the compensation or consideration, its value, and who will or may pay it;
  and
- the Commission does not set commission rates or arbitrate private fee and split disputes.

Rule .0109 also prohibits sharing compensation with an unlicensed person or entity for an act
performed in North Carolina that requires a license, except for the rule's narrow vacation-rental
travel-agent provision. That exception has its own conditions and is not a general referral-fee rule.

**The in-house sale** — where the same firm is on both sides:

- **How the earned brokerage fee is split** is settled between the parties and the firm.
- **A licensee's ability to receive compensation is determined by the employment agreement between the
  broker and the brokerage company.**
- **Affiliated provisional brokers must be compensated by their supervising broker-in-charge** —
  **NCGS 93A-6(a)(5)**.
- **Affiliated "full" brokers must be compensated by their supervising broker-in-charge** —
  **Commission Rule 58A .0120(b)**.

**Those last two are the ones a new licensee must know cold.** Rule 58A .0120(b) says an affiliated
broker may not be paid a commission or referral fee directly by anyone other than the broker's current
BIC or the person who served as BIC at the time of the transaction. That applies to an affiliated
broker whether provisional or nonprovisional. The rule is about lawful payment flow; the affiliation
agreement and other applicable law determine whether and how much is owed.

**The co-brokered sale** — where two firms are involved:

- **The ability to receive compensation from the listing company is negotiated between the parties.**
- **Cooperative listing service agreements** may govern how that negotiation is conducted.

Note what "negotiated between the parties" excludes: **there is no automatic entitlement.** A
cooperating broker's compensation comes from an agreement, and where there is no agreement there is no
claim.

### B.4 Co-brokerage with a broker licensed in another state

The Syllabus states two conditions and both must be met:

- **The out-of-state (foreign) broker must hold an active real estate licence in the state from which
  they are conducting the brokerage activity**, such as placing a referral; **and**
- **The foreign broker does not enter North Carolina at any time during the transaction to engage in
  any brokerage activity related to the transaction.**

**"Does not enter NC at any time during the transaction to engage in any brokerage activity" is the
condition that catches people.** A referring broker who flies in to attend a showing, or to meet the
client at the property, has done brokerage activity in North Carolina without a North Carolina licence.

### B.5 Earning compensation as a buyer agent

**Three legal requirements for establishing a claim:**

1. **A current real estate licence on active status.**
2. **A valid written buyer agency contract.**
3. **Procurement of property described in the agency agreement that the buyer agrees to purchase by
   enforceable contract.**

Note how requirement 3 differs from the listing side. On the listing side the broker procures a **ready,
willing and able buyer**; on the buyer side the broker procures **property described in the agency
agreement** which the buyer then agrees to purchase **by enforceable contract**.

Two compensation structures the Syllabus names on the buyer side:

- a **retainer fee** and a **success fee**; and
- **sharing in the listing broker's fee.**

### B.6 Payment where a licence has expired or become inactive

The rule and the exception, both from the Syllabus:

- **The licence must be on active status when the brokerage activity is performed.**
- **A broker must stop all brokerage activity when their licence expires or becomes inactive.**

**Instructor Note, transcribed:**

> **If the licences of the firm and the individual broker were both on active status at the time that
> any brokerage service was rendered in the transaction, then the firm may be paid compensation and the
> firm may in turn pay the individual broker, even if their licence is not active at the time of
> payment. Payment is forfeited if either of the licences were not active at any time that services
> were being performed.**

Read that twice, because it cuts both ways.

**The permission:** payment may be received after a licence has lapsed, **provided both licences were
active throughout the period services were rendered.** A licence that expires between closing and
disbursement does not destroy a properly earned fee.

**The forfeiture:** **"Payment is forfeited if EITHER of the licences were not active at ANY time that
services were being performed."** Not reduced — forfeited. And it turns on **both** the firm's licence
and the individual's, at **any** point during the work.

**The practical consequence:** know your renewal date, know your firm's, and **stop working the moment
either lapses.** Continuing to work through a lapse does not merely risk discipline; it destroys the
fee for the whole transaction.
