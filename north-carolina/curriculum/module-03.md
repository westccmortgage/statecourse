# North Carolina Broker Prelicensing Course — Module NC-03

## Section III · Real Property Characteristics, Legal Descriptions & Property Use

**Commission Syllabus section:** III — Real Property Characteristics, Legal Descriptions & Property Use
**Instructional Level:** **1** unless otherwise noted. *Assigned by the Commission; providers and
instructors are **REQUIRED to comply**.* Several subtopics carry their own Levels and are marked below —
**Fixtures is Level 3**, and Property Ownership and Interests, Encumbrances, Legal Descriptions, Zoning
concepts, Subdivision regulation and Private controls are **Level 2**.
**Licence examination weight:** **National Exam Category — 9 items.**
**Recommended allocation:** **5.0 instructional hours**, matching the Commission's recommended 1.0
hour for Basic Real Estate Concepts plus 4.0 for Property Characteristics, Description & Use. This
allocation is a plan, not proof of self-paced seat time; measured text/media time is reported separately.
**Course version:** 1.1 development draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No North Carolina prelicensing credit is available.

## Learning objectives

1. Define land, real estate, real property and personal property. *(Level 1)*
2. Name the three physical and four economic characteristics of land. *(Level 1)*
3. Distinguish severance from annexation and state how personal property transfers. *(Level 2)*
4. **Apply the Total Circumstances Test to a disputed item and predict how a court would rule.**
   *(Level 3)*
5. Distinguish fructus naturales from fructus industriales. *(Level 1)*
6. Distinguish an appurtenant easement from an easement in gross, and both from a licence. *(Level 2)*
7. State how easements are created, recorded and terminated. *(Level 2)*
8. Distinguish an encroachment from an easement and state how each is detected. *(Level 2)*
9. Describe the methods of legal description and identify which is primary in North Carolina. *(Level 2)*
10. State the broker's boundary on survey questions.
11. Name the four governmental powers and describe zoning administration in North Carolina.
12. Define the zoning concepts the Syllabus lists and apply them to examples. *(Level 2)*
13. Explain how restrictive covenants operate and which controls where they conflict with zoning. *(Level 2)*

## A. Basic Real Estate Concepts *(Level 1)*

### A.1 Terminology

**Land.** The surface, the space above and the subsurface beneath.

**Real estate.** Land plus everything permanently attached to it.

**Real property.** Real estate plus the bundle of legal rights attaching to it.

**Personal property** — *chattel* or *personalty*. Everything that is not real property.

**Instructor Note, from the Syllabus:** *"The terms real property, real estate and land are often used
interchangeably."* They are used interchangeably in ordinary speech and they are not the same thing,
which is why the definitions are learned rather than assumed.

### A.2 Physical characteristics of land

**Immobility.** **Indestructibility.** **Uniqueness** — non-homogeneity.

### A.3 Economic characteristics of land

**Scarcity.** **Location** — *situs* or area preference. **Improvements** — modifications.
**Permanence of investment** — fixity.

*(Note that the Commission's list differs slightly from the four-item lists used elsewhere: it names
**location** among the economic characteristics and pairs **improvements** with **permanence of
investment**. Learn the Commission's list, because the examination is built on the Syllabus.)*

### A.4 The scope of the real estate business, and the real estate market

Residential and commercial brokerage; property management; appraisal; development; financing; and the
title, closing and inspection businesses surrounding every transaction. The market for real estate is
**local**, because land is immobile, and **slow to respond**, because supply cannot be created quickly.

## B. Property Ownership and Interests *(Level 2 except as noted)*

### B.1 The concept of property

**Real property.** **Personal property** — *chattel* or *personalty*:

- **movable items**;
- **severance versus annexation/attachment** — the two events that move an item across the line; and
- **transferred by Bill of Sale**, not by deed.

### B.2 Fruits of the soil *(Level 1 — this subtopic)*

**Fructus naturales** — naturally occurring growth, treated as real property.
**Fructus industriales**, or **emblements** — annually cultivated crops produced by human labour,
treated as personal property.

### B.3 Fixtures *(Level 3 — this subtopic)*

**This is the highest Instructional Level in the section, and the Commission attaches a Required
Activity to it.**

#### The Total Circumstances Test

Used **in court when contract terms are silent about an item**. Written contract terms should clarify
the intent of the contract parties — which is the practical lesson: **the test exists because somebody
failed to write it down.**

**Four test criteria for determining whether an item is a fixture:**

1. **Intent of the annexor.**
2. **Relationship of the annexor to the property.**
3. **Method of annexation.**
4. **Adaptation to the real estate.**

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"All 4 criteria must be present to indicate that an item is a
fixture. Most standard listing agreements and sales contracts include a list of common items that will
be considered fixtures for the transaction unless otherwise addressed in the document."*

**Read the first sentence carefully.** The Commission states the test as **conjunctive — all four must
be present.** That is stricter than the "weigh the factors" formulation used in some materials, and it
is the formulation this course teaches because it is the Commission's.

> **Required Activity — prescribed by the Commission:** *"Use pictures to help students recognize items
> that might cause confusion in a transaction. Have students apply the Total Circumstances Test to
> predict how a judge might rule if the issue went to court."*
>
> **This activity must be completed.** It is a course completion condition, not an enrichment exercise.
> Work each of the following through all four criteria and state the predicted ruling with reasons:
> a wired-in dining room chandelier the seller intends to take; a mounted television and its bracket;
> a portable dishwasher connected to the sink; custom storm windows cut for these openings; a swing set
> anchored in concrete; an above-ground pool; a tenant's walk-in cooler; a propane tank on a lease.

![Eight fixture-dispute illustrations for the Total Circumstances Test](/media/nc-fixture-decision-lab.svg)

**Student activity.** For every pictured item, record four separate observations — intent, relationship,
method of annexation and adaptation — before predicting the result. A label such as “bolted” or “tenant”
does not decide the question by itself. The prediction must use **all four** Commission criteria, and the
transaction documents should expressly settle what conveys whenever the parties can do so.

#### Trade fixtures

Items a **tenant** attaches for the conduct of their **business**. They remain the tenant's personal
property and must be removed before the term ends, with the tenant repairing removal damage. Note how
the Total Circumstances Test handles them: criterion 2, **the relationship of the annexor to the
property**, is what distinguishes a tenant's installation from an owner's.

### B.4 Improvements *(Level 1 — this subtopic)*

**Improvements to the land** — grading, utilities, streets. **Improvements on the land** — buildings.

**Instructor Note:** *"Depending on context, Improved Land or Improved Lot may have dual meanings."*
And: **"On the National exam section, an improvement is an appurtenance."**

### B.5 Encumbrances to real property *(Level 2 except as noted)*

The Syllabus lists six and cross-references three of them forward:

- **Liens** — discussed in Section VI, Module NC-06.
- **Special assessments** — Section VI.
- **Lis pendens**, notice of pending litigation — Section VI.
- **Restrictive (protective) covenants** — CC&Rs, discussed later in this section at D.2.
- **Easements** — here.
- **Encroachments** — here.

#### Easements

**Terminology:** the **dominant estate or tenement** is benefited; the **servient estate or tenement**
is burdened.

**General categories:**

- **Appurtenant easement** — benefits a parcel; has both a dominant and a servient tenement; runs with
  the land.
- **Easement in gross** — benefits a person or entity. The Syllabus distinguishes **personal versus
  commercial** easements in gross.

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"Distinguish from a **licence**, which is a **personal, revocable,
non-assignable right, but NOT an encumbrance.**"*

That last clause is the tested point: a licence is not an encumbrance at all.

**Creation of easements:** **express**; **implied**; **arising by operation of law**.

**Recordation.** **Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"An easement must be **recorded to be binding
against a purchaser of the servient estate**."*

**Termination of easements**, and **party walls (cross easements)**.

#### Encroachments

**Examples.** **Usually detected by an up-to-date survey.** And the tested point:
**"Not discoverable by title search."**

That is why an encroachment and a recorded easement are found by two entirely different investigations,
and why a buyer who orders title work but no survey has looked for only one of them.

## C. Understanding Legal Descriptions *(Level 2 except as noted)*

### C.1 Adequacy of description, and the methods

**Metes and bounds** — **"Primary method in original 13 colonies, including NC."**

- **Point-of-beginning (POB).**
- **Outside boundaries are described through a series of calls.**
- Descriptions include **monuments**, **distance (metes)** and **course or direction (bounds)**.

> **Required Activity — prescribed by the Commission:** *"Provide a deed and a survey that illustrate
> description by metes and bounds."*

The following StateCourse exhibit is a **fictional teaching facsimile**, not a recorded deed, title
instrument or licensed survey. It shows how deed calls correspond to a plotted boundary, but it does
not yet close the Commission activity: a rights-safe deed and survey acceptable for regulatory review
remain an open course-material dependency.

![Fictional deed calls beside a matching metes-and-bounds plot](/media/nc-metes-bounds-training-exhibit.svg)

**Government (rectangular) survey system** *(Level 1 — this subtopic)* — **"Primary method for states
other than the original 13 colonies."** Division into squares based on the intersection of **prime
meridian lines** running north/south and **base lines** running east/west; **townships**; **sections**.

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"Although this method is **not used in NC**, basic principles are
subject to testing on the **National** section of the exam; **no calculations are tested on the
exam**."*

**That note is worth reading twice.** North Carolina does not use the rectangular survey, students are
examined on its principles, and **no rectangular-survey calculations appear on the examination.**

> **Required Activity — prescribed by the Commission:** *"Provide a visual illustration of an
> accompanying government survey description."*

![Government rectangular survey hierarchy from township to quarter-quarter section](/media/nc-government-survey-grid.svg)

**Reading the illustration.** Begin with the township created by principal meridian and base-line
references, locate the numbered section, then subdivide the section into quarters and quarter-quarters.
The diagram teaches the hierarchy only. Consistent with the Commission note, the activity requires no
acreage calculation.

**Reference to recorded plat (lot and block).**

> **Required Activity — prescribed by the Commission:** *"Provide a copy of an approved subdivision
> plat map."*

**Reference to publicly recorded documents.** **Informal reference** — e.g., a street address, which is
never an adequate legal description.

### C.2 Property survey, and the broker's boundary

> **Required Activity — prescribed by the Commission:** *"Provide a copy of a residential property
> survey. Ideally the survey will show setbacks for buildings, utility easements, and possibly an
> encroachment from an adjacent parcel."*

**Instructor Note, transcribed — and this is the single most important sentence in the section for
daily practice:**

> *"A broker should **never offer an opinion** about accuracy of the parcel boundaries, whether
> improvements are properly located on the tract, where easements are located on the property, or
> similar matters; **refer such queries to a licensed surveyor or civil engineer**."*

**Never. Not "be careful" — never.**

And the consequence for the buyer, transcribed: **"Title defects that would have been discovered by a
property survey are frequently not covered by title insurance, if a survey was not performed as part of
the transaction."** With the Commission's own advice attached: **"A buyer should be strongly encouraged
to order an updated survey as part of their due diligence process."**

## D. Land Use Controls

### D.1 Public controls

**Governmental powers — PETE:**

- **Police power** — for protection of the public; **includes zoning and environmental laws.**
- **Escheat.**
- **Taxation.**
- **Eminent domain.**

#### Zoning

**Administered primarily by the city or county planning department.** **Local boards of adjustment
generally handle requests for variances or special use permits.** And **extra-territorial jurisdictions
(ETJ)** — a North Carolina feature a licensee must know exists: a municipality may exercise zoning
authority beyond its corporate limits.

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"There is **no uniformity of zoning codes**; codes and their meanings
can **vary widely throughout the state**."*

So a licensee who learned "R-3" in one county knows nothing about "R-3" in the next one.

#### Zoning concepts *(Level 2 — this subtopic)*

> **Required Activity — prescribed by the Commission:** *"Provide examples of each of the following
> zoning concepts."*

**Nonconforming use** — with the Commission's own warning attached: *"Buyer should **never assume a
nonconforming use can continue** after transfer of ownership or destruction of improvements."*

**Illegal use. Zoning amendments. Variance. Special-use permit** (or conditional-use permit).
**Overlay district. Historic preservation zoning. Aesthetic zoning. Spot zoning. Cluster zoning.
Buffer zone.**

#### Required zoning examples

These examples identify the concepts; they do **not** state that any particular North Carolina ordinance
allows the result. The current city or county ordinance and the responsible planning office control.

| Concept | Illustrative example |
|---|---|
| Nonconforming use | A neighborhood grocery lawfully operating before the area was rezoned residential continues only to the extent the current ordinance permits. |
| Illegal use | A warehouse begins operating in a residential district after the restriction already applies and without required approval. |
| Zoning amendment | A local government changes a corridor from an industrial classification to a mixed-use classification through its legislative process. |
| Variance | An unusually shaped lot owner asks the board of adjustment for relief from a dimensional setback requirement. |
| Special-use or conditional-use permit | A daycare is a listed conditional use and may operate only after the designated board applies the ordinance's conditions. |
| Overlay district | A flood-hazard overlay adds requirements to property that retains its underlying residential classification. |
| Historic preservation zoning | Exterior work in a designated historic district is reviewed under preservation standards in addition to base zoning. |
| Aesthetic zoning | A sign or facade is reviewed against locally adopted appearance standards. |
| Spot zoning | One small parcel receives materially different treatment from surrounding parcels; whether that action is lawful is a legal and fact-specific question. |
| Cluster zoning | Homes are grouped more closely on one part of a tract while another part remains common open space, as the ordinance permits. |
| Buffer zone | A required landscaped strip separates a commercial use from adjoining residences. |

#### Urban and regional use planning

**Instructor Note:** these exist *"to assure orderly and systematic development of land use."*

#### Subdivision statutes and regulations *(Level 2 — this subtopic)*

**Typical subdivision approval procedures**, in North Carolina and elsewhere: a **preliminary planning
phase**; a **review and approval phase usually by multiple municipal departments**; and a **final
subdivision plat approved and recorded.**

#### Regulation of special land types

**Flood zones. Wetlands.**

#### Highway access restrictions

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"Ability to access an existing public highway or street that abuts
the property **is a material fact**; access may be restricted or forbidden by state or local
municipality."*

#### Interstate Land Sales Full Disclosure Act — 15 USC 1701 et seq.

Purpose; land registration requirement; application; exemptions. **This course states no threshold,
lot count or exemption from the Act, because it was not read from primary text for this project.**

#### Government land ownership

**Instructor Note:** *"Public ownership controls land for the benefit and use of the entire population,
such as national/state/municipal parks, road system, and preservation of natural resources."*

### D.2 Privately-imposed land use controls *(Level 2 except as noted)*

**Restrictive/protective covenants.** Set forth **in a deed or in a separately recorded declaration of
covenants**; also called **covenants, conditions and restrictions (CC&Rs)**.

**Typical matters addressed.** **Covenants run with the land (appurtenant) and are binding on all
subsequent owners.** **Enforcement.**

**Instructor Note, transcribed — the tested point:** *"When covenants differ from zoning regulations on
a particular matter, **the more restrictive of the two will control**."*

## Instructor source note for Module NC-03

Drawn from **Section III** of the Commission's Prelicensing Course Syllabus, March 2024 Edition amended
May 2026, read on 2026-08-20. Every Instructor Note and Required Activity above is **transcribed from
the Syllabus.**

The provisions of **NCGS Chapter 93A** and **21 NCAC Chapter 58** used in Modules NC-01 and NC-02 were
read from current official text. No additional provision from them supplies the property-law rules in
this module. **Not read, and therefore stated nowhere in this module:** the **Interstate Land Sales Full
Disclosure Act**; North Carolina's subdivision, easement and survey statutes; and any municipal zoning
ordinance. No lot threshold, exemption, setback standard, plat requirement or prescriptive period is
stated. Where this module reaches one of those, it names the question and routes it — and on boundaries,
improvements and easement locations the routing is the Commission's own: **to a licensed surveyor or
civil engineer.**
