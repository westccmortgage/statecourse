# North Carolina Broker Prelicensing Course — Module NC-07

## Section VII · Real Estate Contracts & Agency

**Commission Syllabus section:** VII — Real Estate Contracts & Agency
**Instructional Level:** **3** except as noted. *Assigned by the Commission; providers and instructors
are **REQUIRED to comply**.* At Level 3 the instructor treats the topic **in substantial depth** and
**requires students to complete practical work assignments** applying it to common fact situations.
**Basic contract concepts and terminology, special contract forms, preemptive rights, brokerage
employment relationships and implied agency are Level 1**; **reality of consent, other contract
concepts, and the importance of the sales contract are Level 2.**
**Licence examination weight:** **National Exam Category — 16 items — tied for the heaviest section in
the entire examination.**
**Instructional hours:** **6.0**, allocated within the Commission's recommended emphasis for Agency
(13.0) and Contracts and Closing (18.0).
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No North Carolina prelicensing credit is available.

## Learning objectives

1. Classify contracts by formation, obligation, performance and validity. *(Level 1)*
2. Apply the four essential elements to a defective fact pattern. *(Level 3)*
3. Apply the Mailbox Rule correctly, and state its two limits.
4. State what is and is not the consideration in a sales contract and in a lease.
5. Classify capacity defects as **void** or **voidable**. *(Level 3)*
6. Distinguish mistake, fraud, duress and undue influence. *(Level 2)*
7. Name the ways a contract is terminated or discharged, and distinguish that from termination of an
   offer. *(Level 2)*
8. State the assignor's continuing liability.
9. Name the remedies for breach.
10. State the rules for submitting concurrent offers.
11. Explain why brokers must not draft, and which documents require an attorney. *(Level 1)*
12. **State that facilitator/transaction brokerage is not allowed in North Carolina.**
13. Recite the six agent's duties to a principal. *(Level 3)*
14. Explain the automatic subagency consequence of firm agency.
15. Name the ways an agency terminates and the three exceptions to duties ending.

## A. Basic Contract Concepts, Terminology & Classifications *(Level 1)*

**Contract. Express/implied. Bilateral/unilateral. Executed/executory. Valid. Void. Voidable.
Addenda. Amendments. Contingency.**

Ten italicised terms in the Syllabus — which under its own legend means **students should know the
definitions.** Note that the Commission lists **addenda**, **amendments** and **contingency** as basic
terminology rather than as advanced concepts, and it distinguishes them: an **addendum** is added to a
contract, an **amendment** changes one, and a **contingency** is a condition.

## B. Essential Elements of a Contract *(Level 3)*

### B.1 Mutual assent

Also known as **meeting of the minds**, mutual agreement, or offer and acceptance.

**The offer.** **Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"Pay careful attention to terminology. **There is a
huge difference between an offer and a contract.**"*

**Offeror versus offeree.** **Instructor Note:** *"Typically, the buyer is the initial offeror and the
seller is the initial offeree; **but sellers can initiate offers.**"*

**Acceptance.** **Instructor Note, transcribed — and this one reaches into every day of practice:**
*"**Communication to the agent is the same as communication to the principal.**"*

So an acceptance delivered to you is delivered to your client. You cannot hold it and treat the
transaction as still open.

### B.2 Communication of offer and acceptance

- **Oral communication.**
- **Personal delivery.**
- **Mail** — traditional or special mail such as USPS, FedEx, UPS — and the **Mailbox Rule.**

**Instructor Note on the Mailbox Rule, transcribed:** *"**Time of actual receipt by offeror is
irrelevant.** This Rule **only applies to communicating ACCEPTANCE of an offer; not termination of an
offer or contract.**"*

**Two limits in one sentence.** Acceptance is effective on dispatch, so receipt does not matter — **and
the rule does not apply to a revocation, a rejection or a termination**, each of which is effective only
on receipt.

- **Electronic methods** — email, text or fax **unless prohibited by the terms of the offer**, and
  **effective upon receipt by the offeror's communication equipment or computer.**

**Note the asymmetry the Commission has just described.** Mailed acceptance: effective on **dispatch**.
Electronic acceptance: effective on **receipt by the equipment**. They are different rules and the
examination tests the difference.

### B.3 Counteroffer

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"Once an offeree makes a counteroffer, they **cannot change their
mind later and 'accept' the original offer**."*

### B.4 Termination of offer

Distinguish carefully from termination of a **contract** at D.2 — the Commission flags the distinction
in a Required Activity below.

### B.5 Consideration

**Instructor Note, transcribed — and this is a reliably tested point:**

> *"**The sales price is the consideration in a sales contract, NOT the earnest money deposit or the
> due diligence fee. Rent is the consideration in a lease, NOT the tenant security deposit.**"*

### B.6 Legal capacity of the parties — void or voidable?

The Commission's four cases, each with its consequence:

| Party | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Minors** — persons under legal age, **18 in NC** | **voidable** |
| **Persons declared mentally incompetent by a court of law** | **VOID** |
| **Persons mentally incompetent in fact** | **voidable** |
| **Persons intoxicated or under drug influence who temporarily do not understand what they are doing** | **voidable** |

**Only one of the four is void, and it is the one where a court has already adjudicated the
incompetence.** Everything else in the capacity list is voidable. That single distinction is worth
memorising exactly as the Commission states it.

## C. Requirement for Voluntary Reality of Consent *(Level 2)*

> **Required Activity — prescribed by the Commission:** *"Provide real world examples of each of the
> following ways that real estate contracts may be declared **void or voidable**."*

### C.1 Mistake

**Mistake of fact.** A contract **may be voidable if the mistake of fact:**

- **involves a material term or aspect;**
- **is mutual; and**
- **is not the result of fraud or negligence.**

**All three.** And the corollary the Commission states: **"Unilateral mistakes of fact do not release
parties from their obligations."**

**Mistake of law.**

### C.2 Fraud

Its **elements**; and **voidable by the deceived party — not automatic.**

### C.3 Duress

Examples; **voidable by the injured party.**

### C.4 Undue influence

Examples; **voidable by the injured party.**

**Notice the pattern across C.1 to C.4: every one of them produces a VOIDABLE contract at the election
of the injured party, not a void one.** Combined with B.6, the only void case in the whole of Section
VII's consent material is the court-adjudicated incompetent.

## E. Basic Sales Contract Concepts

### E.1 The importance of the sales contract *(Level 2)*

- It **sets forth the parameters of the legally binding agreement** between buyer and seller.
- **Standardized forms give uniformity to transactions and reduce brokers' temptation to draft.**
- **Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"Brokers are expected to **know when it is appropriate to use a
  particular contract form** and to **recognize situations where sellers and buyers should be referred
  to an attorney** to have a suitable contract drafted."*

### E.2 Contract creation, and the prohibition on drafting

**Instructor Note, transcribed:**

> *"**Brokers are prohibited from drafting legal documents for others; this is seen as illegal practice
> of law in most states.** Consumers should be encouraged to seek legal assistance if standard forms do
> not fit their needs."*

- **The Statute of Frauds in most states requires contracts to be in writing to be enforceable.**
- **Offer and acceptance** by traditional methods and by **electronic means** — e-mail, DocuSign.

### E.3 Submitting offers — five stated rules

Transcribed, because each is examinable and each is a daily practice rule:

1. **Concurrent offers must be presented AT THE SAME TIME; no particular order of presentation is
   required.**
2. **This applies to ALL brokers in the transaction regardless of who they represent or how they will
   be compensated.**
3. **The listing broker generally has NO AUTHORITY to accept or reject an offer on behalf of the
   seller-client.**
4. **Only 1 contract signed by all parties is required to be binding.**
5. **The buyer acquires EQUITABLE TITLE once under contract.**

### E.4 Handling offer modifications and counteroffers

- **Any change to an offer rejects the original offer; potentially creates a counteroffer.**
- **Numerous term changes should prompt use of a new offer form** completed with the current terms
  under consideration.

### E.7 Handling contract disputes and modifications

- **Seek legal advice BEFORE breaching contract terms.**
- **Negotiate an amendment to the contract.**

## F. Special Contract Forms *(Level 1)*

> **Important Note — transcribed from the Syllabus:** *"Buyers and sellers **should be referred to an
> attorney for preparation of any of the following documents. Drafting of such documents by a broker
> constitutes the unlawful practice of law.**"*

### F.1 Installment land contract — contract for deed, or land contract

- **A sales contract AND an owner-financing document.**
- **The seller (vendor) retains legal title until the entire purchase price is paid.**
- **The buyer (vendee) receives equitable title and possession.**
- **No standard contract form; consult an attorney.** **Instructor Note: "Do not use a standard sales
  contract."**
- Advantages and disadvantages to both seller and buyer.

### F.2 Option to purchase real estate

- **No standard option form; consult an attorney.** **"Do not use a standard sales contract."**
- **Optionor** and **optionee**.
- **Two types of consideration should be definitely and separately stated:** the **option fee**,
  **generally non-refundable**, and the **purchase price**.
- **Must be in writing and recorded generally per the Statute of Frauds.**
- **Time being of the essence in regard to the date to exercise the option.**
- Residential leases may include an option to purchase. **Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"No standard
  form exists for a residential lease with option to purchase, and **a broker must not suggest that
  landlords/tenants attempt to tie together lease and offer to purchase forms**. A broker should direct
  the parties to enlist an attorney to draft this special type of agreement."*

### F.3 Agreements for preemptive rights *(Level 1)*

**Right of first refusal.** **Right of first opportunity to purchase.**

## G. Overview of Laws and Regulations Governing Brokerage Relationships

### G.1 The three sources

**Common Law of Agency. Contract law. Individual state laws and regulations.**

### G.2 Basic agency terms — and one that does not exist here

**Agency. Principal/client. Agent. Subagent. Third party. Customer.**

**Facilitator/transactional broker** — and the Commission states two things about it:

- **"Disclosure of material facts is normally required."**
- **"NOT ALLOWED IN NORTH CAROLINA."**

**Learn the concept, because it is on the National examination. Never practise it, because North
Carolina does not permit it.** North Carolina is an agency state: a broker working with a party is
either their agent or is dealing with them as a customer. There is no neutral middle status.

*(This is the direct opposite of Oklahoma, where the statute abolished agency and made the neutral
broker the norm. A licensee moving between states cannot carry either assumption across.)*

### G.3 The fiduciary role

**A fiduciary — e.g., an agent — is bound to act primarily for the benefit of the principal — e.g., the
client — BEFORE ANY SELF-INTEREST.**

### G.4 Classifications of agency relationships

**Universal agent. General agent. Special agent.**

### G.5 Creation of real estate agency relationships

**By agency contracts/agreements**, between brokers/firms and transaction parties:

- **Listing contract**
- **Buyer Agency contract**
- **Dual Agency contract**
- **Property Management contract**
- **Tenant Representation contract**

**Between brokers and brokerage companies** *(Level 1)*: **in-house brokerage employment contracts**;
**cooperation agreements between brokers/firms**.

**By conduct of the parties — implied agency** *(Level 1)*: for example, cooperation between firms
**based on brokers' actual conduct and custom rather than an explicit agreement.**

### G.6 Scope of an agent's authority

**Express authority. Implied authority. Apparent authority.**

### G.7 Real estate agency relationships

**The consumer employs a sole practitioner broker** — the agency agreement is between the sole
proprietor's company and the consumer.

**The consumer employs a brokerage company/firm** — and the Commission states three consequences that
follow, transcribed:

- **The agency agreement is between the consumer and the FIRM, not with the individual affiliated
  agents.**
- **A broker affiliated with a firm is an agent of the firm under the law of agency.**
- **ALL brokers affiliated with the firm AUTOMATICALLY become subagents of any principal-client of the
  firm.**

**That third consequence is the structural fact behind everything in Module NC-08.** When your firm
takes a listing, **you** become a subagent of that seller automatically — whether or not you have met
them, whether or not you work in that office. It is why in-house sales raise dual agency, and why the
Commission calls careless conduct here **"illegal undisclosed dual agency."**

**Agency relationships in sales:**

- **Single agency — seller agency only.** The company only represents owners; all affiliated brokers
  represent all seller-clients **as seller subagents**; all potential buyers are shown the company's
  listings **as unrepresented third parties (customers)**.
- **Single agency — buyer agency only.** The company **has no listings**; all affiliated brokers
  represent all buyer-clients **as buyer subagents**; brokers show only properties listed with
  cooperating companies or unrepresented sellers (FSBOs).
- **Cross sale or co-brokered transaction.** **Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"**Beware of illegal
  undisclosed dual agency through careless actions of brokers.**"* Concepts: **exclusive agency**;
  **seller subagency**; **the listing company can refuse to offer subagency to the other company**;
  **switching from seller subagent to buyer agent**.
- **Both seller and buyer agency with dual agency for "in-house" sales.** **Intentional, disclosed dual
  agency**; **unintentional, undisclosed dual agency prohibited**; **dual agency with designated
  agents.**

**Agency relationships in rentals:**

- **Created by a property management agreement.**
- **The management firm/broker is the agent and the landlord is the principal.**
- **Any licensed employees of the management firm/broker are subagents of the landlord.**
- **Residential prospective tenants are usually customers.**
- **Commercial tenants may or may not be represented; any representation agreement should be in writing
  prior to an offer to rent.**

### G.8 The agent's duties to the principal *(Level 3)*

> **Required Activity — prescribed by the Commission:** *"Use real world examples to illustrate each
> duty."*

The Commission's six:

1. **Obedience.**
2. **Loyalty** — including **avoidance of self-dealing by the agent**, and **written client consent to
   represent adverse interests** such as dual agency.
3. **Disclosure of information**, and the Commission states its scope expansively: **"to include the
   agent's AFFIRMATIVE DUTY TO DISCOVER and disclose all transactional information including, but not
   limited to, material facts."** **Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"**Personal information about a
   third party** known to an agent **that might influence the agent's principal** in the transaction
   **must be disclosed to the principal.**"*
4. **Confidentiality.**
5. **Accounting for and safeguarding of goods and funds handled, plus retention of transactional
   records.** **Instructor Note:** *"**No commingling of trust monies with company or personal
   funds.**"*
6. **Skill, care and diligence.**

**Duty 3 is the one that separates North Carolina from a passive-disclosure state.** It is an
**affirmative duty to DISCOVER** — not merely to pass on what you happen to learn.

### G.9 Termination of the agency relationship

> **Required Activity — prescribed by the Commission:** *"Provide examples of **terminations and
> exceptions**."*

**Agency terminates by:**

- **the purpose having been accomplished;**
- **expiration of the term;**
- **mutual agreement to terminate;**
- **breach by a party;**
- **destruction or condemnation of the property;**
- **death or incapacity of either party;**
- **operation of law.**

**Duty generally ends when the agency ends — with three exceptions:**

1. **an express promise or implied obligation;**
2. **a pending transaction at the termination date of the agency agreement;**
3. **self-dealing.**

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"**Duty of confidentiality may be expected to continue as long as the
agent has no duty to disclose to a new client.**"*

### G.10 Liabilities and consequences of an agent's breach *(Level 3)*

- **Disciplinary action by the regulatory agency against the agent.**
- **Civil liability of the agent.**
- **Criminal liability of the agent.**
- **Civil liability of the PRINCIPAL for the agent's misconduct — tort.**

**The fourth is the one clients do not expect**, and it is a reason to be careful with what you say on a
principal's behalf: their exposure can follow from your conduct.
