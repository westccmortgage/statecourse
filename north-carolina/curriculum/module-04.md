# North Carolina Broker Prelicensing Course — Module NC-04

## Section IV · Property Taxes & Insurance

**Commission Syllabus section:** IV — Property Taxes & Insurance
**Instructional Level:** **2** except as noted. *Assigned by the Commission; providers and instructors
are **REQUIRED to comply**.* At Level 2 the instructor reviews and discusses the topic **in moderate
depth** sufficient to illustrate facts, principles and procedures **and their relevance to brokerage
practice**. **Tax Calculations, Excise Tax Calculations, the Broker's Responsibility on insurance, and
Taxation of Capital Gain are Level 3**; the insurance definitions, package insurance, coinsurance and
most of Federal Income Taxation are **Level 1**.
**Licence examination weight:** **State Exam Category — 4 items.**
**Instructional hours:** **4.0**, matching the Commission's recommended emphasis for Taxation &
Insurance.
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No North Carolina prelicensing credit is available.

## Learning objectives

1. Define ad valorem and compute a North Carolina property tax from a rate per $100. *(Level 3)*
2. Convert between a North Carolina rate per $100 and a mill rate, and compute from either. *(Level 3)*
3. State how North Carolina assesses real property and how often it reappraises. *(Level 2)*
4. Recite the North Carolina property tax timetable and explain the two-lien situation it can produce.
5. State the priority of a North Carolina real property tax lien.
6. Compute North Carolina excise tax and state who pays it. *(Level 3)*
7. Define the basic insurance terms and describe HO-3, HO-4 and HO-6 coverage. *(Level 1)*
8. Explain what flood insurance is, who administers it, and what a 100-year flood plain means.
9. **State the broker's five insurance responsibilities.** *(Level 3)*
10. Describe the common income tax deductions for owners of personal residences. *(Level 1)*
11. State the rules for taxation of capital gain on the sale of a principal residence. *(Level 3)*
12. State the licensee's boundary on tax advice.

## A. Ad Valorem Property Tax

### A.1 Ad valorem

**Ad valorem** — according to value. Real property tax is charged as a proportion of the property's
value rather than as a flat amount.

### A.2 Tax rate and calculations

**The North Carolina real property tax rate is set annually by the municipality** — for example the
county and the town or city.

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"A property located within a city or town limit in NC will be
**subject to both county and city/town taxes**."*

That is a real trap for a licensee quoting a tax figure from a county record on a property inside a
municipality: the county rate is not the whole bill.

**The North Carolina tax rate is expressed as $xxx per $100 of assessed value.**

**Other states express the rate as a mill rate.** From the Syllabus: *"A mill is 1/10th of a cent; a
mill rate is described as mills per dollar of assessed value. [Example: A NC tax rate of $1.50 per $100
(which is $0.015 per dollar) is the same as a mill rate of 15 mills per dollar.]"*

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"Either of the above tax rates may be cited in **National** exam
section items and **students must be able to perform calculations using either rate**."*

### A.3 Tax calculations *(Level 3 — this subtopic)*

**North Carolina, rate per $100.**

> Tax = (assessed value ÷ 100) × rate per $100

*Worked.* Assessed value **$284,000**; county rate **$0.6150** per $100; city rate **$0.4425** per $100.

County: (284,000 ÷ 100) × 0.6150 = 2,840 × 0.6150 = **$1,746.60**
City: 2,840 × 0.4425 = **$1,256.70**
**Total annual tax = $3,003.30**

*(The rates above are invented for arithmetic. They are not North Carolina rates for any jurisdiction.)*

**Mill rate.**

> Tax = assessed value × (mills ÷ 1,000)

*Worked.* Assessed value **$284,000**, rate **15 mills**. 284,000 × 0.015 = **$4,260.**

**Check the equivalence the Syllabus states:** $1.50 per $100 is $0.015 per dollar is 15 mills. So
2,840 × 1.50 = $4,260 by the North Carolina method and 284,000 × 0.015 = $4,260 by the mill method.
**The same tax, computed two ways.**

**Converting.** Rate per $100 × 10 = mills. Mills ÷ 10 = rate per $100. ($1.50 × 10 = 15 mills.)

### A.4 All real property in North Carolina is subject to taxation

Stated by the Syllabus as a topic in its own right. Exemptions exist and are a matter for the taxing
authority; **this course states none**, because the governing statutes were not read for this project.

### A.5 Appraisal and assessment

**The NC Machinery Act regulates taxation in North Carolina.**

- **Assessed value of real property for property taxation purposes in NC must be, with few exceptions,
  its market value or "…its true value in money" at the time of assessment.**
- **State authority to tax is delegated to local municipalities** — county, city, town.
- **Octennial Reappraisal: NC real property must be reassessed every 8 years.**
- **Horizontal reappraisal by a municipality is possible in the 4th year.**

**In other states**, the assessed value for taxation may be **less than market value** — such as 50
percent of market value.

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"National exam section items might mention an assessed value that is
less than market value and **these items should be answered based on the given information rather than
on NC practice**. Also, the term *true market value* should be assumed to mean *market value*."*

**That is examination guidance from the Commission and it is worth carrying into the examination room:
on a National item, use the assessed value the item gives you; do not substitute North Carolina's
market-value rule.**

### A.6 The North Carolina property tax timetable

Five dates, transcribed:

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **January 1** | **The tax lien attaches to the property.** |
| **January 31** | **Taxable real and personal property listing deadline.** |
| **July 1** | **The annual tax rate must be set by municipalities.** |
| **September 1** | **The annual tax bill is due and payable.** |
| **January 5 of the next year** | **Last day to pay the current tax bill without late penalty.** |

**Instructor Note on 1 January, transcribed:** *"Real property tax liens attach to the property on the
1st day of the listing period, **even though the tax is not due until later in the year**."*

**Instructor Note on the overlap, transcribed:** *"If the previous year's taxes are not paid by December
31, it is possible on **January 1–5 for property to have tax liens for two successive years that are
both current**."*

**Read the second note carefully — it is exactly the kind of point the state examination tests.** In
that five-day window, two years' liens exist simultaneously and neither is delinquent, because the prior
year's still has until 5 January and the new year's attached on 1 January.

**And the practical consequence for a closing in that window:** a closing on 3 January must deal with
two current tax liens, and the licensee's job is to notice that and get it in front of the closing
attorney early.

### A.7 Special priority of real property tax liens — NCGS 105-356

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"Real property tax liens are **superior to all other liens, charges,
assessments, rights and claims regardless of when acquired**."*

**Regardless of when acquired.** First in time, first in right does not apply to them.

## B. NC Excise Tax — NCGS 105-228.30

### B.1 Key concepts

- **Paid by the grantor in NC** — but **may vary in other states**.
- **Formerly called revenue stamps or deed transfer tax.**

### B.2 The rate

**$1.00 on each $500 (or portion thereof) of the total sales price.**

### B.3 Calculations *(Level 3 — this subtopic)*

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"Excise tax **always rounds up to a full dollar amount** in NC;
**there are no cents**."*

> Excise tax = **round UP** (sales price ÷ 500) × $1.00

*Worked.* Sales price **$284,000.** 284,000 ÷ 500 = **568** exactly. 568 × $1.00 = **$568.00.**

*Worked, with a remainder.* Sales price **$287,300.** 287,300 ÷ 500 = **574.6**. The rule is **or
portion thereof**, so round **up** to **575**. 575 × $1.00 = **$575.00.**

*Worked again.* Sales price **$310,100.** 310,100 ÷ 500 = 620.2 → **621** → **$621.00.**

**Never round down and never carry cents.** A price $1 above a multiple of $500 adds a whole dollar of
excise tax.

### B.4 Local transfer tax

**Possible in some North Carolina counties.** **This course states no county, rate or applicability**,
because no such provision was read from primary text for this project. **Route it to the closing
attorney and the county.**

## C. Homeowner's (Hazard) Property Insurance Basics *(Level 1 — these subtopics)*

### C.1 Definitions

**Insurance. Insured. Insurer. Premium. Condition. Endorsement (rider). Exclusion. Property (casualty)
insurance. Liability insurance.**

Those nine are italicised terms in the Syllabus, which means — from its own legend — **students should
know the definitions.**

### C.2 Package insurance

**A combination of property and liability coverage** — for example **HO-3** — and its typical coverage.

### C.3 Coinsurance clause

A provision requiring the insured to carry coverage of at least a stated proportion of value, failing
which a loss is only partly paid.

### C.4 Specialized policies

- **HO-3** — homeowner's coverage.
- **HO-4** — renter's coverage.
- **HO-6** — condominium coverage.

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"Buyer should make sure insurance is **appropriate for property type
and use**."*

A buyer who buys a condominium and insures it as a house, or who rents out a property insured as an
owner-occupied residence, has coverage that may not respond.

## D. Flood Insurance

- **A separate policy; not included in a standard homeowner policy.**
- **The National Flood Insurance Program is administered by FEMA and delivered through private
  insurance companies.**
- Its **purpose**, and **designated or special FEMA flood hazard zones**.

**Instructor Note, transcribed — and it is a genuinely common misunderstanding:**

> *"The **100-year flood plain** refers to a flood having a **1% chance of equalling or exceeding the
> map elevation any given year**, not a flood that occurs only every 100 years."*

Say that to a buyer in those words. A property in a 100-year flood plain does not flood once a century;
it carries a one percent annual probability, which over a thirty-year loan is a materially different
proposition.

## E. Insurance Issues

### E.1 Insurability

- **High insurance claim history.**
- **Comprehensive Loss Underwriting Exchange (C.L.U.E.)** — the claims database insurers consult.

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"Consumers are entitled to a **free copy of their C.L.U.E. report**
per the **Fair Credit Reporting Act**."*

The consequence a buyer needs: **a property's claims history can affect whether and at what price it can
be insured**, independently of its condition today — and insurability is a financing question, because a
lender will not close without a bound policy.

### E.2 Residential sales transaction concerns

**Unoccupied Building Exclusion.** **Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"Definition of unoccupied varies
widely; **read policy for specific parameters** for a particular property."*

**Early or late possession by the parties** — which changes who is occupying and therefore whether the
exclusion bites.

**Keep insurance in place until recordation/closing; refer to the "Risk of Loss" provision in the sales
contract.**

### E.3 Broker's responsibility *(Level 3 — this subtopic)*

**This is the Level 3 subtopic of the whole insurance topic, and it is a list of five affirmative
duties. Transcribed:**

1. **Advise the seller client about the importance of having and maintaining appropriate insurance
   coverage throughout the listing and the transfer of title/closing.**
2. **A listing agent should advise an absentee seller to verify continued insurance coverage of the
   unoccupied listed property.**
3. **Be alert for red flags** — for example, nearby water sources possibly requiring flood insurance.
4. **Disclose the location of any part of the property in a FEMA special flood zone as a material
   fact.**
5. **A buyer client should be advised to check availability and cost of insurance as part of buyer due
   diligence.**

**Item 4 is a disclosure duty, not a courtesy.** Any part of the property in a FEMA special flood zone
is **a material fact that must be disclosed.**

**Item 2 pairs with the unoccupied building exclusion at E.2**, and together they describe a real and
common failure: a vacant listing whose owner has moved away, whose policy has quietly stopped
responding, and whose agent never raised it.

## F. Federal Income Taxation of Home Ownership *(Level 1 except as noted)*

**Instructor Note, transcribed — read it before the substance:**

> *"Licensees should **never attempt to provide tax advice** to clients or customers unless they possess
> special knowledge and expertise in the tax field that qualifies them to provide such advice. **The
> limited instruction provided in this course in no way qualifies the student to provide tax advice**
> and is merely intended to enable agents to **recognize situations where a consumer might benefit from
> professional tax advice** so that the agent might then **refer the consumer to a qualified tax
> accountant or attorney**."*

**That is the purpose of everything in Part F: recognition and referral. Nothing else.**

### F.1 Common income tax deductions for owners of personal residences

- **Real property taxes** — principal and second homes.
- **Loan expenses** — principal and second homes, including:
  - **mortgage interest**, which **includes regular interest payments plus any late payment charges or
    prepayment penalty**; and
  - **loan discount points paid as prepaid interest at the time of loan origination**.

### F.2 Taxation of capital gain on sale of a principal residence *(Level 3 — this subtopic)*

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"Concepts are testable, but **there are no math calculations on this
topic**."*

**Eligible property**, and the definitions:

- **Basis** and **adjusted basis.**
- **Capital improvement** — with the Commission's note: *"**Maintenance is not a capital
  improvement.**"*
- **Amount realized.**
- **Gain** and **loss.**

**Rules for taxation of capital gain:**

- **Tax-free limits**, stated separately for **a married couple filing jointly** and for **a single
  person or a married couple filing singly**.
- **Restrictions:**
  - **Must have been the primary residence for 2 of the last 5 years.**
  - **The exclusion is allowed only once every 2 years.**

**This course states no dollar exclusion amount**, because the federal provision was not read from
primary text for this project and the Syllabus states the limits as categories rather than figures. **The
two restrictions above are stated because the Syllabus states them.** Every figure goes to a qualified
tax accountant or attorney — which is precisely what Part F's Instructor Note directs.

### F.3 Special tax treatment for some residential transactions

Each at **basic concept** level only:

- **Installment sales** — or **contract for deed** in North Carolina.
- **Like-Kind or 1031 Tax-Deferred Exchange.**
- **Vacation homes** — the basic rule for owner use.
- **Office in home.**

**Recognise these situations and refer them.** A seller who mentions a 1031 exchange has a timetable
that will control the transaction, and the licensee's contribution is to get them to their tax adviser
**before** an offer is accepted rather than after.
