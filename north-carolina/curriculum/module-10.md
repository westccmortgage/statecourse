## A. Selected Regulated Practices

### A.1 Land use controls *(Level 1 except as noted)*

**Current NC statutory authority for governmental zoning — Chapter 160D:**

- **The General Assembly has police power per the state constitution.**
- **A local government may adopt zoning regulations under G.S. 160D-702**, subject to the current
  Chapter's procedures, limits and the local ordinance actually governing the property.

**Currency correction.** The Syllabus still names former county/city enabling provisions in Chapters
153A and 160A. G.S. 160D-111(c) now directs references to repealed or superseded parts of those former
provisions to the most nearly corresponding part of Chapter 160D. A broker verifies the current local
zoning map, ordinance and development approval; the broker does not decide legal validity or vested
rights.

**Highway access restrictions.** **Instructor Note:** *"Ability to access an existing public highway or
street that abuts the property **is a material fact**; access may be restricted or forbidden by state or
local municipality."*

**Interstate Land Sales Full Disclosure Act — 15 USC 1701 et seq.:** purpose; land registration
requirement; application; exemptions.

#### The broker's responsibilities for verification of land use *(Level 3)*

**Instructor Note, transcribed — and this is the section's clearest statement of the North Carolina
standard:**

> *"Under the NC Real Estate License Law, a licensee has **a duty to avoid any misrepresentation
> regarding permitted land use, to recognize potential land use problems, and AN AFFIRMATIVE DUTY TO
> DISCOVER AND DISCLOSE any such problems.** Licensee is **NOT expected to conduct a title search for
> covenants or interpret covenants** as to what activities are permissible; **advising clients to have
> an attorney perform such actions would be appropriate.**"*

**Both halves matter.** There is an affirmative duty to **discover** land use problems — and there is
**no** duty to search title for covenants or to interpret them, and doing the latter would be advising
on the law. **Discover the problem; route the interpretation.**

The five specific requirements repeat Module NC-05 A.1 and are stated in both sections because the
Commission states them in both:

- advise that **ordinances or covenants could affect use and enjoyment**;
- advise that owners **may be subject to owners' association dues and assessments**;
- advise that **careful review of covenants and association governing documents is recommended PRIOR TO
  SIGNING ANY OFFER**;
- **avoid any misrepresentation about zoning or permitted land uses**;
- **recognize "red flag" situations**, **recognize patent signs of flooding**, and **disclose planned
  major highway or public transportation changes in the area as a material fact.**

#### A.1a Level 3 evidence — land-use verification lab

For a listing advertised as suitable for a duplex and short-term rental, the broker records: the
current local zoning map and ordinance source; the parcel's district; whether each proposed use is
permitted, conditional or prohibited; known development approvals; access/flood/transit red flags;
and every unresolved question routed to the local authority or counsel. The broker reports source and
date, never promises that a use is “grandfathered,” and does not interpret private covenants.

### A.2 Environmental issues

**The North Carolina formulation, transcribed — note that it is worded more strictly than Section V's:**

> *"The presence on a property of any of the environmental hazards **that is KNOWN OR SHOULD HAVE BEEN
> KNOWN by a North Carolina broker** is a material fact in North Carolina that **must be disclosed to
> all transactional parties.**"*

**NC Leaking Petroleum Underground Storage Tank Cleanup Act — NCGS 143-215.94A-.94E:**

- G.S. 143-215.94A separately defines the **tank owner**, **operator**, **facility** and **landowner**;
  ownership of the land does not automatically answer who owns or operates the tank.
- When a petroleum release is determined, G.S. 143-215.94E(a) directs the tank owner or operator to
  notify the Department, collect/remove the release and restore the affected area. Current-fact and
  fund-eligibility rules are more specific than the slogan “the present landowner always pays.”
- Current law contains a landowner protection where the person owns the tank only by owning the land,
  did not know or have reason to know it was there, and did not receive the land through a transfer to
  avoid liability. Where the owner/operator cannot be identified or fails to act, G.S. 143-215.94E(b1)
  separately permits the current landowner to undertake work and seek specified fund treatment.
- **Broker responsibilities:**
  - A broker discloses a known or reasonably discoverable release, contamination, abandoned tank or
    other material tank condition. Mere historical tank presence is not converted here into an
    automatic legal-liability conclusion.
  - **The buyer agent should strongly encourage the buyer to hire an expert to inspect the tank and
    conduct a soil contamination test.**

The broker identifies red flags, recommends qualified inspection/DEQ review and discloses the material
facts; counsel and DEQ determine statutory responsibility, cleanup and fund eligibility.

### A.3 Delivery of instruments *(Level 3)* — NCGS 93A-6(a)(13) and (14); Rule 58A .0106

**a. Assist with delivery of completed RPOADS disclosure statements.** *(Module NC-15 now contains the
current Chapter 47E workflow and retained, page-reviewed REC 4.22 and REC 4.25 forms.)*

**b. Submit offers — and this is the rule to know cold:**

> **"Brokers must present ALL offers (including multiple offers and back-up offers) to the offeree (or
> their agent) IMMEDIATELY but in no case LATER THAN 3 DAYS from the broker's receipt of the executed
> offer."** — NCGS 93A-6(a)(13); Rule 58A .0106(a)

**Five qualifications, all transcribed:**

1. **"EVEN IF PROPERTY ALREADY UNDER CONTRACT."**
2. **"Broker CANNOT 'HOLD' offer while negotiating compensation agreement."**
3. **"Concurrent offers must be presented at the same time; no particular order of presentation is
   required."**
4. **"ORAL presentation of the offer terms to the offeree DOES NOT NEGATE the requirement for the broker
   to deliver the offer within 3 days."**
5. **"Applies to ALL brokers in the transaction regardless of who they represent or how they will be
   compensated."**

**Qualification 2 is the one that catches new licensees**, and it is worth stating plainly: **you may
not sit on an offer while you work out what you will be paid.** Qualification 4 closes the other obvious
escape: telling the seller about it over the phone does not satisfy the three-day delivery requirement.

**c. Furnish copies of offers and contracts** — Rule 58A .0106(a).

**d. The broker's responsibilities relating to settlement statements** — NCGS 93A-6(a)(14). See B.6.

#### A.3a Level 3 evidence — offer presentation and delivery clock

At 10:00 a.m. Monday the listing broker receives one signed offer; at noon a second signed offer; the
seller is already under contract. The broker promptly sends both to the seller together rather than
ranking or holding either, records the receipt/presentation times, and delivers each executed offer
document to the proper customer/client no later than three days after receipt. An oral summary does not
replace document delivery. Compensation negotiations do not stop either clock.

### A.4 Advertising — Commission Rule 58A .0105

- A broker needs the BIC's consent to advertise brokerage services or another's real estate and names
  the affiliated firm or sole proprietorship in the advertisement.
- Advertising another's property requires the owner's or authorized agent's written consent.
- A blind ad is prohibited: the advertisement identifies that it is by a broker or firm and is not
  limited to contact information that makes it appear to be the principal's own offer.

### A.5 Record retention — Commission Rule 58A .0108

Transaction records are retained **three years from the later of transaction conclusion or disbursement
of all broker-held funds**. If agency ends before the transaction concludes, the trigger is the later of
agency termination or disbursement. The listed records include contracts, offers, leases, agency and
disclosure documents, trust records, closing statements, advertising and supporting square-footage
work. An affiliated broker provides covered transaction documents to the firm within three days of
receipt. This is not “three years from closing” in every case.

### A.6 Handling trust funds *(Level 2 except as noted)*

#### Definition of trust money — NCGS 93A-6(a)(12); Rule 58A .0116(a)

> **"Trust money: Money belonging to others received by a broker who is acting as an agent in a real
> estate transaction."**
>
> **"Includes money received as an escrow agent or temporary custodian of funds belonging to others."**
>
> **"Most common examples: earnest money deposits, down payments, tenant security deposits, and
> rents."**

#### Trust account basics *(Level 1)*

The Commission lists ten points with their citations. Stated here as the Syllabus states them, with the
citations preserved so a student can read the source:

1. **Basic required features** — NCGS 93A-6(g); Rule 58A .0117(b) and (f).
2. **Proper designation of the trust account and FDIC insurance** — Rule 58A .0117(b).
3. **When a trust account is required** — Rule 58A .0116(a): trust money held in a fiduciary capacity
   is deposited in a qualifying trust or escrow account unless a stated exception applies.
4. **Property owners' association account separation** — Rule 58A .0118(a): a dedicated account for
   one association, not commingled with another association's or person's funds, plus statements at
   least every 90 days.
5. **COMMINGLING OF FUNDS PROHIBITED** — NCGS 93A-6(a)(12).
6. **A limited, separately ledgered company-funds exception for bank service charges** — Rule 58A
   .0117(c)(4)(B). It is not permission to operate from client money or mix ordinary business funds.
7. **Requirements for an interest-bearing trust account** — NCGS 93A-6(a)(12); Rule 58A .0116(c).
8. **THE BROKER-IN-CHARGE IS RESPONSIBLE FOR THE TRUST ACCOUNT** — Rule 58A .0110(g)(4). **Instructor
   Note:** *"BIC remains responsible for trust monies and records **even if others are allowed to assist**
   with maintenance of the trust account."*
9. **Disbursement of earnest money to the settlement agent** — Rule 58A .0116(e).
10. **Handling disputed trust funds** — NCGS 93A-12; Rule 58A .0116(d).

#### Handling of trust money

**The basic deposit rule:** unless a paragraph (b) exception applies, Rule 58A .0116(a) requires trust
money to be deposited no later than **three banking days after receipt**. The current exceptions are:

- a **Provisional Broker delivers it upon receipt to the affiliated broker**;
- a **Limited Nonresident Commercial Broker delivers it to the affiliated resident NC broker** under
  Rule 58A .1808;
- **non-currency earnest money or tenant security deposits received with a pending offer** are
  deposited no later than three days after acceptance, whose date is stated in the agreement;
- **a check or other negotiable instrument payable to the seller for an option or due diligence fee**
  is delivered or returned under buyer instructions and cannot be retained more than three business
  days after contract acceptance;
- **a check or other negotiable instrument payable to a third party escrow agent in a sales transaction
  for trust monies.**

Before an interest-bearing deposit, all interested parties give written authorization that conspicuously
states how and to whom interest is disbursed. A disputed non-residential-security deposit stays in trust
until written release or court order, subject to Rule .0116(d)'s abandoned-claim route; alternatively,
G.S. 93A-12 permits clerk-of-court deposit after its notice and 90-day waiting procedure. Earnest money
may transfer to the settlement agent no more than 10 days before anticipated settlement; another
pre-settlement use requires written party consent. Rule .0116(g) separately prohibits conversion or
misapplication and requires safeguarding.

G.S. 93A-6(g) requires a demand deposit account in a federally insured depository institution lawfully
doing business in North Carolina that makes the records available to the Commission. Rule .0117
requires identifying records and monthly reconciliation. The current LLCR Comments and its detailed
ledger examples remain missing; they must be obtained and reviewed before submission, and this lesson
does not invent or reproduce them.

**Three things a provisional broker must nevertheless carry into their first week:**

- **Trust money is other people's money from the moment it reaches you.**
- **Commingling is prohibited by statute** — NCGS 93A-6(a)(12).
- **Your BIC is responsible for the trust account**, and remains responsible even where others assist —
  so **deliver trust money to the firm and follow the firm's procedure exactly.**

## B. Prohibited Practices

> **Required Activity — prescribed by the Commission:** *"**Compare and contrast duties owed to clients
> vs. third parties.** It is critical that licensees understand the difference in the standard for
> determining what information must be disclosed to a principal/client versus what must be disclosed to
> a third party/customer (such as a lender)."*
>
### B.0a Required Activity evidence — client versus third-party disclosure

| Information | Duty to the client | Duty to a third party/customer |
|---|---|---|
| property defect the broker knows or reasonably should know | disclose promptly; advise investigation/remedy options within competence | volunteer the material fact regardless of whom the broker represents |
| client's motivation or confidential negotiating limit | use for the client's benefit; do not disclose without authority unless law requires | do not disclose merely because it may help the customer |
| buyer's known inability to complete after financing collapses | advise the buyer-client candidly and route legal consequences to counsel | disclose the material ability-to-complete fact; do not help submit a misleading offer |
| lender or settlement-agent document the broker knows is inaccurate | tell the client and correct/route the error | deal honestly; do not transmit or remain silent about a known material falsehood |

The fiduciary layer owed to a client is broader, but the duty not to misrepresent and to disclose
material facts reaches transaction parties. Confidentiality does not authorize concealment of a fact
that law requires the broker to disclose.

### B.1 Misrepresentation and omission of material facts *(Level 3)*

**The disciplinary rule** — NCGS 93A-6(a)(1) permits discipline for any willful or negligent
misrepresentation or omission of material fact; subsection (a)(3) separately reaches a course of
misrepresentation or false promises. Rule 58A .0114(c) preserves the broker's independent duty for
covered residential property even when the owner uses a disclosure statement.

**Concepts: misrepresentation; omission; puffing; material facts.**

#### The four categories of material fact

Transcribed:

1. **Facts about the property itself.**
2. **Facts relating directly to the property.**
3. **Facts relating directly to the ABILITY OF THE PARTIES TO COMPLETE THE TRANSACTION.**
4. **Facts known to be of SPECIFIC IMPORTANCE TO A PARTY.**

**Category 3 is not about the building at all** — it reaches a buyer whose financing has collapsed or a
seller who cannot deliver title. **Category 4 is client-specific:** information identified as important
may create a fiduciary investigation/advice duty even where it is not a fact that must be volunteered to
every third party. Do not relabel confidential motivation as a universally disclosable material fact.

#### Items declared MATERIAL in North Carolina

Transcribed, and these three are examinable:

- **EIFS / synthetic stucco exterior siding — EVEN IF FULLY REPLACED.**
- **A known history of polybutylene leakage or probable defect — including a repaired history.**
- **A current or former "meth" house.**

**Read the remediation clauses.** Remediation does not erase known history. Current September 2025
Commission guidance also draws the other boundary: the **mere presence** of polybutylene piping is not
itself a material fact. A known leak, evidence of damage, probable defect, or relevant neighboring-unit
failure changes the analysis; a buyer broker should consider the client's insurer restrictions.

#### B.1a Level 3 evidence — material-fact classification lab

For each case, record category, known/should-have-known evidence, investigation step, recipient and
written disclosure: fully replaced EIFS; polybutylene present with no failure evidence; a documented
prior polybutylene leak; a radon result received after the first contract terminated; a buyer whose
loan approval was withdrawn; seller motivation; a nearby registered offender; and a direct question
about a prior occupant's death. The correct result cannot be produced by the shortcut “physical equals
material, stigma equals immaterial.”

#### Items declared NOT MATERIAL in North Carolina

Transcribed:

- **Reputation as a haunted property.**
- **Death or illness of a previous or current occupant.**

**Both of these are North Carolina positions and they differ from other states.** They also do not
license a licensee to lie: not material means **no duty to volunteer**, not permission to deny a direct
question falsely.

#### Discover and disclose

Four rules, transcribed:

1. **"Affirmative duty to discover and VOLUNTEER material facts to ALL parties to the transaction,
   REGARDLESS OF WHOM THE BROKER REPRESENTS."**
2. **"Broker cannot avoid responsibility for communicating incorrect and/or incomplete information BY
   ISSUING DISCLAIMERS."**
3. **"Broker has a duty to INVESTIGATE 'red flags' or questionable information."**
4. **"Broker's disclosure responsibilities under Real Estate License Law are NOT AFFECTED BY THE
   DOCTRINE OF CAVEAT EMPTOR; broker must still volunteer material facts to all transactional
   parties."**

**Rule 2 disposes of the "information deemed reliable but not guaranteed" footer as a defence.** Rule 4
disposes of "buyer beware." **Rule 3 is the affirmative one: a red flag creates a duty to investigate,
not merely to mention.**

### B.2 Discriminatory practices

Three current North Carolina controls operate together:

- **G.S. 93A-6(a)(10)** permits discipline for conduct the statute characterizes as improper,
  fraudulent or dishonest dealing.
- **Rule 58A .0120(d)** bars a broker from conducting brokerage or promoting broker status in a manner
  that discriminates because of race, color, religion, national origin, sex, familial status or
  disability.
- **Rule 58A .1601** treats a licensee's violation of the North Carolina State Fair Housing Act as
  improper conduct under G.S. 93A-6(a)(10).

Module NC-09 supplies the current federal framework. Module NC-15 supplies the separate current North
Carolina State Fair Housing Act analysis; its definitions, exemptions and procedures are not presumed
identical to federal law.

### B.3 Unauthorized practice of law — NCGS 93A-4(e); 93A-6(a)(11); Rule 58A .0111

G.S. 93A-4(e) grants no authority to practice law, and G.S. 93A-6(a)(11) makes unauthorized practice a
disciplinary ground. G.S. 84-2.1 and Rule 58A .0111 draw the operational boundary:

- Acting as agent in the transaction, a broker may select and complete a compliant **preprinted**
  offer, option, sales-contract or lease form when a party authorizes or directs completion.
- The broker may store, complete and print the allowed preprinted forms electronically but may not
  alter the preprinted form before presenting it to the parties.
- If the parties propose a deletion or change, the form is marked to show it. Rule .0111 does not let
  the broker independently invent, rewrite or recommend legal language; unique language goes to an
  attorney.
- A broker may make nonbinding notes, memoranda and correspondence that record negotiations.
- Preparing a custom contract, deed, deed of trust or other legal instrument for another, or advising
  what a legal document means, crosses the boundary.

The safe question is not simply “am I filling a blank?” It is whether the broker is completing an
authorized preprinted transaction form under the rule or independently creating or interpreting legal
rights.

### B.4 Improper brokerage commissions *(Level 3)* — Rule 58A .0109

**a. Affiliated brokers.** Rule 58A .0120(b) permits direct payment of a commission or referral fee
only by the broker's **current BIC** or the person who **served as BIC at the time of the transaction**.
That rule applies to affiliated provisional and nonprovisional brokers; “the supervising BIC” alone is
an incomplete statement because the current-BIC alternative also matters.

**b. Ordinary referral or commission to a broker licensed only in another state.** The Commission's
Syllabus and official compensation guidance state the ordinary condition:

- **the out-of-state (foreign) broker holds an ACTIVE real estate licence in the state from which they
  are conducting the brokerage activity**, such as placing a referral; **and**
- **the foreign broker DOES NOT ENTER NC at any time during the transaction to engage in any brokerage
  activity related to the transaction.**

Do not confuse that ordinary out-of-state referral with a **Limited Nonresident Commercial Broker**.
G.S. 93A-9(b) creates a separate North Carolina limited-license route for commercial transactions with
resident-broker affiliation and supervision; Rule .1808 requires that limited licensee to deliver all
trust money to the affiliated resident broker. A referral fact pattern does not silently create that
license.

**c. Payment is prohibited to a broker with an expired or inactive licence, unless:**

- **the licence was on active status when the brokerage activity was performed**; and
- **the broker stopped all brokerage activity when the licence expired or became inactive.**

**Instructor Note, transcribed:** *"If the licences of the firm and the individual broker were on active
status **throughout the period during which brokerage services were rendered**, then the firm may be
paid and the firm may in turn pay the individual broker, **even if their licence is not active at the
time of payment**. **Payment is FORFEITED if EITHER of the licences were not active at ANY time that
brokerage services were being performed.**"*

*(Module NC-02 B.6 states the same rule. It appears in two sections of the Syllabus because it is
tested from both.)*

#### B.4a Level 3 evidence — compensation routing lab

Classify each proposed payment before anyone promises it: a buyer rebate stated in the agency
agreement; a title-company gift card; direct payment from a closing attorney to an affiliated broker;
payment by the broker's former BIC for that BIC's transaction; a referral to an active Virginia broker
who never enters North Carolina; a Virginia broker who conducts a North Carolina showing; and work
performed partly while the North Carolina license was inactive. Record the license/affiliation status,
who may pay whom, required principal disclosure or consent, and the source that controls. Do not treat
“paid after closing” as a cure for unlicensed activity.

### B.5 Disclosure of offers prohibited — Commission Rule 58A .0115

Keep the authority layers separate:

- **Rule 58A .0115 itself** prohibits disclosing the **price or other material terms** in one party's
  offer to a competing party without the **express authority of the offering party**. That is the
  enforceable “do not shop the offer” rule.
- Current Commission multiple-offer guidance says the **existence of multiple offers is not a material
  fact** and need not be volunteered. A listing broker may disclose their existence with the seller's
  consent and, once doing so, should share that information fairly, equally and honestly.
- Seller consent to announce that multiple offers exist is **not** the competing offeror's authority to
  reveal price, escalation terms, due diligence fee, closing date or another material term.
- A broker must not answer a direct question falsely. Follow the seller's disclosure direction while
  responding honestly; if not authorized to reveal the existence of offers, do not invent “no.”

The practical test has two gates: **seller authority for the existence message; offering-party express
authority for the offer's price or other material terms.**

### B.6 Failure to deliver settlement statements to a client — NCGS 93A-6(a)(14)

- **A broker may be subject to disciplinary action for failing to deliver a detailed and accurate
  settlement statement to their client at closing.**
- **A broker MAY rely on the settlement agent who prepared the statement for its delivery — BUT the
  broker MUST REVIEW THE STATEMENT FOR ACCURACY and NOTIFY ALL PARTIES TO THE CLOSING OF ANY ERRORS.**

**The review is the broker's, and it is not discretionary.** The settlement agent prepares and delivers;
**you check it and you speak up.**

### B.7 Commingling

Covered at A.6 above: **prohibited by NCGS 93A-6(a)(12)**, with the single limited exception at Rule 58A
.0117(c)(4)(B) permitting funds to cover bank service charges on the trust account.
