# Washington Real Estate Practices — Module WA-P-04

## Compensation — RCW 18.86.080

**Course:** Real Estate Practices · **Instructional hours: 5.0** of 30.0 · **Clock hour = 50 minutes**
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No Washington clock hours are available.

> **No commission rate, split, minimum or customary figure appears anywhere in this course.** Compensation is
> negotiated between a firm and its principal, and **RCW 18.86.080(7)(a)(i)** requires the services agreement
> to state **the amount the principal agrees to compensate the firm.** Every worked figure in this module is
> supplied inside its problem.

## Learning objectives

1. State who may pay a firm's compensation.
2. State the rule that compensation does not create agency, and apply it.
3. Recite what RCW 18.86.080(7) requires in a services agreement before a firm may be compensated.
4. Explain the significance of RCW 18.86.080(7)(b).
5. State the commercial real estate alternative and its heading.
6. State the two things a firm may be compensated for without a services agreement.
7. Compute compensation, splits and net figures from given rates.

## A. Who may pay — RCW 18.86.080(1), (3), (4), (5), (6)

> **(1)** *"In any real estate transaction, a firm's compensation **may be paid by the seller, the buyer, a
> third party, or by sharing the compensation between firms**."*
> **(3)** *"A **seller may agree** that a seller's agent's firm **may share with another firm** the compensation
> paid by the seller."*
> **(4)** *"A **buyer may agree** that a buyer's agent's firm **may share with another firm** the compensation
> paid by the buyer."*
> **(5)** *"A firm **may be compensated by more than one party** for real estate brokerage services in a real
> estate transaction."*
> **(6)** *"A firm **may receive compensation based on the purchase price without breaching any duty** to the
> buyer or seller."*

### A.1 Four permissions worth naming

**Anyone may pay.** Seller, buyer, a third party, or firms sharing. Washington does not prescribe a source.

**Sharing requires the paying principal's agreement** — (3) and (4) both say *"may agree."* And (7)(a)(ii)
below makes that agreement a required **term of the services agreement**, not an informal understanding.

**More than one party may compensate the same firm** — (5). That is permitted, and (7)(a)(iii) requires the
principal's consent to it as a stated term.

**Percentage compensation does not breach a duty** — (6). A buyer's agent paid on a percentage of the purchase
price is paid more when the buyer pays more, and Washington states expressly that this alone is not a breach.
**It is still, however, a fact the buyer is entitled to have in the agreement**, because (7)(a)(i) requires
**the amount.**

## B. The rule everyone misremembers — RCW 18.86.080(2)

> *"**An agreement to pay or payment of compensation does not establish an agency relationship between the
> party who paid the compensation and the broker.**"*

**Money moves one way; representation is decided another way.**

**Work the case that arises constantly.** A seller's firm offers to share compensation with the buyer's firm,
and the buyer's firm accepts. Who does the buyer's broker represent?

**The buyer** — under **RCW 18.86.020(1)**, a broker performing services for a buyer **is** the buyer's agent
unless one of three exceptions applies, and "being paid by the seller's firm" is not one of them.
**RCW 18.86.080(2)** says the payment does not change it.

**And this is not merely a technical point.** A buyer who hears "the seller pays my commission" may conclude
their broker works for the seller. **Correct it**, and correct it in the direction the statute states: *"I
represent you. Where my firm's compensation comes from is set out in our services agreement, and it does not
change who I work for."*

*(RESPA's referral fee prohibition is a **different** question, addressed in outline in Module WA-F-07. **RCW
18.86.080(2) is about AGENCY**, not about the lawfulness of a payment.)*

## C. The requirement that governs practice — RCW 18.86.080(7)

> *"**To receive compensation for rendering real estate brokerage services from any party or firm, a real
> estate firm MUST HAVE A SERVICES AGREEMENT** containing the following:*
>
> **(a)** *"The **terms of compensation**, including:*
> **(i)** *"**The amount the principal agrees to compensate the firm**;*
> **(ii)** *"The **principal's consent, if any, and any terms of such consent, to compensation sharing between
> firms and parties**; and*
> **(iii)** *"The **principal's consent, if any, and any terms of such consent, to compensation of the firm by
> more than one party**;*
>
> **(b)** *"**In a services agreement with a buyer, whether the appointed broker agrees to show the buyer
> properties if there is no agreement or offer by any party or firm to pay compensation to the firm**; and*
>
> **(c)** *"**Any other agreements between the parties.**"*

### C.1 "Must have" is the operative phrase

**No services agreement, no compensation.** Not "should have," not "ordinarily has." **A firm that renders
brokerage services without a services agreement containing these terms has no statutory basis to be paid for
them.**

**And it reaches compensation "from ANY PARTY OR FIRM"** — including a share offered by the other side's firm.
A buyer's firm cannot rely on a listing firm's offer of compensation in place of its own services agreement
with the buyer.

### C.2 Subsection (7)(b) — the newest and most practical line in the section

> *"In a services agreement with a buyer, **whether the appointed broker agrees to show the buyer properties if
> there is no agreement or offer by any party or firm to pay compensation to the firm**."*

**Washington requires the buyer's services agreement to answer this question in advance, in writing.**

**Why it exists.** A buyer may want to see a property whose seller is not offering to compensate the buyer's
firm. **Whether the broker will show it, and on what basis, is settled at the front of the relationship rather
than in the middle of a house hunt.**

**What it means for a conversation with a buyer.** The broker explains **before** anything is signed: *"If a
seller isn't offering to pay my firm, here is what happens — either you compensate my firm under our agreement,
or we agree now that I won't show those properties. The agreement has to say which."*

**A broker who leaves that blank has left blank a term the statute requires**, and has also set up the
transaction's most predictable argument.

### C.3 (a)(ii) and (a)(iii) — "if any" is not "optional"

Both subsections read *"the principal's consent, **if any**, and any terms of such consent."*

**The consent is optional; addressing it is not.** If the principal consents to sharing, the agreement states
the consent and its terms. If the principal does **not** consent, **the firm may not share.** Either way the
question is answered in the document.

## D. The commercial exception — RCW 18.86.080(8)

> *"**In lieu of obtaining a services agreement**, a broker rendering real estate brokerage services to a buyer
> **solely for commercial real estate** may **disclose in writing to the buyer, before the buyer signs an offer**
> with regard to such commercial real estate, **the sources and amounts of any compensation the broker has or
> expects to receive from any party** in conjunction with such transaction. **The disclosure shall be set forth
> in a separate paragraph titled 'Compensation Disclosure' in the agreement between the buyer and seller or in a
> separate writing titled 'Compensation Disclosure.'**"*

### D.1 Four conditions, all required

1. **A buyer**, not a seller.
2. **Solely for commercial real estate.**
3. **In writing, before the buyer signs an offer.**
4. **Sources AND amounts** of any compensation from any party.
5. **In a separate paragraph titled "Compensation Disclosure,"** or a separate writing so titled.

**The heading is prescribed**, exactly as **RCW 18.86.030(1)(g)** prescribes **"Agency Disclosure."**
Washington names its headings, and a disclosure under a different heading has not complied.

**And note the pairing with RCW 18.86.020(3)**, from Module WA-P-02: a services agreement is **not required**
for a buyer's agent **solely for commercial real estate**. **Subsection (8) is what fills the gap** — the
compensation disclosure replaces the compensation terms the services agreement would have carried.

## E. The two things that need no services agreement — RCW 18.86.080(9)

> *"A firm may receive compensation **without a services agreement** for:*
> - *the provision of a **broker's price opinion**, as defined in RCW 18.85.011, **or***
> - *a **referral by one firm to another firm IF the referring firm provided no real estate brokerage services
>   in the transaction**."*

**Two exceptions, and the second has a condition attached.** A referral fee is outside the services agreement
requirement **only if the referring firm did no brokerage services.** A firm that showed property, advised, or
negotiated and then "referred" has rendered services, and (7) applies.

**RCW 18.85.011's definition of a broker's price opinion was NOT read for this project.** **This course states
no requirement about what a BPO must contain, who may prepare one, or what it may be used for.** Those are
questions for RCW 18.85 and WAC 308-124 in their own text.

## F. The disclosure that sits inside the agency disclosure

**RCW 18.86.030(1)(g)(ii)** — owed to **all parties**, **unwaivable**:

> *"To **disclose in writing** before the broker's principal signs an offer ... **any terms of compensation
> offered by a party or a real estate firm to a real estate firm representing another party**."*

**Read it carefully, because it is easy to invert.** The broker discloses, **to their own principal**, the
**compensation terms offered to the OTHER side's firm.**

**Why it matters.** A seller is entitled to know what is being offered to the buyer's firm, because the seller
is usually funding it. A buyer is entitled to know what a listing firm is offering their firm, because it bears
on their own compensation obligation under (7)(b).

**It goes in the "Agency Disclosure" paragraph**, before the principal signs an offer.

## G. Working the numbers, every rate given

### G.1 A share between firms

*Sale price $742,000; the seller's services agreement provides 5 percent to the listing firm; the seller has
consented in the agreement to sharing, on terms of 2.25 percent of the price to the buyer's firm.*

Total from seller = 742,000 × 0.05 = **$37,100.**
To the buyer's firm = 742,000 × 0.0225 = **$16,695.**
Retained by the listing firm = 37,100 − 16,695 = **$20,405.**

*(Note the structure: the consent and its terms are in the services agreement, as RCW 18.86.080(7)(a)(ii)
requires.)*

### G.2 Where the seller offers nothing to the buyer's firm

*The same buyer's services agreement provides that the buyer compensates the buyer's firm 2.5 percent of the
purchase price, less any amount received from any other party. The seller offers nothing. The purchase price
is $610,000.*

Owed to the buyer's firm = 610,000 × 0.025 = **$15,250.**
Received from other parties = **$0.**
**Payable by the buyer = $15,250.**

**Now the same agreement with a seller offering 2 percent:**
Received from the seller's firm = 610,000 × 0.02 = **$12,200.**
**Payable by the buyer = 15,250 − 12,200 = $3,050.**

**This is exactly the situation RCW 18.86.080(7)(b) requires the agreement to have addressed in advance** —
and it is why the buyer must understand their own obligation before they start looking, not after they find a
house.

### G.3 Net to seller

*Price $742,000; compensation as in G.1 ($37,100 total); excise tax at a combined 1.78 percent given in this
problem; payoff $388,400; other seller charges $2,650.*

Excise tax = 742,000 × 0.0178 = **$13,207.60.**
Net = 742,000 − 37,100 − 13,207.60 − 388,400 − 2,650 = **$300,642.40.**

## H. The section as a checklist

1. **Anyone may pay** — seller, buyer, third party, or shared. *(1)*
2. **Payment never creates agency.** *(2)*
3. **No services agreement, no compensation** — and it must contain **the amount**, **consent to sharing**,
   **consent to compensation by more than one party**, **the buyer's show-without-compensation term**, and
   **any other agreements.** *(7)*
4. **Commercial buyer's agency** may substitute a **"Compensation Disclosure"** before the buyer signs an
   offer. *(8)*
5. **A BPO and a pure referral** need no services agreement. *(9)*
6. **Disclose to your principal what the other side's firm is being offered**, in the **"Agency Disclosure"**
   paragraph, before they sign an offer. *(RCW 18.86.030(1)(g)(ii))*
