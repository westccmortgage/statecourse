## Limited dual agency, duration, imputed knowledge and vicarious liability

## Learning objectives

2. State the limited dual agent's duties, and identify what is absent from the list.
3. Apply RCW 18.86.060(5) — what happens when two brokers in one firm represent the two sides.
4. State the four ways an agency relationship ends and the two duties that survive.
5. Apply RCW 18.86.100 — Washington's reversal of imputed knowledge.
6. Apply RCW 18.86.090 — when a principal is liable for an agent's act.

## A. Limited dual agency — RCW 18.86.060

### A.1 The entry condition

> **(1)** *"A broker may act as a limited dual agent **only with the written consent of both parties to the
> transaction, SET FORTH IN THE SERVICES AGREEMENT**."*

**Two requirements, and the second is specific.** Written consent from **both** parties, and it must be **set
forth in the services agreement** — not in a separate form produced at the last moment.

**And Module WA-P-02 supplied the mechanics.** **RCW 18.86.020(2)(b)(iv)** requires that consent to be
**separately initialed by the principal** and to include **an acknowledgment** in the statute's own words:

> *"**a limited dual agent may not advocate terms favorable to one principal to the detriment of the other
> principal**."*

**The principal must acknowledge, in writing they separately initial, what they are giving up.**

### A.2 The duties — and the one that is missing

> **(2)** *Unless additional duties are agreed to in writing signed by the limited dual agent, the duties are
> limited to those in RCW 18.86.030 and the following, **which may not be waived except as expressly set forth
> in (e) and (f)**:*
>
> **(a)** *"To take **no action that is adverse or detrimental to EITHER party's interest** in a transaction;*
> **(b)** *"To **timely disclose to BOTH parties any conflicts of interest**;*
> **(c)** *"To **advise BOTH parties to seek expert advice** on matters relating to the transaction that are
> beyond the limited dual agent's expertise;*
> **(d)** *"To **not disclose any confidential information from or about EITHER party, except under subpoena
> or court order, even after termination** of the agency relationship;*
> **(e)** and **(f)** *the good faith and continuous effort duties, **for both sides**, each subject to the
> same existing-contract exception.*

**Compare with RCW 18.86.040(1)(a) and 18.86.050(1)(a).**

| | Seller's agent | Buyer's agent | **Limited dual agent** |
|---|---|---|---|
| First duty | *"To be **loyal** to the seller by taking no action adverse or detrimental..."* | *"To be **loyal** to the buyer by taking no action adverse or detrimental..."* | *"To take **no action that is adverse or detrimental to either party's interest**"* |

**The word "loyal" does not appear in RCW 18.86.060.** In its place stands the neutrality obligation alone.

**That substitution is the entire concept, and it is why the statute makes the principal acknowledge it.** A
limited dual agent **cannot advocate for one side against the other**, so the statute does not ask for a
loyalty it could not deliver. What it asks for is **neutrality that runs both ways.**

**Note also (d).** The confidentiality duty now runs to **either party**, with the same two exceptions —
**subpoena or court order** — and it **survives termination.** A limited dual agent holds two sets of
confidences and may use neither.

### A.3 The safe harbours — subsections (3) and (4)

Showing properties not owned by the seller, listing competing properties, showing the buyer's property of
interest to other buyers, and **presenting additional offers while the property is subject to a transaction**
do **not in and of themselves** constitute action adverse or detrimental. Nor does **representation of more
than one seller, or more than one buyer, by different brokers licensed to the same firm** in competing
transactions.

**"In and of itself" is the operative phrase, as it was in Module WA-P-02.** The structural fact is
protected; **conduct is not.**

## B. RCW 18.86.060(5) — the provision that catches every firm

> *"In a transaction in which **different brokers affiliated with the same firm represent different parties**,
> the firm's **designated broker, and any managing broker responsible for the supervision of both brokers, IS
> A LIMITED DUAL AGENT**. In such case, **each appointed broker shall SOLELY REPRESENT the party with whom the
> appointed broker has an agency relationship**."*

### B.1 What it actually does

**Two brokers in the same firm, one on each side. The statute then assigns three positions at once:**

- **The listing broker** — solely represents the seller.
- **The selling broker** — solely represents the buyer.
- **The designated broker, and any managing broker supervising both** — **limited dual agents**, by operation
  of the statute.

**Nobody chose that last position; the statute assigns it.** And it is why **RCW 18.86.020(2)(b)(v)** requires
a **second, separate consent** in the services agreement: consent to the **designated broker and managing
broker** acting as limited dual agents in exactly this situation.

**A broker who obtains the (iv) consent and not the (v) consent has obtained consent to the wrong thing** for
an in-house transaction.

### B.2 And "solely represent" is a real instruction

Each appointed broker **solely represents** their own party. The in-house transaction does **not** convert the
two brokers into dual agents. **The dual agency sits above them, at the supervisory level**, and the two
brokers keep their own loyalty obligations under RCW 18.86.040 and .050.

**The practical burden this creates is on the supervisor.** A designated broker who is a limited dual agent
must supervise both sides while **taking no action adverse or detrimental to either.** Discussing one side's
position with the other broker is exactly what that forbids.

## C. Duration — RCW 18.86.070

> **(1)** *"The agency relationships established pursuant to this chapter **continue until the earliest of the
> following**:*
> **(a)** *"**Completion of performance** by the broker;*
> **(b)** *"**Expiration of the term** agreed upon by the parties;*
> **(c)** *"**Termination of the relationship by mutual agreement** of the parties; or*
> **(d)** *"**Termination of the relationship by notice from either party to the other**. **However, such a
> termination does not otherwise affect the contractual rights of either party.**"*

### C.1 (d) and its qualifier

**Either party may end the relationship by notice.** The relationship is not a cage.

**But: *"such a termination does not otherwise affect the contractual rights of either party."*** Ending the
**agency relationship** does not end the **contract**. A seller who gives notice terminating the relationship
may still owe compensation under the services agreement's own terms — and that is a **contract** question, not
an agency question.

**The distinction between the POWER to terminate and the RIGHT to terminate without consequence is the
distinction that provision draws.**

### C.2 What survives — RCW 18.86.070(2)

> *"Except as otherwise agreed to in writing, a broker **owes no further duty after termination** of the agency
> relationship, **other than the duty**:*
> **(a)** *"To **account for all moneys and property received during the relationship**; and*
> **(b)** *"To **not disclose confidential information**."*

**Exactly two duties survive.** Everything else in RCW 18.86.030, .040, .050 and .060 stops.

**And (b) is consistent with the confidentiality duty's own words** — RCW 18.86.040(1)(d), .050(1)(d) and
.060(2)(d) each say *"**even after termination of the agency relationship**."* The statute says it twice
because it matters: **a former client's confidences are still confidences.**

## D. Imputed knowledge — RCW 18.86.100

> *"Unless otherwise agreed to in writing, **a principal does not have knowledge or notice of any facts known
> by an agent of the principal that are not actually known by the principal**."*

### D.1 Washington reversed the common-law rule

**Under the general common law of agency, an agent's knowledge acquired within the scope of the agency is
imputed to the principal.** Washington provides the **opposite** by statute.

**Work the consequence.** A listing broker learns something material about the property and does not tell the
seller. Later a buyer alleges the seller concealed it.

- **Under the common law**, the seller could be charged with the broker's knowledge.
- **Under RCW 18.86.100**, the seller **does not** have knowledge or notice of a fact the broker knew and the
  seller did not **actually** know.

**The seller is not charged with what their broker knew and did not tell them.**

**This does not help the broker.** **RCW 18.86.030(1)(d)** obliges the **broker** to disclose known material
facts to **all parties**, and **RCW 18.86.031** makes a violation of RCW 18.86.030 a violation of the licence
law. **The statute protects the principal from the broker's silence; it does not protect the broker.**

**And note "unless otherwise agreed to in writing."** The parties can contract into imputation. Almost nobody
does, but the door is open.

## E. Vicarious liability — RCW 18.86.090

> *"A principal is **not liable** for an act, error, or omission by an agent of the principal arising out of an
> agency relationship:*
> **(1)** *"**Unless the principal participated in or authorized** the act, error, or omission; or*
> **(2)** *"**Except to the extent that**: **(a)** the principal **benefited** from the act, error, or
> omission; **and (b)** the court determines that it is **highly probable that the claimant would be unable to
> enforce a judgment against the agent**."*

### E.1 The default and its two exits

**The default is no liability.** A principal is **not** answerable for their agent's acts, errors or omissions.

**Exit one — participation or authorisation.** If the principal **participated in or authorized** it, the
protection is gone. A seller who instructed the broker to conceal a defect participated.

**Exit two has TWO cumulative conditions, and both must be met:**

**(a)** the principal **benefited** from the act, **AND**
**(b)** a court determines it is **highly probable that the claimant would be unable to enforce a judgment
against the agent.**

**"Highly probable" is a demanding standard**, and it makes exit two a genuine last resort — available where
the agent is judgment-proof and the principal kept the benefit. **Benefit alone is not enough.** **Inability
to collect alone is not enough.**

### E.2 Why a broker should understand a rule about the principal's liability

Three reasons.

**It explains the structure of Washington brokerage.** RCW 18.86 makes the **broker** answerable for the
broker's conduct — through RCW 18.86.031 and the licence law — rather than routing liability to the client.

**It is the counterpart to RCW 18.86.100.** Together they say: **the principal is not charged with the
broker's knowledge, and is generally not liable for the broker's acts.** Washington places both the knowledge
and the responsibility where the conduct was.

**It answers a client's question.** A seller who asks *"am I on the hook for what you do?"* is asking about
RCW 18.86.090, and the honest answer is: **generally not, with two narrow exceptions — and you should ask your
attorney about your own situation**, which **RCW 18.86.040(1)(c)** makes a duty.

## F. The four provisions, as one picture

| Provision | What it settles |
|---|---|
| **RCW 18.86.060** | A limited dual agent owes **neutrality, not loyalty** — with both parties' **written consent in the services agreement, separately initialed.** |
| **RCW 18.86.060(5)** | Two brokers, one firm, two sides → each **solely represents** their party; the **designated and managing brokers are limited dual agents** by operation of statute. |
| **RCW 18.86.070** | The relationship ends four ways; **termination does not affect contractual rights**; **two duties survive** — accounting and confidentiality. |
| **RCW 18.86.100** | The principal **is not charged with the broker's knowledge.** |
| **RCW 18.86.090** | The principal is **generally not liable** for the broker's acts — unless they participated or authorised, or benefited **and** the agent is effectively judgment-proof. |
