## A. RCW 18.86.130 — Exclusive real estate property marketing

**Enacted in 2026 and current Washington law**, transcribed in full:

> *"**A broker may not market the sale or lease of residential real estate to a limited or exclusive group of
> prospective buyers or brokers, or any combination thereof, unless the real estate is concurrently marketed
> to the general public and all other brokers**, except as reasonably necessary to protect the health or
> safety of the owner or occupant. **Marketing to the general public does not require an owner to allow
> access onto the residential real estate or into the residence.**"*

### A.1 Take it apart

**What is prohibited:** marketing **the sale or lease** of **residential** real estate **to a limited or
exclusive group** of prospective buyers **or brokers, or any combination.**

**The condition that makes it lawful:** the property is **concurrently marketed to the general public AND all
other brokers.** Both. Not one or the other, and **concurrently** — not afterwards.

**The exception:** *"except as **reasonably necessary to protect the health or safety of the owner or
occupant**."* **Health or safety.** Not convenience, not privacy in the ordinary sense, not the seller's
preference for a quiet sale.

**And the final sentence forecloses the obvious objection:** *"**Marketing to the general public does not
require an owner to allow access** onto the residential real estate or into the residence."*

**So an owner who does not want strangers walking through their home can still comply.** Marketing publicly
and controlling access are different things, and the statute says so expressly. A broker who tells a seller
"we can't market it publicly because you don't want showings" has misstated the statute.

### A.2 Scope

**Residential** real estate. The section does not reach commercial marketing.

**Sale OR lease.** It reaches rentals as well as sales.

**A limited or exclusive group of prospective buyers OR BROKERS.** Marketing exclusively within a brokerage,
a network, a private group or an invitation-only channel is within the prohibition unless the concurrent
public marketing condition is met.

### A.3 The consequence

**RCW 18.86.031:** *"A violation of either **RCW 18.86.030 or 18.86.130** is a violation of RCW 18.85.361."*

**RCW 18.86.130 is one of only two provisions carrying that consequence** — the other being the core duties in
RCW 18.86.030. **A violation is a licence-law violation.**

**RCW 18.85.361 was not read for this project**, and **no penalty, sanction or procedure is stated in this
build.**

### A.4 What a broker actually does with it

**Have the conversation before the listing, not after.** A seller who wants a "quiet" or "pocket" listing is
asking for something RCW 18.86.130 constrains, and the time to explain that is at the listing appointment.

**Separate the two things the seller usually wants.** They almost always want **controlled access**, not
**concealed marketing** — and the statute's last sentence gives them the first without the second.

**Document the health or safety exception if it applies**, and treat it as narrow. It is written to protect
people, not transactions.

## B. Marketing and the duty to make an effort

**RCW 18.86.040(1)(e)** — the seller's agent's duty:

> *"Unless otherwise agreed to in writing **after the seller's agent has complied with RCW 18.86.030(1)(f)**,
> to make a **good faith and continuous effort to find a buyer for the property**; **except that a seller's
> agent is not obligated to seek additional offers to purchase the property while the property is subject to
> an existing contract for sale**."*

### B.1 Three features

**"Good faith and continuous effort."** Not best efforts, not a specified activity level — but **continuous**,
which means marketing that stops is a problem.

**It is the ONE duty in RCW 18.86.040(1) that may be waived** — and only *"in writing **after** the agent has
complied with RCW 18.86.030(1)(f)."* **After the pamphlet has been provided and acknowledged.** The statute
sequences it deliberately: the seller must have the pamphlet in hand before they can agree to relieve the
broker of the effort duty.

**The existing-contract exception is narrow.** A broker is **not obligated to seek additional offers** while
the property is under contract. **That is a relief from an obligation to SEEK — it is not permission to stop
presenting.** **RCW 18.86.030(1)(c)** requires presentation of all written offers **"regardless of whether the
property is subject to an existing contract for sale."**

**Read the two together and the line is exact: you need not go looking, and you must still present whatever
arrives.**

**RCW 18.86.050(1)(e)** states the mirror rule for a buyer's agent — a continuous effort to find a property,
and no obligation to seek additional properties while the buyer is under contract.

## C. The RCW 18.86.030 duties, applied to marketing

Marketing is where several of the seven bite hardest.

**(a) Reasonable skill and care.** Square footage, lot size, bedroom count, year built, school district and
zoning are all **assertions**, and a broker who publishes one carelessly has published a misstatement.
**Verify from a source, or attribute it, or leave it out.**

**(b) Deal honestly and in good faith** — owed to **all parties**, unwaivable. **This reaches every person who
reads the advertisement**, not merely the parties to a transaction.

**(d) Disclose known material facts** not apparent or readily ascertainable. **A listing that omits a known
material fact is a communication, and the duty does not switch off because the medium is marketing.** The
proviso still applies: no duty to investigate what the broker did not agree to investigate.

**And the material fact negative list from RCW 18.86.010(11) applies here too.** Deaths, crimes, drug activity
and **political or religious activity** *"not adversely affecting the physical condition of or title to the
property"* are **not material facts.** A broker neither volunteers them **nor lies about them** — duty (b)
being unwaivable.

## D. Fair housing in marketing

Module WA-F-09 delivered the substance in the statutory three-hour block. **This is the practice application,
and it is a discipline rather than a rule list.**

### D.1 Describe the property, not the people

| Property | People |
|---|---|
| *"Three bedrooms, level lot, quiet street"* | *"Perfect for a young family"* |
| *"Ground-floor unit, no steps"* | *"Great for seniors"* |
| *"Walking distance to the light rail"* | *"Ideal for professionals"* |
| *"Zoned for the district's published school assignments"* | *"Good schools"* |

**The right column names or implies a characteristic.** Some of the phrases are apparently benign, and that is
precisely why the discipline is a habit rather than a checklist: **describe what is there, not who should
want it.**

### D.2 The advertisement is more than the words

**Photographs. Captions. Hashtags. The audience an advertisement is targeted to. The account it is posted
from. The group it is shared in.** All of it is the advertisement.

**Targeting is the modern form of the oldest problem.** An advertisement shown to some people and not others,
on characteristics or on proxies for them, is a distribution decision — and distribution decisions are exactly
what fair housing law has always reached.

### D.3 Route the objective questions

**School performance, crime statistics, demographics, commute times, flood history and walkability are all
published.** **Send the consumer to the source.** A broker who summarises them has (a) risked steering and (b)
made an assertion under duty (a) and duty (b).

*(No protected class list, exemption or advertising rule is stated in this module. RCW 49.60, the federal Fair
Housing Act and WAC 308-124 were not read for this project — Module WA-F-09 records the same gap.)*

## E. What a broker routes rather than asserts in marketing

- **Square footage and lot dimensions** → the county assessor's record with attribution, a survey, or an
  appraisal. **Never your own estimate published as fact.**
- **Bedroom count where the septic permit governs it** → the county health department.
- **Zoning and permitted uses** → the jurisdiction.
- **Whether an addition or outbuilding was permitted** → the jurisdiction's permit record.
- **School assignment** → the district, which can change boundaries.
- **Flood, landslide, seismic and shoreline designations** → the published mapping and the jurisdiction.
- **Whether the property will appraise** → nobody. Do not say it.
- **What the market will do** → nobody. Do not say it.

**RCW 18.86.040(1)(c) and 18.86.050(1)(c) make the referral a statutory duty**, and duty (b) is what an
unsupported assertion will be measured against.

## F. The listing sequence

1. **Pamphlet, and acknowledgment** — RCW 18.86.030(1)(f).
2. **Services agreement between the FIRM and the seller**, containing all five RCW 18.86.020(2)(b) items,
   with dual agency consent **separately initialed**, and the compensation terms RCW 18.86.080(7) requires.
3. **The RCW 18.86.130 conversation** — public and all-broker marketing, concurrently, unless health or
   safety reasonably requires otherwise; and **access control is a separate question.**
4. **Verify what you are about to publish**, or attribute it, or omit it.
5. **Market continuously and in good faith** — RCW 18.86.040(1)(e).
6. **Present every written offer, regardless** — RCW 18.86.030(1)(c).
7. **Disclose your role** in the paragraph titled **"Agency Disclosure,"** with the compensation terms offered
   to the other side's firm — RCW 18.86.030(1)(g).
