7. State the seller's agent's and buyer's agent's duties and their two safe harbours.

## A. How the relationships arise — RCW 18.86.020(1)

> *"A broker who performs real estate brokerage services for a buyer **is a buyer's agent unless**:*
> **(a)** *the broker's firm has **appointed the broker to represent the seller** pursuant to a services
> agreement between the firm and the seller, in which case the broker is a **seller's agent**;*
> **(b)** *the firm has appointed the broker to represent the seller **and** has also appointed the broker to
> represent the buyer, in which case the appointed broker is a **limited dual agent**; or*
> **(c)** *the **broker is the seller or one of the sellers**."*

### A.1 The asymmetry, and why it matters

**Buyer agency arises by DEFAULT, from performing services.** A broker who works with a buyer **is** the
buyer's agent unless one of the three exceptions applies. **Nothing needs to be signed for the relationship
to exist.**

**Seller agency requires an APPOINTMENT under a services agreement between the firm and the seller.**

**The practical consequence is one a new broker gets wrong constantly.** A broker showing property to a buyer
who has signed nothing **is already that buyer's agent** and already owes the duties in RCW 18.86.050 —
loyalty, timely disclosure of conflicts, the duty to advise seeking expert advice, and the confidentiality
duty. **The signature documents the relationship; it does not create it.**

**And note (c).** A broker selling their own property is not the buyer's agent by default, because they are on
the other side.

### A.2 The firm is the party — RCW 18.86.020(2)(a)

> *"**A firm must enter into a services agreement with the principal before, or as soon as reasonably
> practical after, its appointed broker commences rendering real estate brokerage services** to, or on behalf
> of, the principal."*

**Read who does what.** The **firm** enters the agreement. The **firm appoints** a broker as agent. The
individual broker is the agent by appointment.

**And the timing has two halves:** *"before, **or as soon as reasonably practical after**"* the broker
**commences rendering services.** Washington does not require the paper first — it requires the paper
promptly, and it makes the relationship exist regardless.

## B. What the services agreement must contain — RCW 18.86.020(2)(b)

> *"The services agreement **must include** the following:*
>
> **(i)** *"The **term of the agreement**, and **if the principal is a buyer, a default term of 60 days with
> the option of a longer term**;*
> **(ii)** *"The **broker appointed as an agent** for the principal;*
> **(iii)** *"Whether the agency relationship is **exclusive or nonexclusive**, and **if the principal is a
> buyer, checkbox options for the buyer to select either** an exclusive or nonexclusive relationship;*
> **(iv)** *"Whether the principal **consents to the broker appointed as an agent for the principal to act as
> a limited dual agent**, **which consent must be separately initialed by the principal and include an
> acknowledgment from the principal that a limited dual agent may not advocate terms favorable to one
> principal to the detriment of the other principal** and is further limited as set forth in RCW 18.86.060;
> and*
> **(v)** *"Whether the principal **consents to the firm's designated broker and any managing broker
> responsible for the supervision of the broker** appointed as an agent for the principal **to act as a
> limited dual agent** in a transaction in which different brokers affiliated with the same firm represent
> different parties."*

### B.1 Four features worth stopping on

**(i) — the 60-day buyer default.** A buyer's services agreement has a **default term of 60 days**, with the
option of a longer one. **A buyer is not signed to an indefinite arrangement**, and a broker proposing a
longer term is proposing a departure from the statutory default that the buyer should understand.

**(iii) — checkbox options, for buyers.** The statute prescribes the **mechanism**, not merely the
disclosure: the buyer must be able to **select** exclusive or nonexclusive. A form that pre-selects one has
not complied.

**(iv) — separately initialed, with an acknowledgment.** Limited dual agency consent is not covered by the
signature at the end. It must be **separately initialed**, and it must carry the acknowledgment in the
statute's own terms: **a limited dual agent may not advocate terms favorable to one principal to the
detriment of the other.**

**(v) — a second, different consent.** (iv) is about **your** broker acting as a limited dual agent. **(v) is
about the designated broker and managing broker** becoming limited dual agents when **different brokers in the
same firm** represent the two sides. **Two consents, two situations**, and a broker who explains only the
first has explained half.

### B.2 The one exception — RCW 18.86.020(3)

> *"A services agreement is **not required** when a broker performs real estate brokerage services **as a
> buyer's agent solely for commercial real estate**."*

**Commercial buyer's agency only.** Not commercial seller's agency, not residential anything. And note that
**RCW 18.86.080(8)** supplies the substitute in that case — a written **"Compensation Disclosure."** Module
WA-P-04 covers it.

### B.3 Different relationships in different transactions — RCW 18.86.020(4)

> *"A broker **may work with a party in separate transactions pursuant to different relationships** including,
> but not limited to, **representing a party in one transaction and at the same time not representing that
> party in a different transaction** involving that party, if the broker complies with this chapter in
> establishing the relationships for each transaction."*

**Explicitly permitted, and explicitly conditioned:** each relationship must be established in compliance with
the chapter. This is how a broker lawfully represents a client on their purchase while not representing them
on a concurrent sale — but it requires the paperwork for each, separately.

## C. The pamphlet — RCW 18.86.030(1)(f)

> *"To **provide a pamphlet in the form prescribed by RCW 18.86.120 and obtain an acknowledgment of receipt by
> the party**. The pamphlet shall be provided to:*
> **(i)** *"**Any party to whom the broker renders real estate brokerage services as soon as reasonably
> practical but BEFORE the party signs a services agreement**; and*
> **(ii)** *"**Any party NOT REPRESENTED by a broker in a transaction BEFORE the party signs an offer or as
> soon as reasonably practical**."*

### C.1 Two triggers, two different moments

| Who | When |
|---|---|
| **A party you are rendering services to** | As soon as reasonably practical, **but BEFORE they sign a services agreement** |
| **A party NOT represented by any broker** | **Before they sign an offer**, or as soon as reasonably practical |

**The second is the one brokers forget.** An **unrepresented seller** across the table from your buyer, or an
**unrepresented buyer** making an offer on your listing, **must receive the pamphlet from you** — before they
sign an offer. They are not your principal. The duty is owed anyway, because **RCW 18.86.030(1) runs to all
parties.**

### C.2 And the acknowledgment is not optional

> *"and **obtain an acknowledgment of receipt by the party**."*

**Two acts: provide, and obtain the acknowledgment.** The acknowledgment is what proves the first act
happened, and a broker without one has no record.

**The pamphlet's content is prescribed by RCW 18.86.120 and was not transcribed for this build.** It describes
**licensing and supervision of brokers**, **agency relationships**, **for sellers**, **for buyers**, **for both
as a limited dual agent**, and **the duration of the agency relationship**, among other material. **Delivering
it in the prescribed form is a submission gate.**

## D. The agency disclosure — RCW 18.86.030(1)(g)

> *"To **disclose in writing BEFORE the broker's principal signs an offer, or as soon as reasonably practical,
> but BEFORE THE PARTIES REACH MUTUAL AGREEMENT**:*
>
> **(i)** *"Whether the broker **represents the buyer as the buyer's agent, the seller as the seller's agent,
> or both parties as a limited dual agent**. **The disclosure shall be set forth in a separate paragraph
> titled 'Agency Disclosure' in the agreement between the buyer and seller or in a separate writing titled
> 'Agency Disclosure'**; and*
>
> **(ii)** *"**Any terms of compensation offered by a party or a real estate firm to a real estate firm
> representing another party.**"*

### D.1 Four requirements in one subsection

**In writing.** **Before the principal signs an offer**, or as soon as reasonably practical — with an outer
limit of **before the parties reach mutual agreement.**

**In a separate paragraph titled "Agency Disclosure"** — in the purchase and sale agreement **or** in a
separate writing bearing that title. **The heading is prescribed.** A disclosure buried in a paragraph headed
something else has not complied.

**And (ii) is the part students overlook**: the disclosure carries **any terms of compensation offered by a
party or a firm to a firm representing another party.** That is a compensation disclosure sitting inside the
agency disclosure, and **Module WA-P-04** develops what it means in practice.

## E. Seller's agent and buyer's agent duties — RCW 18.86.040 and .050

Each provides that, *"**unless additional duties are agreed to in writing signed by**"* the agent, the duties
are **limited to those in RCW 18.86.030 and** the following, ***"which may not be waived except as expressly
set forth in (e)"***:

> **(a)** *"To be **loyal** to the [principal] by **taking no action that is adverse or detrimental** to the
> [principal]'s interest in a transaction;*
> **(b)** *"To **timely disclose** to the [principal] **any conflicts of interest**;*
> **(c)** *"To **advise** the [principal] **to seek expert advice on matters relating to the transaction that
> are beyond the agent's expertise**;*
> **(d)** *"To **not disclose any confidential information** from or about the [principal], **except under
> subpoena or court order, EVEN AFTER TERMINATION of the agency relationship**; and*
> **(e)** *"Unless otherwise agreed to in writing **after the agent has complied with RCW 18.86.030(1)(f)**,
> to make a **good faith and continuous effort** to find a buyer for the property / a property for the buyer;
> **except that** the agent is **not obligated to seek additional offers while the property is subject to an
> existing contract for sale** / **additional properties while the buyer is a party to an existing contract to
> purchase**."*

### E.1 Five features

**"Limited to."** The duty list is **closed**. RCW 18.86.110 superseded the common-law fiduciary duties, and
these sections state what replaced them. **Additional duties arise only by a writing signed by the agent.**

**Loyalty is defined negatively** — *"taking no action that is adverse or detrimental."* That is a narrower
formulation than a common-law duty of undivided loyalty, and it is the formulation Washington chose.

**(d) has exactly two exceptions and survives termination.** *"Except under **subpoena or court order**, even
after termination."* A broker who learned a seller's bottom line does not become free to mention it when the
listing expires. **Note how narrow the exceptions are compared with other states' lists.**

**(e) is the only waivable one**, and even then only *"in writing **after** the agent has complied with RCW
18.86.030(1)(f)"* — **after the pamphlet has been given and acknowledged.** The statute sequences it: the
party must have the pamphlet before they can waive the effort duty.

**(c) is the routing duty, and it is statutory.** *"To advise ... to seek expert advice on matters ... beyond
the agent's expertise."* Not a courtesy. A duty.

### E.2 The two safe harbours — subsection (2) of each

> *"**(a)** The showing of properties not owned by the seller ... or the listing of competing properties ...
> **does not in and of itself breach the duty of loyalty ... or create a conflict of interest.**"*
> *"**(b)** The **representation of more than one seller by different brokers affiliated with the same firm in
> competing transactions involving the same buyer** does not in and of itself breach the duty of loyalty ...
> or create a conflict of interest."*

**RCW 18.86.050(2)** states the mirror rules for a buyer's agent: showing a property the buyer is interested in
to other buyers, and representing more than one buyer through different brokers in the same firm in competing
transactions involving the same property.

**These exist so that ordinary brokerage is possible.** Without them, every multi-agent firm would be in
permanent conflict.

**But read "in and of itself."** The safe harbour covers the **structural** fact. It does not cover **conduct**
— a broker who actually uses one principal's confidential information to advantage another has done something
the safe harbour never reached.

## F. The sequence, in order

1. **Give the pamphlet and obtain the acknowledgment** — before a services agreement is signed, and to any
   unrepresented party before they sign an offer. *(1)(f)*
2. **Recognise that buyer agency may already exist** by operation of RCW 18.86.020(1).
3. **Enter the services agreement** — the **firm** with the **principal**, before or as soon as reasonably
   practical after services commence, containing **all five** RCW 18.86.020(2)(b) items, with the dual agency
   consent **separately initialed.**
4. **Disclose the role in writing** in a paragraph titled **"Agency Disclosure,"** before the principal signs
   an offer and before mutual agreement — **including any compensation terms offered to a firm representing
   another party.** *(1)(g)*
5. **Perform the duties** in RCW 18.86.030 to everyone, and in .040 or .050 to the principal.
