# Washington Real Estate Practices — Module WA-P-01

## Statutory duties under RCW 18.86 — the duties owed to everyone

**Course:** Real Estate Practices — the second of the two courses **RCW 18.85.101(1)(c)** names.
**Instructional hours:** **5.0** of 30.0 · **Clock hour = 50 minutes**
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No Washington clock hours are available.

> ### The sentence this whole course is built on
>
> **RCW 18.86.110:**
>
> *"**The duties under this chapter are statutory duties and not fiduciary duties. This chapter supersedes
> the fiduciary duties of an agent to a principal under the common law.** The common law continues to apply
> to the parties in all other respects. This chapter does not affect the duties of a broker while engaging
> in the authorized or unauthorized practice of law as determined by the courts of this state. **This
> chapter shall be construed broadly.**"*
>
> **Washington kept the word "agent" and replaced what stands behind it.** A student arriving with a
> national textbook's account of fiduciary duty — loyalty, obedience, disclosure, confidentiality,
> accounting and reasonable care as **common-law fiduciary obligations** — has an account **Washington
> superseded by statute.**
>
> **What remains is the list in RCW 18.86.030, owed to ALL PARTIES, plus the relationship-specific lists in
> .040, .050 and .060 — and nothing else.** Learn the lists.

## Learning objectives

1. State the seven duties RCW 18.86.030(1) owes to the principal and to all parties, and that they may not
   be waived.
2. Apply the presentation duty, and explain the "regardless" clause.
3. Apply the disclosure duty together with its proviso.
4. State the three limits in RCW 18.86.030(2) and what they do not excuse.
5. Apply the five-part definition of confidential information.
6. Apply the material fact definition and its negative list.
7. State the consequence of a violation under RCW 18.86.031.

## A. RCW 18.86.030(1) — the seven, transcribed

> *"A broker owes the following duties **to their principal and to all parties in a transaction, which may
> not be waived**:*
>
> **(a)** *"To exercise **reasonable skill and care**;*
> **(b)** *"To **deal honestly and in good faith**;*
> **(c)** *"To **present all written offers, written notices and other written communications to and from
> either party in a timely manner, regardless of whether the property is subject to an existing contract for
> sale or the buyer is already a party to an existing contract to purchase**;*
> **(d)** *"To **disclose all existing material facts known by the broker and not apparent or readily
> ascertainable to a party**; **provided that this subsection shall not be construed to imply any duty to
> investigate matters that the broker has not agreed to investigate**;*
> **(e)** *"To **account in a timely manner for all money and property received from or on behalf of either
> party**;*
> **(f)** *"To **provide a pamphlet in the form prescribed by RCW 18.86.120 and obtain an acknowledgment of
> receipt by the party** ...;*
> **(g)** *"To **disclose in writing** before the broker's principal signs an offer, or as soon as reasonably
> practical, but before the parties reach mutual agreement ..."*

### A.1 Read the opening words before anything else

**"To their principal AND TO ALL PARTIES in a transaction."** Not to the client. To **everyone** — the buyer
your seller-principal is negotiating against, the unrepresented seller across the table, the tenant your
landlord-principal is screening.

**"Which may not be waived."** No agreement, no signature, no disclosure, no instruction from a principal
removes any of the seven. A broker asked to set one aside has been asked to do something the statute
forecloses, and the answer does not depend on how the request is phrased.

---

## B. Duty (c) — the presentation duty, and the clause that makes Washington different

> *"To present **all written offers, written notices and other written communications to and from either
> party in a timely manner, REGARDLESS OF whether the property is subject to an existing contract for sale
> or the buyer is already a party to an existing contract to purchase**."*

**Four things it reaches:** offers, **notices**, and **other written communications** — and it reaches them
**to and from either party**, in **both directions.**

**"All" means the broker does not filter.** An offer the broker thinks is insulting still goes to the
principal. The principal decides what to do with it; the broker decides nothing.

### B.1 The "regardless" clause

**In many states a listing broker's duty to present offers softens once the property is under contract, or
requires the seller's written waiver to stop.** **Washington states the opposite in the statute itself.**

> ***"regardless of whether the property is subject to an existing contract for sale or the buyer is already
> a party to an existing contract to purchase."***

**A property already under contract still gets every written offer presented.** A buyer already under
contract on another property still gets every written communication presented. **There is no waiver in the
subsection**, and the whole subsection sits inside a paragraph that says these duties **may not be waived.**

### B.2 Note what it is expressed in terms of

**Written** offers, **written** notices and **other written** communications. The statutory duty is stated in
terms of writings. **The practical lesson is the reverse of a loophole: put it in writing, and then it must
be presented.** A verbal indication of interest carries no statutory presentation duty and, under **RCW
18.86.010(15)**, does not even begin a prospective transaction:

> *"a prospective transaction **does not exist until a written offer has been signed by at least one
> party**."*

## C. Duty (d) — disclosure, and its proviso

> *"To **disclose all existing material facts known by the broker and not apparent or readily ascertainable
> to a party**; **provided that this subsection shall not be construed to imply any duty to investigate
> matters that the broker has not agreed to investigate**."*

**Three limits on the duty, and one limit on the limits.**

- **Existing** — not speculative or future.
- **Material facts** — the defined term in Part E.
- **Known by the broker** — actual knowledge.
- **Not apparent or readily ascertainable to a party** — a condition a party can plainly see, or find
  easily, is not the target.

**And the proviso: no duty to investigate what the broker has not agreed to investigate.**

### C.1 The words "has not agreed to" are doing work

The proviso does not say there is never a duty to investigate. It says the subsection does not **imply** one
as to matters the broker **has not agreed to investigate.**

**So a broker who agrees to investigate something has taken it on.** *"I'll check with the county on the
septic capacity"* is an agreement. Having made it, the broker either does it or corrects the record — and
duty (a), reasonable skill and care, now attaches to how it is done.

**Say what you will do, and then do it.** Casual promises create duties in Washington.

---

## D. The limits — RCW 18.86.030(2)

> *"Unless otherwise agreed, a broker **owes no duty to conduct an independent inspection of the property**
> or to **conduct an independent investigation of either party's financial condition**, and owes **no duty to
> independently verify the accuracy or completeness of any statement made by either party or by any source
> reasonably believed by the broker to be reliable**."*

**Three shelters:** no duty to **inspect**, no duty to **investigate finances**, no duty to **verify
statements.**

**And "unless otherwise agreed" opens all three** — the same principle as the (1)(d) proviso. A broker who
agrees to do one of these has agreed to do it.

### D.1 What the shelters do not excuse

**They remove duties to look. They do not remove the duty to disclose what you saw, and they do not license a
statement of your own.**

A broker who walks a crawl space and sees water has not inspected — **they have acquired information**, and
(1)(d) governs.

A broker who repeats the lender's assessment with attribution is reporting. A broker who says *"you're
approved"* has made a representation, and **(1)(b) — deal honestly and in good faith, owed to all parties,
unwaivable — is what it will be measured against.**

**And the verification shelter turns on "reasonably believed ... to be reliable."** It protects reliance on a
source the broker had reason to trust. It does not protect reliance on a statement the broker had reason to
doubt.

## E. Two definitions that decide cases

### E.1 Confidential information — RCW 18.86.010(9), a five-part test

> *"information from or concerning a principal that:*
> **(a)** *Was **acquired by the broker during the course of an agency relationship** with the principal;*
> **(b)** *The principal **reasonably expects to be kept confidential**;*
> **(c)** *The principal **has not disclosed or authorized to be disclosed** to third parties;*
> **(d)** ***Would, if disclosed, operate to the detriment of the principal**; and*
> **(e)** *The principal **personally would not be obligated to disclose to the other party**."*

**All five parts. Not any one of them.** Work an example: a seller mentions they are relocating for work in
sixty days.

(a) Acquired during the relationship — **yes**. (b) Reasonably expected confidential — **yes**. (c) Not
disclosed by them — **check; if they said it at the open house, no**. (d) Detrimental if disclosed —
**yes, it signals urgency**. (e) Not something the seller would personally be obliged to disclose — **yes,
a relocation is not a material fact about the property.**

**Five out of five: confidential.**

**Now change one fact.** The seller mentions the roof leaks. Part **(e) fails** — a known material defect
affecting the physical condition is something the seller would be obliged to deal with, and duty (1)(d) makes
the broker's own disclosure obligation independent of the principal's wishes. **It is not confidential
information**, because it never satisfied part (e).

**Part (e) is the part students miss, and it is the part that resolves the "my client told me not to tell"
question before it arises.**

### E.2 Material fact — RCW 18.86.010(11), with its negative list

> *"'Material fact' means information that **substantially adversely affects the value of the property or a
> party's ability to perform its obligations** in a real estate transaction, or **operates to materially
> impair or defeat the purpose of the transaction**. **The fact or suspicion that the property, or any
> neighboring property, is or was the site of a murder, suicide or other death, rape or other sex crime,
> assault or other violent crime, robbery or burglary, illegal drug activity, gang-related activity,
> political or religious activity, or other act, occurrence, or use NOT ADVERSELY AFFECTING THE PHYSICAL
> CONDITION OF OR TITLE TO THE PROPERTY is not a material fact.**"*

**Two branches in the affirmative definition**, and either suffices: substantial adverse effect on **value or
a party's ability to perform**, **or** operating to **materially impair or defeat the purpose of the
transaction.**

**And a long negative list, governed by one limiting clause: "not adversely affecting the physical condition
of or title to the property."**

**Apply it.** A former methamphetamine laboratory that **contaminated the structure** affected the **physical
condition** — outside the exclusion, inside the duty. Drug activity that left no physical trace and no title
effect — inside the exclusion.

**The test is not how alarming the event was. It is whether it affected the physical condition or the
title.**

**And the exclusion does not authorise a lie.** Duty (1)(b) is owed to all parties and cannot be waived.
**Not being obliged to volunteer something is not permission to misstate it.** A broker asked directly
answers truthfully or declines to answer — and never invents.

## F. Duties (f) and (g), previewed

**(f) — the pamphlet.** *"To **provide a pamphlet in the form prescribed by RCW 18.86.120 and obtain an
acknowledgment of receipt** by the party."* **Module WA-P-02** covers the timing, which differs depending on
whether the party is being served or is unrepresented.

**(g) — the agency disclosure.** In writing, **before the broker's principal signs an offer**, in a **separate
paragraph titled "Agency Disclosure"** — plus **any terms of compensation offered by a party or a firm to a
firm representing another party.** **Module WA-P-02** covers it, and **Module WA-P-04** covers the
compensation half.

## G. What a violation is — RCW 18.86.031

> *"**A violation of either RCW 18.86.030 or 18.86.130 is a violation of RCW 18.85.361.**"*

**A breach of these duties is a licence-law violation**, not merely a private wrong. **RCW 18.85.361 was NOT
read for this project**, and **this course states no penalty, sanction or procedure from it.** What a student
must carry is the linkage: **RCW 18.86.030 is enforceable through the licence.**

*(RCW 18.86.130 — the 2026 exclusive-marketing statute — is in Module WA-P-05, and it is the other provision
that carries this consequence.)*

## H. The seven, as a working checklist

1. **Reasonable skill and care** — be competent at what you do.
2. **Honestly and in good faith** — to everyone, always, unwaivable.
3. **Present everything written, both directions, timely — regardless.**
4. **Disclose known material facts** not apparent or readily ascertainable — **and no duty to investigate what
   you did not agree to investigate.**
5. **Account for every dollar and every item of property.**
6. **Give the pamphlet and get the acknowledgment.**
7. **Disclose your role in writing, under that heading, before your principal signs an offer.**

**None of them is waivable. All of them are owed to everyone in the transaction.**
