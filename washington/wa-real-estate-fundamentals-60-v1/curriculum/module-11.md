## A. The broker's duty, and its exact shape

Three sentences from RCW 18.86, read together, define the whole of it.

**The duty — RCW 18.86.030(1)(d)**, owed to the principal **and to all parties**, **unwaivable**:

> *"To **disclose all existing material facts known by the broker and not apparent or readily ascertainable
> to a party**; **provided that this subsection shall not be construed to imply any duty to investigate
> matters that the broker has not agreed to investigate**."*

**The limits — RCW 18.86.030(2):**

> *"Unless otherwise agreed, a broker **owes no duty to conduct an independent inspection of the property**
> ... and owes **no duty to independently verify the accuracy or completeness of any statement made by either
> party or by any source reasonably believed by the broker to be reliable**."*

**The routing duty — RCW 18.86.040(1)(c) and 18.86.050(1)(c):**

> *"To **advise ... to seek expert advice on matters relating to the transaction that are beyond the agent's
> expertise**."*

### A.1 Read them as one rule

**No duty to dig.** You need not inspect, investigate, or verify.
**Absolute duty to disclose what you know**, where it is material and not apparent or readily ascertainable.
**A statutory duty to send the party to someone who can answer.**

**The proviso in (d) is doing careful work and it is worth a sentence.** *"Shall not be construed to imply
any duty to investigate **matters that the broker has not agreed to investigate**."* The words *"has not
agreed to"* leave the door open: **a broker who does agree to investigate something has taken on the duty.**
Promising a buyer *"I'll find out about the septic"* is an agreement, and it converts an optional act into an
obligation.

### A.2 What the limits do NOT excuse

**(2) removes duties to investigate and verify. It does not remove the duty to disclose what you actually
know, and it does not license a false statement.**

A broker who walks a crawl space and sees standing water has not conducted an inspection — **they have
acquired information**, and (1)(d) governs what happens next.

A broker who repeats a seller's statement with attribution is reporting. A broker who says *"the roof is
fine"* has made a representation, and **RCW 18.86.030(1)(b)** — deal honestly and in good faith, owed to
**all parties**, **unwaivable** — is what it will be measured against.

## B. Material fact, applied to condition

**RCW 18.86.010(11):**

> *"information that **substantially adversely affects the value of the property or a party's ability to
> perform its obligations** in a real estate transaction, or **operates to materially impair or defeat the
> purpose of the transaction**."*

**And the negative list**, which Module WA-F-09 set out in full, ends with the clause that decides condition
questions:

> *"... or **other act, occurrence, or use NOT ADVERSELY AFFECTING THE PHYSICAL CONDITION OF OR TITLE TO THE
> PROPERTY** is not a material fact."*

**Apply it to the case students always ask about.** A property was used as a methamphetamine laboratory.

- **Did the use adversely affect the physical condition of the property?** If the structure was contaminated
  — **yes**, and the exclusion does not reach it. A known contamination substantially adversely affects value
  and may defeat the purpose of the transaction. **It is disclosed.**
- **If the activity left no physical trace and no effect on title** — the exclusion reaches it, and it is not
  a material fact.

**The test is not the shock value of the event. It is whether it affected the physical condition or the
title.** That single distinction resolves most of the negative list.

## C. The conditions a Washington broker actually meets

For each: **what it is, and who answers it.** A broker's job is the second half.

**Lead-based paint.** The federal disclosure obligation attaches to **target housing — generally dwellings
built before 1978** — and reaches **known** lead-based paint and known hazards. **Painting over it does not
remove it.** **The federal regulation was not read for this project**, and this course states **no notice
period, no acknowledgment requirement and no record retention period** from it. → **The seller's own
knowledge; a certified inspector or risk assessor; the EPA's own materials.**

**Radon.** A soil gas measured by testing. **An old test result is not a current reading.** → **A current
test.**

**Asbestos.** Common in materials in older construction — insulation, flooring, siding, textured ceilings. Its
presence is usually a question about **whether it is disturbed.** → **A qualified inspector; a licensed
abatement contractor before any renovation.**

**Mould and moisture.** In much of western Washington this is the most frequently raised condition of all. The
useful question is almost never "is there mould" but **"where is the water coming from."** → **A qualified
inspector; a contractor.**

**On-site septic systems.** Capacity, condition, permit status and whether the soil supports the system. →
**The county health department for the permit and its capacity; a qualified inspector for the condition.**

**Wells and water quality.** Yield, potability, and — separately — the **right** to use the water. → **A
testing laboratory; the health department; and for water rights, the Department of Ecology and counsel.**

**Underground storage tanks**, including residential heating oil tanks. Presence, condition, and any release.
→ **A qualified environmental professional.**

**Landslide, steep slope, liquefaction, seismic and volcanic hazard**, and **flood.** These are genuinely
Washington questions — the state has terrain, faults, volcanoes and rivers. → **Published hazard mapping; the
local jurisdiction's critical areas information; a geotechnical engineer; and for flood insurance, an
insurer.**

**Critical areas and shoreline designation.** Wetlands, habitat, aquifer recharge and shoreline jurisdiction
can limit what may be built. → **The local jurisdiction. Module WA-F-03.**

**Contaminated sites and hazardous substances.** → **A qualified environmental professional and counsel.**

> **This course states no action level, threshold, buffer width, testing protocol, remediation standard,
> permit requirement, hazard designation or insurance term for ANY of the above**, because **no federal
> regulation, no Washington environmental statute, no critical areas ordinance, no health department rule and
> no hazard map was read for this project.** Every one is routed.

## D. What a broker says, and what a broker does not

### D.1 The three-way separation

Say which of these you are doing, every time:

1. **What you know** — you saw it, measured it, or hold the document.
2. **What you were told** — attributed to whoever told you.
3. **What you are guessing** — say nothing, and route it.

**Every disclosure failure in this course traces back to one of the three being presented as another.**

### D.2 Sentences that are defensible, and sentences that are not

| Defensible | Not defensible |
|---|---|
| *"The seller states the roof was replaced in 2019; I have not verified it."* | *"The roof is six years old."* |
| *"There was standing water in the crawl space when I was there in March."* | *"There's no moisture problem."* |
| *"A radon test was run some years ago with that result; it is not a current reading. You can test."* | *"Radon isn't an issue here."* |
| *"I don't know whether the septic supports four bedrooms. The county health department holds the permit."* | *"It's a four-bedroom septic."* |
| *"That's beyond what I can advise on — here is who can."* | *"I wouldn't worry about it."* |

**The left column is a broker doing the job.** The right column is a broker making representations the file
cannot support.

### D.3 Two things a broker should never do

**Never advise a seller to remain ignorant.** *"Don't test — what you don't know you don't have to
disclose"* is bad advice legally and bad advice practically. An undisclosed condition found during inspection
costs more than a known condition priced in from the start.

**Never fill in the seller's disclosure statement.** It is the **seller's** statement of the **seller's**
knowledge, signed on a date. A broker who completes it has made it partly their own.
**RCW 64.06 prescribes that form and was not read for this project**, so **nothing in this build states what
it contains or when it must be delivered** — but the principle above stands on **RCW 18.86.030(1)(b) and
(1)(d)**, which were read.
