## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish the leasehold estates and identify which requires notice to end.
2. Identify the essential terms of a lease and the covenant of quiet enjoyment.
3. Distinguish gross, net, percentage and ground leases.
4. Describe the property manager's functions and the management agreement's essential terms.
5. Compute the figures a manager actually produces — vacancy, effective gross income, NOI, management fee.
6. Apply RCW 18.86's duties to a management relationship, and identify where a manager must route.

## A. Leasehold estates — recap and application

| Estate | How it ends |
|---|---|
| **Estate for years** | Definite beginning, definite end. **Terminates on its own terms; no notice required.** |
| **Periodic tenancy** | Renews period to period **until proper notice**. |
| **Tenancy at will** | Continues at the pleasure of both parties. |
| **Tenancy at sufferance** | A holdover the landlord never agreed to — possession without right. |

**The distinction that matters daily is the first two.** A one-year lease that simply expires needs no
notice. A month-to-month tenancy continues until somebody gives the notice the statute requires.

**Washington's notice periods are in RCW 59.18 and were not read for this project. No notice period is stated
in this course.** A manager who guesses at one has guessed at whether a tenancy ended — which is the single
most consequential thing a manager can get wrong.

## B. The lease

### B.1 Essential terms

**Parties. Premises. Term. Rent — amount, due date, method. Use. Maintenance and repair responsibilities.
Deposits. Utilities. Rules. Default and remedies. Assignment and subletting. Notices — how and to where.**

**A lease is a conveyance and a contract at once**, which is why the **covenant of quiet enjoyment** — the
tenant's right to possess and use without interference by the landlord or those claiming through the landlord
— matters as much as any written clause.

### B.2 Assignment and sublease

**Assignment** transfers the tenant's **entire** remaining interest. **Sublease** transfers **less than all**
of it, leaving the original tenant as a landlord to the subtenant.

**In both cases the original tenant generally remains liable unless expressly released** — and tenants
routinely assume otherwise. **Whether either is permitted at all is a question for the lease's own terms.**

### B.3 Lease types by rent structure

- **Gross lease** — the tenant pays rent; the landlord pays operating expenses.
- **Net lease** — the tenant pays rent **plus** some or all of taxes, insurance and maintenance. *Single,
  double and triple net* describe how many of the three.
- **Percentage lease** — rent includes a percentage of the tenant's gross sales, usually above a breakpoint.
  Common in retail.
- **Ground lease** — the tenant leases land and builds; typically long-term.
- **Index or graduated lease** — rent adjusts on a schedule or by an index.

**Worked example — percentage lease.** *Base rent $4,200 per month; 5 percent of gross sales above a
$70,000 monthly breakpoint. In a month with $118,000 of sales:* overage = 118,000 − 70,000 = **$48,000**;
percentage rent = 48,000 × 0.05 = **$2,400**; total rent = 4,200 + 2,400 = **$6,600.**

## C. Property management

### C.1 What a manager actually does

**Market the space and screen applicants** — under fair housing law, with **written criteria applied
identically to every applicant, and documented.** *(Module WA-F-09 is the substantive treatment; tenant
screening is where fair housing exposure in management is concentrated.)*

**Collect rent and enforce the lease.** **Maintain the property.** **Account to the owner** and report.
**Keep the records.** **Handle deposits** according to statute. **Serve notices** correctly and on time.

### C.2 The management agreement

Essential terms: **the parties and the property; the scope of authority; the term; the compensation; the
owner's approval thresholds for expenditure; reporting and accounting obligations; the handling of tenant
funds and deposits; insurance requirements; indemnity; and termination.**

**An unwritten scope of authority is how a manager ends up having spent an owner's money on something the
owner did not authorise.** Set the threshold in dollars, in the agreement, and get written approval above it.

**And a management agreement is a brokerage relationship.** A firm managing property for an owner is
rendering real estate brokerage services, and **RCW 18.86's duties apply.**

### C.3 Where the money goes

**Rents and tenant deposits are other people's money.** Washington's requirements for how a firm holds them —
which accounts, on what deadlines, with what records and what reconciliation — are in **WAC 308-124 and the
remainder of RCW 18.85, which this project did not read.**

**This course therefore states no account requirement, deposit deadline, reconciliation interval or record
retention period.** What it does state is the statutory duty that sits above all of them, from **RCW
18.86.030(1)(e)**, owed to **all parties** and **unwaivable**:

> *"To **account in a timely manner for all money and property received from or on behalf of either
> party**."*

**And RCW 18.86.070(2):** after the relationship terminates, **two duties survive** — *"(a) to **account for
all moneys and property received during the relationship**; and (b) to **not disclose confidential
information**."*

**A manager who cannot produce a clean accounting has breached a statutory duty**, whatever the mechanics
turn out to require.

## D. The numbers a manager produces

### D.1 The operating statement, in order

**Potential gross income** (all units at market rent, fully occupied)
less **vacancy and collection loss**
plus **other income** (parking, laundry, fees)
= **effective gross income**
less **operating expenses**
= **net operating income**

**Debt service is not an operating expense.** NOI less debt service is **before-tax cash flow**, and it
answers a different question — Module WA-F-08 develops it.

**Watch for the replacement reserve.** Omitting a reserve for roofs, appliances and systems **overstates NOI
and therefore overstates value.**

### D.2 Worked example, all figures given

*A 24-unit building; market rent $1,850 per unit per month; vacancy and collection loss 6 percent; other
income $1,400 per month; operating expenses $246,000 annually; management fee 5 percent of effective gross
income; annual debt service $318,000.*

Potential gross income = 24 × 1,850 × 12 = **$532,800.**
Vacancy and collection loss = 532,800 × 0.06 = **$31,968.**
Other income = 1,400 × 12 = **$16,800.**
Effective gross income = 532,800 − 31,968 + 16,800 = **$517,632.**
Management fee = 517,632 × 0.05 = **$25,881.60** *(and note it is charged on EGI, not on potential gross —
a manager paid on collections is paid on what came in).*
Net operating income = 517,632 − 246,000 = **$271,632.**
Before-tax cash flow = 271,632 − 318,000 = **−$46,368** *(a negative, and a real one — this property does
not cover its debt service at these figures).*

*(Every rate in that example is supplied inside it. **This course states no management fee rate**, because
compensation is negotiated and no course sets it.)*

### D.3 Vacancy and occupancy rates

**Occupancy rate = occupied units ÷ total units.** **Vacancy rate = vacant units ÷ total units.** They sum to
one. **Economic vacancy** — rent not collected, including concessions and delinquency — is a different and
usually larger figure than **physical vacancy**, and an owner comparing the two is comparing different things.

## E. Where a property manager must route, without exception

**Every one of these is a legal or technical question, and RCW 18.86.040(1)(c) and 18.86.050(1)(c) make
sending it on a statutory duty:**

- **Any notice that ends a tenancy or demands compliance.** Form, content, service method and timing are set
  by statute, and an improperly served notice restarts the clock at best.
- **Any eviction.** Unlawful detainer is a court proceeding. **Self-help — changing locks, removing doors,
  shutting off utilities, removing belongings — is not a remedy**, and a manager who does it has created
  liability for the owner and for the firm.
- **Any deposit dispute.** Limits, permitted uses, the accounting deadline and the consequence of missing it
  are statutory.
- **Any habitability complaint**, and the tenant's remedies for one.
- **Any accommodation or modification request.** Module WA-F-09.
- **Any lease drafting or interpretation question.** Completing a form and inventing a term are different
  acts, and **RCW 18.86.110** leaves the practice-of-law boundary exactly where the courts put it.

**"I don't know, and here is who does" is the professionally correct answer to every one of them.**
