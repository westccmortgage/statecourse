# Washington Real Estate Fundamentals — Module WA-F-04

## Transfer of title, deeds, recording and title assurance

**Course:** Real Estate Fundamentals · **Instructional hours: 5.0** of 60.0 · **Clock hour = 50 minutes**
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No Washington clock hours are available.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish voluntary from involuntary transfer.
2. State the essential elements of a valid deed.
3. Distinguish deed types by the covenants each carries — including Washington's statutory warranty deed.
4. Explain what recording accomplishes and what it does not.
5. Distinguish owner's from lender's title insurance.
6. Describe a Washington closing and the broker's role in it.

---

## A. Voluntary transfer

**By deed** during life; **by will** at death — the maker a **testator**, the gift of real property a
**devise**; and **by dedication**, where an owner gives land to public use.

## B. Involuntary transfer

- **Intestate succession** — no will. **Washington's intestacy statute was not read for this project**, and
  no share, priority or spousal provision is stated. Note that Washington's **community property**
  character bears directly on this, which is a further reason it goes to counsel.
- **Escheat** — no heirs and no will.
- **Eminent domain** — condemnation with just compensation.
- **Non-judicial foreclosure under a deed of trust** — Washington's dominant remedy. **RCW 61.24, the Deeds
  of Trust Act, was not read for this project**, and **no notice period, timeline, reinstatement right,
  redemption right or deficiency rule is stated in this course.**
- **Tax foreclosure** for unpaid property taxes.
- **Adverse possession** — title by possession that is, in the usual formulation, **open, notorious,
  continuous, exclusive and hostile under a claim of right for the statutory period.** **Washington's
  statutory period was not read for this project and is not stated here.** What matters to a broker is
  recognising the trigger: a long-standing fence, a neighbour's driveway, an outbuilding across a line —
  all go to a surveyor and counsel.
- **Accretion, erosion, avulsion and reliction** — the work of water on a boundary, which in a state with
  Washington's shoreline and river frontage is not a theoretical topic.

## C. The deed

### C.1 Essential elements

**Grantor** with capacity; **grantee** identified with certainty; **words of conveyance**; **consideration**
recited; **legal description**; **grantor's signature**; and **delivery and acceptance.**

**A deed takes effect on delivery, not on signing and not on recording.** The grantee does not sign a deed.
**Acknowledgement before a notary is required for recording.**

### C.2 Deed types

| Deed | What it warrants |
|---|---|
| **Statutory warranty deed** | Washington's common conveyance instrument. By statute it carries the full covenants of warranty without their being written out. |
| **Special (limited) warranty deed** | Covenants only against defects arising during the grantor's ownership. |
| **Bargain and sale deed** | Implies the grantor holds title; covenants vary by form. |
| **Quitclaim deed** | Conveys whatever interest the grantor has — **possibly none** — with **no warranty**. |

**A statutory warranty deed is short because the statute supplies the covenants.** That is its point: the
form is compact and the protection is full. **This course states no text of Washington's statutory form**,
because the statute prescribing it was not read for this project.

**A quitclaim deed is not a lesser-quality conveyance of good title. It conveys whatever the grantor
happens to hold, warranting nothing** — which is why it is the right instrument for clearing a cloud and
the wrong one for a purchase. **Recording does not improve it: a recorded quitclaim deed is still a
quitclaim deed.**

**Special-purpose deeds** — personal representative's, trustee's, sheriff's, and a **deed in lieu of
foreclosure** — are named here so a broker recognises them in a chain of title. Each raises questions of
authority belonging to a title examiner and counsel.

## D. Recording

### D.1 What recording does

Recording with the **county auditor** in the county where the property lies gives **constructive notice** to
the world. Everyone is treated as knowing what the record contains, whether they looked or not.

- **Actual notice** — what a person in fact knows.
- **Constructive notice** — what the record imputes.
- **Inquiry notice** — what a person would have learned had they investigated a fact that should have
  prompted investigation. **Someone else in possession** is the classic trigger.

### D.2 What recording does not do

**It does not validate an invalid instrument** — a forged deed is void whether recorded or not. **It does
not improve the estate conveyed.** **It does not establish that the grantor had good title.**

**Washington's recording statute was not read for this project.** This course therefore **states no
Washington recording priority rule and no recording deadline.** The practical consequence is universal: an
unrecorded interest is an exposure, and prompt recording is how a purchaser protects what they bought.

### D.3 A note on notice and RCW 18.86.100

Washington reverses one common-law notice rule by statute, and it belongs here:

> **RCW 18.86.100:** *"Unless otherwise agreed to in writing, **a principal does not have knowledge or
> notice of any facts known by an agent of the principal that are not actually known by the principal**."*

**Under the common law an agent's knowledge is generally imputed to the principal. Washington provides the
opposite by statute.** A seller is not charged with what their broker knew and did not tell them. Module
WA-P-03 develops it; it appears here because it is a **notice** rule and this is the notice module.

## E. Evidence of title and title insurance

**Chain of title** — the successive conveyances. **Title search and examination** — running the chain and
reading what it discloses. **Cloud on title** — an outstanding claim or apparent defect: an unreleased deed
of trust, a gap in the chain, an unsatisfied judgment, an heir who never joined in a deed. **Marketable
title** — title a reasonable buyer would accept, free of reasonable doubt. **Quiet title action** — the
lawsuit to resolve a cloud.

### E.1 Two policies, two beneficiaries

- **Lender's (mortgagee's) policy** — protects the **lender**, in the amount of the loan, decreasing as the
  loan is paid. Almost always required by the lender.
- **Owner's policy** — protects the **owner**, in the amount of the purchase price.

**A buyer who pays for a lender's policy has bought the lender protection, not themselves.** That is the
single most useful sentence a broker can say at a Washington closing table, and it takes five seconds.

**Every policy has exceptions** — matters it does not cover, often including matters a **survey** would
disclose. **No policy form or rate manual was read for this project**, and no term, exception, endorsement
or premium is stated in this course.

## F. Closing in Washington

**Closing is conducted by an escrow agent, a title company, or an attorney.** The parties' funds and
documents are delivered into escrow, conditions are satisfied, documents are recorded, and funds are
disbursed.

### F.1 What the broker does, and what the broker does not

**Does:** track deadlines; deliver documents; keep both sides informed; account for money and property
received; recognise questions and route them.

**Does not:** conduct the closing, prepare the deed, opine on title, or advise on the legal effect of an
instrument.

**The statutory basis is two sentences in RCW 18.86.030(1):**

> **(e)** *"To **account in a timely manner for all money and property received from or on behalf of either
> party**";*

and, from **RCW 18.86.040(1)(c)** and **18.86.050(1)(c)**:

> *"To **advise ... to seek expert advice on matters relating to the transaction that are beyond the agent's
> expertise**."*

**And RCW 18.86.110 closes the door on the other side:** *"This chapter **does not affect the duties of a
broker while engaging in the authorized or unauthorized practice of law** as determined by the courts of
this state."* Whatever the practice-of-law boundary is, RCW 18.86 does not move it.

### F.2 Prorations

Items paid in advance or in arrears are **prorated** as of closing — real estate taxes, assessments,
homeowner association dues, rents and deposits on tenanted property, and any fuel remaining.

**A proration is always a double entry.** Whatever is credited to one party is debited to the other. A
prorated item appearing once has been done wrong.

**Which party owns the day of closing, and whether a 360-day or 365-day convention applies, are matters of
contract and local practice. This course states neither as a Washington rule**, because the statutes and
rules read for this project state neither. Practice problems specify the convention inside the problem.

### F.3 Excise tax

Washington imposes a **real estate excise tax** on the sale of real property, and it is a line every
Washington closing statement carries.

**RCW 82.45 and every local excise tax ordinance were not read for this project.** **This course states no
excise tax rate, no threshold, no bracket and no exemption.** Every practice problem supplies the rate as a
given inside the problem, and instructors substitute the current statutory and local rates before delivery.
**Module WA-F-12 works the calculation.**
