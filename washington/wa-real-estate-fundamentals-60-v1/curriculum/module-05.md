# Washington Real Estate Fundamentals — Module WA-F-05

## Contract law fundamentals

**Course:** Real Estate Fundamentals · **Instructional hours: 5.0** of 60.0 · **Clock hour = 50 minutes**
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft

## Learning objectives

1. State the essential elements of a valid contract.
2. Distinguish void, voidable, unenforceable and valid.
3. Apply offer, acceptance, counteroffer and the mirror image rule.
4. Identify the defects in reality of consent.
5. State what a writing is required for, and what RCW 18.86 separately requires in writing.
6. Distinguish the remedies for breach and explain why specific performance is available.
7. Apply RCW 18.86.010(15) — when a prospective transaction begins.

---

## A. Essential elements

**Competent parties. Mutual assent. Consideration. Lawful object.** And **a writing**, where the Statute of
Frauds requires one — which it does for contracts conveying an interest in real property.

**Recording and notarisation are not elements of a contract.** They belong to the conveyance and the
recording statute. A contract can be valid, fully enforceable and never recorded.

### A.1 Four states a contract can be in

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Valid** | Meets all elements; enforceable by either party. |
| **Void** | Never had legal effect. |
| **Voidable** | Valid **until the party holding the power to avoid it elects to do so**. |
| **Unenforceable** | Valid in substance; a court will not enforce it — commonly for want of a writing. |

**The difference between void and voidable is who holds the power.** A contract with a minor is voidable
**at the minor's election** — the adult cannot escape it. A contract induced by fraud is voidable at the
**defrauded** party's election — the party who lied cannot use their own fraud to get out.

### A.2 Classifications

**Bilateral** (promise for promise — most purchase and sale agreements) versus **unilateral** (promise for
an act — most options on their face). **Express** versus **implied**. **Executory** (still being performed)
versus **executed** (fully performed).

**A purchase and sale agreement is executory from mutual acceptance until closing** — and that period, with
deadlines falling and money in escrow, is where a broker does most of the work.

## B. Offer and acceptance

An **offer** is a manifestation of willingness to be bound on stated terms. It may be **revoked at any time
before acceptance**, unless consideration was given to hold it open — which is what an **option** is.

An offer **terminates** by **revocation**, **rejection**, **lapse**, **death or incapacity of either party**,
**destruction of the subject matter**, or **counteroffer**.

### B.1 The mirror image rule

Acceptance must be **unqualified** and **mirror** the offer. A purported acceptance that **changes any term
is a counteroffer** — which **rejects and terminates the original offer** and reverses the roles.

Work it through. A buyer offers Monday. The seller signs Tuesday but strikes the closing date and writes a
new one. That is **not an acceptance.** The buyer's offer is **gone**, and cannot be revived by the buyer
signing the original on Wednesday. What exists is the seller's counteroffer.

**Acceptance must also be communicated.** A signed agreement sitting in a drawer has accepted nothing.

### B.2 The duty that applies whichever side you are on

**RCW 18.86.030(1)(c)** — owed to the principal **and to all parties**, and **not waivable**:

> *"To **present all written offers, written notices and other written communications to and from either
> party in a timely manner, regardless of whether the property is subject to an existing contract for sale
> or the buyer is already a party to an existing contract to purchase**."*

**Read the "regardless" clause, because it is unusually strong.** In many states a listing broker's duty to
present offers softens once the property is under contract, or requires a seller's waiver. **Washington
states the opposite in the statute itself: the duty continues regardless.**

**Note also what it reaches: WRITTEN offers, notices and communications, in BOTH directions.** The statutory
duty is expressed in terms of writings.

### B.3 When a "transaction" begins

**RCW 18.86.010(15):**

> *"For purposes of this chapter, **a prospective transaction does not exist until a written offer has been
> signed by at least one party**."*

That definition decides when several of RCW 18.86's duties attach, and it is precise: **a signed written
offer by at least one party.** Conversations, showings and verbal indications do not start it.

## C. Reality of consent

Assent must be real. It is defective where there is **fraud**, **misrepresentation** (innocent or
negligent), **duress**, **undue influence**, or **mistake** — mutual mistake of material fact may permit
rescission; unilateral mistake usually does not. **In each case the contract is voidable at the election of
the injured party.**

**Puffing** is opinion a reasonable person would not rely on as fact — *"a wonderful home."*
**Misrepresentation** is a false statement of fact — *"2,400 square feet," "the roof is five years old,"
"the crawl space has never taken water."* **The line moves toward misrepresentation as the statement becomes
more specific and more verifiable.**

**And RCW 18.86.030(1)(b) is owed to everyone and unwaivable:** *"To **deal honestly and in good faith**."*

## D. Writings

Contracts for the sale of an interest in real property must generally be **in writing and signed by the
party to be charged** to be enforceable. **Washington's Statute of Frauds was not read for this project**,
and this course **states no Washington lease-term threshold and no Washington exception.**

**What was read is RCW 18.86, and it requires writings of its own** — which are not Statute of Frauds
requirements but licensing-law requirements, and reach further:

- **RCW 18.86.020(2)(a):** *"A firm **must enter into a services agreement with the principal before, or as
  soon as reasonably practical after**, its appointed broker commences rendering real estate brokerage
  services."*
- **RCW 18.86.030(1)(g):** the agency disclosure must be **in writing**, *"in a **separate paragraph titled
  'Agency Disclosure'**"* in the agreement or in a separate writing so titled.
- **RCW 18.86.060(1):** limited dual agency requires *"the **written consent of both parties** to the
  transaction, **set forth in the services agreement**."*
- **RCW 18.86.080(7):** *"**To receive compensation** for rendering real estate brokerage services from any
  party or firm, a real estate firm **must have a services agreement**"* containing stated terms.
- **RCW 18.86.040(1) and .050(1):** the relationship duties may not be waived *"except as expressly set
  forth in (e),"* and additional duties arise only *"if agreed to **in writing signed by**"* the agent.

**A broker who asks "does the Statute of Frauds require this in writing?" has asked a question that often
does not matter. The operative question in Washington practice is what RCW 18.86 requires**, and the answer
is: **the services agreement, the agency disclosure, the dual agency consent, and the compensation terms.**

### D.1 The parol evidence rule

Where a written contract is a complete expression of the agreement, evidence of **prior or contemporaneous
oral agreements offered to vary its terms** is barred. Combined with the writing requirements above, the
working rule is simple: **every change goes on paper, signed.**

## E. Performance, breach and remedies

A contract discharges by **performance**, **mutual agreement**, **operation of law**, or **breach**.

**Assignment** transfers rights and generally **leaves the assignor liable**. **Novation** substitutes a new
party and **releases** the original — which is why it needs everyone's agreement. Confusing the two is how a
seller discovers, after an assignment they approved, that they are still on the hook.

**Remedies:** **damages**; **liquidated damages** (commonly the earnest money, where the agreement so
provides); **rescission**; and **specific performance**.

**Specific performance is available in real estate because each parcel is unique**, so damages are often
inadequate. That is the legal consequence of a physical characteristic of land.

### E.1 Where the money sits while any of this happens

**RCW 18.86.030(1)(e)** — owed to all parties, unwaivable:

> *"To **account in a timely manner for all money and property received from or on behalf of either
> party**."*

**And RCW 18.86.070(2):** after termination of the agency relationship a broker owes **no further duty**
except two — *"(a) **to account for all moneys and property received during the relationship**; and (b) **to
not disclose confidential information**."*

**Two duties survive termination, and one of them is accounting.** Washington's trust account rules sit in
**WAC 308-124 and the remainder of RCW 18.85, which this project did not read** — so **this course states no
deposit deadline, no account requirement and no reconciliation interval.** The duty to account is stated
because the statute states it; the mechanics are routed.

## F. Where a broker stands in relation to a contract

**RCW 18.86.110:** *"This chapter **does not affect the duties of a broker while engaging in the authorized
or unauthorized practice of law as determined by the courts of this state**."*

RCW 18.86 does not move the practice-of-law line, and it does not tell you where the line is. What it does
tell you is what to do when you reach it — **RCW 18.86.040(1)(c) and 18.86.050(1)(c)**:

> *"To **advise ... to seek expert advice on matters relating to the transaction that are beyond the agent's
> expertise**."*

**Completing a form and inventing a term are different acts.** Drafting a clause, construing a provision, or
advising whether a party has a claim or a right to terminate belongs to a lawyer — and sending the party
there is a **statutory duty**, not a courtesy.
