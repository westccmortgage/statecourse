## A. The sequence, from application to funding

1. **Pre-qualification** — an informal assessment on stated figures.
2. **Pre-approval** — a lender's assessment on documented figures, subject to conditions.
3. **Application.**
4. **Disclosure** — the lender issues the initial disclosures the federal rules require.
5. **Processing** — verification of income, assets, employment and credit.
6. **Appraisal** and **title work.**
7. **Underwriting** — the decision, usually with conditions.
8. **Clear to close.**
9. **Closing disclosure and the waiting period the federal rule imposes.**
10. **Signing, funding and recording.**

**A broker's value in that sequence is deadline management and honest expectation-setting**, not lending
advice. **A pre-qualification is not a pre-approval and neither is a commitment**, and a broker who presents
one as another has made a representation the file will not support.

## B. Underwriting — what is actually being assessed

### B.1 The borrower

**Credit history and score. Stability and adequacy of income. Assets sufficient to close and to reserve.
Existing recurring obligations. Occupancy intent.**

**Qualifying ratios** compare **housing expense to gross income** and **total recurring debt to gross
income.** **This course states no ratio figures**, because no agency handbook or lender guideline was read
for this project. What a student should carry away is the **structure**: there are generally **two ratios**,
and a buyer must satisfy **both** — passing one and failing the other is not qualifying.

**Worked example with the ratios supplied inside the problem.** *Gross monthly income $8,400; the programme
in this problem uses 28 percent housing and 36 percent total debt.*
Maximum housing expense = 8,400 × 0.28 = **$2,352.**
Maximum total recurring debt = 8,400 × 0.36 = **$3,024.**
If the buyer has $700 of car and student loan payments, the housing expense available under the debt ratio is
3,024 − 700 = **$2,324** — **less than the housing ratio allows.** **The binding constraint is whichever
ratio produces the smaller figure**, and here it is the debt ratio.

*(The 28 and 36 in that example are given inside the problem. They are not stated by this course as
Washington or federal requirements.)*

### B.2 The property

**Appraisal**, **title examination and insurance**, and — for some programmes — **property condition
standards.**

**Loan value is customarily the lower of contract price and appraised value.** Module WA-F-06 worked it.

## C. Truth in Lending — TILA and Regulation Z

**What it does:** requires disclosure of the cost of consumer credit and regulates the **advertising** of
consumer credit terms.

**The disclosure a student should recognise:** the **annual percentage rate (APR)**, which folds the cost of
credit — points and certain fees — into a single annualised rate. **The APR is not the interest rate**, and
it is normally higher. Two loans at the same note rate can carry different APRs, which is the entire reason
for requiring it.

**Advertising is where a broker meets Regulation Z**, because it reaches advertisements for consumer credit
**regardless of who places them.** Certain **trigger terms** in an advertisement pull in mandatory
disclosures.

> **The safest practice, and the one this course recommends: publish no numbers in an advertisement other
> than the price.** A listing that says *"$3,100 a month!"* has entered territory the regulation governs. A
> listing that says *"$589,000"* has not.

**This course states no trigger term list, no required disclosure and no penalty**, because Regulation Z was
not read for this project. A broker planning an advertisement with financing terms in it clears it with the
firm's designated broker and, where appropriate, with the lender.

## D. Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act — RESPA and Regulation X

**What it does:** governs settlement services on federally related mortgage loans for residential property,
requires certain disclosures, and **prohibits kickbacks and referral fees among settlement service
providers.**

### D.1 The referral fee prohibition, stated as a working rule

**A broker may not pay or receive a fee, kickback or thing of value for referring settlement service
business.** Escrow agents, title companies, lenders, appraisers, inspectors and home warranty companies are
settlement service providers.

**The practical form the problem takes** is rarely an envelope of cash. It is a marketing agreement priced
above the value of what is delivered, a "co-marketing" arrangement, a desk rental at less than market, or a
gift programme keyed to referral volume. **Every one of them is a question for the firm's designated broker
and for counsel, before it is entered into — not after.**

**And there is a Washington overlay that a broker must not lose sight of.** **RCW 18.86.080(2):** *"**An
agreement to pay or payment of compensation does not establish an agency relationship** between the party
who paid the compensation and the broker."* That is a Washington rule about **agency**, not about RESPA, and
the two answer different questions. Module WA-P-04 develops it.

**This course states no RESPA threshold, exemption, disclosure deadline or penalty**, because the statute and
regulation were not read for this project.

### D.2 Affiliated business arrangements

Where a broker refers business to an entity in which the broker or the firm has an interest, federal law
regulates the arrangement and requires disclosure. **The requirements were not read for this project and are
not stated here.** What a student should carry is the trigger: **an ownership or financial interest in a
settlement service provider is a disclosable fact and a compliance question**, and it is also, under **RCW
18.86.040(1)(b) and 18.86.050(1)(b)**, a **conflict of interest the broker must timely disclose to their
principal.**

## E. TRID — the consolidated disclosures

The **TILA-RESPA Integrated Disclosure** rule consolidated the duplicate disclosures the two statutes
required into two forms: the **Loan Estimate**, issued early in the process, and the **Closing Disclosure**,
issued before consummation.

**The rule imposes timing requirements on both**, and changes of certain kinds can restart a waiting period
and delay a closing.

**This course states no TRID deadline, tolerance category or triggering change**, because the rule was not
read for this project. **What a broker must know is that the timing exists and that it is not
negotiable** — so a change requested late in a transaction may move the closing date, and the person who can
say whether it will is **the lender.**

## F. Fair lending — ECOA in outline

The **Equal Credit Opportunity Act** prohibits discrimination in credit transactions on prohibited bases.
**ECOA and its implementing regulation were not read for this project**, and **this course states no
prohibited basis list from them.**

**Module WA-F-09 delivers the statutory three-hour fair housing and consumer protection block** required by
RCW 18.85.101(1)(c), and it is the module in which discrimination is addressed in substance. What belongs
here is the **link**: financing is one of the places housing discrimination happens, and a broker who
notices a pattern in how clients are treated by a lender has noticed something that matters.

## G. The broker's own position

**RCW 18.86.030(2):**

> *"Unless otherwise agreed, a broker **owes no duty to conduct an independent inspection of the property or
> to conduct an independent investigation of either party's financial condition**, and owes **no duty to
> independently verify the accuracy or completeness of any statement made by either party or by any source
> reasonably believed by the broker to be reliable**."*

**That is a real shelter and it has a real limit.**

**No duty to investigate the buyer's finances** — you need not audit a pre-approval letter.
**No duty to verify statements** — including a lender's, where the belief is reasonable.
**But no shelter for an affirmative statement of your own.** Repeating what the lender said, with
attribution, is reporting. Saying *"you're approved"* is a representation, and
**RCW 18.86.030(1)(b)** — deal honestly and in good faith — is owed to **all parties** and **may not be
waived.**

**And the duty to route is statutory: RCW 18.86.040(1)(c) and 18.86.050(1)(c).** Financing questions beyond
your expertise go to the lender. Legal questions go to counsel. Saying so is compliance, not weakness.
