# Washington Real Estate Fundamentals — Module WA-F-01

## The Washington licensing framework, and the rules governing this course

**Course:** Real Estate Fundamentals — one of the two courses **RCW 18.85.101(1)(c)** names.
**Instructional hours:** **5.0** of 60.0
**Clock hours:** a Washington clock hour is **50 minutes of actual instruction** — WAC 308-124H-820(4).
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No Washington clock hours are available.

> ### Four things about this course, stated before anything else
>
> **1. Washington licenses a BROKER, not a salesperson.** The entry-level licence is a **broker**,
> supervised by a **designated broker** or **managing broker** within a **real estate firm**. RCW
> 18.86.010(3): *"'Broker' means broker, managing broker, and designated broker, collectively."* This
> course uses Washington's words throughout, and a student who mentally substitutes "salesperson" will
> misread half the statute.
>
> **2. The statute fixes 90 hours and two courses; current DOL curricula fix 60/30.** RCW 18.85.101(1)(c)
> requires *"90 hours of instruction in real estate,"* and provides that instruction *"must include
> courses **as prescribed by the director** including **fundamentals**, which shall include **three hours
> of instruction on fair housing and consumer protection issues**, and **practices**."* DOL's current
> prescribed documents require **60 hours of Fundamentals** (Rev. 5/2022) and **30 hours of Practices**
> (effective 03-18-2009). StateCourse decides only the module-level allocation within those totals.
>
> **3. The Department prescribes the curriculum, and both current documents are now controlled.** WAC
> 308-124H-810 requires an approved school to *"**utilize the most recent course curriculum prescribed by
> the department**."* Both DOL PDFs were obtained and byte-locked on 2026-09-04. The inherited modules were
> originally written from **RCW 18.85, RCW 18.86 and WAC 308-124H** and have not yet passed objective-level
> mapping to those DOL documents. **Conforming them remains a submission gate.**
>
> **4. This course will carry a large examination, and the size is set by rule.** WAC
> 308-124H-820(8): courses of 30 clock hours or more must include a comprehensive examination of **no
> fewer than three questions per clock hour with a minimum of 90 questions**, and a **passing course grade
> of at least 70 percent.** At 60 clock hours, this course's examination must contain **at least 180
> questions.**

## Learning objectives

1. State the five statutory requirements for a Washington broker's licence.
2. State what a Washington clock hour is and how the two-year completion window runs.
3. State the examination requirements a course of 30 clock hours or more must satisfy.
4. Identify what a course may not be approved to teach for clock hours.
5. State what an approved school's certificate of course completion must contain.
6. Explain what a distance education course must demonstrate to be approved.
7. State the relationship between a broker, a firm, a designated broker and a managing broker.

## A. What RCW 18.85.101(1) requires of you

> **(a)** *"Is **18 years of age or older**";*
> **(b)** *"Has a **high school diploma or its equivalent**";*
> **(c)** *"has furnished proof ... that the applicant has **successfully completed 90 hours of instruction
> in real estate**. Instruction must include courses as prescribed by the director including
> **fundamentals**, which shall include **three hours of instruction on fair housing and consumer
> protection issues**, and **practices**. **Each course must be completed within two years before applying
> for the broker's license examination** and be **approved by the director**. The applicant must **pass a
> course examination, approved by the director, for each course** used to satisfy the requirement";*
> **(d)** *"Has **passed the broker's license examination**."*

### A.1 The two-year window, and where it is measured to

> *"Each course must be completed **within two years before applying for the broker's license
> examination**."*

**Two years, and the anchor is the application to sit the examination** — not the licence application and
not the date the licence issues. A course finished twenty-six months before you apply to test is a course
the statute does not count, however good it was.

This is a **short** window by national standards. Pennsylvania allows ten years; Washington allows two.
The practical consequence is that a Washington student should finish both courses and test promptly.

### A.2 A course examination for EACH course

> *"The applicant must **pass a course examination, approved by the director, for each course** used to
> satisfy the broker's license requirement."*

**Two courses, two course examinations** — and they are separate from **the broker's license examination**
in (d), which is the state test. Three examinations in total before licensure, and this course is
responsible for one of them.

### A.3 What comes after — recorded so you are not surprised

**RCW 18.85.101(2):** *"The education requirements for the **first renewal** of the broker's license must
include **90 hours** of courses as prescribed by the director, including **real estate law**, which shall
include **three hours of instruction on fair housing and consumer protection issues**, **advance
practices**, and **continuing education**."*

**Another 90 hours before your first renewal.** This course does not deliver them. It names them.

**RCW 18.85.101(3):** *"The broker is **licensed to one firm at a time** and is **supervised by a
designated or managing broker**."*

## B. What a Washington clock hour is — WAC 308-124H-820(4)

> *"Have a minimum of three hours of course work or instruction for the student. **A clock hour is a period
> of 50 minutes of actual instruction.**"*

**Fifty minutes of actual instruction.** So this 60-hour course is **3,000 minutes of instruction**, and the
90-hour programme is **4,500 minutes.**

And **WAC 308-124H-820(7)(h)**: *"**Clock hours shall not be awarded for any course time devoted to meals or
transportation.**"*

---

## C. The examination this course must carry — WAC 308-124H-820(8)

> *"**Courses of 30 clock hours or more which are submitted for approval must include a comprehensive
> examination(s) and answer key(s) of no fewer than three questions per clock hour with a minimum of 90
> questions, and a requirement of passing course grade of at least 70 percent**; essay question examination
> keys must identify the material to be tested and the points assigned for each question."*

Work the arithmetic, because it scales:

| Course | Clock hours | Minimum items | Passing grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Real Estate Fundamentals** | **60** | **180** | **70%** |
| **Real Estate Practices** | **30** | **90** | **70%** |

**Three questions per clock hour, with a floor of 90.** This is the strictest examination-size rule in this
project, because it is proportional: a longer course cannot carry a shorter test.

**The 70 percent is the director's figure**, not this school's. The in-module checks you will meet
throughout this course are **not** that examination — they gate progression, and their thresholds are
StateCourse standards.

## D. What a course may NOT be approved to teach — WAC 308-124H-820(7)

Eight exclusions, and the reasoning behind them is one test applied eight ways.

> **(a)** *"**Mechanical office and business skills**, such as, keyboarding, speed-reading, memory
> improvement, grammar, and report writing";*
> **(b)** *"**Standardized software programs** such as word processing, email, spreadsheets or databases";*
> **(c)** *"**Orientation courses for licensees**, such as those offered by trade associations";*
> **(d)** *"**Personal and sales motivation courses** or sales meetings held in conjunction with a
> licensee's general business";*
> **(e)** *"Courses ... designed or developed to **serve other professions**, unless each component of the
> curriculum and content **specifically shows how a real estate licensee can utilize the information in the
> practice of real estate**";*
> **(f)** *"**Personal finance, etiquette, or motivational type courses**";*
> **(g)** *"Courses ... designed to **promote or offer to sell specific products or services to real estate
> licensees** such as warranty programs, client/customer database systems, software programs or other
> devices";*
> **(h)** *"Clock hours shall not be awarded for any course time devoted to **meals or transportation**."*

### D.1 The rule's own example is the clearest statement of the test

> *"an example: **A course using spreadsheet program to demonstrate investment analysis would be
> acceptable, but a course teaching how to use a spreadsheet would not be acceptable**."*

**The subject must be real estate.** A tool is in scope only when it is being used on a real estate problem.
That single sentence resolves nearly every borderline case under (a), (b) and (g).

### D.2 And (g) contains a line worth noticing

> *"**Services or products can be offered during nonclock hour time, such as breaks or lunchtime.**
> **Letterhead, logos, company names or other similar markings by itself, on course material are not
> considered promotional.**"*

The rule is not squeamish about a provider having a name. What it prohibits is **spending clock hours
selling.**

## E. What else a course must satisfy — WAC 308-124H-820

> **(6)** *"Be under the supervision of an **instructor approved to teach the topic area**, who must, at a
> minimum, be **available to respond to specific questions from students on an immediate or reasonably
> delayed basis**";*
> **(9)** *"Include **textbook or instructional materials approved by the director**, which must be **kept
> accurate and current**";*
> **(10)** *"**Not have a title which misleads the public** as to the subject matter of the course";*
> **(11)** *"The provider's course application must **identify learning objectives and demonstrate how these
> are related to the practice of real estate**."*

**(9) is why a course cannot be written once and left alone.** Materials must be **kept accurate and
current** — an obligation that runs continuously, not at approval.

**And WAC 308-124H-810** adds a title rule with teeth: an approved school offering mandated training must
have **"real estate" in the course title** (except business management), and **may not use** the eight
prescribed core curricula phrases in other course titles — *real estate fundamentals; real estate brokerage
management; real estate law; advanced real estate law; business management; real estate practices; advanced
real estate practices; and Washington real estate fair housing.*

## F. Distance education — what this delivery method must prove

If you are taking this course at a distance, the rules below govern how it had to be built.

**WAC 308-124H-830** defines a distance delivery method as one where *"instruction takes place in **other
than a live classroom setting**, the instructor and the student are in **physically separate locations**,
and **interactive** instructional methods ... are used."*

**WAC 308-124H-835** then defines **interactive**, and the definition is a design requirement:

> *"**interactive** means the course structure and technologies promote **active student involvement** with
> the course content, including the ability to: **(a) access or bypass optional content**, if applicable;
> **(b) submit questions or answer test items, and receive direct feedback**; and **(c) communicate with the
> instructor and/or other students on an immediate or reasonably delayed basis.**"*
>
> *"**Interactive instruction specifically excludes courses that only provide passive delivery of
> instructional content.**"*

**WAC 308-124H-840** requires an application for **each separate delivery method**, demonstrating —
among other things — **learning units divided into modules**, **learning objectives for each**, **an
objective, quantitative criterion for mastery**, **a mechanism of individual remediation**, **measurement of
progress at regular intervals**, and, for computer-based instruction, *"the course software **must include
automatic shutdown after a period of inactivity**."* It also requires **reasonable security** so that the
student who receives credit is the student who enrolled — and *"**Both the approved school and the student
must certify in writing that the student has completed the course, and the required number of clock
hours.**"*

**WAC 308-124H-845** provides one shortcut: an applicant with **ARELLO certification** of the delivery
method need not apply separately, *"**provided a clock hour enforcement mechanism is in place**."*

**No ARELLO certification and no delivery-method approval is held for this build. Submission gate.**

## G. Your certificate — WAC 308-124H-855

An approved school **shall issue a certificate of course completion** to students who have satisfactorily
completed the course requirements, containing **nine** items:

1. **Student's name**
2. **School's name and identification number issued by the department**
3. **The course commencement date and completion date**
4. **Course title**
5. **Clock hours for the course**
6. **School administrator's signature**
7. **Course identification number issued by the department**
8. **Instructor name and number**
9. **Completion of a required examination, if applicable**

**Two department-issued numbers appear on it** — the school's and the course's. **Without approval there are
no such numbers**, and so no conforming certificate can issue. That is why this build cannot yet award
clock hours, and says so on every page.

**Keep the certificate.** RCW 18.85.101(1)(c)'s two-year window is measured against the **completion date**
printed on it.

## H. How Washington structures a brokerage — the vocabulary you will need throughout

- **Real estate firm** — the licensed entity. Brokers are licensed **with** a firm.
- **Designated broker** — the person responsible for the firm and for supervising the brokers licensed with
  it.
- **Managing broker** — a broker to whom supervisory duties may be delegated.
- **Broker** — the entry-level licence, and the person doing most of the work in most transactions.

**RCW 18.85.101(3)** puts it plainly: *"The broker is **licensed to one firm at a time** and is **supervised
by a designated or managing broker**."*

This matters more in Washington than the equivalent structure does elsewhere, because **RCW 18.86 assigns
duties to the FIRM and to the individual broker differently.** The companion course develops it. Two
previews:

- **RCW 18.86.020(2)(a):** *"**A firm must enter into a services agreement with the principal**"* — the
  agreement is between the **firm** and the principal, and the firm **appoints** a broker as agent.
- **RCW 18.86.060(5):** where different brokers **in the same firm** represent different parties, *"the
  firm's **designated broker, and any managing broker responsible for the supervision of both brokers, is a
  limited dual agent**"* — while *"each appointed broker shall **solely represent** the party with whom the
  appointed broker has an agency relationship."*

**Supervision has legal consequences in Washington, not merely administrative ones.**

## I. And the sentence that will govern everything in the companion course

**RCW 18.86.110:**

> *"**The duties under this chapter are statutory duties and not fiduciary duties. This chapter supersedes
> the fiduciary duties of an agent to a principal under the common law.** The common law continues to apply
> to the parties in all other respects. ... **This chapter shall be construed broadly.**"*

Washington kept the word **agent** and replaced what stands behind it. A student who arrives with a national
textbook's account of fiduciary duty — loyalty, obedience, disclosure, confidentiality, accounting,
reasonable care as **common-law fiduciary** obligations — has an account **Washington superseded by
statute.**

What remains is **the list in RCW 18.86.030**, owed to **all parties**, plus the relationship-specific lists
in **.040, .050 and .060** — and nothing else. Module WA-P-01 takes it up.
