## A. Vocabulary, with the arithmetic attached

**Principal and interest. Amortization. Debt service. PITI. Equity. Loan-to-value ratio. Points. Yield.**

**PITI** is the four-part housing payment — principal, interest, taxes, insurance — and it is what
qualifying ratios are computed against, not principal and interest alone.

**Equity** is value less what is owed. **Loan-to-value** is the loan divided by the value the lender
recognises, and lenders customarily recognise **the lower of the contract price and the appraised value.**
That convention is why an appraisal below contract price is a problem for the buyer: **the lender lends
against the lower figure and the difference must come from somewhere.**

**A discount point is one percent of the loan amount**, paid at closing, buying the lender yield. A common
rule of thumb in national content is that a point moves yield by roughly **one-eighth of one percent** — an
approximation, not a formula.

### A.1 Worked examples, all figures given inside the problem

**Points.** A $415,000 loan with 1.5 points: 415,000 × 0.015 = **$6,225** paid at closing.

**First month's split.** A $415,000 balance at 6.25 percent with a monthly payment of $2,555:
interest = 415,000 × 0.0625 ÷ 12 = **$2,161.46**; principal reduction = 2,555 − 2,161.46 = **$393.54**;
new balance = **$414,606.46.**

**Interest is always computed on the previous balance**, and only the remainder touches principal. That is
why early payments on a thirty-year amortisation barely move the balance.

**Loan-to-value.** Contract price $520,000, appraised value $505,000, loan $404,000. The lender recognises
**$505,000** — the lower — so LTV = 404,000 ÷ 505,000 = **80 percent**, and the buyer covers the $15,000
gap from their own funds.

## B. The note and the security instrument — two documents, two jobs

**The promissory note** is the borrower's **promise to repay**. It is a contract, and everything in Module
WA-F-05 applies to it.

**The security instrument** **pledges the property**. **Hypothecation** is the idea that ties them: the
borrower pledges the property **while keeping possession of it.** Nothing about a real estate loan moves the
borrower out.

### B.1 Deed of trust versus mortgage — and which Washington uses

| | **Mortgage** | **Deed of trust — Washington's dominant instrument** |
|---|---|---|
| Parties | **2** | **3** |
| Who they are | Mortgagor, Mortgagee | **Grantor, Trustee, Beneficiary** |
| Foreclosure | **Judicial** | **Non-judicial power of sale**, conducted by the trustee |

**The third party is what makes the shortcut possible.** A **trustee** holds the security for the
beneficiary's benefit and can conduct a sale without a lawsuit.

**Washington's Deeds of Trust Act is RCW 61.24, and it was not read for this project.** **This course states
no notice period, no timeline, no reinstatement right, no redemption right and no deficiency rule.** A
broker asked about a foreclosure timeline routes the question — to counsel, and to the trustee's own
notices.

**And a caution for a broker working with a distressed seller.** Whether a short sale is possible, what the
lender will accept, and what happens to any deficiency are **legal and lender questions.** A broker who
negotiates a workout with a lender on a borrower's behalf has stepped outside brokerage, and **RCW 18.86.110**
expressly leaves the practice-of-law boundary where the courts put it.

### B.2 Special note provisions

- **Acceleration clause** — on default the **whole balance** becomes due at once.
- **Prepayment penalty clause** — a charge for paying early.
- **Due-on-sale (alienation) clause** — the balance becomes due if the property is sold.

**The due-on-sale clause is why assumption is rare.** Where the lender may call the loan on transfer, a
buyer taking over the seller's existing financing is the exception, not the routine.

### B.3 Subordinate financing and priority

**First**, **second** and further liens rank by **recording**, subject to statutory priorities such as
property tax liens. **Subordination** is the deliberate exception: a lender agreeing to sit behind one who
would otherwise sit behind it.

**And the reconveyance matters.** When a deed of trust is paid, the trustee reconveys, and **a deed of trust
paid off but never reconveyed of record is still a cloud on title.** Module WA-F-04 met the same idea from
the title side.

## C. Loan programmes

- **Conventional** — not government insured or guaranteed. **Conforming** loans meet secondary-market
  standards; **nonconforming** loans do not and are priced accordingly. **Private mortgage insurance (PMI)**
  typically appears at higher loan-to-value.
- **FHA-insured** — FHA **does not build housing and does not lend**; it **insures approved lenders against
  loss.** Borrowers pay a **mortgage insurance premium (MIP)**.
- **VA-guaranteed** — the VA **guarantees** approved lenders against loss for eligible veterans; a **funding
  fee** applies; the defining feature is the possibility of **no down payment.** A **VA release of liability
  does not by itself release the original borrower from liability to the lender** — that takes the lender's
  own release.
- **USDA Rural Development** — direct and guaranteed loans in eligible areas.
- **Washington State Housing Finance Commission programmes** exist for eligible buyers. **No Commission
  programme document was read for this project**, and **no eligibility limit, income cap, purchase price
  limit or assistance amount is stated in this course.** They are named so a broker knows to ask a lender.

**This course states no loan limit, down payment percentage, MIP or funding fee rate, or qualifying ratio**,
because no agency handbook or programme guide was read. Those figures change, and they come from the lender.

## D. Repayment types

**Fixed-rate level payment (fully amortized)** — the most common residential plan.

**Adjustable rate (ARM)** — with an **index**, a **margin**, a **periodic cap** and a **lifetime cap**. Note
carefully: **a cap on the PAYMENT is not a cap on the INTEREST.** Where a payment cap holds the payment
below the interest accruing, the shortfall is added to principal — **negative amortization** — and the
balance grows.

**Interest-only / term. Balloon (partially amortized). Graduated payment. Growing equity. Buydown**, where a
lump sum at closing reduces the rate temporarily or permanently and may be paid by buyer, seller or third
party.

**By purpose:** **construction**, **blanket** (with a **partial release** clause), **package**, **open-end**,
and **bridge** loans.

**Seller financing** — a **real estate contract** or a **purchase money deed of trust**. Washington has its
own history and its own law here, and **none of it was read for this project.** A broker whose transaction
involves seller financing routes it to counsel, without exception.

## E. Primary and secondary markets

The **primary market** is where a loan is **made** — banks, credit unions, savings institutions, mortgage
banking companies and mortgage brokers. The **secondary market** is where a loan already made is **bought**
— **Fannie Mae**, **Freddie Mac** and **Ginnie Mae** — through **loan pooling** and **mortgage-backed
securities.**

**The link is conforming standards.** A lender writes to those standards **so the loan can be sold**, and
selling it returns capital to lend again. That is why a nonconforming loan is priced differently: the lender
may have to keep it.

**The Federal Reserve** affects mortgage rates and the availability of funds through monetary policy. It
does not set mortgage rates directly, and a broker who tells a client otherwise has misstated it.

## F. What a broker may and may not say about financing

**RCW 18.86.030(2)** removes any duty *"to conduct an independent investigation of either party's financial
condition"* and any duty *"to independently verify the accuracy or completeness of any statement made by
either party or by any source reasonably believed by the broker to be reliable."*

**But no shelter permits an affirmative statement you cannot support.** *"You'll qualify."* *"That'll
appraise."* *"Rates are coming down before your lock expires."* Each is a **representation**, and
**RCW 18.86.030(1)(b)** — deal honestly and in good faith, owed to all parties, unwaivable — is what it will
be measured against.

**The reliable formulation is three words: ask your lender.** And where the question is beyond you,
**RCW 18.86.040(1)(c) and 18.86.050(1)(c)** make sending the client there a duty.
