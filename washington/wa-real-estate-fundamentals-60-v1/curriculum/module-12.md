## Learning objectives

1. Compute area, acreage and cost per unit.
2. Compute commissions, splits and net-to-seller — including the price needed to net a required sum.
3. Compute property tax from a given rate and assessed value.
4. Compute excise tax from a given rate.
5. Compute prorations by both the 360-day and 365-day conventions.
6. Read a settlement statement and place an entry as a debit or a credit to the correct party.
7. Compute the bottom-line figures — cash to close and net to seller.
8. State the boundary between compliance information and tax advice.

## A. Area and unit costs

**Constants worth memorising:** **1 acre = 43,560 square feet.** **1 section = 640 acres = 1 square mile.**
**1 township = 36 sections.** **1 square yard = 9 square feet.**

**Areas:** rectangle = **base × height**; triangle = **½ × base × height**; trapezoid =
**½ × (base₁ + base₂) × height**. An irregular parcel with straight sides is **decomposed** into those shapes,
each computed and added — not estimated.

**Worked example.** *A lot is a rectangle 120 ft × 210 ft with a right-triangular extension 120 ft × 60 ft.*
Rectangle = 120 × 210 = **25,200 sq ft.** Triangle = ½ × 120 × 60 = **3,600 sq ft.**
Total = **28,800 sq ft** = 28,800 ÷ 43,560 = **0.661 acres.**
At **$14.50 per square foot**, the land is 28,800 × 14.50 = **$417,600.**

## B. Commissions and net to seller

**This course states no commission rate, split or minimum.** Compensation is negotiated between a firm and
its principal — and **RCW 18.86.080(7)(a)(i)** requires the services agreement to state **the amount the
principal agrees to compensate the firm.** Every problem below supplies its rate.

### B.1 A straightforward commission

*Sale price $685,000; the seller's services agreement provides 5.5 percent to the listing firm, which shares
half with the selling firm; the listing broker's own split with the firm is 60/40 in the broker's favour.*

Total to listing firm = 685,000 × 0.055 = **$37,675.**
Shared with selling firm = 37,675 ÷ 2 = **$18,837.50** each.
Listing broker's share = 18,837.50 × 0.60 = **$11,302.50.**

### B.2 Net to seller from a known price

*Sale price $685,000; commission 5.5 percent; excise tax at a combined rate of 1.78 percent given in this
problem; payoff $412,000; other closing costs $3,400.*

Commission = **$37,675.** Excise tax = 685,000 × 0.0178 = **$12,193.**
Net = 685,000 − 37,675 − 12,193 − 412,000 − 3,400 = **$219,732.**

### B.3 The price needed to NET a required sum — the one that catches people

This is **algebra, not arithmetic**, because the commission is a percentage **of the unknown price.**

*A seller must net $250,000 after a 5.5 percent commission and $4,900 of other costs, with no loan payoff and
excise tax ignored for this problem.*

Let **P** be the price. Then **P − 0.055P − 4,900 = 250,000**, so **0.945P = 254,900**, and
**P = 254,900 ÷ 0.945 = $269,735.45.**

**Check it:** 269,735.45 × 0.055 = 14,835.45; 269,735.45 − 14,835.45 − 4,900 = **$250,000.** ✓

**The classic error is to add 5.5 percent to $254,900**, which gives $268,919.50 — short by more than $800.
**Adding a percentage to the net is not the same as taking a percentage from the price.**

## C. Property tax

Washington property tax is levied by multiple taxing districts against **assessed value**, and rates are
customarily expressed **per $1,000 of assessed value**.

**Worked example, rate given.** *Assessed value $624,000; the combined levy rate in this problem is
$8.9412 per $1,000.*

Annual tax = (624,000 ÷ 1,000) × 8.9412 = 624 × 8.9412 = **$5,579.31.**

**The national section of a licensing examination may use a MILL RATE instead.** **One mill is one-thousandth
of a dollar — $1 per $1,000 of assessed value.** So a rate of **8.9412 mills** is the same figure as **$8.9412
per $1,000.** A student should be able to work either way and to recognise that they are the same thing said
twice.

**This course states no Washington levy rate, assessment ratio, revaluation cycle, exemption or deferral
programme**, because Washington's property tax statutes were not read for this project. **Assessed value and
the levy rate come from the county assessor.**

## D. Real estate excise tax

Washington imposes a **real estate excise tax** on the sale of real property. It appears on every Washington
closing statement, and it is customarily the **seller's** charge unless the agreement provides otherwise.

**RCW 82.45 and every local excise tax ordinance were not read for this project.** **This course states no
state rate, no local rate, no bracket, no threshold and no exemption.** Practice problems supply the combined
rate as a given, and **instructors substitute the current statutory and local rates before delivery.**

**Worked example, rate given.** *Sale price $585,000; combined state and local excise tax rate for this
problem 1.78 percent.* Excise tax = 585,000 × 0.0178 = **$10,413.**

> **Instructor note and submission gate.** Washington's excise tax is **graduated** in structure, which means
> a single flat rate is a **simplification used here for teaching arithmetic only.** Before delivery, replace
> these problems with the current statutory brackets and local additions, and add at least one problem that
> requires the student to apply more than one bracket. **Obtaining RCW 82.45 is a submission gate for this
> module.**

## E. Prorations

Items paid in advance or in arrears are divided between the parties as of closing: **property taxes**,
**assessments**, **homeowner association dues**, **rents and deposits** on tenanted property, **interest**,
and **fuel** remaining.

**Two conventions, and every problem must say which it uses:**

- **365-day method** — actual days. Most common in practice.
- **360-day / 30-day-month method** — twelve 30-day months. Common on licensing examinations.

**Two rules that never change:**

1. **A proration is always a DOUBLE ENTRY.** Whatever is credited to one party is debited to the other. **A
   prorated item appearing once has been done wrong.**
2. **Whichever party "owns" the day of closing must be stated.** **This course states no Washington rule on
   which party owns it**, because none was found in the statutes and rules read; it is a matter of contract
   and local practice, and **every problem here states it inside the problem.**

### E.1 Worked example — taxes paid in advance, 360-day method

*Annual property tax $5,760, paid in full by the seller for the calendar year. Closing 16 September. The
360-day/30-day method applies and the SELLER owns the day of closing.*

Daily rate = 5,760 ÷ 360 = **$16.00.** Monthly rate = **$480.**
Seller's period = 1 January through 16 September = 8 full months + 16 days = (8 × 480) + (16 × 16) =
3,840 + 256 = **$4,096.**
Buyer's share = 5,760 − 4,096 = **$1,664.**

**The seller prepaid the whole year, so the buyer reimburses the seller for the buyer's portion:**
**credit the seller $1,664 and debit the buyer $1,664.** Double entry. ✓

### E.2 Worked example — rent collected in advance, 365-day method

*A tenant paid $2,450 for September on 1 September. Closing 12 September, 30 days in the month. The SELLER
owns the day of closing.*

Daily rate = 2,450 ÷ 30 = **$81.6667.**
Seller's days = 1 through 12 = **12 days** = **$980.00.**
Buyer's days = 13 through 30 = **18 days** = **$1,470.00.**

**The seller holds money belonging to the buyer's period: debit the seller $1,470 and credit the buyer
$1,470.** Double entry. ✓

**Notice the direction reverses between the two examples**, and the reason is the same in both: **follow who
paid and who benefits.** Prepaid by the seller for a period the buyer will own → the buyer reimburses.
Collected by the seller for a period the buyer will own → the seller hands it over.

## F. The settlement statement

### F.1 Debits and credits, from first principles

A **debit** is an amount a party **owes**. A **credit** is an amount a party **has coming**.

For the **buyer**: the **purchase price is a debit** — it is what they owe. The **earnest money** and the
**loan amount** are **credits** — money already in or arriving on their behalf.

For the **seller**: the **purchase price is a credit** — it is what they have coming. The **loan payoff**,
the **commission** and the **excise tax** are **debits.**

**Cash to close (buyer) = buyer's total debits − buyer's total credits.**
**Net to seller = seller's total credits − seller's total debits.**

### F.2 Worked example — a complete transaction, all figures given

*Sale price $585,000. Earnest money $10,000 held in escrow. New loan $468,000. Buyer's loan origination and
other lender charges $6,240. Buyer's title, escrow and recording charges $2,180. Seller's commission 5.5
percent. Excise tax at 1.78 percent (seller). Seller's loan payoff $301,500. Seller's escrow and other
charges $1,150. Property tax of $6,000 for the year was paid in full by the seller; closing 1 October;
360-day method; the SELLER owns the day of closing.*

**Tax proration.** Daily = 6,000 ÷ 360 = $16.667; monthly = $500.
Seller's period = 1 January through 1 October = 9 months + 1 day = 4,500 + 16.67 = **$4,516.67.**
Buyer's share = 6,000 − 4,516.67 = **$1,483.33** → **credit seller, debit buyer.**

**Buyer's side.**
Debits: price 585,000 + lender charges 6,240 + title/escrow/recording 2,180 + tax proration 1,483.33 =
**$594,903.33.**
Credits: earnest money 10,000 + loan 468,000 = **$478,000.**
**Cash to close = 594,903.33 − 478,000 = $116,903.33.**

**Seller's side.**
Credits: price 585,000 + tax proration 1,483.33 = **$586,483.33.**
Debits: commission (585,000 × 0.055) 32,175 + excise tax (585,000 × 0.0178) 10,413 + payoff 301,500 +
other 1,150 = **$345,238.**
**Net to seller = 586,483.33 − 345,238 = $241,245.33.**

**Check the double entry:** the tax proration appears **once on each side** — a credit to the seller and a
debit to the buyer, both $1,483.33. ✓

### F.3 What a broker does with a settlement statement

**Review it.** Confirm the price, the deposit, the commission and the prorations match the agreement.
**Ask about anything that does not.** **Do not opine on the legal effect of an entry**, and **do not tell a
client what the tax consequences of the transaction will be.**

**RCW 18.86.030(1)(e)** — owed to all parties, unwaivable:

> *"To **account in a timely manner for all money and property received from or on behalf of either
> party**."*

**And RCW 18.86.040(1)(c) / 18.86.050(1)(c)** make routing the rest a statutory duty.

## G. Taxation of ownership — the boundary, stated plainly

A student will be asked about **the exclusion of gain on a principal residence**, the **deductibility of
interest and property taxes**, **depreciation** on investment property, **exchanges of like-kind property**,
and **capital gains treatment.**

**The Internal Revenue Code and Washington's tax statutes were not read for this project.** **This course
states no figure, holding period, limit, exclusion, rate or eligibility requirement for any of them.**

**And the reason is not only that they were not read.** **Tax advice is outside brokerage.** A broker may say
that a transaction has tax consequences and that the client should speak to a tax professional **before**
committing — that is compliance information and it is useful. A broker who says *"you won't owe anything,
you've lived there two years"* has given tax advice on figures they did not verify, in an area where the rules
change.

**RCW 18.86.040(1)(c) and 18.86.050(1)(c) make the referral a duty**, and **RCW 18.86.110** leaves the
practice-of-law boundary exactly where the courts put it.
