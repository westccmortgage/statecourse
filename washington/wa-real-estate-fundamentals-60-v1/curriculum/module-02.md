## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish real from personal property and apply the fixture tests.
2. Identify the bundle of rights and the government powers that limit it.
3. Distinguish freehold from leasehold estates.
4. Distinguish the forms of concurrent ownership, including Washington's community property character.
5. Distinguish liens from non-lien encumbrances.
6. Connect each of these to the broker's statutory duty to route — RCW 18.86.040(1)(c).

## A. Real and personal property

**Land** is the surface, what lies beneath, and the airspace above. **Real property** is land plus what is
permanently attached plus the rights running with it. **Personal property** is everything else.

The distinction decides what conveys. A deed carries the real property. It does not carry the washing
machine unless the agreement says so.

### A.1 Fixtures

An article of personal property that has become real property by attachment. The tests, in order:

1. **Method of annexation** — how firmly attached, and what damage removal would cause.
2. **Adaptation** — was it made or fitted for this property specifically.
3. **Relationship of the parties** — a tenant's attachment reads differently from an owner's.
4. **Intention** — usually controlling, read from the objective circumstances rather than from what
   somebody now says they meant.

**The practical answer is not the doctrine.** Name in writing what stays and what goes, item by item,
before anyone signs. Every fixture dispute a broker ever sees was preventable by a sentence.

### A.2 Trade fixtures and emblements

**Trade fixtures** are articles a **tenant** attaches for a **business** — shelving, coolers, display
counters. They remain the tenant's and are removable **before the term ends**, after which they generally
pass to the landlord by **accession**. The lease can change the default.

**Emblements** are annual crops produced by cultivation; a tenant farmer generally retains the right to
harvest what was planted before the tenancy ended.

## B. The bundle of rights, and what limits it

Ownership is a **bundle** because the sticks can be sold separately: **possession**, **use**,
**exclusion**, **disposition**, and **encumbrance**.

A landowner who grants a utility easement has sold a stick. One who leases has transferred possession and
kept the rest. One who grants a deed of trust has pledged, not conveyed.

### B.1 The four government powers

- **Police power** — regulation for public health, safety and welfare: zoning, building codes, subdivision
  regulation, environmental rules. **No compensation**, because nothing is taken.
- **Eminent domain** — taking for public use, **with just compensation**, through **condemnation**.
- **Taxation** — levy against real property.
- **Escheat** — property reverts to the state when an owner dies with no heirs and no will.

**The compensation line is the distinction to hold: police power regulates without paying; eminent domain
takes and pays.**

### B.2 Appurtenant rights, and a Washington-specific caution

Rights running with the land: **air rights**, **subsurface and mineral rights**, **water rights**, and
**appurtenant easements**.

**Water rights deserve their own sentence in Washington.** Much of the Commonwealth's agricultural and
rural land turns on water availability and on the right to use it, and the governing law is a body of
Washington statute and administrative practice **this project did not read.** A broker who reassures a
buyer that a rural parcel "has water" has made a representation they cannot support. **The question goes to
the Department of Ecology, to a water rights professional, and to counsel** — which is exactly what RCW
18.86.040(1)(c) and 18.86.050(1)(c) require:

> *"To **advise the [principal] to seek expert advice on matters relating to the transaction that are
> beyond the agent's expertise**."*

**That is a statutory duty in Washington, not a courtesy.**

## C. Estates in land

### C.1 Freehold

**Fee simple absolute** — the largest estate: perpetual, inheritable, freely transferable, subject to
government powers and to whatever encumbers it.

**Fee simple defeasible** — a fee that can end on a stated condition:

- **Determinable** — ends **automatically**, with a possibility of reverter. Language of duration: *so long
  as*, *while*, *during*.
- **Subject to a condition subsequent** — the grantor holds a **right of re-entry** and must act. Language
  of condition: *but if*, *provided that*, *on condition that*.

The difference is whether title returns by itself or must be taken back, and it turns on words in a deed a
lawyer drafted. **A broker's job is to notice the language and route it**, not to construe it.

**Life estate** — measured by a life. The **life tenant** may possess and use and must avoid **waste**.
When the measuring life ends the estate passes to the **remainderman** or **reverts**. A life estate **pur
autre vie** is measured by a life other than the holder's.

### C.2 Leasehold

| Estate | How it ends |
|---|---|
| **Estate for years** | Definite beginning, definite end. **Terminates on its own terms; no notice required.** |
| **Periodic tenancy** | Renews period to period **until proper notice**. |
| **Tenancy at will** | Continues at the pleasure of both parties. |
| **Tenancy at sufferance** | A holdover the landlord never agreed to. |

**Notice periods for a Washington periodic tenancy are set by the Residential Landlord-Tenant Act, RCW
59.18, which this project did not read. No notice period is stated in this course.**

### C.3 A lease is two things at once

A **conveyance** of a possessory interest for a term, **and a contract** of covenants. That dual nature is
why a lease dispute can be framed as a breach of contract or as an interference with possession, and why
the **covenant of quiet enjoyment** matters as much as any written clause.

**Assignment** transfers the tenant's **entire** remaining interest; a **sublease** transfers **less than
all** of it. **In both cases the original tenant generally remains liable unless expressly released** — and
tenants routinely assume otherwise.

## D. Concurrent ownership — and Washington's own character

**Tenancy in common.** Undivided interests that may be **unequal**, **no survivorship**, each co-tenant may
generally transfer or encumber their own interest and seek **partition**.

**Joint tenancy.** Requires the four unities — **possession, interest, time, title** — with a **right of
survivorship**. Severing a unity converts the severed share to a tenancy in common.

### D.1 Washington is a community property state

**Washington is one of a minority of states in which property acquired during marriage is presumptively
community property.** That single fact changes several practical questions a broker meets weekly:

- **Who must sign.** A listing or a deed signed by one spouse may not convey or bind what both own.
- **What the title report shows**, and whether a separate-property characterisation is documented.
- **How the estate passes** on death, and what a will can and cannot do with it.

> **What this course does NOT state.** **Washington's community property statutes were not read for this
> project.** This module therefore states **no rule about what is community property, what is separate
> property, how either is characterised, how it may be conveyed, or how it passes at death.** It states
> **that Washington is a community property state**, which is the fact a broker must know in order to ask
> the right question — and it routes the answer to **the title company and to counsel.**
>
> This is not evasion. A broker who guesses at community property characterisation has guessed at whether
> the seller can convey.

**Ownership in trust, by entity, and in common-interest communities** — condominium and planned community
forms — are further structures. **RCW 64.34 and RCW 64.90 were not obtained by this project**, and no
provision of either is stated here.

### D.2 Why the deed matters at a listing appointment

The names on a deed determine **who must sign**. Read the deed. Ask who is on title. Ask whether the
sellers are married and when the property was acquired. Where the answer is not obvious, **route it** —
RCW 18.86.040(1)(c).

## E. Encumbrances

### E.1 Liens — money claims

- **Deed of trust** — Washington's dominant security instrument, and a **three-party** arrangement
  (grantor, trustee, beneficiary) rather than a two-party mortgage. **RCW 61.24, the Deeds of Trust Act,
  was not read for this project**, and no foreclosure procedure, notice period or timeline is stated in
  this course.
- **Property tax lien** — statutory, involuntary, and generally superior in priority.
- **Mechanics' and materialmen's lien** — for work or materials. **Washington's lien statute was not read;
  no filing deadline, threshold or priority rule is stated.**
- **Judgment lien**, and **special assessments** for public improvements.

**General liens** attach to all a debtor's property; **specific liens** to identified property.
**Voluntary** liens are granted; **involuntary** liens arise by law.

### E.2 Non-lien encumbrances — use claims

- **Easement** — an interest in land. **Appurtenant** easements benefit a dominant parcel and run with the
  land; easements **in gross** benefit a person or entity.
- **Licence** — revocable permission, personal, **not** an interest in land.
- **Encroachment** — a physical intrusion across a boundary. Found by a **survey**, not by a fence.
- **Restrictive covenant** — a private limitation running with the land.

**A restrictive covenant is private and enforced privately; zoning is public and enforced by the
municipality.** Where the two differ, **the more restrictive controls what the owner may actually do** — and
a broker who checked only the zoning has checked half of it.

## F. Where this module stops

Everything above is vocabulary, and **none of it authorises a broker to give an opinion.**

**RCW 18.86.030(2)** states the limit from one side:

> *"Unless otherwise agreed, a broker **owes no duty to conduct an independent inspection** of the property
> or to conduct an independent investigation of either party's financial condition, and owes **no duty to
> independently verify the accuracy or completeness of any statement** made by either party or by any
> source reasonably believed by the broker to be reliable."*

**RCW 18.86.040(1)(c) and 18.86.050(1)(c)** state the duty from the other:

> *"To **advise ... to seek expert advice on matters relating to the transaction that are beyond the agent's
> expertise**."*

**Together they define the job.** You need not investigate. You must recognise the question and send it
where it is answered. The vocabulary in this module is what lets you recognise it.
