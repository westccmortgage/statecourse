## Learning objectives

1. State the structure of fair housing law in Washington — federal, state and local — and which controls.
2. Identify the prohibited practices by their conduct, not by their labels alone.
3. Recognise steering, and recognise the requests that invite it.
4. Distinguish a reasonable accommodation from a reasonable modification.
5. State the consumer protection duties RCW 18.86 imposes on every broker, owed to all parties.
6. Apply the material fact definition and its negative list in RCW 18.86.010(11).

## Part 1 — Fair housing

### A. Three layers, and how they interact

**Federal law** sets a floor. **Washington's own antidiscrimination law — the Washington Law Against
Discrimination, RCW 49.60 — adds to it.** **Local ordinances** in some Washington cities and counties add
further.

**The working rule is simple and it is the safe one: where the layers differ, the MOST PROTECTIVE applies.**
A broker who reasons only from the federal list has reasoned from the shortest one.

> **What this course does not state.** **The federal protected class list, the RCW 49.60 list, and every
> local list were not read for this project, and none is stated here.** This is deliberate: a fair housing
> list that is out of date or incomplete is worse than no list, because a broker will rely on it.
>
> **What a broker must do instead is know that the lists differ, that Washington's is broader than the
> federal one, and that local ordinances may be broader still — and then read the current text**, or ask the
> firm's designated broker, before acting on any characteristic at all.
>
> **The safest operating posture, and the one this course teaches: do not screen, sort, steer, describe or
> decide on ANY personal characteristic of a person.** A broker who never uses a characteristic never needs
> the list.

### B. The prohibited practices, taught as conduct

Learn these by **what the broker does**, because that is how they occur.

**Refusal to deal.** Refusing to sell, rent, negotiate with, or show property to a person. It includes
**failing to return calls**, **not sending listings**, and **being unavailable** — a refusal does not have to
be spoken.

**Different terms or conditions.** A different deposit, a different fee, different showing hours, a different
level of service.

**Discriminatory advertising.** Any statement, in any medium, indicating a preference, limitation or
discrimination. **This reaches photographs, captions, hashtags and the description of a neighbourhood's
character**, not only explicit statements.

**Steering.** Directing prospects toward or away from areas on the basis of a personal characteristic —
**including by what is shown, what is described, and what is left out.** Steering by omission is still
steering.

**Blockbusting.** Inducing sales or rentals by representing that persons of a particular characteristic are
entering the neighbourhood.

**Redlining.** Refusing to lend, insure or serve in defined geographic areas.

**Discrimination in services and facilities**, and **retaliation or interference** against a person for
exercising a fair housing right or assisting another to.

### C. Steering, in the form a broker actually meets it

The request almost never sounds like discrimination. It sounds like this:

- *"What kind of people live there?"*
- *"Is it a good neighbourhood for a family like ours?"*
- *"Where would you live if you were us?"*
- *"Which schools are the good ones?"*

**Answering any of them from your own characterisation is steering, whoever asked.** The request coming from
the consumer does not make the answer lawful.

**The reliable response has three parts:**

1. **Say what you can and cannot do**, plainly and without apology: *"I can't characterise neighbourhoods by
   who lives in them — fair housing law prohibits it, and it would be a disservice to you anyway."*
2. **Route the objective questions to objective sources.** School performance data, crime statistics,
   commute times, walkability and demographic data are all published. **Send the client to the source**;
   do not summarise it in your own words.
3. **Ask what they actually need**, in terms of the property and its features. Almost always the underlying
   question is about **something you can lawfully answer** — lot size, schools' published ratings, distance
   to work, noise, whether it floods.

**And notice the fourth question above.** *"Which schools are the good ones?"* is not only a steering risk,
it is an **opinion presented as fact.** Route it.

### D. Disability — accommodation and modification

**A reasonable accommodation** is a change in **rules, policies, practices or services** — waiving a no-pets
policy for an assistance animal, assigning a closer parking space, permitting a different payment date.

**A reasonable modification** is a **physical change to the premises** — a grab bar, a ramp, a widened
doorway.

**The distinction is what is changed: a RULE or the PREMISES.** Who bears the cost differs between the two,
and **the specific rules were not read for this project and are not stated here.**

**An assistance animal is not a pet.** A no-pets policy is not a lawful ground to refuse one, and a pet
deposit is not a lawful charge for one. **The verification a housing provider may lawfully request is
governed by rules this project did not read** — the question goes to counsel and to the firm's designated
broker, and it goes there **before** a refusal, not after.

### E. Advertising, and where the risk actually lives

**Every advertising rule in fair housing law reaches every medium** — print, signage, listing services,
websites, email, text and social media.

**Two habits carry a broker through most of it:**

**Describe the property, not the people.** *"Three bedrooms, level lot, quiet street"* describes property.
*"Perfect for a young family"* describes people, and it names a characteristic.

**Watch the images and the incidental content as closely as the words.** Photographs, captions, the account
you repost from, and the audience you target an advertisement to are all part of the advertisement.

*(Module WA-P-05 develops advertising in the Washington statutory context, including **RCW 18.86.130**, the
2026 exclusive-marketing statute. **WAC 308-124's advertising rules were not read for this project** and are
stated nowhere in this build.)*

## Part 2 — Consumer protection

### F. The duties RCW 18.86.030 owes to EVERYONE

This is where fair housing and consumer protection meet in Washington, because **RCW 18.86.030(1)** owes its
duties *"**to their principal and to all parties in a transaction, which may not be waived**"*:

> **(a)** *"To exercise **reasonable skill and care**";*
> **(b)** *"To **deal honestly and in good faith**";*
> **(c)** *"To **present all written offers, written notices and other written communications** to and from
> either party **in a timely manner, regardless of** whether the property is subject to an existing contract
> for sale or the buyer is already a party to an existing contract to purchase";*
> **(d)** *"To **disclose all existing material facts known by the broker and not apparent or readily
> ascertainable to a party**; provided that this subsection shall not be construed to imply any duty to
> investigate matters that the broker has not agreed to investigate";*
> **(e)** *"To **account in a timely manner for all money and property received** from or on behalf of either
> party";*
> **(f)** *"To **provide a pamphlet in the form prescribed by RCW 18.86.120 and obtain an acknowledgment of
> receipt** by the party";*
> **(g)** *"To **disclose in writing** before the broker's principal signs an offer ... whether the broker
> represents the buyer, the seller, or **both parties as a limited dual agent** ... in a **separate paragraph
> titled 'Agency Disclosure'**"* — and **any terms of compensation offered by a party or firm to a firm
> representing another party.*

**Read (c) again.** The duty to present all written offers continues **regardless of whether the property is
already under contract.** A consumer whose offer is not presented because a listing broker decided it was not
worth presenting has been failed by the broker, and Washington states the duty without the waiver most states
allow.

**And read (d) with its proviso.** **Disclose what you know.** **No duty to investigate what you did not
agree to investigate.** Those two sentences, held together, are the whole Washington disclosure standard for
a broker.

### G. Material fact — and Washington's negative list

**RCW 18.86.010(11):**

> *"'**Material fact**' means information that **substantially adversely affects the value of the property or
> a party's ability to perform its obligations** in a real estate transaction, or **operates to materially
> impair or defeat the purpose of the transaction**. **The fact or suspicion that the property, or any
> neighboring property, is or was the site of a murder, suicide or other death, rape or other sex crime,
> assault or other violent crime, robbery or burglary, illegal drug activity, gang-related activity,
> political or religious activity, or other act, occurrence, or use not adversely affecting the physical
> condition of or title to the property is not a material fact.**"*

**This is one of the most consequential sentences in Washington brokerage, and it belongs in the consumer
protection block for a reason.**

**What the negative list does:** it takes a long list of events — deaths, violent crimes, drug activity,
gang activity, and **political or religious activity** — out of the material fact duty entirely, **so long as
they did not adversely affect the physical condition of or title to the property.**

**Read the limiting clause, because it does real work.** A methamphetamine laboratory that **contaminated the
structure** has affected the **physical condition** — that is outside the exclusion and inside the duty.
Illegal drug activity that left no physical trace is inside the exclusion.

**And notice why "political or religious activity" appears in that list at all.** A broker who volunteered
that information would be characterising a property by the beliefs of the people associated with it — which
is precisely what fair housing law prohibits. **The material fact exclusion and the fair housing prohibition
point the same way.**

**What the exclusion does NOT do is authorise a lie.** **RCW 18.86.030(1)(b)** — deal honestly and in good
faith — is owed to all parties and cannot be waived. **Not being obliged to volunteer something is not
permission to misstate it**, and a broker asked a direct question answers it truthfully or declines to
answer, without inventing.

### H. The consumer protection habits this block is really teaching

1. **Never use a personal characteristic** — not to screen, sort, steer, describe or decide.
2. **Describe property, not people**, in everything you write.
3. **Route objective questions to objective sources** rather than summarising them yourself.
4. **Disclose what you know** that materially affects value, performance or the purpose of the transaction —
   and remember the proviso: **no duty to investigate what you did not agree to investigate.**
5. **Present everything, to everyone, in time** — RCW 18.86.030(1)(c), **regardless.**
6. **Account for every dollar** you touch.
7. **Give the pamphlet and get the acknowledgment**, in the form the statute prescribes.
8. **When it is beyond you, say so and send them where it is answered** — a statutory duty under RCW
   18.86.040(1)(c) and 18.86.050(1)(c).
