## Learning objectives

By the end of this module the student will be able to:

- State what a licensee relationship is under AS 08.88.600, and why Alaska's statutory scheme replaced the common law of agency rather than supplementing it.
- Distinguish representing a person from providing specific assistance, and identify which is happening in a given transaction.
- List the duties owed in every licensee relationship under AS 08.88.615, and the additional duties owed to a represented person under AS 08.88.620.
- Explain the neutral licensee relationship, the preauthorisation requirement, and the exact form Alaska requires to establish it.
- Explain the designated licensee relationship and what it does and does not extend to the broker.
- Apply AS 08.88.391 to a conflict of interest, including the words the written disclosure must begin with.

## 1. Alaska replaced the common law of agency

### 1.1 The sentence that decides everything else

**AS 08.88.675: "The common law of agency related to real estate licensee relationships in real estate transactions is expressly abrogated to the extent inconsistent with AS 08.88.600 – 08.88.695."**

Read that before anything else, because it tells you where the answers come from. In most states a licensee learns the common law of agency — principal, agent, fiduciary duties — and then learns the statutory modifications. In Alaska the statute is the law of the relationship, and common-law agency doctrine survives only where it does not conflict with it.

The practical consequence for a student is that **you cannot answer an Alaska relationship question from general agency principles.** You answer it from AS 08.88.600 through .695. A course, a textbook, or a colleague from another state describing "fiduciary duties" is describing something Alaska's statute does not use as its organising idea.

### 1.2 What the statute is built on instead

Alaska's scheme rests on two ideas that do most of the work.

**Providing specific assistance.** The statute attaches duties to the act of assisting a person in a real estate transaction, whether or not that person is represented. A licensee who helps an unrepresented buyer owes them a list of duties under AS 08.88.615 — not because of any agency relationship, but because assistance was provided.

**Representation.** A licensee who represents a person owes them the AS 08.88.615 duties *plus* the additional duties in AS 08.88.620. Representation is the larger relationship, and it is what a client is buying.

Almost every question in this module is answered by asking which of the two is in play.

### 1.3 One party unless agreed otherwise, in writing

**AS 08.88.600(a): a licensee who provides real estate services to one party represents only that party unless the parties agree otherwise in writing.**

That is the default and it is a strong one. Representation of one side is presumed; anything else requires a written agreement.

**AS 08.88.600(b) then bars serving more than one party in the same transaction**, with four exceptions:

1. Representing one party while providing **specific assistance** to an unrepresented party.
2. Acting as a **neutral licensee** under AS 08.88.610.
3. Being a **party to the transaction** yourself, with the written consent of the parties.
4. The parties agreeing otherwise in writing — subject to AS 08.88.625, which forbids waiving the core duties.

Exception 1 is the one a new licensee meets first and understands last. A listing licensee showing the property to an unrepresented buyer is inside it: they represent the seller and provide specific assistance to the buyer, and they owe the buyer the AS 08.88.615 duties in full.

### 1.4 What the statute does not decide

The scheme governs the relationship. It does not decide who pays — **AS 08.88.655(b) says expressly that payment of compensation does not establish a relationship between the broker and the party who pays.** A seller paying a buyer's broker does not thereby become that broker's client, and a buyer who pays nothing is not thereby unrepresented.

Students and consumers both get this wrong, and correcting it is often the most useful sentence a licensee says in a first meeting.

#### Checkpoint 1.1

A licensee lists a property for a seller. An unrepresented buyer asks to see it and the licensee shows it. Which relationships now exist, and what is owed to the buyer?

*Answer:* The licensee represents the seller and provides specific assistance to the buyer — AS 08.88.600(b)(1). The buyer is owed every duty in AS 08.88.615: reasonable skill and care, honest and good faith dealing, timely presentation of written communications, disclosure of material information about the physical condition of the real estate, timely accounting for money and property, and the consumer pamphlet before assistance begins. What the buyer is not owed are the AS 08.88.620 duties, which run only to a represented person.

## 2. Representation, specific assistance, and the pamphlet

### 2.1 The pamphlet is a precondition, not a formality

**AS 08.88.615(a)(6) requires the licensee, before providing specific assistance to a person — or when entering into a contract with that person to provide it — to give a copy of the consumer pamphlet the Commission establishes.**

Two things follow. The obligation is triggered by **assistance**, not by representation, so it reaches unrepresented buyers at an open house. And it is triggered **before**, so a pamphlet handed over with the purchase agreement is late.

The professional habit is to give it at first substantive contact and record that you did. Months later the file is what shows whether it happened.

### 2.2 What "specific assistance" reaches

Showing property, explaining terms, preparing an offer, conveying a counter, arranging inspections, walking a buyer through a disclosure statement. If you are helping a person move a transaction forward, you are providing specific assistance, and the duties attach whether or not anyone signed anything.

The mistake to avoid is thinking of duties as switched on by a signature. They are switched on by conduct.

### 2.3 Representation is established by agreement

Representation, by contrast, comes from an agreement — a listing, a buyer representation agreement, or another personal services contract. AS 08.88.655(c) contemplates a licensee entering into a personal services contract to act for a person.

A licensee should be able to say, at any point in any transaction, which parties they represent and which they merely assist. If they cannot, the file will not be able to either.

### 2.4 Duties the licensee does not owe

**AS 08.88.630** states what is *not* owed unless agreed otherwise, and it is unusually useful because it protects a competent licensee from a claim built on hindsight. Unless agreed otherwise, a licensee does not owe a duty to conduct an independent inspection of the real estate, or an independent investigation of a person's financial condition.

That does not license silence. AS 08.88.615(a)(4) still requires disclosure of material information the licensee **knows** about the physical condition. The line is between what you must find out and what you must pass on: Alaska does not require you to investigate, and does require you to disclose what you know.

#### Checkpoint 2.1

At a first meeting a buyer asks the licensee to look at three properties this weekend. What must happen before the first showing?

*Answer:* The consumer pamphlet must be given — AS 08.88.615(a)(6) requires it before specific assistance is provided. If the licensee also intends to represent this buyer, that is a separate matter established by agreement. And if the licensee already represents the seller of one of the three properties, the neutral-licensee consent in section 5 has to be dealt with before that property is shown.

## 3. Duties owed in every licensee relationship

### 3.1 The six duties of AS 08.88.615

Owed to **each person to whom the licensee provides specific assistance**, regardless of relationship type, unless additional duties are agreed in a signed writing:

1. **The exercise of reasonable skill and care.**
2. **Honest and good faith dealing.**
3. **Timely presentation of all written offers, written notices, and other written communications** to and from the person — expressly including where the property is already under contract, or where the person is already party to a contract.
4. **Disclosure of all material information known by the licensee regarding the physical condition of the real estate**, where it substantially adversely affects the real estate or a person's ability to perform their obligations, or would materially impair or defeat the purpose of the transaction.
5. **Timely accounting for all money and other property** received from or on behalf of the person.
6. **Providing the consumer pamphlet** before assistance begins or on contracting to provide it.

### 3.2 Duty 3 is stronger than students expect

The statute says written communications must be presented **regardless of whether the real estate is subject to an existing contract for sale or lease**. That closes the common excuse — "we're already under contract, so I didn't pass it on." An offer arriving on a property under contract still gets presented.

### 3.3 Duty 4 and its limit

The disclosure duty is about **material information known by the licensee regarding the physical condition**. Three limits are built in and each matters:

- It is about **physical condition**, not about every fact in the world.
- It is about what the licensee **knows**, which pairs with AS 08.88.630's absence of any duty to investigate.
- It carries a materiality threshold — substantially adverse effect, impairment of ability to perform, or defeat of the transaction's purpose.

AS 08.88.615(b) provides the exception referred to in (a)(4), and a licensee should read it rather than assume its content.

### 3.4 These duties cannot be waived

**AS 08.88.625: a licensee or a person to whom the licensee provides specific assistance may not waive the duties in AS 08.88.615 and AS 08.88.620**, except as allowed under AS 08.88.620(5) and (6).

So a form purporting to release a licensee from the duty of honest dealing, or from presenting offers, does nothing. The two permitted waivers are narrow and are covered in section 4.

#### Checkpoint 3.1

A property is under contract with a financing contingency. A second buyer submits a written backup offer. The seller's licensee decides not to bother the seller with it. Evaluate.

*Answer:* A breach of AS 08.88.615(a)(3), which requires timely presentation of written offers **regardless of whether the real estate is subject to an existing contract**. The decision was not the licensee's to make. Whether the seller would have wanted it is beside the point — the statute gives the seller the choice, not the licensee.

## 4. Additional duties to a represented person

### 4.1 The six additional duties of AS 08.88.620

Owed to a person the licensee **represents**, in addition to the AS 08.88.615 duties:

1. **Not taking action the licensee knows is adverse or detrimental** to the represented person's interest.
2. **Timely disclosure of a conflict of interest** to the represented person.
3. **Advising the represented person to obtain expert advice** on a matter beyond the licensee's expertise.
4. **Not disclosing confidential information** from or about the represented person without written consent, except under subpoena or court order — **even after the relationship terminates**.
5. **For a seller or lessor**: a good faith and continuous effort to find a buyer or lessee, except that no additional offers need be sought while the property is under an existing contract.
6. **For a buyer or lessee**: a good faith and continuous effort to find real estate for them.

### 4.2 Duty 3 is the referral duty, stated as an obligation

Most states leave the boundary of a licensee's competence to professional judgment. Alaska writes it as a duty: **advise the represented person to obtain expert advice on matters beyond your expertise.**

A licensee who is unsure whether an estate can convey, whether a lease binds a buyer, or what a tax consequence will be is not merely permitted to refer — they are obliged to advise the client to get expert advice. Note that this is an Alaska duty and sits alongside AS 08.88.405, covered in module 05, which permits Alaska licensees to prepare certain documents. The two are not in tension: preparing an earnest money agreement is authorised; advising on a legal question beyond your expertise is not the same act.

### 4.3 Duty 4 survives the relationship

Confidentiality does not end when the transaction does, and it does not end when the client leaves. A licensee who tells a colleague two years later what a former seller would have taken has breached AS 08.88.620(4).

### 4.4 The two waivable duties

AS 08.88.625 permits waiver only of AS 08.88.620(5) and (6) — the continuous-effort duties. That makes sense: a seller may not want continued marketing, and a buyer may want the licensee to stop searching. Everything else in the list stands whatever any form says.

#### Checkpoint 4.1

A represented buyer asks their licensee whether the condominium's bylaws permit short-term rental, which is the buyer's whole purpose in buying. What does AS 08.88.620 require?

*Answer:* If the answer is beyond the licensee's expertise — and the enforceability and meaning of association documents generally is — AS 08.88.620(3) requires the licensee to advise the buyer to obtain expert advice. The licensee obtains and delivers the documents, identifies where the provision sits, states plainly that they cannot say what it means, and records that the advice to seek expert help was given. Guessing breaches both (1) and (3).

## 5. Neutral and designated licensees

### 5.1 The neutral licensee

A licensee may provide specific assistance to **both** sides of the same transaction as a **neutral licensee** — AS 08.88.600(c) — if AS 08.88.610 is complied with.

A neutral licensee owes the AS 08.88.615 duties plus, under AS 08.88.645, a further list: not taking action known to be adverse to either party; timely disclosure of a conflict to all parties assisted; advising all parties to obtain expert advice beyond the licensee's expertise; not disclosing confidential information from one assisted party to another without written consent, even after termination; and specifically **not disclosing without consent that the buyer or lessee would pay more, or that the seller or lessor would take less**.

That last item is the whole difference between a neutral licensee and an advocate. A neutral licensee holds both sides' negotiating positions and may use neither.

### 5.2 The consent, and the form Alaska requires

**AS 08.88.610** sets out how the relationship is established, and it is specific in a way students should memorise:

- Preauthorisation may be obtained in advance by written consent — (a).
- If it was not, then **when a buyer or lessee expresses an interest in acquiring or leasing property the licensee is listing, the licensee must obtain written consent to act as a neutral licensee before showing the property** — (b).
- **The written consent must be on a separate form, may not be contained in another writing, and must be entitled "Waiver of Right To Be Represented"** — (c).

Three requirements in one sentence, each of which invalidates a consent obtained the ordinary way: a clause buried in a buyer registration form is not consent, and neither is a paragraph inside a showing agreement.

### 5.3 The designated licensee

**AS 08.88.640** allows a broker to designate different licensees for the seller and the buyer in the same transaction, and states expressly that doing so **does not create dual agency or a conflict of interest** for the broker or for other licensees working for the same broker.

The relationship established with a designated licensee does **not** extend to the broker, to other licensees working for that broker, or to an owner of the business employing the broker — unless the broker is themselves the designated licensee. The extent or limitations of the broker's relationship must be disclosed to the parties.

**AS 08.88.650** carries the companion rule: knowledge is not imputed merely by the designated-licensee structure. What one designated licensee knows is not automatically attributed to the other.

### 5.4 Choosing between them

Both structures let one brokerage serve both sides. They are not equivalent.

| | Neutral licensee | Designated licensee |
|---|---|---|
| Who acts | One licensee, for both parties | Two licensees, one for each party |
| Advocacy | None, for either side | Each client has an advocate |
| Negotiating positions | Held and used for neither | Each licensee holds only their own client's |
| Consent | Written, on a separate form titled "Waiver of Right To Be Represented" | Disclosure of the broker's position to the parties |
| Statutory basis | AS 08.88.610, .645 | AS 08.88.640, .650 |

#### Checkpoint 5.1

A listing licensee's buyer client wants to see the licensee's own listing. The brokerage's buyer registration form contains a paragraph consenting to neutral representation. Is that sufficient?

*Answer:* No. AS 08.88.610(c) requires the consent to be on a **separate form**, expressly forbids it being contained in another writing, and requires it to be entitled "Waiver of Right To Be Represented". A paragraph inside a registration form fails all three requirements. The alternative is the designated licensee structure under AS 08.88.640, which the brokerage may prefer here in any case.

## 6. Conflict of interest, compensation, and termination

### 6.1 The conflict disclosure has prescribed words

**AS 08.88.391** requires a licensee with a conflict of interest to disclose it to the persons adversely affected, or to their licensees, and to confirm it **in writing**, as soon as possible after the conflict is identified. The licensee must **also verbally advise** the person.

And the statute prescribes the opening of the written statement: it must **begin with the words "Disclosure of Conflict of Interest.", underlined and written in bold.**

That is unusually specific drafting and it exists for a reason — a conflict disclosure that reads like ordinary correspondence gets skimmed. A student should treat the formatting requirement as part of the obligation, not decoration.

### 6.2 What counts as a conflict

The statute does not enumerate them, which means the licensee has to recognise them. The recurring ones:

- The licensee, a family member, or an entity they own is a party to the transaction.
- The licensee is receiving something of value from a service provider they are recommending.
- The licensee represents another client whose interest is opposed in this transaction.
- The licensee has an interest in a competing property.

The safe practice is to disclose anything a reasonable client would want to know before relying on the licensee's advice, and to do it in the prescribed form.

### 6.3 Compensation

**AS 08.88.655(a)**: a broker may be compensated by any party, by a third party, or by the parties splitting or sharing it.

**AS 08.88.655(b)**: payment of compensation **may not be construed to establish a relationship** between the broker and the party who pays.

**AS 08.88.401(b)(2)**: an associate broker or salesperson may accept a fee or commission for licensed activity **only from their employing broker** — with the narrow exception of wages paid to a licensed assistant under AS 08.88.398.

Together those three tell a new licensee everything they need about money: the source of payment does not decide representation, and your payment comes from your broker.

### 6.4 When the relationship ends

**AS 08.88.660(a)**: the relationship begins when the licensee represents or provides specific assistance, and continues until the earliest of — completion of the representation or assistance; expiry of an agreed term; mutual termination; or notice by a party.

**AS 08.88.660(c)**: after termination, no further duty is owed **except** the duties of **accounting** for money and property received during the relationship, and **not disclosing confidential information**.

Two duties outlive the relationship, and both are the ones most often breached afterwards.

### 6.5 Vicarious liability, and why it matters to a client

**AS 08.88.665**: a seller, buyer, lessor or lessee is **not liable** for a licensee's act, error, or omission arising out of the licensee relationship unless they participated in or authorised it, and then only to the extent of their participation.

A client is not automatically on the hook for what their licensee does. This is worth knowing because clients sometimes ask, and because it explains why the statute is willing to place real duties on licensees personally.

#### Checkpoint 6.1

A licensee's brother-in-law is buying the property the licensee has listed. What does AS 08.88.391 require, and in what form?

*Answer:* Disclosure to the persons adversely affected — here the seller — or to their licensee, as soon as possible after the conflict is identified. It must be made verbally **and** confirmed in writing, and the written statement must **begin with the words "Disclosure of Conflict of Interest.", underlined and in bold**. Doing it late, doing it only verbally, or burying it in an email without the prescribed opening each fails the statute.

## Key terms

**Designated licensee** — a licensee designated by a broker to represent or assist one party, where the relationship does not extend to the broker or to other licensees working for that broker (AS 08.88.640).

**Neutral licensee** — a licensee providing specific assistance to both sides of a transaction under AS 08.88.610, owing the duties in AS 08.88.645 and advocating for neither.

**Represents** — the larger relationship, established by agreement, carrying the AS 08.88.620 duties in addition to those in AS 08.88.615.

**Specific assistance** — helping a person in a real estate transaction, which triggers the AS 08.88.615 duties whether or not that person is represented.

**Waiver of Right To Be Represented** — the separate written form, so titled, that AS 08.88.610(c) requires before a licensee may act as a neutral licensee.

## Practice problems

**Problem 1.** A licensee representing a seller learns from the buyer's casual remark that the buyer would pay $12,000 more. May the licensee tell the seller?

*Working:* Yes, if the licensee represents the seller and is merely assisting the buyer. AS 08.88.620 requires the licensee not to act adversely to the represented seller and to disclose relevant information to them; the buyer, as a person receiving specific assistance, is owed the AS 08.88.615 duties, which do not include confidentiality. If instead the licensee were a **neutral** licensee, AS 08.88.645(a)(5) would forbid disclosing exactly this. The answer turns entirely on which relationship exists.

**Problem 2.** A buyer asks a licensee to "just be neutral so we can keep it simple," and signs a paragraph to that effect at the bottom of the purchase agreement.

*Working:* Ineffective. AS 08.88.610(c) requires a separate form titled "Waiver of Right To Be Represented" and forbids the consent being contained in another writing. The purchase agreement is another writing. The licensee is not a neutral licensee and has not obtained consent to become one.

**Problem 3.** Two licensees at the same brokerage act for the buyer and the seller. The seller asks whether the broker will now know what the buyer is willing to pay.

*Working:* Under AS 08.88.640 the designated structure does not create dual agency or a conflict for the broker, and AS 08.88.650 provides that knowledge is not imputed by reason of the structure. The extent or limitations of the broker's own relationship must be disclosed to the parties. The honest answer describes the structure and the disclosure; it does not promise that nobody at the firm knows anything.

**Problem 4.** A former client calls a year after closing and asks the licensee to confirm to a neighbour what the property sold for and why they moved.

*Working:* The sale price may be public. The reason for moving is confidential information about a represented person, and AS 08.88.620(4) forbids disclosing it without written consent **even after termination** — a point AS 08.88.660(c) repeats. The licensee needs the former client's written consent, and the request coming from the client themselves is not the same as consent to tell a neighbour.

**Problem 5.** A salesperson is offered $1,000 directly by a grateful seller at closing.

*Working:* AS 08.88.401(b)(2) permits an associate broker or salesperson to accept a fee or commission for licensed activity **only from their employing broker**. The payment must go through the broker. And if it is being paid because of something the licensee recommended, AS 08.88.391's conflict disclosure may be engaged as well.

## Module summary

**Alaska abrogated the common law of agency** for licensee relationships — AS 08.88.675. Answers come from AS 08.88.600 to .695, not from general agency doctrine.

**Duties attach to specific assistance, not only to representation.** An unrepresented buyer at an open house is owed the whole of AS 08.88.615.

**Six duties in every relationship**, including presentation of written offers even where the property is under contract, and disclosure of known material information about physical condition.

**Six more to a represented person**, including the statutory duty to advise them to get expert advice beyond your expertise, and confidentiality that survives termination.

**The core duties cannot be waived** — AS 08.88.625 — except the two continuous-effort duties.

**A neutral licensee needs a separate form titled "Waiver of Right To Be Represented"**, obtained before showing. A designated licensee structure is the alternative and does not create dual agency.

**A conflict disclosure is verbal and written**, as soon as possible, and the writing must begin with "Disclosure of Conflict of Interest." underlined and in bold.

**Who pays does not decide who is represented**, and a salesperson is paid by their broker.

**Two duties outlive the relationship**: accounting, and confidentiality.
