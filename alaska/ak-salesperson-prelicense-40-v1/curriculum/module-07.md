## 1. The licence, and what it takes to get one

### 1.1 What requires a licence

**AS 08.88.161.** Unless licensed as a real estate broker, associate real estate broker, or real estate salesperson in Alaska, a person may not, except as the chapter otherwise provides:

1. sell, exchange, rent, lease, auction, or purchase real estate;
2. list real estate for any of those purposes;
3. **collect rent** for the use of real estate or collect fees for **property management**;
4. practice, or negotiate for a contract to practice, **property management**;
5. collect fees for **community association management**;
6. practice, or negotiate for a contract to practice, community association management;
7. as a business, buy, sell, or deal in options in real estate or in improvements to real estate;
8. **assist in or direct the procuring of prospective buyers and sellers**, communicate with prospective buyers and sellers, or assist in negotiating a transaction calculated to result in a sale, exchange, rent, lease, auction, or purchase;
9. accept or pay a fee for any of these activities except as the chapter specifically provides;
10. **hold out to the public** as being engaged in the business of doing any of them; or
11. **attempt or offer** to do any of them.

Read paragraphs (3) through (6) again. In Alaska, **property management and community association management are licensed activities in their own right** — collecting rent for someone else's property requires a licence. Read paragraphs (8), (10), and (11): assisting, holding out, and even attempting are covered. The statute is deliberately broad.

**AS 08.88.401(g):** a person who violates AS 08.88.401 or AS 08.88.161 is guilty of a **class A misdemeanor**.

**AS 08.88.165** carves out one narrow exception: an unlicensed person may conduct a **real estate auction** if the person completed an auctioneering programme at a school certified by an agency of this or another state and the programme included a course on real estate sales, subject to the section's further conditions.

### 1.2 The salesperson licence

**AS 08.88.171(c)** sets out what a natural person needs for a real estate salesperson licence:

- **passes** the real estate salesperson examination;
- **applies within six months** after passing;
- submits satisfactory proof of completing the education requirements of **AS 08.88.091**;
- is **at least 19 years of age**;
- furnishes satisfactory proof that **errors and omissions insurance** required under AS 08.88.172 has been obtained;
- is not under indictment for, and if convicted has had **seven years elapse** since completing the sentence for, a felony or other crime that in the Commission's judgment affects the ability to practise competently and safely, or a conviction for forgery, theft, extortion, conspiracy to defraud creditors, or fraud.

**AS 08.88.091(b): "An applicant for licensure under AS 08.88.171(c) must complete 40 hours of education approved by the commission before the person may be licensed under that subsection."**

That sentence is why this course exists, and why it is 40 hours and not 39.

**12 AAC 64.063(a)(2)** adds a recency rule students routinely miss: education used to qualify for initial licensure must have been **completed within the 18 months immediately preceding the date of application** — 24 months where the initial licensure is by endorsement. Finish the course, then sit the examination and apply; do not finish the course and wait two years.

**12 AAC 64.063(c)** is the outline this whole curriculum follows: the eight topics and their contact hours.

### 1.3 The broker and associate broker

**AS 08.88.171(a) and (b).** A broker or associate broker must pass the **brokers** examination, apply within six months, complete the education required under AS 08.88.091 — **30 hours** under AS 08.88.091(c), allocated by topic in 12 AAC 64.063(b), including four contact hours on broker supervision requirements and record keeping and three on trust accounting procedures — and have at least **36 months of active and continuous experience as a real estate licensee within the 60 months immediately preceding** application.

The structural difference: a **broker** must be an owner of a real estate business or employed as a real estate broker by a corporation, partnership, limited partnership, or limited liability company. An **associate broker** must be **employed by a licensed real estate broker**. If either stops meeting that condition, the licence is **suspended** from that moment until the licensee satisfies AS 08.88.098's education requirement and re-establishes the qualifying position — and a broker who becomes an associate broker returns the broker licence to the Commission and receives an associate broker licence in its place.

### 1.4 Education after licensure — the trap

**AS 08.88.095(a):** in addition to continuing education, **within one year after** the Commission issues an initial licence, the licensee must complete **30 hours** of Commission-approved education.

**AS 08.88.095(b):** the initial licence document itself must carry "clear and prominent language" stating that the licence is subject to completing those requirements within one year.

**AS 08.88.095(c):** within **30 days after the end of the one-year period**, the licensee must submit, on a Commission form, a certification of completion.

**AS 08.88.095(e): if the licensee fails to complete the education within the year or to provide the certification, the licence AUTOMATICALLY LAPSES 30 days after the end of the one-year period.** The Commission may reinstate it under AS 08.88.241.

There is no notice requirement and no hearing. It lapses. New Alaska salespeople lose their licences to this provision, and the fix is to diary the date on the day the licence issues.

**AS 08.88.095(f)** exempts one case: an individual issued an initial **broker** licence who, immediately before issuance, was licensed as an **associate broker**.

### 1.5 Continuing education and renewal

**AS 08.88.091(d):** a licensee must complete **20 hours** of Commission-approved continuing education before renewal. The Commission may not set limits that prevent satisfying the requirement **within a two-day period**.

**AS 08.88.091(g):** the Commission establishes **core curricula** in four areas — real estate sales, property management, community association management, and commercial sales — and a licensee must complete **at least one of the four** during each biennial licensing period as part of continuing education.

**AS 08.88.091(e):** for a course to be recognised for credit, **the course outline and the instructor must have been approved by the Commission or its designee before the course is conducted**, with limited exceptions in (f)(1) and (f)(3). A course outline or instructor is **considered approved if the Commission or its designee does not disapprove within 45 days** after a complete application. Each approved contact hour of a submitted outline is one credit hour.

**AS 08.88.241** allows reinstatement of a licence lapsed **less than 24 months** on the conditions the section states. **AS 08.88.251** governs going **inactive**, which requires returning the licence to the Commission.

### 1.6 The one thing a salesperson may never forget

**AS 08.88.331:** an active salesperson or associate broker may perform licensed activity **only through the real estate broker who employs or contracts with the licensee**, and all money or other proceeds collected in trust must **immediately** be turned over to the broker or the broker's authorised representative.

**AS 08.88.401(b)(2):** an associate broker or salesperson may accept a fee or commission **only from the licensee's employing broker** — except that a licensed assistant engaged under AS 08.88.398 may accept wages from that assistant's employer.

**AS 08.88.071(a)(3)(H)** makes failing **immediately** to turn money or property collected in a transaction over to the employing broker a ground for suspension, revocation, or other sanction.

#### Checkpoint 1.1

A salesperson is licensed on 14 May 2026. What must she do, and by when, to avoid the automatic lapse in AS 08.88.095(e)?

*Answer:* Two things. She must complete **30 hours** of Commission-approved education **by 14 May 2027**, one year after issuance — **AS 08.88.095(a)**. Then she must submit the Commission's certification form **within 30 days after the end of that one-year period**, so by about 13 June 2027 — **AS 08.88.095(c)**. If either is missed, **AS 08.88.095(e)** lapses the licence **automatically** 30 days after the end of the year. No notice is required and no hearing is held; reinstatement is then a matter for AS 08.88.241.

## 2. The Commission, discipline, and prohibited conduct

### 2.1 The Commission

**AS 08.88.011:** "There is created a Real Estate Commission. It consists of seven members."

**AS 08.88.081** empowers the Commission to adopt regulations — 12 AAC 64 is the product. **AS 08.88.031** provides for an executive secretary; **AS 08.88.037** for investigation and injunction; **AS 08.88.131** applies the Administrative Procedure Act, AS 44.62, to the Commission's proceedings.

### 2.2 Grounds for discipline — AS 08.88.071(a)(3)

After hearing, the Commission may suspend or revoke a licence or impose other sanctions authorised under AS 08.01.075 on a licensee who:

**(A) with respect to a real estate transaction —**
 (i) made a **substantial misrepresentation**;
 (ii) made a **false promise** likely to influence, persuade, or induce;
 (iii) as a broker, pursued a **flagrant course of misrepresentation** or made a false promise through another licensee;
 (iv) **engaged in conduct that is fraudulent or dishonest**;
 (v) **violates AS 08.88.391** — the conflict of interest section from module 01;
 (vi) violates AS 08.88.396;

**(B)** procures a licence by **deceiving the Commission**, or aids another to do so;

**(C)** has engaged in conduct the Commission did not know of at licensing that demonstrates **unfitness**;

**(D)** knowingly authorises, directs, connives at, or aids in publishing a **material false statement** about the licensee's business, about real estate offered for sale, rent, or lease or managed in the licensee's business in this or any other state, or about association management;

**(E)** as a broker, **wilfully violates AS 08.88.171(d) or 08.88.291**;

**(F)** as an associate broker, **claims to be a broker**; or as a salesperson, claims to be a broker or associate broker;

**(G)** as a broker, **employs an unlicensed person** to perform activities requiring a licence;

**(H)** as an employed licensee, **fails immediately to turn money or property over** to the employing broker;

**(I)** **fails to carry and maintain errors and omissions insurance** with the terms the Commission establishes under AS 08.88.172.

Note subparagraph (iv), "conduct that is fraudulent or dishonest." Several regulations point back to it and declare specified failures to be exactly that: **12 AAC 64.260** for the trust account rules, and **12 AAC 64.560** for failing to deposit prepaid rents or security deposits as AS 34.03 requires.

**AS 08.88.071(a)(11)** requires the Commission to **revoke** the licence of a broker, associate broker, or salesperson convicted of a felony or other crime committed while licensed that in the Commission's judgment affects competent and safe practice, or convicted of forgery, theft, extortion, conspiracy to defraud creditors, or fraud — and the person is not qualified to obtain or renew a licence **until seven years have elapsed** since completing the sentence.

**AS 08.88.071(a)(5):** the Commission **releases for publication** in a newspaper of general circulation in the locale of the offender's registered principal office notice of disciplinary action taken. Discipline in Alaska is public by statute.

**AS 08.88.071(a)(10):** the Commission has authority to levy **civil fines** as the chapter establishes.

### 2.3 The regulation's list — 12 AAC 64.130

**12 AAC 64.130** adds nineteen grounds "in addition to those specified elsewhere in this chapter." Several have appeared in earlier modules; the whole list should be read at least once. It includes:

- **(2)** representing **more than one party** in a real estate transaction — the regulatory backstop to Alaska's no-dual-agency scheme in module 01;
- **(3)** failing to account for, remit, or surrender money, documents, or property, or **unreasonably delaying** an accounting or disbursal;
- **(4)** failing to disclose information as required in 12 AAC 64.940, as to the listing contract and the settlement statement;
- **(6)** **advertising without the owner's written authority**;
- **(7)** paying referral fees or sharing commissions with a person prohibited from receiving compensation under AS 08.88.401(b);
- **(8)** advertising without the **broker's registered business name** — including property the licensee owns;
- **(9)** failing to disclose to **all parties** that you are licensed, or the name of your broker;
- **(10)** using third-party purchasers, entities, friends, relatives, or business associates to **conceal the licensee's profit** and identity;
- **(12)** accepting **non-cash earnest money** without the module 05 safeguards;
- **(13)** failing to submit **all written bona fide offers**;
- **(14)** failing to disclose a **known material defect** in condition or a **known legal defect**;
- **(16) – (18)** licence-lending, allowing a salesperson to operate without broker control, and carrying on a business **without broker supervision** as required by 12 AAC 64.125;
- **(19)** being found guilty of violating **local, state, or federal fair housing laws** — which is module 08's subject and a licence matter, not only a federal one.

### 2.4 Prohibited conduct — AS 08.88.401

**(a)** A licensee may not falsely represent having been awarded a **degree or designation**, being a member or affiliate of a **professional organization**, or being a member of a **franchise or business association**.

**(b)(1)** A person not licensed as an Alaska broker may not accept a fee or commission for a licensed act — **except** that a broker validly licensed in another state may accept a fee or a portion of one for **assisting** an Alaska-licensed broker. (12 AAC 64.240(d) states the documentation the Alaska broker must keep for three years.)

**(c)** A licensee may not knowingly make, authorise, direct, or aid in publishing a **false statement or misrepresentation** about land, a subdivision, or other real estate offered for sale, lease, or rent, or about an association being managed.

**(d)** A licensee may not knowingly pay any part of a fee or commission received to (1) an unlicensed person except as (e) allows; (2) another licensee **except through the licensee's responsible broker**; or (3) another licensee knowing that licensee intends to pass it to an unlicensed person.

**(e)** The prohibition in (d)(1) does not bar payments to a person licensed in another jurisdiction who assisted, payments to a **principal** as part of resolving a dispute about the transaction's terms or the property, or **charitable contributions** and advertising the intent to make them.

**(f)** No person may use another's licence, give false or forged evidence to the Commission to obtain a licence, impersonate an applicant, knowingly use an **expired, suspended, revoked, or nonexistent** licence, or falsely claim to be licensed.

**AS 08.88.485:** filing with the Commission a notice, statement, or document required under the chapter that contains a **wilful material misstatement of fact** is a misdemeanor punishable by up to one year, a fine up to $1,000, or both.

#### Checkpoint 2.1

A salesperson pays a $500 thank-you to an unlicensed friend who sent him a buyer. Name two provisions violated.

*Answer:* **AS 08.88.401(d)(1)** — a licensee may not knowingly pay any part of a fee or commission received to a person not licensed under the chapter, and none of the AS 08.88.401(e) exceptions covers a thank-you for a referral. **12 AAC 64.130(7)** — paying referral fees or otherwise compensating a person prohibited from receiving compensation under AS 08.88.401(b) is grounds for revocation or suspension. Add a third if the money came from him rather than through the brokerage: **AS 08.88.401(d)(2)** requires payment to another licensee to move through the responsible broker, and AS 08.88.331 keeps the salesperson out of the money entirely.

## 3. Supervision, records, audits, recovery fund, and E&O

### 3.1 Supervision

A salesperson's licence lives inside a broker's supervision. **12 AAC 64.130(17) and (18)** make it grounds for discipline for a broker to allow a salesperson to operate a real estate business without retaining control as the employing broker, and to establish or carry on a real estate business without the broker supervision 12 AAC 64.125 requires.

**AS 08.88.311(a):** a **branch office** must be under the direct supervision of a real estate broker.

**AS 08.88.301:** a broker must notify the Commission before changing the location of the principal place of business.

**AS 08.88.381:** a broker must maintain a **sign** at each registered office prominently showing the name of the real estate business as registered with the Commission.

**AS 08.88.321(d):** licence certificates must be displayed where they are available for public clients and customers to **verify the current active status** of licensees working in the office.

When a broker is **temporarily absent**, the regulations permit supervision of transactions, trust accounts, and licensees to be carried out by a designated associate broker or another broker under an arrangement notified to the Commission, including the creation and signing of transaction documents during the absence. Supervision is a duty that has to be discharged by a named person, not left vacant.

**AS 08.88.398** provides for **licensed assistants**. An **unlicensed** assistant may do clerical work; the moment the work becomes an activity listed in AS 08.88.161 — communicating with prospective buyers and sellers, assisting in procuring them, negotiating — a licence is required, and **AS 08.88.071(a)(3)(G)** makes employing an unlicensed person for licensed activity a broker's disciplinary exposure. **12 AAC 64.140** sets out the grounds in detail.

### 3.2 Records

Module 05 covered the three-year rule in **AS 08.88.351** and module 06 the trust ledgers in **12 AAC 64.220**. What matters here is who can ask to see them and how.

**12 AAC 64.135(a) — inspections.** The Commission or its designee may inspect a broker's transaction records. The inspection must be conducted **between 8:00 a.m. and 5:00 p.m., Monday through Friday**, unless otherwise agreed, and the broker must be given **at least 72 hours' advance notice**. The broker must make available **all requested transaction records**, including earnest money agreements, listing agreements, trust account records, disbursal records, **broker or licensee communications regarding transactions**, and closing statements for all principals.

Note that the text names communications. Your emails and messages about a transaction are transaction records.

**12 AAC 64.135(b) — audits.** The Commission will, in its discretion, and its designee may, conduct **complete audits** of broker trust accounts, transaction records, and related accounts and records — and **may direct that the audit be held with or without prior notice**. For a non-noticed audit the auditor presents the request during regular business hours. Where a non-noticed audit would be a **grave hardship**, the broker may request a **24-hour delay**; if granted, the records are **sealed or secured by and at the direction of the auditor**, and **the seals may be broken only by the auditor**.

There is no version of this in which a broker gets time alone with unsealed records. A file that is not in order today is not going to be in order when the auditor arrives.

**12 AAC 64.470** permits the Commission to audit an approved education course at any time — the rule that applies to this course rather than to a brokerage, and the reason the module map's contact-hour claim has to be provable.

### 3.3 The real estate recovery fund

**AS 08.88.450:** the real estate recovery fund is established in the general fund, composed of licensee payments, retained filing fees, investment income, and certain fees for Commission items and services.

**AS 08.88.455(a):** a licensee, when applying for or renewing a licence, **in lieu of obtaining a corporate surety bond**, pays the Commission a recovery fund fee **not to exceed $125** in addition to the licence fee. After each two-year licensing cycle, if the average balance was **less than $250,000 or more than $500,000**, the Commission adjusts the fee by regulation so the next cycle's average lands in that band.

**AS 08.88.475(a): the maximum liability of the fund may not exceed $50,000 for any one real estate licensee.**

**AS 08.88.475(b):** if $50,000 is insufficient to pay in full the valid claims of everyone who obtained a final judgment, final arbitration award, or settlement agreement involving that licensee, the $50,000 is distributed **pro rata** among the claimants — or in another manner the Commission considers equitable — **without regard to the order in which claims were filed**.

**AS 08.88.480:** if the fund itself is short at a given time, claims are satisfied, once money is deposited and appropriated, **in the order originally filed**, plus interest at **eight percent a year**.

**AS 08.88.490** gives the fund a **right of subrogation**, and **AS 08.88.495** provides for disciplinary action. **AS 08.88.071(b):** when an award is made, the Commission **may suspend** the licence of the licensee whose actions formed the basis of the award, and the suspension is lifted if the licensee agrees with the Commission on repayment terms and satisfies the section's other conditions.

So the fund is a consumer remedy of last resort, capped per licensee, and it comes back at the licensee personally.

### 3.4 Errors and omissions insurance

**AS 08.88.172(a):** a broker, associate broker, or salesperson **shall, as a condition of licensing, carry and maintain errors and omissions insurance** covering activities for which licensing is required under AS 08.88.161.

**AS 08.88.172(b):** the department establishes coverage requirements, limits, the maximum premium under a master policy, and the method for adjusting those amounts by the Consumer Price Index.

**AS 08.88.172(c):** a licensee may satisfy it through a **master policy** the Commission may make available, or by **independently obtaining equivalent coverage**.

The regulations make the consequences immediate:

**12 AAC 64.600** — proof of coverage is required as a condition of **licensing, renewal, or reinstatement**, through the master policy or a certification of equivalent coverage.

**12 AAC 64.610** — certification of equivalent coverage is filed with the application; a licensee switching between the master policy and equivalent coverage, or between equivalent policies, must file the certification **before 5:00 p.m. on the date coverage is cancelled**. If it is not filed as required, the Commission **will deny** the application or **suspend** the existing licence.

**12 AAC 64.620** — if the insurer or master policy issuer notifies the Commission that a premium was not paid and coverage terminated, the Commission **shall suspend** the licence **as of the date coverage terminated**.

**12 AAC 64.630** — on receiving a suspension notice for failure to provide proof, the licensee **shall immediately surrender** the licence.

**12 AAC 64.640** — proof of insurance is required to reactivate.

And **AS 08.88.071(a)(3)(I)** makes failure to carry and maintain it a disciplinary ground in its own right. A lapsed premium is a suspended licence, backdated to the day coverage stopped — which means transactions worked in the interval were worked without a licence.

#### Checkpoint 3.1

A non-noticed audit arrives at 10:00 a.m. and the broker asks for a delay. What may the broker get, and what happens to the records meanwhile?

*Answer:* **A 24-hour delay, if the Commission grants it** — available under **12 AAC 64.135(b)** where a non-noticed audit would constitute a grave hardship on the broker or the business. Meanwhile the records are **sealed or secured by and at the direction of the auditor** for the duration of the delay, and **the seals may be broken only by the auditor**. There is no point at which the broker has unsupervised time with the records, which is the whole design of the provision.

## 4. AS 34.03: scope, agreements, security deposits

### 4.1 What the Act is and where it applies

**AS 34.03.380** gives the chapter its short title: the **Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act**.

**AS 34.03.010:** the chapter "shall be liberally construed and applied to promote its underlying purposes and policies," which are to simplify, clarify, modernise, and revise the law governing rental of dwelling units and the rights and obligations of landlord and tenant; to encourage both to maintain and improve housing quality; and to make the law uniform among enacting states.

**AS 34.03.330(a):** the chapter applies to and determines rights, obligations, and remedies under a rental agreement, **wherever made**, for a dwelling unit **in this state**.

**AS 34.03.330(b)** excludes eight arrangements — **unless created to avoid the application of the chapter**:

1. residence at an institution incidental to detention or to medical, geriatric, educational, counseling, religious, or similar services;
2. occupancy under a **contract of sale** of the dwelling unit or the property of which it is part, where the occupant is the purchaser or a successor;
3. occupancy by a member of a fraternal or social organization in a portion of a structure operated for the organization's benefit;
4. **transient occupancy** in a hotel, motel, lodgings, or other transient facility;
5. occupancy by a **landlord's employee** whose right to occupy is conditioned on employment substantially for services, maintenance, or repair;
6. occupancy by an **owner of a condominium unit** or a holder of a proprietary lease in a cooperative;
7. occupancy under a rental agreement covering premises used **primarily for agricultural purposes**;
8. occupancy under a rental agreement for premises used as part of a **transitional or supportive housing programme** sponsored or operated by a public corporation or a nonprofit corporation, providing shelter and related support services intended to improve the occupant's opportunity to obtain permanent housing.

The opening qualification matters. Structuring an arrangement to look like one of these in order to escape the Act does not escape it.

### 4.2 The rental agreement

**AS 34.03.020(a):** the parties may include clauses not prohibited by the chapter or by law.

**AS 34.03.020(b):** absent agreement, the tenant pays the **fair rental value**.

**AS 34.03.020(c):** rent is payable **without demand or notice** at the agreed time and place; unless otherwise agreed, at the dwelling unit, at the beginning of a term of a month or less and otherwise in equal monthly installments, **uniformly apportionable day to day**, paid on the date the periodic tenancy begins and on the same date each month until termination.

**AS 34.03.020(d):** unless the agreement fixes a definite term, the tenancy is **week to week** where the tenant pays weekly rent, and **month to month** in all other cases.

**AS 34.03.020(e) — the premises condition statement.** If the landlord requires it, the parties include in the rental agreement, incorporate by reference, or attach a **premises condition statement** setting out the condition of the premises including fixtures, and where applicable a **contents inventory** itemising the furnishings and contents and the condition of each. Signed by both, the statement and inventory **become part of the rental agreement**.

**AS 34.03.335** explains why any competent manager uses one: in an action for damages under the chapter, a premises condition statement and contents inventory prepared under .020(e) is **presumptive evidence** of the condition at the commencement of the occupancy, and unless its authenticity is rebutted by **clear and convincing evidence**, it may be offered **without additional supporting evidence** as the basis for computing damages.

That is an evidentiary advantage available for the cost of walking the unit with a form and a camera at move-in.

**AS 34.03.040** prohibits certain provisions in rental agreements, and **AS 34.03.050** forbids separating rent from the obligation to maintain the property. **AS 34.03.030** addresses the effect of an unsigned or undelivered agreement, and **AS 34.03.060** governs sublease and assignment.

**AS 34.03.130:** a landlord may adopt **rules and regulations**, posted prominently on the premises. A rule is enforceable against a tenant only if its purpose is to promote convenience, safety, health, or welfare, preserve the property from abusive use, or fairly distribute services; it is reasonably related to that purpose; it applies **fairly to all tenants**; it is explicit enough to fairly inform the tenant what to do; it is not for the purpose of evading the landlord's obligations; and the section's remaining conditions are met.

**AS 34.03.080 — disclosure.** At or before the commencement of the tenancy, the landlord or a person authorised to enter into the rental agreement must **disclose to the tenant in writing** the name and address of (1) the person authorised to **manage** the premises, and (2) an **owner** of the premises or a person authorised to act for the owner **for service of process** and for receiving notices and demands. The information must be kept current and binds successors. **AS 34.03.080(c):** a person who fails to comply **becomes an agent** of each landlord for service of process and for performing the landlord's obligations, expending all rent collected from the premises for the purpose.

For a property manager, .080 is the first document of the tenancy.

### 4.3 Security deposits and prepaid rent — AS 34.03.070

**The cap. AS 34.03.070(a):** except as (h) provides, a landlord may not demand or receive prepaid rent or a security deposit, however denominated, **in an amount or value in excess of two months' periodic rent**. The section **does not apply to rental units where the rent exceeds $2,000 a month.**

**The pet deposit. AS 34.03.070(h):** notwithstanding the cap, a landlord may demand or receive an **additional** security deposit from a tenant with a pet **that is not a service animal**. It **may not exceed one month's periodic rent**, must be **accounted for separately**, and may be applied **only to damages directly related to the tenant's pet**. "Service animal" is defined in .070(i)(2) as an animal individually trained to do work or perform tasks directly related to and for the benefit of an individual with a disability, including a physical, sensory, psychiatric, intellectual, or other mental disability.

**Holding the money. AS 34.03.070(c):** all money paid as prepaid rent or a security deposit shall be **promptly deposited, wherever practicable, in a trust account** in a bank, savings and loan association, or licensed escrow agent, and the landlord must provide the tenant **the terms and conditions under which it may be withheld**. The landlord **may commingle prepaid rents and security deposits in a single financial account** but must **separately account for** each tenant's, and **may not commingle them with other funds**. Money held for one tenant may **not** be used to refund another tenant's deposit, pay another tenant's accrued rent, or cover damages from another tenant's noncompliance.

**What may be deducted. AS 34.03.070(b):** on termination, the money may be applied to **accrued rent** and to **damages** the landlord suffered from the tenant's noncompliance with AS 34.03.120, itemised in a **written notice mailed to the tenant's last known address** within the .070(g) time limit, together with the amount due the tenant. "Damages" means deterioration of the premises and, if applicable, its contents — and **does not include** deterioration that is **normal wear and tear** or that was caused by **the landlord's failure to prepare for expected conditions** or to comply with the landlord's own obligations under the chapter.

"Failure to prepare for expected conditions" is an Alaska phrase doing Alaska work. Freeze damage in a unit the landlord did not winterise is not the tenant's deduction.

**AS 34.03.070(i)(1)** defines **normal wear and tear** as deterioration occurring from the intended use of the unit without negligence, carelessness, accident, misuse, or abuse by the tenant, the tenant's household, or their invitees or guests.

**The deadlines. AS 34.03.070(g):**

| Situation | Deadline to mail notice and refund |
|---|---|
| Tenant **gave notice complying with AS 34.03.290** | **14 days** after the tenancy is terminated and possession delivered |
| Same, **but costs are deducted for damages** from the tenant's noncompliance with AS 34.03.120 | **30 days** after termination |
| Tenant **did not give complying notice** | **30 days** after termination, delivery of possession, or the landlord becoming aware the unit is abandoned |

If the landlord does not know the tenant's mailing address but knows or has reason to know how to contact the tenant, the landlord must make a **reasonable effort** to deliver the notice and refund.

**The penalty. AS 34.03.070(d):** if the landlord **wilfully** fails to comply with (b), the tenant may recover **an amount not to exceed twice the actual amount withheld** — and (e) preserves any other damages either party may be entitled to.

**AS 34.03.070(f):** whoever holds the landlord's interest **at the time the tenancy terminates** is bound by the section. Buying a rental property means buying the deposit obligations. This is the module 06 settlement-statement point stated from the tenant's side: deposits transfer to the buyer in full.

#### Checkpoint 4.1

Rent is $1,450. What is the maximum the landlord may take as prepaid rent and security deposit combined, and how much more may be taken for a dog that is not a service animal?

*Answer:* **$2,900** — two months' periodic rent, the **AS 34.03.070(a)** cap on prepaid rent and security deposit combined, however denominated. The cap applies because the rent does not exceed $2,000 a month. For a dog that is not a service animal, **AS 34.03.070(h)** permits an additional deposit **not exceeding one month's periodic rent — $1,450** — accounted for separately and applied only to damages directly related to the pet.

## 5. Habitability, access, and termination

### 5.1 The landlord's duty — AS 34.03.100

**AS 34.03.100(a).** The landlord shall:

1. make all repairs and do whatever is necessary to put and keep the premises **fit and habitable**;
2. keep all **common areas** clean and safe;
3. maintain in good and safe working order all **electrical, plumbing, sanitary, heating, ventilating, air-conditioning, kitchen, and other facilities and appliances**, including elevators, supplied or required to be supplied by the landlord;
4. provide and maintain receptacles for **ashes, garbage, rubbish, and other waste** and arrange removal;
5. supply **running water and reasonable amounts of hot water and heat at all times**, insofar as energy conditions permit — except where the building is constructed so that heat or hot water is generated by an installation within the tenant's exclusive control and supplied by a direct public utility connection, or where the premises have **no well and no direct public utility water connection** and the rental agreement **specifically states** that the tenant waives the landlord's duty to supply running or hot water;
6. if the tenant requests, provide and maintain **locks and keys** reasonably adequate to ensure safety of person and property; and
7. provide **smoke detection and carbon monoxide detection devices** as required under **AS 18.70.095**.

**AS 34.03.100(b)** is Alaska's rural provision: a landlord of a **single family residence in an undeveloped rural area, or where public sewer or water service has never been connected**, is **not liable** for breach of (a)(3) or (a)(5) if the unit **did not have** running water, hot water, sewage, or sanitary facilities from a private system at the beginning of the rental agreement. It excuses what was never there; it does not excuse letting a working system fail.

**AS 34.03.100(c):** for a **one- or two-family residence**, the parties may agree **in writing** that the tenant perform the landlord's duties under (a)(4), (5), (6), and (7); the tenant may agree to perform (a)(3) duties only **where rent exceeds $2,000 a month**; and they may agree in writing that the tenant perform specified repairs, maintenance, alterations, and remodeling — but **the tenant may not agree to maintain elevators**. Such agreements are allowed **only if entered into in good faith and not to evade the landlord's obligations**.

**AS 34.03.100(d):** for a dwelling unit **other than a single family residence**, a tenant-performs-work agreement is valid only if made in good faith and not to evade obligations, **set out in a separate writing signed by the parties and supported by adequate consideration**, and only if it does not diminish or affect the landlord's obligation to other tenants. **AS 34.03.100(e):** the landlord may not treat performance of such an agreement as a condition of an obligation under the rental agreement.

**AS 34.03.110** limits liability on transfer of the landlord's interest, on the terms it states.

### 5.2 The tenant's duty — AS 34.03.120

The tenant shall keep the occupied part clean and safe as conditions permit; dispose of ashes, rubbish, garbage, and waste cleanly and safely; keep plumbing fixtures clean as conditions permit; use facilities and appliances reasonably; and **maintain smoke and carbon monoxide detection devices** as AS 18.70.095 requires.

The tenant **may not**: deliberately or negligently destroy, deface, damage, impair, or remove part of the premises or knowingly permit another to; **unreasonably disturb** a neighbour's peaceful enjoyment or permit guests to; **change the locks** without the landlord's prior written agreement — except in an emergency when the landlord cannot be contacted after reasonable effort, and then the tenant must **within five days** give the landlord a set of keys and written notice of the change; unreasonably engage in or permit conduct resulting in a fee under a municipal ordinance adopted under AS 29.35.125; or allow occupancy to exceed the number allowed by law, by a covenant limiting the landlord's use, or by the rental agreement.

**AS 34.03.120(b):** the tenant may not knowingly engage at the premises in prostitution, illegal activity involving a place of prostitution, illegal activity involving alcoholic beverages, illegal activity involving gambling or promoting gambling, or illegal activity involving a controlled substance or imitation controlled substance — or knowingly permit others on the premises to do so.

**AS 34.03.120(c):** on terminating, the tenant shall leave the premises in **substantially the same condition, except normal wear and tear**, as at the beginning — including, **in the landlord's discretion, professionally cleaning the carpets if the carpets were professionally cleaned immediately before the tenancy began**.

### 5.3 Access — AS 34.03.140

**(a)** The tenant may not **unreasonably withhold consent** for the landlord to enter to inspect, make necessary or agreed repairs, decorations, alterations, or improvements, supply necessary or agreed services, remove the landlord's personal property not covered by a written rental agreement, or **exhibit the unit to prospective or actual purchasers, mortgagees, tenants, workers, or contractors**.

**(b)** The landlord **may enter without consent in an emergency**.

**(c)** The landlord **may not abuse the right of access or use it to harass**. Except in an emergency or where impracticable, the landlord shall give **at least 24 hours notice** of intention to enter, and may enter **only at reasonable times and with the tenant's consent**.

**(d)** The landlord has **no right of access** except as .140 permits, by court order, or as AS 34.03.230(b) permits — unless the tenant has abandoned or surrendered the premises.

**AS 34.03.300** supplies the remedies. If the **tenant** refuses lawful access, the landlord may obtain injunctive relief or terminate, recovering **actual damages or one month's rent, whichever is greater**, and giving at least **10 days'** written notice if terminating. If the **landlord** enters unlawfully, enters lawfully but unreasonably, or makes repeated otherwise-lawful demands that unreasonably harass, the tenant may obtain injunctive relief or terminate, recovering **actual damages or one month's rent, whichever is greater, plus court costs and reasonable attorney fees**, on 10 days' written notice.

**This is a listing problem, not only a management problem.** Selling an occupied rental means 24 hours notice and the tenant's consent for each showing. Build it into the listing conversation before the sign goes up.

### 5.4 Ending a tenancy

**AS 34.03.290(a):** while rent is current, either party may terminate a **week to week** tenancy by written notice given **at least 14 days** before the termination date specified.

**AS 34.03.290(b):** either party may terminate a **month to month** tenancy by written notice given **at least 30 days before the rental due date** specified in the notice.

**AS 34.03.290(c):** if the tenant holds over without consent, the landlord may, after serving a notice to quit under AS 09.45.100 – 09.45.105, bring an action for possession — and if the holdover is **wilful and not in good faith**, may recover **up to one and one-half times the actual damages**. If the landlord consents to continued occupancy, AS 34.03.020 applies.

**AS 34.03.220 — termination for the tenant's breach.**

| Ground | Notice |
|---|---|
| Deliberate infliction of **substantial damage** (loss, destruction, or defacement attributable to deliberate damage **exceeding $400**) in breach of .120(a)(5), **or** prostitution or other illegal activity under .120(b) | Written notice to quit specifying the act; agreement terminates on a date **not less than 24 hours nor more than five days** after service — .220(a)(1) |
| Material noncompliance with the rental agreement, or noncompliance with .120 **materially affecting health and safety**, other than deliberate substantial damage or a .220(e) utility matter | Written notice to quit specifying the acts and omissions; termination **not less than 10 days** after service. **If the breach is remediable and the tenant adequately remedies it before that date, the agreement does not terminate.** In the absence of due care, if substantially the same act recurs **within six months**, the landlord may terminate on **at least five days'** written notice — .220(a)(2) |
| **Rent unpaid when due** | Written notice of nonpayment and of the intention to terminate; if the tenant fails to pay **in full within seven days** after that notice, the tenancy terminates unless the landlord agrees otherwise, and the landlord may immediately recover possession. **Only one written notice of default need be given as to any one default.** The landlord may accept a **partial payment** and extend the eviction date accordingly — .220(b) |
| A **public utility** (electricity, natural gas, water) discontinues service for the tenant's nonpayment | Written notice to quit; tenancy terminates **five days** after service. If **within three days** the tenant reinstates service and repays the landlord any amounts the landlord paid to reinstate it, and no damage occurred from the discontinuance, the agreement does not terminate. Recurrence within six months: **at least three days'** written notice — .220(e) |

**AS 34.03.240** addresses waiver of the landlord's right to terminate — accepting rent with knowledge of a breach can waive it. **AS 34.03.230** covers absence, nonuse, and abandonment; **AS 34.03.260** the disposition of abandoned property; **AS 34.03.270** the remedy after termination; **AS 34.03.280** the limits on recovering possession; and **AS 34.03.285** service of process on a tenant.

**AS 34.03.250: landlord liens and distraint for rent are abolished.** A landlord may not seize a tenant's belongings for unpaid rent.

**AS 34.03.210** gives the tenant remedies for the landlord's **unlawful ouster, exclusion, or diminution of service** — the provision that answers a landlord who changes the locks or shuts off the heat. **AS 34.03.180** covers the landlord's wrongful failure to supply heat, water, hot water, or essential services; **AS 34.03.160** the landlord's noncompliance generally; **AS 34.03.170** failure to deliver possession; **AS 34.03.190** the landlord's noncompliance as a defence to an action for possession or rent; and **AS 34.03.200** fire or casualty damage.

**AS 34.03.310 — retaliation prohibited.** Except as (c) and (d) provide, a landlord **may not retaliate** by increasing rent, decreasing services, or bringing or threatening to bring an action for possession after the tenant has complained to the landlord of a violation of AS 34.03.100, sought to enforce rights and remedies under the chapter, or organized or become a member of a **tenants' union** or similar organization.

**AS 34.03.320:** every duty under the chapter and every act to be performed as a condition precedent to exercising a right or remedy **imposes an obligation of good faith** in its performance or enforcement.

**AS 34.03.345** provides for mediation and binding arbitration; **AS 34.03.350** for attorney fees. **AS 34.03.225** limits a **mobile home park** operator's right to terminate to enumerated grounds — a separate regime worth knowing exists.

#### Checkpoint 5.1

A month-to-month tenant is four days late with rent. The landlord serves a seven-day notice on the fifth day and the tenant pays in full on day six. What is the tenant's position?

*Answer:* The tenancy continues. **AS 34.03.220(b)** terminates the tenancy only if the tenant **fails to pay rent in full within seven days** after the landlord's written notice of nonpayment and intention to terminate. Payment in full on day six is inside the period, so the tenancy does not terminate and the landlord may not recover possession on that default. Note the landlord's two other options in the subsection: only one written notice of default need be given as to any one default, and a landlord who has given notice may accept a **partial** payment and extend the eviction date accordingly.

## 6. Property management under 12 AAC 64.550 – .580

### 6.1 The contract

**12 AAC 64.550(a):** a licensee engaged in property management shall conduct property management activity **in the registered name of the real estate company** with which the licensee is affiliated.

**12 AAC 64.550(b):** a licensee **may not conduct property management activity for another person without a prior written property management contract**, which must at minimum include:

1. the **specific responsibilities** of the property manager;
2. the **authority and powers** given by the owner to the manager;
3. the **period** of the agreement; and
4. the **management fee**.

Compare **AS 08.88.341**, which requires management contracts to be in writing and signed by both, with **exclusive** management contracts carrying a **definite expiration date** extendable only in writing signed by the client.

**12 AAC 64.550(c):** a manager may transfer funds between two or more accounts maintained for the **same** owner **only with written authorization** from that owner, and must make entries **fully identifying the transaction in each ledger affected**.

**12 AAC 64.550(d):** **selling or exchanging** a property subject to an existing management contract must be authorised by **specific language in the management contract or by a separate listing agreement**. Managing a property is not authority to sell it.

**12 AAC 64.550(e):** a licensee **who owns rental property** must disclose in writing to **all tenants and prospective tenants** that the licensee holds a real estate licence, and the name of the affiliated company. Compare **12 AAC 64.130(9)**, the same principle in a sales transaction.

### 6.2 The provision that connects the two halves of this module

**12 AAC 64.560. COMPLIANCE WITH UNIFORM RESIDENTIAL LANDLORD AND TENANT ACT.** "Failure of a licensee engaged in property management to deposit prepaid rents or security deposits in a trust account as required by AS 34.03 (Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act) is considered **fraudulent or dishonest conduct** within the meaning of AS 08.88.071(a)(3)(A)(iv)."

Read that carefully. A landlord who mishandles a deposit faces the AS 34.03.070(d) penalty of up to twice the amount withheld. A **licensee** who does it faces that **and** a disciplinary proceeding in which the failure is already characterised as fraudulent or dishonest conduct. The rules are not parallel; the licensee's exposure is greater.

**12 AAC 64.220(b)** supplies the bookkeeping: for each management contract, a transaction code identified with a specific landlord or property, a ledger tracking the landlord's funds received and disbursed, and **each tenant's funds tracked through a related but separate transaction code and ledger**.

**12 AAC 64.240(c):** the property management fee is disbursed from the trust account on a **regular monthly basis**, after that month's receipts are collected and that month's expenses disbursed.

### 6.3 Managing property you own

**12 AAC 64.570** governs a licensee who owns rental property, and it is detailed because the conflict is obvious.

**(a)** A licensee with **sole ownership** of rental property may manage it through the employing broker, or employ another broker or property manager to do so. Notwithstanding 12 AAC 64.550, the licensee or the owner's broker need not keep records or files of rental agreements or rental transactions **if the licensee being employed keeps them**.

**(b)** In addition to the AS 08.88.615 duties from module 01, such a licensee must:

1. disclose to **each prospective lessee or tenant** (A) the items in **AS 08.88.615(a)(6) – (8)**, and (B) the licensee's **ownership**, in accordance with **AS 08.88.391** — the conflict of interest disclosure, verbal and then written, beginning with the words "Disclosure of Conflict of Interest." underlined and in bold; and
2. provide the licensee's **broker** with, for each property owned: (A) ownership position and percentage; (B) copies of all financial records and rental agreements; (C) an update whenever that information changes; (D) **security deposits and prepaid rents held in the broker's trust account** in compliance with 12 AAC 64.200, **or** held in a licensee's privately owned trust account **to which the broker has access for oversight and review** — where "prepaid rent" means money demanded at the initiation of the tenancy to ensure rent will be paid, and **does not include the first month's rent or money received as security for damage**; and (E) **written documentation** to refund a deposit or deduct from one.

**(c)** Notwithstanding 12 AAC 64.200, **rents and other incidental income may be paid directly to the licensee as lessor** and need not go into the broker's trust account. Deposits are treated differently from rent, and this is the distinction to hold onto.

**(d)** The duration of the licensee–lessee relationship must meet **AS 08.88.660**.

**(e)** The Commission may impose a disciplinary sanction under AS 08.88.071 for violating the section, and a **recovery fund claim** under AS 08.88.460 may be filed by a person whose claim is based on fraud, misrepresentation, or deceit and who suffered a loss as a result.

**(f)** Where the property is owned by a **partnership, corporation, limited liability company, or other legal entity** in which a licensee has an interest, the licensee must disclose the ownership position and the entity to the broker. If one or more licensees **combined** have a **controlling interest**, or **participate in day-to-day operation or management**, they are subject to the section — except that instead of (b)(2)(B) the financial records and copies of rental agreements must be **made available to the broker for oversight and review**.

### 6.4 Community association funds

**AS 08.88.351(a)(4):** a broker authorised by an association's board to collect, control, or disburse association funds must keep a **separate account in a financial institution** for them.

**AS 08.88.351(b)(4):** a licensee must **promptly deposit** association funds or periodic assessment proceeds into a community association **reserve** account or **operating** account — and if at any time the operating account holds more than is estimated to be needed for the **next three months'** budgeted expenditures, transfer the excess to the reserve account **as soon as practicable**.

**AS 08.88.351(b)(5):** a licensee **may not commingle** one association's funds with another association's or with the licensee's own.

And **AS 08.88.161(5) and (6)** put community association management inside the licensing requirement in the first place.

#### Checkpoint 6.1

A licensee manages a fourplex she owns, holding tenants' security deposits in her own account. What must she do to comply with 12 AAC 64.570(b)(2)(D), and what happens under 12 AAC 64.560 if she simply keeps them in her personal chequing account?

*Answer:* She may keep the deposits in her own trust account only if it is a **trust account to which her broker has access for oversight and review** — **12 AAC 64.570(b)(2)(D)** gives her the choice of the broker's trust account in compliance with 12 AAC 64.200 or her own on that condition. She must also give the broker her ownership position and percentage, copies of all financial records and rental agreements, updates when they change, and written documentation for any refund or deduction, and must disclose her ownership to every prospective tenant under 12 AAC 64.550(e) and AS 08.88.391. A **personal chequing account** is neither. Under **12 AAC 64.560**, failing to deposit prepaid rents or security deposits in a trust account as AS 34.03 requires is **fraudulent or dishonest conduct** within AS 08.88.071(a)(3)(A)(iv) — a disciplinary matter on top of the AS 34.03.070(d) exposure to twice the amount withheld.

## Key terms

**Automatic lapse** — the AS 08.88.095(e) consequence of not completing the 30 post-licensing hours within one year and certifying within 30 days after.

**Non-noticed audit** — the 12 AAC 64.135(b) audit conducted without prior notice; a granted 24-hour delay means the auditor seals the records.

**Premises condition statement** — the AS 34.03.020(e) document that becomes part of the rental agreement and is presumptive evidence of condition under AS 34.03.335.

**Recovery fund** — the consumer fund capped at **$50,000 per licensee** by AS 08.88.475(a), distributed pro rata among claimants.

**Retaliatory conduct** — the AS 34.03.310 prohibition on raising rent, cutting services, or bringing or threatening a possession action after protected tenant activity.

**Two months' periodic rent** — the AS 34.03.070(a) cap on prepaid rent plus security deposit, inapplicable above $2,000 a month, with a separate one-month pet deposit under (h).

## Practice problems

**Problem 1.** A property manager holds a $2,400 deposit on a unit renting for $1,200. The tenant gives proper 30-day notice, moves out 31 August, and the manager finds $310 of tenant-caused damage. When must the notice and refund be mailed, and what is the exposure for getting it wrong?

*Working:* The tenant gave notice complying with AS 34.03.290, so the base deadline is **14 days** — but because costs are deducted for damages from the tenant's noncompliance with AS 34.03.120, AS 34.03.070(g) gives **30 days**, to **30 September**. The itemised written notice goes to the tenant's last known address with the $2,090 balance. A **wilful** failure to comply with .070(b) exposes the landlord to up to **twice the amount withheld** under .070(d) — and because a licensee is involved, 12 AAC 64.560 makes a failure to hold the money in trust as AS 34.03 requires **fraudulent or dishonest conduct** under AS 08.88.071(a)(3)(A)(iv).

**Problem 2.** A salesperson licensed 2 February 2026 completes 20 hours of the post-licensing education by February 2027 and nothing more.

*Working:* AS 08.88.095(a) requires **30** hours within one year. She did not complete them and cannot certify under (c). Under **AS 08.88.095(e)** the licence **automatically lapses 30 days after the end of the one-year period** — around 4 March 2027. The Commission may reinstate under AS 08.88.241, which covers a licence lapsed less than 24 months. Any licensed activity after the lapse is activity without a licence, which AS 08.88.401(g) makes a class A misdemeanor.

**Problem 3.** A landlord in an undeveloped rural area rents a dry cabin with no running water. The tenant demands the landlord install plumbing under AS 34.03.100(a)(5).

*Working:* **AS 34.03.100(b)** provides that a landlord of a single family residence in an undeveloped rural area, or where public sewer or water has never been connected, is not liable for breach of (a)(3) or (a)(5) **if the unit did not have running water, hot water, sewage, or sanitary facilities from a private system at the beginning of the rental agreement**. A dry cabin that was dry at the outset falls within it. Note the limits: it applies to a **single family residence**, and it excuses what was never there — not a system that has been allowed to fail.

**Problem 4.** A broker receives a Commission inspection notice on Monday for an inspection Wednesday at 9:00 a.m.

*Working:* 12 AAC 64.135(a) requires **at least 72 hours' advance notice** for an inspection, and this is roughly 48. The broker may raise it. But note that (b) allows a **complete audit with or without prior notice**, so a broker who treats the notice rule as a shield is misreading the regulation. The records required are broad — earnest money agreements, listing agreements, trust account records, disbursal records, **communications regarding transactions**, and closing statements for all principals.

**Problem 5.** Your seller's tenant refuses to allow showings, saying he never agreed to any.

*Working:* Under AS 34.03.140(a) the tenant **may not unreasonably withhold consent** to entry to exhibit the unit to prospective purchasers. But .140(c) requires **at least 24 hours notice**, entry **only at reasonable times and with the tenant's consent**, and prohibits abusing access or using it to harass. If the tenant refuses lawful access, AS 34.03.300(a) gives the landlord injunctive relief or termination on 10 days' notice, plus actual damages or one month's rent, whichever is greater. Practically: schedule showings in blocks with more than 24 hours notice, and settle it with the tenant before listing.

**Problem 6.** A buyer's licensee learns a claim was paid from the recovery fund against a licensee he cooperates with. What does that tell him?

*Working:* An award means someone obtained a final judgment, arbitration award, or settlement and claimed against the fund, which is capped at **$50,000 per licensee** under AS 08.88.475(a) and distributed pro rata under (b). Under **AS 08.88.071(b)** the Commission **may suspend** that licensee's licence, lifting the suspension only on an agreed repayment arrangement. Under AS 08.88.490 the fund is **subrogated**. And under AS 08.88.071(a)(5), disciplinary action is published in a newspaper of general circulation in the locale of the offender's registered principal office.

## Module summary

**AS 08.88.161 is broad**: selling, listing, collecting rent, property management, community association management, assisting, holding out, and even attempting all require a licence, and violating it is a class A misdemeanor.

**A salesperson needs 40 approved hours, age 19, E&O insurance, the examination, and an application within six months** — with the education completed within the **18 months** before application under 12 AAC 64.063(a)(2).

**AS 08.88.095(e) lapses a new licence automatically** if the 30 post-licensing hours are not completed within one year and certified within 30 days after. Diary it on day one.

**Discipline runs on AS 08.88.071(a)(3) and 12 AAC 64.130**, and two regulations — 12 AAC 64.260 for trust accounts and 12 AAC 64.560 for tenant money — pre-characterise specified failures as fraudulent or dishonest conduct.

**Audits may be non-noticed**, and a granted 24-hour delay means the auditor seals the records.

**The recovery fund caps at $50,000 per licensee** and comes back at the licensee through suspension and subrogation. **E&O lapses suspend the licence as of the day coverage stopped.**

**AS 34.03 caps deposits at two months' rent** (plus one month for a non-service-animal pet), requires trust deposit with per-tenant accounting, and imposes **14- or 30-day** refund deadlines with a **double-the-amount-withheld** penalty for a wilful failure.

**Access requires 24 hours notice and consent** except in an emergency — which makes selling an occupied rental a conversation to have before the sign goes up.

**Termination notice depends on the ground**: 24 hours to five days for deliberate substantial damage over $400 or illegal activity, ten days with a cure right for material noncompliance, seven days for unpaid rent, five days for a tenant-caused utility shutoff, and 14 or 30 days to end a periodic tenancy.
