## Learning objectives

By the end of this module the student will be able to:

- State the seven federal protected classes, identify the practices the Fair Housing Act prohibits, and apply them to ordinary licensee conduct.
- Identify the Alaska classes that go beyond the federal list, and explain why a fair housing violation is a licence matter under 12 AAC 64.130(19).
- State what RESPA section 8 prohibits, what section 9 prohibits, and what an affiliated business arrangement requires.
- Recognise the settlement disclosures RESPA and Regulation Z require and the timing that governs them.

## 1. Fair housing: federal law and the Alaska overlay

### 1.1 The federal statutes

**The Civil Rights Act of 1866, 42 U.S.C. 1982.** All citizens have the same right "to inherit, purchase, lease, sell, hold, and convey real and personal property." It bars **racial** discrimination, has **no exemptions**, and is enforced by suit in federal court. It is the oldest of these laws and the reason the Fair Housing Act's exemptions never cover race.

**The Fair Housing Act — Title VIII of the Civil Rights Act of 1968, 42 U.S.C. 3601–3631**, amended in 1974 to add sex and in 1988 to add disability and familial status. Implementing regulations are at **24 C.F.R. parts 100–115**.

### 1.2 The seven federal protected classes

**Race. Color. Religion. National origin. Sex. Disability. Familial status.**

**Sex** includes sexual harassment in housing (24 C.F.R. 100.600). HUD administers the Act as prohibiting discrimination because of **sexual orientation and gender identity** as forms of sex discrimination.

**Disability** — 42 U.S.C. 3602(h) — means a physical or mental impairment substantially limiting one or more major life activities, a record of such an impairment, or being regarded as having one. It expressly **excludes current illegal use of or addiction to a controlled substance**.

**Familial status** — 42 U.S.C. 3602(k) — one or more individuals under 18 domiciled with a parent or a person with legal custody, or with the designee of such a person; and it protects a person who is **pregnant** or in the process of securing legal custody of a minor.

### 1.3 What the Act prohibits

**42 U.S.C. 3604:**

- **Refusing to sell or rent**, or refusing to negotiate, or otherwise making a dwelling unavailable, because of a protected class.
- **Discriminating in terms, conditions, or privileges**, or in services or facilities.
- **Discriminatory advertising** — making, printing, or publishing any notice, statement, or advertisement indicating a **preference, limitation, or discrimination**. Note that this provision has **no exemptions at all**: an owner whose transaction is otherwise exempt still may not advertise discriminatorily.
- **Representing that a dwelling is unavailable** when it is in fact available.
- **Blockbusting** — inducing or attempting to induce a sale or rental for profit by representations about the entry into the neighbourhood of persons of a protected class.
- **Refusing a reasonable accommodation** in rules, policies, practices, or services when necessary to afford a person with a disability equal opportunity to use and enjoy a dwelling.
- **Refusing to permit a reasonable modification** of the premises at the tenant's expense when necessary for full enjoyment.
- Failing to meet the **design and construction requirements** for covered multifamily dwellings first occupied after 13 March 1991.

**42 U.S.C. 3605 — discrimination in residential real-estate-related transactions**, including making or purchasing loans and in appraising residential real property.

**42 U.S.C. 3606 — discrimination in brokerage services**, including denying access to or membership in a multiple listing service or other facility relating to the business of selling or renting dwellings.

**42 U.S.C. 3617 — coercion, intimidation, threats, or interference** with a person exercising or enjoying a fair housing right, or aiding another to do so.

### 1.4 Three practices worth naming

**Steering** — directing prospects toward or away from neighbourhoods based on a protected class. It is often done gently and with good intentions: "you'd be more comfortable over here," "let me show you where families like yours live." Both sentences are steering. **Show what meets the client's stated criteria, and let the client choose.**

**Redlining** — refusing or varying the terms of lending or insurance based on the racial or ethnic composition of an area rather than the merits of the applicant or the property.

**Blockbusting** — profiting from inducing sales through representations about who is moving in.

### 1.5 Assistance animals

An assistance animal is not a pet. Under the Fair Housing Act, allowing an assistance animal for a person with a disability who needs it is a **reasonable accommodation** — including in housing with a no-pets policy — and **no pet deposit or pet fee may be charged for it**.

This connects directly to module 07. **AS 34.03.070(h)** permits an additional deposit of up to one month's rent for a pet **that is not a service animal**, and .070(i)(2) defines a service animal as one individually trained to do work or perform tasks directly related to and for the benefit of an individual with a disability. The Alaska statute and the federal accommodation duty point the same way: the animal that is there because of a disability is not the animal you charge for.

### 1.6 The exemptions, and why they rarely help a licensee

**42 U.S.C. 3603(b)** exempts, from most of section 3604:

- The sale or rental of a **single-family house by an owner** who owns no more than three such houses at a time, **provided** the owner does not use a real estate licensee's services and does not use discriminatory advertising, with further limits on frequency; and
- Rooms or units in a dwelling with **no more than four independent family units**, one of which the **owner occupies** — the "Mrs Murphy" exemption.

**42 U.S.C. 3607** provides exemptions for religious organizations and private clubs on the terms stated, and for **housing for older persons** meeting the statutory criteria (which is the lawful way to have age-restricted housing without violating familial status).

Three limits make these of little use to a licensee:

1. The single-family exemption **evaporates the moment a real estate licensee is used** — which is every transaction you are in.
2. **No exemption reaches the advertising prohibition** in 3604(c).
3. **No exemption reaches 42 U.S.C. 1982**, so racial discrimination is never exempt.

### 1.7 Enforcement

A complaint may be filed with **HUD** within **one year** of the alleged discriminatory housing practice — 42 U.S.C. 3610(a)(1)(A)(i). A private civil action may be brought in federal or state court within **two years** of the occurrence or termination of the practice — 42 U.S.C. 3613(a)(1)(A). The Department of Justice may bring a pattern-or-practice action under 42 U.S.C. 3614.

Remedies can include actual and punitive damages, injunctive relief, attorney fees, and civil penalties.

### 1.8 The Alaska overlay

**AS 18.80.200(a)** declares discrimination against an inhabitant of the state because of "race, religion, color, national origin, **age**, sex, physical or mental disability, **marital status, changes in marital status, pregnancy, or parenthood**" to be a matter of public concern, and (b) makes eliminating it in the sale, lease, or rental of real property state policy.

**AS 18.80.240 — unlawful practices in the sale or rental of real property.** It is unlawful for the owner, lessee, manager, or other person having the right to sell, lease, or rent real property:

1. to **refuse to sell, lease, or rent** because of sex, marital status, changes in marital status, pregnancy, race, religion, physical or mental disability, color, or national origin — though the paragraph does not prohibit sale, lease, or rental of classes of property commonly known as housing for "singles" or "married couples" only;
2. to **discriminate in a term, condition, or privilege** relating to the use, sale, lease, or rental, on the same grounds and with the same proviso;
3. to make a **written or oral inquiry or record** of a person's sex, marital status, changes in marital status, race, religion, physical or mental disability, color, or national origin where the person is seeking to buy, lease, or rent;
4. to **offer, solicit, accept, use, or retain a listing** with the understanding that a person may be discriminated against, on those grounds **or age**;
5. to **represent that property is not available** when it is, or to refuse to allow inspection, on those grounds or age — including because of a class of **any person associated with** the prospect;
6. to engage in **blockbusting**; or
7. to make, print, or publish a **notice, statement, or advertisement** indicating a preference, limitation, or discrimination based on race, color, religion, physical or mental disability, sex, or national origin, or an intention to make one.

**AS 18.80.250** separately prohibits discriminatory financing practices, adding **parenthood** to the list of grounds and covering discriminatory inquiries by lenders.

Three things to carry away:

**Alaska protects classes federal law does not** — marital status, changes in marital status, parenthood, and age appear in Alaska's provisions and are not federal protected classes under the Fair Housing Act. Alaska's list is what governs your conduct here.

**Paragraph (3) prohibits the inquiry itself.** You do not need to act on the information; asking or recording it is the violation. That is a stricter rule than a licensee trained on federal law alone will expect, and it should change how intake forms and casual conversation are handled.

**Paragraph (5) extends to association** — refusing inspection because of the protected class of someone associated with the prospect is covered.

**And it is a licence matter. 12 AAC 64.130(19)** makes "being found guilty of violating **local, state, or federal fair housing laws**" a ground for revocation or suspension of an Alaska real estate licence. Local ordinances count too, and several Alaska municipalities have their own.

### 1.9 What this means in practice

Show every client the properties that meet their stated criteria. Answer questions about schools, crime, and demographics by naming the sources where the client can look, not by characterising a neighbourhood. Take the same steps for every prospect: same qualification questions, same follow-up, same access. Do not accept a listing instruction to exclude anyone. Advertise the property, never the buyer or tenant. Treat a request for an accommodation or modification as a process to work through rather than a request to refuse. And keep records showing you did all of this the same way every time — the file is the defence.

#### Checkpoint 1.1

A seller instructs you not to show the house to families with young children because the stairs are steep. What do you tell her, and which provisions apply?

*Answer:* Tell her you cannot follow the instruction and that she cannot lawfully give it. Refusing to show or sell because of **familial status** violates **42 U.S.C. 3604(a) and (d)**; accepting or retaining a listing with the understanding that a person may be discriminated against is separately unlawful under **AS 18.80.240(4)**; and refusing to allow inspection on a protected ground violates **AS 18.80.240(5)**. Her concern about the stairs is answered by disclosing the condition to every prospect and letting each family decide — which is also what **12 AAC 64.130(14)** requires of you as to a known material defect. And under **12 AAC 64.130(19)**, being found guilty of violating local, state, or federal fair housing law is grounds to revoke your licence.

## 2. RESPA and settlement services

### 2.1 The statute and the rule

The **Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act**, 12 U.S.C. 2601–2617, is implemented by **Regulation X, 12 C.F.R. part 1024**, administered by the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau.

**12 U.S.C. 2601(b)** states the purposes: more effective advance disclosure of settlement costs, elimination of **kickbacks and referral fees** that unnecessarily increase the costs of settlement services, a reduction in the amounts homebuyers must place in escrow accounts, and reform of local title recordkeeping.

RESPA applies to **federally related mortgage loans** secured by a first or subordinate lien on residential real property designed for occupancy by one to four families. Business-purpose loans and certain other categories are excluded — see 12 C.F.R. 1024.5.

### 2.2 Section 8 — the one that ends careers

**Section 8(a), 12 U.S.C. 2607(a):** "No person shall give and no person shall accept any fee, kickback, or thing of value pursuant to any agreement or understanding, oral or otherwise, that business incident to or a part of a real estate settlement service involving a federally related mortgage loan **shall be referred to any person**."

**Section 8(b), 12 U.S.C. 2607(b):** no person shall give and no person shall accept any portion, split, or percentage of any charge made or received for a settlement service **other than for services actually performed** — the prohibition on **unearned fees** and **fee splitting**.

**Section 8(c)** permits, among other things: payment of a fee by a lender to its duly appointed agent for services actually performed; payment to any person of a **bona fide salary or compensation for services actually performed**; payments pursuant to **cooperative brokerage and referral arrangements between real estate agents and brokers** — the provision that makes ordinary commission splitting between cooperating brokerages lawful; and **affiliated business arrangements** meeting the section's conditions.

**Section 8(d), 12 U.S.C. 2607(d):** violation is punishable by a **fine of up to $10,000, imprisonment for up to one year, or both**, and by liability to the person charged in an amount equal to **three times the amount of the charge** paid for the settlement service.

A "thing of value" is construed broadly: money, discounts, salaries, commissions, fees, duplicate payments of a charge, stock, dividends, distributions of partnership profits, retained earnings, services at reduced rates, trips, tickets, meals, opportunities to participate in a money-making programme, and the payment of another person's expenses.

**The practical rules for a licensee:**

- Do not accept anything of value for **directing** business to a title company, lender, inspector, warranty company, or escrow agent.
- **Marketing services agreements** are lawful only if the payment is for **actual marketing services at fair market value** and is not tied to referrals. They are heavily scrutinised, and the enforcement history is unkind to arrangements that look like payment for volume.
- **Co-marketing** must be a genuine split of a real cost, in proportion to actual benefit.
- Ordinary **cooperative brokerage compensation** between real estate brokerages is expressly permitted by section 8(c).

### 2.3 Affiliated business arrangements

Where a licensee or brokerage has an ownership or other beneficial interest in a settlement service provider and refers business to it, section 8(c)(4) and **12 C.F.R. 1024.15** permit the arrangement only if:

1. the person referring gives the consumer a written **affiliated business arrangement disclosure at or before the time of referral**, in the form Regulation X prescribes, describing the relationship and the estimated charge or range;
2. the consumer is **not required to use** the affiliated provider — with narrow exceptions such as an attorney or an appraiser the lender selects; and
3. the only thing of value received is a **return on the ownership interest**, not a payment tied to referrals.

Compare **12 AAC 64.940(a)** from module 05: an Alaska broker must disclose in writing to the broker's principal the dollar amount or percentage of any rebate, compensation, or fee **paid to another broker** in connection with a sales transaction. Federal law and Alaska law each require disclosure of a different thing. Doing one does not satisfy the other.

### 2.4 Section 9 — title insurance

**12 U.S.C. 2608:** a **seller may not require**, directly or indirectly, as a condition of selling the property, that the buyer purchase **title insurance from any particular title company**. A seller who violates it is liable to the buyer for **three times all charges made for the title insurance**.

### 2.5 Escrow accounts and servicing

**12 U.S.C. 2609** limits the amount a lender may require a borrower to deposit into an escrow account for taxes and insurance, and requires an **initial escrow account statement** and **annual escrow account statements**. The cushion is limited to what Regulation X permits.

**12 U.S.C. 2605** governs servicing transfers, requiring notice to the borrower when servicing of the loan is transferred, and establishes the borrower's right to submit a **qualified written request** and the servicer's obligations to respond.

### 2.6 The disclosures and their timing

For most closed-end consumer credit transactions secured by real property, the **TILA-RESPA Integrated Disclosure** rules apply:

**Loan Estimate** — provided within **three business days** of the consumer's application, under 12 C.F.R. 1026.19(e).

**Closing Disclosure** — the consumer must **receive** it at least **three business days before consummation**, under 12 C.F.R. 1026.19(f). Three changes restart the three-day period: an increase in the APR beyond the tolerance, a change in the loan product, and the addition of a prepayment penalty.

**Special information booklet** — under 12 U.S.C. 2604 and 12 C.F.R. 1024.6, provided to applicants for a federally related mortgage loan within the time the rule specifies.

**Affiliated business arrangement disclosure** — at or before referral, as above.

For a licensee the operational point is the three-business-day rule. Build it into the closing calendar, and understand that a late change to a material loan term can move the closing date whether or not anyone wants it moved.

### 2.7 Where RESPA meets Alaska law

**12 AAC 64.130(7)** makes paying referral fees, sharing commissions, or otherwise compensating a person prohibited from receiving compensation under **AS 08.88.401(b)** grounds for revocation or suspension.

**AS 08.88.401(d)** prohibits a licensee from knowingly paying any part of a fee or commission to an unlicensed person (with the AS 08.88.401(e) exceptions), to another licensee **except through the licensee's responsible broker**, or to another licensee knowing that licensee will pass it to someone unlicensed.

So a referral arrangement in Alaska has to clear **both** gates. RESPA section 8 asks whether a thing of value was given for a referral of settlement service business. Alaska licence law asks whether the recipient is a person who may lawfully receive compensation, and whether it moved through the responsible broker. An arrangement can be lawful under one and unlawful under the other, and a licensee has to satisfy both.

#### Checkpoint 2.1

A title company offers to pay your brokerage $600 a month toward the cost of a shared advertisement in which each party's space is roughly equal, and the advertisement's fair market cost is $700. Separately, the title company offers a $50 gift card for each closing you send it. Analyse each under section 8.

*Answer:* The advertisement can be lawful; the gift cards cannot. A genuine **co-marketing** split is permitted where each party pays for the space it actually receives at **fair market value** and the payment is not tied to referrals — but $600 of a $700 advertisement for roughly half the space is not that. Paying more than the fair value of what it receives makes the excess a **thing of value** captured by **RESPA section 8(a)**, and to the extent it is a share of a charge unmatched by services actually performed, by **section 8(b)**. Restructure it to a true half, or decline. The **$50 per closing** is a fee for the referral of settlement service business on its face — squarely section 8(a), exposing a fine up to $10,000, up to a year's imprisonment, and treble the charge under section 8(d), and separately reaching AS 08.88.401(d) and 12 AAC 64.130(7). Decline it outright.

## Key terms

**Affiliated business arrangement** — a referral to a settlement provider in which the referrer has an interest; lawful only with written disclosure at or before referral, no required use, and a return limited to the ownership interest.

**Blockbusting** — inducing sales or rentals for profit by representations about the entry of persons of a protected class; prohibited by 42 U.S.C. 3604(e) and AS 18.80.240(6).

**Familial status** — one or more individuals under 18 with a parent or custodian or the custodian's designee, and persons who are pregnant or securing legal custody.

**Reasonable accommodation** — a change in rules, policies, practices, or services necessary to give a person with a disability equal opportunity to use and enjoy a dwelling.

**Steering** — directing prospects toward or away from areas based on a protected class.

**Thing of value** — construed broadly under RESPA section 8; not limited to cash.

## Practice problems

**Problem 1.** A prospective tenant with a mobility disability asks to install a grab bar in the bathroom and to be allowed a trained service dog in a no-pets building.

*Working:* The grab bar is a **reasonable modification** at the tenant's expense under 42 U.S.C. 3604(f)(3)(A); the landlord may, in appropriate cases, condition permission on restoration at the end of the tenancy. The service dog is a **reasonable accommodation** under 3604(f)(3)(B) — the no-pets policy yields, and **no pet deposit or fee may be charged**. In Alaska, AS 34.03.070(h) permits an additional pet deposit only for a pet **that is not a service animal**, so the statute reaches the same result. Refusing either is a Fair Housing Act violation and, under 12 AAC 64.130(19), a licence matter.

**Problem 2.** An Alaska landlord's application form asks each applicant to state marital status.

*Working:* **AS 18.80.240(3)** makes it unlawful to make a written or oral **inquiry or record** of a person's marital status where the person is seeking to buy, lease, or rent. The inquiry itself is the violation; nothing turns on whether the landlord used the answer. Marital status is not a federal protected class, so a licensee relying on federal training alone would miss this. Remove the field.

**Problem 3.** A buyer asks you which neighbourhood has "the better sort of people."

*Working:* Do not characterise neighbourhoods by who lives in them; that is steering under 42 U.S.C. 3604 and is reachable under AS 18.80.240(1), (2), and (5). Answer with objective sources the buyer can consult directly — school district data, municipal crime statistics, census data — and show every property that meets the buyer's stated criteria. Note in the file what criteria the buyer gave and what you showed.

**Problem 4.** A home warranty company offers your brokerage $75 per policy sold to your clients, and you do nothing but hand over a brochure.

*Working:* That is a **thing of value** for the referral of settlement service business, prohibited by RESPA section 8(a), and to the extent it is a portion of a charge not matched by services actually performed it also engages 8(b). Section 8(d) exposes a fine up to $10,000, up to a year's imprisonment, and treble the charge. Alaska adds AS 08.88.401(d) and 12 AAC 64.130(7). Decline it.

**Problem 5.** Your brokerage and a mortgage broker are commonly owned, and you refer buyers to it.

*Working:* An **affiliated business arrangement** is permitted only if you give the consumer the prescribed written disclosure **at or before the time of referral**, describing the relationship and the estimated charge or range; the consumer is **not required to use** the affiliate; and the only thing of value you receive is a **return on your ownership interest**, not a payment tied to referrals — section 8(c)(4) and 12 C.F.R. 1024.15. Get the disclosure signed and into the file at referral, not at closing.

## Module summary

**Seven federal protected classes**: race, colour, religion, national origin, sex, disability, familial status. **42 U.S.C. 1982 covers race with no exemptions**, and **no exemption reaches the advertising prohibition**.

**Alaska protects more.** AS 18.80.240 reaches **marital status, changes in marital status, pregnancy, parenthood, and age** in addition, and **AS 18.80.240(3) prohibits the inquiry itself**.

**A fair housing violation is a licence matter** — 12 AAC 64.130(19) covers local, state, and federal fair housing laws.

**RESPA section 8(a) prohibits anything of value for a referral**; **8(b) prohibits unearned fees and splits**; **8(c) preserves ordinary cooperative brokerage compensation**; **8(d) makes it a $10,000-and-a-year offence with treble liability**.

**Affiliated business arrangements need written disclosure at or before referral, no required use, and a return limited to ownership.**

**Section 9 forbids a seller requiring a particular title company**, on pain of treble the title insurance charges.

**The Closing Disclosure must be received three business days before consummation.** Put it in the calendar.

**Every RESPA question in Alaska is also an AS 08.88.401(d) and 12 AAC 64.130(7) question.**
