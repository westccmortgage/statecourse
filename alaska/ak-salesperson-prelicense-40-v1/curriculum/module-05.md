## 1. What Alaska requires in writing

### 1.1 The statute of frauds

Alaska's statute of frauds is **AS 09.25.010**. It makes certain agreements unenforceable unless the agreement, "or some note or memorandum of it," is **in writing and subscribed by the party charged or by an agent of that party**.

Four of its paragraphs decide a licensee's working life:

**AS 09.25.010(a)(6)** — "an agreement for leasing for a longer period than one year, or for the sale of real property, or of any interest in real property, or to charge or encumber real property."

**AS 09.25.010(a)(7)** — "an agreement concerning real property made by an agent of the party sought to be charged **unless the authority of the agent is in writing**."

**AS 09.25.010(a)(8)** — "an agreement authorizing or employing an agent or broker to sell or purchase real estate **for compensation or commission**." The paragraph then does something unusual and worth reading twice: if the memorandum is in writing, subscribed by the party to be charged or by a lawfully authorised agent, contains a description of the property sufficient for identification, authorises or employs the named agent or broker to sell the property, and **expresses with reasonable certainty the amount of the commission or compensation to be paid**, then the agreement "is not unenforceable for failure to state a consideration."

**AS 09.25.010(b)** — no estate or interest in real property, **other than a lease for a term not exceeding one year**, may be created, transferred, or declared other than by operation of law or by a written instrument subscribed by the party creating it or that party's agent under written authority.

Read (a)(7) and (b) together. In Alaska, an agent acting on a principal's behalf in a real property matter needs **written authority**, and an agreement made by an agent without it is unenforceable against the principal. That is the legal underpinning of every signed listing agreement and every written buyer representation agreement you will ever take.

### 1.2 Alaska's licence-law writing rule sits on top

The statute of frauds is a rule about enforceability between the parties. **AS 08.88.341** is a rule about your licence, and it is stricter:

> "All real estate listings or management contracts must be in writing and must be signed by the broker or associated licensee of the broker and by the client or an authorized representative of the client for whose benefit the real estate licensee will act. All real estate **exclusive** listings or management contracts must have a **definite expiration date** that may be renewed or extended only by a written agreement signed by the client or the client's authorized representative."

Three obligations, and each is separately enforceable against your licence:

1. **Every** listing and management contract in writing — not only the exclusive ones, and not only the ones over a year.
2. Signed by **both** sides: the broker or the broker's associated licensee, and the client or an authorised representative.
3. **Exclusive** listings and management contracts must carry a definite expiration date, extendable only in writing signed by the client.

An exclusive listing that says "until sold" has no definite expiration date and does not comply. An oral extension of an expiring listing does not comply either, however clearly both parties remember agreeing to it.

### 1.3 And the compensation rule

**AS 08.88.361:** "A commission is earned when the real estate broker fulfills the terms of a written personal services contract."

That single sentence is the whole Alaska law of when you get paid. The measure is not "procuring cause" as a matter of general doctrine; it is the terms of the written contract. If the contract says the commission is earned on a ready, willing, and able buyer, that is when it is earned. If it says on closing, that is when it is earned. **Write it the way you mean it**, because that document is what a court will read.

Note who earns it. The **broker** earns the commission. **AS 08.88.331** requires that an active salesperson or associate broker perform licensed activity only through the employing broker, and that all money collected in trust be **immediately turned over to the broker** or the broker's authorised representative.

### 1.4 Advertising authority

**12 AAC 64.130(6)** makes it grounds for revocation or suspension to advertise a property for sale, lease, or rent "without first obtaining the **written** authority of the owner or the owner's authorized agent." A verbal go-ahead to put it on the market is not written authority.

#### Checkpoint 1.1

A seller tells you on the phone to extend the exclusive listing that expires Friday for another sixty days, and you do. What has gone wrong, and under which provision?

*Answer:* An oral extension. **AS 08.88.341** requires all real estate listings to be in writing signed by both sides, and requires an **exclusive** listing to carry a definite expiration date that "may be renewed or extended only by a written agreement signed by the client or the client's authorized representative." The phone call extends nothing. The listing expires Friday, and after that any advertising continues without written owner authority, which **12 AAC 64.130(6)** makes grounds for revocation or suspension. Prepare a written extension and get it signed before Friday.

## 2. AS 08.88.405 and the limits of preparation

### 2.1 The Alaska difference

This is the part of Alaska practice that most surprises a licensee trained anywhere else, so read the statute exactly as written:

> **AS 08.88.405. Preparation of documents.** "Notwithstanding AS 08.08, a person licensed as a real estate broker, associate real estate broker, or real estate salesperson under this chapter **may prepare real property contracts, earnest money agreements, leases, and other documents related to real property** if the documents are prepared by the person **in the course of the person's work** as a licensed real estate broker, associate real estate broker, or real estate salesperson under this chapter."

AS 08.08 is Alaska's chapter on the practice of law. "Notwithstanding AS 08.08" means the legislature carved this activity out of the unauthorised-practice prohibition on purpose.

In California a licensee filling in anything beyond a standard form runs into unauthorised practice. In Alaska the legislature said the opposite. A course that teaches the California rule in Alaska teaches the student to be afraid of something the statute permits — and, worse, teaches nothing about the conditions that actually bound the permission.

### 2.2 The three conditions

The authority is not unlimited. It is bounded by the words of the statute itself.

**It is personal to the licensee.** The statute names brokers, associate brokers, and salespersons licensed under AS 08.88. An unlicensed assistant does not have it.

**It covers documents "related to real property."** Real property contracts, earnest money agreements, and leases are named. A promissory note, a deed of trust, a business-entity formation, a will, a divorce settlement, and a tax election are not real property documents merely because a house is somewhere in the background.

**It applies only "in the course of the person's work" as a licensee.** Preparing a lease for a transaction you are handling is within it. Preparing a lease as a favour for a neighbour whose property you are not engaged on is not licensed work, and the carve-out from AS 08.08 does not reach it.

### 2.3 Where preparation stops and advice begins

AS 08.88.405 authorises **preparing a document**. It does not authorise **giving legal advice about it**, and the distinction has real content.

Filling in a purchase agreement's price, closing date, contingency dates, personal property, and financing terms as the parties have agreed them is preparation. Drafting a custom clause that allocates a title risk between the parties, or telling a seller what her exposure will be if she signs it, is a legal question about legal consequences.

And Alaska has already told you what to do when you reach one. **AS 08.88.620(3)** makes it a duty owed to a represented person to **advise the person to seek expert advice on matters relating to the transaction that are beyond the licensee's expertise**. Not a courtesy — a statutory duty, covered in module 01.

So the two statutes fit together cleanly:

| The act | Authority |
|---|---|
| Preparing the earnest money agreement the parties agreed to | AS 08.88.405 permits it |
| Drafting a bespoke indemnity to solve a title problem | Beyond ordinary licensee expertise — AS 08.88.620(3) applies |
| Telling a buyer what a clause obliges him to do if the well fails | Legal advice — AS 08.88.620(3) applies |
| Preparing a lease for a management client | AS 08.88.405 permits it |
| Preparing a deed to convey the property | Not a contract, and conveyancing carries consequences that belong with counsel or the title company |

**12 AAC 64.130(14)** is the other side of the same coin: failing to disclose to a prospective buyer a **known material defect** in the condition of, or a **known legal defect** pertaining to, the offered real estate is grounds for revocation or suspension. You must disclose what you know. You need not — and must not — invent a legal opinion about it.

### 2.4 Broker review

**AS 08.88.403** allows a real estate broker to contract with and pay an attorney or an associate broker to **assist the broker to review a real estate transaction before the transaction closes**. Alaska anticipated that documents prepared under .405 would be reviewed, and made room for it to be paid for.

#### Checkpoint 2.1

Your buyer wants a clause making the sale contingent on his ability to subdivide the parcel within eighteen months. Under AS 08.88.405 may you prepare the purchase agreement? Under AS 08.88.620(3) what else do you owe him?

*Answer:* Yes, you may prepare it. **AS 08.88.405** authorises an Alaska licensee to prepare real property contracts and earnest money agreements in the course of licensed work, and a subdivision contingency negotiated by the parties is a term of that contract. What you owe him in addition is **AS 08.88.620(3)**: a duty to advise a represented person to seek expert advice on matters beyond your expertise. Whether an eighteen-month subdivision is achievable, and what happens to his money if it is not, are planning and legal questions. Prepare the document, recommend counsel and the platting authority, and record the recommendation in the file. **AS 08.88.403** exists so the broker can arrange and pay for that review.

## 3. Formation: offer, counteroffer, acceptance

### 3.1 The elements

An enforceable contract requires:

- **Competent parties.**
- **Mutual assent** — an offer and an acceptance of that offer.
- **Consideration** — something of value bargained for on each side.
- **A lawful object.**
- **A writing**, where AS 09.25.010 requires one.

Real estate contracts in Alaska are ordinarily **bilateral** (a promise for a promise), **express**, and **executory** until closing performs them.

### 3.2 Offer

An offer is a manifestation of willingness to enter a bargain, made so that the other party understands assent will conclude it. It must be definite enough that a court could enforce it: parties, property, price, and the essential terms.

An offer can be **revoked** any time before acceptance is communicated, even if it says it will stay open — unless the offeree gave consideration for the promise to keep it open, which makes it an **option**.

An offer terminates by revocation, by rejection, by counteroffer, by lapse of its stated time, by death or incapacity of the offeror before acceptance, or by destruction of the subject matter.

### 3.3 Counteroffer

**A counteroffer rejects the original offer and makes a new one.** This is the point students most often get wrong in practice rather than on the exam. Once your seller counters at $455,000, the buyer's $440,000 offer is dead. If the buyer then walks, the seller cannot accept the $440,000 — it no longer exists to accept.

Any change to a material term is a counteroffer: price, closing date, what conveys, who pays what, the contingency dates. Initialling a changed closing date and sending it back is a counteroffer whether or not the form calls it one.

### 3.4 Acceptance

Acceptance must be **unqualified** and **communicated**. In Alaska practice, communication is a signed acceptance delivered to the offeror or the offeror's licensee within the time the offer allows. A signed acceptance sitting in a drawer has not been communicated, and the offer can still be revoked underneath it.

### 3.5 The duty to present offers

**12 AAC 64.130(13)** makes it grounds for revocation or suspension to fail "to submit to the seller or the seller's licensee **all written bona fide offers** received before the seller accepts another offer in writing and the broker has knowledge of the acceptance."

Read the timing. The obligation to present runs until the seller has accepted another offer **in writing** and the broker knows of that acceptance. A low offer is still an offer. A verbal understanding with another buyer does not cut the obligation off.

This sits alongside **AS 08.88.615**, which imposes duties in every licensee relationship, and **AS 08.88.620**, which adds duties to a represented person — module 01.

### 3.6 Voidable, void, and unenforceable

**Void** — no contract ever existed (an illegal object).
**Voidable** — a contract one party may disaffirm (a minor's contract; a contract induced by fraud, duress, or undue influence).
**Unenforceable** — a real agreement a court will not enforce, most often because AS 09.25.010 required a writing and there is none.

**AS 09.25.020** lists exceptions where an agreement that fails the statute of frauds is still enforceable: full performance on one side accepted by the other; a memorandum defective only for an error in reciting past events; a memorandum defective only in a way reformation would correct; the party against whom enforcement is sought **admits** the agreement in pleadings or at any stage of a proceeding; and a contract of employment not exceeding one year.

These are legal doctrines, and a licensee's job when one comes up is to recognise it and route it — not to advise on it.

#### Checkpoint 3.1

A seller counters, the buyer does not respond, and two days later the seller says "fine, I'll take the original offer." What is the seller's legal position, and what do you do?

*Answer:* There is nothing left to accept. The counteroffer **rejected** the buyer's offer and replaced it, so the $440,000 offer no longer exists. The seller's "acceptance" is a new offer to the buyer, which the buyer is free to reject. What you do is prepare that new offer in writing and present it — and explain to the seller, before the next negotiation, that every counter kills the offer it answers.

## 4. The purchase and sale agreement

### 4.1 The essential terms

Whatever form your brokerage uses, an Alaska purchase and sale agreement has to settle:

- **The parties**, named as they will take title — which is where module 02's forms of ownership arrive in the document.
- **The property**, by a legal description sufficient to identify it, not by street address alone.
- **The price**, and how it is paid: earnest money, additional deposit, financing, cash at closing.
- **What conveys** — fixtures, appliances, and any personal property, itemised. In Alaska add the items the climate creates: fuel oil in the tank and how it is measured and prorated, snow removal equipment, the generator, the water system components, outbuildings.
- **Closing date and possession date**, which are not automatically the same.
- **The conveyance** — the deed type the seller will deliver, from module 04.
- **Title evidence** and who pays for it.
- **Prorations** — taxes, association assessments, fuel, rents.
- **Contingencies**, each with a date and a stated consequence.
- **Default and remedies.**
- **Signatures and dates.**

### 4.2 Contingencies are dates, and dates are the job

A contingency is a condition that must be satisfied or waived or the obligation does not mature. The four that carry most Alaska transactions:

**Financing.** Specify loan type, amount, maximum rate, and the date by which the buyer must have a commitment. Alaska adds AHFC programmes to the ordinary conventional, FHA, VA, and USDA menu — module 06.

**Inspection.** A date to inspect, a date to object, a date for the seller to respond, and a stated consequence if the parties do not agree. In Alaska this is where the well and septic sit, and where a heating system inspection matters more than it does in a temperate state.

**Appraisal.** What happens if the appraisal comes in below the contract price. Say it in the contract rather than discovering that the form is silent.

**Title review.** The buyer's period to review the commitment's exceptions from module 04 and object. This date and the arrival of the commitment must be set so that one precedes the other.

**A contingency without a date is not a contingency.** It is an argument waiting to happen. And a date that passes without written action is, on most forms, a waiver — which is why calendar discipline is a licensee's actual professional skill.

### 4.3 Time is of the essence

Where the contract says time is of the essence, the dates are material terms and a party who misses one is in breach, not merely late. Where it does not, a court may allow performance within a reasonable time. Know which your form says before you tell a client that a date can slip.

### 4.4 Amendments

Any change after signature requires a written amendment signed by both parties. An email in which both sides seem to agree is not an amendment to a contract for the sale of real property; AS 09.25.010(a)(6) required the writing in the first place and a modification of a material term needs the same formality. Prepare the amendment — AS 08.88.405 permits you to — and get it signed.

#### Checkpoint 4.1

A purchase agreement makes the sale contingent on a satisfactory well test but sets no date. Identify the risk to each party.

*Answer:* The buyer's risk is that a contingency with no date has no deadline to satisfy, so on most forms it either never matures or is argued to have been waived at closing — and if the well fails the buyer may find they have no exit. The seller's risk is the mirror image: an open-ended condition the buyer can invoke at any time, which prevents the seller from treating the property as sold or responding to a backup offer. Neither party can plan. Fix it with an amendment stating the date to test, the date to object, the seller's response period, and the consequence if the parties do not agree.

## 5. Earnest money and the trust account

### 5.1 What earnest money is

Earnest money is a deposit accompanying an offer, evidencing the buyer's seriousness. It is not consideration in the technical sense — the mutual promises supply that — and it is not a fee. It is the buyer's money until the contract says otherwise, and it is held in trust.

### 5.2 The chain of custody

**AS 08.88.331:** all money collected in trust related to a real estate transaction "shall **immediately** be turned over to the broker or the broker's authorized representative." A salesperson does not hold earnest money, does not deposit it, and does not keep it in a desk over the weekend.

**AS 08.88.351(a)(3):** the broker shall keep a **separate trust account in a bank** into which the broker deposits all earnest money deposits, purchase money, security deposits, contingency funds, collected rental money, rental receipts, and other money collected in trust, until it is appropriate to distribute it to the proper persons.

**12 AAC 64.180:** the account must be in a **federally insured bank authorised to do business in Alaska**, and its name must include the words **"trust account" or "trustee account."** The broker files the account name, number, and bank with the Commission, is the trustee, and is responsible for every signatory. Maintenance charges may be covered by the broker depositing **not more than $100** — the one permitted piece of broker money in the account. All trust accounts must be **demand accounts**.

**12 AAC 64.200:** money must be deposited in or mailed to the trust account **within five days following receipt**, "unless factors such as geographical location, weather conditions, or transportation facilities make such depositing impossible or unreasonable." That last clause is Alaska's, and it is there because a licensee in a village off the road system cannot always reach a bank in five days. It is an allowance for genuine impossibility, not a general extension.

**12 AAC 64.210:** if the account bears interest, that fact and the rate must be disclosed to the trustor, and **interest earned does not belong to the broker**.

### 5.3 Non-cash earnest money

Two provisions, and they operate together.

**12 AAC 64.130(12)** makes it grounds for revocation or suspension to accept as earnest money anything other than cash **unless** the non-cash substitute is **communicated to the owner before the owner accepts the offer**, and the acceptance of the substitute is **identified as a non-cash substitute on the earnest money receipt**.

**12 AAC 64.271** adds that when a non-cash deposit is approved by the seller to bind an offer, **control over the item deposited must be surrendered to the broker**, and the broker must inform the principal parties.

A promissory note, a post-dated cheque, a snow machine, a boat — each is a non-cash deposit. Each requires disclosure before acceptance, identification on the receipt, and surrender of control to the broker. A seller who accepts an offer believing $10,000 in cash is on deposit, when what is on deposit is a note, has been misled, and the rule exists because that has happened.

### 5.4 Records

**12 AAC 64.220** requires a **transaction code and a transaction ledger for each written offer to purchase** — not for each closing, for each written offer. The ledger carries the code, the legal description, the owner's name, and for every deposit the date, trustor, and purpose, and for every payment the date, cheque number, payee, and purpose.

A ledger is required **for every transaction, including those where the deposit is held by a cooperating broker, a title company, or another third party** — 12 AAC 64.220(d). The broker's file has to show where the money is even when the broker is not holding it.

Trust accounts must be **reconciled monthly** — 12 AAC 64.220(f) — by reconciling the bank statement with the check register, reconciling monthly deposits and expenditures with each transaction ledger, and **cross-checking the bank balance against the sum of the transaction ledgers**. If those three do not agree, something is wrong and it is found in the month it happened rather than in an audit two years later.

### 5.5 What a broker may not do

**12 AAC 64.250.** A broker may not:

1. Pay a salesperson's commission directly from the trust account.
2. Pay business or personal obligations from trust funds.
3. Use trust funds to pay the maintenance expenses of the trust account.
4. **Commingle** by depositing the broker's own funds, except the $100 under 12 AAC 64.180(d).
5. Withdraw funds without fully complying with the recordkeeping requirements of 12 AAC 64.220.
6. Refuse to provide an accounting to the principals for funds held in trust.
7. **Refuse to return a deposit to a prospective buyer whose offer was not accepted** — though the broker may delay until **one business day after the buyer's cheque has cleared**.

**12 AAC 64.240(b):** the broker must withdraw the commission from the trust account **within 15 days after the transaction has closed or otherwise settled**. Leaving earned commission in the trust account is itself a violation, because after the transaction settles it is no longer trust money.

**12 AAC 64.260** states the consequence plainly: failure to comply with 12 AAC 64.180 – 64.271, or failure to turn over records the Commission requests, "is considered **fraudulent and dishonest conduct**" within the meaning of AS 08.88.071(a)(3)(A)(iv).

### 5.6 Disputed deposits

When a transaction fails and the parties disagree about the earnest money, the broker is holding money two people claim. The broker may not simply decide. The path is the contract's dispute provision, a written mutual release signed by both parties, or, failing those, a legal process. What the broker must not do is disburse on one party's say-so, or hold indefinitely without accounting — 12 AAC 64.250(6) forbids refusing an accounting to the principals.

#### Checkpoint 5.1

A buyer offers a $15,000 promissory note as earnest money. List every step required before the seller accepts.

*Answer:* Four. (1) The non-cash substitute must be **communicated to the owner before the owner accepts the offer** — 12 AAC 64.130(12). (2) The acceptance of the substitute must be **identified as a non-cash substitute on the earnest money receipt** — the same paragraph. (3) **Control over the item must be surrendered to the broker** — 12 AAC 64.271. (4) The broker must **inform the principal parties** — also 64.271. Doing any of this after acceptance does not cure it. Whether a note is acceptable is the seller's decision; your obligation is that the seller knows what is actually on deposit.

## 6. AS 34.70 residential disclosure

### 6.1 The requirement

**AS 34.70.010:** "**Before** the transferee of an interest in residential real property makes a **written offer**, the transferor shall deliver by mail or in person a completed written disclosure statement in the form established under AS 34.70.050."

Three things are load-bearing.

**The timing is before the written offer**, not before closing and not with the counteroffer. The buyer is supposed to have the statement in hand when deciding what to offer.

**The obligation is the transferor's**, not the licensee's. But the licensee is the one who knows the rule exists, and a listing taken without the statement prepared is a transaction that will breach the statute the moment an offer arrives.

**Delivery is by mail or in person.** Delivery to the transferee's spouse counts as delivery to the transferee unless the parties agreed otherwise before the delivery.

### 6.2 The form is the Commission's

**AS 34.70.050** requires the Real Estate Commission established under AS 08.88.011 to establish the form. **AS 08.88.071(a)(9)** gives the Commission the power to "establish and periodically revise" it.

So the form is a regulator form and it changes. The statute requires it to include provisions notifying transferees:

1. That **they** are responsible for determining whether a person convicted of a sex offense resides in the vicinity of the property;
2. **Where** information about the location of convicted sex offenders can be obtained; and
3. That they are responsible for determining whether there is, in the vicinity, an **agricultural facility or agricultural operation** that might produce odour, fumes, dust, blowing snow, smoke, burning, vibrations, noise, insects, rodents, machinery operation **including aircraft**, and other inconveniences resulting from lawful agricultural operations.

Note what item 1 does. It puts the sex-offender inquiry on the **buyer**, and the licensee's role is to make sure the notice reaches them — not to research the registry and report on it.

**Because the form is revised periodically, always use the current version from the Commission.** A file closed on last year's form is a file closed on the wrong document.

### 6.3 The buyer's termination right

**AS 34.70.020.** If the disclosure statement or a material amendment is delivered **after** the buyer has made a written offer, the buyer may terminate the offer by delivering written notice of termination to the seller or the seller's agent:

- **within three days** after in-person delivery, or
- **within six days** after delivery by deposit in the mail.

Learn both numbers and what triggers each. A late disclosure hands the buyer a walk-away right the seller did not intend to give, and the clock depends on how it was delivered.

### 6.4 What disclosure buys the seller

**AS 34.70.030:** a transferor is **not liable** for a defect or condition in the property "if the transferor discloses the existence of the defect or condition in the disclosure statement."

This is the argument that actually persuades a reluctant seller. Disclosure is not a confession that creates liability; under Alaska law it is the thing that removes it. A seller who discloses the 1998 water intrusion has protected herself. A seller who conceals it has not.

### 6.5 Later changes and unknown items

**AS 34.70.040(a):** if information becomes inaccurate because of an act or agreement **after** delivery, the resulting inaccuracy is not a violation — but the transferor **is required to deliver an amendment**. And under **AS 34.70.080**, an amendment must be **in writing**.

**AS 34.70.040(b):** if an item is unknown or unavailable and the transferor or the transferor's agent has made a **reasonable effort** to ascertain it, an approximation may be inserted. It must be reasonable, **clearly identified as an approximation**, based on the best information available, and **not used to avoid the requirements of the chapter**.

That last clause matters. "Approximately 1985" for a roof nobody has looked at, when the permit record is a phone call away, is an approximation used to avoid the requirement.

**AS 34.70.060** requires that a person making a disclosure or performing an act under the chapter do so **in good faith**.

### 6.6 The consequences

**AS 34.70.090:**

- (a) A transfer is **not invalidated** solely because someone failed to comply.
- (b) A person who **negligently** violates the chapter or fails to perform a duty under it is liable to the transferee for **actual damages**.
- (c) A person who **wilfully** violates it is liable for **up to three times the actual damages**.
- (d) A court may also award the transferee **costs and attorney fees**.

Treble damages for a wilful violation is the number to remember. Alaska did not make this a technicality.

### 6.7 The two exemptions, and the waiver

**AS 34.70.110 — waiver by agreement.** The chapter does not apply if the transferor and transferee **agree in writing** that the transfer will not be covered under the chapter.

**AS 34.70.120 — exemption for first sales.** The chapter does not apply if the transfer is the **first transfer of the property** and the property **has never been occupied** — new construction, sold new.

**AS 34.70.070** is the limit on both: the requirements of the chapter "do not affect other obligations for disclosure required by law." A waiver under .110 waives AS 34.70. It does not waive **12 AAC 64.130(14)**, which makes failing to disclose a known material defect grounds for revocation of your licence, and it does not waive **AS 08.88.615**, which imposes duties in every licensee relationship. A seller can contract out of the statutory form. **You cannot contract out of your own licence obligations.**

### 6.8 What "residential real property" means here

**AS 34.70.200(3):** real property whose primary purpose is to provide a **single-family dwelling, or two single-family dwellings in one building**, including a manufactured home that has become real property under AS 34.85.010.

**AS 34.70.200(2):** "real property" includes a **unit** subject to AS 34.07 (horizontal property) or AS 34.08 (common interest ownership) — so condominium units are covered.

**AS 34.70.200(4):** "transfer" means transfers by **sale, exchange, installment land sale contract, lease with an option to purchase, other option to purchase, or a ground lease coupled with improvements**. A lease-option is a transfer for this chapter. A triplex is not two single-family dwellings in one building.

#### Checkpoint 6.1

A seller mails the disclosure statement three days after accepting a written offer. What right does the buyer now have, and for how long?

*Answer:* A right to **terminate the offer**. **AS 34.70.010** requires delivery **before** the buyer makes a written offer, and this was three days after acceptance. Under **AS 34.70.020** the buyer may terminate by delivering written notice of termination to the seller or the seller's agent **within six days** after the statement is deposited in the mail — three days if it had been delivered in person. The delivery method sets the clock.

## 7. The other documents a transaction requires

### 7.1 Common interest communities — the resale certificate

Module 02 covered ownership in a common interest community under AS 34.08. The transaction document is **AS 34.08.590**.

Except where a public offering statement is required, or the sale is exempt under AS 34.08.510(b), a **unit owner** must furnish to a purchaser, **before execution of a contract for sale or before conveyance**, copies of the **declaration as amended, the bylaws, the rules or regulations**, and a **certificate** stating fourteen enumerated items, including:

- the effect of any right of first refusal or other restraint on alienability;
- the **monthly common expense assessment** and any unpaid common or special assessment due from the seller;
- any other fee payable by unit owners;
- **capital expenditures over $3,000** approved for the current and next two fiscal years;
- **reserves** for capital expenditures and any designated portions;
- the most recent balance sheet and income and expense statement;
- the **current operating budget**;
- any **unsatisfied judgment** against the association and the status of pending litigation;
- **insurance** provided for unit owners' benefit;
- board knowledge of alterations violating the declaration;
- board knowledge of any **code or law violation**;
- the remaining term of any leasehold estate and renewal provisions;
- restrictions on what an owner may receive on sale, condemnation, casualty, or termination;
- in a cooperative, an accountant's statement on deductibility of taxes and interest.

**AS 34.08.590(b):** the association must furnish the certificate **within 10 days** after a written request and payment of a reasonable fee. A seller who provides it is **not liable** to the purchaser for erroneous information the association supplied.

**AS 34.08.590(c)** is the deadline that will bite you: a purchaser is not liable for an unpaid assessment greater than the certificate states, and the seller is not liable for the association's delay — **but the purchase contract is voidable by the purchaser until the certificate has been provided and for five days after it was provided, or until conveyance, whichever occurs first.**

So the buyer holds a termination right that does not close until the certificate arrives. **Order the resale certificate at listing**, not at closing, because ten days plus five days plus an association that is slow is how a closing date is missed.

**AS 34.08.600** requires deposits on the purchase or reservation of a unit from a declarant required to deliver a public offering statement to be **placed in escrow** — held in Alaska or in the unit's state, in an account designated solely for that purpose by a licensed title insurance company, an attorney, a licensed real estate broker, an independent bonded escrow company, or a federally insured institution — until delivered to the declarant at closing, delivered on the purchaser's default, or refunded.

### 7.2 Federal documents in an Alaska file

**Lead-based paint.** For target housing built **before 1978**, the federal Residential Lead-Based Paint Hazard Reduction Act (42 U.S.C. 4852d) and its implementing rule at 24 C.F.R. part 35 subpart A / 40 C.F.R. part 745 subpart F require the seller to disclose known lead-based paint and hazards, provide available records and reports, deliver the EPA pamphlet, give the purchaser a **10-day opportunity to conduct a risk assessment or inspection** (unless the parties agree in writing to a different period), and include the prescribed **Lead Warning Statement** and signed acknowledgments in the contract. **The agent must ensure compliance.**

**RESPA and TRID.** The Closing Disclosure and Loan Estimate, and the settlement documents generally, are covered in module 08 alongside the rest of RESPA.

**FIRPTA.** Where the seller is a foreign person, the buyer may have a withholding obligation under 26 U.S.C. 1445. Recognise it and send it to the closing agent and the parties' tax advisors — this is squarely AS 08.88.620(3) territory.

### 7.3 Compensation disclosure

**12 AAC 64.940(a):** in a real estate sales transaction, a broker shall **disclose in writing to the broker's principal** the dollar amount or percentage of transaction amount of any **rebate, compensation, or fee paid to another broker** in connection with that transaction.

**12 AAC 64.940(b)** excludes a franchise fee, internal office operating costs, and compensation to a licensee within the broker's own office. **12 AAC 64.940(c)** limits the section to sales and purchases — not rentals or leases.

Separately, **12 AAC 64.130(9)** makes it grounds for discipline to fail to disclose to **all parties** that you are licensed, or to fail to disclose the name of the broker or company under whom you are licensed. And **12 AAC 64.130(8)** requires the broker's registered business name in every advertisement, **including advertisements of property the licensee owns**.

### 7.4 Conflicts

**AS 08.88.391**, from module 01, requires a licensee with a conflict of interest to disclose it verbally and confirm it in writing as soon as possible after it is identified, in a written disclosure that begins with the words **"Disclosure of Conflict of Interest."** underlined and in bold. Buying the listing yourself, or having your spouse buy it, is a conflict, and **12 AAC 64.130(10)** separately prohibits using third-party purchasers, entities, friends, relatives, or business associates in a way that conceals the licensee's profit and identity.

#### Checkpoint 7.1

Your buyer is under contract on a condominium unit and the association has not produced the resale certificate eight days before closing. What is the buyer's position under AS 34.08.590(c)?

*Answer:* The buyer holds a live termination right. **AS 34.08.590(c)** makes the purchase contract **voidable by the purchaser until the certificate has been provided and for five days after it was provided, or until conveyance, whichever occurs first**. With eight days to closing and no certificate, the buyer can walk. The seller is not liable for the association's delay, but the deadline still moves — the association has ten days from a written request under .590(b), and five more run after delivery. This is why the certificate is ordered at listing.

## 8. Performance, breach, and the transaction file

### 8.1 Discharge

A contract is discharged by **performance** (both sides do what they promised — closing), by **agreement** (mutual rescission, novation, or an accord and satisfaction), by **operation of law** (bankruptcy, or the expiry of a limitation period), or by **breach**.

### 8.2 Breach and remedies

A **material breach** goes to the heart of the bargain and excuses the other party's performance. A minor breach gives a claim for damages but not a right to walk.

The remedies a real estate contract usually contemplates:

**Damages** — compensation for the loss caused.
**Liquidated damages** — a sum the parties agreed in advance, most often the earnest money. It has to be a reasonable pre-estimate of loss rather than a penalty, and whether a particular clause qualifies is a legal question.
**Specific performance** — an order to convey. Available for real property because each parcel is treated as unique.
**Rescission** — unwinding the contract and restoring the parties.

Which remedies are available in a given transaction depends on what the contract says, and telling a client which one they will get is legal advice. Explain what the contract provides; route the question of what a court will do.

### 8.3 Assignment

Whether a purchase agreement may be assigned depends on the contract. Many forms restrict it. An assignment transfers the assignor's rights; unless there is a novation, the assignor remains liable on the promises. Where the sale is financed, the lender's consent matters too.

### 8.4 The transaction file

**AS 08.88.351(a)(1):** a broker shall keep a complete record, **for three years**, of all real estate transactions in which the broker or the broker's licensees engaged.

**AS 08.88.351(a)(2):** the broker shall provide, on request, to **any principal** in a transaction an accounting for all money or property collected or held.

**AS 08.88.351(b)(1) and (2):** a licensee shall keep, for a minimum of three years, a complete record of transactions in which the **licensee was a principal**, and shall retain separately kept records about the licensee's own or client properties for three years.

**AS 08.88.351(c)** is the provision people get wrong. The three-year period **begins at the initiation of a transaction** and continues until **three years after** the date a listing agreement ends, a sales transaction closes or otherwise ends, a management contract ends, or another contractual or fiduciary obligation ends. It is not three years from the file being opened.

**AS 08.88.351(a)(6):** if records are delivered on termination of employment to a partnership, corporation, or business entity other than another licensed broker, the broker must **ensure by contract** that those records stay maintained and available for at least three years.

**12 AAC 64.220(e)** adds the trust records to be retained: transaction ledgers; records of deposit showing date, transaction code, amount, and trustor; all disbursement records including voided checks; all cancelled checks, bank statements, and monthly reconciliations; and **all transaction contracts and supporting documents**.

And the Commission can ask for all of it. **AS 08.88.351(a)(5)** requires records be made available to the Commission on request, and the licence-law module covers the Commission's audit and investigation powers.

### 8.5 What a good file contains

Signed listing agreement with a definite expiration date. Written advertising authority. The AS 34.70 disclosure statement with proof of delivery and its date. Every written offer received, and evidence each was presented. The executed purchase agreement and every signed amendment. The earnest money receipt, identifying any non-cash substitute. The trust ledger. The title commitment and the buyer's response to its exceptions. The resale certificate where AS 34.08 applies. The lead-based paint disclosure and acknowledgment where the house predates 1978. The AS 08.88.391 conflict disclosure if there was one. The 12 AAC 64.940 compensation disclosure. The settlement statement. Correspondence recording every deadline decision.

The test is simple and it is the one an auditor applies: **could someone who was not there reconstruct what happened and when, from the file alone?**

#### Checkpoint 8.1

A listing signed in March expires in September without selling. Until when must the broker retain the file, and under which provision?

*Answer:* **Until three years after the listing agreement ended** — so until September three years on. **AS 08.88.351(c)** runs the three-year period from the **ending** date, not from when the file was opened: three years after a listing agreement ends, a sales transaction closes or otherwise ends, a management contract ends, or another contractual or fiduciary obligation ends, whichever applies. The March signing date is irrelevant to the retention calculation.

## Key terms

**Contingency** — a condition that must be satisfied or waived before an obligation matures; without a date, it is not one.

**Counteroffer** — a response that changes a material term; it rejects the original offer and replaces it.

**Non-cash deposit** — earnest money in anything other than cash; requires disclosure before acceptance, identification on the receipt, and surrender of control to the broker.

**Resale certificate** — the AS 34.08.590 statement a unit owner must furnish before a common-interest contract is executed; the contract stays voidable until it is delivered and for five days after.

**Transaction ledger** — the 12 AAC 64.220 record required for **each written offer to purchase**, not each closing.

**Treble damages** — up to three times actual damages, the exposure AS 34.70.090(c) creates for a wilful disclosure violation.

## Practice problems

**Problem 1.** A seller wants to list with you today and provide the AS 34.70 disclosure statement "when we get an offer."

*Working:* AS 34.70.010 requires delivery **before the buyer makes a written offer**. Waiting until an offer arrives guarantees the statement is late, which under AS 34.70.020 hands the buyer a right to terminate — three days from in-person delivery, six from mailing. Explain AS 34.70.030 as well: disclosing the known defects is what removes the seller's liability for them. Prepare the current Commission form at listing.

**Problem 2.** Your buyer's offer includes a $20,000 boat as earnest money. The seller is inclined to accept.

*Working:* Under 12 AAC 64.130(12) the non-cash substitute must be communicated to the owner **before** the owner accepts the offer, and identified as a non-cash substitute **on the earnest money receipt**. Under 12 AAC 64.271, control of the boat must be surrendered to the broker and the broker must inform the principal parties. Doing this after acceptance does not cure it. Whether the seller should accept a boat is the seller's decision — the licensee's job is that the rule is followed and the seller knows what is actually on deposit.

**Problem 3.** A cooperating broker holds the earnest money on your listing. Does your broker need a ledger?

*Working:* Yes. 12 AAC 64.220(d) requires a ledger for **every** transaction, including those where the deposit is held by a cooperating broker, a title company, or another third party. The broker's records must show where the money is even when the broker is not holding it.

**Problem 4.** A buyer asks you to draft a clause allocating the cost of an unknown septic repair between the parties, capped at $8,000 with a shared overage.

*Working:* AS 08.88.405 permits you to prepare the real property contract, and a negotiated cost-allocation term the parties have agreed is within preparation. But a clause that has to survive a dispute about an unknown future cost is a clause with legal consequences, and AS 08.88.620(3) obliges you to advise a represented client to seek expert advice on matters beyond your expertise. Prepare the document reflecting the agreement; recommend counsel review before signature; note the recommendation in the file. AS 08.88.403 exists precisely so the broker can arrange that review.

**Problem 5.** A transaction closes on 12 June 2026. The listing agreement it came from was signed 3 January 2026 and terminated at closing. How long must the broker keep the file?

*Working:* AS 08.88.351(c) runs the three years from the later of the relevant ending dates: three years after the listing ends and three years after the sale closes. Both end at closing here, so the file is retained until **12 June 2029**. Had the listing expired unsold on 3 July and the property sold later under a new listing, each file would run from its own ending date.

**Problem 6.** Your seller accepted an offer in writing this morning. Another buyer's written offer, $12,000 higher, arrives this afternoon. Must you present it?

*Working:* 12 AAC 64.130(13) requires submitting all written bona fide offers received **before** the seller accepts another offer in writing and the broker has knowledge of the acceptance. Both conditions are met here, so the duty to present has ended. Best practice is still to inform the seller it exists — the seller may want it as a backup — but the disciplinary obligation has closed. Reverse the times and the answer reverses with them.

## Module summary

**Alaska requires writing in three overlapping layers**: AS 09.25.010 for enforceability, AS 08.88.341 for your licence, and AS 08.88.361 for whether you are paid.

**AS 08.88.405 lets an Alaska licensee prepare real property contracts, earnest money agreements, leases, and other real property documents in the course of licensed work** — a genuine difference from most states. It does not license legal advice, and AS 08.88.620(3) tells you what to do when you reach one.

**A counteroffer kills the offer it answers.** Every material change is a counteroffer.

**A contingency without a date is not a contingency.**

**Earnest money goes immediately to the broker, into a named trust account within five days, on a per-offer ledger, reconciled monthly** — and non-cash deposits require disclosure before acceptance, identification on the receipt, and surrender of control.

**AS 34.70 runs before the written offer.** Late delivery gives the buyer three days (in person) or six (by mail) to terminate. Disclosure protects the seller under .030. A wilful violation exposes up to treble damages under .090(c). The chapter can be waived in writing and does not apply to a never-occupied first sale — but neither waiver nor exemption touches 12 AAC 64.130(14) or AS 08.88.615.

**In a common interest community the resale certificate keeps the contract voidable** until delivered, plus five days. Order it at listing.

**The file must let a stranger reconstruct the transaction**, and AS 08.88.351(c) runs three years from the ending date, not the opening one.
