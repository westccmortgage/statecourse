**Course:** Alaska Real Estate Salesperson Prelicense Education
**Contact hours sought:** 40
**Delivery:** Correspondence, under 12 AAC 64.430
**Prepared:** August 19, 2026
**Status:** Draft. Not filed. No StateCourse course is certified in Alaska and enrollment is closed.

## The outline is the Commission's, not ours

Most jurisdictions leave a provider to propose a topic allocation and then defend it. Alaska does not. 12 AAC 64.063(c) states the topics and their contact hours, and a course that qualifies an applicant for an initial licence has to deliver exactly them.

So this outline adds nothing to the rule's structure and takes nothing away. Each module is one of the Commission's eight topics, carrying the Commission's own hour figure. Where the material naturally spans two topics — a purchase agreement is both a contract and a conveyance — it is taught in the module whose hours the Commission allocated to it, and cross-referenced rather than duplicated, so that no hour is claimed twice.

| Module | Topic, as 12 AAC 64.063(c) states it | Contact hours |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| 01 | Licensee relationships, disclosure, and conflict of interest | 6 |
| 02 | Forms of ownership | 4 |
| 03 | Property law, public and private rights and limitations | 4 |
| 04 | Forms of conveyances and recording of documents | 4 |
| 05 | Contracts and transaction documents | 8 |
| 06 | Financing instruments and accounting principles | 6 |
| 07 | Alaska real estate license law and Alaska landlord tenant law | 6 |
| 08 | Federal fair housing and RESPA laws | 2 |
| | **Total** | **40** |

**A contact hour is a minimum of 50 minutes of instruction** — 12 AAC 64.063(g)(1). The instructional design of each module is built to that floor: module 05 is 8 contact hours, which is at least 400 minutes of instruction, and the platform records active study time against it.

## What each module contains

Every module follows the same structure, and the structure is what makes the contact-hour claim checkable:

1. **Objectives**, naming what the student will be able to do.
2. **Instructional sections**, each ending in a **checkpoint** the student answers before the section is recorded as viewed. The number of sections scales with the module's contact hours rather than being fixed, so an 8-hour module is not the same shape as a 2-hour one.
3. **Worked Alaska examples** using Alaska statutes, Alaska instruments, and Alaska practice.
4. **Key terms** and **practice problems** with the working shown.
5. **A module assessment**, machine-scored, which the student must pass to proceed.

One instructional section per contact hour. It is a simple rule and it makes the audit trivial: a reviewer counting sections is counting hours.

## The end-of-course instrument, and why it is different here

12 AAC 64.430(b) requires a correspondence course to include **an unanswered examination, or a project activity, to be turned in to the instructor for grading upon completion of the course**, and requires that instrument to be submitted to the Commission for prior approval along with the course material.

This is the first state in this project where automatic scoring does not satisfy the rule on its own. Machine-scored module assessments run inside the course and gate progression; they are not the instrument this rule describes. The instrument is graded by the approved instructor.

**What is proposed:** a written end-of-course project — a transaction file review in which the student works a single Alaska transaction through the eight topic areas, identifying the licensee relationship and its disclosures, the form of ownership and what it requires of a conveyance, the property-law limitations affecting the parcel, the conveyance and recording steps, the contract terms and their deadlines, the financing structure and the settlement figures, the Alaska licence-law and landlord-tenant obligations engaged, and the fair-housing and RESPA obligations engaged.

It is submitted to the approved instructor, graded against a published rubric, and returned with written comments. The platform records the submission, the grader, the grade, and the date — because a grade with no record of who gave it is not evidence that anyone did.

**The instrument itself is filed with the Commission for prior approval**, as the rule requires, and it is not published to students in advance.

## Attendance and the strict attendance policy

12 AAC 64.410(a)(6) requires students to be held to a strict attendance policy whether in a classroom or attending a virtual course. For a correspondence course the platform's equivalent is completion evidence rather than seat presence: every section recorded as viewed, every checkpoint answered, every module assessment passed, and the end-of-course instrument submitted and graded. The course cannot be completed with any of those outstanding, and active study time is recorded against the 50-minute-per-contact-hour floor.

## Certificate

12 AAC 64.410(a)(9) prescribes the certificate's contents, and the platform holds each field:

| Field | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Name of the student | The enrollment record |
| City where the course was held | Recorded for a correspondence course as the student's city of record |
| Course title **and course approval number** | The certification record |
| Date the course was delivered | The completion record |
| Number of contact hours awarded | 40, on completion of all eight modules |
| Signature of the course instructor or sponsor | The approved instructor |
| Name of the course instructor | The approval record |

**No certificate can issue before the Commission assigns a course approval number**, because the number is one of the seven required fields — and none issues before the student has completed the course and instructor evaluation that 12 AAC 64.410(a)(8) requires.

## Materials

The instructional text is written for this course. Federal material cites the official federal source. Alaska material cites the Alaska Statutes or the Alaska Administrative Code by number. No third-party outline, competitor syllabus, or commercial exam-prep material is used, and no material is carried over from another StateCourse jurisdiction: Alaska practice is Alaska practice, and a course assembled from another state's answers is how a school ends up teaching the wrong law.
