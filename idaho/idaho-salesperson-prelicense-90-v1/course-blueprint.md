# Idaho Salesperson Prelicense — 90-Hour Course Blueprint (Draft)

**Status:** Draft for instructor/provider review; not approved and not an offer of course credit.  
**Audience:** Entry-level Idaho salesperson applicants.  
**Delivery intent:** Online self-study. Provider must confirm Idaho authorization and delivery controls before offering.

## Official requirements

Idaho DOPL currently states that a salesperson completes 90 hours: Module 1 (45 hours) covering fundamental real estate concepts and Module 2 (45 hours) covering applied listing, selling, buying, and closing transactions. DOPL also states 100% attendance, a final exam for each course with at least 70% to pass, and completion within the three years immediately before the license application.

Primary sources:
- https://dopl.idaho.gov/rec/rec-education-applicants/
- https://dopl.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Idaho-Real-Estate-License-Law-Rules-Booklet-FINAL-VERSION.pdf

The cited public page does not prescribe exam item counts. Any counts used in the assessment bank are an internal draft design choice and must be checked against Idaho provider/course conditions.

## Course outcomes

Learners will be able to explain property rights and land-use limits; apply Idaho licensing, agency, disclosure, trust-fund, and transaction rules using current primary sources; analyze contracts, financing, valuation, title, closing, and risk scenarios; communicate accurately and protect confidential information; and coordinate a transaction from intake through post-closing file completion.

## Module 1: Fundamental Real Estate Concepts — 45 hours

Nine units of five hours each. Suggested design per unit: 3 hours guided instruction, 1 hour worked examples, 1 hour application/retrieval practice. Actual time allocation and breaks must match provider requirements.

| Unit | Hours | Content | Applied outcome |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Property and rights | 5 | Real/personal property, fixtures, estates, bundles of rights | Classify conveyance items and explain evidence needed |
| Land and use | 5 | Legal descriptions, surveys, easements, encroachments, zoning, environmental constraints | Identify title, survey, and land-use questions |
| Ownership and transfer | 5 | Concurrent ownership, deeds, recording, title evidence | Compare ownership forms and map transfer steps |
| Valuation | 5 | Value vs price, appraisal principles, CMA limits, market conditions | Select comparable sales and explain adjustments |
| Finance | 5 | Loan structures, underwriting concepts, costs, consumer-protection boundaries | Compare options without promising qualification or terms |
| Contracts | 5 | Offer, acceptance, contingencies, performance, breach, remedies | Spot missing terms and calendar deadlines |
| Agency and conduct | 5 | Relationships, fiduciary duties, disclosure, conflicts, confidentiality | Map duties and compliant next steps |
| Fair housing and risk | 5 | Federal/Idaho protections, advertising, antitrust, fraud, cybersecurity | Repair risky communication and respond to a scenario |
| Ownership and management | 5 | Leasing concepts, operating costs, tax, insurance, maintenance | Build an ownership-cost and referral checklist |

### Module 1 assignments

1. **Property classification file:** Classify 12 items as real/personal property or fixture/chattel; state the evidence needed and draft a neutral contract clarification.
2. **Rights-and-title map:** Annotate a fictional parcel for ownership, easement, encroachment, lien, and land-use issues; list questions for title, survey, or legal professionals.
3. **Comparable selection:** Select three comparables from a fictional dataset, identify adjustments, explain limitations, and distinguish a CMA from an appraisal.
4. **Agency decision journal:** Map client/customer interests, duties, disclosure timing, and records in four scenarios.
5. **Fair-housing ad repair:** Replace discriminatory language with objective property facts and explain the change.
6. **Financing comparison:** Compare two fictional loan estimates, identify uncertainty, and draft a referral note without making a loan decision.

## Module 2: Applied Listing, Selling, Buying, and Closing — 45 hours

Nine five-hour units with the same suggested instructional pattern.

| Unit | Hours | Content | Applied outcome |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Idaho licensing and brokerage practice | 5 | License status, supervision, scope, advertising, records, discipline | Identify supervision/referral points |
| Intake and agency setup | 5 | Needs analysis, agency disclosure, representation, conflicts, privacy | Complete a mock intake and explain choices |
| Listing preparation | 5 | Verified facts, disclosures, pricing conversation, authorization, marketing | Build a fact-based listing launch checklist |
| Offers and negotiation | 5 | Offer terms, counteroffers, multiple offers, deadlines | Compare offers neutrally with uncertainties |
| Buyer representation and search | 5 | Buyer needs, search criteria, verification, touring safety | Build a criteria-based plan and due-diligence questions |
| Contract-to-close | 5 | Earnest money, contingencies, title, inspections, appraisal, funding | Maintain deadline and escalation log |
| Disclosure and inspection | 5 | Known facts, inspection requests, property risks, documentation | Separate observations from expert conclusions |
| Closing and settlement | 5 | Settlement statement, prorations, deed, funding, recording, possession | Reconcile a mock closing checklist and flag discrepancies |
| Post-closing and file quality | 5 | Delivery, records, privacy, complaint prevention | Submit a complete mock file and quality-control memo |

### Module 2 assignments

1. **Listing simulation:** Prepare verified-facts sheet, seller follow-up list, marketing authorization checklist, and launch schedule.
2. **Offer comparison:** Compare three fictional offers against seller priorities, including estimated net, contingencies, deadlines, and uncertainty.
3. **Buyer transaction plan:** Convert stated criteria into a search and due-diligence plan using fair-housing-safe language.
4. **Contract calendar:** Track milestones, responsible parties, completion evidence, and escalation triggers from a fictional contract.
5. **Closing reconciliation:** Review a mock settlement statement and list questions for the settlement professional; do not alter figures.
6. **File audit:** Apply a 20-point checklist for signatures, disclosures, timeline evidence, communications, and privacy.

## Assessment and teaching design

Each unit uses a lesson, worked example, application, knowledge check, and feedback. Grade assignments with a published rubric: accuracy 40%, reasoning 30%, documentation/communication 20%, professional boundaries 10%. Use a separate controlled final for each module. The proposed 70% threshold matches the cited DOPL page. The item pool is in assessments/module-exam-banks.md; item count is an internal draft choice.
