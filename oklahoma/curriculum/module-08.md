## Overview

This module exists because Oklahoma made it a **statutory duty**. Section 858-353(B)(1) requires a broker
to **inform the party in writing, when an offer is made, that the party will be expected to pay certain
costs, brokerage service costs and the approximate amount of the costs.** That is a duty with a trigger,
a form and a content requirement, and this module supplies the arithmetic to discharge it.

It covers the categories of cost on each side, proration in both directions, cash to close, seller's net,
and — most importantly — what makes every one of these figures an estimate and how to label it.

## Learning objectives

1. State the § 858-353(B)(1) duty and identify its trigger, form and required content.
2. Name the categories of cost a buyer pays and identify who quotes each.
3. Name the categories of cost a seller pays.
4. Distinguish a debit from a credit and identify which party receives each.
5. Prorate an item paid in arrears, in the correct direction, on a stated basis.
6. Prorate an item paid in advance, in the correct direction.
7. Count proration days correctly rather than estimating months.
8. Compute a buyer's estimated cash to close.
9. Compute a seller's estimated net proceeds.
10. Explain the four reasons every such figure is an estimate.
11. State how a cost sheet must be labelled and where its figures come from.
12. Relate an incorrect net sheet to § 858-312(2) and § 858-353(A)(1).

## Hour 42 — The duty, and the categories

### 42.1 The statutory duty

**§ 858-353(B)** — owed **only to a party for whom the broker is providing brokerage services**, and
**mandatory and unwaivable**:

> 1. **Inform the party in writing when an offer is made that the party will be expected to pay certain
>    costs, brokerage service costs and approximate amount of the costs.**

**Trigger:** when an offer is made. **Form:** writing. **Content:** the costs the party will be expected
to pay, **including brokerage service costs**, and **their approximate amount.**

Note the word **approximate**. The statute does not require a guaranteed figure and could not sensibly do
so. What it requires is a written estimate, made at the right moment, that a party can actually rely on
as an estimate — which is why Hour 46's labelling discipline is part of discharging the duty rather than
an add-on to it.

**And note "brokerage service costs" specifically.** Compensation is named. Section 858-353(A)(7)
independently requires compensation and fees to be disclosed **in writing before the effective date of
the contract**, with a term capped at one year and defaulting to sixty days if unstated. The two
provisions overlap and both must be satisfied.

### 42.2 What a buyer pays

**Loan costs.** Origination and underwriting fees; discount points if any; appraisal; credit report;
flood determination; any lender-required inspections.

**Title and settlement.** Lender's title policy; owner's policy if the buyer pays for it; abstracting or
title examination where used; closing or settlement fee; recording fees; survey if obtained.

**Prepaid items and escrow deposits.** Prepaid interest from closing to month end; the first year's
hazard insurance premium; the initial deposit into the tax and insurance escrow.

**Prorations.** The buyer's share of anything the seller paid in advance; and a **credit** for anything
paid in arrears that the buyer will pay later.

**Inspections** the buyer elects — general, termite, roof, septic, well, structural.

### 42.3 What a seller pays

Payoff of existing loans, **including per-diem interest to the payoff date**; brokerage service
compensation; the seller's share of closing or settlement fees; abstracting or the owner's title policy
where the seller pays; recording of releases; agreed repair or closing-cost credits to the buyer;
prorated items; and any liens or judgments that must be cleared.

### 42.4 Who quotes what — and it is not the licensee

**The lender** quotes loan costs, prepaid interest and escrow deposits. **The closing agent or title
company** quotes title, settlement, recording and the prorations. **The lender's payoff department**
quotes the payoff, to a stated date.

**A licensee assembles the estimate from those sources and labels it.** A licensee does not invent any
line on it.

## Hour 43 — Proration

### 43.1 Three questions before any arithmetic

1. **What is the annual amount?**
2. **What basis?** A 365-day year, or a 360-day banker's year with 30-day months.
3. **Who gets the day of closing**, and is the item paid **in advance** or **in arrears**?

**The convention comes from the contract, not from habit.** Getting the basis or the closing-day
convention wrong produces a number close enough to look right and wrong enough to matter.

### 43.2 The direction

Ask one question: **who has had the use, and who has paid?** **Whoever paid for time they will not have
gets a credit.**

**Paid in arrears — property taxes.** The seller has had the use and has not paid. The buyer will pay the
whole bill. → **Credit the buyer, debit the seller.**

**Paid in advance — annual association dues, an annual insurance premium being assumed.** The seller has
paid for time the buyer will have. → **Credit the seller, debit the buyer.**

### 43.3 Count the days

*Worked, in arrears.* Annual tax **$2,840**, closing **17 October**, 365-day basis, closing day to the
buyer.

Daily: 2,840 ÷ 365 = **$7.7808.**
Seller's days, 1 January through 16 October: 31+28+31+30+31+30+31+31+30+16 = **289 days.**
289 × 7.7808 = **$2,248.65** — a **credit to the buyer, debit to the seller.**

**Add the month lengths. Do not estimate "about ten months."** "About" produces a figure wrong by a
hundred dollars or more, and it will be wrong on the settlement statement too if nobody recomputes it.
And check whether the year is a leap year before dividing by 365.

*Worked, in advance.* Association dues **$480** paid annually on 1 January, same closing, same basis.

Daily: 480 ÷ 365 = **$1.3151.**
Buyer's days, 17 October through 31 December: 15+30+31 = **76 days.**
76 × 1.3151 = **$99.95** — a **credit to the seller, debit to the buyer.**

### 43.4 Debits and credits

A **credit** to a party is money in their favour. A **debit** is money against them.

The buyer's credits reduce cash to close. The seller's debits reduce net proceeds. Every prorated item
appears **twice** — as a credit to one party and a debit to the other — and the two must match.

## Hour 44 — The buyer's estimated cost sheet

*Purchase price $268,000. Loan $241,200 (90 percent). Earnest money $3,000 already deposited. Closing 17
October. Taxes paid in arrears, annual $2,840, 365-day basis, closing day to the buyer.*

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Purchase price | $268,000.00 |
| Loan origination, 0.75% of loan | 1,809.00 |
| Appraisal | 650.00 |
| Credit report and flood determination | 135.00 |
| Lender's title policy | 465.00 |
| Owner's title policy | 1,040.00 |
| Closing fee | 425.00 |
| Recording | 96.00 |
| Prepaid interest, 15 days at $41.86 | 627.90 |
| Hazard insurance, first year | 1,780.00 |
| Escrow deposit, taxes and insurance | 1,215.00 |
| **Subtotal** | **$276,242.90** |
| Less loan amount | (241,200.00) |
| Less earnest money | (3,000.00) |
| Less tax proration credit to buyer | (2,248.65) |
| **Estimated cash to close** | **$29,794.25** |

**Check the arithmetic yourself.** 268,000 + 1,809 + 650 + 135 + 465 + 1,040 + 425 + 96 + 627.90 + 1,780
+ 1,215 = **276,242.90.** Then 276,242.90 − 241,200 − 3,000 − 2,248.65 = **$29,794.25.**

**And note what the buyer actually needs to hear**, beyond the total: that the earnest money is not an
additional cost but a credit against it; that the tax proration is a credit because they will pay the
whole bill in due course; and that the escrow deposit is their own money going into an account, not a
fee.

## Hour 45 — The seller's estimated net

*Same transaction. First mortgage payoff $186,400 quoted good through 15 October. Brokerage service
compensation 6 percent. Seller's closing costs $1,540. Agreed repair credit to buyer $1,200. Tax
proration debit to seller $2,248.65.*

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Sale price | $268,000.00 |
| Less first mortgage payoff | (186,400.00) |
| Less brokerage service compensation, 6% | (16,080.00) |
| Less seller's closing costs | (1,540.00) |
| Less repair credit to buyer | (1,200.00) |
| Less tax proration | (2,248.65) |
| **Estimated seller's net** | **$60,531.35** |

268,000 − 186,400 − 16,080 − 1,540 − 1,200 − 2,248.65 = **$60,531.35.**

### 45.1 The payoff date, which is not a detail

**The payoff was quoted good through 15 October and the closing is 17 October.** Payoffs carry per-diem
interest. Two extra days at, say, $30.50 a day is another $61 — small here, and not small on a larger
loan or a longer slip.

**Always check the payoff's good-through date against the closing date**, and tell the seller in writing
that the figure moves if the closing moves. That is § 858-353(B)(2) — keeping the party informed — doing
real work.

### 45.2 Compensation, stated correctly

**Compensation is negotiable.** It is set by agreement between the broker and the party, not by any
board, association, listing service, custom or rule.

**§ 858-359(B)** provides that a broker receiving a fee **based on a selling price or lease cost** does
not thereby breach a duty. **§ 858-359(D)** provides that nothing requires or prohibits charging a
separate fee for each duty or service, and nothing requires a broker or owner of record to offer
compensation to any real estate professional.

**And § 858-312(4):** an **associate** may accept compensation **only from the broker with whom the
associate is associated.** Money moves from the closing to the broker, and from the broker to you.

## Hour 46 — Why it is an estimate, and how to label it

### 46.1 Four reasons every figure moves

1. **Payoffs carry per-diem interest and are quoted only to a stated date.**
2. **Closing costs are the closing agent's and the lender's figures**, and they change as underwriting
   settles.
3. **Prorations may rest on a tax bill that has not yet issued**, in which case the parties estimate from
   the prior year — and the contract should say what happens if the actual bill differs.
4. **Late changes move the total** — a repair credit agreed two days before closing, a change in the loan
   amount, a concession. Under **TRID** a change close to closing can trigger a redisclosure and a new
   three-business-day waiting period before consummation.

**That fourth point makes early decision-making a real service.** Getting changes settled early and
telling the lender promptly costs nothing and saves closing dates.

### 46.2 So label it

A cost sheet or net sheet:

- **says "estimate" on its face**;
- **shows its assumptions** — the basis, the closing-day convention, the payoff's good-through date, the
  tax year used;
- **names its sources** — lender, closing agent, payoff statement;
- **is delivered in writing**, which is what § 858-353(B)(1) requires anyway; and
- **is updated when a figure changes**, which is what § 858-353(B)(2) requires.

### 46.3 What happens when it is wrong

**A licensee who hands a seller a confident net figure that turns out $3,000 optimistic has created a
problem no apology fixes.** And depending on how it was presented, it is more than a customer-service
problem:

- **§ 858-353(A)(1)** — honesty and reasonable skill and care to all parties.
- **§ 858-312(2)** — **substantial misrepresentations or false promises in the conduct of business**,
  intended to influence, persuade or induce. A net figure presented as certain, relied on in deciding to
  accept an offer, is exactly the shape of that ground.

The prevention is not caution about producing estimates. **It is producing them from the right sources,
labelling them honestly, and updating them.**

### 46.4 The one-page routine

At every offer, for the party you serve:

1. **Assemble** the figures from the lender, the closing agent and the payoff statement.
2. **Compute** the prorations by counting days on the contract's basis.
3. **Write** it, labelled as an estimate, with assumptions and sources shown.
4. **Deliver** it in writing — § 858-353(B)(1).
5. **Update** it when anything moves — § 858-353(B)(2).

Do that every time and the statutory duty is discharged and the client is genuinely served, which in this
instance are the same thing.
