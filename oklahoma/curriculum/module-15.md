## Overview

What must be disclosed, what must be recognised and routed, and how a licensee manages the risk that runs
through everything else in this course. Oklahoma makes disclosure a **statutory duty to all parties** —
§ 858-353(A)(5) — and an independent **ground for discipline** — § 858-312(23) — through the Residential
Property Condition Disclosure Act, whose own text this project has not read and whose content is
therefore stated nowhere here.

## Learning objectives

1. State the § 858-353(A)(5) disclosure duty and to whom it is owed.
2. State the § 858-312(23) licence consequence and route the Act itself.
3. Explain why confidentiality does not override a disclosure required by law.
4. Recognise the common environmental concerns and route each correctly.
5. State the federal lead-based paint obligations and the licensee's own part in them.
6. Identify Oklahoma-specific property conditions that must be recognised and routed.
7. Explain what a licensee may and may not say about condition.
8. Define risk management and name the four responses to a risk.
9. Identify the recurring risk areas in a residential practice.
10. Describe the documentation discipline that manages most of them.
11. Describe errors and omissions coverage at the level of principle.
12. State what a licensee does on discovering their own error.

## Hour 83 — The Oklahoma disclosure duty

### 83.1 The duty — § 858-353(A)(5)

Among the seven duties owed to **all parties in a transaction**, **mandatory and unwaivable**:

> **Disclose information pertaining to the property as required by the Residential Property Condition
> Disclosure Act.**

**To all parties.** Not to a client. The other side of your transaction is owed this by you, personally, as
a matter of statute.

### 83.2 The licence consequence — § 858-312(23)

**Violation of the Residential Property Condition Disclosure Act** is an independent ground for the
Commission to impose sanctions.

**So an Oklahoma disclosure failure runs on two tracks at once:** it is a breach of the
broker-relationships duties under § 858-353(A)(5), reachable through § 858-312(3); **and** it is a
freestanding ground under § 858-312(23).

### 83.3 And what this course does not tell you

**The Residential Property Condition Disclosure Act's own text was not read from primary text for this
project.** So **this course states no form, no deadline, no exemption, no delivery requirement and no
content requirement from it.**

**That is a real gap and it is named rather than filled.**

**What you must do:** **read the Act.** It is the operative source for the single most common disclosure
question an Oklahoma residential licensee will face — which properties it covers, which transactions are
exempt, what form is used, who completes it, when it must be delivered, and what happens if it is
delivered late or not at all. Get the current form from the Commission and read the Act alongside it,
before your first listing.

### 83.4 Confidentiality does not override it

Module OK-03 Hour 14 established that **§ 858-353(A)(4)** names **four items and only four** as
confidential — willingness to pay more or accept less, willingness to accept different financing terms,
motivating factors, and anything a party **specifically designates** as confidential.

**And the same paragraph provides that confidential information may be disclosed where "the disclosure is
required by law."**

**So a seller cannot designate a defect as confidential and thereby suppress it.** If § 858-353(A)(5) and
the Act require it disclosed, it is disclosed — and the statute says so in the same paragraph that creates
the confidentiality duty.

**Say it at the time**, when the seller tells you: *"I have to tell you this is something I'll need to
disclose. Let's talk about how we handle it."* Sellers accept that far better than they accept finding out
afterwards.

## Hour 84 — Environmental concerns

### 84.1 One rule governs the whole hour

**Recognise. Disclose. Route.**

**Recognise** the issue. **Disclose** what you know or should know, under § 858-353(A)(1) honesty and
reasonable skill and care and § 858-353(A)(5). **Route** the determination to a qualified professional or
the responsible agency.

**A licensee assesses no environmental condition.** Not radon, not mould, not soil, not asbestos, not
water. Not ever.

### 84.2 The concerns a residential licensee will meet

**Radon.** Testing is by a qualified tester and mitigation by a qualified contractor. A licensee neither
tests nor interprets a result.

**Asbestos.** Common in pre-1980 construction — insulation, floor tile, siding, pipe wrap, popcorn
ceilings. **Intact material is often best left undisturbed; disturbance is the risk.** Renovation plans on
an older house are a routing trigger.

**Mould.** Route to a qualified professional. The underlying question is almost always a **moisture
source**, and a licensee who opines on health effects has strayed a long way outside the licence.

**Underground storage tanks.** Heating oil tanks on older properties; agricultural and fuel tanks on rural
land. Route to a qualified consultant and the responsible state agency.

**Methamphetamine contamination.** A serious and specific issue. A known history is disclosed, and
remediation is routed to the responsible agency and a qualified professional.

**Water supply and waste.** **Private wells and septic systems**, which on rural Oklahoma property decide
habitability and financeability. Route to the county health authority and a qualified inspector — **early,
because a failed septic can stop a loan.**

**Wetlands, floodplain and flood insurance.** **An inventory map is an inventory, not a determination.**
Route to the responsible agency and a consultant. And note that **the standard homeowners policy excludes
flood** — a buyer in or near a mapped zone needs a quote **early**, because it can change what they can
afford.

**Formaldehyde, groundwater and soil contamination.** Routed, always.

### 84.3 Oklahoma-specific conditions to recognise

**Oil and gas activity.** Producing wells, plugged and abandoned wells, gathering lines, tank batteries,
saltwater disposal, and surface use rights held by a severed mineral owner. **A licensee recognises that a
severed mineral estate may carry surface access rights, discloses what is known, and routes every question
to counsel and the title company.** This course states no rule of Oklahoma oil and gas law, because those
provisions were not read from primary text for this project.

**Seismicity.** Oklahoma has experienced induced seismic activity. **A licensee does not assess it, does
not predict it, and does not characterise a property's exposure.** What a licensee does is disclose known
damage, route structural questions to a qualified engineer, and tell a buyer to ask their insurer about
earthquake coverage — which is generally a separate policy or endorsement.

**Severe weather and storm shelters.** Storm shelters and engineered safe rooms are a recognised feature of
this market. **A licensee describes what is there and does not certify it** — whether a shelter meets any
standard is a question for the manufacturer's documentation and a qualified inspector. Registration
practices with local emergency management vary and are routed to the municipality.

**Expansive soils and foundation movement.** Present in parts of the state. **Recognise, disclose, route
to a qualified engineer.** Never characterise a crack.

### 84.4 What a licensee may say about condition

**May:** describe what is visible; report what the seller has disclosed, attributed to the seller; report
what an inspection found, attributed to the inspector; say that a question exists and who answers it.

**May not:** assess; opine on cause, severity, cost or health effect; predict; or reassure. *"That's
nothing to worry about"* is the single most dangerous sentence available to a licensee.

## Hour 85 — Lead-based paint, and the licensee as participant

### 85.1 The federal regime

For **target housing built before 1978**, the federal Residential Lead-Based Paint Hazard Reduction Act and
its implementing rule require, in a sale:

- delivery of the **EPA-approved pamphlet**;
- **disclosure** of any known lead-based paint and lead-based paint hazards, and delivery of any
  **available records and reports**;
- a **ten-day opportunity** for the purchaser to conduct a risk assessment or inspection, which the parties
  may agree in writing to change;
- a **disclosure form signed by the parties AND by the agent**; and
- **record retention** of the completed disclosure.

### 85.2 Why this one is different

**The agent's signature is on that form.**

This is one of the very few places where a licensee is **not a bystander to someone else's disclosure
obligation but a participant with an obligation of their own** — including an affirmative duty to ensure
compliance.

**1978 is a line about the housing, not about the paint.** A pre-1978 house triggers the requirement
whether or not anyone believes there is lead in it, and whether or not the house has been repainted.

### 85.3 What a licensee actually does

Identify the year built from the record. If it is **before 1978**, the regime applies: pamphlet,
disclosure, records, the ten-day opportunity, the signed form, retention. **Do not wait to be asked.**

**This course states no penalty amount or enforcement provision**, because the federal Act and its rule
were not read from primary text for this project. The obligations above are stated at the level of
principle from the general body of pre-license knowledge, and the operative text is read directly.

## Hour 86 — Risk management

### 86.1 What it is

**Identifying what can go wrong, and deciding in advance what to do about it.** Four classical responses:

- **Avoid** — do not take on the risk at all. Declining a property type you do not understand; declining
  an instruction you cannot lawfully follow.
- **Reduce or control** — practices that lower the chance or the consequence. Every documentation
  discipline in this course.
- **Transfer** — insurance, and contractual allocation.
- **Retain** — accept it knowingly, having priced it.

### 86.2 The recurring risk areas in a residential practice

**Misrepresentation** — Module OK-09 Hour 50. Almost always by **repeating without checking**. Prevented
by **attribute or verify, never assert.** Grounds: § 858-312(2), and § 858-353(A)(1).

**Disclosure failure** — this module Hours 83 and 84. Grounds: § 858-312(23) and § 858-353(A)(5).

**Deadline failure** — Modules OK-09 and OK-10. Prevented by calendar work done the day the contract is
accepted. Ground: § 858-353(A)(1), reasonable skill and care.

**Money handling** — Modules OK-10 Hour 59 and OK-12 Hour 72. Grounds: § 858-312(6), (16) and (22), and
§ 858-353(A)(3).

**Fair housing** — Module OK-13. Grounds: § 858-312(17) and § 858-353(A)(6), plus federal exposure.

**Practising law** — Modules OK-05, OK-09 and throughout. Prevented by routing.

**Confidentiality** — Module OK-03 Hour 14, and § 858-358 after termination.

**Advertising** — Module OK-06 Hour 35. Grounds: § 858-312(20), (11), (12), (10) and (2).

**Failure to communicate** — § 858-353(B)(2). **Most complaints are about silence, not fraud.**

**Regulator response** — § 858-312(26)'s **fifteen days.**

### 86.3 Agency risk — or in Oklahoma, its absence

A licensee elsewhere carries risk from the fiduciary duties. **In Oklahoma § 858-360(A) abrogated them**,
and the risk instead attaches to **the statutory list**: the seven duties to all parties, the two to the
party served, and the written disclosure and confirmation requirements of §§ 858-355.1 and 858-356.

**The risk is not smaller. It is more precisely defined**, which means it is more precisely auditable — and
§ 858-312(3) makes any failure to comply with §§ 858-351 through 858-363 a ground.

**Which is good news, if you take it seriously:** a closed statutory list is a checklist, and a checklist
can be worked.

## Hour 87 — Documentation, insurance, and the honest correction

### 87.1 The documentation discipline

**Write it down. Put it in writing. Keep it.**

- **Every disclosure**, with the date it was given.
- **Every material conversation**, confirmed by email the same day.
- **Every figure**, with its source.
- **Every offer received and presented**, with dates and times — § 858-353(A)(2).
- **Every deadline**, computed the day the contract is accepted.
- **Every instruction from a party**, and every refusal of an unlawful one.
- **Every accounting** for money and property — § 858-353(A)(3).

**Write every file as though someone else will read it, because someone may.** The Commission **shall**
investigate upon written complaint by any person, and **§ 858-312(26) gives you fifteen days to respond
adequately in writing.** You cannot do that from memory.

### 87.2 Errors and omissions coverage

**Professional liability insurance** covering claims of negligent acts, errors or omissions in professional
services.

Three things every licensee should know about their own coverage: **what it covers**; **what it excludes**
— fraud, intentional acts, and often fair housing and antitrust; and **that it is usually claims-made**,
meaning the policy in force when the **claim** is made responds, not the one in force when the work was
done. **Coverage is a question for your broker and the carrier**, and it is asked before it is needed.

**This course states no Oklahoma insurance requirement for licensees**, because no such provision was read
from primary text for this project.

### 87.3 The honest correction

**Every licensee will eventually make a mistake.** A deadline missed, a figure transposed, a disclosure
sent late, a statement made that turns out to be wrong.

**The professional response, every time:**

1. **Correct it in writing, immediately**, to everyone who received the wrong information.
2. **Tell your broker.**
3. **Document what happened**, when you learned it, and what you did.
4. **Do not alter the earlier record.** Add to it.

**The alternative — quietly fixing it and hoping — converts an error into a concealment.** And under
§ 858-312(8), **any other conduct which constitutes untrustworthy, improper, fraudulent, or dishonest
dealings**, concealment is what turns a survivable problem into a licence problem.

### 87.4 The whole module in one page

**Disclose** what § 858-353(A)(5) and the Residential Property Condition Disclosure Act require — **to all
parties**, and read the Act because this course could not. **Confidentiality does not override a disclosure
required by law.** **Recognise, disclose, route** every environmental question, and **assess none of them.**
**Pre-1978 housing** triggers the federal lead regime, and **your signature is on that form.** **Manage risk
by documenting**, calendaring, routing and communicating. **And when you get it wrong, correct it in
writing immediately.**

## Supplemental source review — 2026-09-06

This required reading clarifies the existing topics; no additional clock-hour credit or regulator approval is claimed.

### disclosure

Related module 15, §§83.1 and 83.4. Oklahoma's Residential Property Condition Disclosure Act distinguishes
a disclosure statement from a disclaimer. Under 60 O.S. 833(A)(1), a disclaimer route requires both
that the seller has never occupied the property and that the seller has no actual knowledge of a
defect. "I did not live there" alone does not erase known defects. Under 832(10), the disclosure is
based on actual knowledge and is not a warranty. The broker's own actual knowledge of an omitted
defect requires disclosure under 836(C). Section 836(E) does not impose an independent property
inspection or independent verification duty on the licensee; referral to an inspector is not a reason
to conceal known facts.

Timing is part of the duty. Under 834(B), if the buyer receives the disclosure or disclaimer after
making the offer, acknowledgment and confirmation are required before acceptance. Under 834(C), a
newly discovered defect before acceptance requires prompt written, signed and dated information;
paragraph (D) requires buyer acknowledgment. Retain the delivered version, update and receipt, not
just a note saying "disclosures done." An out-of-date assertion cannot be treated as current merely
because it once appeared in the file.

Sources: [Oklahoma Legislature, Title 60 — Property](https://www.oklegislature.gov/OK_Statutes/CompleteTitles/os60.pdf); [OREC License Code and Rules, as of November 1, 2025](https://oklahoma.gov/content/dam/ok/en/orec/documents/meetings/Nov%201%202025%20Oklahoma%20Real%20Estate%20Commission%20Code%20and%20Rules%20Book.pdf).

### lead

Related module 15, §85.1. For covered pre-1978 housing, federal lead disclosure requires the applicable
pamphlet, known information, available records/reports and contractual warning/acknowledgments before
the buyer or tenant becomes obligated. Relevant common-area reports can matter in multifamily
housing. A purchaser generally receives a ten-day opportunity for a lead inspection or risk assessment;
the parties can change that period by written agreement and a buyer may waive the opportunity. The
rule does not itself require the seller to test or guarantee absence of lead. Do not treat a seller's
lack of knowledge as permission to omit the required paperwork. Source: EPA real-estate lead disclosure
guidance and its linked federal disclosure rule.

Sources: [EPA — Real Estate Disclosures About Potential Lead Hazards](https://www.epa.gov/lead/real-estate-disclosures-about-potential-lead-hazards).

### environment

Related module 15, §§84.1–84.3. Radon cannot be reliably detected by ordinary senses; testing is the
way to determine its level (EPA Home Buyer's and Seller's Guide to Radon). A seller's inability to
smell it is not evidence of absence. Do not disturb suspect asbestos material to make a showing look
better; professional evaluation is appropriate because disturbance can release fibers (EPA asbestos
guidance). Flood risk is not zero outside a mapped high-risk zone; FEMA explains that maps, insurance
requirements and actual exposure are related but different questions. Refer detailed determinations
to qualified sources and disclose known facts. Do not guarantee environmental safety from appearance.

Sources: [EPA — Home Buyer’s and Seller’s Guide to Radon](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-05/documents/hmbuygud.pdf); [EPA — Protect Your Family from Exposures to Asbestos](https://www.epa.gov/asbestos/protect-your-family-exposures-asbestos); [FEMA NFIP — Flood Zones and Maps](https://www.floodsmart.gov/flood-zones-and-maps/what-is-my-flood-zone).

### wire

Related module 15, §86.2. A changed wire instruction received by email may be business-email compromise.
Verify independently using a previously established number or other trusted channel, not the contact
details in the suspect message. A convincing sender name or logo does not establish authenticity. If
funds have been sent, immediately contact the financial institution and follow its fraud-response
instructions, preserve evidence and report through the appropriate law-enforcement channels. The FBI
recommends immediate bank contact; neither investigation nor recovery is guaranteed. Never ask a
customer to post account numbers publicly to prove a transfer.

Sources: [FBI — Business Email Compromise](https://www.fbi.gov/how-we-can-help-you/common-frauds-and-scams/business-email-compromise).
