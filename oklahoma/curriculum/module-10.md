## Overview

Module OK-09 covered contract law. This module covers the transaction: what a purchase contract must
settle, how offers are prepared and presented under § 858-353(A)(2), how contingencies are managed
through their deadlines, and what happens in a multiple-offer situation. It also covers earnest money,
which in Oklahoma carries three separate § 858-312 grounds a licensee should know before touching a
cheque.

## Learning objectives

1. Name the questions every purchase contract must settle.
2. Prepare an offer accurately, from sourced facts.
3. State the § 858-353(A)(2) offer-handling duty and its written-waiver carve-out.
4. Present an offer correctly and record that it was presented.
5. Manage a multiple-offer situation without breaching § 858-353(A)(4).
6. Identify the four elements of a contingency and diarise them.
7. Describe the common contingencies and what each actually protects.
8. State what earnest money is and is not.
9. Identify the § 858-312 grounds that govern earnest money handling.
10. State who decides a disputed earnest money disposition.
11. Handle a party's request to cancel correctly.
12. State the compensation disclosure timing § 858-353(A)(7) requires.

## Hour 54 — What a purchase contract must settle

Whatever form is used — and in Oklahoma that will be a promulgated form this course does not describe —
a residential purchase contract has to answer the same questions, and a licensee must be able to find
each of them:

- **Parties.** Full legal names of everyone who must sign. Module OK-05 established this is not answered
  by looking at the deed.
- **Property.** The legal description, taken from the deed or supplied by the title company. **And in
  Oklahoma: whether the conveyance includes the mineral estate.**
- **Price, and how it will be paid** — cash, financing, or a combination, and the terms of the financing
  the buyer will seek.
- **Earnest money** — the amount, who holds it, when it is deposited, and what happens to it in each way
  the contract can end.
- **Included and excluded items** — the fixture discipline from Module OK-01, reduced to two lists.
- **Contingencies** — each with its condition, deadline, method of notice and effect of silence.
- **Title evidence** — abstract and attorney's opinion, or a title commitment and policy; who furnishes
  it, by when, and what happens if the buyer objects.
- **Closing date and possession.** **These are not the same date.** If they differ, the contract must say
  so, and possession before or after closing needs its own written agreement.
- **Prorations** — taxes, assessments, rents, association dues, and the basis for each.
- **Risk of loss** before closing.
- **Default and remedies** — what each party may do if the other fails.
- **Signatures and the date of acceptance.**

### 54.1 Equitable title, and the gap

Between formation and closing the buyer holds **equitable title** — a recognised interest in the property
— while the seller retains legal title. The contract allocates the **risk of casualty loss** in that
interval, and it should be read rather than assumed.

**Possession before closing** is a bad idea executed badly more often than it is a bad idea. If it must
happen it needs a separate written agreement addressing rent, insurance, utilities, damage, maintenance,
and what happens if the sale does not close. **A buyer already living in the house who cannot close is a
problem with no good solution.**

## Hour 55 — Preparing an offer

### 55.1 Fill nothing in from memory

- **Legal description** — from the deed or the title company. Not a tax record, not a prior listing.
- **Names** — from identification, spelled as they will be spelled on the deed.
- **Figures** — from the documents. Loan amount from the lender. Payoff from the payoff statement.
- **Dates** — computed, not estimated.

### 55.2 What the buyer must understand before signing

**What they are committing to**, in plain terms: the price, the deposit, the deadlines, and what happens
to their money in each way this can end.

**That a pre-approval is not a commitment**, which is why the financing contingency exists — Module OK-11.

**Their costs.** And this is not optional: **§ 858-353(B)(1)** requires the broker to **inform the party
in writing, when an offer is made, that the party will be expected to pay certain costs, brokerage
service costs and the approximate amount of the costs.** Module OK-08 supplies the arithmetic. **The
duty attaches when the offer is made** — not at closing, not when the buyer asks.

**And § 858-353(A)(7)**: compensation and fees disclosed **in writing before the effective date of the
contract**, with the compensation agreement's term **not exceeding one year** and **defaulting to sixty
days** if unstated.

### 55.3 What a licensee does not do

**Does not compose new legal language.** Module OK-09 Hour 53.

**Does not advise on how to take title.** Legal and tax question.

**Does not predict what the seller will accept**, or characterise the other side's position beyond what
is known and disclosable.

## Hour 56 — Presenting offers

### 56.1 The duty — § 858-353(A)(2)

To **all parties**, mandatory and unwaivable except as the paragraph itself provides:

> **Unless specifically waived in writing by a party to the transaction:**
> **a.** receive all written offers and counteroffers,
> **b.** **reduce offers or counteroffers to a written form upon request of any party** to a transaction,
> and
> **c.** **present timely** all written offers and counteroffers.

**Three obligations. Read (b) again.** Upon request of **any party** — including a party you are not
serving, including an unrepresented buyer who walks up at an open house — you must reduce their offer or
counteroffer to written form.

**And the waiver is narrow:** it is the **party's**, it must be **specific**, and it must be **in
writing.** A seller who says "don't bother me below $250,000" has not waived anything.

### 56.2 Nothing here stops at contract

There is no language in § 858-353(A)(2) suspending the duty when the property goes under contract. **A
written offer that arrives is received and presented.** What the seller may lawfully do about it, given
their existing contract, is a legal question routed to counsel.

### 56.3 Presenting well

- **Present promptly**, and confirm receipt to whoever submitted it.
- **Present all terms**, not just price. A cash offer at less, closing in fourteen days with no
  contingencies, may be worth more to a particular seller than a higher financed offer — and it is the
  seller's judgment to make, not yours.
- **Present the net.** Module OK-08's arithmetic, labelled as an estimate.
- **Record that you presented it**, with the date and time. If it ever matters, that record is the whole
  case.
- **Do not characterise the buyer.** Not their circumstances, not their family, not anything that would
  offend Module OK-13. Present the offer.

### 56.4 Communicating the response

Acceptance, rejection or counter-offer, communicated in the manner the offer requires, and confirmed in
writing.

**And § 858-353(B)(2) throughout: keep the party informed.** Silence between presentation and response is
where most complaints begin.

## Hour 57 — Multiple offers

### 57.1 The structural problem

**A counter-offer rejects the offer it responds to.** Countering three offers destroys all three. Where
the forms supply a multiple counter-proposal instrument reserving the right to accept only one, that is
the tool; where they do not, **a licensee does not invent one.**

### 57.2 What may be disclosed, and what may not

**§ 858-353(A)(4) is the controlling provision, and it is a closed list.** The **only** confidential
information in an Oklahoma transaction is:

**(a)** that a party or prospective party **is willing to pay more or accept less** than what is being
offered; **(b)** that a party **is willing to agree to financing terms different from those offered**;
**(c)** **the motivating factors** of a party; and **(d)** **information specifically designated as
confidential by a party**, unless public.

So in a multiple-offer situation:

- **The terms of a competing offer are NOT on the confidential list** — unless the offering party
  specifically designated them confidential under (d), or unless disclosing them would reveal a listed
  item such as that buyer's willingness to pay more.
- **The seller's willingness to accept less IS on the list** — item (a) — and stays in, from everyone.
- **A buyer's willingness to pay more IS on the list** — item (a) — and stays in, from everyone,
  including your own seller if you are also serving that buyer.

**Knowing where the legal line is is not the same as practising up to it.** The seller decides what is
disclosed about the process, within those limits, and a licensee follows lawful instructions and records
them. What a licensee must never do is **misstate the situation** — inventing a competing offer, or
implying one exists when it does not, is a § 858-312(2) misrepresentation and a § 858-312(28) deceptive
practice.

### 57.3 A workable procedure

1. **Agree the process with the seller in advance**, in writing: how offers will be handled, whether a
   deadline will be set, whether the existence or terms of other offers will be disclosed.
2. **Tell every submitting party what the process is**, consistently and identically.
3. **Present every written offer** — § 858-353(A)(2)(c).
4. **Record everything**: what was received, when, what was presented, when, what the seller instructed.
5. **Treat every party the same way.** Different handling by protected characteristic is a fair housing
   violation before it is anything else — Module OK-13.

## Hour 58 — Contingencies and deadlines

### 58.1 The four elements, again

**Condition. Deadline. Method of notice. Effect of silence.** All four are read before the contingency is
relied on, and the fourth differs by form.

### 58.2 The common contingencies

**Financing.** The buyer's obligation conditioned on obtaining a loan of a stated type and amount on
stated terms. **A pre-approval is not a commitment** — which is the whole reason this exists.

**Inspection.** The buyer may inspect within a period and then, depending on the form, object, request
repairs, or terminate. Note what it is **not**: a warranty of condition, a price renegotiation clause, or
a general escape — though it is used as the last of these constantly.

**Appraisal.** The property must appraise at or above a value for the lender's purposes. **A low
appraisal does not lower the price by itself**; it creates a gap someone must close — Module OK-07 Hour
41.4.

**Title.** The buyer may examine the abstract or commitment and object to defects within a period.

**Survey.** Where boundaries, encroachments and easements must be established — and the only way to remove
the survey exception from a title policy.

**Sale of the buyer's existing home.** The most fragile contingency in residential practice, because it
makes this transaction depend on another one nobody in the room controls.

**And in Oklahoma, worth naming: a mineral or oil-and-gas question.** Where the conveyance of minerals
matters to the buyer, that is settled in the contract and confirmed in the title work — not assumed.

### 58.3 The method that prevents contingency failure

1. On the day of acceptance, **write every deadline in the file and the calendar**, computed from that
   date.
2. **Work backwards** from each deadline to the date the underlying work must **start.**
3. **Confirm every notice in the form the contract requires**, and keep proof of delivery.
4. **When a deadline is at risk, raise it with the party BEFORE it passes, in writing.**

**This is what § 858-353(A)(1) reasonable skill and care and § 858-353(B)(2) keeping the party informed
look like in daily practice. It is almost entirely calendar work**, and it is the single highest-value
thing a residential licensee does.

## Hour 59 — Earnest money

### 59.1 What it is, and is not

**A deposit evidencing the buyer's good faith**, held pending closing and applied to the purchase price.

**It is not the consideration for the contract** — the mutual promises are. **It is not a fee.** **It is
not the licensee's or the broker's money at any point.** And a contract without earnest money is not
automatically invalid; it is a commercial feature, not an element of formation.

Its functions: signalling seriousness, and constituting a fund from which a seller may be made whole on a
buyer's default — which is why many forms designate it as **liquidated damages**, and why the amount
matters.

### 59.2 Three § 858-312 grounds, and one duty

**§ 858-353(A)(3)** — **timely account for all money and property received.** A duty to **all parties**,
mandatory and unwaivable.

**§ 858-312(6)** — **failing, within a reasonable time, to account for or to remit any monies, documents,
or other property coming into possession of the licensee which belong to others.**

**§ 858-312(16)** — **commingling with the licensee's own money or property the money or property of
others**, unless received and held in **an escrow account that contains only money or property of
others.**

**§ 858-312(22)** — and this is the one licensees do not expect: **offering, loaning, paying, or making to
appear to have been paid, a down payment or earnest money deposit for a purchaser or seller.** Funding a
buyer's earnest money to save a transaction is not generosity. It is a named ground for suspension or
revocation.

### 59.3 The three rules

**One: it is other people's money from the moment it reaches your hands.** It goes to the broker for
handling under the firm's escrow procedures. It never touches an operating account.

**Two: you do not hold it.** Not uncashed in a drawer as a favour to a buyer waiting on a contingency.
Not over a weekend because the broker is away. If a buyer wants a later deposit, that is a **term of the
contract** to be negotiated in writing — not an informal arrangement with you.

**Three: you do not release it on request.** Not to a seller as a show of good faith. Not to a buyer who
changed their mind. Not on both parties saying it is fine, unless the release is documented as your
firm's procedures require.

**This course states no Oklahoma escrow deadline, threshold or procedure**, because those provisions were
not read from primary text for this project. **Read them and your broker's written procedures before you
accept your first cheque.**

### 59.4 A disputed disposition

**Not the licensee's decision, and not the broker's on their own judgment.** The questions are **what the
contract provides** and **what the parties or a court determine.**

**On any dispute: escalate to your broker immediately and do nothing else.** Releasing disputed funds on
your own judgment is a licence problem regardless of who turns out to have been right about the contract.

### 59.5 The routine

1. **Receive it and deliver it to the broker the same day**, following the firm's procedure exactly.
2. **Document** what you received, from whom, in what form, and when.
3. **Confirm the deposit to the buyer in writing.**
4. **Diarise** the deadline the contract sets for the deposit.
5. **On any dispute, escalate.**
