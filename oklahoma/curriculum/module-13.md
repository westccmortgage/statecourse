## Overview

The federal fair housing framework, the practices it forbids, and the disciplines that prevent a licensee
from committing them by accident — which is how most fair housing violations by licensees actually
happen.

Oklahoma adds a licence consequence that runs alongside the federal one: **§ 858-312(17)** makes
**conviction in a court of competent jurisdiction of having violated any provision of the federal fair
housing laws, 42 U.S.C. § 3601 et seq.**, a ground for suspension or revocation of an Oklahoma real
estate licence. And **§ 858-353(A)(6)** requires compliance with all applicable statutes, which folds
fair housing into the broker-relationships duties as well.

## Learning objectives

1. Name the seven federal protected classes and the statutes that created them.
2. Describe the acts the Fair Housing Act prohibits.
3. Recognise steering in ordinary language, including when unintended.
4. Distinguish blockbusting from redlining.
5. Distinguish reasonable accommodation from reasonable modification and apply each.
6. Audit an advertisement against the advertising prohibition.
7. Describe the exemptions and explain why they change nothing for a licensee.
8. Describe enforcement, remedies and timing at the level of principle.
9. State what a licensee does when a client gives a discriminatory instruction.
10. Describe ECOA and the two classes it protects that the Fair Housing Act does not.
11. Describe the ADA's application in a real estate context.
12. State the § 858-312(17) and § 858-353(A)(6) licence consequences.
13. Apply the objective-criteria method to a buyer's question about an area.

## Hour 73 — The classes and the acts

### 73.1 The seven federal protected classes

**Race. Colour. Religion. National origin. Sex. Familial status. Disability.**

**Race and colour came first**, in the **Civil Rights Act of 1866**, which prohibits **all** racial
discrimination in the sale or rental of property and has **no exemptions at all.**

**Title VIII of the Civil Rights Act of 1968** — the Fair Housing Act — added religion, colour and
national origin. **Sex** was added in 1974. **Familial status and disability** — the statute's term is
*handicap* — were added by the **Fair Housing Amendments Act of 1988.**

**Familial status** means households with children under eighteen, and includes **pregnant persons** and
those **in the process of securing custody.** The principal exemption is qualified **housing for older
persons.**

### 73.2 The federal list is a floor

State and local law may protect **additional** classes, and a licensee's actual obligations are the sum of
federal, state and any applicable local ordinance.

**This course states no Oklahoma or local protected class**, because Oklahoma's own fair housing
provisions and municipal ordinances were not read from primary text for this project. **Read them.** A
licensee who works from the federal list alone, in a state or a city that protects more, will eventually
be wrong — and being wrong here is not a paperwork problem.

### 73.3 What the Act prohibits

- **Refusing to sell, rent or negotiate**, or otherwise **making housing unavailable**, because of a
  protected characteristic.
- **Discriminating in the terms, conditions, privileges, services or facilities.**
- **Advertising** that indicates a preference, limitation or discrimination.
- **Representing that housing is unavailable when it is available.**
- **Blockbusting.**
- **Steering.**
- **Redlining** — discrimination in lending or insurance based on the composition of an area.
- **Denying access** to a multiple listing service or other facility on a protected basis.
- **Coercing, intimidating, threatening or interfering** with a person exercising a fair housing right, or
  **retaliating** against someone who asserted one or helped another to.

### 73.4 The exemptions, and why they change nothing for you

The Act contains narrow exemptions — certain single-family sales by an owner without a broker and without
discriminatory advertising, and owner-occupied dwellings of up to four units.

**Every one of them is lost the moment a licensee is involved.** They also do not apply to **race** under
the 1866 Act, and they never permit discriminatory advertising.

**Know the exemptions exist. Never expect to operate inside one.**

## Hour 74 — Steering, blockbusting, redlining

### 74.1 Steering, in ordinary conversation

Steering is directing prospects toward or away from areas on the basis of a protected characteristic. **It
rarely announces itself.** It arrives as a helpful remark:

> "You'd probably be more comfortable on the north side."
> "That area is really more for young families."
> "Let me show you where people like you tend to buy."
> "I'd stay west of the highway if I were you."

**None of those names a protected class.** All of them are steering.

**And steering is committed by OMISSION as easily as by statement** — by simply not showing, not
mentioning, or not searching areas that meet the buyer's own stated criteria.

### 74.2 The method that prevents it

**Answer with data the buyer can verify. Describe property, never people.**

| The buyer asks | What you do |
|---|---|
| "How are the schools?" | Give them the district's published data and the state report card, and tell them to visit. **Do not characterise a school as good or bad** — "good schools" functions as a proxy in practice. |
| "Is it safe?" | Route them to the police department's published statistics. |
| "What kind of people live there?" | *"I can't characterise the people in an area, and I wouldn't be much good at it. Tell me what you need in a property and I'll search on that."* |
| "I'd like a neighbourhood with more people like me." | The answer is still no — gently and clearly. **A buyer's own request does not create an exemption.** |

**Then redirect to criteria you CAN research and supply:** price, size, age, condition, lot, commute time
to a named address, school district boundary as published, property tax, association status, flood zone
determination.

### 74.3 Blockbusting

**Inducing or attempting to induce a sale or rental for profit by representations about the entry or
prospective entry into a neighbourhood of persons of a protected class.** Historically called panic
selling.

Modern forms are subtler: a solicitation implying an area is "changing," a mailer implying values will
fall, a conversation implying now is the time to sell before something happens.

### 74.4 Redlining

**Discrimination in lending or insurance based on the racial or ethnic composition of an area** rather
than the merits of the borrower or the property.

A licensee is not a lender, but a licensee who **steers a buyer toward or away from a lender**, or who
**tells a buyer an area is hard to finance** without knowing it, is close to the same territory. Route
financing questions to lenders and let the buyer choose.

### 74.5 The client's discriminatory instruction

A seller says: *don't show it to anyone who would change the character of the street.* A landlord says:
*no children upstairs.*

**Refuse. Say plainly that you cannot follow the instruction. Withdraw from the engagement if it stands.**

Three things that are **not** compliance:

1. Following the instruction while noting your objection in the file.
2. Applying it "informally."
3. Asking the client to put it in writing. **A written discriminatory instruction is evidence against the
   licensee, not authority for them.**

And note the Oklahoma overlay: § 858-353(A)(1) requires **honesty and reasonable skill and care to ALL
parties** — including the buyer being steered away — and § 858-353(A)(6) requires compliance with all
applicable statutes. **Following such an instruction breaches the broker-relationships duties as well as
federal law.**

## Hour 75 — Disability

### 75.1 Accommodation and modification

**Reasonable accommodation** — a change in a **rule, policy, practice or service** necessary to give a
person with a disability equal opportunity to use and enjoy a dwelling. Waiving a no-pets rule for an
assistance animal; a reserved accessible parking space; a rent payment date matching a benefit deposit.
Generally at the **housing provider's** expense.

**Reasonable modification** — a **physical change** to the premises. A grab bar, a ramp, a widened
doorway, a lowered counter. Generally at the **requesting person's** expense, and the provider may in
appropriate cases require restoration of the interior at the end of the tenancy.

**Rule-change versus physical-change is the whole distinction**, and it is tested constantly and confused
constantly.

### 75.2 Assistance animals

**An assistance animal is not a pet.** Pet rules, pet rent and pet deposits **do not apply** to it, and the
questions that may be asked about it are narrowly limited.

### 75.3 What may not be asked

Whether a prospect **has** a disability. The **nature or severity** of one. To see **medical records**.

A prospect may volunteer information, and a request for an accommodation or modification may itself
require limited verification in some circumstances — but the licensee does not go looking.

### 75.4 Design and construction

**Covered multifamily dwellings built for first occupancy after March 1991** must meet the Act's
accessibility requirements. A licensee marketing new multifamily should know the requirement exists and
route the compliance question to the developer and counsel.

## Hour 76 — Advertising

### 76.1 The rule

**Describe the property. Never the buyer, the tenant, or the neighbours.**

| Phrase | Problem |
|---|---|
| "Perfect for a young professional couple" | age, familial status, marital status |
| "Great family neighbourhood" | familial status |
| "Walk to First Baptist" | religion |
| "Adult building" | familial status |
| "Must be able to climb stairs" | disability |
| "Exclusive area" | historically coded, and describes people |
| "Bachelor pad" | sex, familial status |
| "Safe neighbourhood" | frequently a proxy, and a claim you cannot support |
| "Ideal for empty nesters" | familial status, age |

**Acceptable in the same breath:** "three bedrooms"; "second floor, no lift"; "0.4 miles from the Route 66
frontage"; "1,940 square feet"; "attached two-car garage"; "in the Deer Creek school district, per the
district's published boundary map, which buyers should confirm."

### 76.2 Auditing an advertisement, line by line

Three questions for every sentence:

1. **Who is speaking?** **§ 858-312(20)** makes it a ground to advertise to buy, sell, rent or exchange
   real estate **without disclosing in writing that the licensee is a real estate licensee.** Every
   advertisement, including one placed to buy.
2. **What is claimed?** Can every factual claim be supported by something written down? Is any of it a
   guarantee of future profit — **§ 858-312(10)** — or a misrepresentation — **§ 858-312(2)**?
3. **Who is described?** **If the answer is a person rather than a building, rewrite it.**

### 76.3 Photographs and virtual tours

The same rule applies to images. Photographs showing only certain kinds of people, or staged to signal
who the property is "for," carry the same message as the words. And a photograph must show the property
**as it is** — an image edited to remove a defect or a neighbouring condition is a misrepresentation
under § 858-312(2) whatever the caption says.

## Hour 77 — Enforcement, other federal law, and the Oklahoma licence consequence

### 77.1 Enforcement and remedies

A complaint may be filed with **HUD**, generally within **one year** of the alleged act, or a **civil
action** brought in federal court within **two years**. Remedies include **actual and punitive damages,
injunctive relief, civil penalties and attorney's fees.** The **Department of Justice** may bring a
pattern-or-practice action.

**And a fair housing complaint does not require proof of intent.** Disparate treatment and disparate
impact are both theories of liability, and "I didn't mean it that way" is not a defence.

### 77.2 The Oklahoma licence consequence

**§ 858-312(17)** makes it a ground for the Commission to impose sanctions:

> **Conviction in a court of competent jurisdiction of having violated any provision of the federal fair
> housing laws, 42 U.S.C. Section 3601 et seq.**

And **§ 858-353(A)(6)** — a duty to **all parties**, mandatory and unwaivable — requires the broker to
**comply with all requirements of The Oklahoma Real Estate License Code and all applicable statutes and
rules**, which includes the federal fair housing laws.

**So a fair housing violation by an Oklahoma licensee runs on more than one track**, and the tracks do not
wait for each other.

### 77.3 ECOA

The **Equal Credit Opportunity Act** prohibits discrimination in **credit** on the basis of race, colour,
religion, national origin, sex, **marital status**, **age** (provided the applicant can contract), because
income derives from a **public assistance programme**, or because the applicant exercised a right under
the Consumer Credit Protection Act.

**Note two classes ECOA covers that the Fair Housing Act does not: marital status and age.** And a
rejected applicant is entitled to **notice of the reasons.**

### 77.4 The ADA

The **Americans with Disabilities Act** governs **places of public accommodation** and commercial
facilities — Title III — and employment — Title I. In a real estate context it reaches offices, retail,
restaurants and other commercial premises, requiring removal of architectural barriers **where readily
achievable.**

**A real estate office is itself a place of public accommodation.** So is an open house held in a
commercial property.

**FHA and ADA cover different ground and are frequently confused.** FHA is about **housing**; ADA Title
III is about **public accommodations.** Some property is both.

### 77.5 The one-page summary

**Seven federal classes** — race, colour, religion, national origin, sex, familial status, disability —
and that is a **floor**. **Steering, blockbusting, redlining, discriminatory advertising, and making
housing unavailable** are prohibited. **Accommodation changes a rule; modification changes the premises.**
**Describe the property, never the people.** **A client's instruction is not a defence, and documenting an
objection is not compliance.** **Answer questions about areas with published data, not characterisation.**
**Treat every party identically, at every stage, in every transaction.**

And in Oklahoma: **§ 858-312(17)** and **§ 858-353(A)(6)**.
