# Oklahoma Basic Course of Real Estate, Part I of II — Module OK-03

## Hours 12–18 · Law of agency — and why Oklahoma does not have one

**Commission area of study covered:** 605:10-3-1(a)(1)(V) Law of Agency
**Clock hours:** 7 — the largest single allocation in this course · **Hour allocation:** a StateCourse
proposal for Commission review; 605:10-3-1(a)(1) allocates no hours among its twenty-two areas.
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Development. Not approved. No Oklahoma pre-license credit is available.

> **The information presented in this course should not be used as a substitute for competent legal
> advice.** — 605:10-5-3(i)(6).

## Overview

The Commission's area of study is called *Law of Agency*. **The most important thing an Oklahoma
licensee can learn about it is that Oklahoma abolished it.**

Section 858-360(A) of the Oklahoma Real Estate License Code provides that the statutory duties in
§§ 858-351 through 858-363 **"shall replace and abrogate the fiduciary or other duties of a broker to a
party based on common law principles of agency."** Section 858-360(B) provides that **"a broker shall
not be an agent, subagent, or dual agent and an offer of subagency shall not be made to other
brokers."**

So this module teaches the common-law vocabulary — because it is the frame the statute displaced, and
because it is what every national textbook and every out-of-state colleague will use — and then teaches
what Oklahoma actually requires, which is a closed statutory list. **A licensee who learns Oklahoma
agency out of a national textbook will learn it wrongly.**

## Learning objectives

1. State the common-law agency vocabulary and explain what § 858-360(A) did to it.
2. State what § 858-360(B) forbids, and identify the three relationships Oklahoma brokers cannot have.
3. Recite the seven § 858-353(A) duties owed to **all** parties.
4. Recite the two further § 858-353(B) duties owed only to a party the broker is serving.
5. State the four items § 858-353(A)(4) makes confidential, and explain why the list being exhaustive
   changes practice.
6. State the three circumstances in which confidential information may nevertheless be disclosed.
7. Apply § 858-353(A)(7) to a compensation disclosure, including the one-year cap and the sixty-day
   default.
8. Describe the § 858-355.1 and § 858-356 written disclosure and confirmation requirements.
9. Explain what a broker must disclose when providing **fewer** services than a transaction requires.
10. State what survives termination under § 858-358.
11. Apply § 858-359(A) to a question about who pays.
12. Explain §§ 858-362 and 858-363 and what each means for a provisional sales associate.
13. Identify a § 858-312 ground for discipline arising from a breach of these duties.
14. Describe the § 858-364 deed theft notification obligation and route its form.

## Hour 12 — Common-law agency and its abrogation

### 12.1 The common-law frame

Agency is the relationship in which one person, the **agent**, acts on behalf of another, the
**principal**, in dealings with third parties. The common law described the agent's duties with **OLD
CAR**: Obedience, Loyalty, Disclosure, Confidentiality, Accounting, Reasonable care.

It classified agents by scope — **universal**, **general** (a property manager), **special** (a listing
broker, traditionally) — and authority as **express**, **implied**, or **apparent**, the last created by
the principal's conduct leading a third party reasonably to believe authority exists.

It also produced the relationships every other state's course spends hours on: **seller's agent**,
**buyer's agent**, **subagent**, **dual agent**, and **designated agent**.

**Learn this vocabulary.** You will meet it in national materials, in conversation with out-of-state
colleagues, and in the general body of real estate knowledge the licensing examination draws on.

### 12.2 And then read § 858-360

> **A.** The duties and responsibilities of a broker specified in Sections 858-351 through 858-363 of
> The Oklahoma Real Estate License Code **shall replace and abrogate the fiduciary or other duties of a
> broker to a party based on common law principles of agency.** The remedies at law and equity
> supplement the provisions of Sections 858-351 through 858-363…
>
> **B.** A broker may cooperate with other brokers in a transaction. Pursuant to Sections 858-351
> through 858-363…, **a broker shall not be an agent, subagent, or dual agent and an offer of subagency
> shall not be made to other brokers.**

Read both subsections twice.

**(A) means the fiduciary duties are gone.** Not modified, not codified — **replaced and abrogated.** An
Oklahoma broker does not owe a party undivided loyalty as a matter of common-law fiduciary obligation,
because that obligation no longer attaches. What the broker owes is **the statutory list, and the
statutory list is what this module spends the next six hours on.**

**(B) means three relationships do not exist in Oklahoma real estate brokerage:**

- **No agency.** A broker is not a party's agent.
- **No subagency.** And an **offer of subagency shall not be made** to other brokers — so the
  cooperative-compensation-creates-subagency structure that other states spent decades unwinding does
  not arise here at all.
- **No dual agency.** Which is why Oklahoma has no dual agency consent form, no dual agency disclosure
  regime and no list of things a dual agent may not say. A broker serving both parties in Oklahoma is
  not occupying a special reduced-duty status — see Hour 16.

**What remains is "brokerage services" provided by a "broker" to a "party."** Those are defined terms —
§ 858-351(2), (1) and (5) — and they carry the whole structure.

### 12.3 The word "agent" survives; the relationship does not

**§ 858-361:**

> A real estate broker and the associates of a real estate broker **are permitted** under the provisions
> of Sections 858-351 through 858-363 of this title **to use the word "agent" in a trade name and as a
> general reference for designating themselves as real estate licensees.**

So "agent" is permitted **vocabulary** — on a sign, on a card, in conversation. It is not a description
of a legal relationship. A licensee who says "I'm your agent" has not committed a violation; a licensee
who *believes* it, and acts on common-law fiduciary instincts instead of the statute, will breach the
statute sooner or later.

### 12.4 The definitions that carry the structure — § 858-351

- **"Broker"** — a real estate broker, an associated broker associate, sales associate, **or provisional
  sales associate** authorised by a real estate broker to provide brokerage services. **Note this
  carefully: the statutory word "broker" includes you**, once you hold a provisional sales associate
  licence. Every duty in § 858-353 is yours personally, not merely your broker's.
- **"Brokerage services"** — those services provided by a broker to a party in a transaction.
- **"Party"** — a seller, buyer, landlord or tenant, or a person involved in an option or exchange.
- **"Firm"** — a sole proprietor, corporation, association or partnership.
- **"Transaction"** — an activity or process to buy, sell, lease, rent, option or exchange real estate,
  which **may include soliciting, advertising, showing or viewing real property, presenting offers or
  counteroffers, entering into agreements, and closing them.**

That last definition is broader than new licensees expect. **Showing a property is part of a
transaction.** So is advertising one. The duties do not wait for a signature.

## Hour 13 — The seven duties to all parties, part one

### 13.1 The opening words, which matter

> **A broker shall have the following duties and responsibilities to ALL PARTIES in a transaction, which
> are MANDATORY and MAY NOT BE ABROGATED OR WAIVED by a broker.**

Three things in one sentence. The duties run to **all parties**, not to a client. They are
**mandatory**. And a broker **cannot waive them** — not by agreement, not by disclosure, not by the
party's consent.

*(One narrow exception appears inside duty 2 itself and is examined below: a **party** may specifically
waive the offer-handling duty in writing. The broker cannot waive it unilaterally.)*

### 13.2 Duty 1 — honesty, reasonable skill and care

> **Treat all parties with honesty and exercise reasonable skill and care.**

To **all parties**. Not to a client. The other side of your transaction is owed honesty and competence
by you, personally, as a matter of statute.

In daily practice "reasonable skill and care" is mostly unglamorous: calendaring every deadline the day
the contract becomes effective; confirming material conversations in writing; checking that what you
repeated matches what the document says; raising a problem before it becomes one.

**And it is where misrepresentation lives.** § 858-312(2) makes **substantial misrepresentations or
false promises in the conduct of business, or through licensees, or advertising** a ground for
suspension or revocation. The habit that prevents it is **attribute or verify, never assert** — the same
rule as Module OK-01 Hour 5.

### 13.3 Duty 2 — offers and counteroffers

> **Unless specifically waived in writing by a party to the transaction:**
> **a.** receive all written offers and counteroffers,
> **b.** **reduce offers or counteroffers to a written form upon request of any party** to a
> transaction, and
> **c.** present timely all written offers and counteroffers.

Three obligations, and (b) is the one that surprises people. **Upon request of ANY party** — not your
party — you must reduce an offer or counteroffer to written form. A buyer with no relationship to you at
all may ask you to put their offer in writing, and you must.

**"Unless specifically waived in writing by a party"** is the statute's own carve-out. A seller
inundated with offers below a threshold may waive in writing. **Specifically, and in writing.** An oral
instruction is not a waiver, and a general "don't bother me with lowballs" is not specific.

**Note what is absent.** Nothing here says the duty stops when the property goes under contract. If a
written offer arrives, it is received and presented.

### 13.4 Duty 3 — accounting

> **Timely account for all money and property received by the broker.**

Earnest money, deposits, rents, keys, documents. It is other people's property from the moment it comes
into your hands.

Two § 858-312 grounds attach directly. **(6)** — failing within a reasonable time to account for or
remit monies, documents or other property belonging to others. And **(16)** — **commingling** the money
or property of others with the licensee's own, **unless held in an escrow account containing only the
money or property of others.**

**This course states no Oklahoma trust or escrow account threshold, deadline or procedure**, because
those provisions were not read from primary text for this project. The principle from § 858-312(16) was
read and is stated: **other people's money never mixes with the firm's.** Read the trust account rules
and your broker's written procedures before you accept your first cheque.

And a related ground worth knowing now: **§ 858-312(22)** makes it a ground **to offer, loan, pay, or
make to appear to have been paid, a down payment or earnest money deposit for a purchaser or seller.**
Funding a buyer's earnest money to save a deal is not generosity; it is a licence problem.

## Hour 14 — Confidentiality, and an exhaustive list

### 14.1 The duty

> **Keep confidential information received from a party or prospective party confidential.** The
> confidential information shall not be disclosed by a firm without the consent of the party disclosing
> the information unless **consent to the disclosure is granted in writing** by the party or prospective
> party disclosing the information, **the disclosure is required by law**, or **the information is made
> public or becomes public as the result of actions from a source other than the firm.**

**Three exceptions and only three:** written consent; disclosure required by law; the information
becoming public otherwise than through the firm.

### 14.2 And then the sentence that makes Oklahoma different

> **The following information shall be considered confidential and SHALL BE THE ONLY INFORMATION
> CONSIDERED CONFIDENTIAL in a transaction:**
>
> **a.** that a party or prospective party **is willing to pay more or accept less** than what is being
> offered,
> **b.** that a party or prospective party **is willing to agree to financing terms that are different
> from those offered**,
> **c.** **the motivating factors** of the party or prospective party purchasing, selling, leasing,
> optioning or exchanging the property, and
> **d.** **information specifically designated as confidential by a party** unless such information is
> public.

**"Shall be the only information considered confidential in a transaction."**

That is a closed list, and it is unusual. Other states define confidential information broadly and let
practice work out the edges. Oklahoma names four items and shuts the door.

### 14.3 What follows in practice

**First, the four items are learnable and should be learned.** (a) will pay more or accept less. (b)
will accept different financing terms. (c) motivating factors. (d) anything a party specifically
designates — the escape valve, and the one a licensee should actually use.

**Second, item (d) is how anything else becomes confidential.** A seller who wants their divorce, their
job relocation or their health kept quiet should **specifically designate it as confidential** — and a
competent licensee prompts them to. Note the qualifier: *unless such information is public.* Designating
something already public does not make it confidential.

**Third, and most importantly: confidentiality is not a defence to disclosure duties.** The exception
"the disclosure is required by law" does real work here. **§ 858-353(A)(5)** requires disclosure of
information pertaining to the property as required by the Residential Property Condition Disclosure Act.
A seller cannot designate a defect as confidential and thereby suppress it. If the law requires it out,
it goes out — and the statute says so in the same paragraph.

**Fourth, note what is NOT on the list.** The price a seller paid. The existence of other offers. The
fact a property has been on the market twice. None of those is confidential unless a party specifically
designates it under (d). That does not make it *wise* to broadcast them — but the licensee should know
where the legal line actually is rather than guessing.

### 14.4 And it survives the relationship

**§ 858-358**, taken up in Hour 18: after termination, expiration or completion, one of only two
surviving duties is **to keep confidential all confidential information received during the broker's
relationship with a party.**

## Hour 15 — The disclosure duties

### 15.1 Duty 5 — property condition

> **Disclose information pertaining to the property as required by the Residential Property Condition
> Disclosure Act.**

The duty is stated by cross-reference, and § 858-312(23) makes **violation of the Residential Property
Condition Disclosure Act** an independent ground for suspension or revocation.

**This course states no form, deadline, exemption or content requirement from that Act**, because the
Act's own text was not read from primary text for this project. What was read, and is therefore stated,
is that the duty exists, that it is a duty owed to **all parties**, and that breaching it is a licence
ground on two separate tracks.

**Read the Act.** It is short, it is the operative source, and it governs the single most common
disclosure question an Oklahoma residential licensee will face.

### 15.2 Duty 6 — comply with everything else

> **Comply with all requirements of The Oklahoma Real Estate License Code and all applicable statutes
> and rules.**

Which folds the whole Code and Title 605 into the broker-relationships duties, and means a violation
anywhere is also a § 858-353 violation — and § 858-312(3) separately makes **failing to comply with
§§ 858-351 through 858-363** a ground for discipline.

### 15.3 Duty 7 — compensation, and two numbers

> **Disclose:**
> **a.** information pertaining to **compensation and fees assessed on each transaction to the
> represented party**, which shall be **communicated in writing BEFORE THE EFFECTIVE DATE of the
> contract for sale or lease**, and
> **b.** **the time frame for which the compensation agreement is valid, NOT TO EXCEED ONE (1) YEAR. If
> no time frame is specified, the compensation agreement shall default to SIXTY (60) DAYS.**

Four operative facts:

1. **In writing.**
2. **Before the effective date of the contract** — not at closing, not when the question arises.
3. **A compensation agreement may not run more than one year.**
4. **Silence is not indefinite — it is sixty days.** A compensation agreement with no stated term
   expires in sixty days by operation of the statute.

That fourth point is worth stating twice, because a licensee who assumes an undated compensation
agreement runs until someone cancels it is wrong by statute, and will discover it at the worst possible
moment.

## Hour 16 — Duties to the party served, and serving both parties

### 16.1 The two additional duties — § 858-353(B)

> A broker shall have the following duties and responsibilities **only to a party for whom the broker is
> providing brokerage services** in a transaction, **which are mandatory and may not be abrogated or
> waived**:
>
> 1. **Inform the party in writing when an offer is made that the party will be expected to pay certain
>    costs, brokerage service costs and approximate amount of the costs**; and
> 2. **Keep the party informed regarding the transaction.**

**Duty (B)(1) is a cost-estimate obligation with a trigger and a form.** The trigger is **when an offer
is made**. The form is **writing**. The content is **the costs the party will be expected to pay,
including brokerage service costs, and their approximate amount.**

This is Module OK-08's subject — Estimating Transaction Expenses — arriving as a statutory duty rather
than a courtesy. It is also why that module insists a cost sheet is labelled an estimate and shows its
sources: the statute requires an approximate amount, and an approximation presented as a promise is a
different thing.

**Duty (B)(2) — keep the party informed — is the one most complaints are actually about.** Not fraud.
Silence. A party who cannot reach their licensee and does not know what is happening will complain, and
under this subsection they will be right.

### 16.2 Serving both parties — § 858-353(C)

> **When working with both parties to a transaction, the duties and responsibilities set forth in this
> section shall remain in place for both parties.**

And § 858-355.1(B): **a broker may provide brokerage services to one or both parties in a transaction.**

**This is not dual agency, because Oklahoma has no dual agency.** § 858-360(B) forbids it outright.
Serving both parties in Oklahoma is not a special status with a consent form and a reduced duty set. It
is the ordinary duty set, **applied in full to both sides at once.**

Which raises the obvious question and answers it. *How can I owe both sides the same duties?* Because
the duties are honesty, skill and care, offer handling, accounting, confidentiality, disclosure and
compliance — **not loyalty.** Loyalty is precisely the fiduciary duty § 858-360(A) abrogated. Nothing in
the statutory list requires you to advance one party's interests against the other's, so there is no
contradiction to resolve.

**What still has to happen** is § 858-355.1(D): a firm providing brokerage services to both parties
**shall provide written notice to both parties that it is doing so, prior to the parties signing a
contract** to purchase, lease, option or exchange.

**And the confidentiality list becomes load-bearing.** Serving both sides, you may hold each party's
willingness to pay more or accept less. Those are §§ 858-353(A)(4)(a) items and they stay in — from
everyone, including the other party you are also serving.

## Hour 17 — Brokerage agreements and the written disclosures

### 17.1 § 858-355.1(A) — the duties go into the agreement

> **All brokerage agreements shall incorporate as material terms the duties and responsibilities set
> forth in Section 858-353.**

Not summarised, not referenced in passing — **incorporated as material terms.** A party reading their
brokerage agreement is entitled to see what they are owed.

### 17.2 § 858-355.1(C) — disclose in writing, before signing

> A broker who is providing brokerage services to one or both parties **shall describe and disclose in
> writing the broker's duties and responsibilities set forth in Section 858-353 prior to the party or
> parties signing a contract to sell, purchase, lease, option, or exchange real estate.**

**The trigger is the contract, not the brokerage agreement.** The disclosure must be made before the
party signs the **transaction** contract. In practice it happens far earlier, and should.

### 17.3 § 858-356 — and then it is confirmed

> The written disclosures as required by subsection C of Section 858-355.1 **shall be confirmed by each
> party in writing in a separate provision, incorporated in or attached to the contract** to purchase,
> lease, option, or exchange real estate. In those cases where **a broker is involved in a transaction
> but does not prepare the contract**, compliance with the disclosure requirements **shall be documented
> by the broker.**

Two mechanisms. Ordinarily the confirmation is **a separate provision in or attached to the contract**,
signed by each party. Where the broker did not prepare the contract, the broker **documents compliance**
some other way — and that documentation is the broker's own protection.

### 17.4 § 858-355.1(E) — providing fewer services

The provision new licensees never expect and should:

> **If a broker intends to provide fewer brokerage services than those required to complete a
> transaction**, the broker shall provide **written disclosure** to the party for whom the broker is
> providing brokerage services. Such disclosure shall include **a description of those steps in the
> transaction for which the broker will not provide brokerage services**, and also state that **the
> broker assisting the other party in the transaction is not required to provide assistance with these
> steps in any manner.**

So limited-service brokerage is permitted in Oklahoma, and it is permitted **on conditions**: written
disclosure, a **description of the specific steps** not covered, and an express statement that **the
other side's broker is not required to pick them up.**

That last requirement exists because the predictable harm of limited service is a party who assumes
somebody will handle the gap. The statute requires them to be told, in writing, that nobody will.

**Note also what § 858-355.1(E) does NOT permit.** It allows fewer *services*. It does not allow fewer
**duties** — § 858-353(A) and (B) are mandatory and unwaivable.

## Hour 18 — Compensation, termination, liability, and your own position

### 18.1 Compensation — § 858-359

**(A)** **The payment or promise of payment or compensation by a party to a broker does not determine
what relationship, if any, has been established** between the broker and a party.

**Who pays does not decide whom you serve.** In Oklahoma this is even cleaner than elsewhere, because
there is no agency to be created by implication in the first place.

**(B)** Receiving a fee or compensation **based on a selling price or lease cost** does not constitute a
breach of duty or obligation to any party. A percentage fee is not, by itself, a conflict.

**(C)** A broker **or a real estate owner of record** may compensate or offer compensation to **any real
estate professional licensed by the Commission** for providing brokerage services in connection with a
transaction of Oklahoma real estate.

**(D)** Nothing **requires or prohibits** charging a separate fee for each duty or service, and nothing
**requires** a broker or owner of record **to offer compensation** to any real estate professional.

**And the associate's own position — § 858-312(4):** it is a ground for discipline for an associate to
accept **a commission or other valuable consideration for the performance of any acts as an associate,
except from the real estate broker with whom the associate is associated.** You are paid by your broker.
Not by a seller, not by a buyer, not by a cooperating firm.

**Related ground — § 858-312(21):** paying any part of a fee or commission **to any person not
licensed.** Referral fees to unlicensed persons are a licence problem.

### 18.2 Termination — § 858-358

> Except as may be provided in a written brokerage agreement…, the broker owes **no further duties or
> responsibilities to the party after termination, expiration, or completion of performance** of the
> transaction, **except**:
> **1.** To **account for all monies and property** relating to the transaction; and
> **2.** To **keep confidential all confidential information** received during the broker's relationship
> with a party.

**Two duties survive; everything else stops.** A listing expires and six months later you are working
the same house for a buyer. The former seller's motivating factors and their willingness to accept less
are still § 858-353(A)(4) items, and § 858-358(2) keeps them in.

Note the opening words: **"except as may be provided in a written brokerage agreement."** The agreement
can extend duties past termination. Read it.

### 18.3 Liability — § 858-362

> **A party to a real estate transaction shall not be vicariously liable for the acts or omissions of a
> real estate licensee** who is providing brokerage services under Sections 858-351 through 858-363.

A seller is not automatically on the hook for what their broker did. This follows naturally from the
abolition of agency: vicarious liability was a consequence of the principal-agent relationship, and
Oklahoma removed the relationship.

**It says nothing about the LICENSEE's own liability**, which is untouched, nor about the broker's
responsibility for their associates.

### 18.4 Your own position — § 858-363

> Each broker associate, sales associate, and provisional sales associate **shall be associated with a
> real estate broker.** Associates **shall not enter into a brokerage agreement with a party in the
> associate's name** and shall only be allowed to enter into the agreement **in the name of the
> broker.** A real estate broker **may authorize associates to provide brokerage services in the name of
> the real estate broker**, which may include the execution of written agreements.

Three things you will need on your first day:

1. **You must be associated with a broker.**
2. **Every brokerage agreement is in the broker's name.** Never yours.
3. **You act under the broker's authorisation**, which may extend to executing written agreements — and
   you should know the scope of yours.

**§ 858-312(5)** adds the corollary: representing or attempting to represent a broker **other than the
one with whom you are associated**, without that broker's express knowledge and consent, is a ground for
discipline.

### 18.5 Deed theft — § 858-364, and something new

> As part of **each closing** of a transaction, notification of the signs and risks of **deed theft**, as
> defined in Section 858-351, **shall be required to be given to the buyer by a licensed real estate
> broker. The buyer shall confirm receipt of such notification by affixing his or her signature** to
> such notification. The Oklahoma Real Estate Commission shall promulgate rules to enact the provisions
> of this section.

**Deed theft** is defined at § 858-351(3) as intentionally altering, falsifying, forging or
misrepresenting a document relating to real property with intent to deceive, defraud or unlawfully
transfer or encumber ownership rights; misrepresenting oneself as the owner or the owner's authorised
representative to obtain ownership or possession; or, with intent to defraud, taking, obtaining,
stealing or transferring title or ownership by fraud, forgery, larceny or any other fraudulent or
deceptive practice.

**The obligation is on the broker, at every closing, and the buyer signs.**

**This course states no wording or format for the notification**, because the Commission's implementing
rules and the form itself were not located in the publication read for this project. **Obtain the current
form from the Commission**, and confirm the rules have issued before relying on any version of it.

Relatedly, **§ 858-307.2(F)** requires licensees to complete Commission-specified continuing education
on deed theft **on or before 1 November 2028.**

### 18.6 The whole module in one page

**There is no agency.** § 858-360(A) abrogated the fiduciary duties; § 858-360(B) forbids agency,
subagency and dual agency.

**To ALL parties, mandatory and unwaivable** — § 858-353(A): honesty and reasonable skill and care;
receive, reduce to writing on any party's request, and timely present all written offers and
counteroffers, unless a party specifically waives in writing; timely accounting; confidentiality of
**four listed items and only those**; property disclosure as the Residential Property Condition
Disclosure Act requires; compliance with the Code and all applicable statutes and rules; and written
compensation disclosure **before the contract's effective date**, with a term **capped at one year** and
**defaulting to sixty days** if unstated.

**To the party you serve, additionally** — § 858-353(B): written notice of the costs they will be
expected to pay when an offer is made, including brokerage service costs and their approximate amount;
and keeping them informed.

**Both parties?** Same duties, both sides, plus written notice to both under § 858-355.1(D).

**In writing, before signing, confirmed in or attached to the contract** — §§ 858-355.1(C) and 858-356.

**Fewer services?** Written disclosure of exactly which steps you will not perform, and a statement that
the other broker need not perform them either — § 858-355.1(E).

**Afterwards:** account, and keep confidences — § 858-358.

**And breach of any of it is § 858-312(3): failing to comply with §§ 858-351 through 858-363.**

## Supplemental source review — 2026-09-06

This required reading clarifies the existing topics; no additional clock-hour credit or regulator approval is claimed.

### supervision

Related module 03, §18.4. Opening a branch is not just creating a web page. Under 59 O.S. 858-310(B),
branch licensing and separate broker direction requirements apply. The responsible broker must
supervise licensed activity; an associate cannot appoint themselves independent broker by using a
different trade name. OAC 605:10-9-1 supplies further brokerage responsibilities. Check the actual
license, assignment and activity, not merely an office sign or an individual's seniority.

Sources: [OREC License Code and Rules, as of November 1, 2025](https://oklahoma.gov/content/dam/ok/en/orec/documents/meetings/Nov%201%202025%20Oklahoma%20Real%20Estate%20Commission%20Code%20and%20Rules%20Book.pdf).

### conflicts

Related module 14, §80.2 and module 03. A broker serving both parties must provide the written notice
required by 59 O.S. 858-355.1(D). Section 858-312(3), not (6), makes failure to comply with the Broker
Relationships Act a disciplinary ground. Section (6) concerns accounting and remittance. Payment alone
does not establish a brokerage relationship under 858-359(A). The Oklahoma Broker Relationships Act
abrogates common-law agency duties within its scope (858-360), but permits additional agreed services
that do not displace statutory duties. The word "agent" as a trade description does not recreate a
different common-law regime (858-361).

Confidential information under 858-353(A)(4) includes specified price/financing flexibility, motivation
and designated information; the statutory exceptions include written consent, legal requirement and
information becoming public from a source other than the firm. Do not call every physical defect
confidential or disclose negotiations just because a transaction ended. Both-side service does not
permit a broker to sacrifice honesty, accounting or timely communication. Fees can be separately
negotiated under 858-359(D); competing firms cannot agree on a common fee floor. An independent firm's
own fee policy is not, by itself, an agreement among competitors. Sources include the current OREC
Code and the DOJ's primary horizontal-price-fixing enforcement record.

Sources: [OREC License Code and Rules, as of November 1, 2025](https://oklahoma.gov/content/dam/ok/en/orec/documents/meetings/Nov%201%202025%20Oklahoma%20Real%20Estate%20Commission%20Code%20and%20Rules%20Book.pdf); [DOJ — United States v. Atlanta Real Estate Board, horizontal price fixing](https://www.justice.gov/atr/case/us-v-atlanta-real-estate-board).
