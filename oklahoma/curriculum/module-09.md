## Overview

Contract law as a licensee must know it: the elements, the classifications, the statute of frauds, the
life of an offer, and then performance — discharge, breach and remedies. The module closes on the
boundary between completing a form and drafting one, which in Oklahoma has a particular shape because
the state uses **promulgated contract forms** that this project has not read and therefore does not
describe.

## Learning objectives

1. Name the elements of a valid contract and identify which is missing from a defective fact pattern.
2. Distinguish valid, void, voidable and unenforceable.
3. Distinguish executory from executed, bilateral from unilateral, express from implied.
4. Explain the statute of frauds as it applies to real estate.
5. Trace an offer through acceptance, rejection, counter-offer, revocation and lapse.
6. Identify the moment a contract is formed and why every deadline runs from it.
7. Distinguish an amendment from a notice and use each correctly.
8. Explain genuine assent and the ways it fails.
9. Name the ways a contract is discharged.
10. Name the remedies for breach and explain why specific performance is available in real estate.
11. Distinguish assignment from novation and state who remains liable.
12. State the licensee's drafting boundary and route accordingly.
13. Relate offer handling to § 858-353(A)(2) and identify the written-waiver carve-out.

## Hour 47 — Elements of a valid contract

### 47.1 The elements

A contract is a legally enforceable agreement to do or refrain from doing a legal act.

1. **Offer and acceptance** — mutual assent. A definite offer and an unqualified acceptance of it.
2. **Consideration** — something of legal value exchanged. In a purchase contract the consideration is
   the **mutual promises**: the buyer promises to pay, the seller promises to convey. **Earnest money is
   not the consideration.**
3. **Legal capacity** — parties competent to contract. Minors, persons adjudicated incompetent, and
   persons acting under an inadequate power of attorney all raise capacity questions, and every one is a
   question for counsel and the title company.
4. **Lawful object** — a legal purpose. A contract to do something illegal is void.
5. **Genuine assent** — agreement not produced by fraud, misrepresentation, mistake, duress or undue
   influence. Hour 50.
6. **A writing**, for a conveyance of an interest in real estate, under the statute of frauds.

### 47.2 Where a licensee supplies the defect

**Almost always at element 5.** A misrepresentation — even an innocent one, even one the licensee
believed, even one merely repeated from a tax record — can undermine the assent the contract depends on.

Which is why **attribute or verify, never assert** is a contract-formation rule and not merely a
courtesy, and why § 858-312(2) makes substantial misrepresentations in the conduct of business a ground
for suspension or revocation.

## Hour 48 — Classifications and the statute of frauds

### 48.1 By validity

**Valid** — meets all elements and is enforceable.

**Void** — never had legal effect at all. There is nothing to enforce and nothing to disaffirm.

**Voidable** — **valid and binding unless and until** a party with the power to disaffirm elects to do
so. A contract with a minor is the standard example: binding on the adult, disaffirmable by the minor.

**Unenforceable** — has the elements but cannot be enforced in court, typically because it is not in
writing or a limitation period has run.

**Void versus voidable is tested relentlessly.** Void is a nullity. Voidable is real and binding on at
least one party until the other gets out of it.

### 48.2 By performance, promise and formation

**Executory** — something remains to be done. A signed purchase contract before closing is executory.
**Executed** — fully performed. **Note the trap:** "executed" in this sense means *completed*, not
*signed*, and ordinary practice uses the word both ways.

**Bilateral** — a promise for a promise; both bound. A purchase contract. **Unilateral** — a promise for
an act; only the offeror is bound until someone performs. An **open listing** and an **option** are the
standard examples.

**Express** — stated in words. **Implied** — inferred from conduct.

### 48.3 The statute of frauds

Certain contracts must be **in writing and signed by the party to be charged** to be enforceable.
Contracts for the sale of an interest in real estate are within it, as are leases beyond a stated
duration.

Two consequences. **An oral real estate contract is not void — it is unenforceable**, which is a
different thing producing different arguments. And the writing must contain the essential terms: the
parties, an adequate description, the price, and the signature of the party against whom enforcement is
sought.

**This course states no Oklahoma lease-duration threshold** for the writing requirement, because
Oklahoma's statute of frauds was not read from primary text for this project. **The practice rule does
not depend on the threshold: get it in writing.**

And in Oklahoma the practice rule has a statutory partner. **§ 858-353(A)(2)(b)** requires a broker to
**reduce offers or counteroffers to a written form upon request of any party to a transaction.** Not your
party. **Any** party.

## Hour 49 — Offer, acceptance, and the date everything runs from

### 49.1 Offer

A **definite proposal, communicated to the offeree, showing an intention to be bound on acceptance.**

An advertisement is generally **not** an offer; it is an invitation to make one — which is why a listing
at a price is not a promise to sell at that price.

An offer ends by:

- **Acceptance** — it becomes a contract.
- **Rejection** by the offeree.
- **Counter-offer**, which is a rejection plus a new offer.
- **Revocation** by the offeror **before acceptance is communicated**.
- **Lapse** — the stated time expires, or a reasonable time passes where none is stated.

**Death or incapacity** of a party before acceptance also terminates an offer.

### 49.2 Acceptance

Must be **unqualified** — the mirror image of the offer — and **communicated in the manner the offer
requires.**

**Signature and delivery are different events and may fall on different days.**

### 49.3 The date everything runs from

**Every deadline in the contract runs from the moment the contract is formed** — on most forms, the date
acceptance is **delivered**, not the date the last party signed.

**The discipline:** on the day acceptance is delivered, **write the date in the place the contract
provides, and compute every deadline from it that same day**, into the file and into your calendar. Then
work backwards from each deadline to the date the underlying work must **start** — an inspection deadline
is useless if the inspector is booked out ten days.

**A deadline miscounted from the wrong anchor shifts every subsequent date.** It is among the most common
and most avoidable failures in residential practice, and it is squarely a § 858-353(A)(1) reasonable
skill and care problem.

### 49.4 Counter-offers, and the multiple-offer problem

**A counter-offer rejects the original and substitutes a new one.** The original is dead and can no
longer simply be accepted.

So a seller with three offers cannot counter all three without destroying all three. Where the firm's
forms supply a **multiple counter-proposal** instrument reserving the right to accept only one, that is
the tool. **Where they do not, a licensee does not invent language to solve it** — Hour 53.

### 49.5 Three instruments, distinguished

- An **offer** or **counter-offer** proposes terms **before** there is a contract.
- An **amendment** changes a contract that **already exists** and requires the agreement of all parties.
- A **notice** exercises a right the contract **already gives** — removing a contingency, giving notice of
  a defect or of an inability to obtain financing. A notice is **unilateral**: no agreement needed, but
  the **form and the deadline the contract specifies** are.

**A buyer who "amends" when they should have given notice has asked permission for something they were
entitled to do**, and handed the other side a negotiation they did not have to have.

### 49.6 And the Oklahoma offer-handling duty

**§ 858-353(A)(2)**, to **all parties**, **unless specifically waived in writing by a party**:

**(a)** receive all written offers and counteroffers; **(b)** **reduce offers or counteroffers to a
written form upon request of any party**; **(c)** **present timely** all written offers and counteroffers.

**Nothing in it stops when the property goes under contract.** If a written offer arrives, it is received
and presented. What the seller then does about it is a legal question about their existing contract,
routed to counsel.

And the waiver is the **party's**, **specific**, and **in writing.** An oral "don't bring me lowballs" is
neither specific nor written.

## Hour 50 — Genuine assent and how it fails

### 50.1 The five failures

**Fraud** — a knowing or reckless misstatement of material fact, or active concealment, made to induce
reliance, and relied on.

**Misrepresentation** — a false statement of material fact. **Innocent** (believed true, on reasonable
grounds), **negligent** (made without reasonable care as to truth), or **fraudulent**.

**Mistake** — mutual, where both parties are wrong about a material fact; or unilateral, which rarely
avoids a contract.

**Duress** — assent compelled by wrongful threat.

**Undue influence** — assent obtained by improper pressure in a relationship of trust or dependence.

### 50.2 The one licensees actually commit

**Negligent misrepresentation, by repeating something without checking it.**

Square footage from a county record. Acreage from a prior listing. A school assignment that changed.
Zoning nobody confirmed. "The seller says the basement is dry." "The roof is about ten years old." A
flood zone read off a map rather than determined. A payment figure restated from a lender's estimate. A
net sheet presented as a promise.

**Attribute or verify. Never assert.**

### 50.3 Puffing, and where it stops

**Puffing** is obvious opinion nobody could reasonably rely on — *lovely home; wonderful light; you'll
love the kitchen.* Not actionable, because not a statement of fact.

**It stops the moment a statement is specific, factual and checkable.**

| Puffing | A statement of fact |
|---|---|
| "Great location" | "It's twelve minutes to the turnpike entrance" |
| "Beautiful lot" | "The lot is half an acre" |
| "Solid house" | "The roof was replaced in 2019" |
| "Plenty of room" | "It's 2,100 square feet" |
| "Won't last long" | "There are two other offers" |

**If you cannot show where the number came from, do not say it.**

### 50.4 And the guarantee that is a named ground

**§ 858-312(10)** makes it a ground for suspension or revocation to guarantee, or to authorise or permit
any licensee to guarantee, **future profits which may result from the resale of real estate.**

*"You'll make money on this."* *"This'll be worth $X in five years."* *"You can't lose."* Every one of
those is the conduct paragraph (10) names.

## Hour 51 — Performance and discharge

### 51.1 Performance

Most contracts end by **performance** — everyone does what they promised. In a real estate purchase that
is closing: the buyer pays, the seller conveys, the deed is delivered and recorded.

**Time is of the essence** where the contract says so, and where it does, a deadline missed is a breach
rather than a delay. Read whether the contract says so before assuming either way.

**Substantial performance** — performance meeting the essential purpose with minor deviations — may
suffice in some contexts and not in others. Whether it does in a given case is a legal question.

### 51.2 The ways a contract is discharged

- **Performance.**
- **Agreement** — mutual rescission; **novation**, substituting a party or an obligation; **accord and
  satisfaction**, agreeing to and performing a different obligation.
- **Operation of law** — bankruptcy; expiration of a limitation period; **destruction of the subject
  matter**; **impossibility**; illegality arising after formation.
- **Breach.**

### 51.3 Contingencies

A **contingency** is a condition that must be satisfied or waived before a party is obliged to perform.
**It is not a general right to change one's mind.**

Every contingency has **four** parts and all four must be read:

1. **The condition** — what must happen.
2. **The deadline** — by when.
3. **The method of notice** — how, in what form, delivered how.
4. **The effect of silence** — what happens if the deadline passes with no notice. Sometimes waiver,
   sometimes termination. **It depends entirely on the form**, and assuming is how transactions die.

**Contingencies fail through inattention, not through the condition.** The prevention is the calendar
work in Hour 49.3.

## Hour 52 — Breach, remedies, assignment

### 52.1 Breach

A failure to perform without legal excuse. **Material** breach goes to the essence and excuses the other
party's performance; **minor** breach sounds in damages only.

**Anticipatory breach** — a party states before performance is due that they will not perform — may allow
the other party to treat the contract as breached immediately.

### 52.2 The remedies

- **Compensatory damages** — money to put the injured party where performance would have.
- **Liquidated damages** — an amount the parties fixed in advance. In residential practice earnest money
  is frequently the agreed liquidated damages for a buyer's default, which is one reason the amount
  matters.
- **Specific performance** — a court order compelling the actual transfer. **Available in real estate
  because each parcel is unique**, so money may not make the injured party whole. This is Module OK-01's
  uniqueness characteristic doing legal work.
- **Rescission** — unwinding the contract and restoring the parties to their prior positions.
- **Reformation** — correcting a writing to reflect what the parties actually agreed.

**None of these is a licensee's to advise on.** A party asking what they can do about a breach is asking a
legal question. **What a licensee contributes is an accurate factual record of what happened and when** —
which is exactly what the file will be worth if it goes anywhere.

### 52.3 Assignment and novation

**Assignment** transfers rights under a contract. Unless the contract prohibits it or the duties are
personal, contract rights are generally assignable — **but the original party ordinarily remains liable.**

**Novation** substitutes a new party for an original one **and releases the original.** It requires the
consent of **all** parties.

**The practical difference is who can be sued when performance fails.** A buyer who assigns a purchase
contract and believes they have walked away usually has not. Only a novation does that, and a novation is
a document everyone signs.

### 52.4 Earnest money on a failed contract

**Its disposition is decided by what the contract provides and by the parties or a court — not by the
licensee.**

Two Oklahoma provisions bear. **§ 858-353(A)(3)** — timely account for all money and property received.
**§ 858-312(6)** — failing within a reasonable time to account for or remit monies belonging to others is
a ground for suspension or revocation. And **§ 858-312(16)** — commingling.

**This course states no Oklahoma escrow deadline or procedure**, because those provisions were not read
from primary text for this project. Read them and your broker's written procedures before you accept your
first cheque. **On any dispute: escalate to your broker immediately and do nothing else.**

## Hour 53 — The drafting boundary, and Oklahoma's promulgated forms

### 53.1 The boundary

**Inside the licence:** completing the forms your firm supplies by filling in the facts of the
transaction; selecting among the provisions the forms offer; explaining what a provision says in its
ordinary sense; and telling a party to get expert advice.

**Outside the licence:** composing new legal language to solve a party's specific legal problem; advising
which form of ownership to take title in; opining on the legal effect of a provision; drafting an
addendum to cover something the forms do not.

**When the forms do not fit, counsel drafts it and it is attached.** A licensee who composes a clause has
practised law — and has usually produced a clause that does not do what the party wanted.

### 53.2 Oklahoma uses promulgated forms — and this course does not describe them

**Oklahoma residential transactions are conducted on contract forms promulgated by the Oklahoma Real
Estate Contract Form Committee.** That is a fact about Oklahoma practice that a licensee must know.

**This project has not read those forms, and no document in this build reproduces, describes, paraphrases
or explains any of them or any of their provisions.** Not the contingency structures, not the deadlines,
not the earnest money provisions, not the notice mechanisms, not the default remedies.

**That is a real gap and it is named rather than filled.** Every structural statement in Hours 47 through
52 is general contract law from the body of pre-license knowledge. **How any of it is implemented in the
Oklahoma promulgated forms must be learned from the forms themselves**, from your broker, and from the
Commission — and it must be learned before you write your first offer.

### 53.3 What that means for you, practically

1. **Get the current promulgated forms** and read every one of them, front to back, before you use one.
2. **Read them again when they change**, and they do change. Note that 605:10-5-3(k) requires all
   materials distributed to students to be **current and up-to-date with the License Code and Rules and
   state or federal laws** — the same currency discipline applies to the forms you practise on.
3. **Learn where each of Hour 51.3's four contingency elements lives in the form** — the condition, the
   deadline, the method of notice, the effect of silence.
4. **Never improvise.** If the form does not cover it, counsel drafts it.

### 53.4 The one-page summary

**Elements:** offer and acceptance, consideration, capacity, lawful object, genuine assent, writing.
**Validity:** valid, void, voidable, unenforceable. **Performance:** executory, executed.
**Offer ends by:** acceptance, rejection, counter-offer, revocation before communication of acceptance,
lapse. **A counter-offer kills the original.** **Deadlines run from formation.** **Discharge:**
performance, agreement, operation of law, breach. **Remedies:** damages, liquidated damages, specific
performance, rescission, reformation. **Assignment leaves you liable; novation does not.**

**And the Oklahoma overlay:** receive, reduce to writing on any party's request, and timely present all
written offers and counteroffers — § 858-353(A)(2) — unless a party specifically waives in writing.
