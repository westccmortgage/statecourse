## Overview

The agreements under which a broker provides brokerage services, and what a licensee does with a
property once one is signed. In Oklahoma the first half of that is governed by §§ 858-355.1, 858-356 and
858-363, and the second half by a set of advertising provisions in § 858-312 that a new licensee should
read before their first listing rather than after their first complaint.

## Learning objectives

1. Describe the types of listing arrangement by who is owed compensation and when.
2. State who enters a brokerage agreement in Oklahoma and in whose name.
3. State the three § 858-355.1 disclosure requirements and the § 858-356 confirmation requirement.
4. Apply § 858-353(A)(7)'s one-year cap and sixty-day default to a compensation agreement.
5. Describe the § 858-355.1(E) limited-service disclosure and what it must contain.
6. Take a listing appointment through the sequence an Oklahoma licensee must follow.
7. Identify the § 858-312 grounds that govern advertising and marketing.
8. Audit an advertisement for the licensee-status disclosure § 858-312(20) requires.
9. Explain what § 858-312(12) permits and forbids about prizes, gifts and inducements.
10. Explain why § 858-312(10) forbids guaranteeing future profits.
11. Describe the marketing sequence and the licensee's role at each step.
12. State the rule that prevents most marketing misrepresentation.

## Hour 30 — Types of listing arrangement

### 30.1 Distinguished by one question

**In what circumstances is the broker owed compensation?**

**Exclusive right to sell.** The broker is owed on a sale during the term **regardless of who procures
the buyer, including the owner.** The ordinary residential arrangement, and ordinary because it removes
the broker's incentive problem: the firm can spend on marketing without worrying that the owner's cousin
will appear and eliminate the fee.

**Exclusive agency.** The broker is the only one authorised, **but the owner reserves the right to sell
to a buyer they find themselves** without owing compensation. Which produces the obvious dispute — who
found the buyer? — and the answer belongs in the agreement, in writing, usually by naming the reserved
prospects.

**Open.** Non-exclusive. The owner may authorise any number of brokers and **only the one who procures
the buyer is compensated.** The owner may also sell themselves and owe nothing.

*(The words "listing" and "agency" survive in ordinary usage. Remember Module OK-03: § 858-360(B) means
there is no agency relationship underneath them. What exists is a **brokerage agreement** under which a
**broker** provides **brokerage services** to a **party**.)*

### 30.2 Net arrangements

A **net** arrangement is one in which the owner receives a fixed net amount and the broker keeps whatever
is above it.

The potential conflict is that **the broker receives the increase above a fixed seller net**, so the
owner may not appreciate how much market value is being allocated to the broker. The owner's stated
net does not fall merely because the sale price rises; the previous inverse-price description was wrong.
Under § 858-353(A)(1) a broker owes **honesty and reasonable skill and care to all parties**, and under
§ 858-353(A)(7) compensation must be disclosed in writing before the contract's effective date — an
arrangement whose amount is unknown until the sale price is known makes that disclosure difficult to
make meaningfully.

**This course states no Oklahoma rule permitting or prohibiting net arrangements**, because 605:10-3-1
and the broker-relationships sections do not address them and no other provision on the point was read
from primary text for this project. The professional position does not depend on it: any such proposal
goes to your broker before it goes anywhere else.

### 30.3 Termination

An arrangement ends by **expiry**, by **performance**, by **mutual agreement**, or by **operation of
law** — destruction of the property, death or incapacity in some circumstances.

**And § 858-353(A)(7)(b) supplies an Oklahoma-specific termination rule that catches people:** the time
frame for which a compensation agreement is valid **may not exceed one (1) year**, and **if no time frame
is specified it defaults to sixty (60) days.** An undated compensation agreement does not run until
someone cancels it. It expires in sixty days by statute.

Whether a party who ends an arrangement early owes anything is a question about the contract, and it goes
to your broker and counsel. A licensee who says "you can cancel any time, no consequences" has given
legal advice.

## Hour 31 — Elements of an Oklahoma brokerage agreement

### 31.1 Who enters it — § 858-363

> Associates **shall not enter into a brokerage agreement with a party in the associate's name** and
> shall only be allowed to enter into the agreement **in the name of the broker.** A real estate broker
> **may authorize associates to provide brokerage services in the name of the real estate broker**, which
> may include the execution of written agreements.

**Every brokerage agreement is in the broker's name.** You sign under your broker's authorisation, and
you should know the scope of yours before your first appointment.

### 31.2 What must be in it — § 858-355.1(A)

> **All brokerage agreements shall incorporate as material terms the duties and responsibilities set
> forth in Section 858-353.**

Not summarised. **Incorporated as material terms.**

### 31.3 The compensation terms — § 858-353(A)(7)

- **In writing.**
- **Communicated before the effective date of the contract** for sale or lease.
- **Covering compensation and fees assessed on each transaction** to the represented party.
- **With a stated time frame not exceeding one year** — and remember the sixty-day default.

### 31.4 The commercial elements

Beyond the statutory minimum, every agreement must settle:

- **The parties.** Everyone who must sign to convey — which Module OK-05 established is not answered by
  looking at the deed. Route it to counsel and the title company **at the appointment.**
- **The property**, with a legal description from the deed or the title company — never from a tax
  record, a prior listing or memory. **And in Oklahoma, whether minerals are included.**
- **The price**, which is the owner's decision.
- **The term**, with a definite end date, within the one-year compensation cap.
- **How compensation is earned** — the event that triggers it.
- **Included and excluded items**, in two written lists — Module OK-01 Hour 2.
- **Authorisations**: marketing, photography, lockbox, sign, showings, internet display, and any opt-outs.
- **Any reserved prospects**, on an exclusive-agency arrangement.
- **The owner's disclosure obligations** under the Residential Property Condition Disclosure Act, and the
  broker's own § 858-353(A)(5) duty.

### 31.5 The disclosures that accompany it — §§ 858-355.1(C) and 858-356

**Describe and disclose the § 858-353 duties in writing, prior to the party signing a contract** to sell,
purchase, lease, option or exchange. Then the disclosure is **confirmed by each party in writing in a
separate provision, incorporated in or attached to the contract.** Where the broker did not prepare the
contract, **the broker documents compliance.**

Do it at the appointment. Nobody has ever been disciplined for disclosing too early.

## Hour 32 — The listing appointment, in sequence

**Before you arrive:** confirm your authority under § 858-363; pull the tax record, the plat, the prior
sales and the comparables; and have the written § 858-353 duties disclosure with you.

**On arrival, in order:**

1. **Describe and disclose the § 858-353 duties in writing** — § 858-355.1(C). Note that you did, with
   the date.
2. **Explain what your firm can do**, including whether it will provide full or limited services —
   § 858-355.1(E).
3. **Walk the property.** Settle included and excluded items as you go. Ask specifically about **leased or
   financed equipment**, tenant-installed items, recent construction, and any unpaid or disputed
   contractor.
4. **Ask the ownership questions.** Who is on the deed; is anyone deceased; is there a trust, an estate, a
   life estate, a divorce; is there a tenant; **and who owns the minerals.** Route every complication to
   counsel and the title company rather than resolving it in the driveway.
5. **Present the pricing evidence in writing**, as a range, with adjustments shown — Module OK-07.
6. **Complete the agreement**, in the broker's name, incorporating the § 858-353 duties, with compensation
   disclosed in writing and a term inside the one-year cap.
7. **Explain the owner's disclosure obligations** under the Residential Property Condition Disclosure Act
   and your own § 858-353(A)(5) duty.
8. **Confirm in writing** what was decided, the same day.

### 32.1 The four questions most licensees do not ask

- **"Is any equipment on this property leased or financed?"** A buyer cannot discover it by looking.
- **"Has any work been done in the last year, and is everyone paid?"** Recent construction plus a payment
  dispute is a lien risk, routed to the title company at listing.
- **"Who owns the minerals?"** In Oklahoma, asked every time, routed every time.
- **"Who else has to sign?"** Five minutes now; a postponement later.

## Hour 33 — Buyer service agreements and limited service

### 33.1 Working with a buyer

The same statutory structure applies. A broker **may provide brokerage services to one or both parties**
— § 858-355.1(B) — the § 858-353(A) duties run to **all parties** regardless, and the § 858-353(B) duties
run to the party the broker is serving.

**Serving both parties is not dual agency**, because Oklahoma has none. It requires **written notice to
both parties that the firm is doing so, prior to the parties signing a contract** — § 858-355.1(D) — and
the full duty set applies to both.

### 33.2 The § 858-353(B) duties, in buyer practice

**(B)(1)** — **inform the party in writing, when an offer is made, that they will be expected to pay
certain costs, brokerage service costs and the approximate amount.** This is a statutory duty with a
trigger and a form, and Module OK-08 is where the arithmetic lives.

**(B)(2)** — **keep the party informed regarding the transaction.** Most complaints are about silence
rather than fraud, and this is the subsection they are made under.

### 33.3 Limited service — § 858-355.1(E)

> If a broker intends to provide **fewer brokerage services than those required to complete a
> transaction**, the broker shall provide **written disclosure** to the party for whom the broker is
> providing brokerage services. Such disclosure shall include **a description of those steps in the
> transaction for which the broker will not provide brokerage services**, and also state that **the
> broker assisting the other party in the transaction is not required to provide assistance with these
> steps in any manner.**

Three requirements, and all three must be met:

1. **In writing.**
2. **A description of the specific steps** not covered. Not "limited services" as a label — a description.
3. **An express statement that the other side's broker need not pick them up.**

The third exists because the predictable harm of limited service is a party who assumes somebody will
handle the gap. The statute requires them to be told, in writing, that nobody will.

**And note the limit of what (E) permits.** It permits fewer **services**. It does not permit fewer
**duties** — § 858-353(A) and (B) are mandatory and unwaivable.

## Hour 34 — Marketing activities — 605:10-3-1(a)(1)(J)

### 34.1 What marketing actually is

Not advertising. **The sequence: price from evidence, prepare, present accurately, expose to the market
that will buy it, and manage the response.** Advertising is one step and it is not the one that decides
outcomes.

**Price from evidence** — Module OK-07. **Prepare** — condition, access, information assembled before the
first showing rather than after the first question. **Present accurately** — photographs that show the
property as it is; a description whose every factual claim has a source. **Expose** — the multiple
listing service where the firm participates, digital syndication, signage with the owner's consent,
open houses, direct outreach. **Manage the response** — showing feedback, offer handling under
§ 858-353(A)(2), and keeping the party informed under § 858-353(B)(2).

### 34.2 The two rules that govern all of it

**Describe the property, never the people.** Never the buyer, never the tenant, never the neighbours.
Module OK-13 takes fair housing up in full; the discipline starts here.

**Attribute or verify. Never assert.** Every figure in a listing came from somewhere. Square footage from
a county record is an assessment record and not a measurement. Acreage from a prior listing is somebody
else's figure. School assignment changes. Zoning is the municipality's determination. Flood zone is a
determination, not a glance at a map.

*"The county record shows 1,940 square feet; if the exact figure matters to you, have it measured."*
That sentence identifies the source. Section 858-515.1 permits specified third-party area sources,
including tax-assessor records, with source identification and without independent verification. Its
limited protection is not a guarantee of accuracy or immunity for every marketing statement. Honesty
and reasonable care under 858-353(A)(1) still matter. This clarification was source-reviewed 2026-09-06.

### 34.3 Showings and feedback

Showings happen with the owner's authorisation and on the owner's terms, and what may be said about the
owner's position is limited by § 858-353(A)(4): **willingness to accept less, willingness to accept
different financing terms, motivating factors, and anything specifically designated confidential** stay
in. Everything else about the transaction is on a different footing — but knowing where the line is is
not the same as broadcasting up to it.

## Hour 35 — Advertising and the § 858-312 grounds

### 35.1 The provisions to read before your first listing

**§ 858-312(20)** — it is a ground for suspension or revocation to advertise **to buy, sell, rent, or
exchange any real estate without disclosing in writing that the licensee is a real estate licensee.**

**Every advertisement discloses licensee status, in writing.** Including one placed to buy. Including a
personal purchase. The **blind advertisement** — one that hides that a licensee is involved — is the
classic failure and it is a named ground here.

**§ 858-312(11)** — advertising or offering real estate for sale, rent or lease, or **placing a sign on
any real estate**, **without the consent of the owner or the owner's authorised representative.** Consent
for the sign is part of the authorisations settled in the agreement — Hour 31.

**§ 858-312(2)** — **substantial misrepresentations or false promises in the conduct of business, or
through real estate licensees, or advertising, which are intended to influence, persuade, or induce
others.** Advertising is named expressly.

**§ 858-312(10)** — **guaranteeing, or authorising or permitting any licensee to guarantee, future
profits which may result from the resale of real estate.**

Read (10) carefully, because it forbids something licensees say casually. *"You'll make money on this."*
*"This will be worth $X in five years."* *"You can't lose."* **Guaranteeing future profits from resale is
a named ground for suspension or revocation**, and nobody can support such a statement anyway.

**§ 858-312(12)** — prizes, money, gifts and inducements. This one has a permission inside it and both
halves matter:

> Using prizes, money, gifts or other valuable consideration **as an inducement to secure customers or
> clients to purchase specific property**; however, licensees **may use prizes, money, gifts or other
> valuable consideration for marketing purposes provided they are not contingent or limited to
> individuals making an offer or purchasing a specific property.**

**Forbidden:** an inducement tied to buying a **specific property**. **Permitted:** general marketing use
— a giveaway at an open house, a promotional item — **provided it is not contingent on, or limited to,
people who make an offer or purchase that property.**

**§ 858-312(28)** — soliciting, selling or offering for sale real property **by means of a deceptive
practice.**

**§ 858-312(21)** — paying any part of a fee, commission or other valuable consideration **to any person
not licensed.** Referral arrangements with unlicensed persons are a licence problem.

**§ 858-312(14)** — paying compensation to any person **performing the services of a real estate licensee
who has not first secured a licence.**

### 35.2 The school-side parallel, worth knowing

605:10-5-1(d) forbids an education entity to advertise a course as approved **before approval**, and to
advertise that it is **endorsed, recommended or accredited by the Commission** — though it may say a
course has been approved. The distinction between "approved this course" and "endorses this school" is
the same distinction a licensee must keep between what is verified and what is claimed.

**And this course, accordingly: it is a version 1.0 submission draft, it is not approved, and no Oklahoma
pre-license credit is available for it.**

### 35.3 Auditing an advertisement, line by line

Three questions for every sentence:

1. **Who is speaking?** Is licensee status disclosed in writing — § 858-312(20)?
2. **What is claimed?** Can every factual claim be supported by something written down, and is any of it
   a guarantee of future profit — § 858-312(10) — or a misrepresentation — § 858-312(2)?
3. **Who is described?** If the answer is a person rather than a building, rewrite it.
