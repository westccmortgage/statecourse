## Hours 60–67 · Financing real estate

**Commission area of study covered:** 605:10-3-1(a)(1)(O) Financing Real Estate

> **The information presented in this course should not be used as a substitute for competent legal
> advice.** — 605:10-5-3(i)(6).

## Overview

How a residential purchase is paid for, and the arithmetic a licensee must be able to do. The module
covers underwriting and the qualifying ratios, the note and the security instrument and the clauses that
decide outcomes, the loan programme categories, default and its alternatives, the secondary market, and
the mathematics — loan-to-value, points, interest, amortisation, and the calculations Commission topic
(H) and this module share.

Throughout, one boundary holds: **a licensee explains structures and routes pricing.**

## Learning objectives

1. Describe what underwriting examines and compute both qualifying ratios.
2. Distinguish pre-qualification, pre-approval and commitment and explain the financing contingency.
3. Distinguish the note from the security instrument and state what each does.
4. Name the clauses that decide outcomes and explain each.
5. Distinguish assumption with a release from taking subject to.
6. Describe each loan programme category structurally without recommending one.
7. Distinguish fixed from adjustable and compute an ARM's worst case from its caps.
8. Describe default and the alternatives to foreclosure at the level of principle.
9. Explain the secondary market and what a borrower actually experiences from it.
10. Compute loan-to-value, down payment and the effect of a low appraisal.
11. Compute points, simple interest and a commission split.
12. Read an amortisation schedule and explain why early payments are mostly interest.
13. State what a licensee may and may not say about financing.
14. Identify the § 858-312 grounds that bear on financing conduct.

## Hour 60 — Underwriting

### 60.1 The four Cs

**Capacity** — can the borrower repay from income? The ratios below.

**Credit** — have they repaid before? Score, and the record behind it.

**Capital** — what do they have for down payment, closing costs and reserves, **and where did it come
from?** Sourcing and seasoning of funds are real underwriting steps and they surprise buyers who were
gifted money last week.

**Collateral** — is the property worth enough and acceptable as security? Where the appraisal enters —
Module OK-07.

### 60.2 The ratios

**Housing ratio** = proposed monthly housing payment ÷ gross monthly income. The housing payment is
**PITI** — principal, interest, taxes and insurance — plus mortgage insurance and any association dues.

**Total debt ratio** = housing payment plus all other recurring monthly obligations ÷ gross monthly
income. Car payments, student loans, minimum card payments, court-ordered support.

*Worked.* Gross monthly income $6,800. Proposed PITI $1,564. Car $385, student loan $240, card minimums
$95.

Housing ratio: 1,564 ÷ 6,800 = **23.0%.**
Total obligations: 1,564 + 385 + 240 + 95 = 2,284. Total debt ratio: 2,284 ÷ 6,800 = **33.6%.**

**The thresholds are the lender's and the programme's, not the licensee's.** They differ by programme,
they move, and they yield to compensating factors and automated underwriting decisions no licensee sees.
**A licensee who quotes a qualifying ratio as a rule has stated an underwriting standard as law.**

## Hour 61 — Pre-qualification, pre-approval, commitment

**Pre-qualification** — an estimate from information the borrower stated. Nothing verified.

**Pre-approval** — follows verification and underwriting of the borrower. Stronger, and still conditional.

**Commitment** — the lender's actual undertaking to lend, on conditions.

**Neither pre-qualification nor pre-approval is a commitment.**

**That gap is the entire reason the financing contingency exists.** A buyer who believes a pre-approval is
a promise will not understand why the contingency matters, will resent the seller's anxiety about it, and
will be blindsided if underwriting turns.

**Say it at the beginning, plainly:** *this is what the lender expects today, based on what has been
verified; the commitment comes later; the contingency protects you until it does.*

### 61.1 What can still go wrong after pre-approval

New debt taken on before closing. A change of employment. A credit inquiry. A large unexplained deposit.
A gift that was not documented. The **property** failing — appraisal, condition, insurability, or a
condominium project the lender will not lend on.

**Tell the buyer, in writing, at the beginning:** do not buy a car, do not open credit accounts, do not
change jobs, do not move money around, until after closing. That advice costs nothing and saves
transactions.

### 61.2 The contingency

Four elements — **condition, deadline, method of notice, effect of silence.** Module OK-10 Hour 58.

**The licensee's job is calendar work:** the application deadline, the loan-commitment deadline, the date
the appraisal must be ordered, and the point at which a slipping date must be raised in writing.

## Hour 62 — The note, the security instrument, and the clauses

### 62.1 Two documents, two jobs

The **promissory note** is the borrower's promise to repay — **the debt**. Amount, rate, term, payment,
consequences of default.

The **security instrument** pledges the property as security — **the lien**. Without it the lender has a
promise and no property; without the note, a lien securing nothing.

Two forms exist across the United States. A **mortgage** is a two-party instrument enforced by
**judicial** foreclosure. A **deed of trust** is a three-party instrument — borrower, lender, and a
**trustee** holding a **power of sale** — typically enforced **non-judicially.**

**Oklahoma practice uses the mortgage, and Oklahoma foreclosure is predominantly JUDICIAL** — a court
proceeding rather than a trustee's sale. That is a real structural difference from neighbouring states,
and it changes the timetable.

**This course states no Oklahoma foreclosure timetable, notice requirement, redemption right, deficiency
rule or election-of-remedies provision**, because Oklahoma's foreclosure provisions were not read from
primary text for this project. **The structural fact — judicial foreclosure — is what a licensee carries;
every specific is routed to counsel.**

### 62.2 The clauses that decide outcomes

**Acceleration.** On default, the lender may declare the entire balance immediately due. Without it,
default would mean suing over each missed payment.

**Alienation, or due-on-sale.** On transfer of the property, the lender may call the balance. **This is
why "taking subject to" an existing loan is not a workaround a licensee arranges**, and why **assumption
requires the lender's consent.**

**Prepayment.** Whether early repayment is permitted and on what terms.

**Escrow, or impound.** The lender collects taxes and insurance monthly and pays them when due. **This is
why the payment is PITI**, and why an **escrow analysis can change a payment after closing without the
rate having moved** — warn buyers, because it arrives as a shock otherwise.

**Subordination.** An agreement that a lien takes a lower priority than it otherwise would.

**Release, or partial release.** Releasing part of the security as principal is repaid — used in
subdivision and land development lending.

**Defeasance.** The clause under which the lien is discharged on full repayment.

### 62.3 Assumption, subject to, release

**Assumption with the lender's written release** — the buyer takes over the loan and **the seller is off
the obligation.**

**Assumption without a release** — the buyer pays; **the seller remains liable** for a loan on a house
they no longer own.

**Taking subject to** — the buyer makes payments without assuming the obligation. The seller remains
fully liable and the alienation clause is generally triggered by the transfer.

**A licensee explains these structures and routes the decision to counsel. A licensee does not arrange
them.**

## Hour 63 — Loan programme categories

**Conventional.** Not insured or guaranteed by a government agency. Below a threshold down payment the
lender requires **private mortgage insurance**, which **protects the lender, not the borrower** — buyers
consistently believe otherwise and telling them is a genuine service — and which can generally be removed
once equity conditions are met.

**FHA-insured.** Insured by the Federal Housing Administration. Lower down payment requirements, mortgage
insurance premiums on their own terms, and **property condition standards applied through the
appraisal**, which can affect whether a particular house qualifies at all.

**VA-guaranteed.** For eligible veterans and service members. Typically no down payment, a **funding fee**
in place of monthly mortgage insurance, and its own appraisal and property requirements.

**USDA rural development.** For eligible properties in eligible areas with eligible borrowers. **Relevant
across a great deal of Oklahoma**, and worth knowing whether a given address qualifies before a buyer
falls in love with it.

**State and local assistance programmes** — down payment assistance, first-time buyer and bond
programmes. **This course states no eligibility criterion, income limit or purchase price limit for any
Oklahoma programme**, because none was read from primary source for this project. Routed to the
administering agency and the lender.

**Seller financing**, including the **land contract or contract for deed**, in which the buyer takes
possession and equitable title while the seller retains legal title until the payments are complete.
**This is the arrangement in which a buyer has the least protection**, the consequences of default are a
legal question with real stakes, and **a licensee explains the structure and routes the decision to
counsel.**

### 63.1 The rule that governs the hour

**A licensee explains structures and routes pricing.**

Which programme is cheapest for a particular borrower depends on rate, term, mortgage insurance
treatment, fees, tax position and holding period. A licensee controls none of those and knows some of
them not at all.

**Naming the categories is education. Recommending one is advice a licensee is not licensed to give.**

## Hour 64 — Rate and payment structures

**Fixed rate.** The rate does not change for the life of the loan.

**Adjustable rate.** An **index plus a margin**, adjusting on a schedule, with **caps** on the periodic
adjustment and on the **lifetime** rate.

*Worked.* A 5/1 ARM at 5.75% start, index plus 2.75 margin, caps 2/2/5. First adjustment may rise at most
2 points to **7.75%**; each later adjustment at most 2 points; the **lifetime cap of 5** puts the maximum
possible rate at **10.75%.**

**A buyer considering an ARM needs the worst case, not the teaser — and the worst case is computable from
the caps.** Compute it and put it in writing.

**Interest-only** — payments cover interest only for a period, after which the payment rises sharply.

**Balloon** — a large sum falls due on a stated date. **A buyer who has not diarised that date has a
problem waiting for them.**

**Graduated payment** — payments start low and rise on a schedule.

**Biweekly** — half a payment every two weeks, producing thirteen monthly equivalents a year and shortening
the term.

## Hour 65 — Default, foreclosure, and the alternatives

### 65.1 Default and acceleration

**Default** is failure to perform under the note or the security instrument — most often non-payment, but
also failure to pay taxes, failure to insure, or waste.

**Acceleration** makes the whole balance due, and it is what makes foreclosure practical.

### 65.2 Foreclosure

**Oklahoma foreclosure is predominantly judicial** — a court proceeding culminating in a sheriff's sale.

**This course states no Oklahoma timetable, notice requirement, redemption right or deficiency rule**,
because those provisions were not read from primary text for this project. **Every one of them is routed
to counsel**, and a licensee who states one from memory or from another state's practice will state it
wrongly.

**What a licensee must understand structurally:** a foreclosure extinguishes the interests junior to the
lien being foreclosed and leaves senior ones in place; a purchaser at such a sale takes subject to what
survived; and title after a foreclosure is a title question for the title company and counsel.

### 65.3 The alternatives

**Reinstatement** — curing the default and restoring the loan.

**Forbearance or modification** — agreements with the lender to change the terms.

**Deed in lieu of foreclosure** — conveying voluntarily to the lender. **It does not automatically
eliminate junior liens**, which is why lenders often refuse it.

**Short sale** — the lender agrees to accept less than the balance. It is a lender approval process with
its own timetable and its own tax and credit consequences.

**A licensee working a distressed file identifies the questions and routes them.** The seller's deficiency
exposure, tax position and credit consequences go to **counsel and an accountant** — every one of them,
every time. Section 858-353(A)(1) requires honesty and reasonable skill and care; guessing at any of
these is neither.

## Hour 66 — The secondary market

### 66.1 Primary and secondary

The **primary market** is where loans are made. The **secondary market** is where they are bought and sold
after origination.

It exists because it **returns capital to originators**, who can lend again. Without it, a lender's
capacity would be limited to its own balance sheet, and thirty-year fixed-rate lending at scale would not
exist.

### 66.2 What a borrower actually experiences

**Standardisation.** Originators write to the purchaser's guidelines, which is why underwriting looks
broadly similar across lenders, why documentation requirements feel identical, and **why an unusual
property or an unusual borrower is harder to finance than the numbers alone suggest.**

**Servicing transfer.** The right to collect payments is an asset and is bought and sold. A borrower may
make their first payment to one company and their second to another they never chose. **Tell buyers this
will probably happen and that it does not change their loan terms** — otherwise the notice arrives looking
like a scam.

**Rate transmission.** Mortgage rates track the yields investors demand on mortgage-backed securities more
closely than any single policy rate, which is why mortgage rates sometimes move opposite to the direction
a news story implies. **It is also why a licensee does not forecast them.**

## Hour 67 — Real estate mathematics

### 67.1 The one relationship

> **Part = Whole × Rate**

Rearranged: Rate = Part ÷ Whole. Whole = Part ÷ Rate. **Nearly every commission, interest, appreciation,
ratio and profit problem on any examination is this relationship in a costume.**

### 67.2 Loan-to-value and the low appraisal

**LTV = loan amount ÷ the LESSER of price or appraised value.**

*Worked.* Price $268,000, appraised $268,000, 10 percent down. Down payment 268,000 × 0.10 = **$26,800**;
loan **$241,200**; LTV 241,200 ÷ 268,000 = **90%.**

*Worked, low appraisal.* Same price, appraisal **$256,000**. The lender lends 90 percent **of the lesser
figure**: 256,000 × 0.90 = **$230,400.** The buyer now needs 268,000 − 230,400 = **$37,600** in cash, not
$26,800. **A $12,000 appraisal gap became a $10,800 cash problem.** That is what the appraisal contingency
exists to handle.

### 67.3 Points and interest

**A point is one percent of the LOAN AMOUNT**, not the price. Get that wrong and every points problem is
wrong.

*Worked.* 1.25 points on $241,200: 241,200 × 0.0125 = **$3,015.**

**Simple annual interest = principal × rate.** Monthly = ÷ 12.

*Worked.* $241,200 at 6.5%: annual 241,200 × 0.065 = $15,678. Monthly 15,678 ÷ 12 = **$1,306.50.**

### 67.4 Amortisation

The payment is constant; its **composition** changes every month. Interest is computed on the **current**
balance; the remainder reduces principal; the balance falls; next month's interest is smaller.

*Worked, first three months.* $241,200 at 6.5%, monthly payment $1,524.53.

| Month | Interest | Principal | Ending balance |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 241,200 × .065 ÷ 12 = **1,306.50** | 218.03 | 240,981.97 |
| 2 | 240,981.97 × .065 ÷ 12 = **1,305.32** | 219.21 | 240,762.76 |
| 3 | 240,762.76 × .065 ÷ 12 = **1,304.13** | 220.40 | 240,542.36 |

**Three payments totalling $4,573.59 reduced the balance by $657.64.** That is not a defect; it is what
amortisation is. A buyer who understands it will not be shocked by their first annual statement, and
explaining it is genuinely useful.

### 67.5 Commission

*Worked.* Sale price $268,000, total compensation 6 percent, split evenly between two firms, and the
associate's agreement with their broker is 60/40 in the associate's favour.

Total: 268,000 × 0.06 = **$16,080.** Listing broker: ÷ 2 = **$8,040.** Associate: 8,040 × 0.60 =
**$4,824.** The broker retains $3,216.

**Compensation is negotiable and is set by no board, association, listing service or rule.** And
**§ 858-312(4)**: an associate may accept compensation **only from the broker with whom the associate is
associated.**

### 67.6 Area and per-unit

Rectangle: length × width. Triangle: ½ × base × height. Irregular: divide, compute, add.

**1 acre = 43,560 sq ft. 1 section = 640 acres. 1 mile = 5,280 ft.**

*Worked.* A house of 46 × 28 feet with a 24 × 22 addition: 1,288 + 528 = **1,816 sq ft.** At $152 per
square foot of construction cost: 1,816 × 152 = **$276,032.**

### 67.7 What a licensee may and may not say

**May:** explain what the categories are and how they differ structurally; explain what the ratios
measure; explain the difference between pre-qualification, pre-approval and commitment; insist the buyer
speak to a lender early; explain what a rate lock is and that it expires; compute and explain the
arithmetic above.

**May not:** recommend a programme as cheapest; **compute a payment and give it to the buyer as their
payment**; quote a rate; predict what rates will do; characterise what a lender will approve; or —
**§ 858-312(10)** — guarantee future profits from resale.
