## Overview

How ownership actually moves from one person to another — voluntarily by deed, at death by will or
intestacy, and involuntarily by foreclosure, tax sale, condemnation, adverse possession and the
processes of nature. The module covers the requirements of a valid deed, the deed types and what each
warrants, and the transactions where a licensee most often meets an authority problem: estates,
trusts, entities and powers of attorney.

## Learning objectives

1. Name the requirements of a valid deed and explain why recording is not one of them.
2. Explain delivery and acceptance and identify the moment title passes.
3. Distinguish the deed types by what the grantor warrants and say when each is used.
4. Identify a fiduciary's deed and state what question it raises.
5. Distinguish testate from intestate succession and describe probate's role.
6. State what a licensee must establish before taking a listing from an estate.
7. Name the forms of involuntary alienation and identify which is operating in a fact pattern.
8. Describe adverse possession's elements and route the question correctly.
9. Explain the natural processes that move boundaries along watercourses.
10. Identify a power-of-attorney or entity-authority question and route it.
11. State the deed theft obligation under § 858-364 and route the form.
12. State why a licensee never advises which deed to use or how to take title.

## Hour 25 — The valid deed

### 25.1 What a deed requires

A **deed** is the instrument that conveys an interest in real estate. To be valid it needs:

- a **grantor** with legal capacity;
- a **grantee** identified with certainty;
- **words of conveyance** — the granting clause;
- an **adequate legal description**;
- the **grantor's signature**, and in practice **acknowledgement** before a notary so the instrument may
  be recorded;
- **delivery** by the grantor and **acceptance** by the grantee.

**And note what is NOT required:** the grantee's signature; consideration in any particular amount —
deeds routinely recite nominal consideration; and **recording**, which is not a requirement of validity
at all.

### 25.2 Delivery and acceptance — where title actually passes

**Title passes at delivery and acceptance**, not at signature and not at recording.

**Delivery** requires the grantor's present intent to pass title. A deed signed and locked in a drawer
has not been delivered. A deed handed over with instructions to record it only after the grantor's death
raises a delivery question that ends up in court.

**Acceptance** by the grantee is generally presumed where the conveyance benefits them.

**The consequence:** an unrecorded but delivered deed is **valid between the parties** — and can still
lose to a later interest taken and recorded by someone without notice of it. Which is why the rule is
**record immediately.**

### 25.3 The parts of a deed

Beyond the essentials, a deed customarily contains: the **premises** clause naming the parties and the
consideration; the **granting** clause; the **habendum** clause — "to have and to hold" — describing the
estate conveyed; the **legal description**; any **reservations or exceptions** — and in Oklahoma this is
frequently where **minerals** are reserved; the **covenants**, if any; the **execution** and the
**acknowledgement**.

**Read the reservations and exceptions.** A deed conveying the surface and reserving the minerals says so
there, and a buyer who did not have it explained will not have understood what they bought.

## Hour 26 — Deed types

### 26.1 By what the grantor warrants

**General warranty deed.** The broadest covenants. The grantor warrants title against defects arising at
**any** time, including before the grantor's own ownership. Traditional covenants: seisin, right to
convey, against encumbrances, quiet enjoyment, further assurance, warranty forever. This is the ordinary
residential sale deed.

**Special or limited warranty deed.** The grantor warrants **only against defects arising during the
grantor's own ownership.** Common where the grantor is an entity, a lender, or a fiduciary that cannot
speak to what came before.

**Quit claim deed.** Conveys whatever interest the grantor has — **if any** — and **warrants nothing.**
It is a **clearing** instrument: removing a cloud, correcting a name, releasing a possible interest,
transferring between related parties. It is **not** a sale instrument, and a buyer offered one in an
arm's-length purchase should ask why and take the answer to counsel.

### 26.2 Fiduciary deeds

**Personal representative's deed. Trustee's deed. Guardian's deed. Sheriff's deed.**

Each carries the authority of the proceeding behind it and **warrants little or nothing.** The question
each raises is the same: **does this person actually have authority to convey?** That authority comes
from a court order, letters, a trust instrument or a statute — and it is a **legal determination**
established **before** a listing is taken, not discovered at closing.

### 26.3 The licensee's boundary

**Which deed is used in a particular transaction is a legal decision for counsel and the closing agent.**
A licensee who tells a client which deed to take or give has practised law.

And so is **how to take title** — tenancy in common, joint tenancy, a trust, an entity. That is a legal
and tax question for counsel and the buyer's tax adviser. The licensee's contribution is to raise it
early enough that it is decided deliberately.

## Hour 27 — Transfer at death, and estate sales

### 27.1 Testate and intestate

**Testate** — the owner died leaving a valid will. Real property passes by **devise** to a **devisee**.

**Intestate** — no valid will. Property passes to heirs under the state's descent and distribution
statute.

**This course states no Oklahoma rule of descent and distribution, no will formality and no probate
timetable**, because Oklahoma's probate provisions were not read from primary text for this project.

### 27.2 Probate

**Probate** is the court proceeding that proves the will, appoints a personal representative, gives
notice to creditors, settles claims and authorises distribution.

**Why a licensee cares:** because probate answers **who may sign**, and because **probate timing can
control the closing date.**

### 27.3 What must be established before taking an estate listing

1. **Who has authority to convey**, and what document establishes it. Letters testamentary or of
   administration; a court order; a trustee's certificate where the property is in trust.
2. **Whether court confirmation of the sale is required**, and if so what that adds to the timetable.
3. **Whether all necessary parties will sign** — heirs, devisees, a surviving spouse.
4. **What deed will be used**, which will typically be a fiduciary's deed warranting little.

**Estate sales fail on authority and on timetable far more often than on price.** Building a closing date
around a probate step nobody has confirmed is the avoidable error, and it is the one that happens most.

### 27.4 What a licensee says to the family

That the questions above must be answered by counsel and the title company; that the answers will shape
the timetable; and that the sale should not be marketed with a closing date the probate cannot support.

That is honesty and reasonable skill and care under § 858-353(A)(1), owed to **all parties** — including
the buyer who is being asked to rely on a date.

## Hour 28 — Involuntary alienation

### 28.1 The forms

**Escheat.** To the state where an owner dies with no heirs and no will.

**Eminent domain.** Through condemnation, with just compensation.

**Foreclosure.** Enforcement of a lien — Module OK-11's subject.

**Tax sale.** Enforcement of the tax lien.

**Adverse possession.** A court's determination that long possession has ripened into title.

**Natural processes.** **Accretion** — gradual addition of soil by water action, which the riparian owner
gains. **Erosion** — gradual loss. **Reliction** — land uncovered by permanently receding water.
**Avulsion** — sudden removal of land by water, which traditionally does **not** change the boundary.

### 28.2 Adverse possession

Possession that is **actual, open and notorious, hostile** to the true owner's interest, **exclusive**,
and **continuous for the statutory period** may ripen into title. **Tacking** allows successive
possessors in privity to add their periods together.

**It is established by a court, not asserted by a party.**

**This course states no Oklahoma statutory period**, because Oklahoma's limitations provisions were not
read from primary text for this project.

**What a licensee carries is a practice rule:** a **fence, drive, dock, garden, outbuilding or gate in
the wrong place, in place for a long time**, is routed to counsel and a surveyor. It is never resolved by
asking the neighbours what they think, and it is never described to a buyer as "nothing to worry about."

### 28.3 Tax sale and foreclosure, at the level of principle

Both extinguish the owner's interest through a process the law prescribes, and both may extinguish junior
interests while leaving senior ones. **The specifics — notice, timing, redemption, deficiency, the effect
on junior liens — are entirely statutory**, and **this course states none of them for Oklahoma**, because
Oklahoma's foreclosure and tax provisions were not read from primary text for this project.

A licensee working a distressed file identifies the questions and routes them: the seller's deficiency
exposure, tax consequences and credit consequences go to counsel and an accountant, and the timetable
goes to counsel and the title company.

## Hour 29 — Authority problems and the deed theft notification

### 29.1 The recurring question: who may sign?

Most closing-table failures in transfer are authority failures, and they are all the same question in
different costumes:

- **A life estate.** The life tenant cannot convey the fee; the remainderman must join — Module OK-02.
- **An estate.** Authority comes from the probate proceeding.
- **A trust.** Authority comes from the trust instrument, and the title company will want to see it or a
  certificate of trust.
- **An entity** — corporation, LLC, partnership. Authority comes from the governing documents and usually
  a resolution or consent. **A signature by someone who turns out not to have been authorised does not
  convey.**
- **A power of attorney.** Must be valid, must be broad enough to cover a real estate conveyance, must
  usually be recorded, and **must still be in force** — a power of attorney generally terminates on the
  principal's death, and sometimes on incapacity unless it is durable.
- **Co-owners**, and any spouse whose joinder may be required.

**One question, asked at the listing appointment, catches nearly all of them: "Who else has to sign?"**
Asked then, it is a five-minute conversation. Asked at closing, it is a postponement.

**Every answer is a legal determination for counsel and the title company.** The licensee asks, routes,
and manages the timetable.

### 29.2 Deed theft — 59 O.S. § 858-364

> As part of **each closing** of a transaction, notification of the signs and risks of **deed theft**, as
> defined in Section 858-351 of Title 59 of the Oklahoma Statutes, **shall be required to be given to the
> buyer by a licensed real estate broker. The buyer shall confirm receipt of such notification by
> affixing his or her signature to such notification.** The Oklahoma Real Estate Commission shall
> promulgate rules to enact the provisions of this section.

**Deed theft** is defined at § 858-351(3) as:

- intentionally **altering, falsifying, forging or misrepresenting a document relating to real property**
  with intent to deceive, defraud, or unlawfully transfer or encumber the ownership rights of the owner;
- **misrepresenting oneself as the owner or the owner's authorised representative** in order to obtain
  ownership or possession; or
- with intent to defraud, **taking, obtaining, stealing or transferring title or ownership** by fraud,
  forgery, larceny or any other fraudulent or deceptive practice.

**The obligation is on the broker, at every closing, and the buyer signs.**

**This course states no wording or format for the notification**, because the Commission's implementing
rules and the form itself were not located in the publication read for this project. **Obtain the current
form from the Commission** and confirm the rules have issued before relying on any version of it.

**§ 858-307.2(F)** separately requires licensees to complete Commission-specified continuing education on
deed theft **on or before 1 November 2028.**

### 29.3 Why this sits in the transfer module

Because deed theft is a **transfer** crime. It attacks the same machinery this module has described —
delivery, acknowledgement, recording, authority to sign — and the defences against it are the same
disciplines: verify identity, verify authority, use the title company, and treat an unusual instruction
about how a conveyance is to be executed as a reason to slow down rather than a reason to hurry.

A licensee who has understood Hours 25 through 29 already knows what the notification is warning about.
