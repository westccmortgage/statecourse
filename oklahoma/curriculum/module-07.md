## Overview

What value is, how it is estimated, and where a licensee's competence ends and an appraiser's begins.
The module works through the principles of value, the four tests of highest and best use, and all three
approaches, with a fully worked comparison grid. It closes on the boundary, which in Oklahoma carries a
specific licence consequence: § 858-312(13) makes contingent appraisal compensation and undisclosed
interest a ground for suspension or revocation.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish value, price and cost and explain why an owner's improvement cost is not value.
2. Name the principles of value and apply substitution, contribution, conformity and anticipation.
3. Apply the four tests of highest and best use in the correct order.
4. Select and reject comparables on stated grounds.
5. Build a comparison grid with adjustments in the correct direction.
6. Reconcile to a range by weighting evidence rather than averaging.
7. Describe the cost approach and compute a value from it.
8. Describe the income approach and compute net operating income and value.
9. State the boundary between a licensee's market analysis and an appraisal.
10. State what § 858-312(13) forbids about appraisal compensation and interest.
11. Handle a low appraisal correctly, including what may and may not be sent to an appraiser.
12. Respond accurately to an automated valuation, an old appraisal and a neighbour's figure.

## Hour 36 — Value, price, cost, and the principles

### 36.1 Three words

**Value** is worth to a typical buyer under stated conditions — most often **market value**: the most
probable price a property should bring in a competitive and open market under conditions requisite to a
fair sale, buyer and seller each acting prudently and knowledgeably, neither under undue duress.

**Price** is what was actually paid in a particular transaction. A historical fact, possibly influenced
by a motivated seller, an uninformed buyer, an unusual concession, or a relationship between the parties.

**Cost** is what it took to produce the improvements.

**Cost does not become value.** The owner who spent $92,000 on a pool, a shop building and a kitchen is
frequently astonished by the appraisal, and the explanation is the **principle of contribution**: an
improvement adds what it adds **to the value of the whole**, not what it cost.

### 36.2 The principles

**Supply and demand.** Value rises when demand exceeds supply.

**Substitution.** A buyer will pay no more than the cost of acquiring an equally desirable substitute.
**This is the foundation of all three approaches.**

**Contribution.** As above, and measured by the change in the value of the whole.

**Conformity.** Value is maximised where a property conforms reasonably to those around it. Corollaries:
**progression** — a modest house gains from better neighbours; **regression** — an over-improved house
loses to worse ones. The most over-built house on the street is the standard regression case and the
standard listing where the owner cannot understand the price.

**Anticipation.** Value reflects expected future benefits, which is why a rumoured interchange moves
prices before it is built.

**Change.** Nothing is static.

**Increasing and diminishing returns.** Additional investment adds value up to a point, then does not.

**Plottage** — assembling adjacent parcels to produce a whole worth more than the sum of the parts, the
increment being **assemblage** value. **Externalities** — influences from outside the property.

### 36.3 The four forces

**Social** — demographics, household composition, preferences. **Economic** — employment, income, rates,
credit. **Governmental** — zoning, codes, taxation, public investment. **Environmental** — physical
characteristics, topography, transport, location.

**In several Oklahoma markets the economic force carries a distinct component** — energy sector
employment and income, which can move a local market faster than any other single factor. Naming it is
analysis. Predicting it is not, and Module OK-01 Hour 4 explains why.

## Hour 37 — Highest and best use, and selecting comparables

### 37.1 Highest and best use

The use that is:

1. **Legally permissible**,
2. **Physically possible**,
3. **Financially feasible**, and
4. **Maximally productive**.

**All four, in that order.** A use that is not legally permissible never reaches the profitability
question — which is also where a licensee stops, because whether a use is permissible is the
municipality's determination.

Note that highest and best use is analysed **as though vacant** and **as improved**, and the two can
differ. Where the value as vacant exceeds the value as improved, the improvement has become a
**demolition candidate** — which happens in changing commercial corridors and occasionally on a large
residential lot.

### 37.2 Selecting comparables

**Use:** recent **closed** sales; similar in size, age, style, condition and location; in the same market
area and ideally the same submarket; **arm's-length** transactions between unrelated parties.

**Do not use, or use only with explanation:** current **listings** — asking prices, which prove only what
someone hoped for; **pending** sales, whose prices are not yet known; **distressed** sales, unless the
subject is itself distressed; and sales between family members or otherwise not arm's-length.

**The quality test:** the **fewer and smaller** the adjustments a comparable needs, the better it is. A
comparable requiring $60,000 of gross adjustment is telling you it is not really comparable.

**And an Oklahoma-specific selection question:** does the comparable convey the same mineral interest as
the subject? A surface-only sale and a sale conveying minerals are not comparable without an adjustment
nobody in this room is qualified to make. Where the question arises, it is named in the analysis and
routed — not silently ignored.

## Hour 38 — The sales comparison approach: a worked grid

### 38.1 The rule that governs every grid

**Adjust the COMPARABLE, never the subject.** The subject is what is being valued; it has no price yet
and there is nothing on it to adjust.

- Comparable **superior** to the subject → adjust it **DOWN**.
- Comparable **inferior** to the subject → adjust it **UP**.

Getting the direction wrong moves the answer **twice** as far as leaving the adjustment out. **Check
every line before you present.**

### 38.2 The subject

*1,940 sq ft two-storey, 4 bed, 2.5 bath, attached 2-car garage, unfinished storm shelter, 0.28 acre,
built 2004, original kitchen.*

### 38.3 The grid

| | Comp A | Comp B | Comp C | Comp D |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Sale price | $268,000 | $241,000 | $294,500 | $259,000 |
| Sold | 2 months ago | 6 months ago | 1 month ago | 9 months ago |
| Market conditions +0.35%/mo | +$1,876 | +$5,061 | +$1,031 | +$8,159 |
| Living area | 2,010 (+70) | 1,830 (−110) | 2,120 (+180) | 1,905 (−35) |
| Area adj. at $54/sq ft | −$3,780 | +$5,940 | −$9,720 | +$1,890 |
| Bathrooms | 2.5 | 2 (−0.5) | 3.5 (+1) | 2.5 |
| Bath adj. at $6,500 each | $0 | +$3,250 | −$6,500 | $0 |
| Storm shelter | unfinished | none | finished safe room | unfinished |
| Shelter adj. | $0 | +$4,500 | −$5,500 | $0 |
| Kitchen | original | original | renovated | renovated |
| Kitchen adj. | $0 | $0 | −$19,000 | −$19,000 |
| **Gross adjustment** | **$5,656** | **$18,751** | **$41,751** | **$29,049** |
| **Net adjustment** | **−$1,904** | **+$18,751** | **−$39,689** | **−$8,951** |
| **Adjusted price** | **$266,096** | **$259,751** | **$254,811** | **$250,049** |

### 38.4 Check the directions, line by line

- **Comp A** is larger — superior — so the area adjustment is **negative**. Correct.
- **Comp B** is smaller, has fewer baths and no shelter — inferior on all three — so all three are
  **positive**. Correct.
- **Comp C** is larger, has more baths, a finished safe room and a renovated kitchen — superior on all
  four — so all four are **negative**. Correct.
- **Comp D** is slightly smaller — inferior, so **positive** — and has a renovated kitchen — superior, so
  **negative**. Correct.

**Market conditions are positive on every comparable**, because the market has risen since each sale and
each would sell for more today. Time adjustments are applied before the physical adjustments.

*(The storm shelter line is Oklahoma-specific and real: an in-ground shelter or an engineered safe room
is a recognised feature in this market. The dollar amounts in this grid are illustrative and are not
Oklahoma market data.)*

## Hour 39 — Reconciliation

### 39.1 Reconciliation is not averaging

The four adjusted figures: **$266,096 · $259,751 · $254,811 · $250,049.**

The arithmetic mean is **$257,677.** That number tells you **nothing about which evidence was
strongest**, which is exactly why reconciliation is judgment.

### 39.2 Weighting the evidence

- **Comp A** — sold **2 months ago**, needed only **$5,656 gross**. **Most reliable by a distance.**
- **Comp B** — 6 months, $18,751 gross. Moderate.
- **Comp D** — **9 months**, $29,049 gross. Weak on both counts.
- **Comp C** — **$41,751 gross**, adjusted on four separate characteristics. **Least reliable.**

**A defensible conclusion:** the weight of the evidence supports roughly **$256,000 to $266,000**, with
Comp A arguing for the upper part of the range.

### 39.3 And say what the grid actually shows

The three heavily adjusted comparables cluster between $250,000 and $260,000 while the single most
reliable one lands at $266,000 — and the largest single adjustment in the whole grid is the **kitchen**.

**Tell the seller that.** It is a real fact about their choices: renovate, price into the cluster, or
price at the top of the range and expect the market to test it.

**A market analysis that hides the disagreement between its own comparables is not evidence. It is
decoration.**

### 39.4 The four rules for presenting one

1. **In writing**, with the comparables and the adjustments shown.
2. **As a range**, not a single figure.
3. **The price is the owner's decision.**
4. **Labelled for what it is** — a licensee's market analysis, not an appraisal.

## Hour 40 — The cost approach and the income approach

### 40.1 The cost approach

**Land value + (reproduction or replacement cost of improvements − accrued depreciation) = value.**

**Reproduction cost** builds an exact replica. **Replacement cost** builds a structure of equal utility
with current materials and methods, and is the more common basis.

**Accrued depreciation** has three sources:

- **Physical deterioration** — wear, age, deferred maintenance. Curable or incurable.
- **Functional obsolescence** — a defect in the design itself. A bedroom reachable only through another
  bedroom; one bathroom in a four-bedroom house. Curable or incurable.
- **External obsolescence** — caused by something **outside** the boundaries. A new highway, a closed
  plant, a declining area. **Always incurable**, because the owner cannot fix it.

*Worked.* Land value $42,000. Replacement cost of a 1,940 sq ft house at $158/sq ft = **$306,520**.
Accrued depreciation: physical $52,000, functional $14,000, external $0. Value = 42,000 + (306,520 −
66,000) = **$282,520.**

The cost approach **governs new construction and special-purpose property** where comparables are thin —
a church, a school, a fire station, a grain facility. It is **weakest on older buildings**, because
estimating accrued depreciation on a seventy-year-old house is largely judgment.

### 40.2 The income approach

**Potential gross income**, less **vacancy and collection loss** → **effective gross income**, less
**operating expenses** → **net operating income**. Then **NOI ÷ capitalisation rate = value.**

*Worked.* A twelve-unit building. Potential gross income $129,600. Vacancy and collection loss 7% =
$9,072. **Effective gross income $120,528.** Operating expenses $51,800. **NOI = $68,728.** At a
capitalisation rate of 8.0%: 68,728 ÷ 0.08 = **$859,100.**

**Debt service and depreciation are NOT operating expenses.** They sit below the NOI line. Two identical
buildings with different financing have the **same NOI** and different cash flow, which is why NOI is the
figure a buyer compares.

**The gross rent multiplier** — sale price ÷ gross rent — is a screening device, not a valuation. It
ignores expenses entirely.

### 40.3 Three checks on any pro forma

1. **Is there a replacement reserve line?** Roof, mechanicals, parking. No reserve understates expenses,
   overstates NOI, and overstates value **by the reserve divided by the cap rate** — a large number.
2. **Is the vacancy assumption real?** 2 percent in a market running at 8 is a wish.
3. **Are the rents actual or projected?** "Market rents after renovation" is a plan, not income.

## Hour 41 — The appraisal process and the boundary

### 41.1 The process

Define the problem — property, interest, purpose, date, definition of value. Determine the scope of
work. Collect and analyse general and specific data. Analyse highest and best use. Estimate land value.
Apply the applicable approaches. **Reconcile.** Report.

### 41.2 The boundary

**An appraisal is an opinion of value produced by a licensed or certified appraiser under that person's
own licence and professional standards. A salesperson is not an appraiser and does not perform
appraisals.**

**A licensee's market analysis** uses the same comparison logic and is **not** an appraisal. It must not
be labelled or described as one, and must not be presented so that a client can believe it is one.

**This course states no Oklahoma appraiser licensing requirement**, because Oklahoma's appraiser
licensing provisions were not read from primary text for this project. What **was** read is the licence
consequence in the next paragraph.

### 41.3 § 858-312(13) — read it before you accept any valuation work

It is a ground for suspension or revocation to:

> **Accept employment or compensation for appraising real estate contingent upon the reporting of a
> predetermined value**, or **issue any appraisal report on real estate in which the licensee has an
> interest unless the licensee's interest is disclosed in the report.** **All appraisals shall be in
> compliance with the Oklahoma real estate appraisal law**, and the person performing the appraisal or
> report **shall disclose in writing to the employer whether the person performing the appraisal or
> report is licensed or certified by the Oklahoma Real Estate Appraiser Board.**

Four separate obligations:

1. **No compensation contingent on reaching a predetermined value.** Ever.
2. **Disclose your interest** in the property in any appraisal report you issue.
3. **Comply with the Oklahoma real estate appraisal law.**
4. **Disclose in writing to whoever engaged you whether you are licensed or certified by the Oklahoma
   Real Estate Appraiser Board.**

**That fourth one applies to a real estate licensee producing a valuation-type report.** If you are asked
for a "broker price opinion," a "valuation report," or anything a reader might treat as an appraisal, the
disclosure obligation in (13) is live.

### 41.4 The low appraisal

The most common appraisal event in residential practice.

**What happened:** the lender lends on the **lesser of price or appraised value**, so a gap has opened.
The buyer covers it in cash, or the price moves, or another route is found, or the contract ends
according to whatever the appraisal contingency says.

**What the licensee does:**

- **Read the appraisal contingency and its deadline first**, before saying anything to anyone.
- **Review the report for factual errors** — wrong square footage, wrong bedroom count, a comparable that
  is not comparable, a missed permit, a mineral conveyance difference. Factual errors are the legitimate
  basis for a **reconsideration of value**, submitted **through the lender.**
- **Present the options neutrally** and route the decision to the party.
- **Do not attempt to influence the appraiser.** Pressure on an appraiser to reach a number is misconduct,
  it engages appraiser independence rules in a federally related transaction, and § 858-312(13) shows
  what Oklahoma thinks of predetermined values.

**What you may properly provide:** **factual information** — a list of improvements with dates and costs,
a survey, a plat, the seller's disclosure, comparable sales you believe relevant. **Facts, yes. A target,
no.**

### 41.5 The three figures a seller will show you

**An automated online valuation.** A model's output from public and listing data. It has never been inside
the house and cannot see condition or finish. **Evidence to weigh, not a valuation.** Do not adopt it; do
not dismiss it; put your own written analysis beside it.

**An old appraisal.** A 2019 appraisal is a 2019 opinion, for a purpose that may not be this one, on a
property that may since have changed.

**A neighbour's figure.** Ask what it is based on. It is almost always somebody's **list price**, which is
an asking price and evidence of nothing.

**And under no circumstances confirm in writing a figure you did not derive.** That is not honesty and
reasonable skill and care under § 858-353(A)(1), and it is how a licensee ends up defending a number they
never computed.
