> **The information presented in this course should not be used as a substitute for competent legal
> advice.** — 605:10-5-3(i)(6).

## Overview

What happens between contract and keys, who does each part of it, and what a licensee's actual role is —
which is narrower and more useful than new licensees expect. The module covers the closing sequence, the
settlement statement, the walkthrough, federal disclosure timing, and the two Oklahoma obligations that
attach at or before closing: the § 858-353(A)(3) accounting duty and the § 858-364 deed theft
notification.

## Learning objectives

1. Describe the closing sequence in order and identify who performs each step.
2. State what a licensee does at closing and what they must not do.
3. Read a settlement statement against the contract and identify a discrepancy.
4. Distinguish a debit from a credit on each side of a settlement statement.
5. Explain the purpose of the final walkthrough and what it is not.
6. State what RESPA section 8 and section 9 prohibit.
7. State the TRID three-business-day rule and the licensee's part in protecting it.
8. Describe what happens after closing — recording, funding, disbursement.
9. State the § 858-364 deed theft notification obligation and route its form.
10. Apply § 858-353(A)(3) and § 858-312(6) to post-closing accounting.
11. Identify what can delay a closing and raise it in time.
12. Explain what a licensee says when a closing will not happen on time.

## Hour 68 — The closing sequence

### 68.1 What closing is

The simultaneous exchange in which the buyer's funds and the seller's deed change hands and the
instruments are recorded. In Oklahoma practice it is customarily conducted by a **title company or closing
agent**, and it may be conducted with the parties present or in escrow.

### 68.2 The steps, in order

1. **Title work ordered and examined.** The abstract brought current and examined, or the title commitment
   issued. Module OK-04.
2. **Schedule B-I requirements cleared** — payoffs and releases, corrective instruments, evidence of
   authority, probate, judgments.
3. **Buyer's inspections completed** and any repair agreement reached, within the contingency deadlines.
4. **Appraisal completed** and any valuation issue resolved.
5. **Loan underwriting completed** and a clear-to-close issued.
6. **Closing Disclosure delivered** — and the three-business-day clock starts. Hour 71.
7. **Settlement statement prepared** and reviewed against the contract.
8. **Final walkthrough** by the buyer.
9. **Signing.**
10. **Funding.**
11. **Recording** of the deed and the security instrument.
12. **Disbursement**, including payoff of the seller's loans and payment of compensation.

**Note the order of 10, 11 and 12.** Money does not become the seller's until it is disbursed, and title
is not protected until it is recorded.

### 68.3 Who does what

**The closing agent or title company** prepares the settlement statement, handles funds, records the
instruments and issues the policy. **The lender** funds and sets its own conditions. **Counsel** advises
on legal questions and drafts anything needing drafting. **The abstractor** brings the abstract current
where one is used.

**The licensee does none of those things.**

### 68.4 What the licensee actually does

- **Makes sure everyone has what they need before the day.**
- **Reviews the settlement statement against the contract** for the items the contract allocated — the
  prorations, the credits, the agreed repairs, the compensation — and **raises any discrepancy before
  signing.**
- **Attends**, where the parties want them there.
- **Declines to answer questions that belong to the closing agent, the lender or counsel.**

That last one is the discipline most often broken. A buyer at a closing table asking "what does this
paragraph mean" is asking the closing agent or counsel, not you.

## Hour 69 — The settlement statement

### 69.1 Debits and credits

A **credit** to a party is money in their favour. A **debit** is money against them.

**Buyer's side.** Debits: the purchase price, the buyer's closing costs, prepaid items, escrow deposits,
and the buyer's share of anything the seller prepaid. Credits: the loan amount, the earnest money already
deposited, seller concessions, and prorated items paid in arrears that the buyer will pay later. **The
difference is cash to close.**

**Seller's side.** Credits: the sale price, and prorated items the seller prepaid. Debits: loan payoffs
with per-diem interest, brokerage service compensation, the seller's closing costs, agreed credits to the
buyer, and prorated items paid in arrears. **The difference is net proceeds.**

**Every prorated item appears twice** — a credit to one party and a debit to the other — and the two must
match.

### 69.2 What to check, in order

1. **The parties and the property.** Names spelled as they will be on the deed; the legal description
   matching the contract and the commitment.
2. **The price.**
3. **The earnest money**, credited to the buyer in the amount actually deposited.
4. **Every prorated item**: the annual figure, the basis, the closing-day convention, the day count, and
   the direction. Module OK-08 Hour 43.
5. **Every credit the contract created** — repair credits, closing-cost concessions.
6. **The payoff**, and its good-through date against the closing date.
7. **The compensation**, against the brokerage agreement.
8. **The totals.**

**Do this before the parties sit down**, not while they are signing.

### 69.3 When something is wrong

Raise it with the closing agent, in writing where there is time, **before signing.** A discrepancy caught
before signing is a correction. The same discrepancy caught after is a dispute.

**And do not fix it yourself.** The settlement statement is the closing agent's document.

## Hour 70 — The walkthrough, and what delays closings

### 70.1 The final walkthrough

**What it is:** a confirmation that the property is in the agreed condition, that agreed repairs were
made, that the included items are still there, and that the excluded items are gone.

**What it is not:** an inspection. Not a second chance to discover defects. Not a renegotiation.

**Do it as close to closing as the contract allows** — a walkthrough three days early misses what happens
in the last three days, which is when the seller moves out.

**What buyers actually find:** items that were supposed to convey and are gone; damage from moving;
utilities shut off; agreed repairs done badly or not at all; a garage full of things nobody removed.

**What a licensee does with a problem:** document it — photographs, in writing, immediately — tell the
other side's broker and the closing agent at once, and route the remedy to the contract and, if it is
disputed, to counsel. **Do not agree on the buyer's behalf to a holdback, a credit or a repair
arrangement.** Those are the parties' decisions and usually the closing agent's mechanics.

### 70.2 What delays closings

In rough order of frequency:

- **Title requirements nobody started** — a release, an heirship affidavit, a judgment to clear. Module
  OK-04.
- **Underwriting conditions** — a document the borrower has not produced, a deposit not sourced, an
  employment verification.
- **The appraisal** — late, or low. Module OK-07.
- **Repairs** — agreed late, scheduled late, done late, or done by someone whose invoice arrives after
  closing.
- **Survey**, ordered late.
- **Insurance**, not bound — which in parts of Oklahoma can be a real problem rather than a formality,
  and is worth starting early.
- **A TRID redisclosure**, triggered by a late change. Hour 71.
- **Probate or entity authority**, unresolved. Module OK-05.
- **The other transaction**, where a party's purchase depends on their own sale closing first.

### 70.3 What a licensee says when a closing will slip

**Immediately, in writing, to the party they serve** — § 858-353(B)(2), keep the party informed — with:
what the cause is; who is working on it; what the realistic new date is; and what the contract says about
the consequences, routed to counsel where it matters.

**What a licensee does not do is wait and hope.** A closing that slips is a problem. A closing that slips
and nobody was told is a complaint.

## Hour 71 — RESPA, TRID, and the licensee's part

### 71.1 RESPA

The **Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act** governs settlement services on most federally related
residential mortgage loans. Two prohibitions matter directly:

**Section 8** prohibits **kickbacks and unearned fees** for the referral of settlement service business. A
payment, a thing of value, or a fee for a service not actually performed, in exchange for referrals, is
prohibited. **This is the provision that ends careers**, and it does not require anyone to have intended
anything corrupt.

**Section 9** prohibits a **seller from requiring the buyer to use a particular title company** as a
condition of sale.

RESPA also requires disclosure of **affiliated business arrangements** — where the licensee or the firm
has an ownership interest in a settlement service provider they refer business to. The interest is
disclosed in writing, and the consumer is told they are not required to use the affiliate.

**The Oklahoma parallel.** § 858-312(21) makes it a ground to pay any part of a fee, commission or other
valuable consideration **to any person not licensed**, and § 858-312(14) to pay compensation to any person
performing the services of a licensee who has not secured a licence. **Referral arrangements are a place
where a licensee can breach federal law and state licence law with the same act.**

**This course states no dollar threshold, fee tolerance or exemption under RESPA**, because neither the
statute nor Regulation X was read from primary text for this project.

### 71.2 TRID

The **TILA-RESPA Integrated Disclosure** rule requires:

- the **Loan Estimate**, delivered within **three business days of application**; and
- the **Closing Disclosure**, which the consumer must receive **at least three business days before
  consummation.**

**Certain changes restart that three-day period.**

### 71.3 The licensee's part in TRID: protect the timeline

**Late-breaking changes move closing dates.** A repair credit agreed two days before closing. A change in
the loan amount. A concession added at the last minute.

**Getting changes decided early and telling the lender promptly is a real service and costs nothing.**
When a change is unavoidable late, tell the party in writing what it may do to the date — before they
plan a move around it.

## Hour 72 — At and after closing

### 72.1 Recording

The deed and the security instrument are recorded, giving **constructive notice to the world.** Recording
does not transfer title — delivery and acceptance did that — **but the entire risk lives in the interval
before it**, which is why it happens immediately.

### 72.2 Disbursement and accounting

Funds are disbursed: payoffs, prorations, costs, credits, and compensation.

**§ 858-353(A)(3)** — a duty to **all parties**, mandatory and unwaivable — requires the broker to
**timely account for all money and property received.** That obligation does not end at closing; it covers
anything still held.

**§ 858-312(6)** makes **failing, within a reasonable time, to account for or to remit any monies,
documents, or other property coming into possession of the licensee which belong to others** a ground for
suspension or revocation.

**And § 858-358** — after termination, expiration or completion, the two surviving duties are **to account
for all monies and property relating to the transaction** and **to keep confidential all confidential
information received.** Closing does not release you from either.

### 72.3 The deed theft notification — § 858-364

> **As part of each closing of a transaction**, notification of the signs and risks of **deed theft**, as
> defined in Section 858-351 of Title 59 of the Oklahoma Statutes, **shall be required to be given to the
> buyer by a licensed real estate broker. The buyer shall confirm receipt of such notification by affixing
> his or her signature to such notification.** The Oklahoma Real Estate Commission shall promulgate rules
> to enact the provisions of this section.

**Every closing. To the buyer. By a licensed broker. Signed by the buyer.**

**Deed theft** is defined at § 858-351(3) as intentionally **altering, falsifying, forging or
misrepresenting a document relating to real property** with intent to deceive, defraud or unlawfully
transfer or encumber ownership rights; **misrepresenting oneself as the owner or the owner's authorised
representative** to obtain ownership or possession; or, with intent to defraud, **taking, obtaining,
stealing or transferring title or ownership** by fraud, forgery, larceny or any other fraudulent or
deceptive practice.

**This course states no wording or format for the notification**, because the Commission's implementing
rules and the form itself were not located in the publication read for this project. **Obtain the current
form from the Commission**, and confirm the rules have issued before relying on any version of it.

**§ 858-307.2(F)** separately requires licensees to complete Commission-specified continuing education on
deed theft **on or before 1 November 2028.**

### 72.4 The file after closing

**Keep it.** Everything: the agreement, the disclosures, the offers, the notices, the settlement
statement, the correspondence, the record of what was presented and when.

Two reasons. **§ 858-312(26)** makes **failure to provide an adequate written response to the Commission
within fifteen (15) days** after written notice of a complaint or investigation a ground for suspension or
revocation — and you cannot respond adequately from memory. And the Commission **may on its own motion,
and SHALL upon written complaint filed by any person**, investigate the business transactions of any
licensee — § 858-312.

**This course states no Oklahoma licensee record retention period**, because that provision was not read
from primary text for this project. *(605:10-5-1(i)(2)'s five-year retention applies to education
entities' enrolment records and roll sheets, not to a licensee's transaction files, and the two must not
be confused.)* **Read the retention rule and your broker's written policy.**

### 72.5 What closing feels like when it is done properly

Nothing happens. Everyone signs, the funds move, the deed records, and the parties leave with what the
contract said they would get.

**That outcome is produced weeks earlier**, by the licensee who ordered title work in week one, calendared
every deadline the day the contract was accepted, read Schedule B-I the day it arrived, checked the
settlement statement against the contract before anyone sat down, and told the party in writing every time
something moved.

## Supplemental source review — 2026-09-06

This required reading clarifies the existing topics; no additional clock-hour credit or regulator approval is claimed.

### closing

Related module 12, §§71.2–71.3. The Closing Disclosure must be distinguished from an earlier estimate
and reconciled to the actual contract, lender instructions and settlement figures. Under Regulation
Z, 12 CFR 1026.19(f)(2)(ii), a new three-business-day waiting period is triggered by an inaccurate APR,
a loan-product change, or addition of a prepayment penalty. Other corrections can require a corrected
disclosure without automatically restarting that period. The lender determines the applicable APR
tolerance and timing; do not substitute the phrase "every correction restarts the clock."

In a settlement worksheet, a seller credit reduces buyer cash needed and seller proceeds. Recording
it twice understates both amounts. Reconcile credits, deposits, payoff figures and prorations against
their source; do not fix an imbalance by inventing a fee. Possession follows the agreement, not merely
the delivery of keys or a signature. Under 59 O.S. 858-364 a broker gives the required deed-theft notice
at closing and obtains the buyer's signed acknowledgment. Account and confidentiality duties can
survive termination under 858-358; closing does not erase them.

Sources: [Regulation Z, 12 CFR 1026.19](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/19/); [OREC License Code and Rules, as of November 1, 2025](https://oklahoma.gov/content/dam/ok/en/orec/documents/meetings/Nov%201%202025%20Oklahoma%20Real%20Estate%20Commission%20Code%20and%20Rules%20Book.pdf).

### records

Related module 12, §72.4. OAC 605:10-13-1(l) requires transaction records for at least five years from
consummation or termination, and trust records for five years from disbursal. The different triggering
events matter. Paragraph (m) addresses original trust documents, later retention of qualifying copies
and record accessibility. Five years is a minimum, not permission to destroy material subject to a
longer legal hold. Preserve legible, retrievable files and audit trails rather than merely an empty
folder. Section 858-312(26) addresses an adequate written response to a Commission request within
fifteen days; acknowledgment alone is not necessarily adequate.

Sources: [OREC License Code and Rules, as of November 1, 2025](https://oklahoma.gov/content/dam/ok/en/orec/documents/meetings/Nov%201%202025%20Oklahoma%20Real%20Estate%20Commission%20Code%20and%20Rules%20Book.pdf).
