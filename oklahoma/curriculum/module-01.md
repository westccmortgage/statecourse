## Overview

*(605:10-5-3(i)(2) requires an overview statement for each course. This is Module OK-01's.)*

This module establishes what real estate is as a legal object and how the market for it behaves. It
begins with the vocabulary — land, real estate, real property, the bundle of rights — because every
later module depends on it, and ends with the economics that decide why a property is worth what it
is worth in a particular Oklahoma market in a particular year. It also sets out what this course is
and where it sits: **the Basic Course of Real Estate is Part I of a two-part series**, and 90 hours
does not complete a student's education.

## Learning objectives

## Hour 1 — Land, real estate, and real property

### 1.1 Three words that are not synonyms

**Land** is the surface of the earth, the space above it, and the subsurface beneath it. Air rights are
severable and real; a developer may buy the right to build above a corridor without buying the
corridor. **Subsurface rights are severable too, and in Oklahoma that is not a footnote.** Mineral,
oil and gas rights are routinely severed from surface ownership across the state, and a great deal of
Oklahoma land is sold by an owner who does not own what is under it.

**Real estate** is land plus everything permanently attached, by nature or by human effort — trees,
water, buildings, fences, in-ground pools, the wiring inside the walls.

**Real property** is real estate plus **the bundle of legal rights** attaching to it. This is the term
that matters, because it is the only one that describes what is actually sold. A buyer is not
purchasing bricks; they are purchasing a set of enforceable rights against the rest of the world.

### 1.2 The bundle of rights

Five rights, and the metaphor is a bundle of sticks precisely because sticks come out one at a time:

- **Possession** — to occupy and hold.
- **Use or enjoyment** — to use the property in any lawful manner.
- **Exclusion** — to keep others out.
- **Disposition** — to transfer the interest, by sale, gift, will or otherwise.
- **Encumbrance** — to pledge it as security, or to grant lesser interests in it.

Every stick may be sold, leased, mortgaged, granted or reserved separately. A tenant holds possession,
use and exclusion for a term while the landlord keeps disposition. A lender holds a stick that becomes
decisive only on default. **An Oklahoma seller may convey the surface and reserve the minerals** — and
that is the single most common bundle question a new Oklahoma licensee will meet.

### 1.3 The mineral question, stated once and correctly

Because it comes up immediately and because getting it wrong is expensive:

- **Surface and mineral estates can be, and frequently are, separately owned in Oklahoma.**
- A deed conveys what it says it conveys. **Whether minerals are included is a title question**, and it
  is answered by the title examination and by counsel, not by a licensee, not by the seller's belief,
  and not by what the last listing said.
- **A licensee never states what mineral interest a property carries.** The correct professional
  handling is to raise the question at listing, get it into the title work early, and tell a buyer to
  have it answered by counsel before their contingencies expire.

**This course states no Oklahoma rule of mineral title**, because Oklahoma's oil and gas and title
provisions were not read from primary text for this project. What a licensee carries is the
recognition and the routing, which is enough to keep them out of trouble and to serve the client.

### 1.4 Personal property, and the line between

**Personal property** — *chattels* — is everything that is not real property. It is movable, transfers
by **bill of sale**, and is not conveyed by a real estate contract unless the contract says so.

Two events cross the line. **Annexation** turns personal property into real property by attaching it —
the subject of Hour 2. **Severance** turns real property into personal property by detaching it:
standing timber is real estate, cut timber is personal property; growing crops are generally real
estate, harvested crops are personal property.

## Hour 2 — Fixtures, trade fixtures, and what a licensee actually does

### 2.1 The test

A **fixture** is personal property attached to real estate so as to have become part of it. Once it is
a fixture it conveys unless expressly excluded, and the buyer is entitled to find it there at closing.

Whether an item has become a fixture is decided by **the intention of the party who attached it, judged
objectively** — not by what that party privately meant, but by what the circumstances show. Courts read
intention through:

- **Method of annexation.** How firmly attached, and what damage would removal do?
- **Adaptation to the real estate.** Was it made or fitted for this particular property? Custom storm
  windows cut for these openings are adapted; generic screens stacked in the garage are not.
- **Relationship of the parties.** Tenant and owner are treated differently, because expectations differ.
- **Agreement.** A written agreement settles the question, which is why the absence of one leaves it to
  be argued.

### 2.2 Trade fixtures

A **trade fixture** is an item a tenant attaches for the conduct of their business — a walk-in cooler,
a hydraulic lift, shelving fitted to a shop floor. Trade fixtures **remain the tenant's personal
property and may be removed before the term ends**, with the tenant repairing damage from removal.

**The right expires with the term.** Property left behind after the lease ends generally becomes the
landlord's by **accession**. A departing business that abandons its equipment has usually made a gift
of it.

### 2.3 Three patterns, all preventable at listing

**The item everyone assumes.** The seller's grandmother's chandelier, wired in. It is a fixture and it
conveys unless excluded. The correct handling is not to note it and hope — it is to have the seller
**remove and replace it before the photographs are taken.** A buyer who fell in love with a photograph
has a reasonable expectation, and the licensee who allowed that photograph created the problem.

**The item that is not the seller's to convey.** Leased or financed equipment — propane tanks, water
treatment, solar arrays, security systems, sometimes HVAC. It looks like a fixture and the seller does
not own it, and a buyer cannot discover that by looking.

**The item installed by a tenant.** On a property with a business tenant, some of what is bolted down
is going with them, and a buyer bidding on a partly equipped building needs to know which.

### 2.4 The discipline

**Write down what conveys.** At the listing appointment walk the property with the seller and settle
every arguable item — appliances, window treatments, mounted televisions, light fixtures, playsets,
sheds, tanks, treatment equipment. Two written lists: included and excluded. Twenty minutes at
listing; a saved transaction later.

Ask one question explicitly, every time: **"Is any equipment on this property leased or financed?"**

## Hour 3 — The characteristics of land

### 3.1 Physical characteristics

**Immobility.** A parcel cannot be moved, so demand in Tulsa cannot be met by supply in Lawton. This is
why real estate markets are local and why a national statistic describes no market anyone can actually
buy in. It is also why *location* dominates value.

**Indestructibility, or durability.** Land endures. Improvements depreciate; the land beneath them does
not disappear. This is why lenders write thirty-year loans against it and why the overwhelming majority
of any Oklahoma market's housing stock was built in the past rather than this year.

**Uniqueness, or non-homogeneity.** No two parcels are identical; even two identical houses sit on
different ground. This is the legal foundation of **specific performance** as a remedy in real estate
contracts, because damages cannot buy an equivalent parcel.

### 3.2 Economic characteristics

**Scarcity.** Land is finite — not scarce everywhere, and Oklahoma has a great deal of it, but scarce
where people want to be, which is what drives price.

**Improvements.** What is built on and around a parcel changes its value, and improvements to
neighbouring property affect it too — a new interchange, a new school, a new plant.

**Permanence of investment.** Capital sunk into grading, utilities, foundations and streets is fixed
and long-lived. It cannot be recovered and redeployed, which is why development decisions are slow and
hard to reverse.

**Area preference, or *situs*.** People's preference for a particular area, partly rational and partly
not. Situs is the economic expression of immobility and it is what an appraiser measures when adjusting
for location.

## Hour 4 — Real estate economics — 605:10-3-1(a)(1)(A)

### 4.1 Supply is inelastic in the short run

Bringing new housing to market takes years: land assembly, entitlement, financing, platting,
infrastructure, construction. So when demand rises — an employer expands, a rate cut brings buyers off
the fence, a demographic wave reaches first-purchase age — **supply cannot respond, and the adjustment
happens entirely in price and in time on market.**

That lag is the structural reason real estate cycles, and it is why the cycle overshoots in both
directions: by the time the supply response arrives, the demand condition that caused it may have
passed.

### 4.2 What moves demand

Population and household formation; **employment and income**; interest rates and credit availability;
consumer confidence; and the price of the alternative, which is rent.

**In Oklahoma, one demand factor deserves separate naming: the energy sector.** Employment, income and
in-migration in several Oklahoma markets move with oil and gas activity, and a commodity price
movement can change a local housing market within a quarter — faster than employment data reports it
and far faster than supply can respond. A licensee working such a market should understand that they
are working a market with a cycle inside the cycle.

### 4.3 What moves supply

Availability of land; construction cost, including labour and materials; the cost and availability of
construction finance; the entitlement and permitting environment; and the existing stock coming to
market.

### 4.4 The market cycle

**Recovery** — absorption of existing supply, falling vacancy, prices flat then rising. **Expansion** —
demand exceeds supply, prices rise, new construction starts. **Hypersupply** — completions overtake
absorption, vacancy rises, price growth slows. **Recession** — supply exceeds demand, vacancy high,
prices flat or falling, construction stops.

A licensee should be able to say where a local market appears to sit and what evidence says so. A
licensee should **not** predict when it will move.

### 4.5 What may and may not be said

**May:** name the factors; cite recorded data with its source; describe what closed sales show; say
what inventory and days on market are doing.

**May not:** state where prices will go. **Nobody can**, and a licensee who states a forecast as fact
has made a representation they cannot support. Oklahoma's rules put the same principle on the school
itself — 605:10-5-1(d) forbids advertising a course as approved before approval and forbids claiming
Commission endorsement — and the professional standard for a licensee is no lower.

## Hour 5 — Marketing fundamentals and the Oklahoma licensing path

### 5.1 What marketing a property actually is

Not advertising. **Marketing is the sequence: price the property from evidence, prepare it, present it
accurately, expose it to the market that will buy it, and manage the response.** Advertising is one
step and it is not the one that decides outcomes.

Two rules govern every piece of it, and both come back in Module OK-13 on Fair Housing:

- **Describe the property, never the people.** Never the buyer, never the tenant, never the neighbours.
- **Attribute or verify. Never assert.** Every figure in a listing came from somewhere. Square footage
  from a tax record is an assessment record and not a measurement; acreage from a prior listing is
  somebody else's figure. *"The county record shows 1,940 square feet; if the exact figure matters to
  you, have it measured"* costs nothing and is complete protection.

### 5.2 The Oklahoma path to a licence

**Ninety clock hours.** 59 O.S. § 858-302(A): a person eighteen or older who submits evidence of
successful completion of **ninety clock hours** of basic real estate instruction in a
Commission-approved course of study may apply to take the examination for a **provisional sales
associate** licence.

**And the three-year clock.** The same subsection: *"The education required in this subsection shall
only be valid for a period of three (3) years from the date the school certified successful completion
of the course; thereafter, the applicant shall be required to successfully complete an additional
ninety (90) clock hours."*

**Three years, measured from the date the SCHOOL CERTIFIED completion** — not from the day you finish
the last module and not from your application date. Plan around it from the day you enrol.

**Then the state examination.** 605:10-3-5(f): **a score of seventy percent (70%) or more is a passing
grade on the provisional sales associate/sales associate examination.** Passing results are valid for
**one year** — 605:10-3-5(g) — and an approved application is valid for **one year** — 605:10-3-3(a)(1).

### 5.3 Part I of II — and why that matters to you

**605:10-5-3(j)** requires each entity to promote the Basic Course of Real Estate as **Part I of a two
part series**, with the **Provisional Postlicense Course of Real Estate as Part II.**

So: ninety hours qualifies you to sit the examination and to hold a **provisional** sales associate
licence. Section 858-302 then requires **further postlicense education after the provisional licence
issues** before a full sales associate licence can be obtained.

**A student who believes ninety hours is the whole of their education has been misled.** This course
says so here, in Hour 5, rather than in a footnote at Hour 90.

### 5.4 Three percentages, three examinations — do not confuse them

Oklahoma's rules use three different passing scores and they belong to three different things:

| Percentage | Examination | Rule |
|---:|---|---|
| **75%** | **this course's** end-of-course final examination | 605:10-5-3(f) |
| **70%** | the **state licensing** examination, provisional sales associate / sales associate | 605:10-3-5(f) |
| **80%** | an **instructor** applicant's qualifying examination | 605:10-5-1(f) |

A document that says "the passing score is 70 percent" without naming the examination has misstated
the regulator. **This course's final examination is passed at 75 percent**, and 605:10-5-3(f) permits
an entity to allow a student who fails to take another examination **without repeating instruction.**

### 5.5 What Oklahoma requires you to be told before you pay

**605:10-5-1(m)** requires every entity to post or provide a notice, easily observed by anyone desiring
to enrol, containing at least this language:

> "Applicants convicted of felony crimes referenced in Title 59 Section 858-301.1 or 858-4000.1 may be
> ineligible to obtain an Oklahoma Real Estate License for a predetermined number of years. For
> clarification, please contact the Commission and/or review the cited section of law as referenced
> herein. **The Commission will allow the applicant to seek preapproval prior to enrolling in a
> pre-license course.**"

and a statement that applicants who are **not permanent U.S. citizens** should review their eligibility
on the Commission's website **prior to enrolling** in pre-license education.

**Read the emphasised sentence.** Oklahoma expressly offers **preapproval before enrolment**. If the
felony provisions may apply to you, use it — before you pay for ninety hours, not after you finish
them. **This course states no offence, period or eligibility rule from §§ 858-301.1 or 858-4000.1**,
because those sections were not read from primary text for this project. The notice routes you to the
Commission and to the sections themselves, and so does this course.

Eleven items: one applied scenario, five engagement items and five remediation items. The applied item
