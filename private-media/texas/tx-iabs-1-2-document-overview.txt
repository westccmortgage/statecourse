Texas law requires all real estate license holders to give the following information about brokerage services to prospective buyers, tenants, sellers and landlords. The Commission writes that notice. You do not. This is the current version — Information About Brokerage Services, form number IABS 1-2. Let's read it block by block.

Here is the whole notice. One page, and the revision stamp in the corner reads 11-03-2025 — that is how you check you are holding the current version. Notice what it is not. Not a contract, not a listing, not a representation agreement. Every block on this page describes a relationship. None of them creates one.

The first block sets the vocabulary. A broker is responsible for all brokerage activities, including acts performed by sales agents sponsored by the broker. A sales agent must be sponsored by a broker and works with clients on behalf of the broker. The broker holds the relationship; you act for the broker.

Now the duties block — and read the heading including the words in parentheses. A client is the person or party that the broker represents. That parenthesis does real work, because the Act itself never defines the word. Three of the four bullets beneath it run to that client. Put the interests of the client above all others, including the broker's own interests; inform the client of any material information about the property or transaction received by the broker; answer the client's questions and present any offer to or counter-offer from the client.

The fourth bullet does not. Treat all parties to a real estate transaction honestly and fairly. Three duties to the client. One owed to everybody in the transaction. Representation decides advocacy. It has never decided honesty.

The next block is headed, a license holder can represent a party in a real estate transaction. As agent for owner: the broker becomes the property owner's agent through an agreement with the owner, usually in a written listing to sell or property management agreement.

As agent for buyer or tenant: the broker becomes the buyer or tenant's agent by agreeing to represent the buyer, usually through a written representation agreement. Both paragraphs close the same way — fees are not set by law and are fully negotiable.

Two more blocks finish the page. As agent for both — intermediary. And below it, when a license holder can show property without representing the buyer or tenant. Later modules take both apart. Today, know where they sit on the page.

At the foot is the block students skip. This notice is being provided for information purposes. It does not create an obligation for you to use the broker's services. Then four contact rows, each with a license number — which is why a completed notice carries several. And a line for the consumer's initials and a date. Initials acknowledge receipt. They are not consent to anything.

One page, three jobs. It names the license types, states the broker's minimum duties, and describes every relationship a Texas broker may lawfully occupy. What it never does is create one. A relationship comes from an agreement. This comes from a statute.
