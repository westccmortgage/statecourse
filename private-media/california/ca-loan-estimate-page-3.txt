Page 1 covered the loan terms. Page 2 showed where the money goes. Page 3 is built for comparing loans, and it includes a signature line many borrowers misunderstand. Let's locate five things. This is Page 3, Additional Information About This Loan. We will use three areas: Comparisons at the top, Other Considerations below, and Confirm Receipt at the bottom.

The comparison figures help borrowers evaluate one Loan Estimate against another. First, In 5 Years. The first figure shows the total paid in principal, interest, mortgage insurance, and loan costs after five years. The second shows how much principal has been paid off. Together, they show the difference between total spending and principal reduction. Next is the Annual Percentage Rate, or APR. APR is not the note rate. It expresses the cost of credit, including interest and certain loan costs, as a yearly rate.

Two loans with the same note rate may have different APRs. Below it is the Total Interest Percentage, or TIP. TIP shows the total interest paid over the full loan term as a percentage of the amount borrowed. Other Considerations covers practical terms, including appraisal, assumption, late-payment charges, and servicing. Always check the Late Payment line and whether the lender intends to service the loan or transfer servicing. Finally, Confirm Receipt.

The sentence above the signature explains that signing only confirms receipt of the form. Signing this line does not mean the borrower accepts the loan. In fact, the borrower is not required to sign the Loan Estimate. Remember the professional purpose of Page 3: comparison. In 5 Years, APR, and TIP help borrowers compare Loan Estimates, while Other Considerations identifies important operating terms.

Confirm Receipt acknowledges delivery; it does not commit the borrower to the loan. On the next screen, you will use these figures yourself.
