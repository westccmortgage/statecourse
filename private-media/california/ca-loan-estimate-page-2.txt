On Page 1, we located the loan terms and the estimated cash to close. Page 2 answers the next question every borrower asks: where does that money actually go? In this lesson, we are going to locate five areas of the closing cost detail — and find one section that most borrowers never realize is an invitation. This is Page 2, the Closing Cost Details.

It is organized into two columns. The left column shows Loan Costs — what the borrower pays in connection with getting the loan itself. The right column shows Other Costs — items like taxes, prepaids, and initial escrow. Both columns flow into one total at the bottom. First, locate Section A, Origination Charges. These are amounts charged by the lender for making the loan — and this is where discount points appear, when the borrower is paying points.

If a borrower asks what the lender itself is charging, Section A is where you look. Next is Section B, Services You Cannot Shop For. These are services the lender requires and selects the provider for — items such as the appraisal. The borrower pays for them, but does not choose who performs them. Now look carefully at Section C, Services You Can Shop For.

The estimate lists providers here, but the borrower is permitted to choose different ones. This section is an invitation, not a list of decisions that have already been made — and it is the section borrowers most often ignore, because nobody explains that shopping is actually allowed. Both columns come together in Section J, Total Closing Costs. Notice the line for Lender Credits — amounts the lender contributes toward the borrower's costs, which reduce the total.

A borrower comparing two estimates should always compare Section J, not a single line item. Finally, locate the Calculating Cash to Close table. It starts with total closing costs and walks line by line — the down payment, deposits already paid, and any credits — to the estimated cash to close. This is the arithmetic behind the single number the borrower saw on Page 1.

Here is the professional habit for Page 2: read it by sections, not by lines. Section A is what the lender charges. Sections B and C are services — and Section C belongs to the borrower. A professional who never mentions that the borrower may shop has left money on the table that was never theirs to leave.
