### (c) At least one learning objective for each module, such that meeting all of them means the entire content of the course is understood

Each module opens with a stated set of objectives, each naming something a licensee must be able to do rather than something they must have read. The full set is in Attachment 1.

The rule's test is that the **union** of the objectives is the course. The module map at `michigan/submission/module-map.md` sets out how the ten sets cover the transaction from the definition of the regulated activity through to the broker's records, which is what "principles of real estate" means in MCL 339.2504.

### (d) A structured learning method to enable the student to attain each learning objective

Every module has the same structure, and the structure is the method:

1. **Objectives** stated at the outset.
2. **Six instructional sections**, each ending in a **checkpoint** — a question the student answers before the section is recorded as viewed. The checkpoint is where an objective is exercised rather than merely read.
3. **Worked Michigan examples** inside the sections, using Michigan instruments, Michigan statutes, and Michigan practice.
4. **Key terms** and **practice problems** with the working shown.
5. **A written applied exercise** requiring the student to produce a memorandum of the kind a licensee actually writes, reviewed by the approved instructor against the common 10-point rubric.
6. **A one-item applied confirmation check** and a **five-item scored engagement check** delivered by the secure question service.

### (e) A method of assessment of the student's performance during each module of instruction

Three performance records run **inside** every module, not after the course:

- The **written applied memorandum**, submitted by the student and reviewed by the BPL-approved instructor under `michigan/submission/bpl/applied-memo-rubric.md`. It is satisfactory at 8 of 10 points with no zero on legal/rule accuracy or professional-boundary handling; otherwise the instructor gives feedback and the student revises until satisfactory.
- The **applied confirmation check**, one machine-scored scenario item requiring 100 percent.
- The **scored engagement check**, five items, mastery threshold 80 percent.

Only the applied confirmation and engagement/remediation items are held in the secure question bank. The written memorandum, rubric result, feedback, reviewer, version, and timestamps are retained in the student record. The assessment manifest is a reviewer appendix; Attachment 4 is the separate asynchronous-attestation form.

**The 8/10 memo threshold, 100 percent applied-confirmation threshold, and 80 percent engagement/remediation thresholds are StateCourse instructional standards, not Michigan requirements.** Michigan prescribes no passing score for a prelicensure assessment; R 339.22154(2)(c) records a grade only "if an examination is required to determine successful completion." The Department is asked to accept the filed standards, and StateCourse will adopt any different direction before release.

### (f) A remediation for any student who is deficient in the assessment to repeat the module until the student understands the course content material

An applied memorandum marked **needs revision** receives criterion-level instructor feedback and remains incomplete until a revised version earns satisfactory. A missed applied-confirmation item returns the student to the relevant instruction and keeps the module incomplete until the confirmation is correct. A failed engagement check does three things automatically:

1. Sets the module to a remediation state, so it cannot be completed.
2. Serves an **alternate explanation** of the concepts the student missed — different framing, not the same text again.
3. Serves an **alternate item set** on the same concepts. The remediation items are authored separately from the engagement items and no prompt is shared between them, so a student cannot pass by recalling which answer was marked wrong.

The module remains incomplete until the student reaches 80% on the five-item remediation set. Because course credit requires every module to be complete, the course cannot be finished with an unremediated module. Three unsuccessful remediation attempts also raise a support flag to the school. The 80% remediation threshold, like the engagement threshold, is a StateCourse instructional standard submitted for review rather than a Michigan-prescribed score.

### (5) A detailed description of how the school remedies hardware and software failures

Supplied at Attachment 7 and summarised here.

**Progress is stored server-side, not in the browser.** Section views, written-memo versions, instructor rubric/feedback records, applied-confirmation and assessment attempts, remediation state, and recorded study time are written to the school's database as they occur. A student who loses power, closes the browser, or changes device resumes exactly where they were, on the same module, with the same completion state.

**No completion state is derived from the client.** The browser reports events; the server decides what they mean. A failure of the student's device cannot advance, complete, or credit anything.

**Assessment answers are graded on the server**, from a key that is never delivered to the browser. A failure during an assessment cannot corrupt a grade or expose an answer.

**Session interruption does not consume an attempt.** An attempt is recorded when answers are submitted, not when an assessment is opened.

**Support.** Students reach school support by email with a one business day response commitment. Where a platform fault is confirmed, the school corrects the affected record and retains the correction with its reason, so that the student's record shows both what happened and what was done.

**Availability and incident handling.** The Michigan launch design records an incident affecting student access with its start, cause, resolution, and any student records corrected as a result. Before PSS filing, acceptance evidence must prove that monitoring, preservation, and Department export work as described; this draft is not itself evidence of operation.

## Makeup policy — R 339.22141(1)(i)

An asynchronous course has no scheduled class sessions to miss, so the policy addresses what a student actually experiences: an interrupted module.

1. **A module in progress remains in progress.** Progress is stored server-side and a student resumes at the point they left, on any device, without penalty. Before filing, acceptance tests must prove preservation and recovery of memorandum versions, instructor rubric/feedback, confirmation/assessment attempts, and remediation state.
2. **No module expires on its own.** Enrollment carries an overall period stated in the enrollment agreement; within it, a student may return to an incomplete module at any time.
3. **Every deficiency is completed, not waived.** A needs-revision memorandum receives criterion-level instructor feedback and is resubmitted until satisfactory; a missed applied confirmation returns the student to relevant instruction and is retried until correct; and an unsuccessful engagement assessment serves alternate explanation/remediation until the student reaches 80% on the five-item set. There is no attempt limit.
4. **An interruption creates no false attempt or submission.** A machine-scored attempt exists only after submission. A memorandum version is submitted only after server acknowledgment; a partial receipt is reconciled before retry, and confirmed versions/feedback are never overwritten.
5. **A student who cannot progress reaches an instructor.** The approved instructor reviews every memorandum within the filed service standard; three unsuccessful machine-scored remediation attempts raise a support flag, and the school contacts the student.
6. **Nothing here shortens the course.** Michigan requires attendance at 100 percent, and this policy governs *when* a student completes a module, never *whether* the hours are completed.

Beginning with the first calendar year in which students enroll, StateCourse LLC will publish the percentage calculated as students who successfully completed the course divided by students who enrolled during that calendar year, and will update the disclosure no later than February 1 each year as R 339.22141(1)(j) requires.

**The figure cannot be stated in this filing**, because the course has never been offered and there is no previous calendar year. ## Solicitation separation — R 339.22159

The 40 credited hours contain education only. Organizational-membership, employment, business-related, recruiting, account-upgrade, Complete Licensing Path, and other noneducational presentations or solicitations are excluded from module pages, checkpoints, assessments, remediation, study-time surfaces, and every clock-hour calculation. Any separate noncredit product or career information appears only outside the active course and earns no credit. This separation is a tested release gate, not a marketing convention.
