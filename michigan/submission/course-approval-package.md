### (c) At least one learning objective for each module, such that meeting all of them means the entire content of the course is understood

Each module opens with a stated set of objectives, each naming something a licensee must be able to do rather than something they must have read. The full set is in Attachment 1.

The rule's test is that the **union** of the objectives is the course. The module map at `michigan/submission/module-map.md` sets out how the ten sets cover the transaction from the definition of the regulated activity through to the broker's records, which is what "principles of real estate" means in MCL 339.2504.

### (d) A structured learning method to enable the student to attain each learning objective

Every module has the same structure, and the structure is the method:

1. **Objectives** stated at the outset.
2. **Six instructional sections**, each ending in a **checkpoint** — a question the student answers before the section is recorded as viewed. The checkpoint is where an objective is exercised rather than merely read.
3. **Worked Michigan examples** inside the sections, using Michigan instruments, Michigan statutes, and Michigan practice.
4. **Key terms** and **practice problems** with the working shown.
5. **An applied exercise** requiring the student to produce a memorandum of the kind a licensee actually writes.
6. **A scored engagement check.**

### (e) A method of assessment of the student's performance during each module of instruction

Two assessments run **inside** the module, not after the course:

- The **applied exercise**, a scenario item requiring the student to apply the module's material to a Michigan transaction problem.
- The **scored engagement check**, five items, mastery threshold 80 percent.

Both are held in the platform's question bank and are supplied at Attachment 4.

**The 80 percent threshold is a StateCourse instructional standard, not a Michigan requirement.** Michigan prescribes no passing score for a prelicensure assessment; R 339.22154(2)(c) records a grade only "if an examination is required to determine successful completion", which leaves the question to the school. The Department is asked to accept the threshold, and StateCourse will adopt any figure the Department prefers.

### (f) A remediation for any student who is deficient in the assessment to repeat the module until the student understands the course content material

A failed engagement check does three things automatically:

1. Sets the module to a remediation state, so it cannot be completed.
2. Serves an **alternate explanation** of the concepts the student missed — different framing, not the same text again.
3. Serves an **alternate item set** on the same concepts. The remediation items are authored separately from the engagement items and no prompt is shared between them, so a student cannot pass by recalling which answer was marked wrong.

The module remains incomplete until the remediation item is answered correctly. Because course credit requires every module to be complete, the course cannot be finished with an unremediated module. Three unsuccessful attempts also raise a support flag to the school.

### (5) A detailed description of how the school remedies hardware and software failures

Supplied at Attachment 7 and summarised here.

**Progress is stored server-side, not in the browser.** Section views, applied responses, assessment attempts, remediation state, and recorded study time are written to the school's database as they occur. A student who loses power, closes the browser, or changes device resumes exactly where they were, on the same module, with the same completion state.

**No completion state is derived from the client.** The browser reports events; the server decides what they mean. A failure of the student's device cannot advance, complete, or credit anything.

**Assessment answers are graded on the server**, from a key that is never delivered to the browser. A failure during an assessment cannot corrupt a grade or expose an answer.

**Session interruption does not consume an attempt.** An attempt is recorded when answers are submitted, not when an assessment is opened.

**Support.** Students reach school support by email with a one business day response commitment. Where a platform fault is confirmed, the school corrects the affected record and retains the correction with its reason, so that the student's record shows both what happened and what was done.

**Availability and incident handling.** The platform is monitored, and an incident affecting student access is recorded with its start, its cause, its resolution, and any student records corrected as a result. That log is available to the Department on request.

## Certificate of completion — R 339.22141(1)(f)

The certificate must contain five things, and it carries all five:

| Field | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Date of course completion | The completion record |
| Identification of the course, including its name as approved by the Department | The approval record |
| The name **and approval number** of the school | The approval record |
| The name of the student | The enrollment record |
| The number of clock hours completed by the student | 40, on completion of all ten modules |

**No certificate can be issued before the Department assigns an approval number**, because the number is one of the five required fields. The platform holds the completion record and the issuance path, and refuses to produce a certificate while the approval number is absent.

The certificate issues only after the student presents photo identification, and for this distance course only after the school has satisfied itself that the student whose attendance is reported is the student who completed the course — R 339.22154(5).

## Makeup policy — R 339.22141(1)(i)

An asynchronous course has no scheduled class sessions to miss, so the policy addresses what a student actually experiences: an interrupted module.

1. **A module in progress remains in progress.** Progress is stored server-side and a student resumes at the point they left, on any device, without penalty.
2. **No module expires on its own.** Enrollment carries an overall period stated in the enrollment agreement; within it, a student may return to an incomplete module at any time.
3. **A failed assessment is not a failed course.** Remediation is served automatically and the module is completed when the remediation item is answered correctly. There is no limit on remediation attempts.
4. **A student who cannot progress reaches an instructor.** Three unsuccessful attempts raise a support flag, and the school contacts the student.
5. **Nothing here shortens the course.** Michigan requires attendance at 100 percent, and this policy governs *when* a student completes a module, never *whether* the hours are completed.

## Enrollment application — R 339.22141(1)(j)

The enrollment application collects the student's identity, contact details, the information the student record requires under R 339.22154(2), and the acknowledgments below, and carries the completion-percentage disclosure the rule requires.

**Completion percentage disclosure, proposed text:**

> **Completion rate.** Of the students who enrolled in this course during the previous calendar year, ___ percent successfully completed it. This figure covers enrollments in the calendar year stated, is computed as students who completed the course divided by students who enrolled, and is updated no later than February 1 each year as R 339.22141(1)(j) requires. StateCourse publishes how this figure is produced, including what counts as an enrollment and what counts as a completion, at the address shown on this application.

**The figure cannot be stated in this filing**, because the course has never been offered and there is no previous calendar year. StateCourse will state it from the first February 1 after the first calendar year of operation. Until then the disclosure reads that the course was not offered in the previous calendar year.

**A published figure needs a published method.** StateCourse will publish the methodology alongside the rate — the definition of an enrollment, the definition of a completion, the treatment of withdrawals and refunds, and the cohort window — so that the number can be checked rather than merely read.

## Open items before filing

1. **Proprietary Schools Act.** R 339.22143(2) binds a proprietary real estate school to 1943 PA 148. Whether StateCourse is a proprietary school within that act, and whether an out-of-state distance provider is one at all, is a legal question. It is Michigan's analogue of the California BPPE question and carries the same risk profile: the wrong answer surfaces after filing.
2. **The instructor route.** See Part C.
3. **The program coordinator.** One named individual, responsible for supervising the programme and for compliance — R 339.22145.
4. **ARELLO or equivalence.** R 339.22157(3) accepts an ARELLO-certified delivery system and (4) permits the Department to approve an equivalent one. This package is drafted on the **equivalence** route, which means the Department evaluates the delivery system directly against the six elements in Part B. The screening register at `docs/states/online-delivery-screening.md` sets out why certification is worth pursuing regardless: four of the states screened so far make it a precondition to approval, and Montana's approval ceases the moment a certification lapses.
5. **The school address and entity record**, from the filed formation documents.
6. **The one-year approval term.** R 339.22143(1) expires approval one year after it is granted, with renewal filed not less than 60 days ahead. Renewal is an annual operational routine in Michigan, and it should be diarised at the moment approval is received rather than discovered eleven months later.

## What this package deliberately does not claim

- It does not claim approval. No StateCourse course is approved in any jurisdiction.
- It does not describe the StateCourse final examination as a Michigan requirement. Michigan prescribes none; the examination is a provider standard and is labelled as one everywhere it appears.
- It does not describe proctoring as a Michigan requirement. Michigan's identity requirement attaches to certificate issuance and to the accuracy of reported attendance, not to supervised examination.
- It does not import a rule from another jurisdiction. Every requirement answered here is Michigan's, cited to Michigan's own text.
