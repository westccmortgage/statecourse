# Michigan Prelicensure Course — Summary of Topics and Hours

**Attachment for R 339.22141(1)(e):** a summary of topics completed for the prelicensure course, including the number of hours allocated to each topic.
**Course:** Principles of Real Estate - Michigan Salesperson Prelicensure
**Approved hours sought:** 40 clock hours
**Delivery:** Distance (online), asynchronous
**Prepared:** August 19, 2026

## How the hours are allocated

Ten modules of four clock hours each. The allocation is deliberately even, for three reasons that all come from the rules rather than from convenience.

R 339.22152(2) makes distance credit conditional on hours attended equalling hours approved, so every hour has to be attributable to a module a student can be shown to have completed. Uneven modules make that arithmetic harder to audit, not easier.

R 339.22157(2)(c) requires the learning objectives to be such that meeting all of them means the whole course is understood. Ten modules with published objectives make that claim checkable in one page.

And MCL 339.2504 sets a floor of four clock hours on civil rights law and equal opportunity in housing. Putting exactly one module on that subject, and counting nothing else toward it, means the statutory minimum is satisfied by a single identifiable block rather than by adding up fragments scattered through a syllabus. If the Department wants to audit the four hours, it reads one module.

## Statutory minimum, isolated

Module 07 carries the four clock hours required by MCL 339.2504 for civil rights law and equal opportunity in housing. It covers the federal Fair Housing Act, the Elliott-Larsen Civil Rights Act, the Persons with Disabilities Civil Rights Act, advertising and steering, reasonable accommodation and modification, and the licensee's own exposure.

Fair-housing material appears in other modules where the transaction raises it — advertising in module 05, the loan application in module 08 — but **none of that time is counted toward the statutory four hours.** The four hours are module 07 alone.

## Learning objectives, and why the union is the course

R 339.22157(2)(c) sets an unusual test: the objectives must be written so that a student who meets all of them has understood the entire content of the course. Each module states its objectives at the top, and each objective names the thing a licensee has to be able to do. Taken together the ten sets cover the transaction from the definition of the regulated activity through to the closing statement and the broker's records, which is what "principles of real estate" means in MCL 339.2504.

Each module is structured the same way, which is the structured learning method R 339.22157(2)(d) asks for:

1. **Objectives** — what the student will be able to do.
2. **Six instructional sections**, each ending in a checkpoint that has to be answered before the section is recorded as viewed.
3. **A written applied exercise** — a Michigan transaction memorandum reviewed by the approved instructor under a common 10-point rubric; satisfactory requires 8/10 with no zero on legal accuracy or professional boundaries.
4. **A one-item applied confirmation check** at 100% and a **five-item engagement check** at 80%.
5. **Remediation** — a written memo marked needs revision receives feedback and is resubmitted; a missed applied confirmation returns the student to the relevant instruction; and a failed engagement check returns the student to an alternate explanation and separately authored five-item set. The module remains incomplete until every applicable requirement passes. This is R 339.22157(2)(f): the module repeats until the content is understood.

## Time, and what a clock hour means here

Michigan does not define a clock hour by minutes in these rules, and this course does not invent a definition. What it does instead is measure: the platform records active study time per module against a fixed instructional design of four hours, and credit for the module requires the module's own completion requirements — every section, the applied exercise, the engagement check, and any remediation — not merely elapsed time. A student who reads faster than the design still completes every requirement; a student who leaves a page open completes none of them.

## Materials

The instructional text is written for this course. Federal material cites the official federal source. Michigan material cites the Michigan Compiled Laws or the LARA rule by number. No third-party outline, competitor syllabus, or commercial exam-prep material is used, and no material is reproduced from another StateCourse jurisdiction: Michigan practice is Michigan practice, and a course that recycles another state's answers is how a school ends up teaching the wrong law.
