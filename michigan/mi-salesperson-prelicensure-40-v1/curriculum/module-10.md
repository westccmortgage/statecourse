# Principles of Real Estate - Michigan Salesperson Prelicensure — Module 10

## Closing, trust accounts, and professional practice

**Course version:** 1.0-submission-draft
**Module time:** 4 clock hours (240 instructional minutes)
**Regulatory status:** Not approved. No Michigan prelicensure credit is available for this course.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module the student will be able to:

- Describe what happens at a Michigan closing, who does what, and what the licensee is responsible for before it.
- Read a settlement statement line by line and identify what each entry does to each party's bottom line.
- Apply Michigan's trust and escrow account rules, including what must never happen to client money.
- Apply the broker's obligation when a transaction fails and the parties dispute a deposit.
- Identify the professional-practice obligations that survive a closing — records, supervision, advertising, and continuing education.
- State what a new licensee should establish in their first weeks to avoid the failures this course has described.

## 1. Closing

### 1.1 What a closing is

Closing is the performance of the purchase agreement: the price is paid, the deed is delivered, the mortgage is executed and recorded, liens are paid and released, and possession passes according to the contract.

In Michigan the closing is customarily conducted by a **title company**, which acts as the settlement agent, disburses the funds, and records the instruments. Lawyers are involved in some transactions and not others.

### 1.2 The licensee's work happens before

Almost everything a licensee does for a closing is done in the weeks before it. By closing day the licensee's job is largely to have made the day uneventful.

The pre-closing checklist that matters:

- Every contingency satisfied, waived in writing, or extended in writing.
- Title commitment reviewed, Schedule B-I requirements assigned to whoever must satisfy them, Schedule B-II exceptions delivered to the client.
- Payoffs ordered. Discharges of old mortgages identified and chased.
- Seller's sworn statement and unconditional lien waivers obtained where work was done — module 04.
- Association documents, dues status, and any pending assessment confirmed.
- Municipal requirements — point-of-sale inspection, certificate of occupancy, well and septic — identified and satisfied.
- Utilities arranged for transfer, and final readings scheduled.
- Insurance bound for the buyer, effective on closing.
- The final walkthrough scheduled close to closing, not days before.
- Closing Disclosure timing confirmed with the lender — module 08.
- The parties told what identification and what funds they must bring, and in what form.

### 1.3 The walkthrough

The buyer's opportunity to confirm that the property is in the agreed condition, that agreed repairs were done, and that included items are present.

It is not a re-inspection and not a renegotiation, though it becomes one when something is wrong. The licensee's role is to be present, to document with dated photographs, and to raise problems immediately rather than at the closing table where the pressure to overlook is greatest.

### 1.4 Possession

Closing and possession are different events. Where they differ, the occupancy agreement from module 06 governs — the daily rate, the escrow, the condition required, and the remedy if the seller does not leave.

A licensee should treat "the seller will be out by the weekend" as an unwritten agreement that has not been made.

#### Checkpoint 1.1

At the walkthrough the agreed furnace repair has not been done and the seller's contractor cannot come for two weeks. What are the options?

*Answer:* Delay closing; hold funds in escrow at the title company against the repair with a written agreement on amount, scope, and deadline; or the buyer accepts a credit and takes the work on. All three are contract amendments requiring signatures. What must not happen is a verbal promise at the table and a closing on the strength of it.

## 2. The settlement statement

### 2.1 What it is

The settlement statement — for most consumer mortgages, the Closing Disclosure — shows every dollar moving in the transaction: what the buyer pays and finances, what the seller receives, and every charge against each.

A licensee should be able to read one and explain any line to a client, and should review it **before** closing day so that errors are corrected while there is time.

### 2.2 Debits and credits

**Debit** — a charge against a party. **Credit** — an amount in their favour.

The purchase price is a debit to the buyer and a credit to the seller. Earnest money already paid is a credit to the buyer. The loan amount is a credit to the buyer. Payoff of the seller's mortgage is a debit to the seller. Commission is a debit to the seller, or to whoever the agreement charges.

Prorations run both ways depending on what has been paid — module 09, section 6.3.

### 2.3 A worked statement

Price $310,000; earnest money $5,000 held by the title company; buyer's loan $248,000; seller's mortgage payoff $186,420; total commission 5.5 percent; seller pays state and county transfer tax of $2,635; taxes of $4,380 paid in advance by the seller with closing on 1 October, 365-day method; buyer's closing costs $4,150; seller's other closing costs $1,180.

**Buyer:**
- Debit — purchase price: $310,000
- Credit — earnest money: $5,000
- Credit — loan: $248,000
- Debit — closing costs: $4,150
- Debit — tax proration, 92 days at $12.00: $1,104
- **Cash to close: 310,000 + 4,150 + 1,104 − 5,000 − 248,000 = $62,254**

**Seller:**
- Credit — purchase price: $310,000
- Debit — mortgage payoff: $186,420
- Debit — commission at 5.5 percent: $17,050
- Debit — transfer tax: $2,635
- Debit — other costs: $1,180
- Credit — tax proration: $1,104
- **Net proceeds: 310,000 + 1,104 − 186,420 − 17,050 − 2,635 − 1,180 = $103,819**

The tax proration appears on both sides in opposite directions, which is the structure students most often mis-handle: the seller prepaid the year, so the buyer reimburses the seller for the 92 days from 1 October to 31 December.

### 2.4 Reviewing it

Check the price, the credits, the commission, the payoff, the prorations, and the names and capacities of the parties. A settlement statement with a seller named differently from the deed is a problem discovered too late — module 03.

#### Checkpoint 2.1

A seller's statement shows a $1,900 charge for a repair credit the parties agreed to, but the purchase agreement was never amended. What is the issue?

*Answer:* The statement reflects an agreement that does not exist in writing. Either the amendment gets signed before closing or the charge does not belong on the statement. And where the transaction is financed, an undisclosed credit raises the lender-disclosure problem from module 08 — the credit must be visible to the lender, not arranged around it.

## 3. Trust and escrow accounts

### 3.1 Whose money it is

Money a broker receives belonging to someone else — earnest money, security deposits, rent collected for owners, funds held for repairs — is **not the broker's money**. It is held in trust, and the rules exist because the temptation is obvious and the harm is severe.

R 339.22134 governs trust and escrow accounts for Michigan brokers.

### 3.2 The rules

**Deposit promptly** in a designated trust or escrow account, separate from the broker's operating funds.

**Never commingle.** The broker's money does not go into the trust account, beyond any minimum the bank requires to keep it open, and trust money never goes into the operating account.

**Never convert.** Using client money for any purpose other than the one it was received for is conversion, and it is the fastest route from a licence to a criminal matter.

**Keep records** identifying whose money is in the account, for what, and when it moved. A ledger per transaction and a reconciliation of the whole.

**Disburse only as authorised** — by the contract, by the parties' written agreement, or by a court.

**A salesperson does not hold trust money.** Funds received go to the broker promptly for deposit. This is module 01's chain of authority in its most consequential form.

### 3.3 Property management

Property management magnifies every one of these. Rent collected belongs to owners. Security deposits are held under Michigan's security deposit statute, with its own requirements about where deposits are held, the notice a tenant receives, and the timeline for accounting after a tenancy ends.

A property manager's trust accounting is not a variation on brokerage trust accounting; it is the same discipline applied to more money moving more often.

### 3.4 Reconciliation

The trust account is reconciled regularly against the individual ledgers, and the total of the ledgers must equal the account balance. A discrepancy is investigated immediately, not at month end.

This is the control that catches errors before they become shortfalls, and the absence of it is what turns a mistake into a pattern.

#### Checkpoint 3.1

A broker's operating account is short for payroll and the trust account holds $60,000 of earnest money. The broker intends to move $8,000 and replace it Friday. Evaluate.

*Answer:* This is conversion, and the intention to repay does not change it. Trust money may be disbursed only for its own purpose and only as authorised. The fact that the money would be back before anyone noticed is a description of concealment, not a defence. A licensee who is asked to participate refuses and escalates outside the brokerage if necessary.

## 4. When a transaction fails

### 4.1 The disputed deposit

A transaction collapses and both parties claim the earnest money. This is common, and it is the situation where a broker's obligations are most misunderstood.

**The broker does not decide who is right.** The broker holds the funds until the parties agree in writing, or a court orders their disposition, or the applicable procedure for resolving the dispute is followed. Releasing to one party because their argument seems better exposes the broker to the other.

### 4.2 What the licensee does

Get the facts in writing from both sides. Escalate to the broker immediately. Tell each party that the licensee cannot adjudicate the dispute and cannot advise them on their rights. Route both parties to counsel. Document every step and every communication.

The licensee's instinct here is to solve it, and that instinct is the problem. A licensee who tells one party they are entitled to the money has given legal advice and taken a side.

### 4.3 The other failures

**Financing falls through.** Whether the contingency was live, whether it was properly exercised, and by when, decides the outcome. The file's dates answer it.

**A party refuses to close.** Remedies from module 06 — damages, specific performance, forfeiture — are legal questions.

**A death or incapacity** during a transaction raises authority questions that stop everything until an estate or a fiduciary is in place.

**A title defect surfaces late.** Whether the seller can cure and by when, and what the buyer may do, comes from the contract and from counsel.

### 4.4 What survives the failure

Confidentiality survives. The obligation to account for money survives. The record survives, and it is the only thing that will speak for the licensee if the failure becomes a complaint.

#### Checkpoint 4.1

A buyer terminates two days after the inspection deadline and demands the deposit. The seller refuses. The buyer's agent believes the buyer is right. What does the agent do?

*Answer:* Nothing that resolves it. The agent escalates to their broker, tells the buyer that the deposit's disposition is not the agent's to decide and that the question is a legal one, refers them to counsel, and documents the timeline — particularly the deadline and the date of the termination notice, which is what the dispute will turn on. Believing the buyer is right is not authority to act on it.

## 5. Professional practice

### 5.1 Records

For the brokerage, the transaction file is the evidence: the agreements, the disclosures, the amendments, the correspondence, the settlement statement, the notes of what was said and when. For a school, R 339.22154 requires a permanent record of each student.

The habit from module 01 stands: an action without a record is, in a dispute, an action that may not have happened.

### 5.2 Supervision

The broker's supervisory duty is affirmative — module 01. A new licensee should expect their broker to review files, approve advertising, and be available for the escalations this course keeps recommending. A brokerage where none of that happens is a brokerage where the new licensee will be alone with problems they are not equipped for.

### 5.3 Advertising, again

Michigan requires advertising to identify the brokerage. Every factual claim is a representation. Fair-housing rules apply to words, images, and targeting — module 07. Online material outlives its deletion.

### 5.4 Continuing education

Michigan licensees complete continuing education on the renewal cycle, and R 339.22161 governs it. Two points a new licensee should know: prelicensure credit does not substitute for continuing education, and continuing education does not substitute for prelicensure credit — MCL 339.2504 says so expressly in both directions.

### 5.5 Discipline

Section 602 of the Occupational Code supplies penalties. R 339.22155 adds education-specific grounds. Conduct that leads to discipline is, almost always, conduct this course has already named: misrepresentation, failure to disclose, mishandling trust money, discrimination, unlicensed activity, and advertising that misleads.

### 5.6 Ethics beyond the rules

The rules set a floor. Most of what makes a licensee trustworthy sits above it: telling a client something they do not want to hear, declining a listing at a price you cannot support, saying "I don't know" and then finding out, refusing a transaction that would work only if a document said something untrue.

None of that is enforceable and all of it is what a client is actually buying.

#### Checkpoint 5.1

A broker's office has no file review, no advertising approval, and tells new licensees to "ask if you need anything." Is that supervision?

*Answer:* No. Supervision is an affirmative duty discharged by a system, not by availability. The broker is accountable for the licensees' conduct whether or not they look at it. For a new licensee choosing a brokerage, the presence of real file review is one of the few reliable indicators of what their first year will be like.

## 6. The first year

### 6.1 What to establish in the first month

**A file standard.** One folder per transaction, every document dated, every conversation that matters recorded the same day.

**A diary.** Every deadline from every contract, entered when the contract is signed, with a reminder before it — module 06.

**Scripts.** The agency disclosure sentence, the neighbourhood-question answer from module 07, the referral sentence from module 01. Written down and rehearsed, because they are needed under pressure.

**A referral list.** Inspectors, lenders, title companies, surveyors, lawyers — more than one of each, with any financial relationship disclosed.

**A relationship with your broker.** Know how to reach them, and use it early rather than late.

### 6.2 The failures this course has described

Every one of them is preventable, and they recur:

- Practising law by answering a question that needed a lawyer.
- Repeating a figure — square footage, acreage, taxes, school district — without its source.
- Quoting the seller's tax bill as the buyer's future tax, without the uncapping warning from module 04.
- Missing a contingency deadline.
- Letting an agency disclosure happen after the buyer has explained their maximum.
- Steering while trying to be helpful.
- Holding a cheque instead of getting it to the broker.
- Preparing a document that says something untrue to a lender.
- Advertising without identifying the brokerage.
- Having no record of what was said.

### 6.3 What competence looks like

A competent licensee is not one who knows every rule. It is one who notices when a question has left their authority, routes it to the right person, meets the deadline, and writes it down.

That is the whole discipline, and it is available to a licensee on their first day.

### 6.4 After this course

Completing this course satisfies, if the Department approves it, the education requirement in MCL 339.2504. It does not make anyone a licensee: the examination, the application, and association with a broker follow.

**Nothing in this course is legal advice, and completing it does not create Michigan credit while the course is unapproved.**

#### Checkpoint 6.1

A new licensee's first client asks a question the licensee cannot answer, in front of the client's spouse, and the licensee feels the pressure to say something. What is the right response?

*Answer:* "I don't know, and I'm not going to guess on something this important. Let me find out and come back to you today." It costs nothing, it is more impressive than a confident wrong answer, and it is the sentence that separates the licensees who last from the ones who appear in complaints.

## Key terms

**Commingling** — mixing trust funds with the broker's own money; prohibited.

**Conversion** — using trust money for a purpose other than the one it was received for.

**Credit** — an amount in a party's favour on a settlement statement.

**Debit** — a charge against a party on a settlement statement.

**Reconciliation** — matching the trust account balance against the individual transaction ledgers.

**Settlement agent** — the party conducting the closing, in Michigan customarily a title company.

**Walkthrough** — the buyer's pre-closing confirmation of condition and included items.

## Practice problems

**Problem 1.** Price $268,500, earnest money $3,000, loan $214,800, buyer's closing costs $3,890, tax proration debiting the buyer $940. Compute cash to close.

*Working:* 268,500 + 3,890 + 940 − 3,000 − 214,800 = **$55,530**.

**Problem 2.** Same transaction: seller's payoff $151,200, commission 6 percent, transfer tax $2,283, other seller costs $960, tax proration crediting the seller $940. Compute net proceeds.

*Working:* Commission 268,500 × 0.06 = $16,110. Net = 268,500 + 940 − 151,200 − 16,110 − 2,283 − 960 = **$98,887**.

**Problem 3.** A salesperson receives a $4,000 earnest money cheque on a Saturday. The office is closed until Tuesday. What should happen?

*Working:* The cheque goes to the broker for deposit as promptly as the broker's procedure allows, and the salesperson should know that procedure before they need it. A salesperson holding client funds over a weekend is a problem waiting for a complaint; if the brokerage has no weekend procedure, that is a question to ask in week one, not week forty.

**Problem 4.** Both parties instruct the broker in writing to release the deposit to the buyer. May the broker?

*Working:* Yes. Written agreement of both parties is one of the authorised bases for disbursement. What the broker may not do is release on one party's instruction, or on the broker's own view of who is right.

**Problem 5.** A seller asks the licensee to delay recording the deed until January for tax reasons.

*Working:* Recording timing affects notice and priority — module 03 — and the tax consequence is a legal and accounting question. The licensee does not control recording, does not advise on the tax effect, and should route the request to the title company and to the seller's accountant or lawyer. Agreeing to it as a favour is advice the licensee is not licensed to give.

**Problem 6.** Six weeks after closing, a buyer calls to say the basement flooded and the seller must have known. What does the licensee do?

*Working:* Take the facts in writing, do not speculate about what the seller knew, do not offer a view on the buyer's remedy, notify the broker, preserve the file including the seller's disclosure statement and every communication, and refer the buyer to counsel. This is exactly the moment the record from module 06 either protects the licensee or does not exist.

## Module summary

**Closing is the performance of the contract**, conducted in Michigan customarily by a title company — and the licensee's work is done in the weeks before it.

**Read the settlement statement before closing day.** Debits charge, credits favour, and prorations appear on both sides in opposite directions.

**Trust money is never the broker's.** Deposit promptly, never commingle, never convert, record, reconcile, and disburse only as authorised. A salesperson does not hold it.

**A disputed deposit is not the broker's to decide.** Hold, escalate, refer, document.

**Records, supervision, advertising, and continuing education are the practice obligations that outlast any transaction.**

**Every failure this course describes is preventable**, and the prevention is the same each time: notice the limit, route it, meet the deadline, write it down.

## Required applied exercise

Prepare a one-page closing file review memorandum for the transaction supplied with this module. Build the buyer's cash to close and the seller's net proceeds line by line; identify every entry that does not match the agreement; list every pre-closing item that remains outstanding and who must satisfy it; identify any trust-money handling issue; and state what you would raise with your broker before closing day. The approved instructor scores it under the common 10-point applied-memo rubric; 8/10 with no zero on legal accuracy or professional boundaries is satisfactory, and a needs-revision result receives feedback and must be resubmitted.
