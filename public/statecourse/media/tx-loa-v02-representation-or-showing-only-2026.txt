Representation or Showing Only: the 2026 Decision

Since January first, 2026, there are exactly two lawful ways to work with a residential buyer in Texas. You have to know which one you are in before the appointment starts — not afterwards, and not once somebody asks.

One prospect, two postures. Either your broker represents the buyer, under a written agreement that says so. Or your broker shows property without representing the buyer, under section 1101.562. There is no third position and no working it out later.

The choice is made before the showing, in writing. The notice states the rule in the consumer's own words. A license holder who performs brokerage activity for a prospective buyer of residential property must enter into a written agreement with the buyer before showing any residential property to the buyer or if no residential property will be shown, before presenting an offer on behalf of the buyer. Two moments, and only two.

The form then points you to section 1101.563 for what that agreement must contain, because the notice summarises and the statute defines.

Now the other posture, and the form lists its conditions. The broker has not agreed with the buyer or tenant, either orally or in writing, to represent the buyer or tenant. The broker is not otherwise acting as the buyer or tenant's agent at the time of showing the property. The broker does not provide the buyer or tenant opinions or advice regarding the property or real estate transactions generally. And the broker does not perform any other act of real estate brokerage for the buyer or tenant.

Four conditions, joined by and. Satisfy three and you are not inside this section at all.

Read the paragraph underneath, because the non-representation posture is papered too. Before showing a residential property to an unrepresented prospective buyer, a license holder must enter into a written agreement that contains the information required by section 1101.563 of the Texas Occupations Code. The agreement may not be exclusive and must be limited to no more than 14 days.

One more gate stands before any showing without representation, and which side you are on decides it. Represent the owner, and you make the representation disclosure — orally or in writing — under section 1101.558(b). Represent nobody, and you deliver the written notice under section 1101.558(b-1). That is the IABS.

Here is where this fails, and it is never at the paperwork. It fails in a hallway, when someone you are not representing asks whether the price is fair. Section 1101.562(c) lets you confirm the size, price and terms. Confirm — not evaluate.

Have the sentence ready before you need it: “I can't give you an opinion on that, because I'm showing you this house without representing you.” Then stop talking.
