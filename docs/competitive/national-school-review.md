# The established schools — who they actually are, and what the regulator's own database says

**Reviewed:** August 19, 2026. **Reviewer:** StateCourse build.
**Method:** fifteen schools were retrieved and read directly — home pages, California pre-license pages, pricing tables, guarantee terms, and head metadata. Market structure was then taken from the controlling source rather than from the schools themselves: the California DRE public database of approved statutory/pre-license courses at `secure.dre.ca.gov/publicasp/cestatutory.asp`, queried per course for correspondence delivery.

Two schools could not be read (McKissock and Duane Gomer return 403 to a plain request; Chamberlin's site did not respond). Both appear in the DRE database, so their California footprint is recorded from the regulator instead.

## 1. The headline: the DRE database tells us more than the fifteen websites combined

Approved **correspondence** (online, self-study) sponsors in California, by course:

| Real Estate Principles | **105** |

| Real Estate Practice | 63 |
| **Mortgage Loan Brokering and Lending** | **16** |

California requires 135 hours: Principles, Practice, and one elective. Nearly every school picks Finance or Legal Aspects as that elective. Intersecting the sponsor lists:

- Approved for **Principles + Practice + Finance** — the conventional bundle: **46 sponsors.**
- Approved for **Principles + Practice + Mortgage Loan Brokering and Lending** — our exact three courses: **14 sponsors.**

And of those 14, not one is a national brand. They are firsttuesday (S0110), Duane Gomer (S0098), Mike Russ (S0156), Premier Schools (S0184), Lumbleau (S0577), Accredited (S0011), Agent Real Estate Schools (S0655), US Realty Training (S0713), and six smaller California operators. Colibri, The CE Shop, Kaplan, Aceable, 360training and Allied are **absent from the mortgage elective entirely.**

That is the single most useful fact in this review. The course mix already chosen for California sits in the least crowded corridor in the state — a third as crowded as the conventional bundle — and it is the corridor closest to the owner's own licensed profession.

**Second structural fact:** the market is more concentrated than the brand count suggests. Three California sponsor IDs — S0410 "Real Estate Express, a McKissock company," S0639 "Allied Real Estate Schools," S0693 "Colibri Real Estate" — are one owner. S0639 and S0693 are registered to the *same street address*, 399 South Spring Ave Suite 108, St. Louis. What looks like three competitors on Google is one company buying three positions on the results page.

## 3. What is universal — table stakes, not differentiation

**A pass guarantee is standard, not a feature.** Colibri and Allied both run "Pass or Don't Pay." Aceable: "Pass the test or your money back," plus a 30-day satisfaction trial. The CE Shop, US Realty Training: pass guarantee. RealEstateU: 30 days, "every penny, no questions asked." firsttuesday: 100% money back and you keep the books.

This corrects the recommendation in the Click2CE review, which treated the guarantee as a risky import. It is not optional positioning — entering this market without some refund promise reads as weakness. What varies, and what actually matters, is the *type*: a **satisfaction refund** inside a defined window (firsttuesday, RealEstateU, Aceable's 30-day trial) carries far less exposure than an **outcome guarantee** tied to passing a state exam, which requires proof of failure, completion thresholds, and a claims process. The safe version to adopt is the satisfaction window; the outcome guarantee still needs counsel.

**Tiered packages, three or four deep.** Every serious school does this: bare courses at the bottom, exam prep in the middle, tutoring or post-license or the first CE renewal at the top. Price spread top-to-bottom is 2–3×.

**Permanent discount anchoring.** Colibri shows $448.34 struck through to $269 with code CAREER40. The list price exists to be discounted. Buyers in this category expect it.

**Exam prep bundled into the middle tier** is the standard upsell, and it is also what makes Click2CE's stand-alone exam-prep model interesting: the nationals treat exam prep as an attachment to education, so a good stand-alone product faces weak direct competition.

**Everyone states the California timing rules.** Colibri, Kaplan, VanEd and US Realty all publish the 18-day minimum per course and the 54-day floor for all three. This is the marketing-visible face of RE 303's "2½ weeks" rule.

**A note on our own constant.** The competitors round to 18 days; our engine uses 420 hours (17.5 days), measured from the date of access. Checked against the controlling text: RE 303 page 3 says a single correspondence course may not be offered to be completed in less than **2½ weeks**, timed "from the date the student receives access to the course material to the date the final exam is successfully passed." 420 hours from access is exactly that, so the engine is correct and the competitors are rounding up for safety. The same page carries a second limit almost nobody advertises — **no more than two courses completed in any five-week period** — which our engine already enforces and which is the real ceiling on how fast a three-course bundle can be finished.

## 4. Where the real differentiation is

Only four things separate these schools once the table stakes are stripped away:

1. **Course-mix position.** Which elective, and therefore which sponsor list you appear on. This is a regulatory asset, not a marketing choice, and it is where we already hold an unusual position.
2. **Delivery quality.** Aceable is the only one competing seriously on interface. Everyone else ships text in a dated wrapper. Their $199 basic tier undercuts Kaplan's $399 while looking ten years newer.
3. **Local depth versus national reach.** firsttuesday and ADHI win California business by being *only* California — forms libraries, DRE application walkthroughs, local instructors. The nationals win on breadth. Both work; the middle does not.
4. **Channel.** US Realty Training built 75,000 YouTube subscribers and converts from video. Champions built forty years of Texas classroom reputation. Colibri buys three brands' worth of search results. Nobody wins this category on product alone.

## 5. What this means for us

- **Keep the mortgage elective, and say why it is there.** Fourteen approved sponsors statewide, no national brands, and it maps to the owner's own licensed practice. That is a defensible position that cannot be copied quickly — a competitor needs DRE approval for a course they do not have.
- **Match the tier structure, not the discount theatre.** Three tiers is the market's expected shape. A permanently struck-through list price is not something to imitate.
- **Adopt a satisfaction refund window; leave the outcome guarantee for counsel.** §3 explains the distinction.
- **The interface is a real opening.** Only one of fifteen competes on it.
- **Publish per-course approval numbers when they exist.** §3004(b) requires it for approved-course advertising, and the schools mostly show only a sponsor ID. Being the one that shows the course number, next to a link into the DRE database, is both required and differentiating.
- **Do not enter California on price.** The floor is already at $99 (Colibri after discount) and the mass market sits at $199–$450. The mortgage-elective corridor is not a price fight.

## 6. Conclusion

The established schools are older and larger, but they are not better built. Their advantages are approval portfolios, brand-buying, and channel — not instructional engineering or compliance. The regulator's own database shows the market is both more crowded than it looks at the top (105 correspondence Principles sponsors) and far emptier where we happen to stand (14 sponsors for our exact three courses, none of them national). The right reading is not "the market is full." It is "the front door is crowded, and we already chose a different door."
