02What was checked, and what it found

Hours

Every course's module hours were summed from the module files and compared against the engine constant and the public registry figure. All twenty-four agree. Three differences turned out to be deliberate, and were confirmed rather than flagged:

  • Arizona is 96, not 90. A.R.S. § 32-2124(B) requires ninety hours and § 32-2124(L) requires six more after completing them. Two course records, two certificates; the registry's 90 is the (B) figure only.
  • North Carolina's examination sits inside the 75. 72 instructional hours plus a three-hour end-of-course examination. Every other state here puts the examination outside the required hours, so the engine throws if that arithmetic drifts either way.
  • Maine's 55 is 3,000 instructional minutes plus 300 assessment minutes. The Commission fixes both, and the assessment share is checked against the Commission's own ceiling at import.

Each state also keeps its regulator's own unit of account rather than a project standard — Alaska counts contact hours of at least 50 minutes, Missouri counts clock hours of 60 minutes containing at least 50 of instruction, Michigan counts 240 instructional minutes per four-hour module, California counts instructional minutes. No state's unit is converted into another's anywhere in the build.

Question banks

All 24 banks and 2,464 items were walked: no duplicate prompt inside any bank, no item with fewer than three choices, no repeated or empty choice, no missing rationale, and no answer authored anywhere but index 0. Fifteen prompts sat below the character floor Arizona's bank already met, and were rewritten.

Approval language

All 224 module files carry an explicit regulatory-status line stating the course is not approved and no credit is available. A sweep for affirmative approval claims returned fourteen hits, every one of them instructional content about approval — lender approval, course approval rules, plat approval — and none claiming this course is approved.

Cross-state contamination

Every string in every registry entry and catalog course record was matched against the distinctive citation forms and agency names of all thirteen jurisdictions. One state was carrying another's regulator.

03Findings

Nine findings. Six fixed, one verified against the regulator's own page, one that this audit itself got wrong and corrected — and one, found last and left open, that is larger than any of them.

3.1

Arizona's public record carried manufactured regulatory content

Fixed

Three claims in the Arizona registry and catalog entries had no source behind them, and they were live on four generated pages: the national requirements table, the course catalog, the Arizona jurisdiction hub, and the Arizona salesperson course page.

Claim by claim

As publishedStatus
“SPS 2025.01 controls”Wrong state. SPS is Wisconsin's Department of Safety and Professional Services. Nothing in Arizona law is numbered that way.
“2:1 bank ratio”Invented. ADRE states no item-bank ratio.
“not on a student-supplied device”Invented — and it displaced the rule that is real.
“75% passing”Correct — A.A.C. R4-28-401(A)(1). Now cited.
“final outside instructional hours”Correct in substance — R4-28-404 excludes examination time from credit hours where the examination is used for overall evaluation. Now cited.

The device rule is the worst of the three, because the statute states a proctoring requirement that the invented one crowded out:

The applicant must complete a proctored examination on the live classroom course or the distance learning course IN PERSON.
A.R.S. § 32-2124(B)

Arizona's asynchronous-delivery flag moved from true to null. Nothing read establishes asynchronous delivery: § 32-2124(B) names in-person or synchronous remote segments, and R4-28-404 caps third-party recorded material at 20 percent of instructional time. The public requirements table now reads “Pending verification” for Arizona rather than “Yes”.

A guard now exists. A test asserts that no state's registry or catalog record cites another jurisdiction's statute, rule or agency, and refuses the two manufactured claims by shape rather than by wording. Its scope stops at the regulatory record on purpose: curriculum modules compare jurisdictions deliberately — the Arizona duties module records that Arizona is a fiduciary state where Washington's statute calls its duties “statutory duties and not fiduciary duties” and Oklahoma abrogated them — and that contrast is the lesson.

3.2

Fifteen bank prompts tested recall rather than reasoning

Fixed

Arizona's test enforced a floor on prompt length; no other state's did, so the rule never reached them. Fifteen bare definition prompts — “What is commingling?”, “What is a variance?”, “Which lien is voluntary?” — were rewritten to ask what the student must work out, keeping the same answer. One structural standard now applies to all 24 banks, so a rule adopted in one state reaches the rest.

3.3

Four states' catalog courses were not claimed by their own registry entry

Fixed

Alaska, Maine, Michigan and Virginia each had a catalog course whose state registry entry carried no course list at all. The linkage is now asserted in both directions.

3.4

Colorado's REC-33 references were untraced

Verified

REC-33 appeared in Colorado's registry entry without a primary source behind it. It is confirmed from the Division's own Broker Application Instruction Guide, which states that an applicant must “complete 168 course work and obtain proof of completion, (REC-33 certificate(s)) from your course provider”, and that “Exam results are good for one year from the date of each portion's passing score.” The page names PSI as the current exam vendor and states no passing score, item count, time limit or fee — and none is imported.

3.5

Colorado's asynchronous delivery claim was not supported

Corrected

Colorado's record asserted “DPOS-approved asynchronous/distance delivery”. What the Commission's Practical Applications outline actually says is that “Courses taught online must meet the requirements set forth in the Online Course Approval Checklist created by the Division of Real Estate.” That checklist was searched for again and is still not obtained; the form the Division does publish — the Real Estate Broker Live Online Virtual Classroom Checklist, Rev. 2025-05 — is continuing-education-scoped by its own terms and is not the same document. Colorado's delivery entry now names the unobtained checklist.

3.6

One state does not state what it did not read

Corrected, then open

This finding was reported wrongly in the first pass. The audit matched the literal phrase “not read”, and so missed Maine's heading — what has not been read from primary text — and Virginia's — what is not yet read from primary text. Both sections existed. Reported as “Maine and Virginia have neither”, which was false.

Twelve of the thirteen states state their boundary. Eight head it what was NOT read, and is therefore stated nowhere in this build; Alaska and Michigan head it open questions that must be answered before filing. California was the only state with no source register at all. It now has one, and writing it produced finding 3.9 — which is larger than the missing register was.

Checking the two states this finding wrongly accused turned up two real defects instead.

Virginia's boundary section contradicted itself. It listed §§ 54.1-2131 through 54.1-2135, Title 55.1 Chapters 7 and 12, and Title 36 Chapter 5.1 as not yet read — and the section immediately below it opened “The sections listed as ‘not yet read' above were read the same day.” A reviewer working from that list would have been told the build was silent on material it teaches from primary text.

In both states the primary text was read but never recorded. Maine's §§ 13067-A, 13178, 13179, 13197, 13271, 13274, 13277, 13279 and 13280, and Virginia's §§ 54.1-2101.1, 54.1-2133, 54.1-2134, 54.1-2135, 54.1-2139 and 18VAC135-20-155, -240, -260 and -335, were all fetched during the build, and both curricula are written correctly from them — Virginia already taught ordinary care rather than reasonable care, the 90-day property management default, and the ten-day board production rule. Only the reading files never caught up. Both are now recorded, and both boundary sections rewritten to the standard the other states meet.

3.7

Virginia flattened the one asymmetry in Article 3

Fixed

Module 06 said §§ 54.1-2133 and 54.1-2134 “carry the same duty architecture into leasing.” They do not, in the one respect that changes what a licensee does first:

Enter into a brokerage agreement with the prospective tenant PRIOR TO SHOWING PROPERTY to such prospective tenant.
§ 54.1-2134 A 1

Virginia imposes that requirement on tenant representation exactly as § 54.1-2132 A 1 imposes it on buyer representation, and imposes it on neither § 54.1-2133 (landlord) nor § 54.1-2135 (management). On the tenant and buyer side the agreement comes before the showing; on the landlord and owner side it does not.

A second leasing duty had no coverage at all: § 54.1-2133 B's lead-free pipe disclosure, which carries a federal definition by citation — 42 U.S.C. § 300g-6 — and must be made in writing.

3.8

Maine deferred a statute to rules that the statute itself decides

Fixed

The trust-account hour routed the whole of § 13178 to the Commission's rules. The statute fixes five things itself — a federally insured account held for the sole purpose of client money; the account, the withdrawal orders and every cheque naming the agency and identified as a real estate trust account; freedom from trustee process except for the depositors themselves; and the fee rule:

… shall withdraw from the accounts all fees due within 30 days after but not until consummation or termination of the transaction when the designated broker makes or causes to be made a full accounting to the broker's principal.
32 M.R.S. § 13178

What the statute genuinely delegates now stands separately, so the refusals that remain are the real ones: the trust-account cushion, the deposit timing, the record retention period, the relationship-form fields, and every sanction figure — all in Chapter 360 or in 10 M.R.S. § 8003, neither read.

3.9

California's 386,565 words carry no citation to California law

Open

Writing California's source register produced the most significant finding of this audit. The build is three 45-hour courses — Principles, Practice, and Mortgage Loan Brokering and Lending — across 45 lesson assignments, the largest in the project by a wide margin. Not one of those 45 lessons cites a California statutory or regulatory section. A sweep of every four- and five-digit number in the whole California curriculum returns calendar years and arithmetic, and nothing else.

On the approval side the build is fully traceable. Every structural constant rests on primary regulatory text obtained from the Department:

On the substantive side it rests on nothing that was read. The instruction on agency, trust funds, disclosure, finance, fair housing and licence law is written without a citation to the California law it describes, because the Business and Professions Code was not read in its own text — none of it — and neither were the Commissioner's Regulations beyond §§ 3000, 3002 and 3003, the Civil Code, FEHA, the Unruh Act, or California's finance and lending statutes. That last omission is the sharpest: one of the three courses is Mortgage Loan Brokering and Lending.

Nothing has been manufactured. No California statutory figure appears anywhere. Where a retention period is discussed at all, a firm that “discards loan files three years after closing, on the shortest period it could find” is presented as the error, not the rule. California states no retention period because the Department states none — its record rule is open-ended:

… as long as the school is in business, the school must furnish a certificate to the student upon request.
Course Provider Handbook, Rev. 01/2026

What it means for the review. Every other state can be checked citation by citation against a quoted provision. California cannot. The question to put to a California reviewer is therefore not is the citation right but is the statement of California law correct — and the register says so in those words. Closing the gap means reading the Business and Professions Code and the Commissioner's Regulations directly and citing them through the curriculum; until then no California module states a section number, and none may be added without the reading behind it.

05Standing rules

Not aspirational. Each of these is enforced by a test, or by an import-time invariant that throws.

  1. No course, page or record claims approval, and no enrollment opens, until an approval record exists. Payment status never determines completion, examination eligibility, credit or a certificate.
  2. No rule, fee, form version, passing score, proctor requirement or retention period appears anywhere unless a source states it. Where a source is silent, the record says the source is silent.
  3. Answer keys never leave the server. Each state engine strips the correct choice and the rationale before an item is delivered, and a test asserts no key reaches the published site.
  4. Marketing material and portal browsing never count as instructional time.
  5. Reviewer activity never contaminates student records.
  6. Completion and audit records are never silently edited.
  7. Where a StateCourse standard fills a regulator's silence — Alaska's eight-hour daily cap is the clearest case — the record labels it a provider standard and forbids describing it as a state requirement.

06What the reviewer is asked

The build can check arithmetic, structure and internal consistency. It cannot check whether the law is stated correctly. That is what the review is for.

Is anything here wrong as a matter of this state's law?

Each state's readings file quotes the primary text it relied on, so a disagreement can be traced to a specific quotation rather than argued in the abstract.

Is anything stated that the cited source does not actually say?

This is the failure mode finding 3.1 is an instance of, and the one a reviewer catches that no test can.

Is anything missing that the state requires and this build is silent about?

Section 4 and each state's own “what was NOT read” section are the place to start.

Reviewers should receive, per state: the readings file under <state>/regulatory/, the curriculum modules under <state>/<course>/curriculum/, and the course engine under config/, which states the arithmetic and the completion gates in one place.
