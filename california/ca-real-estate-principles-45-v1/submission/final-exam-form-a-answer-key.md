## Items, with the material reference for each answer

### 1. A landowner sells the right to extract minerals beneath her parcel while continuing to live on it. What has she done in terms of the bundle of rights?

A. Converted the parcel from real property to personal property.
B. Created a leasehold estate in the buyer of the mineral rights.
C. Terminated her fee simple estate, because the bundle cannot be divided.
**D. Separated and conveyed one right out of the bundle while retaining the rest.**

> **Correct answer: D.** The bundle of rights can be separated and conveyed individually. Selling one stick neither destroys the estate nor changes the classification of the land.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (The real estate profession and California regulatory structure), section 1.3

### 2. Why does a change in credit conditions move real estate prices even when nothing has happened to any particular property?

**A. Most transactions depend on financing, so credit conditions transmit directly into what buyers can pay.**
B. Appraisers are required to apply a rate adjustment to every comparable sale.
C. Sellers are required to adjust list prices when rates change.
D. Assessed values are recalculated automatically whenever interest rates change.

> **Correct answer: A.** Financing dependence is a characteristic of the market. Buying power moves with credit conditions, and prices follow, without any change to the properties themselves.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (The real estate profession and California regulatory structure), section 1.5

### 3. Which fact determines whether an activity requires a real estate license?

A. Whether the person described himself to the public as an agent.
B. Whether the person kept the compensation or passed it to someone else.
**C. What the person actually did and whether it was done for compensation or the expectation of it.**
D. Whether the transaction ultimately closed.

> **Correct answer: C.** Licensure turns on the activity performed and the compensation element, not on labels, outcomes, or what happened to the money afterward.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (The real estate profession and California regulatory structure), section 2.3

### 4. A broker delegates file review to an experienced salesperson and a defect later appears in a reviewed file. What is the broker's position?

**A. The supervisory duty remains the broker's; delegating the task did not transfer responsibility for it.**
B. The reviewing salesperson has assumed the supervisory duty by accepting the assignment.
C. The duty is shared equally once the delegation is documented in writing.
D. The broker is relieved of responsibility because supervision may be delegated to any licensee.

> **Correct answer: A.** Supervision is an affirmative duty of the responsible broker. A task can be delegated; the responsibility for its performance cannot.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (The real estate profession and California regulatory structure), section 3.3

### 5. A buyer asks his agent whether a contingency clause still protects him if he cancels on Friday. Applying the scope test, what should the agent do?

A. Answer directly, because the agent has read the contract and knows what it says.
B. Decline to say anything at all, since any comment on a contract is the practice of law.
C. Answer only if the agent puts the answer in writing, which converts it into permissible information.
**D. State what the document provides procedurally and what the deadline is, and route the question of the client's legal rights to counsel.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Explaining what the document provides for is within scope; stating its legal effect on the client's rights is not. Silence is not required and writing it down does not change its character.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (The real estate profession and California regulatory structure), section 4.2

### 6. A licensee cites a passage from a trade association publication as the reason a disclosure is not required. What is wrong with that reasoning?

A. Nothing, because industry publications are incorporated into the Commissioner's Regulations.
B. The reasoning fails only if the publication is more than one year old.
**C. Publications and forms are not sources of law; the obligation is set by statute and regulation regardless of what a publication says.**
D. Nothing, provided the publication is current and issued by a recognized association.

> **Correct answer: C.** Forms and publications describe practice; they do not create or excuse legal obligations, and currency does not convert one into a source of law.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (The real estate profession and California regulatory structure), section 5.3

### 7. Two tests do most of the work in deciding whether an item has become a fixture. Which are they?

A. The seller's stated sentimental attachment and the buyer's expectation.
**B. The method of attachment and the intent of the parties as objectively evidenced.**
C. The cost of the item and the length of time it has been in place.
D. Whether the item is listed in the purchase agreement and whether it is insured.

> **Correct answer: B.** Attachment and objectively evidenced intent decide most cases. Cost, duration, insurance, and a party's private sentiment do not control.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Real property, personal property, and fixtures), section 2.3

### 8. Custom window coverings were cut to fit each window of a particular house. Which test most directly supports treating them as fixtures?

**A. Adaptability, because they were fitted to this property specifically and have little use elsewhere.**
B. Relationship of the parties, because the seller installed them personally.
C. Method of attachment, because fabric is affixed to the structure with permanent fasteners.
D. Trade fixture doctrine, because the coverings serve the use of the premises.

> **Correct answer: A.** Adaptation to the specific property is the controlling factor for items custom-fitted to it. The trade fixture doctrine concerns items a tenant installs for a business.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Real property, personal property, and fixtures), section 2.5

### 9. A commercial tenant installs display shelving and refrigeration units to operate a grocery. Under the trade fixture rule, what is the general position at lease end?

A. They became the landlord's property on installation because they are attached to the building.
B. They belong to the landlord unless the tenant recorded a notice of the installation.
C. They may be removed only if the landlord consented in writing before installation.
**D. The tenant may generally remove them within the time the lease allows, repairing damage caused by removal.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Trade fixtures installed for the tenant's business are generally removable by the tenant, subject to timing and repair obligations, and the lease commonly settles the details.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Real property, personal property, and fixtures), section 3.2

### 10. A seller digs up mature rose bushes on the morning of the buyer's final walkthrough. What has occurred?

A. A fixture dispute, resolved by the method of attachment test.
**B. Real property was severed and removed after the buyer contracted for the property including it.**
C. Personal property was removed, since plants are always personal property.
D. A permitted removal, because landscaping is excluded from every purchase agreement by default.

> **Correct answer: B.** Perennial growth is part of the real property until severed. Severing it after the contract removes something the buyer contracted for; the timing of severance is the operative fact.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Real property, personal property, and fixtures), section 4.4

### 11. A preliminary report shows that mineral rights were severed from the parcel decades ago. What follows for the buyer?

A. The buyer acquires the mineral rights at closing because they are appurtenant to the surface.
B. The severance affects taxation only and has no effect on use of the surface.
**C. Another party holds those rights and may hold rights of access; the buyer should obtain the recorded instrument and route its interpretation.**
D. The severance lapsed automatically because no extraction has occurred.

> **Correct answer: C.** A severed mineral estate remains with its holder and may carry access rights. Non-use does not extinguish it automatically, and the recorded instrument states its scope.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Real property, personal property, and fixtures), section 5.4

### 12. At the walkthrough, the parties disagree about whether a shed resting on gravel conveys. What is the licensee's correct role?

A. Apply the fixture tests and tell the parties which one prevails, since the analysis is objective.
B. Advise the buyer to accept the seller's position to preserve the closing date.
C. Tell the parties the shed conveys because anything on the land at closing conveys.
**D. Identify the issue, decline to declare who is right, and take it to the broker while the parties consider their options.**

> **Correct answer: D.** The classification of a contested item is a legal question. The licensee identifies it and escalates rather than announcing an outcome the parties will rely on.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Real property, personal property, and fixtures), section 6.6

### 13. A life tenant contracts to sell 'the property' to a buyer. What can the buyer actually acquire from him?

**A. An interest measured by the life that measures the estate, which ends when that life ends.**
B. Fee simple absolute, because a life tenant is the present owner of record.
C. Nothing, because a life estate is not transferable under any circumstances.
D. A leasehold estate for a term equal to the life tenant's remaining life expectancy.

> **Correct answer: A.** A grantor cannot convey more than he holds. What passes is measured by the same life, and it terminates with it.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Estates, ownership, and common interests), section 2.3

### 14. Which consequence most sharply distinguishes joint tenancy from tenancy in common?

A. Only joint tenants may hold unequal shares in the property.
**B. On the death of a co-owner, a joint tenancy interest passes by right of survivorship rather than through the decedent's estate.**
C. Only joint tenancy permits more than two owners.
D. Only tenants in common may convey their interest during life.

> **Correct answer: B.** Survivorship is the operative distinction. Unequal shares are characteristic of tenancy in common, and both forms permit lifetime transfer and more than two owners.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Estates, ownership, and common interests), section 3.2

### 15. One of two joint tenants conveys his interest to a third party during his lifetime. What is the general effect?

**A. The unities supporting the joint tenancy are disturbed as to that interest, and the transferee generally takes as a tenant in common.**
B. The remaining joint tenant automatically acquires the whole interest immediately.
C. The joint tenancy continues unchanged, with the transferee substituted as a joint tenant.
D. The conveyance is void, because a joint tenant cannot transfer without the other's consent.

> **Correct answer: A.** A lifetime transfer severs as to the transferred interest. It is neither void, nor an immediate accretion to the other owner, nor a substitution preserving the joint tenancy.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Estates, ownership, and common interests), section 3.4

### 16. A married couple asks their listing agent which form of ownership they should use for a property they are buying. What is the correct response?

A. Recommend the form producing the simplest transfer at death, since that is the usual objective.
B. Decline to discuss ownership at all, since the subject is outside the transaction.
C. Recommend the form most commonly used in the county, since it is the market standard.
**D. Explain that the choice carries legal and tax consequences and refer them to counsel and a tax advisor.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Vesting affects survivorship, creditors, and taxation. The licensee explains that the question exists and routes it; recommending a form is advice he is not qualified to give.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Estates, ownership, and common interests), section 4.5

### 17. Which document in a common interest development package is most predictive of a future special assessment?

A. The association's rules governing pets and parking.
B. The declaration of covenants, conditions, and restrictions.
**C. The reserve study, read together with the association's budget and recent minutes.**
D. The insurance certificate for the common area.

> **Correct answer: C.** A thinly funded reserve is a special assessment that has not been announced yet, and the minutes are where it first appears. The other documents govern use and coverage rather than funding.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Estates, ownership, and common interests), section 5.2

### 18. A seller says the property is 'in my trust.' Who has authority to convey?

A. The person who created the trust, in every case.
**B. The currently serving trustee, whose identity is established from the trust instrument rather than from the seller's description.**
C. The seller personally, since a trust is not a separate owner.
D. Any named beneficiary of the trust.

> **Correct answer: B.** Title is held by the trustee in that capacity, and the office may have passed to a successor. The instrument, not the conversation, establishes who currently holds it.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Estates, ownership, and common interests), section 6.2

### 19. Most California residential property in recorded subdivisions is described by which method?

**A. Lot and block, by reference to a recorded subdivision map.**
B. The government survey system, by township, range, and section.
C. The assessor's parcel number assigned by the county.
D. Metes and bounds, running from a monument and closing back to it.

> **Correct answer: A.** Lot and block by reference to a recorded map is the residential norm. The assessor's parcel number is a tax identifier, not a legal description.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Land description, measurement, and boundaries), section 3.1

### 20. Why is an assessor's parcel number not a substitute for the legal description in a purchase agreement?

**A. The assessor's records exist for taxation, and a tax parcel does not necessarily describe the land conveyed.**
B. Assessor's parcel numbers are not public records.
C. A parcel number may only be used for improved property.
D. Assessor's parcel numbers change every fiscal year.

> **Correct answer: A.** The assessor maintains parcels for taxation. The description that conveys is the one in the deed, and using the parcel number in its place is a recurring source of error.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Land description, measurement, and boundaries), section 4.1

### 21. A seller states that a fence has marked the property line for twenty years. What has he described?

A. The boundary of record, since long acquiescence establishes it conclusively.
**B. A boundary in use, which may or may not correspond to the boundary of record.**
C. A survey monument, which controls over the recorded description.
D. An easement by prescription in favor of the neighbor.

> **Correct answer: B.** The boundary in use and the boundary of record are different things. Whether long use has any legal effect is a legal question, not something the fence itself establishes.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Land description, measurement, and boundaries), section 5.1

### 22. A buyer asks whether the title policy will protect him if the fence turns out to be three feet onto the neighbor's land. What is the accurate answer?

A. Yes, provided the fence appears in the preliminary report.
B. Yes, the policy insures the physical boundaries of the parcel as fenced.
**C. A title policy is not a survey, and matters that an accurate survey would disclose are commonly outside standard coverage — the buyer should obtain a survey and review coverage with the title officer.**
D. No, because title policies never cover boundary matters under any circumstances.

> **Correct answer: C.** Title insurance addresses matters of record and its own terms; it does not perform the function of a survey. The precise coverage question belongs to the title officer.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Land description, measurement, and boundaries), section 5.3

### 23. A licensee is preparing a listing and has three different figures for the property's area. What is the professional handling?

A. Use the largest figure, since it is the one the seller supplied.
B. Omit the area entirely, since any statement of it creates liability.
C. Average the three figures and state the result as approximate.
**D. Report a figure with its source named, and do not reconcile sources into a new number no source stands behind.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Every measurement carries its basis. Averaging or adjusting produces a number the licensee is asserting on his own authority; omission is not required.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Land description, measurement, and boundaries), section 6.6

### 24. A parcel is stated at 2.5 acres. Approximately how many square feet is that?

A. 43,560
B. 1,089,000
**C. 108,900**
D. 10,890

> **Correct answer: C.** An acre is 43,560 square feet, so 2.5 acres is 108,900. The distractors are the one-acre figure and order-of-magnitude errors of the kind unit conversions produce.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Land description, measurement, and boundaries), section 6.4

### 25. Which best distinguishes a general lien from a specific lien?

A. A general lien expires automatically, while a specific lien continues indefinitely.
**B. A general lien attaches to the debtor's property generally, while a specific lien attaches to an identified parcel.**
C. A general lien arises voluntarily, while a specific lien arises involuntarily.
D. A general lien must be recorded, while a specific lien need not be.

> **Correct answer: B.** The general-versus-specific axis concerns what property is reached. Voluntariness is a separate axis, and recording and duration are separate questions again.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Encumbrances, liens, easements, and restrictions), section 1.3

### 26. An easement benefits an adjoining parcel rather than a person. What follows when that parcel is sold?

**A. The easement is appurtenant and generally runs with the land to the new owner.**
B. The easement terminates because the benefited owner has changed.
C. The easement converts to a license revocable at will.
D. The easement must be re-recorded by the new owner to remain effective.

> **Correct answer: A.** An appurtenant easement benefits the dominant parcel and passes with it. An easement in gross benefits a person or entity rather than a parcel.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Encumbrances, liens, easements, and restrictions), section 3.2

### 27. What most clearly distinguishes a license from an easement?

A. A license must be in writing, while an easement may be oral.
B. A license is always exclusive, while an easement is always shared.
C. A license runs with the land, while an easement is personal to the holder.
**D. A license is generally personal and revocable, while an easement is an interest in land.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Revocability and the absence of an interest in land are what mark a license. The last option reverses the two.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Encumbrances, liens, easements, and restrictions), section 3.5

### 28. A recorded declaration prohibits a use that the zoning ordinance permits. What is the practical result for a buyer who wants that use?

A. The declaration controls only if it was recorded after the zoning was adopted.
B. The buyer may proceed if he obtains a variance from the jurisdiction.
**C. Both systems apply independently, and the private restriction can defeat a use the zoning allows.**
D. Zoning controls, because a public ordinance supersedes a private instrument.

> **Correct answer: C.** Public land use controls and private recorded restrictions operate separately. Satisfying one does not satisfy the other, and a variance addresses only the public side.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Encumbrances, liens, easements, and restrictions), section 4.2

### 29. Why does the order of recording matter so much to the outcome of a lien dispute?

A. It determines the interest rate each lienholder may charge.
**B. The recording system establishes notice and, with it, the priority that determines who is paid and in what order.**
C. It determines which lien is valid; later-recorded liens are void.
D. It determines which lienholder may foreclose; only the last may do so.

> **Correct answer: B.** Recording gives constructive notice and orders priority. Later liens are not void — they are subordinate, which is what decides the money.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Encumbrances, liens, easements, and restrictions), section 5.3

### 30. A preliminary report lists an item under 'requirements' rather than as an exception. What does that mean?

A. It is a matter excluded from coverage that the buyer must accept.
B. It is a lien that will be paid from the seller's proceeds at closing.
C. It is an informational note carrying no consequence for closing.
**D. It is a condition the title company will insist be satisfied before it issues the policy.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Requirements are conditions to issuance; exceptions are matters not covered. Confusing the two produces a file that is surprised at title clearance.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Encumbrances, liens, easements, and restrictions), section 6.3

### 31. A city adopts an ordinance restricting building heights near an airport approach path. Which government power is being exercised?

**A. Police power, regulating use in the interest of public health, safety, and welfare.**
B. Taxation, because height limits affect assessed value.
C. Eminent domain, because the restriction reduces what owners may build.
D. Escheat, because unused development capacity reverts to the public.

> **Correct answer: A.** Regulation of use without taking title is the police power. Eminent domain involves taking property, and escheat concerns property passing to the state when there is no owner.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 06 (Government powers, land use, and environmental controls), section 1.2

### 32. Which pair of elements is essential to an exercise of eminent domain?

A. A public hearing and the consent of the affected owner.
B. A recorded restriction and a zoning amendment.
**C. A public use and the payment of just compensation.**
D. A finding of blight and a court order of foreclosure.

> **Correct answer: C.** Public use and just compensation are the defining elements. Owner consent is not required — that is what distinguishes the power from a purchase.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 06 (Government powers, land use, and environmental controls), section 1.3

### 33. A building lawfully constructed under a prior ordinance no longer complies with current zoning. What is its status?

**A. A nonconforming use, whose continuation and limits are governed by the ordinance's own provisions.**
B. A variance, granted automatically by operation of the ordinance change.
C. A conforming use, because zoning changes apply only to new construction in every case.
D. An illegal use requiring immediate removal.

> **Correct answer: A.** Lawful use predating the change becomes nonconforming, and the ordinance itself sets what may continue, expand, or lapse. It is neither automatically illegal nor a variance.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 06 (Government powers, land use, and environmental controls), section 2.4

### 34. A buyer says he will 'just get a variance' if the ordinance does not permit his intended use. Why is that a dangerous assumption for a licensee to encourage?

A. Variances are prohibited in California residential zones.
B. A variance can only be sought by the owner of record after closing.
C. A variance would convert the property to a nonconforming use.
**D. Relief mechanisms are discretionary processes with standards, timelines, and no guaranteed outcome.**

> **Correct answer: D.** A variance or conditional use permit is a process with standards and a hearing, not an entitlement. Encouraging reliance on it is how buyers remove contingencies on a plan that later fails.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 06 (Government powers, land use, and environmental controls), section 2.3

### 35. A seller discloses that a bedroom addition was built without permits. What is the licensee's correct handling of the marketing?

**A. Verify what the records support before publishing a room count or square footage, and disclose the condition.**
B. Advertise the room count the seller states, since the seller has personal knowledge of his own house.
C. Omit the addition from all marketing and disclosures to avoid drawing attention to it.
D. Advertise the addition as permitted, because a permit can be obtained retroactively.

> **Correct answer: A.** The published figures must be supportable from a source, and the condition is disclosable. Concealment and a claim the records do not support are both misrepresentations.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 06 (Government powers, land use, and environmental controls), section 3.3

### 36. A natural hazard report states the property is not within a mapped zone. What is the accurate reading?

A. The report replaces the need to investigate insurance availability.
**B. The determination is based on maps at a scale with approximate boundaries that are revised over time; it is not a statement that no risk exists.**
C. The property carries no natural hazard risk of any kind.
D. The determination is conclusive and cannot change for the life of the property.

> **Correct answer: B.** Mapped determinations are qualified by scale and revision. Reading 'not in a zone' as 'not at risk' is the most common misreading, and insurance availability is a separate inquiry.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 06 (Government powers, land use, and environmental controls), section 5.1

### 37. Which of the following can create an agency relationship without any signed agreement?

A. Delivery of a written disclosure form to the party.
B. Showing a property listed by another brokerage.
**C. Conduct — acting for a person and being permitted to do so, such that a reasonable person would understand representation exists.**
D. The payment of a commission by one party to the brokerage.

> **Correct answer: C.** Agency may arise by conduct as well as by express agreement or ratification. Payment does not create it, and the disclosure form is notice rather than the relationship itself.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 07 (Agency and brokerage relationships), section 1.2

### 38. A seller instructs his listing agent to say nothing about a known roof leak. Which analysis is correct?

A. The agent may stay silent as long as he makes no affirmative false statement.
B. The presence of a buyer's agent transfers the disclosure duty to the buyer's side.
C. Confidentiality controls, because the duty runs to the principal and outranks duties to non-clients.
**D. Confidentiality protects the seller's negotiating position, not concealment of a material physical condition, so the agent must not follow the instruction.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Honesty and disclosure of material facts run to everyone and set the limit of confidentiality. Careful silence is concealment, and the duty is not transferred by the other side having representation.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 07 (Agency and brokerage relationships), section 2.4

### 39. A licensee misses a contingency deadline through inattention, with no dishonesty involved. Which duty is breached?

A. Confidentiality.
B. Loyalty.
**C. Reasonable care and diligence.**
D. Accounting.

> **Correct answer: C.** Careless performance breaches the care duty, which is why most claims against licensees are negligence claims rather than fraud claims.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 07 (Agency and brokerage relationships), section 2.5

### 40. One brokerage represents both the seller and the buyer in a transaction. What does the law require?

A. Oral acknowledgment from both parties at the time the offer is presented.
B. Written consent from the seller only, since the listing agreement is the brokerage's contract.
**C. Informed written consent from both parties, obtained before the brokerage acts on both sides.**
D. No consent, provided two different salespersons handle the two sides.

> **Correct answer: C.** Both parties must consent, in writing and informed. Because the agency belongs to the brokerage, using two salespersons does not avoid the dual agency.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 07 (Agency and brokerage relationships), section 3.2

### 41. A buyer's agent is compensated from the seller's side of the transaction. What does that establish about representation?

A. That a dual agency has been created by operation of the payment.
B. That the buyer must consent in writing to the agent's compensation before representation begins.
C. That the agent represents the seller, because the seller is paying.
**D. Nothing — the agency disclosure and confirmation establish who represented whom, not the source of payment.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Source of payment does not determine representation. The disclosure, election, and confirmation do — though the compensation arrangement must still be disclosed.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 07 (Agency and brokerage relationships), section 6.2

### 42. An agency disclosure is delivered to a buyer on the day the purchase agreement is signed, five weeks after the working relationship began. What is the defect?

**A. The document exists but arrived after the decisions it was meant to inform, and late delivery is not cured by delivery.**
