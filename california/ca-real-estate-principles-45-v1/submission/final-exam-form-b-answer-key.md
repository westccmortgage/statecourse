## Items, with the material reference for each answer

### 1. Which statement correctly distinguishes land from real property?

**A. Land is the surface, what lies beneath, and the space above; real property is land together with what is affixed and appurtenant to it and the rights it carries.**
B. Land is the legal concept and real property is the physical concept.
C. The terms are interchangeable in California law.
D. Land includes improvements, while real property is the unimproved surface only.

> **Correct answer: A.** Real property is the broader concept, taking in the land plus attachments, appurtenances, and the bundle of rights.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (The real estate profession and California regulatory structure), section 1.4

### 2. What is the function of the Real Estate Recovery Account?

A. To reimburse licensees for losses caused by clients who fail to pay commissions.
B. To fund the Department's investigation and enforcement operations.
C. To insure brokerages against errors and omissions claims.
**D. To provide a limited source of recovery to a member of the public who obtains an unsatisfied judgment against a licensee for specified conduct.**

> **Correct answer: D.** The account exists for injured members of the public who cannot collect a judgment. It is not licensee insurance, an operating fund, or an errors and omissions policy.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (The real estate profession and California regulatory structure), section 2.5

### 3. Which of the following may a salesperson NOT do?

A. Present an offer to a seller on behalf of a buyer.
B. Prepare a comparative market analysis for a seller.
**C. Accept compensation for licensed activity directly from a party rather than through the employing broker.**
D. Hold a listing agreement in the brokerage's name.

> **Correct answer: C.** Compensation for licensed activity flows through the employing broker. The other three are ordinary salesperson activity performed under the broker's authority.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (The real estate profession and California regulatory structure), section 3.5

### 4. In the hierarchy of California real estate law, what is the relationship between the Real Estate Law and the Commissioner's Regulations?

A. The regulations are advisory interpretations that carry no independent force.
**B. The statute is enacted by the Legislature and the regulations are adopted under authority the statute grants; both are binding.**
C. The statute applies to brokers and the regulations apply to salespersons.
D. The regulations supersede the statute where the two address the same subject.

> **Correct answer: B.** Regulations are adopted under statutory authority and are binding. They neither supersede the statute nor divide by license level.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (The real estate profession and California regulatory structure), section 5.1

### 5. Which of the following will escrow NOT do?

**A. Notice that a term the parties agreed to never reached escrow and adjust the closing accordingly.**
B. Prepare a closing statement from the documents and instructions it holds.
C. Hold funds and documents pending satisfaction of the conditions in its instructions.
D. Follow written instructions signed by both parties.

> **Correct answer: A.** Escrow is neutral and acts on the instructions it holds. Detecting an agreement it never received is not its function, which is why transmission is the licensee's task.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (The real estate profession and California regulatory structure), section 6.3

### 6. A licensee receives a document request in a Department investigation. What is the correct first step?

A. Organize and clean up the file before producing it so the record reads clearly.
B. Decline to respond until served with a formal accusation.
C. Answer immediately in his own words to demonstrate cooperation.
**D. Preserve everything, respond with the broker and, where appropriate, counsel, and produce what is requested rather than answering personally and quickly.**

> **Correct answer: D.** The process is largely documentary. Preservation is immediate and total, and an improvised personal response is how licensees say what they did not need to say.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (The real estate profession and California regulatory structure), section 2.6

### 7. Which task in a residential transaction has no assigned owner outside the brokerage?

**A. Building and watching the deadline calendar the contract creates.**
B. Underwriting the buyer's loan.
C. Searching title and issuing the preliminary report.
D. Performing the appraisal.

> **Correct answer: A.** Title, underwriting, and valuation each have a named owner outside the brokerage. Deadlines belong to no participant by default, which is why they are the most failure-prone element.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (The real estate profession and California regulatory structure), section 6.5

### 8. Standing timber is cut and stacked for sale. What has happened to its classification?

A. It moved from personal property to real property by annexation.
**B. It moved from real property to personal property by severance.**
C. Its classification is determined by the intent of the buyer rather than by severance.
D. It remained real property, because it originated from the land.

> **Correct answer: B.** Severance converts real property to personal property; annexation moves an item the other way. Origin does not fix the classification permanently.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Real property, personal property, and fixtures), section 1.2

### 9. Which factor does the 'relationship of the parties' test consider?

**A. Whether the person who installed the item was an owner or a tenant, because that context bears on whether it was meant to become part of the realty.**
B. Whether the listing and selling agents work for the same brokerage.
C. Whether the installer was licensed for the work performed.
D. Whether the buyer and seller are related by blood or marriage.

> **Correct answer: A.** The test looks at the installer's relationship to the property — owner or tenant — as evidence of intent. The distractors describe unrelated relationships.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Real property, personal property, and fixtures), section 2.6

### 10. Crops requiring annual cultivation are generally treated how?

**A. As personal property, even while growing and attached to the soil.**
B. As trade fixtures, because they serve the agricultural business.
C. As real property until harvested, like all growing things.
D. As personal property only after the harvest is complete.

> **Correct answer: A.** Annually cultivated crops are treated as personal property while growing, which distinguishes them from perennial growth such as trees and shrubs.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Real property, personal property, and fixtures), section 4.3

### 11. A buyer is purchasing rural acreage and intends to build. Which water question matters most at intake?

A. Whether the seller's water bills have been paid current.
B. Whether the parcel has ever flooded.
C. Whether the county assesses a water availability fee.
**D. Whether a confirmed and lawful supply exists for the intended use, and on what basis it is held or shared.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Without a confirmed supply the buyer may have purchased land he cannot use as intended. Billing, fees, and flood history are separate questions.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Real property, personal property, and fixtures), section 5.2

### 12. What is the purpose of naming items in the purchase agreement?

**A. To settle in advance the items whose classification is genuinely uncertain, so the analysis is never needed.**
B. To transfer title to personal property without a bill of sale in every case.
C. To satisfy a statutory requirement that all fixtures be itemized.
D. To create a complete inventory of every item present at the property.

> **Correct answer: A.** The naming list targets the uncertain items — the ones the fixture tests would have to resolve. A complete inventory is neither required nor useful.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Real property, personal property, and fixtures), section 6.2

### 13. Why can the same item's classification be contested by parties who were not at the negotiation?

A. Because any third party may intervene in a purchase agreement dispute.
B. Because personal property disputes are resolved in a different forum than real property disputes.
**C. Because the classification also determines what a security instrument covers, how the transfer is documented, and how the item is taxed.**
D. Because classification is determined by the county assessor for all purposes.

> **Correct answer: C.** One classification answers four separate systems' questions, so a lender, an assessor, or a lienholder can have a stake the buyer and seller never considered.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Real property, personal property, and fixtures), section 1.5

### 14. A tenancy that continues from period to period until one party gives proper notice describes which leasehold estate?

**A. An estate from period to period.**
B. An estate for years.
C. An estate at will.
D. An estate at sufferance.

> **Correct answer: A.** A periodic tenancy renews automatically until notice. An estate for years has a fixed term, and an estate at sufferance describes a holdover after the right to possession ended.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Estates, ownership, and common interests), section 1.2

### 15. An estate that may terminate if a stated condition occurs is best described as which of the following?

A. A fee simple absolute.
B. A life estate.
C. A leasehold estate.
**D. A defeasible fee.**

> **Correct answer: D.** A defeasible fee is a fee that can end on a stated event. A fee simple absolute has no such limitation, and a life estate is measured by a life rather than by a condition.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Estates, ownership, and common interests), section 2.2

### 16. Which act is generally outside a life tenant's authority?

A. Leasing the property for a term within the life estate.
B. Collecting rents produced by the property.
**C. Committing waste that permanently impairs the value of the future interest.**
D. Occupying the property.

> **Correct answer: C.** A life tenant has the use and its income but may not impair the interest that follows. The other three are ordinary incidents of the estate.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Estates, ownership, and common interests), section 2.4

### 17. Which set states the four unities of a joint tenancy?

A. Interest, possession, consideration, and capacity.
**B. Time, title, interest, and possession.**
C. Time, transfer, intent, and possession.
D. Title, interest, survivorship, and recording.

> **Correct answer: B.** Time, title, interest, and possession must all be present. Survivorship is the consequence of the unities rather than one of them.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Estates, ownership, and common interests), section 3.3

### 18. Co-owners cannot agree on the disposition of jointly held property. What remedy addresses the deadlock?

**A. A partition action, which may divide the property or order it sold and the proceeds divided.**
B. A quiet title action, which determines the validity of competing claims to title.
C. A specific performance action against the non-consenting owner.
D. An action for adverse possession by the owner in possession.

> **Correct answer: A.** Partition is the remedy for co-owners who cannot agree. Quiet title resolves competing claims, and neither of the last two applies between co-owners in this posture.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Estates, ownership, and common interests), section 3.5

### 19. A seller says the property is owned by his limited liability company and that he is a member. What establishes his authority to sign?

A. His membership, which carries authority to convey entity property by operation of law.
B. A statement of information filed with the Secretary of State.
C. The entity's tax identification number and its bank signature card.
**D. The entity's governing documents and any resolution they require, not the title he uses.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Authority comes from the governing documents. Membership alone does not confer it, and none of the other documents establishes signing authority.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Estates, ownership, and common interests), section 6.3

### 20. In the government survey system, how many acres are in a full section?

A. 160
B. 43,560
C. 36
**D. 640**

> **Correct answer: D.** A section is nominally one mile square, or 640 acres. A quarter section is 160 acres, a township contains 36 sections, and 43,560 is the square feet in one acre.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Land description, measurement, and boundaries), section 2.3

### 21. How many acres are described by 'the NW 1/4 of the SE 1/4' of a section?

**A. 40**
B. 80
C. 160
D. 20

> **Correct answer: A.** Multiply the fractions and apply them to 640: 1/4 × 1/4 × 640 = 40 acres.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Land description, measurement, and boundaries), section 2.4

### 22. In a metes and bounds description, a call to a natural monument conflicts with a stated distance. Which generally controls?

A. The distance, because it is the more precise measurement.
B. Neither; the description fails for uncertainty.
**C. The monument, under the established order of priority among conflicting calls.**
D. Whichever appears first in the description.

> **Correct answer: C.** Monuments generally prevail over courses and distances in the priority order, on the reasoning that a physical marker reflects what was actually intended on the ground.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Land description, measurement, and boundaries), section 1.6

### 23. Why must a lot and block description include the recording reference for the map?

A. Because the reference establishes the property's assessed value.
B. Because the reference is what transfers title at closing.
**C. Because the lot and block have meaning only by reference to the specific recorded map that defines them.**
D. Because the recorder assigns a new reference each time the property is sold.

> **Correct answer: C.** Lot and block numbers are meaningless without the map that defines them, which is why the book and page reference is part of the description.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Land description, measurement, and boundaries), section 3.2

### 24. Which circumstance most clearly calls for recommending a survey?

A. The property is described by lot and block rather than metes and bounds.
B. The buyer is obtaining conventional rather than government-backed financing.
C. The preliminary report lists more than three exceptions.
**D. A fence, wall, or structure appears to sit near or across a boundary, or the parties describe the line differently.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Physical indications of a boundary problem are the trigger. The description method, the loan type, and the number of exceptions are not.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Land description, measurement, and boundaries), section 5.6

### 25. A rectangular lot measures 120 feet by 90 feet. What is its area in square feet, and approximately what fraction of an acre is that?

A. 1,080 square feet, about one fortieth of an acre.
B. 10,800 square feet, about half an acre.
**C. 10,800 square feet, about one quarter of an acre.**
D. 21,600 square feet, about half an acre.

> **Correct answer: C.** 120 × 90 = 10,800 square feet; 10,800 ÷ 43,560 ≈ 0.248 acre. The distractors misplace a decimal, double the area, or misstate the fraction.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Land description, measurement, and boundaries), section 6.2

### 26. Which characteristic distinguishes a property tax lien from most other liens?

A. It expires automatically if not enforced within one year.
**B. It generally takes priority over other liens regardless of when they were recorded.**
C. It must be recorded to attach to the property.
D. It attaches to all of the owner's property rather than to the taxed parcel.

> **Correct answer: B.** Property tax liens are specific to the parcel and generally hold priority ahead of recorded liens, which is what makes them different from the ordinary priority rule.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Encumbrances, liens, easements, and restrictions), section 2.2

### 27. What distinguishes a mechanic's lien from most other involuntary liens?

**A. Its priority may relate back to a point earlier than its recording, tied to the commencement of the work or improvement.**
B. It attaches to the contractor's property rather than the owner's.
C. It requires a court judgment before it can be recorded.
D. It may be recorded by anyone who believes he is owed money by the owner.

> **Correct answer: A.** The relation-back feature is what makes mechanic's liens dangerous to a lender and a buyer: a lien recorded later can outrank an intervening instrument.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Encumbrances, liens, easements, and restrictions), section 2.3

### 28. Which of the following is a recognized way an easement may be created?

A. By continuous use for any period, without more.
B. By the county assessor's designation on a parcel map.
C. By an oral agreement between neighbors, in every case.
**D. By express grant or reservation, by implication, by necessity, or by prescription.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Grant, reservation, implication, necessity, and prescription are the recognized routes. Use alone, an oral arrangement, and an assessor's map do not create an interest in land.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Encumbrances, liens, easements, and restrictions), section 3.3

### 29. Who generally enforces a private recorded restriction in a common interest development?

**A. The association or a benefited owner, through the remedies the declaration and the law provide.**
B. The Department of Real Estate, through its disciplinary process.
C. The city or county planning department.
D. The title insurer that issued the owner's policy.

> **Correct answer: A.** Private restrictions are privately enforced. Public agencies enforce public land use controls, and the title insurer's role is coverage rather than enforcement.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Encumbrances, liens, easements, and restrictions), section 4.4

### 30. A neighbor's garage extends two feet onto the subject property. What is this?

**A. An encroachment, which is a physical intrusion and a matter a survey rather than a title search reveals.**
B. A nonconforming use under the zoning ordinance.
C. A license, revocable by the owner at any time.
D. An easement by prescription, established by the structure's presence.

> **Correct answer: A.** An encroachment is a physical intrusion. Whether it has ripened into any legal right is a separate question, and it is the classic survey matter.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Encumbrances, liens, easements, and restrictions), section 5.1

### 31. What is the essential difference between an owner's policy and a lender's policy of title insurance?

A. The owner's policy covers survey matters, while the lender's policy does not.

### 32. Property passes to the state because an owner died without a will and without heirs. Which power is this?

### 33. What is the relationship between a general plan and a zoning ordinance?

### 35. Which fact triggers the federal lead-based paint disclosure obligation for residential property?

### 37. What is the recurring due diligence failure this lesson identifies?

### 39. What does a licensee owe to a party who is not his client?

C. Nothing, because duties run only to the principal.

### 40. What may a dual agent NOT do?

C. Present offers and counteroffers between the parties.

### 41. A salesperson commits a violation in the course of licensed activity. What is the broker's exposure?

B. None, because discipline runs only against the individual who acted.
C. The broker is responsible only if he directed the specific act.
D. None, provided the salesperson signed an independent contractor agreement.

> **Correct answer: A.** Vicarious responsibility follows from the supervisory duty. Independent contractor status for tax and payroll purposes does not reduce it.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 07 (Agency and brokerage relationships), section 5.5

### 42. Which obligation survives the termination of an agency relationship?

C. The right to compensation on any future sale, regardless of the agreement's terms.
**D. Confidentiality as to the former principal's confidential information.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Confidentiality continues after the engagement ends. Performance duties end with it, and compensation after termination is governed by the agreement's own terms.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 07 (Agency and brokerage relationships), section 6.8

### 43. Why is the referral fee prohibition different from an ordinary conflict of interest?

A. Referral fees are permitted if the referring licensee performs no work.

**C. Where a payment is prohibited, disclosing it does not make it lawful — the first question is whether it may be received at all.**

> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 07 (Agency and brokerage relationships), section 6.5

### 44. Which describes a unilateral contract?

**C. A promise given in exchange for an act, where the offeree is not obligated to perform.**

>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 08 (Contracts and transaction formation), section 1.3

### 45. Which event does NOT terminate an offer before acceptance?

C. Rejection by the offeree.

> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 08 (Contracts and transaction formation), section 2.4

### 46. What distinguishes an option from an ordinary offer?

C. An option obligates the optionee to purchase.
**D. Consideration has been given to keep the offer open, so it cannot be revoked during the option period.**

>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 08 (Contracts and transaction formation), section 2.5

### 47. A contract falls within the Statute of Frauds and was never reduced to writing. How is it best described?

**A. Unenforceable.**

### 97. A file contains a purchase agreement confirming the same brokerage on both sides and no consent from either party. How should this finding be ranked against a disclosure delivered three days late?

### 99. A closed file contains a deed with no date of delivery and an escrow instruction amendment signed by only one party. Which is the more serious finding?
