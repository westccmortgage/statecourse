## Items, with the material reference for each answer

### 1. What is the operative difference between a broker license and a salesperson license?

A. A broker must be a natural person and a salesperson may be an entity.
B. A broker may handle trust funds and a salesperson may not touch any transaction document.
**C. A broker may operate independently and supervise licensees; a salesperson's authority flows through an employing broker.**
D. A broker may list property and a salesperson may only sell it.

> **Correct answer: C.** The distinction is independent operation and supervisory authority. Salespersons perform the full range of licensed activity under a broker's authority.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (California practice standards and the regulated professional), section 1.4

### 2. An independent contractor agreement states the salesperson controls his own schedule and methods. What does it do to the broker's supervisory duty?

A. It transfers the supervisory duty to the salesperson for his own files.
B. It limits supervision to the files the broker personally reviews.
C. It eliminates vicarious responsibility for the salesperson's acts.
**D. Nothing — independent contractor status addresses tax and payroll treatment and does not reduce supervision.**

> **Correct answer: D.** The status is a tax and payroll characterization. The supervisory duty and vicarious responsibility are unaffected by it.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (California practice standards and the regulated professional), section 2.3

### 3. Which activity crosses from real estate practice into the practice of law?

**A. Drafting a provision that alters the parties' legal rights, rather than completing an approved form with negotiated terms.**
B. Explaining what a deadline in the agreement is and when it falls.
C. Recommending that a client consult an attorney about a clause.
D. Delivering a document prepared by the other party's counsel.

> **Correct answer: A.** Authorship of provisions that change legal rights is the boundary. Explaining the document's own procedure, referring, and delivering are all within scope.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (California practice standards and the regulated professional), section 3.2

### 4. Which belongs in a working brokerage policy set?

A. The brokerage's commission splits and recruiting terms.
**B. What must be submitted for review and when, the escalation path, trust fund handling, and the file standard.**
C. The marketing calendar for the coming year.
D. A directory of preferred vendors and their pricing.

> **Correct answer: B.** The policy set governs supervision, escalation, funds, and records. Compensation and marketing are business documents rather than the compliance framework.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (California practice standards and the regulated professional), section 4.2

### 5. What should a confirming message after a consequential client conversation contain?

**A. The date, what the client asked or decided, what he was told, and what happens next by when — in a few sentences, sent the same day.**
B. The licensee's assessment of the client's likely motives.
C. A statement that the client agreed with the licensee's recommendation.
D. A complete transcript of the conversation.

> **Correct answer: A.** Short, same day, specific. A transcript is not the point, and characterizing motives or asserting agreement puts the licensee's conclusions into the record.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (California practice standards and the regulated professional), section 5.5

### 6. What is wrong with a business plan that consists of a transaction target and a gross income figure?

A. It cannot be evaluated by a brokerage during recruiting.
B. It omits the commission split, which determines net income.
C. It fails to account for market conditions that may change during the year.
**D. It does not work backward from the hours a transaction actually requires, including the compliance work, so the target is met by skipping steps.**

> **Correct answer: D.** A target exceeding capacity is still met — by cutting the work that is invisible until something goes wrong, which is precisely the work that gets cut.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Building a lawful real estate practice), section 1.1

### 7. A lead vendor assures a licensee that its call list has been screened against do-not-call registries. Where does the obligation sit?

A. With the brokerage, which purchased the list.
B. With the vendor, which contracted to supply a compliant list.
**C. With the caller — a licensee cannot rely on a vendor's assurance.**
D. With whichever party the calling script identifies.

> **Correct answer: C.** The obligation belongs to the person placing the call, and identification of the caller is a separate requirement rather than a way of allocating this one.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Building a lawful real estate practice), section 2.2

### 8. Which prospecting statement is prohibited in every channel?

A. "I sold a property on your street last month," when that is true.
**B. "I have a buyer for your house," when no such buyer exists.**
C. "May I ask whether you have considered selling?"
D. "I would be glad to prepare a market analysis at no cost."

> **Correct answer: B.** A representation creating a false impression of urgency, endorsement, or authority is prohibited, and prospecting pressure is not a defense.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Building a lawful real estate practice), section 2.5

### 9. A team markets itself under a name displayed more prominently than the brokerage, styled like a separate firm. What is the defect?

**A. It creates the impression of an independent company that is not licensed, which the identification requirements exist to prevent.**
B. The team name must be registered with the Department before use.
C. Nothing, provided the brokerage's name appears somewhere in the advertisement.
D. Team names may not be used in California advertising at all.

> **Correct answer: A.** Team names are permitted; presenting one as though it were the licensed entity is the problem, and mere presence of the brokerage name does not cure prominence.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Building a lawful real estate practice), section 3.3

### 10. Which control most reduces a brokerage's exposure when a vendor it uses suffers a data breach?

A. Using a vendor located in the same state as the brokerage.
B. Requiring the vendor to carry cyber liability insurance.
**C. Sending the vendor only the data it actually needs, rather than the whole file.**
D. Encrypting the brokerage's own devices.

> **Correct answer: C.** Data not sent cannot be leaked, and most over-sharing happens because sending the whole file is faster than assembling the part. The other measures help but do not reduce what the vendor holds.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Building a lawful real estate practice), section 5.4

### 11. Why is shortening a retention period a poor economy?

A. The listing service requires records to be kept indefinitely.
B. Destroyed records must be reconstructed at the brokerage's expense.
C. Storage costs are trivial compared with brokerage revenue.
**D. The obligation runs from the transaction rather than from convenience, and a complaint can arrive long after a file was destroyed.**

> **Correct answer: D.** The period runs from the transaction, and retention extends where a complaint has been made or a proceeding is pending. Cost is not the reason the rule exists.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Building a lawful real estate practice), section 6.3

### 12. When must the agency disclosure be delivered to a buyer the brokerage is working with?

**A. At the outset of the working relationship, before advice is given — not at the point an offer is written.**
B. Within a defined period after the buyer identifies a property of interest.
C. Before escrow opens, so the parties' roles are settled for closing.
D. With the purchase agreement, where the confirmation also appears.

> **Correct answer: A.** By the time an offer exists, weeks of advice have been given. The confirmation is a later, separate step.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Agency disclosure and fiduciary performance), section 2.2

### 13. A seller instructs his agent not to show the property to a category of buyers. Why can the agent not follow it, even documenting that it came from the client?

A. The agent may follow it if the brokerage's policy manual permits.
**B. The duty of obedience extends only to lawful instructions, and documentation does not transfer responsibility for the act.**
C. The agent may follow it provided he does not state the reason to anyone.
D. The agent may follow it if the property is owner-occupied.

> **Correct answer: B.** Following it informally achieves the same exclusion, and the documentation records that the agent knew rather than shielding him.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Agency disclosure and fiduciary performance), section 3.3

### 14. Which duty continues after the agency relationship has ended?

**A. Confidentiality, including as to the lowest price a former seller would have accepted.**
B. The duty to continue marketing the property.
C. Obedience to the former principal's instructions.
D. The duty to account for funds not yet received.

> **Correct answer: A.** A duty that evaporated at the end of the engagement would not be worth having, and it applies even when the licensee later works with a different client.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Agency disclosure and fiduciary performance), section 3.4

### 15. Both parties have signed a dual agency consent. What does the consent NOT authorize?

A. Presenting offers and counteroffers between the parties.
B. Explaining the agreement's provisions to both parties.
C. Disclosing material facts about the property's condition to both.
**D. Disclosing to one party the price or terms the other is willing to accept.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Confidential negotiating information may not move between sides even with consent to the dual agency itself. Everything else in the list remains required.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Agency disclosure and fiduciary performance), section 4.3

### 16. When should a possible dual agency be raised with the parties?

**A. As soon as it becomes foreseeable, typically when a buyer the brokerage works with becomes interested in a brokerage listing.**
B. Only if one of the parties asks whether the brokerage represents the other.
C. At closing, when the compensation from both sides becomes visible.
D. When the offer is written, since that is when both sides are represented in one transaction.

> **Correct answer: A.** Raising it at the offer is late, because both parties have already invested effort under an assumption that may be wrong. Consent obtained after the conduct records the problem.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Agency disclosure and fiduciary performance), section 4.5

### 17. A licensee wants to buy a property his brokerage has listed. What does loyalty require?

A. Nothing beyond offering a fair price, since price is what the seller cares about.
B. Obtaining an appraisal to demonstrate the price is market value.
**C. Disclosing that he is acting as a principal rather than an agent, on terms heightened beyond ordinary disclosure, and routing the matter through the broker.**
D. Withdrawing from the brokerage's representation of the seller and proceeding as an ordinary buyer.

> **Correct answer: C.** The interest is directly opposed to the principal's on the term that matters most, and a fair price does not substitute for disclosure of the licensee's role.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Agency disclosure and fiduciary performance), section 3.5

### 18. What does a protection clause in a listing agreement generally address?

**A. Compensation where the property is sold after expiration to a buyer identified to the seller during the term, on the clause's own terms.**
B. The seller's obligation to reimburse marketing costs on cancellation.
C. The brokerage's right to renew the listing automatically.
D. The buyer's obligation to work exclusively with the listing brokerage.

> **Correct answer: A.** The clause operates on its own terms as to buyers identified during the term, which is why a written protected-buyer list delivered before expiration is what makes it usable.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Seller counseling and listing agreements), section 2.5

### 19. At intake a seller mentions the garage was converted to a bedroom years ago by a family member. What must be established?

A. Whether the conversion is structurally sound, which the licensee should assess during the walkthrough.
B. Nothing, since the seller's description of his own property may be relied on.
C. Whether the buyer will want the space returned to a garage.
**D. Whether the work was permitted and how the records describe the property, before any room count or square footage is published.**

> **Correct answer: D.** The published figures become the licensee's representation. Structural assessment is outside his competence and the buyer's preferences are a marketing question.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Seller counseling and listing agreements), section 1.3

### 20. A seller says a defect is 'fixed, so there's nothing to disclose.' What is the counseling response?

A. Agree, provided the repair is more than five years old.
B. Disclose it only if the buyer's inspector identifies the underlying condition.
C. Agree, provided the repair was performed by a licensed contractor.
**D. The repair is exactly what the buyer wants to evaluate, and disclosing it with the repair records is stronger than silence.**

> **Correct answer: D.** A repaired condition is still disclosed. Neither the contractor's licensure nor the passage of time removes it, and waiting for the inspector concedes the point.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Seller counseling and listing agreements), section 4.3

### 21. A seller needs the property off the market temporarily for a family matter. What is the correct mechanism?

**A. Withdrawal — the listing continues and the marketing pauses, documented with an accurate listing service status change.**
B. Expiration, by allowing the term to run out.
C. Cancellation, which ends the engagement and can be reinstated later.
D. A verbal instruction to stop showings, since no document is needed to pause marketing.

> **Correct answer: A.** Withdrawal and cancellation are different things, and misrepresenting a property's status in a listing service is its own problem.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Seller counseling and listing agreements), section 6.4

### 22. A listed property is occupied by tenants. What changes about showings?

A. Nothing, since the seller owns the property and controls access.
**B. The tenants' rights govern access, including notice, and the showing plan is built around them rather than around the seller's preference.**
C. The tenants may be required to vacate for open houses.
D. Showings are prohibited until the tenancy ends.

> **Correct answer: B.** A tenancy carries possession rights that a sale does not suspend, and entry requirements apply regardless of who wants the showing.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Seller counseling and listing agreements), section 5.5

### 23. What should the file show when a listing engagement ends?

**A. The date and method, who authorized it for the brokerage, any agreement about compensation or protected buyers, and the listing service status change with its date.**
B. Nothing, if the engagement simply expired by its own terms.
C. A written statement of the seller's reasons for ending it.
D. Only the expiration date stated in the original agreement.

> **Correct answer: A.** An engagement that ends informally by everyone ceasing to act is the one that generates a dispute six weeks later when the property sells.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Seller counseling and listing agreements), section 6.6

### 24. What defines a defensible competitive set?

A. The three closest sales by distance regardless of characteristics.
B. The sales producing the tightest range of indicated values.
C. Every sale in the subject's zip code in the last twelve months.
**D. Properties a buyer would genuinely consider instead of the subject, with each inclusion and exclusion justified.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Substitution is the principle; proximity, an arbitrary geographic filter, and selecting for convergence are all ways of choosing the answer first.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Market analysis and pricing strategy), section 1.3

### 25. How should active listings be used in a market analysis?

**A. As evidence of current competition and an upper boundary, not as evidence of what buyers have paid.**
B. As the primary indication, because they reflect present market conditions.
C. As a substitute for closed sales where recent closings are scarce.
D. They should be excluded entirely from any analysis.

> **Correct answer: A.** An asking price is an offer to the market. Actives inform strategy and mislead when used as value evidence, but they are not excluded.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Market analysis and pricing strategy), section 3.3

### 26. What does the absorption rate measure?

A. The share of listings that expire without selling.
B. The percentage of list price that sellers actually receive.
**C. The rate at which available inventory is being sold, which indicates how long the current supply would last.**
D. The average number of days a property remains on the market.

> **Correct answer: C.** Absorption relates inventory to sales pace. Days on market and list-to-sale ratio are separate measures of different things.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Market analysis and pricing strategy), section 3.4

### 27. Three comparables each required adjustments approaching a fifth of their sale prices, and all three converge on the number the seller mentioned. What does that convergence show?

A. That the comparables were correctly selected.
B. That the seller's expectation is consistent with the market.
**C. Little — convergence produced by heavy adjustment is the analyst's judgment restated three times.**
D. That the analysis is well supported, since three independent indications agree.

> **Correct answer: C.** Gross adjustment measures how much work the grid is doing. A comparable requiring large gross adjustment is weak however close its adjusted figure lands.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Market analysis and pricing strategy), section 2.7

### 28. What must the pricing file contain for the opinion to be reconstructable later?

A. The final range and the date it was delivered.
B. The seller's signature acknowledging the recommendation.
C. A copy of the listing agreement showing the price selected.
**D. The comparables as they existed on that date, the selection reasoning, the derivation of the adjustments, and the market conditions statement.**

> **Correct answer: D.** A listing service re-queried later returns different data and can contradict the original analysis, so the contemporaneous export and the reasoning are what answer a claim.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Market analysis and pricing strategy), section 6.2

### 29. A subject property is a five-acre rural parcel and the nearest similar sale is eleven months old and four miles away. What does the analysis owe the seller?

A. A figure adjusted upward for the passage of time since the comparable sold.
B. A single figure derived from the nearest available sale.
**C. A range with its reasoning and a plain statement of what the data does and does not support.**
D. A refusal to provide any opinion, since the method does not apply.

> **Correct answer: C.** Applying the method where its assumptions fail produces false precision, which is worse than no number. Stating the limit is the professional obligation, and refusing entirely is not required.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Market analysis and pricing strategy), section 1.6

### 30. An appraisal comes in below the contract price. What may the licensee do?

**A. Submit additional data through the prescribed reconsideration channel, with the appraiser free to reject it.**
B. Ask the lender to order a second appraisal from a different appraiser who is more familiar with the area.
C. Advise the seller that the appraisal is wrong and should be disregarded.
D. Contact the appraiser directly to explain what the transaction needs.

> **Correct answer: A.** A low appraisal is a fact, not a problem to solve. Data may be submitted through the channel; a target, direct pressure, or shopping for a different result may not.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Market analysis and pricing strategy), section 5.4

### 31. Which statement crosses from puffery into misrepresentation?

A. "You will love the natural light."
**B. "The addition is fully permitted," where the records do not show a permit.**
C. "A wonderful home for entertaining."
D. "One of the nicest streets in the neighborhood."

> **Correct answer: B.** Puffery is obvious opinion a buyer does not rely on as fact. A statement about permitting is a verifiable fact and, if unsupported, a misrepresentation.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 06 (Marketing, advertising, and fair access), section 1.4

### 32. A licensee promotes a listing on a personal social account. What applies?

**C. The same identification requirements as any other advertisement — the platform does not change what the advertisement is.**

### 33. Which listing phrase must be rewritten?

### 34. How should accessibility features be handled in listing copy?

### 40. Why is the property search documented against the buyer's stated criteria?

### 41. A buyer is purchasing new construction and asks whether an inspection is necessary.

### 42. What is the central risk a buyer should understand about an escalation clause?

A. It is prohibited in California residential transactions.

### 43. What is the function of the deposit in a residential purchase agreement?

**A. It is consideration held under the agreement's terms, whose disposition on cancellation follows the contract's procedure.**
B. It is a fee earned by the listing brokerage when the offer is accepted.
C. It is applied to the buyer's closing costs and cannot be at risk.
D. It is a penalty automatically forfeited if the buyer does not close.

> **Correct answer: A.** Disposition follows the contract's procedure and, where disputed, a legal determination. It is neither automatically forfeited nor immune from risk.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 08 (Purchase agreements, counteroffers, and contingencies), section 1.3

### 44. What does it mean that time is a term of a purchase agreement?

A. The agreement expires automatically if any deadline passes.
**B. The dates are substantive obligations rather than administrative targets, and missing one has contractual consequences.**
C. Deadlines are enforceable only if the agreement states that time is of the essence for that item.
D. Escrow may extend any deadline to accommodate the parties.

> **Correct answer: B.** Dates carry obligations. The agreement does not evaporate on a missed date, escrow does not extend terms, and treating deadlines as targets is how files fail.

### 100. An owner instructs a property manager to change the locks on a delinquent tenant. What does the manager do?
