## Items, with the material reference for each answer

### 1. Which activity is outside what a California real estate license authorizes?

A. Completing an approved form by filling in the terms the parties negotiated.
B. Presenting an offer and explaining the deadlines the agreement sets.
C. Preparing a comparative market analysis to support a listing decision.
**D. Advising a client on how title should be taken and what the tax consequences would be.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Vesting and tax consequences belong to counsel and a tax advisor. Market analysis, approved-form completion, and explaining the document's own procedure are all within scope.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (California practice standards and the regulated professional), section 1.6

### 2. A broker assigns advertising approval to an office manager who holds a salesperson license. What is the defect?

**A. A supervisory function has been delegated to someone without supervisory authority, and the duty remains the broker's regardless.**
B. None, because advertising approval is an administrative rather than a supervisory function.
C. The manager becomes personally responsible for every advertisement approved.
D. None, provided the delegation is recorded in the office policy manual.

> **Correct answer: A.** Delegation does not transfer responsibility, and a supervisory function performed by a person without supervisory authority is a structural defect rather than a staffing arrangement.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (California practice standards and the regulated professional), section 2.2

### 3. Which three categories must a consumer be able to tell apart in what a licensee says?

A. Advice, opinion, and instruction.
**B. Fact, estimate, and assurance — and an assurance is never statable.**
C. Written, oral, and implied statements.
D. Disclosure, negotiation, and marketing.

> **Correct answer: B.** Facts are statable, estimates are statable when identified as estimates, and assurances about approval, appraisal, or value are not statable at any confidence level.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (California practice standards and the regulated professional), section 5.1

### 4. Why is evidence of delivery, rather than a signature, the thing a transaction file must carry?

**A. Rights and deadlines run from receipt, so a file that cannot show when a document reached the party cannot show the timing was honored.**
B. The regulations require electronic delivery for all transaction documents.
C. Signatures can be forged, while delivery records cannot.
D. A signature is only valid if it is accompanied by a delivery receipt.

> **Correct answer: A.** A signature proves execution; delivery is what starts the clock. That gap is where most disclosure findings live.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (California practice standards and the regulated professional), section 6.3

### 5. A closed file is found to contain an undated contingency removal. Which response is prohibited?

A. Recording the finding and escalating it to the broker.
B. Adding a memorandum dated today describing what the file shows.
C. Contacting the party who signed to ask whether a dated copy exists.
**D. Entering a date now so the file appears complete.**

> **Correct answer: D.** A documented gap is survivable; a document altered to say something it did not say is falsification. The other three are all proper responses.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (California practice standards and the regulated professional), section 6.5

### 6. A brokerage policy prohibits something the law permits. What is a licensee's obligation?

**A. Follow the policy — a brokerage may lawfully hold its licensees to a stricter standard than the law requires.**
B. Follow the policy only where it is repeated in the licensee's independent contractor agreement.
C. Follow whichever standard the client prefers.
D. Follow the law, since a private policy cannot restrict lawful activity.

> **Correct answer: A.** Stricter than the law is a legitimate brokerage choice, and a prohibition the licensee did not know about is one he will violate — which is why the policy set is read before the first transaction.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (California practice standards and the regulated professional), section 4.4

### 7. A visitor writes a phone number on an open house sign-in sheet that says nothing about future contact. May the licensee add the number to a marketing text campaign?

A. Yes, because providing the number at a marketing event implies consent.
B. Yes, provided the first message identifies the licensee and the brokerage.
**C. No — consent is defined by what the person was actually told when the information was collected.**
D. Yes, provided every message includes an opt-out instruction.

> **Correct answer: C.** An opt-out mechanism is necessary but not sufficient — it does not create the consent required to send the first message, and identification addresses a different requirement.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Building a lawful real estate practice), section 2.3

### 8. Two brokers agree at a caravan that neither will show properties listed by discount brokerages. What is this?

**A. A group boycott, which is among the most serious conduct in this area and does not require a written agreement.**
B. A conflict of interest requiring disclosure to affected clients.
C. A permissible business decision, since each brokerage sets its own showing practices.
D. A steering violation under fair housing law.

> **Correct answer: A.** Concerted refusal to deal is a group boycott. An agreement can be inferred from a conversation followed by a course of conduct, and the participants who said nothing are participants.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Building a lawful real estate practice), section 4.2

### 9. A colleague at a professional event says, 'Nobody around here should be going below a certain rate.' What should the licensee do?

A. Say nothing at the time and mention it privately to the speaker afterward.
B. Remain and listen, since he did not initiate the topic and has not agreed to anything.
C. Change the subject so the conversation does not continue.
**D. Say aloud that he sets his own fees independently and does not discuss them with other firms, leave the conversation, and write a dated note that day.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Silence in the room reads as agreement, and the contemporaneous note is what distinguishes a witness from a participant years later. Changing the subject and private follow-up leave no record.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Building a lawful real estate practice), section 4.6

### 10. Which is the most common advertising identification defect?

A. Advertising a property before the seller has signed the disclosure package.
**B. Personal branding and a mobile number with no brokerage named, so the consumer cannot tell which supervising brokerage stands behind the advertisement.**
C. Using a photograph of the licensee rather than of the property.
D. Failing to state the property's list price.

> **Correct answer: B.** Identification exists so the consumer can tell there is a supervising brokerage and which one. Photographs, price, and disclosure timing are separate questions.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Building a lawful real estate practice), section 3.2

### 11. A licensee receives an email from an address closely resembling escrow's, giving new wire instructions two days before closing. What is the correct step?

A. Forward the email to the client with a warning to be careful.
B. Send a small test wire and confirm receipt before sending the balance.
**C. Verify by voice using a number the file already held, obtained independently of the message.**
D. Reply to the email and ask escrow to confirm the change.

> **Correct answer: C.** Verification must run through a channel the attacker does not control. Replying, forwarding, and test wires all remain inside the compromised channel.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Building a lawful real estate practice), section 5.2

### 12. Why is the close-out routine performed within days of closing rather than later?

A. Because the brokerage's retention period runs from the date of the audit.
B. Because escrow destroys its records shortly after closing.
C. Because the file cannot be retained until it has been audited.
**D. Because a missing document is usually still obtainable in week one and usually is not in year two.**

> **Correct answer: D.** The obtainable-now test is the entire argument for the routine: the other participants still answer the phone, and the facts are still fresh.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Building a lawful real estate practice), section 6.7

### 13. What are the three steps of the California agency disclosure sequence?

**A. Disclose, elect, confirm.**
B. Advise, agree, record.
C. Present, negotiate, close.
D. Notify, consent, deliver.

> **Correct answer: A.** Disclosure comes first, the relationship is elected in the agreement, and the confirmation appears in or with the purchase agreement — and the dates must run in that order.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Agency disclosure and fiduciary performance), section 2.1

### 14. A listing agent at an open house tells an unrepresented visitor what the seller would probably accept and suggests an offer strategy. What has happened?

A. Nothing, because the visitor is unrepresented and no agreement was signed.
**B. The agent has given advice against his own principal's interest and may have created a relationship with the visitor by conduct.**
C. The agent has satisfied his duty of fair dealing to a non-client.
D. The visitor has become a customer, to whom only honesty is owed.

> **Correct answer: B.** Disclosing what the seller would take is confidential negotiating information given to the party negotiating against the seller, and agency can arise from conduct without any signed agreement.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Agency disclosure and fiduciary performance), section 1.6

### 15. Which duty is breached most often, and why does it produce most claims against licensees?

**A. Reasonable care — it can be breached without any dishonesty, by ordinary pressure applied to an ordinary practice.**
B. Confidentiality — because most licensees discuss transactions socially.
C. Accounting — because most licensees handle trust funds.
D. Loyalty — because most licensees have undisclosed interests.

> **Correct answer: A.** Fraud requires intent and is comparatively rare. Negligence requires only work below the standard of a competent licensee, which is what a missed deadline or an unverified number produces.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Agency disclosure and fiduciary performance), section 3.6

### 16. Two salespersons in the same brokerage represent the buyer and the seller in one transaction, and have never discussed the file. What is the position?

A. No dual agency, because they work from different offices of the brokerage.
B. A dual agency exists only if the two salespersons share compensation.
C. No dual agency, because the two salespersons kept the sides separate.
**D. A dual agency exists, because the agency belongs to the brokerage rather than to the individual salesperson.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Agency belongs to the brokerage. Internal separation is a control on confidential information, not something that prevents the dual agency from existing.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Agency disclosure and fiduciary performance), section 4.6

### 17. A buyer's agent is paid from the seller's side. What must the file show for the arrangement to be internally consistent?

A. A written waiver by the buyer of his right to independent representation.
B. A dual agency consent signed by both parties.
**C. A confirmation of buyer agency together with disclosure of the compensation arrangement — payment does not determine representation.**
D. An amendment converting the buyer's agent to a subagent of the seller.

> **Correct answer: C.** A file showing seller-side compensation and confirmed buyer agency is consistent, not contradictory. What must still happen is disclosure of the arrangement, since the client cannot evaluate advice without knowing the adviser's economics.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Agency disclosure and fiduciary performance), section 5.1

### 18. A closed file shows an agency disclosure signed three days after the listing agreement, a purchase agreement confirming the brokerage as the buyer's agent while the listing file shows it as the seller's agent, and no dual agency consent from either party. Which finding is the most serious, and why?

A. None of them, because the transaction closed and no party has complained.
**B. The contradiction, because it describes an undisclosed dual agency — duties actually owed to both parties were breached, rather than a documentation step being missed.**
C. The late disclosure, because a disclosure signed after the listing agreement is void and takes the listing agreement with it.
D. All three equally, because each is a dated document appearing out of sequence in the file.

> **Correct answer: B.** The disclosure signed after the listing means the seller committed before being informed, which is a real defect. But the contradiction is different in kind: the file shows the brokerage acting on both sides with consent from neither, which is undisclosed dual agency. The absence of a complaint establishes nothing, and no finding may be corrected by adjusting a date after the fact.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Agency disclosure and fiduciary performance), section 6.2

### 19. Why must a listing agreement's term be definite?

**A. So both parties know when the engagement ends, which determines when protection and compensation provisions operate.**
B. Because an indefinite term converts the listing into an open listing by operation of law.
C. Because the seller may otherwise cancel at will without consequence.
D. Because the listing service will not accept an entry without one.

> **Correct answer: A.** A definite termination date is what makes expiration, protection, and compensation computable. The other statements assert consequences that do not follow from indefiniteness itself.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Seller counseling and listing agreements), section 2.3

### 20. A caller says she inherited a house and wants to list it. What must be established before the listing proceeds?

A. Whether the property has been appraised in the last year.
B. Whether any siblings agree with the asking price.
**C. How title is currently held and who has authority to convey, from the documents rather than from her description.**
D. Whether she intends to buy a replacement property.

> **Correct answer: C.** Authority is established from documents. Whether the property passed by trust, by will requiring administration, or by operation of law determines who must sign, and it is expensive to discover during escrow.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Seller counseling and listing agreements), section 1.2

### 21. A seller wants to defer the disclosure paperwork until an offer arrives. How should the licensee respond?

A. Refuse the listing, since deferral makes the transaction impossible.
**B. Explain that disclosure runs on its own schedule because the buyer's rights depend on when it is delivered, and begin it now while there is no deadline pressure.**
C. Agree, since disclosures are delivered with the purchase agreement in any event.
D. Agree, provided the seller signs an acknowledgment that he chose to defer.

> **Correct answer: B.** Price, showing schedule, and cancellation terms are negotiable; disclosure timing is not. Deferring compresses the buyer's review period at exactly the wrong moment.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Seller counseling and listing agreements), section 4.1

### 22. How should an item on a seller's disclosure be handled when the seller genuinely does not know the answer?

**A. Answer 'unknown', which is an honest and available answer — a blank is not.**
B. Leave it blank, since the seller cannot assert what he does not know.
C. Answer 'no', since the seller has no knowledge of a problem.
D. Have the licensee answer it from his own observation.

> **Correct answer: A.** A blank is ambiguous about whether the item was addressed, 'no' asserts knowledge the seller does not have, and the licensee completing the seller's disclosure misattributes the statement.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Seller counseling and listing agreements), section 4.4

### 23. A seller insists on listing above the licensee's recommendation. What is the correct handling?

A. Decline the listing, since marketing a price the licensee cannot support is a misrepresentation.
B. Take the listing and adopt the seller's number as the licensee's own professional opinion.
C. Take the listing at the seller's price and reduce marketing effort until he agrees to a reduction.
**D. Price it at the seller's number, work it professionally, document the recommendation and the seller's decision, and agree in advance on a review date.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Price is the seller's decision and the recommendation is the licensee's obligation. Adopting the number as his own opinion is a representation he cannot support, and reduced effort is a reasonable-care breach.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Seller counseling and listing agreements), section 3.6

### 24. A seller says he wants to 'cancel' the listing. What is the licensee's first task?

**A. Determine which of expiration, cancellation, and withdrawal he actually means, since the three operate differently.**
B. Tell the seller the agreement cannot be ended before its term expires.
C. Remove the property from the listing service immediately.
D. Prepare a release for the seller's signature.

> **Correct answer: A.** The three are frequently conflated and a request often means the marketing should pause rather than the engagement end. Whether to release the seller is a broker-level decision in any case.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Seller counseling and listing agreements), section 6.1

### 25. Why do closed sales dominate a comparative market analysis?

A. They are the only category the listing service makes available to licensees.
B. They require no adjustment because the transactions are complete.
**C. They record what buyers actually paid after negotiation, which is the only category that proves a completed transaction.**
D. They are the most recent data in every market.

> **Correct answer: C.** Active listings show what sellers ask and pending sales show what buyers agreed to but have not completed. Only closed sales prove what was paid, and they still require adjustment.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Market analysis and pricing strategy), section 3.2

### 26. A comparable sale has one more bathroom than the subject. How is the adjustment applied?

**A. Downward, to the comparable's sale price, because the adjustment is always made to the comparable rather than to the subject.**
B. Downward, to the subject's indicated value.
C. Upward, to the comparable's sale price, to reflect the additional bathroom.
D. The comparable is excluded, since a difference in bathroom count makes it unusable.

> **Correct answer: A.** Adjustments run to the comparable: superior features are adjusted down, inferior features up. The subject is never adjusted, and one differing feature does not disqualify a comparable.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Market analysis and pricing strategy), section 2.1

### 27. A seller says his kitchen remodel cost $85,000 and should be added to the price. What is the correct response?

A. Add the full cost, since improvements are recoverable at sale.
B. Exclude the remodel entirely, since improvements do not affect market value.
C. Add half the cost, which is the customary recovery rate.
**D. Explain that the market decides what a component contributes, and that contribution and cost are frequently different numbers.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Contribution is what the market pays for the component, which may be more or less than its cost. There is no customary fraction, and improvements plainly can affect value.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Market analysis and pricing strategy), section 2.5

### 28. Why can a grid built on recorded sale prices overstate value in a market where seller credits are common?

**A. A recorded price that carried a large credit overstates the economic price the parties actually agreed to.**
B. Credits affect only the buyer's loan amount and not the price.
C. Credits are added to the recorded price by the county recorder.
D. Recorded prices exclude the buyer's closing costs, which understates the transaction.

> **Correct answer: A.** A credit means the buyer paid a higher nominal price in exchange for cash back. Comparing nominal numbers in a market that trades in net ones is a systematic overstatement, and it is invisible on the page.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Market analysis and pricing strategy), section 3.6

### 29. Two years after a closing a seller alleges the pricing analysis was careless. What answers the claim?

A. A fresh listing service query run today showing the property was priced correctly.
B. The fact that the property sold quickly, which establishes the price was right.
**C. The comparables as they existed then, the selection reasoning, the derivation of the adjustments, and the record of what the seller was shown and decided.**
D. The licensee's recollection of the pricing conversation.

> **Correct answer: C.** A re-run query returns different data and can contradict the original analysis. A fast sale proves nothing by itself, and recollection is the interested party's, two years old.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Market analysis and pricing strategy), section 6.6

### 30. A seller's net sheet comes back showing insufficient proceeds to pay off the existing loans. What is the licensee's obligation?

A. Assure the seller the lienholder will approve, since the alternative costs it more.
B. Proceed with the listing and address the shortfall if an offer arrives.
C. Recommend a short sale and explain the likely tax treatment.
**D. Tell the seller now, route the credit, tax, and deficiency questions to the appropriate professionals, and promise no outcome that depends on a lienholder.**

> **Correct answer: D.** The bad news arrived early, which is the net sheet's value. Tax and deficiency consequences are not the licensee's to explain, and whether a lienholder approves is its decision.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Market analysis and pricing strategy), section 4.6

### 31. Photographs of a listing were taken in spring; the property is being marketed in autumn and the landscaping has died. What is required?

A. Nothing, provided the listing states when the photographs were taken.
B. Nothing, provided the photographs were accurate when taken.
**C. Update the images, because truthfulness is measured against the property as it is now.**
D. Nothing, because landscaping is not a material feature.

> **Correct answer: C.** Images that no longer show the property's condition create a false impression regardless of when they were accurate, and a date stamp does not cure it.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 06 (Marketing, advertising, and fair access), section 1.3

### 32. A brokerage uses a platform's audience tools to restrict who sees a housing advertisement. How is that analyzed?

A. It is a media-buying decision outside the fair housing framework.
**B. The targeting is part of the advertisement and is a fair housing question; using the platform's tool does not move the responsibility to the platform.**
C. It is the platform's compliance obligation, not the advertiser's.
D. It is permissible as long as no protected characteristic is named in the parameters.

> **Correct answer: B.** Who was permitted to see the advertisement is part of the advertisement. A parameter that operates as a proxy is exclusion even though no characteristic is named.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 06 (Marketing, advertising, and fair access), section 2.5

### 33. A listing includes an equal housing opportunity statement but the copy describes the neighborhood as 'perfect for young professionals.' What is the effect of the statement?

**A. None — the disclaimer does not cure copy that sorts people, and the copy must be rewritten.**
B. The statement is sufficient if it appears in the same size type as the copy.
C. The statement shifts the analysis to whether anyone was actually excluded.
D. The statement cures the copy by declaring the brokerage's non-discriminatory intent.

> **Correct answer: A.** The equal housing statement is required in its own right and is not a cure. Copy conveying a preference about who a property is for is the violation, whatever accompanies it.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 06 (Marketing, advertising, and fair access), section 3.6

### 34. A seller instructs his agent not to advertise the property in a particular publication because of the readership it reaches. What must the agent do?

A. Follow the instruction and record that it came from the seller.
B. Withdraw from the listing without explaining the reason.
C. Follow it informally by not placing the advertisement without discussing why.
**D. Refuse the instruction, explain why, and escalate to the broker — an instruction that restricts who learns of the property on a protected basis is unlawful.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Documenting an unlawful instruction does not transfer responsibility, informal compliance achieves the same exclusion, and a silent withdrawal leaves the seller free to repeat it.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 06 (Marketing, advertising, and fair access), section 3.7

### 35. What must an advertising archive capture?

**A. The advertisement as it was actually published, with its date, because platforms crop, edit, expire, and delete.**
B. The invoice establishing where and when the advertisement ran.
C. The draft submitted for approval, which shows what the brokerage authorized.
D. A description of the advertisement's content prepared by the licensee.

> **Correct answer: A.** The archive answers what the consumer saw. A draft shows what was intended, and neither an invoice nor a description reproduces the published item.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 06 (Marketing, advertising, and fair access), section 6.3

### 36. An applicant states she has an assistance animal and the property has a no-pets policy. What is the correct treatment?

A. The policy applies, since it is applied uniformly to every applicant.
B. The animal is permitted subject to the standard pet deposit.
**C. The animal is not a pet for this purpose; waiving the policy is a reasonable accommodation and the ordinary pet deposit and pet rent do not attach.**
D. The animal is permitted only after the applicant provides a diagnosis.

> **Correct answer: C.** An assistance animal is not a pet, so pet fees do not attach — though the tenant remains responsible for damage the animal actually causes. The diagnosis may not be demanded.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 06 (Marketing, advertising, and fair access), section 4.2

### 37. Which three things must a licensee keep distinct when assessing a buyer's readiness?

**A. What a lender will lend, what the buyer can carry, and what cash he will actually have at closing.**
B. The buyer's income, his credit score, and his debt.
C. The down payment, the loan amount, and the purchase price.
D. The preapproval amount, the appraised value, and the list price.

> **Correct answer: A.** The three numbers answer different questions, and a buyer approved for an amount he cannot comfortably carry, or without the cash to close, is a transaction that fails during escrow.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 07 (Buyer representation and property selection), section 3.1

### 38. What must drive the properties shown to a buyer?

A. The licensee's judgment about where the buyer would be comfortable.
B. The areas where the brokerage holds the most listings.
C. The neighborhoods with the strongest recent appreciation.
**D. The buyer's own stated criteria, documented, so the search can be shown to have been built on nothing else.**

> **Correct answer: D.** A search documented against the buyer's stated criteria is the evidence that it was not built on anything else. Judgments about comfort are how steering happens with a benign motive.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 07 (Buyer representation and property selection), section 4.1

### 39. A general inspection report recommends further evaluation of a foundation by a qualified professional. What is the licensee's obligation?

A. Obtain the evaluation himself so the buyer has the answer before the deadline.
B. Summarize the finding for the buyer and let him decide without a written recommendation.
**C. Recommend the further evaluation in writing, record that it was recommended, and record the buyer's response if he declines.**
D. Advise whether the item is serious enough to warrant the additional cost.

> **Correct answer: C.** A general inspection that recommends further evaluation has said it did not answer the question. The licensee recommends and records; he does not assess the seriousness of a building system.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 07 (Buyer representation and property selection), section 5.3

### 40. A buyer decides to waive his investigation contingency entirely. What is the correct handling?

**A. Explain the consequence, document the recommendation that preceded the decision and the buyer's response, and proceed on his instruction.**
B. Refuse to submit the offer, since waiving investigation is against the buyer's interest.
C. Submit the offer and note that the property's condition is now the seller's responsibility.
D. Submit the offer and obtain an indemnity from the buyer in the brokerage's favor.

> **Correct answer: A.** The decision is the buyer's and the record is the licensee's. The property's condition is unchanged by the waiver, and an indemnity is not what the file needs.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 07 (Buyer representation and property selection), section 5.6

### 41. A buyer asks his agent what to offer. What is within the licensee's role?

A. State the number the buyer should offer, since that is the advice he is paying for.
B. Decline to discuss price, since offer terms are the client's alone.
C. Recommend the list price, which avoids advising on value.
**D. Supply the analysis — what the data supports, what the competition looks like, what each term costs — while the number and the terms remain the buyer's decision.**
