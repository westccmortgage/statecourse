# Real Estate Practice — Lesson Assignment 01

## California practice standards and the regulated professional

**Course version:** 1.0 development draft  
**Assignment time:** 180 instructional minutes  
**Regulatory status:** Not approved; no California credit is available.

This opening assignment establishes the professional framework used throughout Real Estate Practice. It does not treat a license as permission to improvise. The California license identifies a regulated role, and that role operates through defined authority, broker supervision, accurate communication, consumer protection, and reconstructable records.

## Learning objectives

After completing this assignment, the student will be able to:

- distinguish the California Department of Real Estate, a responsible broker, a broker-associate, a salesperson, and an unlicensed administrative assistant;
- identify activities that require a California real estate license and recognize when an issue must be escalated;
- explain why a salesperson’s authority flows through the responsible broker rather than directly from a consumer;
- apply the supervision principles reflected in Business and Professions Code sections 10159.2 and 10177(h) and Commissioner’s Regulation 2725;
- separate a verified fact, a client statement, a professional opinion, and an unresolved assumption;
- build a transaction record that shows who acted, under what authority, from which source, and at what time; and
- respond to pressure for speed without concealing uncertainty or exceeding the licensee’s competence.

## 1. The California regulatory structure

### 1.1 Why the structure matters

The California Department of Real Estate, commonly called DRE, administers and enforces the Real Estate Law within its jurisdiction. DRE licenses brokers and salespersons, maintains public license records, reviews education providers and statutory courses, investigates complaints, audits appropriate records, and may pursue administrative discipline when the evidence supports it. Its stated mission centers on safeguarding and promoting the public interest in real estate matters.

That public-protection purpose changes how a student should understand professional practice. A transaction is not compliant merely because it closes. A file must also show that the consumer received lawful service, material information was handled correctly, licensed activity was performed by authorized people, the broker exercised reasonable supervision, and funds and records were controlled appropriately.

The DRE does not replace the courts, the Legislature, local government, federal regulators, escrow, title, lenders, inspectors, appraisers, attorneys, or the parties to a contract. Each has a different source and scope of authority. A professional must identify which source controls the question instead of treating every form, custom, or online explanation as law.

### 1.2 A working hierarchy of authority

Use the following hierarchy when analyzing a California practice issue:

1. **Current law.** Begin with applicable statutes and regulations. The Business and Professions Code and the Regulations of the Real Estate Commissioner are central sources for licensing and brokerage conduct.
2. **The parties’ valid written agreements.** Listing agreements, buyer-representation agreements, purchase agreements, amendments, and escrow instructions allocate rights and duties within legal boundaries.
3. **DRE forms, advisories, and publications.** These explain regulatory expectations and recurring risks but should be read in context and checked for current revision dates.
4. **Broker policies and supervision.** The responsible broker translates legal duties into office procedures, review points, delegation limits, advertising approval, record controls, and escalation requirements.
5. **Professional custom.** Custom may explain how participants commonly coordinate work, but it cannot override law, contract language, a principal’s lawful instruction, or a broker’s supervisory decision.

An answer such as “that is how our office always does it” is incomplete. The student should be able to name the controlling source, identify the material facts, explain the application, and preserve the evidence.

### 1.3 License status is a fact to verify

California maintains a public license lookup. Before relying on a person’s authority, a brokerage should verify the license type, status, responsible broker relationship when relevant, and any restrictions shown in the official record. A business card, website biography, prior transaction, or completed education certificate is not proof that the person currently may perform licensed acts.

Pre-license education is not a license. Passing an examination is not, by itself, a license. A salesperson license that is not affiliated with a responsible broker does not authorize the person to perform acts requiring a real estate license. The distinction protects consumers from believing that a student, applicant, inactive licensee, or unaffiliated salesperson has authority that does not exist.

## 2. Licensed acts and license categories

### 2.1 Broker activity

Business and Professions Code section 10131 describes core activities that bring a person within the definition of a real estate broker when performed for another or others and for compensation or in expectation of compensation. The statute includes, among other categories, selling or offering to sell, buying or offering to buy, soliciting prospective sellers or buyers, negotiating a purchase, sale, or exchange, leasing or renting, collecting rents, and performing specified loan-related activity.

Students should not memorize only the verbs. The analysis usually requires four questions:

- What act is being performed?
- Is it being performed for another person rather than solely as a principal?
- Is compensation received or expected, directly or indirectly?
- Does an exemption or different regulatory framework apply?

The same physical task can have a different legal character depending on context. Handing a consumer a blank form is not the same as selecting terms, advising how to complete the form, or negotiating its provisions. Uploading a broker-approved advertisement is not the same as creating unverified claims or responding to a prospect with price and strategy advice.

### 2.2 Salesperson authority

A California salesperson is licensed to perform brokerage acts only through the responsible broker with whom the salesperson is affiliated. The salesperson does not become an independent brokerage merely because the client communicates directly with the salesperson, the salesperson generated the lead, or the salesperson expects part of the compensation.

This relationship has practical consequences:

- employment and affiliation must be accurately reflected in DRE records;
- licensed activity is conducted on behalf of the broker;
- compensation for licensed acts must be handled through the authorized broker relationship;
- brokerage contracts and trust-fund systems belong within broker control;
- advertising and team identity must not obscure the responsible brokerage; and
- the salesperson must follow lawful supervisory policies and escalate exceptions.

The responsible broker is not merely a payment processor. The broker’s role includes supervision and control necessary to secure compliance. A salesperson who intentionally bypasses review does not cure the problem by later placing a copy in the file.

### 2.3 Broker-associates and designated officers

A licensed broker may work in a relationship in which another broker is responsible for the brokerage activity. The label “broker-associate” is commonly used for a broker working under another responsible broker. The individual’s broker license does not automatically make every activity independent of the employing broker’s systems.

Corporate broker licensees act through designated broker-officers. Business and Professions Code section 10159.2 places specified supervision and control responsibility on the designated officer for activity conducted on behalf of the corporation. When responsibility is assigned within an organization, the assignment must comply with governing law; internal job titles alone do not rewrite statutory responsibility.

### 2.4 Unlicensed administrative assistants

An unlicensed administrative assistant may support a brokerage, but the assistant may not perform activity requiring a real estate license or a mortgage loan originator endorsement. DRE’s guidance emphasizes adequate supervision and the limits imposed by Business and Professions Code section 10137.

Examples of administrative support may include assembling records, scheduling under defined instructions, entering information without exercising licensed judgment, and distributing material already reviewed and authorized by the broker. The precise facts matter. An assistant crosses the line when administrative execution becomes solicitation, property or transaction advice, negotiation, discretionary showing activity, or another licensed act.

The safest method is not a vague rule such as “assistants can do paperwork.” A broker should maintain a written task matrix:

| Task | Authorized role | Required review | Evidence retained |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enter broker-approved listing data | Trained assistant | Licensee verifies before publication | Source sheet and approval record |
| Schedule using fixed instructions | Trained assistant | Escalate questions or changes | Appointment log |
| Explain contract terms | Licensee only | Broker escalation as required | Communication record |
| Recommend offer price or strategy | Licensee only | Broker supervision | Client instruction and analysis |
| Discuss loan qualification | Properly authorized professional | Mortgage and broker controls | Source and referral record |

### 2.5 Boundary exercise

Classify each request as administrative execution, licensed activity, or “insufficient facts—escalate”:

1. A seller asks the receptionist what price the seller should accept.
2. An assistant copies data from an approved source sheet into an MLS draft that a licensee must review before publication.
3. A prospect asks an assistant whether the neighborhood is safe for a particular family.
4. A salesperson asks an assistant to negotiate a showing condition with an occupant.
5. A broker gives an assistant a written script limited to confirming an appointment time.

For each answer, identify the act rather than relying on the person’s job title.

## 3. Reasonable broker supervision

### 3.1 Supervision is a system

Commissioner’s Regulation 2725 addresses broker supervision. A reasonable system ordinarily includes policies, rules, procedures, and review appropriate to the brokerage’s activities. Supervision should address regulated transactions, documents, trust funds where applicable, advertising, licensed and unlicensed personnel, records, and recurring legal requirements.

The word “reasonable” does not mean optional or informal. It recognizes that an effective control system depends on the organization’s actual work. A two-person residential brokerage and a multi-office company may implement different processes, yet each must create supervision that fits its risk, people, technology, and transaction volume.

A broker cannot reasonably supervise work the broker cannot see. Therefore, a functioning system establishes observable control points:

- license and affiliation verification;
- onboarding and role-based training;
- written policies available to personnel;
- approval paths for advertising and representations;
- contract and disclosure review;
- trust-fund receipt and reconciliation controls where applicable;
- access permissions for transaction and consumer data;
- exception reports for missing signatures, deadlines, and conflicting versions;
- periodic review and documented correction; and
- escalation for legal, ethical, fair-housing, safety, and competency concerns.

### 3.2 Delegation does not erase responsibility

A broker may delegate tasks and may assign supervisory functions appropriately, but delegation does not convert an unauthorized person into a licensee or eliminate the responsible broker’s legal duties. The broker must select people suited to the task, define authority, supply training and resources, monitor performance, and respond to exceptions.

Similarly, a salesperson cannot defend an unauthorized action by saying, “the transaction coordinator handled it.” The correct analysis asks who performed the act, who had authority, what supervision existed, what the licensee knew or should have known, and how the problem was addressed.

### 3.3 Remote work and technology

Modern supervision must include technology used for brokerage work. Remote access, automated drafting, electronic signatures, customer-relationship systems, artificial intelligence, messaging platforms, and shared drives can improve service, but they do not become responsible licensees.

DRE’s March 17, 2026 advisory on AI in California real estate emphasizes that licensees remain responsible for reviewing output and that broker supervision extends to tools used in licensed and unlicensed activity. A system-generated statement about a property, consumer, contract, or market must be verified before professional reliance. “The software wrote it” is not a compliance defense.

A prudent technology control asks who may use the system, which data may be entered, what output requires human review, who approves external communication, how changes and approvals are logged, how confidential information is protected, and what happens when the system produces conflicting information.

### 3.4 Responding to a supervisory exception

When a salesperson discovers a possible mistake, the professional response is prompt and reconstructable:

1. Stop further dissemination or irreversible action when reasonably possible.
2. Preserve the original communication or document.
3. Notify the responsible broker through the approved escalation path.
4. Identify affected parties, deadlines, and decisions.
5. Verify the controlling facts and source.
6. Correct the record through an authorized communication or amendment.
7. Document delivery, response, and follow-up.
8. Adjust the process if the event reveals a systemic weakness.

Concealing a mistake to protect a commission can compound the original issue. Risk management is not the absence of errors; it is a system that prevents, detects, escalates, corrects, and learns from them.

### Real-world example

A salesperson copies an earlier listing description stating that a converted garage is permitted living area. No current permit record has been reviewed. The broker’s advertising checklist requires source verification, but the salesperson publishes before approval.

The proper response is not merely to add “buyer to verify.” The listing should be paused or corrected, the original version preserved, the factual basis investigated through reliable records and appropriate specialists, affected consumers informed as required, and the broker’s review documented. The office should also determine why its approval control did not prevent publication.

## 4. Competence, scope, and professional judgment

### 4.1 The license is not universal expertise

A real estate license authorizes regulated activity within its legal and supervisory framework. It does not make the licensee an attorney, tax adviser, structural engineer, environmental professional, land surveyor, licensed appraiser, insurance producer, or lender underwriter. A professional can explain transaction process and known facts without pretending to provide a specialist conclusion.

Competence begins with three distinctions:

- **What I know:** information verified from a reliable source.
- **What I was told:** a statement attributed to a principal, professional, public record, or other source.
- **What remains unresolved:** a conflict, missing record, or question requiring investigation or specialist judgment.

Weak practice collapses these categories. For example, “the seller said the addition was permitted” becomes “the addition is permitted.” Strong practice preserves the attribution and recommends verification appropriate to the decision.

### 4.2 The fact–authority–application–record method

StateCourse uses the following method throughout the California curriculum:

1. **Fact:** Identify the material known facts and the facts still missing.
2. **Authority:** Locate the current law, contract, broker policy, or professional source that governs the issue.
3. **Application:** Explain how the authority applies to the verified facts, including reasonable alternatives and limitations.
4. **Record:** Preserve the source, analysis, communication, instruction, decision, delivery evidence, and follow-up.

This method slows down unsupported conclusions without creating artificial delay. It is especially useful when a participant pressures the agent to move before a material question is resolved.

### 4.3 When to escalate

Escalation is appropriate when facts conflict, a material record is missing, the controlling authority is unclear, a deadline may cause waiver or deposit risk, a client asks for concealment, a fair-housing concern appears, a task may exceed the person’s authority, consumer funds or information may be at risk, specialist advice is needed, or a prior instruction conflicts with a new fact.

Escalation does not mean abandoning the consumer. The licensee should explain what can be done, what cannot yet be concluded, what source or professional is needed, and what deadline remains.

### 4.4 Neutral communication under pressure

A professional message separates fact from accusation:

> The file presently contains two different descriptions of the garage conversion. We have the seller’s statement, but we do not yet have a permit record confirming the current legal status. Because this information may affect your decision and today’s contingency deadline, I have asked my broker to review the file. You may also wish to obtain information from the appropriate public agency and qualified professionals. Please do not treat the conversion as verified until that review is complete.

The message does not diagnose a violation, guarantee a result, or hide the deadline. It identifies the evidence, uncertainty, impact, supervision, and next step.

### Applied exercise: authority map

A seller tells a newly affiliated salesperson to advertise a detached structure as a “legal guest house.” The seller supplies an old appraisal using the phrase “guest unit,” but the public record available to the brokerage describes the property differently. The seller insists that the listing go live before the weekend and says the issue can be corrected later. An unlicensed marketing assistant has already drafted the advertisement.

Prepare a written authority map containing:

1. the known facts and their sources;
2. the unresolved facts;
3. the licensed acts involved;
4. the people who may act and the limits of each role;
5. the responsible broker’s required control point;
6. the consumer risk created by the unverified statement;
7. the authorized next steps before publication; and
8. the documents and timestamps the file should preserve.

The strongest answer does not decide whether the structure is legal. It explains why that conclusion is not yet supported and builds a lawful path to verification.

## 5. Consumer-first communication

### 5.1 Information must support a real decision

Consumer-first communication enables a person to understand material facts, costs, choices, risks, deadlines, conflicts, and uncertainty before making a decision. The licensee should use plain language without removing legally important meaning.

Good communication answers what happened, why it matters now, which information supports the statement, what remains unknown, what choices the consumer controls, what deadline applies, what assistance is available, and how the decision will be documented.

Pressure, concealment, and selective explanation undermine informed choice. A licensee should not create false urgency, guarantee a financial or legal result, minimize a known risk to preserve the transaction, or give materially different service based on assumptions about a consumer.

### 5.2 Consistent service and fair access

Professional workflows should create comparable access for similarly situated consumers. Objective intake questions, written response standards, documented property-selection criteria, accommodation procedures, and broker review reduce the risk that unexamined assumptions change who receives information or opportunity.

Consistency does not mean refusing legally appropriate accommodation. It means that differences in service have a legitimate, documented basis rather than a protected characteristic, stereotype, accent, appearance, family status, source of lawful income, disability, or another improper factor.

### 5.3 Communication channels and evidence

Text messages, email, electronic signatures, portals, phone calls, and in-person discussions can all carry material information. The brokerage should know how relevant communication enters the transaction record. A promise made in a text does not become unimportant because the formal file uses another system.

After a material oral discussion, a neutral written confirmation can prevent misunderstanding:

> This message confirms our discussion at 2:15 p.m. You instructed us not to publish the advertisement until the broker completes review of the property-use statement. We will update you after the source record is received. The proposed weekend publication remains on hold.

The confirmation identifies time, instruction, issue, and next step without unnecessary rhetoric.

### Knowledge check

A buyer asks whether a hillside property is “completely safe.” The file contains a seller disclosure, a general inspection report, and a recommendation for further geological evaluation. The professional response is to avoid guaranteeing safety, identify the records received, highlight the recommendation and deadline, encourage review by an appropriate professional, and document the buyer’s instruction and contractual action.

## 6. Transaction-file accountability

### 6.1 The file should reconstruct the decision

A transaction file is not simply a storage folder. It is a chronological record showing authority, information, disclosure, instruction, professional action, and outcome. A later reviewer should be able to determine what the licensee knew at a given time and why the next action occurred.

A defensible file commonly includes verified identity and authority information; agency and employment documents; property facts and source records; advertisements and approval history; offers, counteroffers, amendments, and delivery evidence; disclosures and acknowledgments; investigation records; deadline communications; escrow, title, closing, and possession records; material client instructions and broker review; receipts and trust-fund records when applicable; and corrections that preserve prior versions.

### 6.2 Version control

Draft, executed, superseded, and corrected documents are not interchangeable. A file should show the document title and version, who created or supplied it, when it was received, who signed or approved it, when and how it was delivered, whether a later document amended it, and which version controlled the decision.

Deleting the original to make a file look clean destroys part of the history. Correction should add a transparent record rather than silently rewrite the past.

### 6.3 Exception log

An exception log makes unresolved items visible:

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Issue | Public record and appraisal describe detached structure differently |
| Discovered | March 4, 2026, 10:20 a.m. |
| Affected action | Listing publication |
| Owner | Listing salesperson under broker review |
| Source requested | Current public-agency record; seller documentation |
| Deadline | Proposed publication March 6 |
| Interim control | Advertising held |
| Resolution | Pending |

The log does not decide legal status. It prevents the brokerage from treating an unresolved fact as completed.

### 6.4 Assignment synthesis

The regulated professional follows a repeatable chain:

**Authorized person → verified fact → current authority → supervised application → clear consumer communication → preserved record.**

If one link is missing, the professional stops at the exception. Speed is valuable only after authority and material facts are controlled.

### Required student response

Using the guest-house scenario from Section 4, draft a six-sentence consumer communication. It must identify the conflicting sources, state that legal status is not verified, avoid a legal conclusion, identify broker review and verification, state that publication is paused, and preserve the timing issue.

### Assignment summary

- DRE regulates California real estate licensees and administers the Real Estate Law within its jurisdiction.
- Licensed activity is defined by the act and context, not merely by a job title.
- A salesperson performs licensed acts only through the responsible broker relationship.
- Unlicensed assistants may provide controlled administrative support but may not perform licensed acts.
- Reasonable supervision is an observable system of policies, review, monitoring, correction, and records.
