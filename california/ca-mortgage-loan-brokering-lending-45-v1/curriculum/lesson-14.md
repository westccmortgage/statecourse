## 1. ECOA and fair housing lending protections

### 1.1 Two overlapping frameworks

A mortgage transaction is reached by **two federal frameworks that overlap and are not identical**, plus California law that is broader than both.

| Framework | Reaches |
|---|---|
| **ECOA / Regulation B** | Discrimination in **any aspect of a credit transaction** |
| **Fair Housing Act** | Discrimination in **residential real-estate-related transactions**, including lending |
| **California law** | The federal characteristics **and more** |

### 1.2 The prohibited bases

**ECOA:** race, color, religion, national origin, sex, **marital status**, age (with capacity to contract), **receipt of public assistance income**, and the good faith exercise of a right under the Consumer Credit Protection Act.

**Fair Housing Act:** race, color, religion, sex, national origin, **familial status**, and **disability**.

**Neither list contains the other.** Marital status and public assistance income are distinctive to ECOA; familial status and disability are distinctive to the Fair Housing Act.

> **California protects the federal characteristics and adds others** — including source of income, sexual orientation, gender identity and expression, ancestry, primary language, immigration status, military and veteran status, and more. **The list has been added to repeatedly.**

### 1.3 Why the practice cannot be memorization

Because the list grows, and because a decision that turns on a characteristic is a problem regardless of which list it appears on.

> **The reliable practice is to apply criteria consistently and to document the basis of every decision** — which produces defensible outcomes against a list that changes.

**"It's not one of the federal bases" is not clearance.** It is the reasoning that produces California violations.

### 1.4 "Any aspect" is broad

ECOA reaches far more than the approve-or-deny decision:

- **Advertising and solicitation** — including who is reached.
- **The application process** — who is encouraged, who is discouraged.
- **Information requested** and how it is treated.
- **Evaluation** and the application of criteria.
- **Terms and pricing**, including discretion exercised over them.
- **Exceptions** and to whom they are granted.
- **The treatment of an existing account.**

### 1.5 What may and may not be asked

**Generally may not be requested** in most consumer credit contexts: race, color, religion, national origin, or sex — **except** for monitoring purposes.

**Marital status inquiries are limited** to defined categories in defined circumstances, and **a creditor generally may not require a spouse's signature where the applicant qualifies individually.**

**Alimony, child support, or separate maintenance** need not be disclosed unless the applicant chooses to rely on it, **and the applicant must be told so.**

**Public assistance income may not be discounted** because of its source.

### 1.6 The monitoring information paradox

For applications for credit secured by a dwelling, a creditor is generally **required to request** ethnicity, race, and sex for monitoring purposes.

> **The information the creditor may not use in the decision is the information it is required to collect.**

**The applicant may decline and must be told so** — and where the application is taken in person and the applicant declines, the rule prescribes noting the information on the basis of visual observation or surname, **so the data is not defeated by non-response.**

**An originator who skips the request because it feels invasive, or fills it in from assumption where the rule does not call for it, has corrupted the data the entire fair lending framework depends on.**

### 1.7 Adverse action

**Notice within a defined period**, containing **either the actual specific reasons or a disclosure of the right to obtain them**, the ECOA notice identifying the enforcement agency, and — where a consumer report was used — the **FCRA content**: the agency's name, address, and telephone; a statement that the agency did not make the decision and cannot supply the reasons; and the rights to a free copy and to dispute.

> **"Does not meet our credit standards" tells an applicant nothing usable and does not satisfy the requirement's purpose.**

### 1.8 Checkpoint

You should be able to name both federal lists and what is distinctive to each, explain why practice cannot be memorization, state what "any aspect" reaches, and explain the monitoring paradox.

## 2. Redlining and pricing disparities

### 2.1 What redlining is

**Denying or varying the terms of credit or insurance by area**, rather than by the characteristics of the applicant or the transaction.

**Its historical operation is part of why present-day patterns exist.** Federally influenced appraisal and lending practice once graded neighborhoods for lending risk, with grades correlated to racial composition — and **areas graded poorly received less mortgage capital for decades**, which suppressed maintenance, values, and the accumulation of housing equity in exactly the neighborhoods already excluded.

### 2.2 Why it compounds

> **Housing is the principal vehicle of wealth accumulation for most American families. Wealth built through housing is inherited, and inherited wealth becomes the down payment for the next generation.**

**Exclusion from credit therefore does not end when the exclusionary practice ends. It compounds** — which explains why equal treatment starting now does not by itself produce equal outcomes.

**What this does and does not imply for a licensee.** It does **not** imply that a licensee should attempt to correct history through decisions about who gets which product — steering a borrower on a protected basis is prohibited regardless of the intent behind it, **including a corrective intent.**

It **does** imply recognizing inherited patterns rather than reading them as natural facts about places, refusing to transmit them as market wisdom, applying uniform practices, and giving consumers objective information.

### 2.3 The modern forms

Redlining today rarely looks like a map with a line on it.

| Form | How it operates |
|---|---|
| **Marketing reach** | Advertising and solicitation that reach some areas and not others |
| **Branch and staffing distribution** | Where the firm places people who take applications |
| **Referral source composition** | A business built entirely on referrals reproduces the existing client base |
| **Lead purchasing criteria** | Buying leads filtered in ways that correlate with protected characteristics |
| **Digital audience targeting** | Selecting who sees a housing credit advertisement |
| **Algorithmic and data-driven decisions** | Models that reproduce historical patterns without anyone intending it |
| **Loan product availability by area** | Offering different products in different places without a documented neutral basis |

> **Digital targeting deserves particular attention.** Restricting who may see a credit or housing advertisement can deny groups access to the opportunity, and **using a platform's tool does not shift responsibility** — "the platform offered that option" is not an answer.

### 2.4 Pricing disparities

Where **pricing discretion** exists — including discretion an originator exercises over compensation within a permitted structure, or over whether to grant a concession — **disparities are what the data surfaces.**

**HMDA data includes a rate spread measure**, which supports comparison of pricing across applicants. **A firm whose pricing differs by group without a documented neutral explanation is visible in data long before anyone complains.**

> **This is the answer to "nobody has complained."** Complaints are one route. **Data is another, and it does not require anyone to be dissatisfied.**

### 2.5 Exceptions

**Exceptions to guidelines are a fair lending issue**, not merely an underwriting one.

**Where exceptions are granted more readily to some applicants than others without a documented neutral basis, the pattern is exactly what a fair lending examination looks for** — and the exceptions themselves are the evidence, because each one was recorded.

**Every exception is documented with its rationale**, and the rationale must be a reason someone else could apply to a similar file.

### 2.6 Discouragement

**The form that produces a denial with no record of one**, and therefore the one that is hardest to detect and most dangerous to permit.

> **"They probably wouldn't qualify" is not an assessment an originator is entitled to make**, and acting on it — by not taking the application, by not returning the call, by suggesting the consumer "look at something more affordable" — is discouragement.

**ECOA reaches discouragement directly**, because it reaches any aspect of a credit transaction including the application process.

### 2.7 The controls

**Structure beats effort**, because effort depends on noticing and much of what produces unequal outcomes operates outside awareness.

| Control | What it constrains |
|---|---|
| **Written criteria applied consistently** | The decision itself |
| **Uniform intake scripts and response times** | Differential treatment at first contact |
| **Take every application** | Discouragement |
| **Documented exception rationales** | Exception patterns |
| **Constrained or documented pricing discretion** | Pricing disparities |
| **Advertising and targeting review** | Reach |
| **Outcome review** | Patterns no individual decision reveals |

### 2.8 Outcome review

**The practice most likely to detect a problem, and the least used.** Periodically examine **outcomes rather than intentions**:

- Application, approval, denial, and withdrawal rates.
- Pricing and rate spread.
- Exceptions granted and to whom.
- Response times, grouped by source.
- Where marketing reached, and whom.

> **Individual decisions rarely reveal anything. Patterns do** — and a firm that reviews its own patterns finds problems while they are still correctable.

### 2.9 Checkpoint

You should be able to describe redlining's modern forms, explain why exclusion compounds, identify where pricing discretion creates exposure, and describe outcome review.

## 3. Occupancy, income, and appraisal fraud

### 3.1 What mortgage fraud is

**A material misrepresentation or omission relied on by a lender in making a credit decision.**

Two conventional categories:

**Fraud for housing** — misrepresentation by a borrower who intends to repay and wants the property.

**Fraud for profit** — schemes intended to extract money from the transaction, frequently involving multiple participants including industry professionals.

> **Both are fraud. The second is where a licensee is most likely to be recruited**, and it rarely arrives labeled.

### 3.2 Occupancy fraud

**Occupancy affects pricing, program eligibility, and which consumer protections apply.** It is therefore **a material fact the lender relies on, and misstating it is loan fraud.**

**The patterns:**

| Pattern | What it suggests |
|---|---|
| A "primary residence" the borrower will not move into | Occupancy misrepresentation |
| A second home in the same city as the primary | Investment property described otherwise |
| **A lease on the subject property dated before the application** | The file contains its own contradiction |
| **Rental income on the returns for a property represented as owner-occupied** | Contradicted by the borrower's own filings |
| A borrower who cannot describe the property relative to his work | A straw or absentee arrangement |
| Multiple simultaneous "primary residence" applications | Coordinated misrepresentation |
| A borrower who asks what occupancy "gets the best rate" | Selection by pricing rather than by fact |

**The two bolded rows are different in kind.** They are not ambiguities to resolve by asking — **the file contains the evidence contradicting a material representation**, and the response is escalation rather than a judgment call.

> **When a borrower says "I can afford either payment anyway," the answer is that affordability is beside the point.** The misstatement is about **obtaining terms on a false representation of the risk being priced.**

### 3.3 Income and employment fraud

| Pattern | Signal |
|---|---|
| Documents that appear altered | Inconsistent fonts, alignment, arithmetic that does not total |
| An employer that cannot be independently verified | No independent listing, a mobile number, an address that is a mail drop |
| Income inconsistent with the occupation or the documented history | A sharp unexplained increase |
| Deposits inconsistent with documented income | Sourcing |
| A verbal verification answered by someone who seems prepared for it | Coached |
| Tax documents that do not reconcile with paystubs | Fabrication |
| A borrower reluctant to authorize independent verification | The verification is the point |

**The originator's discipline is not detection expertise. It is refusing to be the person who passed it along:**

- **Never alter a document.**
- **Never complete one in the borrower's voice**, including a letter of explanation.
- **Never submit one known to be inaccurate.**
- **Never advise seasoning, splitting a deposit, or recharacterizing a loan as a gift.**
- **Never represent that a verification occurred** when it did not.

### 3.4 Straw buyers and identity misuse

**A straw buyer** applies in his own name for someone else's benefit, frequently for compensation and frequently without understanding the exposure.

**The signals:** a borrower who does not know basic facts about the property; someone else answering the questions; a borrower whose profile does not fit the transaction; an intermediary controlling all communication; and a borrower who has never seen the property.

**Elder financial abuse** is a related and serious pattern — an older borrower brought in by a third party who controls the conversation, on a transaction that benefits the third party. **California has specific reporting frameworks for elder abuse**, and this belongs on the escalate-immediately list.

### 3.5 Appraisal fraud and value inflation

**The patterns:**

- **Pressure on the appraiser** — a target communicated in any form.
- **Appraiser selection by favorable history**, which builds coercion into the ordering pattern without anyone saying anything prohibited.
- **Comparable manipulation** — sales selected or adjusted to reach a number.
- **Flipping schemes** — a rapid resale at a sharply higher price with no corresponding improvement.
- **A value inconsistent with the market** that nobody questions.

**The discipline:** supply **information, never a target**; use the **defined channel** rather than contacting the appraiser; and **request a reconsideration with data and factual corrections and no number.**

> **A request to "take another look" that arrives with a number attached is the pattern to refuse — including when it comes from your own borrower, your own agent, or your own manager.**

### 3.6 Transaction-level schemes

| Scheme | Shape |
|---|---|
| **Silent second** | An undisclosed second financing the down payment, so the borrower has no equity the lender knows about |
| **Undisclosed seller concession** | Value inflated to fund a concession, so the price is not what it appears |
| **Air loan** | A loan on a property, borrower, or both that do not exist |
| **Shotgunning** | Multiple loans on the same property closed before any record |
| **Builder bailout** | Concessions and incentives structured to move inventory at an apparent price |
| **Flipping with inflated appraisals** | Rapid resale at an unsupported value |

**What most of them have in common: a number that is not what it appears to be, and a participant who benefits from nobody checking.**

### 3.7 What a licensee must never do

- **Participate**, however small the requested act.
- **Investigate alone.**
- **Confront a suspected participant.**
- **Warn a suspected participant** that concerns have been raised.
- **Withdraw quietly**, leaving the next licensee to face the file blind.
- **Assume someone else will catch it.**

### 3.8 Checkpoint

You should be able to define mortgage fraud, name the occupancy and income patterns, identify a file that contradicts itself, and state what a licensee must never do.

## 4. Identity and cyber fraud

### 4.1 Why this sits in a mortgage course

Because **a mortgage transaction moves large sums on a schedule attackers can read**, and because the industry's communications are predictable — the same parties, the same documents, the same week.

### 4.2 Wire fraud

**The most damaging single loss a consumer can suffer in a real estate transaction.**

**The pattern:** an attacker monitors a compromised email account, learns the parties and the schedule, then sends altered wire instructions from an address one character different from the genuine one, **timed to the moment funds are expected.**

**The defenses, in order of value:**

1. **The written warning at application** — wire instructions will never be changed by email, and the consumer must **verify by voice at an independently obtained number** before sending anything.
2. **Treat any change to instructions as presumptively fraudulent** until verified through a channel the attacker does not control.
3. **Never verify using a number, link, or address from the email carrying the instructions.**

**What does not work:** replying to the email to confirm — the attacker receives the reply; forwarding it with "be careful"; a small test wire, which confirms nothing about who controls the account; or proceeding because the message arrived in a familiar thread.

> **Sent at application the warning costs nothing and prevents the loss. Sent the day funds move, it arrives after the attacker has already established the thread.**

**Report any incident or near-miss to the broker immediately**, whether or not funds moved.

### 4.3 Business email compromise generally

Wire instructions are the highest-value target, but the same compromise supports **payoff demand alteration**, **fraudulent document requests**, and **impersonation of a party** in negotiation.

**The control is the same: verify anything consequential through a channel the attacker does not control.**

### 4.4 Identity fraud and red flags

Rules require programs to detect and respond to indicators of identity theft. **The recurring red flags:**

| Red flag |
|---|
| Identification documents that appear altered or inconsistent |
| An address inconsistent with the credit report or other file documents |
| A Social Security number inconsistent with other records |
| A credit report showing activity the applicant does not recognize |
| A borrower unable to answer basic identifying questions |
| An unusual number of recent inquiries or newly opened accounts |
| A mail drop or commercial address given as a residence |
| A borrower who will not appear in person or on video |

> **A licensee who notices a red flag escalates rather than resolving it.** The point of a program is that the response is defined in advance rather than improvised.

### 4.5 Seller impersonation

An increasingly common pattern, and one a mortgage licensee should recognize because it reaches refinances and purchases alike:

**Someone purporting to own a property — frequently unimproved land or a non-owner-occupied property — attempts to sell or encumber it.**

**The signals:** a seller who will not appear; contact only by email or text; urgency; a property with no mortgage and an absent owner; instructions to send proceeds somewhere unexpected; and identification that does not withstand ordinary verification.

**Escalate to the broker and to escrow and title.** Do not confront, and do not proceed while unresolved.

### 4.6 Information security

**Access control** — limit who can reach consumer information to those who need it for the work.

**Protected transmission** — **a loan file emailed unencrypted to a personal account is a security failure whatever the intention.**

**Secure disposal** — destroy documents and media so the data cannot be reconstructed. **A recycling bin is not disposal.**

**And a breach is a reportable event with timelines.** Severity is determined by **what data was exposed and whether it was protected** — not by whether anyone has complained. **Password protection is not encryption, and waiting is a decision with a deadline attached.**

### 4.7 Checkpoint

You should be able to describe the wire fraud pattern and the defenses in order, state what does not work, name the identity red flags, and describe the seller impersonation signals.

## 5. Suspicious facts and escalation

### 5.1 Fraud presents as a pattern

**The most useful thing a licensee can learn about fraud is that it rarely presents as one obvious falsehood. It presents as a pattern of small inconsistencies, each individually explainable.**

> **That is why "something doesn't add up but no single item is clearly false" is the moment to escalate rather than the moment to proceed.**

**Proceeding because suspicion is not proof is how licensees end up as the documented participant in someone else's scheme.**

### 5.2 The signals worth recognizing

| Signal | What it may indicate |
|---|---|
| Pressure to close unusually fast, with changing parties, funding sources, or documents | The urgency exists to defeat verification |
| A principal who never appears, acting through an intermediary | Identity or authority problems |
| Identification inconsistent with other documents | Identity fraud |
| A price materially inconsistent with the property and market, unexplained | Value or flipping fraud |
| Funds from parties unconnected to the transaction | Money laundering exposure |
| **A file containing its own contradiction** | Occupancy or purpose misrepresentation |
| Requests to alter, backdate, or omit documents | A direct participation request |
| A seller who cannot produce evidence of authority to convey | Title or authority fraud |
| Changed or unexpected funding instructions | Wire fraud |

### 5.3 What is not suspicious

**Equally important, so scrutiny is not applied selectively — which is its own violation:**

- An aggressive negotiator.
- A borrower who insists on attorney review.
- Self-employed, commission-based, or variable income.
- A borrower whose income has risen substantially, with documentation.
- A borrower who asks many questions.
- A borrower who is difficult to reach because of a work schedule.
- **A borrower's name, accent, appearance, or the neighborhood of the property.**

> **Applying heightened scrutiny based on characteristics rather than on the pattern above is where fair lending violations begin** — which is why this section sits in the same lesson as Section 1.

### 5.4 The escalation

**Escalate to the responsible broker.** Promptly, in writing, with the facts rather than a conclusion.

**What the escalation contains:** what was observed, when, from what source, what documents are involved, and what has not yet been done. **Not** an accusation, and **not** a legal characterization.

**The broker determines what reporting is required.** Suspicious activity reporting obligations, where they apply, attach to institutions under defined frameworks, and **whether and how a report is made is not an individual originator's decision.**

### 5.5 What must never happen

- **Investigating alone.**
- **Confronting a suspected participant.**
- **Warning a suspected participant.**
- **Withdrawing quietly** and leaving the file for the next licensee.
- **Participating**, however small the requested act.
- **Documenting an improper instruction and following it anyway.**

> **Following an improper instruction and documenting that it came from someone else does not transfer responsibility for the act. It documents that you knew.**

### 5.6 The instruction from inside the firm

The hardest version, and the one licensees actually face.

> A manager says: move the employment date earlier, leave off the small debt, mark the verbal verification complete, process it as business-purpose, add our name so the loan is exempt, or print and backdate the missing acknowledgment.

**Refuse. Do not transmit the file. Escalate above the person who gave the instruction.**

**Two justifications will be offered and neither works:** that the file would have been fine anyway — **if that were true the alteration was unnecessary, and it was still made** — and that the manager directed it.

### 5.7 What the file must show

- **What was observed**, when, and from what source.
- **What was escalated**, to whom, and when.
- **What the licensee declined to do**, and why.
- **The instruction received**, where one was declined.
- **The outcome**, or that it remains open.

> **A licensee who escalated and documented it is in a completely different position from one who noticed and said nothing** — even where both files ended the same way.

### 5.8 Checkpoint

You should be able to explain why fraud presents as a pattern, separate signals from noise, describe a correct escalation, and state the response to an improper instruction from a manager.

## 6. Ethical product presentation and documentation

### 6.1 The standard

**Honesty and fair dealing run to everyone in the transaction** — the borrower, the lender, investors, escrow, title, the appraiser, and any purchaser of the loan.

**And most claims against licensees are not fraud claims.** They are **negligence** claims: a statement made without reasonable basis or reasonable care, breached by carelessness alone with no dishonesty at all.

### 6.2 The three categories of statement

The practical discipline that prevents most of it:

1. **Verified fact** — stated with its source.
2. **Unverified report** — attributed and flagged as unverified.
3. **Professional opinion** — identified as opinion.

> **Nearly every misrepresentation in mortgage practice is a category collapse:** an unverified figure delivered as a fact, or a prediction delivered as a certainty.

### 6.3 The sentences that produce complaints

| Sentence | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| **"You can just refinance before the balloon."** | A prediction stated as a plan. **The single most damaging sentence in mortgage sales**, because the borrower stops evaluating the risk |
| "Rates will be lower by spring." | A prediction about markets nobody controls |
| "You definitely qualify." | An underwriting conclusion the licensee may not make |
| "That fee is standard." | Unsupportable, and a fee question belongs to compliance |
| "You need a 680 score." | A threshold stated as a rule |
| "Your reserves will make up for it." | An underwriting determination |
| "It'll be fine." | Reassurance that cannot be supported |
| "This is basically approved." | Misdescribes the file's status |
| "Those panels come up all the time." | A specialist's assessment |

### 6.4 The presentation standard

Products are presented **on a common basis**, and **a comparison that omits a material difference is misleading even when every number in it is accurate.**

1. Same amount, term, and structure — or the difference stated.
2. Note rate, payment, points, and lender charges, **each separately.**
3. The **break-even** where points differ.
4. The **APR**, identified as a comparison tool with its assumptions.
5. What is a lender charge and what is a third-party or prepaid item.
6. **The worst-case path** for any adjustable or non-amortizing feature.
7. **PITI**, not principal and interest alone.

> **The most common and least noticed misleading comparison is a worst case shown for one product and not the other.**

### 6.5 The features requiring plain language

For each: **state that it exists, what it does, the number it produces, and when.**

**Adjustable rate** — index, margin, caps, the **fully indexed rate**, and the worst-case payment.
**Interest-only** — principal is not retired, and the payment recalculates over a shorter remaining term.
**Balloon** — the amount due, and that all three paths depend on conditions then.
**Negative amortization** — the balance can grow while payments are made.
**Temporary buydown** — the payment after it ends, with equal prominence.
**Prepayment penalty** — what triggers it, how long, and what it costs.
**Mortgage insurance** — that it protects the lender, what it costs, and whether it ends.
**Escrow** — that taxes and insurance are included and **the payment can change.**

### 6.6 Steering and suitability

**The compensation framework prohibits steering a consumer to a transaction to increase the originator's compensation where the consumer is not better off.**

**And the ethical standard is broader than the rule:** a licensee must be able to show **why this borrower is in this product**, and the file must support it.

> **The recurring failure is not a wrong calculation. It is presenting the product the borrower could be approved for as though it were the product that fit him** — and the borrower could not tell the difference.

**The controls are simple:** ask the holding-period question first, present the worst case alongside the best, present alternatives on a common basis, and **record what was presented and what was decided.**

### 6.7 Acknowledgment is not understanding

> **A signature proves delivery. It does not prove comprehension**, and treating an acknowledgment as evidence that a feature was explained is the error behind most disclosure complaints.

**What actually evidences an explanation:** a record of what was said, when, and by whom; **a written summary sent to the consumer** so it exists in his hands; his questions and the answers; and **a worst-case illustration in writing**, not only spoken.

### 6.8 Corrections

Where an error is discovered — by anyone, at any stage — it is **corrected immediately, in writing, to everyone who received the original**, and before anyone acts on it if that is still possible.

**The instinct to wait and see whether it matters is the instinct that converts a mistake into a misrepresentation.** **An error you know about and leave uncorrected is no longer an error.**

**Corrections are made visibly**, with the original preserved and the change attributable.

### 6.9 The line

> **Everything in this lesson is survivable — errors, complaints, findings, even discipline — provided the licensee's conduct in response was honest.**
>
> **Falsifying the record is not.** Creating a document after the fact and dating it to match, altering an executed document, deleting records that reflect badly, or removing surrounding documents so an inconsistency does not show converts a compliance problem into a question about integrity.
>
> **A documented gap is survivable. A manufactured or destroyed record ends a career.**

### 6.10 Checkpoint

You should be able to state the three categories, name the sentences that produce complaints, present products on a common basis, and explain why acknowledgment is not understanding.

| **Red flags** | Identity theft indicators requiring a **defined response** rather than improvisation. |
| **Seller impersonation** | A purported owner — often of unimproved or non-owner-occupied property — attempting to sell or encumber. |
| **Fraud presents as a pattern** | Small inconsistencies, each individually explainable. **"It doesn't add up but nothing is clearly false" is the moment to escalate.** |
| **Selective scrutiny** | Applying heightened suspicion to characteristics rather than the pattern. **Where fair lending violations begin.** |
| **The escalation** | Prompt, written, factual, to the responsible broker — **not an accusation and not a legal characterization.** The broker determines reporting. |
| **Documenting an improper instruction** | Does not transfer responsibility. **It documents that you knew.** |
| **Category collapse** | An unverified figure as fact, or a prediction as certainty. **Nearly every misrepresentation.** |
| **"You can just refinance"** | A prediction stated as a plan, and the single most damaging sentence in mortgage sales. |
| **Selective worst case** | Showing one for one product and not the other. The least noticed misleading comparison. |
| **Acknowledgment is not understanding** | A signature proves delivery. **"He signed it" answers a different question than "did he understand it."** |
| **Falsification** | The one category that is not survivable. |

## Practice problems

### Problem 1

An originator takes an application in person and skips the demographic request because it "feels
invasive." He tells a married applicant her husband must sign though she qualifies individually,
discounts another applicant's public assistance income as "not real income," and denies a third with a
notice reading only "does not meet our credit standards" — though a credit report contributed.

**Assess each under the fair lending frameworks.**

> **Solution.** **Four violations, and the first is the one originators most often get backwards.**
>
> **The monitoring information must be requested** on applications for credit secured by a dwelling. The
> paradox is real: **the information that may not be used in the decision is the information required to
> be collected.** The applicant **may decline and must be told so**, and where the application is taken in
> person and she declines, the rule prescribes noting it on visual observation or surname **so the data
> is not defeated by non-response.** Skipping it **corrupts the data the entire framework depends on.**
>
> **Requiring the spouse's signature is marital status discrimination** where the applicant qualifies
> individually — marital status being one of the two bases **distinctive to ECOA.**
>
> **Public assistance income may not be discounted because of its source** — the other distinctive basis.
>
> **The notice is deficient twice.** It must contain **the actual specific reasons or a disclosure of the
> right to obtain them** — "does not meet our credit standards" tells the applicant nothing usable — and
> because a **consumer report** contributed, it must additionally carry the **FCRA content**: the
> agency's name, address, and telephone; that the agency did not make the decision and cannot supply the
> reasons; and the rights to a free copy and to dispute.

### Problem 2

A firm's business comes entirely from three referral sources in two ZIP codes, its digital advertising
targets homeowners aged 30–50 in those areas, it buys leads filtered by "high-response neighborhoods,"
and its pricing discretion is used more often for referred applicants. The owner says the firm has never
denied anyone for a prohibited reason and has never received a complaint.

**Assess.**

> **Solution.** **Every element is a modern form of redlining, and the defense misunderstands how this is
> detected.**
>
> **Redlining today rarely looks like a map with a line on it.** Referral composition reproduces the
> existing client base; digital targeting restricts who may **see** the advertisement; lead criteria
> filtered by "response" correlate with characteristics nobody named; and pricing discretion exercised
> more readily for one group is **exactly what a rate spread comparison surfaces.**
>
> **Age-based targeting is itself a prohibited basis under ECOA**, and **"the platform offered that
> option" is not an answer** — using a platform's tool does not shift responsibility.
>
> **"Never denied anyone for a prohibited reason" answers the wrong question.** ECOA reaches **any aspect
> of a credit transaction** — advertising and solicitation, the application process, information
> requested, evaluation, **terms and pricing**, exceptions, and account treatment. Most of what this firm
> does happens before an application exists.
>
> **"Never received a complaint" is not a defense.** Complaints are one route. **HMDA data is another,
> and it does not require anyone to be dissatisfied** — a firm whose pricing or denial pattern differs by
> group without a documented neutral explanation is visible long before anyone complains.
>
> **The control the firm lacks is outcome review:** application, approval, denial, and withdrawal rates;
> pricing and rate spread; exceptions and to whom; response times by source; and where marketing reached.

### Problem 3

A manager grants underwriting exceptions "case by case, based on gut feel for the borrower," documents
none of the rationales, and tells originators not to take applications from people who "probably
wouldn't qualify — it just hurts our pull-through."

**Identify both problems.**

> **Solution.** **Both are fair lending exposures, and the second is the more dangerous.**
>
> **Undocumented exceptions are a fair lending issue, not merely an underwriting one.** Where exceptions
> are granted more readily to some applicants than others without a documented neutral basis, **the
> pattern is exactly what an examination looks for — and the exceptions themselves are the evidence,
> because each one was recorded.** "Gut feel" is the definition of a rationale nobody else can apply to a
> similar file. **Every exception is documented with a reason someone else could apply.**
>
> **The instruction not to take applications is discouragement**, and it is the form that **produces a
> denial with no record of one** — which is why it is hardest to detect and most dangerous to permit.
> **ECOA reaches discouragement directly**, because it reaches the application process.
>
> **"They probably wouldn't qualify" is not an assessment an originator is entitled to make.** Take the
> application, apply the criteria, and let the decision be recorded.
>
> **The controls:** written criteria applied consistently, uniform intake and response times, **take every
> application**, documented exception rationales, constrained or documented pricing discretion,
> advertising and targeting review, and outcome review.

### Problem 4

A file shows the borrower will occupy the property as his primary residence. It also contains a lease on
the subject property dated three weeks before the application, tax returns showing rental income from it,
and an address three hours from his employer. He tells the originator he can afford either payment
anyway, and his manager says to proceed since the ratios work.

**Assess.**

> **Solution.** **This is occupancy fraud, and two items are different in kind from the rest.**
>
> **Occupancy affects pricing, program eligibility, and which consumer protections apply**, which makes
> it **a material fact the lender relies on** — and misstating it is loan fraud.
>
> **"I can afford either payment anyway" is beside the point.** The misstatement is not about whether he
> will pay. It is about **obtaining terms on a false representation of the risk being priced.**
>
> **The distance is a fact to weigh. The lease and the tax returns are not.** They are not ambiguities to
> resolve by asking — **the file contains the evidence contradicting a material representation**, and the
> response is **escalation, not a judgment call about whether it matters.**
>
> **"The ratios work" is the same error as the affordability argument**, one level up. If the file would
> have been fine on accurate facts, the misrepresentation was unnecessary — **and it was still made.**
>
> **Decline, do not transmit the file, and escalate above the manager.** Following the instruction while
> noting it was directed **does not transfer responsibility for the act. It documents that you knew.**

### Problem 5

A borrower never appears in person or on video, communicates only through an intermediary, cannot
describe the property's location relative to his work, and provides paystubs whose arithmetic does not
total from an employer with no independent listing and a mobile contact number. An elderly co-borrower
was brought in by the same intermediary. The file is under pressure to close in nine days.

**Identify the patterns and state the response.**

> **Solution.** **This is a pattern rather than a single falsehood, which is exactly how fraud presents.**
>
> **Straw buyer signals:** a borrower who does not know basic facts about the property, someone else
> answering, and an intermediary controlling all communication.
>
> **Income fraud signals:** arithmetic that does not total, an employer that cannot be independently
> verified, a mobile number, no independent listing.
>
> **Identity red flags:** a borrower who will not appear in person or on video.
>
> **Urgency:** pressure to close fast, which frequently **exists to defeat verification.**
>
> **Elder financial abuse:** an older borrower brought in by a third party who controls the conversation,
> on a transaction that benefits the third party. **California has specific reporting frameworks**, and
> this belongs on the escalate-immediately list.
>
> **The response: escalate to the responsible broker promptly, in writing, with the facts rather than a
> conclusion** — what was observed, when, from what source, which documents, and what has not yet been
> done. **The broker determines what reporting is required.**
>
> **What must never happen: investigating alone, confronting or warning a suspected participant,
> withdrawing quietly and leaving the file for the next licensee, or participating in any requested act.**
>
> **And "suspicion is not proof" is not a reason to proceed** — it is how a licensee becomes the
> documented participant in someone else's scheme.

### Problem 6

Two days before funding, a borrower forwards an email from the existing escrow thread with updated wire
instructions. The reply-to address differs by one character, the amount and date match expectations. The
originator replies to the email asking escrow to confirm, then tells the borrower to send a small test
wire first.

**Assess and state the correct protocol.**

> **Solution.** **Both responses fail, and the first fails in the way that matters most: the attacker
> receives the reply.**
>
> **The pattern is textbook.** An attacker monitors a compromised email account, learns the parties and
> the schedule, and sends altered instructions **from an address one character different, timed to the
> moment funds are expected.** Matching the amount and date is evidence of surveillance, not of
> legitimacy.
>
> **What does not work:** replying to the email — the attacker receives it; forwarding it with "be
> careful"; **a small test wire, which confirms nothing about who controls the account**; and trusting a
> familiar thread.
>
> **The correct protocol:** **treat any change to instructions as presumptively fraudulent until verified
> through a channel the attacker does not control**, and **verify by voice at an independently obtained
> number** — from the engagement documents or the company's published listing, **never a number, link, or
> address from the email carrying the instructions.**
>
> **And the defense with the most value came earlier: the written warning at application** that
> instructions will never change by email and that the consumer must verify by voice before sending.
> **Sent at application it costs nothing and prevents the largest single loss a consumer can suffer.**
>
> **Report the incident to the broker immediately, whether or not funds moved.**

### Problem 7

An originator applies extra scrutiny to a borrower with an accent and to files on properties in one
neighborhood, while waving through an aggressive negotiator, a self-employed borrower with variable
income, and a borrower who insisted on attorney review — "those are the easy ones."

**Assess.**

> **Solution.** **He has inverted the analysis, and the inversion is itself a fair lending violation.**
>
> **None of what he waved through is suspicious.** An aggressive negotiator, a borrower who insists on
> attorney review, self-employed or variable income with documentation, a borrower who asks many
> questions, and one hard to reach because of a work schedule — **none of these is a fraud signal.**
>
> **And the two he scrutinized are characteristics, not patterns.** A borrower's **accent** and the
> **neighborhood of the property** are exactly what may not drive treatment — national origin under both
> federal frameworks, and area-based treatment being redlining's defining shape.
>
> > **Applying heightened scrutiny based on characteristics rather than on the pattern is where fair
> > lending violations begin** — which is why fraud recognition and fair lending sit in the same lesson.
>
> **The actual signals are transactional:** urgency with changing parties or funding, a principal who
> never appears, identification inconsistent with other documents, a price materially inconsistent with
> the market, funds from unconnected parties, a file containing its own contradiction, requests to alter
> or backdate, a seller who cannot show authority, and changed funding instructions.
>
> **Scrutiny is applied to the pattern, uniformly, and documented.**

### Problem 8

An originator presents one product to a borrower relocating on a two-year contract: a 30-year hybrid with
a five-year fixed period, 2 points, an interest-only period, and a prepayment penalty for three years. He
quotes principal and interest only, shows no worst-case table, says "you can just refinance before any of
that matters," and files a signed acknowledgment page as evidence the terms were explained.

**Identify every failure.**

> **Solution.** **Six failures, and the presentation was never a presentation.**
>
> **The holding-period question was never asked** — and it is the one that decides points, buydowns, and
> hybrid terms. A borrower on a two-year contract paying **2 points** faces a break-even he will never
> reach, and a **three-year prepayment penalty penalizes his actual plan.**
>
> **"You can just refinance" is a prediction stated as a plan** — the single most damaging sentence in
> mortgage sales, because **the borrower stops evaluating the risk.** All three paths depend on conditions
> at maturity.
>
> **P&I rather than PITI**, so the quoted payment is not the payment.
>
> **No worst case**, and no **fully indexed rate** — the fact that tells a borrower whether his start rate
> is a discount that expires even if markets never move.
>
> **The interest-only period was never explained** — principal is not retired, and the payment recalculates
> over a shorter remaining term.
>
> **Only one product was shown.** Products are presented **on a common basis**, and **a comparison that
> omits a material difference misleads even when every number is accurate.**
>
> **And the acknowledgment proves nothing.** **A signature proves delivery, not comprehension.** What
> evidences an explanation is a record of what was said and when, **a written summary sent to the
> consumer**, his questions and the answers, and **a worst-case illustration in writing.**
>
> **The underlying failure:** presenting the product the borrower could be approved for **as though it
> were the product that fit him** — and the borrower could not tell the difference.

## Chapter summary

**Two federal frameworks overlap and neither list contains the other**, and **California is broader than
both** and keeps growing — so the practice is **consistent criteria and documented decisions**, not
memorization.

**ECOA reaches any aspect of a credit transaction**, including advertising, the application process,
pricing, and exceptions.

**Ask what may be asked, never require a spouse's signature where the applicant qualifies alone, never
discount public assistance income, and request the monitoring information** even though it may not be
used.

**Adverse action requires the actual reasons or the right to obtain them, plus the FCRA content where a
report was used.**

**Redlining today operates through reach, referrals, leads, targeting, algorithms, and product
availability** — and **exclusion compounds**, which does not license corrective steering.

**Pricing discretion and undocumented exceptions are what the data surfaces**, and **"nobody complained"
is not a defense.**

**Discouragement produces a denial with no record of one.** Take every application.

**Review outcomes, not intentions**, because patterns reveal what individual decisions never do.

**Fraud is a material misrepresentation relied on by a lender**, and **it presents as a pattern of small
explainable inconsistencies** — which is why "it doesn't add up but nothing is clearly false" is the
moment to escalate.

**Occupancy is material and affordability is beside the point.** A file containing its own contradiction
is escalated, not weighed.

**Never alter a document, author one in the borrower's voice, submit one known to be wrong, advise
seasoning or recharacterizing funds, or represent a verification that did not occur.**

**Supply an appraiser information, never a target** — including at your own manager's request.

**Verify anything consequential through a channel the attacker does not control**, and send the wire
warning at application.

**Apply scrutiny to the pattern, never to characteristics** — selective scrutiny is where fair lending
violations begin.

**Escalate promptly and in writing with facts, not conclusions.** Never investigate, confront, warn,
quietly withdraw, or participate — and **documenting an improper instruction does not transfer
responsibility for the act.**

**Keep the three categories of statement distinct**, present on a common basis with the worst case for
every product, explain every feature with its number, and remember that **acknowledgment is not
understanding.**

**Correct errors immediately and visibly** — and **falsifying the record is the one thing that is not
survivable.**
