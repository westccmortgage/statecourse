## 1. Inquiry and prequalification

### 1.1 Where the file begins

Every loan file begins in a conversation, and **most files that fail late were damaged early** — by an expectation set, a question not asked, or a slow item not started.

The stage has three jobs:

1. **Understand what the consumer is actually trying to do.**
2. **Give accurate general information without creating an obligation or an expectation the file cannot meet.**
3. **Identify the facts that will drive the timeline.**

### 1.2 The questions that determine everything downstream

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **What are you trying to accomplish?** | Purchase, refinance, cash-out, or a business objective — and it drives the purpose classification |
| **How long do you expect to hold the property and the loan?** | Decides points, buydowns, and hybrid terms |
| **What payment are you comfortable with**, as distinct from what you might qualify for? | The number that should drive the search |
| **What is the property, and how will you occupy it?** | Occupancy and purpose, and the requirements that follow |
| **What cash do you have**, beyond the down payment? | Closing costs, reserves, and the costs of occupancy |
| **Is there a deadline**, and what drives it? | A contract date, a lease, a maturing loan |
| **Has any work been done on the property recently?** | Mechanic's lien exposure, permits, condition |
| **What do you expect to change** — income, employment, a sale? | Re-verification exposure |

**The answers are recorded**, because they are what makes a later product presentation defensible.

### 1.3 What a licensee may say at inquiry

**May:** explain how the process works and roughly how long each stage takes; explain the general characteristics of program types; explain what preapproval is and is not; explain what documentation will be needed; recommend the consumer speak with more than one lender; and give general rate information with its assumptions stated.

**May not:** state that the consumer qualifies; state a qualifying threshold as a rule; predict rates; predict what an underwriter or an appraiser will do; or quote a payment without its assumptions.

### 1.4 The three levels, restated

| | Basis | Establishes | Does not |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prequalification** | Largely stated information | A rough range | **Verify anything** |
| **Preapproval** | Lender review of supporting information | A likely amount subject to conditions | **Commit to lend** |
| **Loan commitment** | Underwriting complete | Substantially more certainty | Immunity from changed circumstances |

> **A licensee who tells a consumer he is "approved" when a preapproval was issued has misdescribed the file's status — and the consumer will write an offer on that description.**

### 1.5 The prequalification letter

A letter that overstates what was done damages the consumer twice: it makes his offer look stronger than the file supports, and it collapses when the documents arrive.

**A letter should state what was actually reviewed, what remains, any assumptions, and an expiration** — because the seller's agent will read it exactly that way, and a letter that verifies nothing tells the seller something real.

### 1.6 The trigger nobody watches

**An application exists when the six items are in hand** — name, income, Social Security number, property address, an estimate of value, and the loan amount sought — **regardless of whether a form was signed.**

An inquiry conversation can become an application without anyone deciding it has. **Firms address this with a written policy**, and an originator's job is to follow it rather than to judge case by case.

### 1.7 Checkpoint

You should be able to run the intake questions, state what may and may not be said at inquiry, and explain when an inquiry becomes an application.

## 2. Application and disclosures

### 2.1 Taking the application

**Every item is answered**, because a blank is ambiguous and an ambiguous application is one nobody can later show was accurate.

**The borrower's information is the borrower's statement.** A licensee who supplies an answer the borrower did not give has misattributed a representation the lender will rely on.

**Estimates are identified as estimates**, so a file can distinguish a value estimate from a documented value.

### 2.2 The disclosure clock

The application starts defined periods running, and **the periods are legal deadlines rather than service commitments.**

**What is generally owed early**, in outline: the Loan Estimate within a defined short period after application; a written list of providers for services the consumer can shop for; the notice of the right to receive appraisal copies; the California **mortgage loan disclosure statement** on its own timing; and other disclosures the transaction and the program require.

### 2.3 The disclosure discipline

Four things, on every file:

**Deliver within the period.**

**Deliver by an authorized method**, and where electronic, only with the consent the framework requires.

**Evidence delivery** — what, to whom, when, by what method, with proof.

**Track it in a system**, not in memory. Nobody remembers which of forty files received which disclosure on which day.

> **A file that cannot show when a disclosure reached the consumer has a gap exactly where an examiner looks — and exactly where the consequence attaches.**

### 2.4 The California statement

The **mortgage loan disclosure statement** is a **California requirement with its own timing**, stating the loan's costs and the broker's compensation from every source.

> **Satisfying a federal disclosure obligation does not satisfy it.** Treating the Loan Estimate as covering it is among the most common findings in an examination of a mortgage brokerage.

### 2.5 The written warning that prevents failures

At application, in writing:

> **Do not open new credit, finance a purchase, change or leave your job, move large sums between accounts, pay off debts with funds needed to close, co-sign for anyone, or miss a payment on any obligation — without telling us first.**

**An approval rests on the file as underwritten**, and lenders commonly **re-verify credit and employment before funding.**

**This single communication prevents more failed closings than anything else an originator sends**, and it costs nothing at application while costing the closing when learned late.

### 2.6 The wire fraud warning

Also at application, in writing:

> **Wire instructions will never be changed by email. Before sending any funds, call escrow at a number you obtained independently — from your engagement documents or the company's published listing, never from the email carrying the instructions.**

**Sent early it costs nothing and prevents the largest single loss a consumer can suffer.** Sent the day funds move, it arrives after an attacker has already established the thread.

### 2.7 Setting the timeline honestly

The consumer should leave the application stage knowing: what happens next; what he must provide and by when; roughly how long each stage takes; what could move the closing date; and what to tell the originator immediately.

**A consumer who understands the sequence makes better decisions inside it**, and he is also the person who will notice when something has stalled.

### 2.8 Checkpoint

You should be able to state what is owed at application and by when, describe the delivery evidence discipline, and draft both written warnings.

## 3. Document collection and verification

### 3.1 Order by lead time, not convenience

The organizing principle of this section, and it is the same one that governs due diligence in a real estate transaction:

> **Start the slow items first. Most files that fail do not fail because something was impossible — they fail because something started late.**

| Slow item | Why |
|---|---|
| **Appraisal** | Scheduling, inspection, report, and any review |
| **Title** | The report itself, plus **requirements** that can take weeks — a reconveyance from a lender that no longer exists, a subordination, authority evidence |
| **Insurance in a mapped hazard area** | Availability and cost, now a common cause of failed transactions in California |
| **Condominium or planned development project documents** | Association response times |
| **Payoff demands** | Servicer turnaround |
| **Self-employment documentation** | Returns, business statements, and any accountant letter |
| **Permit research** | Jurisdiction response times |
| **Gift documentation and sourcing** | Requires the donor and often a paper trail |

### 3.2 What verification means

**Documentation the lender can rely on, matched to what the file represents.**

| Category | Verified against |
|---|---|
| **Income** | Paystubs, returns, business documentation, benefit statements, leases |
| **Employment** | Written and, near funding, **verbal** verification |
| **Assets** | Statements covering a period, not a single day |
| **Sourcing** | Documentation of where funds came from |
| **Liabilities** | The credit report **plus what it does not show** — private loans, obligations others pay, co-signed debts |
| **Identity** | Government identification and the framework's requirements |
| **Property** | Appraisal, title, insurance, and program-specific items |

### 3.3 The sourcing conversation

A deposit inconsistent with documented income requires explanation, and **the reason is substantive**: an undisclosed loan deposited as though it were savings is a hidden obligation that **makes the entire capacity analysis wrong.**

**What a licensee must never suggest:** depositing funds earlier so they "season," splitting a deposit into smaller amounts, or characterizing a loan as a gift. **Each is a suggestion to defeat the verification**, and the last is a misrepresentation to the lender.

### 3.4 Gaps, inconsistencies, and what they mean

Processing surfaces things, and **the response to each is the same: document it, address it, and do not paper over it.**

| Finding | Response |
|---|---|
| A document contradicting the application | Reconcile it with the borrower and correct the application if it was wrong |
| An employment gap | Explanation in the borrower's own words, dated |
| A debt not disclosed | Add it and re-run the analysis |
| **A lease dated before the application on an owner-occupied file** | **Escalate.** The file contains its own contradiction |
| Rental income on the returns for a property represented as owner-occupied | **Escalate**, same reason |
| A document that appears altered | **Escalate.** Do not confront, do not investigate alone |

**The last three are different in kind from the others.** They are not gaps to fill — **the file contains evidence contradicting a material representation**, and the response is escalation rather than a judgment call about whether it matters.

### 3.5 Handling documents

**Access control** — limit who can reach consumer information to those who need it.

**Protected transmission** — a loan file emailed unencrypted to a personal account is a security failure whatever the intention.

**Secure disposal** — destroy so data cannot be reconstructed. **A recycling bin is not disposal.**

**Version integrity** — the file retains what was received, and a correction does not overwrite what it corrected.

### 3.6 What a licensee never does with a document

- **Alter one.**
- **Complete one on a borrower's behalf** in the borrower's voice.
- **Draft a letter of explanation** and present it as the borrower's statement.
- **Submit one known to be inaccurate.**
- **Backdate anything.**
- **Sign for a borrower.**

**Each is a misrepresentation, and each occurs on files that would probably have closed anyway** — which is exactly what makes them so costly.

### 3.7 Communication during processing

**Weekly status in writing**, even when the status is "nothing changed." It converts a surprise into a managed problem, and it creates the record.

**Immediate communication of anything that moves the date.**

**Plain language about what the borrower must do**, with a deadline and a reason.

### 3.8 Checkpoint

You should be able to sequence document collection by lead time, describe verification and sourcing, distinguish a gap from a contradiction, and state what a licensee never does with a document.

## 4. Submission and condition management

### 4.1 Submitting a complete file

**A file submitted incomplete returns as conditions**, and every hour saved by submitting early is returned several times over in cycle time.

**Before submission:** the application is complete and accurate; the documentation matches what the file represents; discrepancies are reconciled; explanations are in the borrower's words and dated; and the automated finding, where used, was run on **verified** data.

> **A favorable finding produced from unsupported data is worth nothing and will reverse when the documents arrive.** "The system approved it" is not a defense for a file whose data was wrong.

### 4.2 Conditions are the work

| Type | When it must be satisfied |
|---|---|
| **Prior to document** | Before loan documents are drawn |
| **Prior to funding** | Before the lender releases funds |
| **At funding** | Concurrently |
| **Post-closing** | After funding, typically documentation cleanup |

> **An unsatisfied prior-to-funding condition means the loan does not fund** — whatever the schedule says and whoever has booked movers.

**An originator who describes conditions to a borrower as "just paperwork" has set an expectation the file cannot meet, and removed the borrower's motivation to respond quickly.**

### 4.3 Managing them

**Read the condition as written.** Half of condition responses fail because the response addressed something adjacent.

**Respond completely.** A partial response restarts a cycle and consumes days a rate lock does not have.

**Assign an owner and a date to each**, because a condition owned by everyone is owned by nobody.

**Escalate early.** **A condition that cannot be satisfied is a problem on the day it is identified**, not on the day it is due — and there is usually an alternative if there is time to find one.

**Communicate honestly with the borrower** about what is outstanding and what it means.

### 4.4 What must never happen to a condition

- **Altering a document** to satisfy one.
- **Backdating** anything.
- **Submitting something known to be inaccurate.**
- **Representing that a verbal verification occurred** when it did not.
- **Signing on a borrower's behalf.**
- **Telling a borrower a condition is satisfied** to relieve pressure.

### 4.5 The re-verification exposure

**An approval rests on the file as underwritten**, and credit and employment are commonly re-checked before funding.

That is why the events warned about at application matter here: new credit, a job change, large unexplained deposits, moving money, paying off debt with closing funds, co-signing, and missed payments can each **undo an approval after it was granted.**

**A verbal verification of employment is commonly required shortly before funding**, and a verification from a month ago is not that condition.

### 4.6 The lock and the calendar

Conditions consume the days a **rate lock** provides. The borrower needs in writing what is locked, **for how long**, what happens if the loan does not close in time, whether an extension is available and at what cost, and what voids it.

> **A lock that expires because the file moved slowly is a foreseeable event, not an accident.**

**The disclosure calendar runs alongside it**, because certain changes require a revised disclosure or restart a waiting period — and a change made to satisfy a condition can move the closing date.

### 4.7 Coordination

Nobody else holds the whole picture. The lender sees the loan file, escrow sees its instructions, the appraiser saw the property on one day, and the borrower sees only what he is told.

**The originator's coordination obligations:** build the calendar from the actual deadlines and share it; start slow items first; confirm status weekly in writing; escalate early; and **reconcile what the closing figures say against what was disclosed and agreed.**

### 4.8 Checkpoint

You should be able to state what a complete submission contains, manage conditions with owners and dates, name what must never be done to satisfy one, and explain the re-verification exposure.

## 5. Clear-to-close and settlement

### 5.1 Clear to close

**Clear to close means the lender's conditions to document and fund have been satisfied**, and documents may be prepared.

**It is not funding, and it is not closing.** A borrower told he is "clear to close" who understands that as "done" will be surprised by the sequence below.

### 5.2 The Closing Disclosure and its waiting period

The Closing Disclosure must generally be **received by the consumer a defined period before consummation**, and **certain changes restart the period** — in outline, an APR change beyond tolerance, a product change, or adding a prepayment penalty.

> **The waiting period is a timing rule with legal consequences, not a scheduling courtesy.**

**Do not promise a closing date that a late change makes impossible**, and tell a borrower proposing a change what it does to the date **before he decides.**

### 5.3 The four events, in order

Licensees conflate these constantly, and borrowers rely on the conflation.

| # | Event | What happens |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **Signing** | Documents are executed. **The transaction is not closed** |
| 2 | **Funding** | The lender releases proceeds, subject to its conditions |
| 3 | **Recording** | The deed and security instrument are recorded, giving constructive notice. **Generally when title transfers on a purchase** |
| 4 | **Disbursement** | Escrow disburses once recording is confirmed |

**Recording precedes disbursement deliberately**, preventing a gap in which money has moved but the interests are not of record. And **where multiple instruments record, the order establishes lien priority** — which is why closing is sequenced rather than simultaneous.

### 5.4 Rescindable transactions are different

On a transaction carrying a **right of rescission** — in outline, certain transactions secured by the consumer's **principal dwelling**, notably a refinance with a different lender, and **not** a purchase-money loan on the dwelling being acquired — **funds are not disbursed until the rescission period expires.**

**Each consumer entitled to rescind must receive the required notices**, and the period runs from **the later** of consummation, delivery of the notice, or delivery of the material disclosures — **so a defective or undelivered notice can extend it.**

**A refinance closing is therefore not the same as a purchase closing**, and a borrower expecting funds at signing should be told so in advance.

### 5.5 Escrow's role

**Escrow is neutral and acts only on mutual written instructions.** It does not advise either party, decide disputes, or release funds because one party asserts entitlement. **Where the parties disagree, escrow stops.**

**Escrow's instructions implement the agreement**, and **where they conflict the parties' agreement governs**, reconciled by the parties in writing.

**Nobody else reconciles the closing figures against what was disclosed and agreed** — which is the originator's contribution at this stage.

### 5.6 Wire fraud at settlement

**The highest-risk moment in the transaction is when funds move.**

1. **The written warning was sent at application** — wire instructions will never change by email, and verification is by voice at an independently obtained number.
2. **Treat any change to instructions as presumptively fraudulent** until verified through a channel the attacker does not control.
3. **Never verify using a number from the email carrying the instructions.**

**Report any incident or near-miss to the broker immediately**, whether or not funds moved.

### 5.7 Funding conditions and the final checks

**Prior-to-funding conditions are not formalities**, and an unsatisfied one means the loan does not fund.

**Common final-stage items:** the verbal verification of employment; a final credit re-check; evidence of insurance in force with the correct mortgagee clause; the payoff demand good through the disbursement date; and confirmation that funds to close have arrived and cleared.

**A payoff includes accrued interest to the payoff date and other amounts owed under the note** — routinely more than the last statement balance, and routinely a surprise to a borrower nobody warned.

### 5.8 Confirming closing to the borrower

**When funding and recording are confirmed, tell the borrower in writing, with the date and what it means.**

Borrowers frequently do not know whether their loan closed, when their first payment is due, or where to send it — and **on a purchase-money loan interest is paid in arrears**, so the first payment is typically not the following month.

### 5.9 Checkpoint

You should be able to sequence the four events, explain the waiting period and what restarts it, describe how a rescindable closing differs, and state the wire fraud protocol.

## 6. Post-closing quality control

### 6.1 Why post-closing review exists

**Because defects are systemic rather than random.** A process failure repeats on every file until someone notices, which is why one examination produces findings across dozens of transactions.

**Post-closing review is how a firm finds its own failures before an examiner does** — and how it finds them while the participants still respond to messages.

### 6.2 What quality control examines

| Area | What is tested |
|---|---|
| **Data integrity** | The file's data against the documents |
| **Disclosure timing** | What was delivered, to whom, when, by what method, with evidence |
| **Compensation** | **Disclosed against received** |
| **Tolerance** | Estimated against final charges, and whether any revision had a valid changed circumstance |
| **Underwriting** | The decision against the guidelines and the documentation |
| **Occupancy and purpose** | The representation against what the file shows |
| **Appraisal** | Support, and whether independence was maintained |
| **Trust funds** | Receipts, disbursements, beneficiary ledgers, reconciliation |
| **Advertising** | What ran, with identifiers, and whether it was approved and archived |
| **Licensing** | That everyone who acted was authorized when they acted |
| **Disposition** | That every application has one, coded as what actually happened |

### 6.3 The findings that recur

**None of these requires anyone to have intended anything:**

- Disclosure delivery not evidenced.
- The California mortgage loan disclosure statement missing or late, because the federal disclosure was treated as covering it.
- Compensation received not matching compensation disclosed.
- A tolerance exceeded with no documented changed circumstance.
- An application never dispositioned.
- Advertising without identifiers, and no archive.
- A trust reconciliation done bank-to-book only.
- Activity during an endorsement lapse.

### 6.4 Early payment default and repurchase

**A loan that defaults very early draws scrutiny**, because early default correlates with misrepresentation somewhere in the file.

**Where a loan was sold with representations and warranties, a breach can trigger a repurchase demand** — the purchaser requiring the seller to buy the loan back.

**Two consequences for an originator:** the file's accuracy has a financial consequence long after funding; and **an early default triggers a file review that will find anything else wrong with it.**

### 6.5 What to do with findings

**Obtain what legitimately exists** — a missing copy, an acknowledgment not retained, a document a participant still has.

**Record what does not**, plainly.

**Escalate to the broker.**

**Correct the instance and the process.** A response that fixes twelve files and leaves the process unchanged produces the same finding next time — and a regulator reading the second report sees a firm that did not learn.

**Document the corrective action** — what changed, when, by whom, and how it will be monitored.

### 6.6 The line

> **Creating a document now and dating it to match, altering an executed document, deleting a record that reflects badly, or removing surrounding documents so an inconsistency does not show is falsification.**
>
> **A documented gap is a compliance problem a firm can survive. A manufactured or destroyed record is a question about integrity, and it ends careers.**

**Corrections are made visibly**, with the original preserved and the change attributable — who changed what, when, and why. **A record that can be silently altered is not a record.**

### 6.7 Retention

**Records are retained for the required period**, and where California and federal periods differ, **the longest governs the record's fate in practice.**

**Retention extends where a complaint has been made or a proceeding is pending** — and **denied, withdrawn, and failed files are retained too**, disproportionately the ones that later produce disputes.

### 6.8 What the complete file shows

- The intake record and the borrower's stated objectives, with dates.
- The application as taken and every subsequent version.
- What was stated and what was **documented**, distinguished.
- Every disclosure, with delivery evidence.
- Every condition and its resolution.
- Compensation disclosed and received, reconciled.
- Every communication material to a term or a decision.
- Corrections issued, with originals retained.
- The disposition, its date, and any notice delivered with evidence.
- Anything a licensee declined to do, and the escalation.

### 6.9 The test

> **Reconstructability: could a person who was not there determine what happened, when, and on what basis?**

**A file that passes survives an examination even where it contains a problem. A file that does not fails even where it contains none**, because nobody can show what happened.

### 6.10 Checkpoint

You should be able to describe what post-closing review examines, name the recurring findings, explain early payment default scrutiny, and state what the complete file must show.

## Practice problems

### Problem 1

A consumer calls asking about rates. Over the call the originator obtains her name, income, Social
Security number, the address of the property she is under contract on, her estimate of its value, and
the amount she wants. He tells her she "definitely qualifies," quotes "about $2,400 a month," and says
he will "start the file when she sends the paperwork."

**Identify every problem.**

> **Solution.** **Four problems, and the first is a compliance deadline running unnoticed.**
>
> **An application exists.** All **six items** are in hand — name, income, Social Security number,
> property address, an estimate of value, and the loan amount sought — and **an application exists when
> they are in hand, regardless of whether a form was signed.** The Loan Estimate period and the other
> disclosure clocks are running while he waits for "the paperwork."
>
> **"Definitely qualifies" states an underwriting conclusion** he is not authorized to make, and she will
> act on it.
>
> **"About $2,400" is a quote without its assumptions** — loan amount, rate, term, and whether it is P&I
> or PITI. **A quote without its assumptions is a number the borrower will hold you to**, and in
> California the tax component is the one most often understated.
>
> **The intake questions were never asked.** What is she trying to accomplish, how long will she hold the
> loan, what payment is she comfortable with as distinct from what she might qualify for, how will she
> occupy it, what cash does she have beyond the down payment, what deadline drives it, has work been done
> on the property, and what does she expect to change. **The answers are recorded**, because they are what
> makes a later product presentation defensible.

### Problem 2

An originator issues a prequalification letter stating the borrower is "fully approved for $850,000."
Nothing was documented. The letter has no expiration and no conditions listed. The borrower writes an
offer with it.

**Assess.**

> **Solution.** **The letter misdescribes the file's status and damages the borrower twice.**
>
> **A prequalification rests on largely stated information and verifies nothing.** A **preapproval** is a
> lender review of supporting information establishing a likely amount **subject to conditions — not a
> commitment to lend.** A **commitment** follows completed underwriting. **None guarantees funding**, and
> this letter reflects the weakest of the three while claiming the strongest.
>
> **The two harms:** it makes his offer look stronger than the file supports, so a seller relies on it —
> and **it collapses when the documents arrive**, at which point he has a contract and no financing.
>
> **What a letter should state:** what was actually reviewed, what remains, any assumptions, and an
> expiration. A letter that verifies nothing tells a seller's agent something real, and that is a feature
> rather than a defect.
>
> **"Fully approved" is the specific failure.** A licensee who tells a consumer he is approved when a
> prequalification was performed has misdescribed the status, and the consumer writes an offer on that
> description.

### Problem 3

At application the originator sends nothing in writing beyond the required disclosures. Three weeks
later the borrower has financed a car, changed jobs for a better title at the same salary, and moved
$30,000 from savings into checking "to have it ready." Two days before funding he receives an email in
the escrow thread with updated wire instructions.

**Identify what should have been sent and address the wire email.**

> **Solution.** **Two written warnings belonged at application, and both would have prevented what
> happened.**
>
> **The start-of-file warning:** *do not open new credit, finance a purchase, change or leave your job,
> move large sums between accounts, pay off debts with funds needed to close, co-sign for anyone, or miss
> a payment — without telling us first.* **An approval rests on the file as underwritten**, and lenders
> commonly **re-verify credit and employment before funding.** All three of his acts attack that file:
> the car changes the debt-to-income analysis; the job change may end the approval even at the same
> salary, because a **verbal verification of employment** is commonly required shortly before funding; and
> the $30,000 transfer complicates asset verification and may require sourcing.
>
> **This single communication prevents more failed closings than anything else an originator sends**, and
> it costs nothing at application.
>
> **The wire warning:** *instructions will never be changed by email; call escrow at a number you obtained
> independently before sending funds.* **Treat the email as presumptively fraudulent until verified
> through a channel the attacker does not control** — and **never verify using a number from the email
> carrying the instructions.** Report the incident to the broker immediately, whether or not funds moved.

### Problem 4

Processing begins. The originator orders the credit report and paystubs immediately, and plans to order
the appraisal and title "once we know the file is solid," to request the condominium documents "when
underwriting asks," and to have the borrower get insurance quotes after the appraisal. The property is
in a mapped fire hazard area and the borrower is self-employed.

**Reorder the work and justify it.**

> **Solution.** **Everything slow was scheduled last.** The principle is to **order by lead time, not by
> convenience — most files fail because something started late, not because something was impossible.**
>
> **Week one should carry:** the **appraisal**, which requires scheduling, inspection, report, and any
> review; **title**, because the report itself is only the beginning and its **requirements** can take
> weeks — a reconveyance from a lender that no longer exists, a subordination, authority evidence;
> **insurance quotes**, which in a mapped hazard area are now a common cause of failed transactions in
> California and are the item borrowers most reliably treat as a formality; the **condominium project
> documents**, which move at the association's speed rather than the file's; any **payoff demands**; and
> the **self-employment documentation**, which involves returns, business statements, and often an
> accountant.
>
> **Credit and paystubs are the fast items**, and starting with them feels productive while consuming the
> days the slow items needed.
>
> **The self-employment point deserves emphasis:** the figure the borrower thinks of as his income and the
> figure underwriting uses can differ substantially after adjustments, so **nothing is quoted as final
> before the documents are read.**

### Problem 5

During processing four things surface: a $22,000 deposit the borrower says his brother lent him; a
four-month employment gap; a $2,300 debt not on the credit report; and a lease on the subject property
dated a month before the application, on a file taken as owner-occupied.

**Handle each, and identify which is different in kind.**

> **Solution.** **Three are gaps to address. One is a contradiction to escalate.**
>
> **The deposit requires sourcing**, and the source is the problem: it is **a loan, not a gift**, and
> therefore a liability. **An undisclosed loan deposited as though it were savings is a hidden obligation
> that makes the entire capacity analysis wrong.** Disclose it, document its terms, and let underwriting
> treat it as what it is. **Never suggest depositing earlier so it "seasons," splitting it, or calling it
> a gift** — each is a suggestion to defeat the verification, and the last is a misrepresentation.
>
> **The employment gap** gets an explanation **in the borrower's own words, dated.** The originator does
> not draft it — **drafting it authors a representation the borrower will sign.**
>
> **The undisclosed debt** is added, and the analysis re-run. The credit report does not show everything:
> private loans, obligations others pay, and co-signed debts are all real.
>
> **The lease is different in kind.** It is not a gap to fill — **the file contains evidence contradicting
> a material representation to the lender.** Occupancy affects pricing, program eligibility, and which
> protections apply, which makes it **a material fact**, and misstating it is fraud regardless of
> affordability. **The response is escalation to the responsible broker, not a judgment call about whether
> it matters.**

### Problem 6

Four days before funding, three conditions remain: a verbal verification of employment, sourcing of a
deposit, and a corrected disclosure requiring the borrower's signature. The borrower is unreachable, the
lock expires in five days, and the branch manager suggests marking the verbal verification complete
"since we verified last month" and having the processor sign the correction so the file can move.

**Respond.**

> **Solution.** **Refuse both suggestions and treat the conditions as what they are.**
>
> **An unsatisfied prior-to-funding condition means the loan does not fund** — whatever the schedule says.
> Describing conditions to a borrower as "just paperwork" is what produces this situation, because it
> removes his motivation to respond quickly.
>
> **A verbal verification is required shortly before funding precisely because credit and employment are
> re-checked**, and an approval rests on the file **as underwritten.** A verification from last month is
> not the condition, and **representing that a verbal verification occurred when it did not is a
> misrepresentation.**
>
> **Signing on the borrower's behalf is a misrepresentation** and is never available.
>
> **The deposit still requires sourcing**, for the reason above.
>
> **What should happen:** escalate today rather than on the due date — **a condition that cannot be
> satisfied is a problem on the day it is identified**, and there is usually an alternative if there is
> time to find one. Reach the borrower through every channel, tell him plainly that funding depends on
> these three items, and address the lock honestly: **a lock that expires because the file moved slowly is
> a foreseeable event, not an accident.**

### Problem 7

A refinance borrower is told he is "clear to close" and assumes the transaction is done. He asks when he
gets his money, why there is a delay after signing, why his payoff is $8,200 more than his last
statement, and when his first payment is due. His originator also proposes changing to a different
product two days before the scheduled signing.

**Answer each and assess the product change.**

> **Solution.** **"Clear to close" means the lender's conditions to document and fund are satisfied. It is
> not funding and not closing** — and a borrower who hears it as "done" will be surprised by everything
> that follows.
>
> **The four events, in order:** **signing** — documents executed, **the transaction is not closed**;
> **funding** — the lender releases proceeds subject to its conditions; **recording** — establishing the
> interests of record, with **recording order setting lien priority**; and **disbursement** — after
> recording is confirmed, deliberately, so money does not move before the interests are of record.
>
> **On a rescindable transaction the delay is longer and required.** A refinance with a different lender
> secured by his **principal dwelling** generally carries a **right of rescission**, and **funds are not
> disbursed until the period expires.** Each entitled consumer must receive the notice, and the period
> runs from **the later** of consummation, delivery of the notice, or delivery of the material disclosures
> — so a defective notice extends it. **A refinance closing is not a purchase closing**, and he should
> have been told that in advance.
>
> **The payoff includes accrued interest to the payoff date and other amounts owed under the note** —
> routinely more than the last statement balance.
>
> **Interest is paid in arrears**, so his first payment is generally not the following month.
>
> **The product change is the serious item.** A **product change generally requires a new Closing
> Disclosure and a new waiting period.** He must be told **what it does to the closing date before he
> decides** — never after.

### Problem 8

A post-closing review of one quarter finds: eleven files with no delivery evidence for the California
mortgage loan disclosure statement; four files where compensation received exceeded compensation
disclosed; two tolerance overages with revised estimates citing "changed circumstances" that were
actually vendor prices the firm had used incorrectly for a year; three applications never dispositioned;
and one loan that defaulted on its second payment. A manager proposes printing and backdating the
missing acknowledgments before the annual audit.

**Assess the findings and the proposal.**

> **Solution.** **Every finding is a process failure, and none required anyone to intend anything** — which
> is the point. **A process failure repeats on every file until someone notices**, which is why one review
> produces findings across a quarter.
>
> **The California statement is a separate obligation with its own timing**, and firms most often miss it
> by treating the federal disclosure as covering it. It does not.
>
> **Compensation received exceeding compensation disclosed** is the finding with the widest consequences —
> reaching the California disclosure, the federal framework, and potentially the points-and-fees
> calculation. **The control is reconciling disclosed against received on every file**, and it is the one
> firms skip because one function prepares the disclosure and another receives the payment.
>
> **The revisions are invalid.** Using vendor pricing the firm had wrong for a year is **discovering the
> estimate was wrong, not a changed circumstance** — a changed circumstance is **a change, not a discovery
> of an error** — so the baseline never reset and **cures are owed.**
>
> **"Stopped responding" is not a disposition**, and an application never dispositioned is exactly the file
> an examiner samples.
>
> **The second-payment default draws scrutiny** because early default correlates with misrepresentation,
> and **it triggers a file review that will find everything else wrong with that file** — and where the
> loan was sold with representations and warranties, a breach can support a **repurchase demand.**
>
> **The manager's proposal is falsification**, and it is the one item here that is not survivable.
> **Obtain what legitimately exists, record what does not, escalate to the broker, and correct the instance
> and the process** — because fixing eleven files and leaving the process unchanged produces the same
> finding next time. **A documented gap is survivable; a manufactured record ends careers.**

## Chapter summary

**Most files that fail late were damaged early.** Ask the intake questions, record the answers, and know
that **an application exists when the six items are in hand** regardless of any form.

**Never say a consumer qualifies, never quote a payment without its assumptions, and never issue a letter
claiming more than was done.**

**Deliver disclosures within their periods, by authorized methods, with evidence, tracked in a system** —
and remember the California statement is separate.

**Send both written warnings at application.** The start-of-file warning prevents more failed closings than
anything else, and the wire fraud warning prevents the largest single loss a consumer can suffer.

**Order document collection by lead time.** Appraisal, title requirements, hazard-area insurance, project
documents, payoffs, self-employment documentation, permits, and gift sourcing are the slow items.

**Verification means documentation the lender can rely on**, and sourcing exists because an undisclosed
loan makes the whole analysis wrong. **Never advise seasoning, splitting, or recharacterizing funds.**

**Fill gaps; escalate contradictions.** A lease predating the application is not a gap.

**Submit complete files, run findings on verified data, and assign every condition an owner and a date.**
Escalate on the day a condition cannot be satisfied, not on the day it is due.

**Never alter, backdate, sign for a borrower, or represent a condition satisfied when it is not.**

**Clear to close is not closing.** Signing, funding, recording, disbursement — and on a rescindable
transaction, funds wait for the period to expire.

**Certain changes restart the waiting period**, so a borrower proposing one is told what it does to the
date before he decides.

**Escrow acts only on mutual written instructions**, and nobody but the originator reconciles the closing
figures against what was disclosed and agreed.

**Post-closing review finds the firm's own failures before an examiner does**, and the recurring findings
are process failures rather than intentions. **Correct the instance and the process.**

**Falsification is the one thing that is not survivable**, and the governing test remains
**reconstructability**.
