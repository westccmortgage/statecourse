## 1. Private investor loans

### 1.1 What private money is

**A loan funded by a private investor or group of investors rather than by an institutional lender**, typically arranged by a broker who brings the borrower and the money together.

**The broker's position is different from institutional origination in one decisive way:**

> **The broker has duties to the investor as well as to the borrower** — and the investor is relying on the broker's presentation of the loan, the collateral, and the borrower in a way an institutional underwriter never does.

### 1.2 Legitimate uses

Private money is not a lesser product. It exists because institutional channels cannot serve some transactions:

| Situation | Why private money fits |
|---|---|
| **Bridge financing** | A short gap between transactions that conventional timelines cannot serve |
| **Speed** | A transaction that cannot wait for institutional underwriting |
| **Property condition** | Collateral no conforming channel will accept |
| **Unusual collateral or structure** | Mixed use, unpermitted improvements, unusual parcels |
| **Business-purpose transactions** | Where the consumer framework does not apply |
| **Credit or documentation profile** | A legitimate borrower a conforming channel cannot document |

**The characteristics that follow:** shorter terms, higher rates, more fees, greater emphasis on collateral than on income, and frequently a balloon.

### 1.3 The broker's duties on the investor side

The investor is a client too, and the duties are real.

**Present the loan accurately.** The borrower, the collateral, the position, the term, and the risks — **without characterizing a weak file as strong.**

**Disclose what the broker knows.** Including anything about the borrower, the property, or the transaction that a reasonable investor would want.

**Disclose the broker's compensation and interest**, including whether the broker or an affiliate has any interest in the transaction, the property, or the borrower.

**Handle the funds as trust funds** — Section 5.

**Not represent an outcome.** No projection of repayment, no assurance the collateral will hold value, and no statement that a loan is "safe."

### 1.4 What an investor must be told about the collateral

**The value and its basis** — an appraisal, and its date. Not the broker's estimate presented as a valuation.

**The lien position**, and what sits ahead of it.

**The loan-to-value or combined loan-to-value**, computed on the documented value.

**The condition of the property**, including anything that would affect a sale.

**Title exceptions and requirements**, including anything unresolved.

**Occupancy**, and any tenancy that would survive a foreclosure.

### 1.5 Multi-lender arrangements

Where a broker assembles **more than one investor into a single loan**, additional California requirements attach — including requirements about how the transaction is structured, documented, and serviced, and about the number of lenders and their interests.

> **A broker assembling investors into a single loan is operating inside a regulated structure**, not merely finding money for a borrower — and **whether a particular arrangement satisfies the requirements is a compliance and legal determination.**

### 1.6 Threshold activity

Where a broker's private money activity exceeds defined volume or structural thresholds, **further California requirements attach**, including reporting.

**An originator's contribution is accurate transaction records**, because the reports are compiled from them — and because the thresholds are computed from activity nobody tracks by memory.

### 1.7 Servicing

Where a broker services a private money loan — collecting payments and remitting to investors — **the funds collected are trust funds**, and the servicing obligations of Lesson 13 apply.

**Servicing a loan the broker arranged, for investors the broker recruited, on collateral the broker presented, concentrates every duty in this lesson in one firm.** That is not prohibited, and it is why the documentation and the trust accounting must be exact.

### 1.8 Checkpoint

You should be able to state why private money exists, name the broker's duties to an investor, list what an investor must be told about collateral, and explain what a multi-lender arrangement triggers.

## 2. Business-purpose lending

### 2.1 Why the classification carries so much

**Consumer residential mortgage requirements — including the MLO endorsement requirement and much of the federal disclosure regime — attach to consumer-purpose loans secured by a dwelling.** Loans made **primarily for a business purpose** are treated differently.

**That makes the classification consequential, and it makes it a target for misuse** — because a business-purpose classification removes obligations that a consumer classification imposes.

### 2.2 The determination

A loan is business-purpose when it is made **primarily for a business, commercial, agricultural, or investment purpose** rather than for personal, family, or household purposes.

**The analysis considers**, among other things: **what the proceeds are actually used for**; the borrower's relationship to the property and to the activity; whether the borrower is engaged in the business the loan supports; the property's use and occupancy; and the transaction's overall character.

> **The determination rests on the actual purpose and the surrounding facts.**

### 2.3 What a certification does and does not establish

> **A borrower's signature on a business-purpose certification records his statement. It does not establish the fact.**

**A file assembled around a certification that the surrounding facts contradict is a file with a problem in it** — visible to any examiner who reads the occupancy line, the stated use of proceeds, or a lease dated before the application.

**The certification is evidence, and it is one piece of evidence among several.** A file whose only support for the classification is the certification has not documented a determination; it has documented that someone signed a form.

### 2.4 The errors run in both directions

**Treating a consumer loan as business-purpose.** The more common error and the more serious one, because it **strips the borrower of protections he was entitled to** — the ability-to-repay determination, the disclosure regime, and the rescission right among them.

**Treating every investment property loan as exempt from everything.** This sweeps in requirements that still apply.

> **A business-purpose classification does not suspend fair lending obligations, licensing requirements that attach independently, fraud prohibitions, usury analysis, or the firm's own standards.**

### 2.5 Where the line is genuinely hard

Some transactions are close, and pretending otherwise produces bad files:

- A borrower refinancing a rental he once occupied.
- A cash-out on a primary residence to fund a business.
- A loan on a property with both a residence and a commercial use.
- A fourplex the borrower will occupy — **which points hard toward consumer purpose.**
- A borrower with one rental property versus one with twelve.
- A construction loan on a property the borrower intends to occupy when finished.

> **The response to a genuinely close case is not to pick the classification that is convenient. It is to route it to compliance and, where necessary, counsel — before the file is built around an answer.**

**A file constructed on the wrong classification is expensive to unwind**, because the disclosures, the licensing analysis, and sometimes the product all rest on it.

### 2.6 The instruction an originator will receive

> A manager says: process it as business-purpose since it is an income property, and have the borrower sign the certification.

**Decline, do not transmit the file, and escalate above the manager.** "It's an income property" is not the test, the certification does not decide it, and **following an improper instruction while noting that it was directed does not transfer responsibility for the act — it documents that you knew.**

### 2.7 What still applies

Even on a properly classified business-purpose loan:

| Still applies |
|---|
| **Fair lending obligations** |
| **Licensing requirements** that attach on their own terms |
| **Fraud prohibitions** and misrepresentation liability |
| **California broker law** — trust funds, records, advertising, supervision |
| **Usury analysis**, unless an exemption applies |
| **Duties to investors** where private money is involved |
| **Honest presentation** of terms and risks |

### 2.8 Checkpoint

You should be able to state the determination and what it rests on, state what a certification establishes, name both directions of error, and state the response to a close case and to an improper instruction.

## 3. Seller carryback financing

### 3.1 What it is

**The seller finances all or part of the purchase price**, taking a note secured by a deed of trust on the property sold.

**Two common structures:** a **carryback second** behind institutional first financing, and a **carryback first** where the seller finances the whole price.

### 3.2 Why it happens

**For the buyer:** access where institutional financing is unavailable or too slow, and sometimes better terms.

**For the seller:** a sale that would not otherwise close, a return on the carried amount, and potentially a different tax treatment — **which is a question for a tax professional, not for a licensee.**

### 3.3 What must be documented

| Element | Why |
|---|---|
| **The note** | Amount, rate, payment, term, maturity, late charge, default terms |
| **The security instrument** | Recorded, with the correct legal description |
| **Priority** | Which position, and what sits ahead |
| **Prepayment** | Whether permitted and on what terms |
| **Acceleration and alienation** | What triggers each |
| **Subordination** | Whether the seller has agreed to subordinate to future financing, and on what conditions |
| **Servicing** | Who collects, where payments go, and how they are recorded |
| **Insurance and tax obligations** | And how the seller learns if they lapse |
| **Default remedies** | And the procedure |

### 3.4 The seller's risks

**Priority risk.** A carryback second is **extinguished as a claim against the property by a senior foreclosure**, leaving the seller with an unsecured claim subject to California's deficiency restrictions.

**The subordination clause.** An open-ended agreement to subordinate to future financing commits the seller to move behind loans that do not yet exist, on terms nobody has seen.

> **A seller who signs an instrument with an open-ended subordination clause has agreed to something he may not have understood**, and it is the single most consequential provision in carryback documentation.

**Payment and default risk.** The buyer may not pay, and the seller's remedy is a foreclosure he must conduct.

**Insurance and tax lapse**, which impairs the collateral without the seller knowing unless he arranged to be told.

**Waste and condition**, since the seller's security is a property he no longer controls.

### 3.5 The buyer's risks

**A balloon he must refinance**, and refinancing depends on conditions at maturity — his credit, the property's value, and whether credit is available.

**Terms he cannot evaluate**, where the documentation was assembled informally.

**A due-on-sale problem** where an underlying loan's **alienation clause** is triggered by the transfer.

**Servicing failures**, where payments are made and not recorded.

### 3.6 The disclosure obligations

Where a licensee arranges a carryback in defined circumstances, **California requires a disclosure statement to the parties** covering the terms, the security, the parties' creditworthiness information, and other required particulars.

**Two points:**

**It exists to give both parties information they cannot otherwise obtain** — a seller carrying paper is extending credit and generally has none of the information a lender would have.

**A licensee arranging a carryback without addressing it has left both parties in a transaction neither can evaluate.**

### 3.7 What a licensee does and does not do

**Does:** identify that a carryback is being contemplated; explain the mechanics and the risks to each party in plain terms; ensure the disclosure obligations are addressed; **recommend that each party obtain counsel and, for the tax questions, a tax professional**; ensure the note and security instrument are prepared properly and recorded; and document what was explained and recommended.

**Does not:** draft the note and deed of trust from a form and call it done; advise on the tax treatment; predict whether the buyer will pay; characterize the arrangement as safe; or tell a seller that a subordination clause is standard.

> **Seller-carry documentation belongs with counsel.** The provisions that matter — subordination, acceleration, alienation, prepayment, and remedies — are exactly the ones a form will not resolve for a particular transaction.

### 3.8 Checkpoint

You should be able to name what must be documented, state the seller's and the buyer's risks, identify the most consequential provision, and state what a licensee does and does not do.

## 4. Hard-money risk and suitability

### 4.1 What hard money is

**Collateral-focused lending, typically short-term and higher-rate**, where the underwriting emphasis is on the property rather than on the borrower's documented income.

**It overlaps heavily with private money** and is often the same transaction described from the pricing side rather than the funding side.

### 4.2 The characteristics

| Characteristic | Consequence for the borrower |
|---|---|
| **Higher rate** | Materially higher carrying cost |
| **Points and fees** | Substantial, and frequently paid at closing from proceeds |
| **Short term** | Often one to three years |
| **Balloon at maturity** | Refinance, sell, or pay — all depending on conditions then |
| **Lower loan-to-value** | Requires more equity |
| **Interest-only or partial amortization** | Little or no principal reduction |
| **Prepayment provisions** | May penalize the exit the borrower is planning |
| **Fast funding** | The reason many borrowers are there |

### 4.3 The suitability question

A licensee is not an investment adviser and does not make a suitability determination in the sense a regulated adviser would. **But the licensee must be able to show why this borrower is in this product**, and the file must support it.

> **The question the file must answer: why is this borrower in a hard-money loan rather than a conventional one, and what is his exit?**

**Legitimate answers:** speed a conventional channel cannot provide; collateral no conforming channel will accept; a business-purpose transaction outside the consumer framework; a documented credit or documentation profile; a defined bridge with a specific end.

**Answers that are not answers:** it was the product available; the borrower was in a hurry; conventional would have taken too long and nobody asked why the deadline existed; or the borrower said he would refinance.

### 4.4 The exit

**Every short-term collateral loan is an exit plan wearing a loan's clothes**, and the exit is where these transactions fail.

**What must be examined and recorded:**

- **What is the exit** — sale, refinance, or a defined event?
- **What does it depend on** — a permit, a market, a credit improvement, a buyer?
- **What happens if it does not occur on schedule?**
- **Is there an extension provision, and at what cost?**
- **Does a prepayment provision penalize the intended exit?**

> **"I'll just refinance" assumes a market.** All three balloon paths — refinance, sell, or pay — **depend on conditions at maturity**, and naming that assumption is the disclosure that matters.

### 4.5 The consumer-purpose problem

**Where a hard-money loan is consumer-purpose and secured by a dwelling, the consumer framework applies in full** — the ability-to-repay determination, the disclosure regime, and the rest.

> **Hard money is not a category that escapes the consumer framework. Business purpose is.** Conflating the two is the mechanism by which consumer borrowers end up in loans for which no ability-to-repay determination was ever made.

**And the ability-to-repay determination is precisely what a collateral-focused underwrite does not perform** — which is why the classification cannot be a convenience.

### 4.6 Presenting hard money honestly

**State the total cost**, not only the rate: points, fees, and interest over the expected term.

**State the maturity and the amount due.**

**State what happens if the exit does not occur**, in plain terms.

**Compare it to the alternative** the borrower could obtain, if any, on a common basis — and if there is no alternative, say so and say why.

**Do not present speed as free.** It is purchased, and the price is the whole difference.

### 4.7 The patterns that should stop a file

- A consumer-purpose borrower being routed to a business-purpose classification.
- A borrower whose stated exit is a refinance he could not obtain today.
- An owner-occupant on a short-term balloon with no documented exit.
- A borrower who does not understand that the balance is due at maturity.
- A loan sized to the collateral where the borrower cannot service the payment.
- **Equity-stripping indicators** — a distressed owner, substantial equity, a loan he cannot repay, and a lender positioned to take the property.

**The last is the pattern that produces the most serious enforcement in this area**, and a licensee who sees it escalates rather than participating.

### 4.8 Checkpoint

You should be able to state what the file must show about product fit, examine an exit, explain why hard money does not escape the consumer framework, and name the patterns that should stop a file.

## 5. Broker-arranged loan documentation

### 5.1 Two sides to document

A broker-arranged private money loan has **a borrower side and an investor side**, and both must be documented.

**Institutional origination has one client. This has two, with opposed interests on price and risk** — which is why the documentation obligations are heavier rather than lighter.

### 5.2 The borrower-side file

Everything an institutional file requires, plus the disclosures the arrangement triggers:

- The application and its versions, with what was stated and what was **documented**, distinguished.
- **The purpose determination and the facts supporting it** — not merely the certification.
- The appraisal or valuation, with delivery evidenced.
- Title, with exceptions and requirements resolved.
- Insurance evidence with the correct mortgagee clause.
- Every disclosure owed, **with delivery evidence** — including the California **mortgage loan disclosure statement**, and the consumer disclosures where the loan is consumer-purpose.
- **The product-fit record** — why this product, what the exit is, and what was explained.
- Compensation **disclosed and received, reconciled.**
- The note and security instrument as executed and recorded.

### 5.3 The investor-side file

| Item | Why |
|---|---|
| **The loan presentation as given** | What the investor actually relied on |
| **The appraisal or valuation, with its date and basis** | Not the broker's estimate |
| **The title report, exceptions, and requirements** | Including anything unresolved |
| **The lien position and what sits ahead** | Computed, not asserted |
| **Loan-to-value on the documented value** | The exposure |
| **Borrower information as presented** | And what was verified |
| **The broker's compensation and any interest** | Including affiliates |
| **Servicing arrangements** | Who collects, and how funds flow |
| **The investor's instructions** | Signed |
| **Trust fund records** | Receipt, holding, and disbursement of the investor's funds |

### 5.4 Investor funds are trust funds

**From receipt until properly disbursed.**

**Separated** from the broker's own funds. **Recorded by beneficiary.** **Disbursed per the arrangement's terms and the investor's instructions.** And **returned where the transaction does not close** rather than applied elsewhere.

> **Using one investor's funds to complete another transaction is conversion** — however temporary, however well intentioned, and regardless of whether the money is replaced. **Intent to replace is a description of the plan, not a defense.**

**Reconciliation compares three balances:** the bank balance, the book balance, and **the total of the individual beneficiary ledgers.** Bank-to-book alone cannot detect money held for the wrong beneficiary, because **the total is right the whole time.**

### 5.5 Conflicts

Private money concentrates conflicts, and each requires disclosure before reliance:

- **The broker or an affiliate having an interest** in the property, the borrower, or the loan.
- **The broker acting as principal** — lending its own funds, or buying the note.
- **The broker servicing** the loan it arranged, for investors it recruited.
- **Compensation from both sides.**
- **A relationship with the borrower or the investor** a party would want to know about.

> **A conflict is managed by disclosure that arrives before the party relies on the advice it affects** — and a broker acting as a principal while an investor believes it is being advised has a serious problem.

### 5.6 What must never appear in an investor presentation

- **A projected return stated as a fact.**
- **An assurance of repayment or of the collateral's value holding.**
- **A characterization of the loan as "safe" or "secured" in a way implying no risk.**
- **A valuation the broker produced, presented as an appraisal.**
- **A loan-to-value computed on an unsupported value.**
- **An omission of what sits ahead of the position.**

### 5.7 The reconstructability test

> **Could a person who was not there determine what the investor was told, on what basis, what the borrower was told, what was disclosed to each, and where every dollar went?**

**In private money that test is the whole compliance posture**, because there is no institutional underwriter whose file substitutes for the broker's.

### 5.8 Checkpoint

You should be able to list what each side's file must contain, state the trust fund obligations for investor funds, name the conflicts requiring disclosure, and state what must never appear in an investor presentation.

## 6. Usury exemptions and legal referral

### 6.1 What usury is

**Usury is charging interest above the maximum rate the law permits.** California's usury framework establishes limits, with a substantial set of exemptions.

**The consequences of a usurious loan are significant and are legal questions**, which is why this section is about recognition rather than analysis.

### 6.2 Why it matters in private money specifically

**Institutional lending sits comfortably inside exemptions.** Private money frequently involves rates and fee structures near or above the limits, arranged by parties who may or may not fall within an exemption.

> **Usury is the risk that private money participants most often do not know they are running.**

### 6.3 The exemption structure, in outline

California's framework provides exemptions of several kinds, including for **certain classes of lenders**, for **loans arranged by a licensed real estate broker**, and for other defined categories.

**The broker-arranged exemption is the one that matters most in this course**, and it carries requirements about what "arranged by" means and what the broker must actually do.

> **Whether an exemption applies to a particular transaction is a legal determination.** It depends on the lender's status, the broker's actual role, the loan's characteristics, and the facts of the arrangement — and **it is not established by writing "arranged by a licensed real estate broker" on a document.**

### 6.4 What "arranged by" requires

The exemption depends on the broker having **actually performed the arranging role**, not on a nominal designation.

**A broker whose name appears on a transaction it did not arrange has not created an exemption**, and the arrangement raises a further question about why the designation was made.

**This is the pattern to recognize:** a lender who cannot lend at the rate proposed, and a broker's name added to the file. **Escalate it.**

### 6.5 What counts toward the rate

The analysis is not limited to the stated interest rate. **Charges and structures can be treated as interest** for the purpose, and the determination is legal.

**Two consequences:**

**A loan whose stated rate sits below a limit can still be examined**, where fees and structures are considered.

**A licensee structuring fees to keep a stated rate low, while the economics sit elsewhere, has made a decision belonging to counsel** — and the pattern is one that examiners and courts both recognize.

### 6.6 The referral discipline

**Every usury question is a referral.**

**What a licensee may do:** identify that a usury question is present; state that exemptions exist and that whether one applies depends on facts and status; ensure the question is routed **before the loan is made**; and document the referral.

**What a licensee may not do:** conclude that an exemption applies; state that the broker's involvement makes a loan exempt; compute whether a structure sits inside a limit; or reassure an investor or a borrower that a rate is permissible.

> **The cost of routing a usury question is a conversation. The cost of getting it wrong is the loan.**

### 6.7 The other referrals in this lesson

Private money and nontraditional transactions concentrate questions outside the license:

| Question | Referral |
|---|---|
| Usury and exemption | **Counsel** |
| Whether a multi-lender structure satisfies the requirements | **Compliance and counsel** |
| Whether a transaction is consumer or business purpose, where close | **Compliance and counsel, before the file is built** |
| Seller carryback documentation | **Counsel for each party** |
| Tax treatment of a carryback, an exchange, or interest | **A tax professional** |
| Whether a subordination should be given | **The subordinating party's counsel** |
| Whether a foreclosure or remedy is available | **Counsel** |
| Whether an investor arrangement implicates securities requirements | **Counsel** |

**That last row deserves naming.** Arrangements pooling investor money can raise questions under securities law depending on their structure. **A licensee who recognizes the question routes it; a licensee who assembles the structure without asking has answered it.**

### 6.8 Where this lesson concentrates the risk

Private money, hard money, seller carrybacks, and business-purpose classification share four features that make them the highest-exposure area in this course:

1. **The consumer protections that constrain institutional lending are frequently absent or claimed to be.**
2. **The broker owes duties to two sides with opposed interests.**
3. **The documentation is assembled rather than standardized.**
4. **The money frequently passes through the broker.**

> **The controls are correspondingly simple and non-negotiable:** classify purpose on the facts and route close cases before building the file; document product fit and the exit; present the loan to investors accurately and without projections; handle investor funds as trust funds with three-way reconciliation; disclose every conflict before reliance; route usury, securities, and structure questions to counsel; and keep a file that lets someone who was not there reconstruct what each side was told.

### 6.9 Checkpoint

You should be able to state what usury is and why private money runs the risk, explain what "arranged by" requires, state what a licensee may and may not say about exemptions, and name the referrals this lesson concentrates.

## Practice problems

### Problem 1

A broker assembles six investors to fund a single $1.4 million loan on a mixed-use property. He tells
them the loan is "well secured at 55% loan-to-value," using his own estimate of value; does not mention
a $310,000 first he is arranging simultaneously; deposits the investor funds into an account that also
holds the firm's operating balance; and projects "a solid 11% return."

**Identify every failure.**

> **Solution.** **Six failures, and each maps to a distinct duty.**
>
> **The value is the broker's estimate presented as a valuation.** An investor must be told the value
> **and its basis** — an appraisal, with its date — not the broker's number.
>
> **The loan-to-value is therefore unsupported**, and it must be computed on the **documented** value.
>
> **Omitting the senior lien is the most serious presentation failure.** An investor must be told the
> **lien position and what sits ahead of it.** At 55% of an unsupported value with an undisclosed
> $310,000 first, the investors do not know what they own.
>
> **"A solid 11% return" is a projected return stated as fact**, and the duty is explicit: **no
> representation of outcome** — no projection of repayment, no assurance the collateral holds value, and
> no statement that a loan is safe.
>
> **The deposit is commingling**, complete on deposit. **Investor funds are trust funds from receipt
> until properly disbursed** — separated, recorded by beneficiary, and returned if the transaction does
> not close.
>
> **And six investors in one loan is a multi-lender arrangement**, triggering additional California
> requirements about structure, documentation, and servicing. **A broker assembling investors into a
> single loan is operating inside a regulated structure**, and whether this one satisfies it is a
> compliance and legal determination.

### Problem 2

A manager tells an originator to process a loan as business-purpose on a duplex the borrower will
occupy, "since it produces income," and to have him sign the certification. The file also contains a
lease on the subject property dated three weeks before the application.

**Assess the instruction and the lease.**

> **Solution.** **The instruction inverts the analysis, the certification does not fix it, and the lease
> is different in kind.**
>
> **"It produces income" is not the test.** A loan is business-purpose when made **primarily for a
> business, commercial, agricultural, or investment purpose**, and the analysis considers what the
> proceeds actually fund, the borrower's relationship to the property and the activity, the property's
> use and occupancy, and the transaction's overall character. **A borrower occupying a unit points hard
> toward consumer purpose.**
>
> **A signature records the borrower's statement; it does not establish the fact.** A file supported only
> by the certification **has documented a signature rather than a determination.**
>
> **The consequence of getting it wrong here is the serious direction of the error:** treating a consumer
> loan as business-purpose **strips the borrower of protections he was entitled to** — the
> ability-to-repay determination, the disclosure regime, and the rescission right among them.
>
> **The lease is the file contradicting itself.** It is not a gap to fill — **the file contains evidence
> contradicting a material representation.**
>
> **Decline, do not transmit the file, and escalate above the manager.** Following the instruction while
> noting it was directed **does not transfer responsibility for the act — it documents that you knew.**

### Problem 3

A seller carries a $400,000 second behind institutional first financing. The documentation was assembled
from a form. It contains an open-ended clause agreeing to subordinate to future financing, no
requirement that the seller be notified if insurance or taxes lapse, and no servicing arrangement. Two
years later the first forecloses.

**Identify the seller's exposures and the provision that mattered most.**

> **Solution.** **The subordination clause is the single most consequential provision in carryback
> documentation**, and an open-ended one **commits the seller to move behind loans that do not yet exist,
> on terms nobody has seen.** A seller who signs it **has agreed to something he may not have
> understood** — and no licensee should ever describe it as standard.
>
> **Priority risk is what materialized.** A carryback second is **extinguished as a claim against the
> property by a senior foreclosure**, leaving the seller an unsecured claim subject to California's
> substantial **deficiency restrictions.**
>
> **The other gaps compounded it.** With no notification arrangement, **insurance or tax lapse impairs
> the collateral without the seller knowing.** With no servicing arrangement, payments may be made and
> not properly recorded. And the seller's security was a property **he no longer controlled**, exposing
> him to waste and condition risk.
>
> **What the licensee should have done:** explain the mechanics and risks to each party in plain terms;
> ensure the **California carryback disclosure statement** obligations were addressed — they exist
> precisely because a seller extending credit has none of the information a lender would have;
> **recommend counsel for each party** and a tax professional for the tax questions; and document what was
> explained. **Seller-carry documentation belongs with counsel** — the provisions that matter are exactly
> the ones a form will not resolve.

### Problem 4

An owner-occupant with damaged credit needs $180,000 against his home to pay medical debts. A hard-money
lender will fund in eight days at 11.5% interest-only with 4 points and a two-year balloon. The
originator plans to classify it business-purpose because the borrower also owns a small landscaping
company, and notes the borrower "says he'll refinance when his credit recovers."

**Assess.**

> **Solution.** **This is a consumer-purpose loan on the facts, and the proposed classification is the
> mechanism by which the protections would disappear.**
>
> **The proceeds fund personal medical debts**, secured by his dwelling. Owning a business does not make
> a loan business-purpose — **the analysis rests on what the proceeds actually fund** and the
> transaction's character.
>
> **Hard money is not a category that escapes the consumer framework. Business purpose is.** Conflating
> them is exactly how consumer borrowers end up in loans for which **no ability-to-repay determination
> was ever made** — and a collateral-focused underwrite is precisely the one that does not perform it.
>
> **The exit is not an exit.** "He'll refinance when his credit recovers" is a refinance **he could not
> obtain today**, depending on conditions at maturity: his credit, the property's value, and whether
> credit is available. **"I'll just refinance" assumes a market**, and naming that assumption is the
> disclosure that matters.
>
> **The pattern to recognize:** an owner-occupant on a short-term balloon with no documented exit, and —
> given substantial equity and a loan he may not be able to repay — the **equity-stripping** indicators
> that produce the most serious enforcement in this area.
>
> **Route the classification to compliance and counsel before the file is built around an answer.**

### Problem 5

A broker arranges a bridge loan for a builder: 12 months, 10.5%, 3 points, interest-only, funding in
eleven days. The borrower is refinancing into construction financing already conditionally approved. The
originator's file contains the application, the appraisal, and title.

**Is this an appropriate use, and what is missing?**

> **Solution.** **The use is legitimate and the file is incomplete.**
>
> **Private money exists because institutional channels cannot serve some transactions**, and **speed** —
> a transaction that cannot wait for institutional underwriting — is among the clearest legitimate uses.
> A builder bridging to conditionally approved construction financing is a **defined bridge with a
> specific end**, which is exactly what a documented exit looks like.
>
> **What is missing is the product-fit record.** The file must answer: **why is this borrower in this
> product rather than a conventional one, and what is his exit?** Here the answers are good — speed, and
> a specific approved takeout — **but they must be in the file, not in the originator's memory.**
>
> **The exit examination is also missing:** what the takeout depends on, what happens if it does not close
> on schedule, whether an extension provision exists and at what cost, and **whether a prepayment
> provision penalizes the intended exit** — which on a twelve-month loan a builder plans to exit early is
> a real question.
>
> **And the presentation must state total cost, not only the rate** — 3 points plus twelve months at
> 10.5% — because **speed is purchased, and the price is the whole difference.**

### Problem 6

A broker arranged a $600,000 private money loan and holds $600,000 of investor funds. The transaction is
delayed. A different transaction closes Friday and is $95,000 short. The broker uses $95,000 of the
first investors' funds, intending to replace it from the next funding. All investor funds sit in one
account tracked by spreadsheet, reconciled monthly bank-to-book.

**Assess.**

> **Solution.** **Two violations, and the first is conversion.**
>
> **Using one investor's funds to complete another transaction is conversion** — using trust funds for a
> purpose other than the one for which they are held. **However temporary, however well intentioned, and
> regardless of whether the money is replaced.** **Intent to replace is a description of the plan, not a
> defense.** And where the first transaction does not close, the funds must be **returned**, not applied
> elsewhere.
>
> **The account arrangement is commingling if the firm's own funds are there**, and in any case the
> **records fail.** Investor funds are trust funds from receipt until properly disbursed — **separated,
> recorded by beneficiary**, and disbursed per the arrangement's terms and the investors' instructions.
>
> **The reconciliation misses the balance that matters.** It must compare **the bank balance, the book
> balance, and the total of the individual beneficiary ledgers.** **Bank-to-book alone cannot detect
> money held for the wrong beneficiary, because the total is right the whole time** — which is precisely
> the condition this account is now in.

### Problem 7

An investor asks a broker whether a proposed loan is "safe," what return she can expect, and whether the
broker has any interest in the transaction. The broker owns 20% of the borrowing entity, will service
the loan, and is being paid by both the borrower and the investor.

**Answer the investor's questions and address the conflicts.**

> **Solution.** **Two of her three questions must be declined, and the third exposes three conflicts.**
>
> **"Safe" may not be answered.** The duties are explicit: **no representation of outcome** — no
> projection of repayment, no assurance the collateral will hold value, and **no characterization of a
> loan as safe or as "secured" in a way implying no risk.**
>
> **A projected return may not be stated as a fact.** What she is entitled to is the loan's terms and the
> facts: the value **and its basis**, the **lien position and what sits ahead**, the loan-to-value on the
> **documented** value, the property's condition, title exceptions and requirements including anything
> unresolved, and occupancy including any tenancy that would survive a foreclosure.
>
> **Three conflicts require disclosure before she relies on anything:** the broker's **20% interest in
> the borrowing entity**; the broker **servicing the loan it arranged for investors it recruited**; and
> **compensation from both sides.**
>
> **A conflict is managed by disclosure that arrives before the party relies on the advice it affects** —
> and **a broker acting as a principal while an investor believes it is being advised has a serious
> problem.** The 20% interest makes the broker something close to a principal on the borrower side while
> presenting the loan to the investor.
>
> **None of this is prohibited as such**, which is the point: it is why the documentation and the trust
> accounting must be exact, and why the test is whether someone who was not there could reconstruct what
> she was told and on what basis.

### Problem 8

A lender who cannot lend at 13% asks a broker to add its name to the file "so the loan is exempt." The
broker also proposes moving part of the yield into a separately labeled fee to keep the stated rate at
9.9%, and is assembling twelve investors into a pooled fund to make several such loans.

**Identify each issue and state what the broker must do.**

> **Solution.** **Three legal questions, none of which the broker may answer, and one clear pattern.**
>
> **The exemption is not created by a designation.** California's usury framework has a substantial
> exemption structure, including for **loans arranged by a licensed real estate broker** — but that
> exemption depends on the broker **actually performing the arranging role**, not on a nominal
> designation. **A broker whose name appears on a transaction it did not arrange has not created an
> exemption**, and the request raises a further question about **why** the designation was wanted.
>
> **This is the pattern to recognize: a lender who cannot lend at the rate proposed, and a broker's name
> added to the file. Escalate it.**
>
> **The fee restructuring is a decision belonging to counsel.** The usury analysis is **not limited to
> the stated rate** — charges and structures can be treated as interest — so **a loan whose stated rate
> sits below a limit can still be examined.** A licensee moving yield into a fee label to keep a rate low
> has made a legal determination, and it is a pattern examiners and courts both recognize.
>
> **The pooled fund raises a securities question.** Arrangements pooling investor money can raise issues
> under securities law depending on structure — and **a licensee who recognizes the question routes it;
> one who assembles the structure without asking has answered it.**
>
> **What the broker must do:** route all three to counsel **before the loans are made**, document the
> referral, and decline the designation. **The cost of routing a usury question is a conversation. The
> cost of getting it wrong is the loan.**

## Chapter summary

**Private money exists for real reasons**, and a broker arranging it **owes duties to the investor as
well as the borrower** — accurate presentation, disclosure of what is known and of compensation and
interest, trust fund handling, and **no representation of outcome.**

**An investor must be told the value and its basis, the position and what sits ahead, the loan-to-value
on documented value, condition, title, and occupancy.**

**Assembling investors into a single loan is a regulated multi-lender structure**, and volume triggers
further requirements.

**Business purpose rests on the actual purpose and the surrounding facts.** A certification records a
statement and does not establish a fact, and **the errors run both directions — with the serious one
stripping protections the borrower was entitled to.** Route close cases before the file is built.

**A business-purpose classification suspends nothing else** — fair lending, licensing, fraud, California
broker law, usury, investor duties, and honest presentation all continue.

**Seller carrybacks must be documented properly**, and the **open-ended subordination clause** is the
provision that matters most. Address the California disclosure obligations and send each party to
counsel.

**Hard money must answer the product-fit question and examine the exit**, because every short-term
collateral loan is an exit plan wearing a loan's clothes. **Hard money does not escape the consumer
framework — business purpose does**, and conflating them is how consumer borrowers end up without an
ability-to-repay determination.

**Recognize the equity-stripping pattern and escalate rather than participate.**

**Document both sides.** Investor funds are trust funds from receipt, **conversion is conversion even
when replaced**, and reconciliation compares three balances.

**Disclose every conflict before reliance**, and never state a projected return as fact, assure
repayment, call a loan safe, present a broker valuation as an appraisal, or omit what sits ahead.

**Usury is the risk private money participants most often do not know they are running.** The
broker-arranged exemption depends on actually arranging, the analysis is not limited to the stated rate,
and **every usury question is a referral — as are securities, structure, carryback documentation, and
tax questions.**

**In private money the file is the whole compliance posture**, because there is no institutional
underwriter whose record substitutes for the broker's.
