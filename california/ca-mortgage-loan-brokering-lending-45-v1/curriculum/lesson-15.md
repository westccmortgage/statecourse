## 1. Consumer inquiry and scope

### 1.1 The chronology, and why it is a chronology

Every prior lesson in this course examined one framework at a time: licensing in Lesson 01, instruments in Lesson 03, applications in Lesson 05, the federal overlay in Lesson 07, brokerage records and trust funds in Lesson 08, disclosure and compensation in Lesson 09, underwriting in Lesson 11, closing in Lesson 12, servicing in Lesson 13, fair lending and fraud in Lesson 14.

A real file does not arrive framework by framework. It arrives as **one sequence of events in time**, and the frameworks attach themselves to particular moments in that sequence, most of them without announcing that they have.

This is the practical difficulty of mortgage compliance and the reason this lesson is built as a chronology rather than as a review. **Almost every serious defect in a mortgage file is a timing defect** — something that happened, but after the moment at which it would have protected the consumer, or before the moment at which the file could support it.

> A disclosure delivered late is not a disclosure delivered. A comparison performed after the borrower has already been steered toward one product is not a comparison. A condition cleared with a document the originator helped construct is not a cleared condition.

The chronology below is the one this course has been assembling. Read it as a single file moving forward in time, and notice at each step **what has just become true that was not true a moment earlier.**

1. **Inquiry.** A consumer contacts the brokerage. Nothing is owed yet except honesty, and the honesty obligation is already complete in scope.
2. **Scope.** The engagement is defined: what the brokerage is doing, for whom, in what capacity, and how it is paid.
3. **Application.** The defined items are in hand. Disclosure clocks start, adverse action obligations attach, monitoring information is collected where applicable, and the file must be retained from here forward regardless of outcome.
4. **Disclosure and comparison.** Federal and California disclosures are delivered on their own timing, and the products under consideration are presented on a common basis.
5. **Underwriting.** The file is assembled to support an ability-to-repay determination that a third party could verify.
6. **Conditions.** Gaps are closed with evidence obtained from its source, not constructed.
7. **Closing.** Actual terms and costs are disclosed, waiting periods run, and figures reconcile.
8. **Compensation.** What was disclosed and what was received are the same number, in the same place, paid by the same party.
9. **Post-closing.** The file is audited, retained, and made capable of answering questions asked years later by people who were not present.

**The nine steps are not equally forgiving.** Steps 3, 4, 7, and 8 carry deadlines and legal consequences that cannot be repaired after the fact. Steps 5 and 6 carry the fraud exposure. Step 9 determines whether any of the rest can be proved.

### 1.2 What the first conversation establishes

The first conversation feels informal and is not. Four things are settled in it, usually without anyone saying so.

**Capacity.** Is the brokerage acting as the borrower's agent, arranging a loan with third-party lenders, or as a principal making or buying the loan with its own or investor funds? The answer determines which duties run, which disclosures are owed, and how compensation may be structured. Lesson 08 established that this is a disclosure obligation, not an internal characterization.

**Authority.** Does the individual having the conversation hold the license and, where the activity requires it, the mortgage loan originator endorsement? Lesson 01 established that the authority question is answered before the activity, not after a complaint.

**Scope.** What is the brokerage engaged to do, and what is it not? A consumer who believes he has retained a financial advisor, a tax advisor, or counsel has misunderstood the engagement, and **the misunderstanding is the licensee's problem to correct in the moment it appears.**

**Compensation.** Who pays the brokerage, and how. The answer cannot be adjusted later based on which product the consumer selects, and the reasons are in Lesson 09.

> **The failure mode here is silence.** None of these four is difficult to state. They go unstated because the conversation is friendly and stating them feels like formality — and then the file has no record that the consumer was ever told what the brokerage was, what it would do, or who paid it.

### 1.3 What the consumer is actually asking

Consumers rarely open with the question they need answered. The stated question is usually a rate question. The real question is almost always one of four:

- **Can I qualify at all?** — an eligibility question, answered by the file, not by an opinion offered on the phone.
- **What will this cost me, in total?** — a comparison question, answered by disclosure documents on a common basis.
- **Is this the right structure for what I am doing?** — a suitability question, and the one most often skipped.
- **What happens if something goes wrong?** — a risk question, and the one that determines whether the borrower is later a servicing complaint. Lesson 13 established that most servicing complaints are origination failures arriving late.

**Answering only the stated question is how files go wrong.** A borrower who asked about rate, received a rate, and closed on a product whose structure did not fit his holding period was not given bad information. He was given incomplete information, and the file will show that no one asked.

### 1.4 Statements that must not be made in the first conversation

Lesson 09 separated three categories of statement, and the separation matters most here, because the first conversation is where a licensee is most tempted to be reassuring.

| Category | Example | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Fact** | "The Loan Estimate must be delivered within a defined period after application." | Statable |
| **Estimate** | "Based on what you have described, a file like this typically prices in this range." | Statable **if identified as an estimate and not as an assurance** |
| **Assurance** | "You will be approved." "Your rate will not change." "The appraisal will come in." | **Never statable** |

**The third category is not a matter of confidence level.** An originator who is very confident of approval still may not say the applicant will be approved, because the statement is not his to make and the applicant will rely on it.

Three further statements to refuse, all of which appear in real intake calls:

1. **"Don't worry about that, we'll take care of it later."** Whatever "that" is, it is now undocumented and unresolved.
2. **"Everyone puts it that way on the application."** Lesson 14 identified this as an invitation to participate in a misrepresentation.
3. **"Let's not put the property address in yet."** Lesson 09 identified this for what it is: an attempt to delay the application trigger, which does not work and is evidence of intent when examined.

### 1.5 Advertising arrived before the consumer did

The consumer's expectations were set before the call, by whatever advertisement produced it. Lesson 08 established the identification requirements and the misleading standard; Lesson 14 established that the fair lending analysis reaches marketing, including where a digital audience is defined.

**The audit consequence:** the advertisement is part of the file. An examiner comparing the advertised terms against the disclosed terms is performing the same comparison the consumer performed, and **a difference the consumer noticed is a complaint while a difference he did not notice is still a violation.**

### 1.6 The failure that occurs here

**Nothing is written down.** The inquiry stage produces no document in most brokerages, which means the capacity, scope, and compensation conversation either happened and cannot be proved, or did not happen and the file looks identical.

The control is small and almost never used: **a dated intake note recording what the consumer said he wanted, what he was told about capacity and compensation, and what he was told the brokerage would not do.** It costs two minutes and it is the only evidence that will exist about the conversation that framed everything after it.

## 2. Application and identity

### 2.1 The moment an application exists

Lesson 05 established the operative point and it is worth restating exactly, because more mortgage compliance failures trace to this line than to any other single rule.

> **An application exists when the defined items are in the originator's hands** — the consumer's name, income, Social Security number to obtain a credit report, the property address, an estimate of the property value, and the loan amount sought.

**It does not wait for a signature.** It does not wait for a form to be submitted through a portal. It does not wait for the originator to decide he is ready. It does not care whether the conversation was labeled a pre-qualification.

**And it does not require that the consumer supply the sixth item deliberately.** If the consumer names the property he is buying in the course of describing his situation, the property address is in the originator's hands.

### 2.2 Everything that starts at that moment

| Obligation | What begins |
|---|---|
| **Loan Estimate** | Delivery required within a defined short period after application, and a defined period before consummation |
| **California mortgage loan disclosure statement** | A separate California requirement on its own timing, not satisfied by the federal form |
| **Adverse action** | If the file does not proceed, the disposition must be coded correctly and notice given where required |
| **Retention** | The file is retained regardless of whether it funds |
| **Monitoring information** | Collected where applicable, on the terms Lesson 14 described |
| **Endorsement** | The activity now unambiguously requires the mortgage loan originator endorsement |

**The retention point catches brokerages repeatedly.** A file that went nowhere is still a file. Lesson 08 established that denied and abandoned files are retained, and Lesson 14 established why: **they are disproportionately the files a fair lending examination is interested in**, because a pattern of applications that quietly stopped is exactly what discouragement looks like in data.

### 2.3 Disposition coding is not clerical

Every application ends in one of a small number of dispositions, and the code chosen is a substantive statement.

- **Approved and closed** — the loan funded.
- **Approved but not accepted** — the lender approved; the applicant did not proceed.
- **Denied** — the creditor declined. Adverse action obligations attach.
- **Withdrawn** — the applicant affirmatively withdrew, before a decision.
- **Incomplete** — the applicant did not supply what was requested, after a proper notice of incompleteness.

> **Coding a denial as a withdrawal is the single most consequential clerical act in mortgage origination.** It removes the applicant from the denial data, removes the adverse action notice the applicant was owed, and — where it happens more often to some applicants than others — creates precisely the pattern Lesson 14 described as invisible discouragement.

**"He stopped answering" is not a withdrawal.** It is incomplete or denied depending on what preceded it, and the distinction is made by what the file shows was requested and when.

### 2.4 Identity

Identity verification serves two separate purposes that are easy to conflate.

**The first is program compliance.** The institution's customer identification obligations require identifying information, verification, and retention on defined terms. This is an institutional requirement with an institutional procedure, and the originator's obligation is to follow it exactly rather than to exercise judgment about it.

**The second is fraud prevention**, and it belongs to the originator. Lesson 14 established the patterns: identity documents that do not reconcile with the credit file, an applicant whose credit history begins abruptly, a third party directing the transaction, and an applicant whose knowledge of his own history is oddly thin.

**The verification discipline that defeats both identity fraud and wire fraud is the same one:** verify anything consequential **through a channel the other party does not control.** A callback goes to the number the file already held, not to the number in the message asking for the callback.

### 2.5 The documents, and the line the originator does not cross

The application is supported by evidence: income documentation, asset documentation, identity documentation, and whatever the program requires beyond it.

**The originator's role is to request, receive, review, and transmit.** It is never to create.

The line is crossed in ways that feel helpful at the time:

| The act | What it actually is |
|---|---|
| Retyping a pay stub "because the copy was unreadable" | Creating a document the lender will treat as the employer's |
| Filling in the applicant's employment dates from memory of an earlier conversation | Authoring the applicant's representation |
| Writing the letter of explanation and sending it for signature | Authoring the borrower's voice |
| Suggesting the applicant wait sixty days so a deposit "seasons" | Advising the concealment of a fact the lender relies on |
| Marking a verification complete because the employer "confirmed it verbally last week" | Representing a verification that did not occur in the form claimed |

**A letter of explanation is a place where the line is genuinely subtle.** Explaining to a borrower what the lender is asking about and what a responsive answer would address is assistance. **Drafting the content of the borrower's explanation is not**, because the resulting document is offered to the lender as the borrower's account of his own facts.

### 2.6 The three-way reconciliation

Before the file leaves the brokerage, three sources should agree: **what the applicant stated, what the documents show, and what the credit report reflects.**

Where they disagree, there are exactly two honest responses:

1. **Inquire**, where the discrepancy is plausibly an ambiguity — a name variant, a rounding difference, a date the applicant misremembered.
2. **Escalate**, where the discrepancy is a contradiction — the file contains evidence inconsistent with a material representation.

Lesson 14 established the boundary and it governs here: **when the file contradicts itself on a material point, the response is escalation rather than a judgment call.** The originator is not the trier of fact, and resolving the contradiction himself is how a person who was not part of a fraud becomes part of one.

### 2.7 The failure that occurs here

**The application trigger is treated as discretionary**, and the file records a later date than the facts support.

The defect is invisible in the file itself, because the file shows a date. It becomes visible when an examiner compares the application date against the earliest email, the credit report pull date, or the note recording the property address — **and the credit report pull date is the one that most often exposes it**, because a permissible purpose to pull it existed by virtue of an application the file says had not yet occurred.

> The control is a single rule applied without exception: **the application date is the date the sixth item arrived, whatever else the file says.**

## 3. Product comparison and disclosures

### 3.1 Two disclosure systems running at once

A California mortgage brokerage operates under **two disclosure regimes simultaneously**, and the most common examination finding in this area is the assumption that satisfying one satisfies the other.

**The federal system** produces the Loan Estimate early and the Closing Disclosure before consummation. Both are designed for comparison, which is why their layout is prescribed rather than left to the originator.

**The California system** requires the **mortgage loan disclosure statement**, a separate obligation with its own timing and its own content.

> **They overlap in subject and not in law.** A file containing a timely Loan Estimate and no California disclosure statement is a file with a California violation, and the Loan Estimate does not cure it.

Both are dated. Both are delivered. **Both need evidence of delivery**, because delivery is where the consumer's protection attaches and the file is what proves it happened.

### 3.2 Delivery evidence

The elements are the same for every disclosure this course has covered:

1. **What** was delivered — identified specifically, including its version where versions differ.
2. **To whom** — each consumer entitled to receive it, not one of two co-applicants.
3. **When** — the date, and the time where a same-day sequence matters.
4. **By what method** — mail, hand delivery, or electronic transmission.
5. **With what proof** — the acknowledgment, the transmission record, the certified mail receipt.

**Electronic delivery carries its own consent requirements.** A disclosure emailed to an address the consumer never consented to receive documents at is a disclosure the file may not be able to claim was delivered — and the consent itself is a document the file must contain.

### 3.3 The comparison on a common basis

A borrower comparing two products can only compare them if they are presented the same way. Lesson 09 established the requirement; here is what it means operationally.

**Held constant across every option presented:**

- The same loan amount.
- The same assumed closing date.
- The same assumption about points paid, or each option shown at more than one point level.
- The same time horizon for the total-cost figure.
- The same treatment of costs that are financed rather than paid at closing.

**Shown for every option, including the one the originator expects the borrower to decline:**

- Rate, and whether it is fixed for the full term or for a period.
- Payment, and **the worst case payment** where the payment can change.
- Total cost over the stated horizon.
- Every feature that changes the borrower's obligation: prepayment penalty, balloon, negative amortization, escrow waiver.

> **The worst case is the number the presentation exists to convey.** A borrower shown only the initial payment on an adjustable product has been shown the one figure guaranteed to be true for the shortest time.

**Every feature is explained with its number, in plain language.** "It adjusts after five years" is not an explanation. "After five years the rate can change once a year, by up to this much each time, to a maximum of this rate — and at that maximum your payment would be this amount" is.

### 3.4 Suitability is a conversation about the borrower, not the product

The comparison answers which product costs less. It does not answer which product fits, and the fit question requires facts about the borrower that no rate sheet contains:

- **How long does he expect to hold the property or the loan?** A borrower who will refinance or sell within a short horizon is buying a different thing than one who intends to hold for thirty years.
- **How stable is the income, and how variable?** Payment shock tolerance is not a function of income size.
- **What is the reserve position?** A borrower with no reserves is differently exposed to an adjustable payment than one with a year of expenses saved.
- **What else is coming?** A known future expense — tuition, a lease expiration, a planned reduction in work — changes which structure survives.

**These questions are asked and the answers recorded**, because the file is later read by someone deciding whether the product presented was consistent with what the borrower described.

### 3.5 Changed circumstances

Disclosures get revised. Lesson 09 established the tolerance framework and the restricted list of valid reasons; the discipline here is documentary.

**A revised disclosure is supported by a contemporaneous record of the reason**, created when the circumstance changed, not assembled later when the revision is questioned.

| The pattern | What it looks like in the file |
|---|---|
| **Legitimate** | An appraisal returns below contract, the loan amount changes, the revision follows within the required period, and the appraisal is in the file dated before the revision |
| **Not legitimate** | A revision issued close to closing citing "changed circumstances" with no document in the file identifying what changed |

> **"Changed circumstances" is a category with contents, not a phrase.** A revision whose stated reason cannot be traced to a document is a tolerance violation with a label on it.

### 3.6 The steering question

Lesson 14 established that steering on a protected basis is prohibited regardless of intent. Lesson 09 established that compensation may not vary with the terms of the transaction, and the two rules meet here.

**The structural protection is that the originator's pay does not depend on which option the borrower picks.** Where that is true, the presentation has no incentive to distort. Where a brokerage's arrangement makes one option more profitable to the individual originator, **the comparison is compromised before it is delivered**, and no amount of care in the presentation repairs it.

### 3.7 The failure that occurs here

**The comparison is delivered orally and disappears.** The originator explains three options carefully and correctly, the borrower chooses, and the file contains the disclosures for the chosen product and no evidence that any other product was ever discussed.

Two years later the borrower complains that he was never told about a fixed-rate option. **The originator remembers telling him. The file cannot say so.**

> The control is a one-page comparison sheet, dated, listing every option presented on a common basis, retained in the file whether or not the borrower kept his copy.

## 4. Underwriting and conditions

### 4.1 What the file has to prove

Lesson 11 established the ability-to-repay framework. Its practical form is a question the file must answer without the originator present:

> **Could a reviewer who has never spoken to this borrower determine, from the documents alone, that a reasonable good-faith determination of ability to repay was made — and see the facts it rested on?**

The elements the file demonstrates:

1. **Income and employment**, verified from sources reasonably relied upon and documented.
2. **Assets**, where relied on, with their source traceable.
3. **Obligations**, from the credit report and from what the file otherwise reveals.
4. **The payment used in the determination**, and — where the payment can change — that the determination did not rest on the lowest payment the product will ever produce.
5. **Credit history**, considered on the terms the framework requires.

**The originator does not make the credit decision** in a brokerage arrangement. He assembles the file the decision rests on, which means the quality of the determination is largely determined before the file leaves his hands.

### 4.2 Conditions

An approval arrives with conditions. Clearing them is where the origination file is most often quietly corrupted, because each individual condition is small and the pressure is time.

**The discipline is one rule:** every condition is cleared with **evidence obtained from its source, in the form the source produces it.**

| Condition | Cleared correctly | Cleared incorrectly |
|---|---|---|
| Explain a large deposit | The borrower supplies the source document and his own written explanation | The originator writes the explanation and sends it for signature |
| Verify current employment | The verification is performed through the prescribed channel and documented | A note records that the borrower says he is still employed |
| Missing page of a bank statement | The borrower supplies the page from the institution | The page is omitted and the total is annotated by hand |
| Address a credit inquiry | The borrower states in writing whether new debt resulted | The originator concludes from the credit report that none did |
| Confirm gift funds | The donor executes the letter and the transfer is traced | The letter is prepared for the donor's signature and the transfer is not traced |

**The right column is not a set of shortcuts.** Each entry produces a document that says something the source did not say, and Lesson 14 established that a lender relying on it makes the misrepresentation material.

### 4.3 Conditions the borrower cannot meet

Some conditions cannot be satisfied because the underlying fact is not what the file assumed. This is the moment the file either becomes honest or becomes a problem.

**The honest path:** report the fact, restructure the transaction around it, or let the transaction not proceed. A borrower who does not qualify has not been failed by an originator who tells him so.

**The dishonest paths**, all of which appear in enforcement matters:

- Substituting a different program to avoid the condition, without disclosing why.
- Re-characterizing the borrower's occupancy intent, which Lesson 14 identified as material and therefore as fraud.
- Adding an occupant co-borrower whose participation is nominal.
- Advising the borrower to move funds so that the condition is not asked again.

> **A condition that cannot be met is information.** Treating it as an obstacle to route around is the point at which an originator's problem stops being a compliance problem.

### 4.4 The appraisal

Lesson 11 and Lesson 14 both addressed valuation, from different directions, and the rules combine into three:

1. **Supply information, never a target.** Comparable sales, permits, a list of improvements, and the contract are information. A number the file needs is a target, and communicating it is coercion of the valuation regardless of how it is phrased — **including at your own manager's request.**
2. **Deliver the appraisal to the applicant** on the terms the framework requires, and retain the evidence of delivery.
3. **A low appraisal is a fact, not a problem to solve.** It may support a reconsideration through the prescribed channel with additional data, or it may end the transaction at that price. It does not support pressure.

### 4.5 If the file does not proceed

Lesson 07 and Lesson 14 established the adverse action requirements. Restated for the moment they apply:

- **Notice within the defined period**, containing either the specific reasons or a disclosure of the right to obtain them.
- **The ECOA notice** identifying the enforcement agency.
- **The FCRA content** where a consumer report was used — the agency's name, address, and telephone; a statement that the agency did not make the decision and cannot supply the reasons; and the rights to a free copy and to dispute.
- **Correct disposition coding**, per Section 2.3.
- **Retention** of the file, on the applicable periods, extended where a complaint has been made or a proceeding is pending.

> **A denial handled properly is not a failure of the file.** A denial handled by letting the applicant go silent is a violation with no document to mark it.

### 4.6 The failure that occurs here

**The file clears conditions with documents the brokerage helped produce**, and does so at the end of the process when everyone involved wants the transaction over.

The defect compounds because it is cumulative: one retyped statement is an error in judgment, and a file in which four conditions were cleared with originator-assisted documents is a file that no longer represents the borrower's own evidence.

> The control is procedural rather than motivational: **every condition-clearing document identifies who produced it**, and any document produced inside the brokerage on the borrower's behalf is escalated rather than transmitted.

## 5. Closing and compensation

### 5.1 The Closing Disclosure and the waiting period

Lesson 12 established the closing sequence. Its two operative facts:

**The Closing Disclosure must be received by the consumer a defined period before consummation**, and **certain changes restart the waiting period.**

The changes that restart it are narrow and specific, and the ones that do not restart it are far more numerous. **The originator's obligation is not to memorize which is which in the abstract. It is to know that the question exists** and to raise it before a change is agreed, because a borrower who asks for a change the day before closing is entitled to know what it does to his closing date **before he decides.**

> A borrower who learns after the fact that his request delayed closing by a week has been surprised by his own decision, which is the outcome the disclosure regime exists to prevent.

### 5.2 Reconciliation, line by line

Closing is where every earlier estimate becomes a number someone actually pays, and the audit question is simple: **does each figure trace to something?**

1. **Loan amount** — matches the approval and the note.
2. **Rate and term** — match the lock, and the lock is in the file with its expiration.
3. **Each cost** — traces to the tolerance category it was placed in on the Loan Estimate, and each variance is either within tolerance or cured.
4. **Payoffs** — trace to demands obtained from the holders, with per diem stated and a good-through date.
5. **Prorations** — trace to the closing date actually used.
6. **Compensation** — matches Section 5.4 below.
7. **The cash figure** — is the arithmetic result of the above and not a plug.

**A figure that cannot be traced is the definition of an audit finding**, whether or not it is wrong.

### 5.3 Tolerance variances and cures

Lesson 09 established the tolerance categories. The operational rule at closing:

**A variance outside tolerance without a valid changed circumstance is cured**, and the cure is delivered on the terms the framework requires, with the file showing what was cured, how much, and when.

> **A cure performed is a compliance record. A cure omitted because "the borrower did not notice" is the finding.**

Two habits produce most tolerance failures, and neither is about the rules:

- **Estimating optimistically at application** to make an offer look better, which guarantees the variance.
- **Not re-checking the categories before the Closing Disclosure is issued**, which means the variance is discovered by an auditor rather than by the brokerage.

### 5.4 Compensation, the whole picture

This is the single most examined area in mortgage brokerage, and Lesson 09 established the rules. The integrated version, applied to a closing file:

**The restrictions:**

- **Compensation may not vary with the terms of the transaction**, other than by loan amount on the terms permitted.
- **Dual compensation is restricted** — the arrangement determines who may pay, and the brokerage does not collect from both sides where the restriction applies.
- **Referral fees and unearned fee splits are prohibited**, and Lesson 07 established that disclosure does not cure a prohibited referral payment. It is not a disclosure problem.
- **Affiliated business arrangements have conditions**, and meeting them is a factual question about the arrangement, not a matter of providing a form.

**The reconciliation the file must survive:**

| The question | Where the answer must come from |
|---|---|
| What was disclosed? | The Loan Estimate, the Closing Disclosure, and the California disclosure statement, in the prescribed places |
| What was received? | The brokerage's own accounting records |
| Who paid it? | The settlement statement, and it must be the party the disclosure named |
| Do the three agree? | **They must, exactly** |

> **A discrepancy between disclosed and received compensation is not a bookkeeping issue.** It is the fact pattern of the most common enforcement matter in mortgage brokerage, and it is discoverable by an examiner comparing two documents the brokerage produced itself.

### 5.5 Trust funds and advance fees

Lesson 08 established both frameworks, and both surface at closing.

**Funds received that belong to another** are handled as trust funds: placed where the framework requires, recorded, never commingled with brokerage operating funds, never used for brokerage purposes, and reconciled on the required schedule.

**A shortage is corrected by the brokerage immediately**, and the correction is documented. **An overage is not brokerage income** and does not become brokerage income through the passage of time or the difficulty of identifying its owner.

**The advance fee framework** applies to fees collected before the service is performed, and it is characterized by what the fee is for and when it is taken, not by what it is called. **Renaming an advance fee does not remove it from the framework.**

### 5.6 What the consumer is entitled to understand

The signing table is not the place to explain the loan, and it is the last place it can be explained.

The features Lesson 09 required be explained with their numbers — the adjustment mechanics, the prepayment penalty, the balloon, the escrow arrangement, and what happens if the payment changes — should be familiar to the borrower by now. **Where they are not, the presentation failed earlier**, and the signing is not a cure.

> **Acknowledgment is not understanding.** A signature on a disclosure establishes that the document was presented. It does not establish that its contents were explained, and a complaint alleging they were not is answered by what the file shows about the earlier conversation, not by the signature.

### 5.7 The failure that occurs here

**Disclosed compensation and received compensation differ**, usually by a small amount, usually because a lender credit or a rebate was recorded in one place and not the other.

The amount is rarely large. **The finding is not about the amount.** It is that the brokerage's own records contradict its own disclosure, on the one subject where the consumer has no independent way to verify what he was told.

> The control is a pre-funding reconciliation performed by someone other than the originator: **compare the compensation on the Closing Disclosure against the brokerage's expected receipt, and resolve any difference before funding rather than after.**

## 6. Post-closing audit and the regulator-ready file

### 6.1 The standard

**Reconstructability:** could a person who was not present determine what happened, when, on what basis, and who decided it?

Every retention rule, dating requirement, and delivery-evidence obligation in this course serves that one standard. **A file satisfies it or it does not, and no amount of accurate recollection substitutes**, because the people who will read the file are examiners, auditors, litigants, and successors — none of whom were there.

### 6.2 The audit sequence

1. **Completeness** — every required document present, including for the parts that did not go smoothly.
2. **Dates** — everything dated; delivery evidenced wherever timing carries a consequence.
3. **Consistency** — names, amounts, property descriptions, and dates reconcile across documents.
4. **Chronology** — the order matches the rules: disclosure before decision, application date before credit pull, appraisal before the revision it justified.
5. **Nothing improper present** — no unexecuted draft filed as final, no superseded figure presented as current, no document produced inside the brokerage in a party's voice.
6. **Compensation reconciled** — disclosed equals received, paid by the disclosed party.
7. **Retention** — filed where it will be found, for the longest applicable period, with the period extended where a complaint has been made or a proceeding is pending.

**Superseded versions are kept.** The sequence of versions is frequently what proves when a term changed and who agreed to it, which is the question a dispute usually asks.

### 6.3 A worked audit, ranked

> A funded California brokerage file contains:
>
> - **(a)** A Loan Estimate dated **nine days after** the earliest email in the file identifying the subject property, the loan amount, and the borrower's income.
> - **(b)** A revised Loan Estimate issued **two days before closing** citing "changed circumstances," with **no document in the file identifying what changed.**
> - **(c)** A Closing Disclosure showing broker compensation of one figure; the brokerage's ledger shows a **different figure received.**
> - **(d)** **No California mortgage loan disclosure statement**, the file relying on the Loan Estimate.
> - **(e)** A letter of explanation for a large deposit, **in the originator's writing style, on the brokerage's letterhead**, signed by the borrower.
> - **(f)** A product comparison referenced in an email but **not in the file.**
> - **(g)** A trust ledger showing a **small unexplained overage** from the transaction, still on the books.
>
> **Ranked by seriousness:**
>
> **1. (e) The originator-authored explanation.** Different in kind from everything else here. It is a document offered to the lender as the borrower's own account of a material fact, produced by the party with an interest in the outcome. Lesson 14 placed this on the fraud side of the line, not the documentation side.
>
> **2. (c) The compensation discrepancy.** The brokerage's records contradict its own disclosure on the one subject the consumer cannot independently verify. This is the fact pattern of the most common enforcement matter in the field.
>
> **3. (a) The late Loan Estimate.** The application existed nine days before the file says it did. The consumer's comparison window was shortened by the length of the delay, and the earliest email proves it.
>
> **4. (d) The missing California disclosure.** A separate California requirement, unsatisfied, with a documented pattern of examiners looking for exactly this substitution.
>
> **5. (b) The unsupported revision.** A tolerance reset with no traceable reason, two days before closing — the timing that makes it least likely to be a coincidence.
>
> **6. (g) The trust overage.** Small, and still not the brokerage's money. It is corrected and documented, not aged into income.
>
> **7. (f) The missing comparison.** The presentation may have been exemplary. The file cannot say so, and in a dispute that is the same outcome as not having made it.
>
> **The response:** record every finding, escalate to the responsible broker, obtain what legitimately exists elsewhere, correct the trust ledger now, and fix the processes that produced items (a), (b), and (d) — because each of those is a procedure failure that will recur on every file until the procedure changes.

### 6.4 The line

> **What must not happen:** back-dating the Loan Estimate, drafting a changed-circumstance memorandum now and filing it as contemporaneous, adjusting the ledger to match the Closing Disclosure, producing a California disclosure statement today and dating it to the application, or reconstructing the missing comparison sheet from memory and filing it as the original.
>
> A **documented gap** is a compliance problem a brokerage can survive. Findings are made, corrections are ordered, procedures change, and the firm continues.
>
> A **manufactured document** is falsification. It converts a defensible examination into a question about the integrity of every other file the brokerage has produced, and it ends careers.

**Corrections are made visibly**, with the original preserved, the change attributable to a person, and the date of the correction shown as the date of the correction. **A record that can be silently altered is not a record**, and a brokerage whose files can be silently altered has no defensible files at all — including the ones that were always correct.

**The distinction is worth stating in its most useful form:** it is always permissible to add a document that is true today and says what it is. "Memorandum prepared on this date, reconstructing what the file shows about the presentation made on that date" is an honest document. The same content, undated and filed as though it were created at the time, is not.

### 6.5 The course in four questions

When you meet a mortgage situation this course did not cover, four questions will usually produce the right answer:

1. **What is my capacity, and what does it authorize?** Broker or lender, agent or principal, licensed for this activity or not. Lesson 01 answered this and every arrangement re-poses it.
2. **What has already started running?** Almost every mortgage obligation is a clock started by an event — application, receipt of a document, a decision, a request. If you cannot say what has started, something has, and unnoticed.
3. **Does the consumer have the information this decision requires, on a basis he can compare?** If not, the decision is being made without the protection the framework was built to provide.
4. **What will the file show?** If the answer is "nothing," create the record now, while it is contemporaneous and true — and if the only way to make the file say the right thing is to make it say something untrue, the answer is that the file says what happened.

### 6.6 Checkpoint

You should be able to take an unfamiliar mortgage file, place its events on the chronology in Section 1.1, identify which obligations attached at which moment, rank the defects you find by seriousness rather than by order of discovery, and state for each one whether the remedy is a document you may create today or an escalation you must make instead.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **The chronology** | Inquiry, scope, application, disclosure and comparison, underwriting, conditions, closing, compensation, post-closing. Frameworks attach to moments in it, mostly **without announcing that they have.** |
| **Timing defect** | The form almost every serious mortgage file defect takes: the right act, after the moment at which it protected anyone. |
| **The four things the first conversation settles** | **Capacity, authority, scope, and compensation** — usually without anyone saying so, which is why nothing is in the file. |
| **The four real questions** | Can I qualify, what will it cost in total, is this the right structure, and what happens if something goes wrong. **Answering only the stated rate question is how files go wrong.** |
| **Fact, estimate, assurance** | Statable, statable if identified as an estimate, and **never statable.** Confidence does not convert the third into the second. |
| **"Let's not put the address in yet"** | An attempt to delay the application trigger. It does not work, and it is **evidence of intent** when examined. |
| **When an application exists** | When the defined items are in hand — name, income, Social Security number, property address, estimated value, loan amount sought. **It does not wait for a signature or a form.** |
| **What starts at application** | Loan Estimate timing, the separate California disclosure statement, adverse action obligations, retention, monitoring collection, and the endorsement requirement. |
| **The application date rule** | **The date the sixth item arrived, whatever else the file says.** The credit pull date is what most often exposes a later one. |
| **Disposition coding** | Approved and closed, approved not accepted, denied, withdrawn, incomplete. **Coding a denial as a withdrawal** removes the applicant from the denial data and the notice he was owed. |
| **"He stopped answering"** | Not a withdrawal. **Incomplete or denied**, decided by what the file shows was requested and when. |
| **The verification channel rule** | Verify anything consequential **through a channel the other party does not control.** Defeats identity fraud and wire fraud with one discipline. |
| **Request, receive, review, transmit** | The originator's role in documentation. **Never create.** |
| **The letter of explanation line** | Explaining what the lender is asking is assistance. **Drafting the borrower's account of his own facts is authorship**, and the lender receives it as the borrower's. |
| **The three-way reconciliation** | What the applicant stated, what the documents show, what the credit report reflects. **Inquire on ambiguity; escalate on contradiction.** |
| **Two disclosure systems** | Federal and California, overlapping in subject and **not in law.** The Loan Estimate does not satisfy the California mortgage loan disclosure statement. |
| **Delivery evidence** | What, to whom, when, by what method, with what proof — **and electronic delivery carries its own consent requirement.** |
| **Common basis** | Same amount, same closing date, same point assumption, same horizon, same treatment of financed costs — **for every option, including the one expected to be declined.** |
| **The worst case** | The figure the presentation exists to convey. The initial payment on an adjustable product is **the one number guaranteed to be true for the shortest time.** |
| **Suitability facts** | Holding period, income stability, reserves, and known future expenses. **Recorded, because the file is later read by someone assessing fit.** |
| **Changed circumstance** | **A category with contents, not a phrase.** A revision whose reason cannot be traced to a document is a tolerance violation with a label on it. |
| **The structural steering protection** | Compensation that does not vary with which option the borrower selects. Where it does, **the comparison is compromised before it is delivered.** |
| **Ability to repay, in file terms** | Could a reviewer who never met the borrower see the determination and the facts it rested on — **including that it did not rest on the lowest payment the product will ever produce.** |
| **The condition rule** | Cleared with **evidence obtained from its source, in the form the source produces it.** |
| **A condition that cannot be met** | **Information.** Routing around it — program substitution, occupancy recharacterization, a nominal co-borrower, moving funds — is where the problem stops being a compliance problem. |
| **Information, never a target** | The appraisal rule. Comparables, permits, improvements, and the contract are information. **A number the file needs is coercion, including at your manager's request.** |
| **Adverse action content** | Specific reasons or the right to obtain them, the ECOA notice, and the **FCRA content** where a report was used — plus correct coding and retention. |
| **Waiting period restart** | Certain changes restart it. **The obligation is to raise the question before a change is agreed**, so the borrower is not surprised by his own decision. |
| **Closing reconciliation** | Loan amount, rate and term, each cost against its tolerance category, payoffs, prorations, compensation, and the cash figure as a result rather than a plug. **A figure that cannot be traced is a finding whether or not it is wrong.** |
| **Cure** | Performed and documented — what, how much, when. **Omitted because "he did not notice" is the finding.** |
| **The compensation reconciliation** | Disclosed, received, and paid-by must agree **exactly**, across the disclosures, the ledger, and the settlement statement. |
| **Referral fees** | Prohibited, and **disclosure does not cure them.** Not a disclosure problem. |
| **Trust overage** | Not brokerage income, and **does not become income through the passage of time** or the difficulty of identifying its owner. |
| **Advance fee** | Characterized by what the fee is for and when it is taken. **Renaming it does not remove it from the framework.** |
| **Reconstructability** | Could someone who was not present determine what happened, when, on what basis, and who decided. **The standard every retention rule serves.** |
| **The audit sequence** | Completeness, dates, consistency, chronology, nothing improper present, compensation reconciled, retention. |
| **Superseded versions** | Kept. The sequence of versions proves **when a term changed and who agreed**, which is what a dispute usually asks. |
| **Documented gap** | A compliance problem a brokerage survives. |
| **Manufactured document** | Falsification. It converts a defensible examination into **a question about every other file the brokerage has produced.** |
| **The honest late document** | "Prepared on this date, reconstructing what the file shows" — permissible. The same content filed as contemporaneous — **not.** |
| **The four questions** | What is my capacity, what has already started running, does the consumer have what this decision requires, and what will the file show. |

## Practice problems

### Problem 1

On a Tuesday a consumer emails an originator: "We're buying 418 Cedar Lane, asking 640,000, we'd put
20 percent down, I make about 175,000 and my wife makes 90,000, my social is on the attached form."
The originator replies that he will "start looking at options." He pulls credit Wednesday. He sends the
Loan Estimate the following Friday, nine days later, dating the application to the Thursday before it
because that is when the borrower signed the brokerage's intake form.

**When did the application arise, what does the file now show, and what will an examiner compare?**

> **Solution.** **The application arose Tuesday.** All six items were in the originator's hands in the
> email: name, income, Social Security number, property address, an estimate of value — the asking price
> in a purchase context — and the loan amount sought, derivable from price and stated down payment.
>
> **The signature is irrelevant.** The trigger does not wait for a form, a portal submission, or the
> originator's readiness. The intake form documents an application that had already existed for eight
> days.
>
> **What the file now shows:** an application date that is nine days later than the facts support, and a
> Loan Estimate whose timing is measured from the wrong event.
>
> **What the examiner compares:** the earliest email in the file, the credit report pull date, and the
> stated application date. **The credit pull is the one that closes it** — a permissible purpose to pull
> the report existed by virtue of an application the file says had not yet occurred. The file
> contradicts itself, and the contradiction was produced by the brokerage.
>
> **The correct handling on Tuesday:** record the application date as Tuesday, start the Loan Estimate
> clock and the California disclosure statement clock from it, and treat the intake form as what it is —
> a document collected during an application already in progress.

### Problem 2

An originator presents three products to a borrower over the phone, carefully and accurately: a
thirty-year fixed, a seven-year adjustable, and the same fixed with two points. He explains the
adjustment mechanics and the worst-case payment on the adjustable. The borrower selects the fixed. The
file contains the disclosures for the fixed and nothing else. Two years later the borrower complains
that the adjustable was never explained and that he would have chosen it.

**What is the brokerage's position, and what would have changed it?**

> **Solution.** **The brokerage's position is that it cannot prove a presentation that in fact occurred**
> — which, for every practical purpose, is the same position as not having made it.
>
> The originator's recollection is honest and probably accurate. It is also unverifiable, it is the
> recollection of the interested party, and it is two years old. The complaint is answered by the file
> or it is not answered.
>
> **What would have changed it:** a one-page comparison sheet, dated, listing all three options on a
> common basis — same loan amount, same assumed closing date, same horizon for the total-cost figure —
> with rate, payment, **worst-case payment**, and total cost for each, retained in the file whether or
> not the borrower kept his copy.
>
> **Two additional notes.** First, the sheet is retained even where the borrower declines it, because
> the file's purpose is not to serve the borrower's convenience. Second, the complaint here is about a
> product the borrower did **not** select, which is why the comparison must include **the option the
> originator expected to be declined.** The options a file omits are the ones later disputes are about.

### Problem 3

Underwriting conditions a loan on an explanation for an 18,400 deposit. The borrower is traveling and
hard to reach. The processor drafts a letter on brokerage letterhead explaining that the deposit was
proceeds from the sale of a vehicle, emails it to the borrower, who replies "yes that's right, go
ahead," and the processor signs the borrower's name with his permission and transmits it. The
underwriter clears the condition.

**Identify every defect and state the correct handling.**

> **Solution.** **Three defects, escalating.**
>
> **1. The brokerage authored the borrower's account of a material fact.** The lender receives the letter
> as the borrower's own statement about the source of his funds. It is the processor's statement. This is
> the line in Section 2.5 and Lesson 14 placed it on the fraud side, not the documentation side.
>
> **2. The condition was not cleared with source evidence.** The condition asks where the money came
> from. A narrative — from anyone — is not the answer. **The bill of sale, the buyer's payment
> instrument, and the deposit record** are the answer, and the borrower's own written explanation
> accompanies them rather than replacing them.
>
> **3. Signing the borrower's name.** Verbal permission by email does not make the brokerage the
> borrower's signatory on a document a lender relies on. The document now bears a signature the borrower
> did not make, on a statement he did not write, about a fact the lender is relying on. **That is three
> separate misrepresentations in one page.**
>
> **The correct handling:** send the borrower the condition in the underwriter's words, ask him for the
> source documents and his own explanation in his own words with his own signature, and tell the lender
> the condition is pending while the borrower travels. **A delayed closing is a scheduling problem. This
> letter is not.**
>
> **And if it has already been transmitted:** escalate to the responsible broker in writing, with facts
> rather than a characterization. The remedy is a corrected submission with the real evidence, made
> visibly — **not the quiet substitution of a better letter.**

### Problem 4

An appraisal returns at 605,000 against a 640,000 contract. The originator's manager tells him to call
the appraiser, mention that "the contract is at 640 and everyone is expecting it to work," and send
over three sales the manager selected. The manager adds: "And next time use Whitfield — his numbers
come in."

**Assess each instruction.**

> **Solution.** **All three are improper, and the third is the worst.**
>
> **Mentioning the contract price as an expectation is a target.** The rule is **information, never a
> target.** The contract itself is information and the appraiser typically already has it. "Everyone is
> expecting it to work" communicates the number the file needs, which is coercion regardless of the
> polite phrasing.
>
> **Manager-selected comparables are a target in disguise.** Supplying sales the appraiser may not have
> is legitimate when the sales are supplied because they are relevant. Supplying three chosen because
> they support a number is the same instruction with an extra step. The correct form is a
> **reconsideration through the prescribed channel**, with the data submitted as data and the appraiser
> free to reject it.
>
> **Selecting appraisers by favorable history builds coercion into the ordering pattern**, which Lesson
> 14 identified specifically. It is worse than a single improper call because it is systemic, it affects
> every future file, and it is discoverable in the brokerage's own ordering records.
>
> **"My manager told me to" does not transfer responsibility**, and Lesson 14 established that
> documenting an improper instruction does not either — **it documents that you knew.**
>
> **The correct response:** decline all three, put the reconsideration through the prescribed channel
> with data rather than a target, and escalate the appraiser-selection instruction, because it describes
> a practice rather than a single transaction.

### Problem 5

Two days before closing the originator issues a revised Loan Estimate increasing two fees, citing
"changed circumstances." The file contains no document identifying what changed. At closing, the
Closing Disclosure shows broker compensation of 8,900. The brokerage ledger shows 9,400 received — the
difference being a lender rebate recorded by accounting but not reflected on the disclosure. The
brokerage also holds a 214 overage in trust from the transaction that no one has been able to allocate.

**Rank these findings and state the remedy for each.**

> **Solution.** **Ranked:**
>
> **1. The compensation discrepancy (500).** The brokerage's own records contradict its own disclosure
> on the one subject the consumer has no independent way to verify. This is the fact pattern of the most
> common enforcement matter in mortgage brokerage, and it is found by comparing two documents the
> brokerage produced itself. **The amount is not the point.**
>
> **2. The unsupported revision.** "Changed circumstances" is **a category with contents, not a phrase.**
> A revision with no traceable reason is a tolerance violation, and its timing — two days before closing,
> when the borrower is least able to walk away — is what makes it least likely to be read as an
> oversight.
>
> **3. The trust overage (214).** Small, on the books, and **not the brokerage's money.** It does not
> become income through the passage of time or the difficulty of identifying its owner.
>
> **The remedies.** For the compensation: determine which figure is correct, and where the consumer was
> under-disclosed, cure on the terms the framework requires and document what was cured, how much, and
> when. For the revision: if a valid changed circumstance actually existed, the contemporaneous document
> proving it either exists somewhere or does not — **and if it does not, the fees revert to the prior
> baseline and the excess is cured.** For the overage: research the source, correct the trust ledger now,
> and follow the framework for funds whose owner cannot be identified.
>
> **What must not happen:** adjusting the ledger to match the disclosure, or writing a
> changed-circumstance memorandum today and filing it as though it were written then. **The first is
> falsifying an accounting record; the second is manufacturing a document.**

### Problem 6

A borrower's file is denied for insufficient documented income. The originator, who has spent six weeks
on it, tells the borrower to "hang on" while he moves the file to a second lender with a different
program. He does not send an adverse action notice. He codes the first submission as "withdrawn by
applicant." The second lender approves.

**Assess.**

> **Solution.** **The coding is the violation, and it is not a clerical one.**
>
> **The applicant did not withdraw.** He was denied. Coding it as a withdrawal removes the file from the
> denial data, removes the adverse action notice he was owed, and — where this practice is applied more
> readily to some applicants than others — produces **exactly the pattern Lesson 14 described as
> invisible discouragement**, because it converts denials into events that appear in no denial record.
>
> **Moving the file to a second lender is legitimate**, and often the right service to the borrower. It
> does not change what happened at the first lender.
>
> **The adverse action obligation attached at the first denial** and is not extinguished by a later
> approval elsewhere. Where a creditor denied, the notice is owed with either the specific reasons or the
> right to obtain them, the ECOA notice, and the **FCRA content** if a consumer report was used.
>
> **The correct handling:** code the first submission as denied, issue the notice, tell the borrower
> plainly that the first lender declined and why, and place the second submission in the file as its own
> event with its own dates. **The borrower ends up in the same house either way. The difference is
> whether the brokerage's data is true.**
>
> **The originator's motive here is worth naming:** he was not concealing a denial from the borrower — he
> was avoiding an awkward conversation and a data point. That is how the great majority of
> miscoded dispositions happen, and it is why the control has to be procedural rather than a matter of
> good intentions.

### Problem 7

An originator's file for a borrower with limited English proficiency contains: marketing in English
only, a nephew who did most of the talking and is not on the loan, an adjustable product where the
initial payment was discussed and the worst case was not, a signed acknowledgment of every disclosure,
and a note reading "borrower confirms he understands all terms."

**What is present here, and what does the acknowledgment establish?**

> **Solution.** **Several distinct problems, and the note establishes almost nothing.**
>
> **The acknowledgment establishes delivery, not understanding.** A signature proves the document was
> presented. "He signed it" answers a different question than "was it explained," and a self-serving note
> that the borrower confirmed understanding is the brokerage's own statement about the borrower's mental
> state. **The presentation is proved by what the file shows was explained, with what numbers.**
>
> **The worst case was not shown**, which Lesson 09 identified as the least noticed misleading
> comparison. On an adjustable product the initial payment is the number guaranteed to be true for the
> shortest time, and a presentation built on it is incomplete regardless of accuracy.
>
> **The third party controlling the conversation is a signal requiring attention**, not an assumption.
> It may be an ordinary family arrangement. It may be the pattern Lesson 14 described. **The response is
> to establish direct communication with the borrower** — through a competent interpreter where needed —
> and to record that it occurred.
>
> **Language access is a live California issue.** California's protected characteristics include primary
> language, and California has statutory frameworks addressing the language in which certain
> negotiations were conducted and what must be provided as a result. **Do not improvise here** — this is
> a question routed to the responsible broker and, where the answer is unclear, to counsel.
>
> **What must not be done:** treat the nephew's fluency as satisfying the brokerage's obligation, treat
> the signature as the record of the explanation, or apply heightened scrutiny to the borrower because of
> his language. **The scrutiny goes to the pattern of third-party control, never to the characteristic.**

### Problem 8

You are the responsible broker. A post-closing audit of one originator's last twenty files finds: four
Loan Estimates dated after the earliest email identifying all six items, six files with no California
mortgage loan disclosure statement, two condition letters written on brokerage letterhead in the
originator's phrasing, three product comparisons referenced in emails but absent from the files, and
one compensation figure that does not match the ledger.

**State what you do, in order, and what you do not do.**

> **Solution.** **The ranking first, because the response differs by category.**
>
> **The two authored condition letters are different in kind** from everything else on the list. Every
> other item is a documentation or procedure failure. Those two are documents offered to lenders as
> borrowers' own statements about material facts. **They are escalated as such** — to counsel, and on
> counsel's direction to the lenders — and they are the reason this audit does not end at a training
> memo.
>
> **The compensation mismatch is next**, for the reasons in Problem 5.
>
> **The six missing California disclosures are a procedure failure, not an originator failure** — six of
> twenty means the process does not produce the document, and no amount of counseling one originator
> fixes a process.
>
> **The four late Loan Estimates are the same conclusion:** the brokerage's application-date rule is
> being applied by judgment. It is replaced by the rule in Section 2.7 — **the date the sixth item
> arrived** — enforced by a system check against the credit pull date.
>
> **The three missing comparisons are a retention failure**, remedied by making the comparison sheet a
> required file document rather than an optional courtesy.
>
> **What you do, in order:** record every finding with its file and date; preserve everything, including
> emails and drafts; escalate the authored letters to counsel; cure the compensation discrepancy on the
> framework's terms; change the three processes that produced the recurring items; retrain and
> re-examine this originator's files going back further, because twenty files is a sample; and document
> the audit itself, because **the audit is the evidence that the brokerage supervises.**
>
> **What you do not do:** back-date anything, produce the six California disclosures today with
> application-era dates, reconstruct the three comparison sheets and file them as originals, adjust the
> ledger, or handle the authored letters by asking the borrowers to sign replacements that say the same
> thing. **The gaps are survivable. The repairs would not be.**
>
> **And one thing you must do that is easy to skip:** consider whether the pattern of missing California
> disclosures falls disproportionately on any group of applicants. A procedure failure that is uniform is
> a procedure failure. **The same failure distributed unevenly is a fair lending finding**, and the only
> way to know which one you have is to look.

Completion requires all six instructional segments, the applied memorandum, the quiz submission, and any triggered remediation. Assignment completion does not waive the separate final examination, minimum elapsed-time, identity, proctor, or approval requirements.
