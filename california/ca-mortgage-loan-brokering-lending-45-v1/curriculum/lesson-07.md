## 1. Truth in Lending and Regulation Z

### 1.1 Why federal law is a separate lesson

**Federal consumer mortgage law applies according to its own terms**, not by reference to which state license a firm holds.

A licensee cannot reason from "we're a DRE broker" to a conclusion about a federal obligation. That is why this material sits apart from the California licensing lessons — and why "we're exempt" from a state requirement says nothing about a federal one.

**Each statute below has a common name and an implementing regulation**, and both are used in practice:

| Statute | Regulation |
|---|---|
| Truth in Lending Act | **Regulation Z** |
| Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act | **Regulation X** |
| Equal Credit Opportunity Act | **Regulation B** |
| Home Mortgage Disclosure Act | **Regulation C** |
| Fair Credit Reporting Act | **Regulation V** |

### 1.2 What TILA does

**Truth in Lending exists to promote the informed use of credit through disclosure of its terms and cost**, so a consumer can compare offers.

It does not set rates or fees. **It requires that they be disclosed, in a defined form, at defined times** — and in the mortgage context it adds substantive requirements as well.

### 1.3 Coverage

TILA generally applies to consumer credit — credit extended primarily for **personal, family, or household purposes.**

> **Business-purpose credit is generally outside it.**

That is precisely why the Lesson 04 classification discipline matters so much. **A loan misclassified as business-purpose is a loan whose consumer disclosures may never have been given**, and the borrower was stripped of protections he was entitled to. A signed certification records a statement; **the actual purpose governs.**

### 1.4 The finance charge and the APR

**The finance charge** is the cost of consumer credit expressed as a dollar amount, including interest and certain other charges imposed as a condition of the credit.

**The annual percentage rate** expresses that cost as a yearly rate, so offers with different rate-and-fee combinations can be compared.

**What the APR does not do**, restated because borrowers rely on it wrongly:

- **It is not the note rate**, and the payment is computed from the note rate.
- **It does not include every cost** — its contents are defined, and some third-party charges sit outside it.
- **It assumes the loan runs its full term**, making it a poor guide for a borrower who will sell or refinance in five years.
- **It behaves oddly on adjustable-rate loans**, because it must assume future rates.

> **A licensee explains what the APR is for and points the borrower to it. A licensee does not compute it, interpret a difference between two APRs as a conclusion, or tell a borrower which loan is cheaper for him.**

### 1.5 The integrated disclosures

For most closed-end consumer mortgage transactions, TILA and RESPA disclosure requirements are delivered through two integrated forms:

| Form | When | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Loan Estimate** | Early, within a defined period after application | States estimated terms and costs so offers can be compared |
| **Closing Disclosure** | Before consummation, with a waiting period | States the actual terms and costs |

**The Closing Disclosure carries a waiting period before consummation**, and **certain changes can restart it.** Lesson 09 develops the forms, the tolerances, and the changed-circumstance framework in detail.

**The operative point here:** these are **timing rules with legal consequences**, not service commitments. A licensee who promises a closing date that a late change makes impossible has promised something the rule will not permit.

### 1.6 The right of rescission

For certain transactions secured by the consumer's **principal dwelling** — notably refinances with a different lender and home equity transactions, and **not** a purchase-money loan on the dwelling being acquired — the consumer has a **right to rescind** within a defined period after the later of consummation, delivery of the required notice, or delivery of the material disclosures.

**Three points a licensee must hold:**

**Each consumer entitled to rescind must receive the required notices**, and the period runs from the later of the defined events — which means a defective or undelivered notice can extend it.

**Funds are not disbursed until the period expires** on a rescindable transaction, which is why such a closing is not the same as a purchase closing.

**Whether a particular transaction is rescindable, and whether a particular consumer may rescind, is a legal question.** A licensee explains that the right exists in defined circumstances and refers the application of it.

### 1.7 High-cost and higher-priced loans

Regulation Z establishes categories of loans carrying **additional restrictions and requirements**, defined by rate and points-and-fees thresholds.

**For a broker the consequential point is that compensation can count toward a points-and-fees calculation** — so a compensation arrangement can move a loan into a category with requirements the firm may not be prepared to meet. **That determination belongs to compliance**, and Lesson 09 develops it.

### 1.8 Advertising under Regulation Z

Advertising rules attach to what a mortgage advertisement may state.

**The trigger-term principle:** where an advertisement states certain specific terms, **additional disclosures are required.** An advertisement that mentions a payment amount, a term, or a rate can trigger obligations that a general advertisement does not.

**And the general standard:** an advertisement may not be **misleading**, including by prominence — a low teaser figure displayed prominently with the qualifying terms buried is misleading even where every number is accurate.

### 1.9 Checkpoint

You should be able to state what TILA does and its coverage, explain what the APR does not capture, describe the two integrated disclosures, and state what the rescission right is and is not.

## 2. RESPA and Regulation X

### 2.1 What RESPA does

**RESPA addresses the settlement process**, with two distinct purposes:

1. **Disclosure** of settlement costs, so consumers understand what they are paying and to whom.
2. **Prohibition of practices that inflate costs** — kickbacks, referral fees, and unearned fee splits.

**The second purpose is the one that produces enforcement against mortgage brokers**, and it is the one this section develops.

### 2.2 Coverage

RESPA generally applies to **federally related mortgage loans** secured by residential real property. Business-purpose transactions are generally treated differently, and — the same theme — **the classification matters.**

### 2.3 The referral prohibition

> **No person may give or accept any fee, kickback, or thing of value pursuant to an agreement or understanding that business incident to a real estate settlement service will be referred to any person.**

**And separately:** no person may give or accept any portion, split, or percentage of a charge for a settlement service **other than for services actually performed.**

**Three things a licensee must understand about this prohibition:**

> **Disclosure does not cure it.** Unlike an ordinary conflict of interest, which disclosure manages, a payment for referrals is prohibited **whether or not it is disclosed.**

**"Thing of value" is broad.** It reaches money, but also gifts, trips, event tickets, discounted or free services, below-market rent, marketing paid on someone's behalf, and reciprocal arrangements.

**An agreement or understanding need not be written.** A pattern of conduct can establish one.

### 2.4 The arrangements that look like ordinary business

The prohibition catches people precisely because the arrangements feel like normal commerce:

| Arrangement | Problem |
|---|---|
| An escrow or title company paying per file sent | Payment for referrals |
| A lender paying a broker's marketing costs in exchange for volume | Thing of value for referrals |
| A warranty company paying per policy sold | Payment for referrals |
| Free or below-cost services provided to a referral source | Thing of value |
| A "marketing agreement" paying more than fair market value for actual services | The excess is payment for referrals |
| Desk rental below fair market value to a referral source | Thing of value |
| Paying an unlicensed person per funded loan | Both unlicensed activity **and** a referral payment |

**The last row is the one that appears in DRE files**, because it fails under two frameworks at once.

### 2.5 What is permitted

**Payment for services actually performed**, at a value reasonably related to those services.

**Ordinary business courtesies** that are not tied to referrals and are not compensation in substance.

**Affiliated business arrangements**, subject to conditions — see below.

> **The test a licensee should apply: is this payment for something actually done, at a value reasonably related to it — or is it for producing business?** If the second, escalate. Assessing whether a structure works belongs to compliance and counsel, not to the people inside it.

### 2.6 Affiliated business arrangements

Where a person in a position to refer settlement business has an **ownership or beneficial interest** in a provider it refers to, that is an **affiliated business arrangement.**

It is permitted subject to conditions, in outline: **disclosure of the relationship and an estimate of charges at or before referral**, no **required use** of the affiliate except as permitted, and **no thing of value beyond a return on ownership interest.**

**A licensee must not tell a consumer he must use an affiliate** where the conditions require that the choice be his.

### 2.7 Servicing disclosure and transfer

RESPA addresses **servicing transfers**, including notice to the borrower of a transfer and a period during which payments to the prior servicer are treated as timely.

**A borrower confused about where to send a payment during a transfer is asking a real question**, and the framework anticipates the confusion.

### 2.8 Escrow accounts

RESPA governs **escrow (impound) accounts** — how much may be collected, the analysis performed, and statements provided.

**Two practical points:** the cushion a servicer may maintain is limited, and **an escrow shortage after a tax or insurance increase produces a payment increase on a loan the borrower believed was fixed.** Explaining that in advance prevents a call the licensee will otherwise receive. Lesson 13 develops servicing.

### 2.9 Error resolution and information requests

RESPA and its implementing rules provide procedures under which a borrower may submit a **notice of error** or a **request for information** to a servicer, with response obligations attached.

**A licensee's role is to recognize these as defined procedures with deadlines**, route the borrower correctly, and not attempt to resolve a servicing dispute informally.

### 2.10 Checkpoint

You should be able to state both RESPA purposes, state the referral prohibition and that disclosure does not cure it, apply the services-actually-performed test, and describe an affiliated business arrangement's conditions.

## 3. ECOA and Regulation B

### 3.1 What ECOA does

**ECOA prohibits discrimination in any aspect of a credit transaction** on prohibited bases.

**"Any aspect" is broad**: it reaches advertising and solicitation, the application process, information requested, evaluation, terms and pricing, and the treatment of an existing account — not only the approve-or-deny decision.

### 3.2 The prohibited bases

| Basis |
|---|
| **Race** |
| **Color** |
| **Religion** |
| **National origin** |
| **Sex** |
| **Marital status** |
| **Age** (provided the applicant has capacity to contract) |
| **Receipt of public assistance income** |
| **Good faith exercise of a right under the Consumer Credit Protection Act** |

**Two of these are distinctive to ECOA and are the ones licensees forget.**

**Marital status** — which is why the questions a creditor may ask about it are constrained, and why a creditor generally may not require a spouse's signature where the applicant qualifies individually.

**Receipt of public assistance income** — which cannot be discounted because of its source.

**And note the overlap with fair housing**, which prohibits discrimination in residential real-estate-related transactions on its own bases including familial status and disability. **The two frameworks overlap and are not identical**, and California adds further protections — which Lesson 14 develops.

### 3.3 What may and may not be asked

Regulation B constrains information collection.

**Generally may not be requested** in most consumer credit contexts: the applicant's **race, color, religion, national origin, or sex** — **except** where required for monitoring purposes, which is the exception below.

**Marital status inquiries are limited** to defined categories in defined circumstances.

**Income from alimony, child support, or separate maintenance** need not be disclosed unless the applicant chooses to rely on it, and the applicant must be told so.

**Public assistance income may not be discounted** because of its source.

### 3.4 Government monitoring information

For applications for credit secured by a dwelling, a creditor is generally **required to request** ethnicity, race, and sex information for monitoring purposes.

**The distinction that confuses people:** the information the creditor may not use in the decision is the information it is **required to collect** for monitoring.

**Two disciplines:**

- **The applicant may decline to provide it**, and must be told so.
- **Where the application is taken in person and the applicant declines, the creditor generally notes the information on the basis of visual observation or surname**, as the rule prescribes. That instruction strikes originators as strange until they understand it exists so the monitoring data is not defeated by non-response.

**An originator who skips the request, or who fills it in from assumption where the rule does not call for it, has corrupted data the fair lending framework depends on.**

### 3.5 Adverse action

Restated from Lesson 06 in its ECOA setting.

**Adverse action** includes a denial or revocation, a change in terms unfavorable to the applicant, or a refusal to grant credit in substantially the amount or on substantially the terms requested.

**The requirement:** notice within a defined period, containing **either a statement of specific reasons or a disclosure of the right to obtain them**, together with other required content — including the ECOA notice identifying the enforcement agency, and, where a consumer report was used, the additional content Section 5 describes.

> **The reasons must be the actual, specific reasons.** "Does not meet our credit standards" tells an applicant nothing usable and does not satisfy the requirement's purpose.

### 3.6 Appraisal and valuation copies

For applications secured by a **first lien on a dwelling**, a creditor is generally required to provide copies of appraisals and other written valuations **promptly upon completion, or a defined period before consummation, whichever is earlier** — and to notify the applicant of the right early in the process.

**Delivery is evidenced**, and a file that cannot show when the valuation reached the applicant has a gap exactly where an examiner looks.

### 3.7 Record retention

ECOA requires retention of applications and related records for defined periods, and **retention is longer where the applicant has notified the creditor of a complaint or an enforcement proceeding is pending.**

**"We closed that file" is not a reason to discard it.**

### 3.8 Where the exposure actually is

Not usually an originator who intended to discriminate. **The exposure sits in discretion:**

- Which applicants are encouraged and which are **discouraged** before an application exists.
- How quickly inquiries are returned, and to whom.
- Which files receive extra effort to clear conditions.
- Where **exceptions** are granted, and to whom.
- How **pricing discretion**, where it exists, is exercised.

> **"They probably wouldn't qualify" is not an assessment an originator is entitled to make**, and acting on it is discouragement — which produces a denial with **no record of one.**

Lesson 14 develops the controls.

### 3.9 Checkpoint

You should be able to name the prohibited bases including the two distinctive to ECOA, explain the monitoring information paradox, and state what an adverse action notice must contain.

## 4. HMDA and Regulation C

### 4.1 What HMDA does

**HMDA requires certain institutions to collect, record, and report data about mortgage applications and originations**, and to make the data available.

**It is a data statute, not a lending standard.** It does not tell a lender whom to lend to. It creates a record that supports three purposes:

1. Determining whether institutions are serving the housing needs of their communities.
2. Assisting in the distribution of public investment.
3. **Identifying possible discriminatory lending patterns and assisting enforcement.**

### 4.2 Why it matters even where a firm does not report

A broker may or may not be a reporting institution depending on the framework's coverage tests. **The data still matters to a broker for two reasons:**

**The data a lender reports comes from the file the broker assembled.** Application dates, action taken, dates of action, rate spread, and demographic information all originate in the origination record.

**Pattern detection reaches conduct no single decision reveals.** A firm whose denial rate, pricing, or withdrawal pattern differs by group without a documented neutral explanation is visible in data long before any individual complains.

> **This is the answer to "nobody has complained."** Complaints are one route. Data is another, and it does not require anyone to be dissatisfied.

### 4.3 What is reported, in outline

The data points include, among others: the application and action dates; the **action taken** — originated, approved but not accepted, denied, withdrawn, closed for incompleteness, purchased; the reason for denial where applicable; loan amount, type, purpose, and lien status; property location and type; occupancy; **applicant demographic information**; income; **rate spread**; and various loan feature and cost fields.

### 4.4 Why data accuracy is an originator's problem

Because the data comes from the origination record, and **the errors are systematic rather than random:**

| Error | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Application date recorded wrongly | Timing analysis distorted; disclosure timing questions follow |
| Action taken miscoded — "withdrawn" where it was denied | **The denial disappears from the data** |
| Incompleteness used where the file was declined | Same effect |
| Monitoring information skipped or assumed | Corrupts the analysis the statute exists to support |
| Occupancy or purpose miscoded | Distorts comparisons and connects to the Lesson 04 misclassification problem |

> **Coding a denial as a withdrawal is the one to understand.** It may be a clerical error. It is also exactly what a firm suppressing an adverse pattern would do — which is why examiners test it, and why a file must show **which disposition actually occurred, when, and on what communication.**

### 4.5 Rate spread and pricing

The data includes a **rate spread** measure for covered loans, which supports comparison of pricing across applicants.

**Where pricing discretion exists — including discretion a broker exercises over compensation within a permitted structure — pricing disparities are exactly what the data surfaces.** Lesson 09 develops the compensation rules, and Lesson 14 the fair lending analysis.

### 4.6 What a licensee does

**Record the facts accurately as they occur**, including dates.

**Request monitoring information as the rule requires**, and record the applicant's response — including a decline.

**Disposition every application**, because "stopped responding" is not a disposition.

**Never adjust a data field to improve how a file or a firm looks.** That is falsification, and it is the category that ends careers rather than producing a finding.

### 4.7 Checkpoint

You should be able to state what HMDA does and its purposes, explain why the data matters even absent complaints, and identify the coding errors that distort it.

## 5. FCRA and credit reporting

### 5.1 What FCRA does

**The Fair Credit Reporting Act governs the collection, use, and disclosure of consumer report information**, promoting accuracy, fairness, and privacy.

It reaches **consumer reporting agencies**, **users** of reports — which includes a mortgage firm — and **furnishers** of information to agencies.

### 5.2 Permissible purpose

> **A consumer report may be obtained only for a permissible purpose.** In this context, that means the consumer's credit transaction, initiated by the consumer.

**Two disciplines:**

**Obtain and document authorization.** Pulling a report without one is a problem regardless of intention.

**Do not pull a report for any other purpose.** Curiosity, a favor, checking a relative, evaluating someone who is not an applicant, or re-pulling after a file dies are not permissible purposes.

**This is one of the few areas in this course where an individual originator's single act, done casually, is itself the violation.**

### 5.3 Adverse action based on a report

Where adverse action is taken **based in whole or in part on information in a consumer report**, the notice must include additional content:

- **The name, address, and telephone number of the consumer reporting agency** that furnished the report.
- **A statement that the agency did not make the decision** and cannot supply the specific reasons.
- **Notice of the consumer's right to obtain a free copy** of the report from the agency within the defined period, and the **right to dispute** its accuracy.

**This content is separate from and additional to the ECOA reasons requirement**, and a notice missing it is deficient even where the reasons are correct.

### 5.4 Risk-based pricing

Where a consumer is granted credit on **materially less favorable terms than a substantial proportion of other consumers**, based on a consumer report, a **risk-based pricing notice** is generally required — with a **credit score disclosure** alternative available in the mortgage context.

**The operative point for a licensee:** this is a disclosure obligation attaching to **pricing**, not only to denial. A borrower who was approved is not outside the framework.

### 5.5 Accuracy and disputes

**Consumers have the right to obtain their file information and to dispute inaccuracies**, and agencies and furnishers have investigation obligations.

**What a licensee may do:** explain that the right exists, tell the borrower what the report actually shows, and direct him to the agency.

**What a licensee may not do:**

- **Advise disputing accurate information.** That is advising the borrower to file a false dispute.
- **Predict a score** or name a score he "needs" — a threshold stated as a rule.
- **Promise that an action will raise a score.**
- **Advise opening or closing accounts as a strategy.**

> **Credit repair advice is a distinct service**, and it is where originators most often exceed the line — because the advice feels helpful and the borrower is asking for it.

### 5.6 Information security and disposal

FCRA and related rules impose obligations regarding the **safeguarding and disposal** of consumer report information.

**Three practical requirements:**

**Access control** — limit who can reach consumer information to those who need it for the work.

**Protected transmission** — a loan file emailed unencrypted to a personal account is a security failure whatever the intention.

**Secure disposal** — destroy documents and media so the data cannot be reconstructed. **A recycling bin is not disposal.**

**And red flags.** Rules require programs to detect and respond to indicators of **identity theft** — an address inconsistent with the file, a report showing activity the applicant does not recognize, documents that appear altered. **An originator who notices one escalates rather than resolving it.**

### 5.7 Privacy notices

Financial privacy rules require notices about information-sharing practices and, in defined circumstances, an opportunity to **opt out** of certain sharing.

**A licensee should know these are required and delivered**, and should not describe what a particular firm's practices permit without reading the notice.

### 5.8 Checkpoint

You should be able to state permissible purpose, name the additional adverse action content required when a report is used, describe risk-based pricing, and state what a licensee may not advise about credit.

## 6. SAFE Act and federal registration context

### 6.1 What the SAFE Act does

**The SAFE Act established a nationwide framework for the licensing and registration of mortgage loan originators**, implemented through state licensing systems and a federal registry.

Its purposes: increase uniformity, reduce regulatory burden, enhance consumer protection, and **reduce fraud** — by creating a system in which an individual's history follows them.

### 6.2 Licensed versus registered

The distinction licensees confuse, and it determines which requirements apply:

| | **State-licensed** MLO | **Federally registered** MLO |
|---|---|---|
| Works for | A state-licensed entity — including a **DRE broker** | A depository institution or its regulated subsidiary |
| Requires | Education, testing, background and credit review, **state licensing** | **Registration** |
| Testing and pre-licensing education | Required | Generally not |
| Continuing education | Required annually | Generally not |
| Unique identifier | Yes | Yes |

> **A licensee working under a California DRE broker is in the state-licensed column**, and the endorsement requirements of Lesson 01 apply in full.

### 6.3 What triggers the requirement

A person acts as a **mortgage loan originator** when they, **for compensation or gain or in the expectation of it**:

- **Take a residential mortgage loan application**, or
- **Offer or negotiate terms** of a residential mortgage loan.

**Both elements must be present: the activity, and the compensation or its expectation.**

**"Residential mortgage loan" is the scope limiter** — the requirement is tied to consumer residential lending, which is why business-purpose and certain commercial transactions are analyzed differently. **And the certification does not decide the classification; the actual purpose does.**

### 6.4 The unique identifier

Each MLO receives a **unique identifier** through the registry, and **the identifier follows the individual** rather than the employer.

**It must appear as required on communications and advertising**, alongside the company's identifier — which is why a compliant mortgage advertisement carries more identification than a compliant real estate sales advertisement.

**An originator who moves firms keeps the identifier.** That is the point: history follows the person.

### 6.5 Renewal and lapse

**The endorsement renews on its own cycle**, separate from the underlying license, and requires continuing education.

> **A licensee whose broker or salesperson license is current but whose endorsement has lapsed is not authorized to originate.**

This is a recurring and entirely avoidable failure, and its consequence is that every application taken during the lapse was taken without authorization.

### 6.6 The registry as a public record

The registry supports **consumer access to information about an originator and the firm**, and it records employment history and disciplinary actions.

**Two consequences:** a consumer can verify an originator, and **an action taken in one state is visible everywhere.** The framework was built so that a person barred in one jurisdiction cannot simply reappear in another.

### 6.7 How the federal framework fits the California one

Restated because it is the lesson's organizing idea:

**The state framework** — DRE licensure, the MLO endorsement, trust funds, advertising, supervision, records — governs the license and its conduct.

**The federal framework** — TILA, RESPA, ECOA, HMDA, FCRA, the SAFE Act structure — applies **on its own terms**, across state licensing frameworks.

**Neither answers the other's questions**, and the recurring error is answering a federal question from a state premise or the reverse.

### 6.8 What a licensee does with all of it

Nothing in this lesson makes an originator a compliance officer. What it makes possible is **recognizing which framework a question belongs to and routing it**:

| Question | Belongs to |
|---|---|
| "When must this disclosure go out?" | Regulation Z and X timing, and the firm's compliance function |
| "Can we accept this payment from the title company?" | RESPA — and the answer is usually no, and **disclosure will not cure it** |
| "Can we ask the applicant this?" | Regulation B |
| "How do we code this disposition?" | Regulation C and the firm's policy — **coded as what actually happened** |
| "Can I pull his report?" | FCRA permissible purpose |
| "Am I authorized to take this application?" | SAFE Act and the endorsement's status |
| "Is this loan consumer or business purpose?" | Compliance, and counsel where close — **before the file is built** |

### 6.9 Checkpoint

You should be able to distinguish licensed from registered, state what triggers the MLO requirement, explain the identifier and the renewal cycle, and route a set of questions to the right framework.

| **Licensed versus registered** | State-licensed MLOs — including under a DRE broker — face education, testing, background review, and annual continuing education; federally registered MLOs at depositories generally do not. |
| **Unique identifier** | Follows **the individual**, appears on communications and advertising as required, and persists across firms. |
| **Endorsement lapse** | The endorsement renews on its own cycle. A current underlying license with a lapsed endorsement **does not authorize origination.** |

## Practice problems

### Problem 1

A borrower asks his originator to explain the APR on his Loan Estimate, why it differs from the rate on
the note, and whether the loan with the lower APR is "the cheaper one." The originator says the APR is
"the real rate" and that the lower one is always better.

**Correct both statements.**

> **Solution.** **"The real rate" is wrong and misleads about the payment.** The **finance charge** is
> the cost of consumer credit as a dollar amount; the **APR** expresses that cost as a yearly rate so
> offers with different rate-and-fee combinations can be compared. **The payment is computed from the
> note rate**, not the APR.
>
> **"Lower is always better" is wrong for four reasons.** The APR **does not include every cost** — its
> contents are defined and some third-party charges sit outside it. It **assumes the loan runs its full
> term**, which makes it a poor guide for a borrower who will sell or refinance in five years. It
> **behaves oddly on adjustable-rate loans** because it must assume future rates. And it is a comparison
> tool rather than a conclusion.
>
> **What the originator does:** explain what the APR is for, point the borrower to it, present the offers
> on a common basis with note rate, payment, points, and lender charges stated separately and the
> break-even where points differ — and **not tell the borrower which loan is cheaper for him.**

### Problem 2

A borrower is refinancing his primary residence with a new lender. He asks why funds are not disbursed
at signing, whether he can waive the wait, and — on a separate purchase transaction — why there is no
similar right. His originator gave the required notice to him but not to his wife, who is also on
title.

**Address each.**

> **Solution.** **This is a rescindable transaction.** For certain transactions secured by the consumer's
> **principal dwelling** — notably a refinance with a different lender — the consumer has a **right to
> rescind** within a defined period, and **funds are not disbursed until the period expires.** That is
> why a rescindable closing is not the same as a purchase closing.
>
> **The purchase is different because a purchase-money loan on the dwelling being acquired is not
> rescindable.** That is the distinction, and it is the one borrowers find counterintuitive.
>
> **The notice failure is the serious item.** **Each consumer entitled to rescind must receive the
> required notices**, and the period runs from **the later** of consummation, delivery of the notice, or
> delivery of the material disclosures — which means a defective or undelivered notice **can extend the
> period.** His wife did not receive hers.
>
> **On waiver and on whether he may rescind: those are legal questions.** A licensee explains that the
> right exists in defined circumstances and refers its application. Escalate the notice defect to
> compliance immediately rather than proceeding to disbursement.

### Problem 3

A title company offers a brokerage $300 per file sent. A lender offers to pay for the brokerage's
holiday marketing mailer in exchange for volume. A warranty company offers $75 per policy. The
brokerage's owner says all three are fine because the firm will disclose them to borrowers.

**Assess, and state the test.**

> **Solution.** **All three are prohibited, and the disclosure plan does not help.**
>
> **No person may give or accept any fee, kickback, or thing of value pursuant to an agreement or
> understanding that settlement business be referred**, and no person may accept a **split of a charge
> other than for services actually performed.**
>
> > **Disclosure does not cure it.** Unlike an ordinary conflict of interest, which disclosure manages,
> > a payment for referrals is prohibited **whether or not it is disclosed.**
>
> **"Thing of value" is broad** — it reaches the marketing mailer as squarely as the cash. And **an
> agreement or understanding need not be written**; a pattern of conduct can establish one.
>
> **The test:** is this payment for **something actually done, at a value reasonably related to it** — or
> is it **for producing business**? All three are the second.
>
> These arrangements catch people precisely because they feel like ordinary commerce. **Assessing
> whether a structure works belongs to compliance and counsel**, not to the people inside it — and a
> "marketing agreement" paying above fair market value for real services has the excess treated as
> payment for referrals.

### Problem 4

A brokerage owns 40% of an escrow company. Its originators tell borrowers "we use our escrow company on
all our files." No estimate of charges is given, and the firm receives a per-file payment from the
escrow company on top of its ownership distributions.

**Identify each defect.**

> **Solution.** **This is an affiliated business arrangement, which is permitted subject to conditions —
> and this firm has failed all three.**
>
> **Disclosure with an estimate of charges at or before referral** was not given.
>
> **"We use our escrow company on all our files" states a required use**, where the conditions require
> that the choice be the consumer's except as permitted. **A licensee must not tell a consumer he must
> use an affiliate.**
>
> **The per-file payment is a thing of value beyond a return on ownership interest** — which is the
> condition that fails hardest. The ownership distribution is the permitted return; the per-file payment
> is payment for referrals, and it converts a permissible structure into a prohibited one.
>
> Note what makes this dangerous: the firm probably believes its ownership interest legitimizes the
> arrangement. **It legitimizes the return on ownership and nothing else.**

### Problem 5

An originator, taking an application in person, skips the demographic information request because it
"feels invasive," tells a married applicant her husband must sign even though she qualifies
individually, asks another applicant whether she plans to have children, and discounts a third
applicant's public assistance income because it is "not real income."

**Assess each under ECOA.**

> **Solution.** **Four problems, and the first is the one originators most often get backwards.**
>
> **The monitoring information must be requested** on applications for credit secured by a dwelling. The
> paradox is real and worth stating: **the information the creditor may not use in the decision is the
> information it is required to collect** for monitoring. The applicant may **decline and must be told
> so** — and where the application is taken in person and she declines, the rule prescribes noting the
> information on the basis of visual observation or surname, so the data is not defeated by
> non-response. **Skipping the request corrupts the data the fair lending framework depends on.**
>
> **Requiring the spouse's signature is marital status discrimination** where the applicant qualifies
> individually — marital status being one of the two bases distinctive to ECOA that licensees forget.
>
> **The childbearing question** touches sex and familial considerations and has no legitimate place in
> the evaluation.
>
> **Public assistance income may not be discounted because of its source** — the other distinctive basis.
>
> Remember the scope: ECOA reaches **any aspect** of a credit transaction, from advertising and
> solicitation through the information requested and the treatment of an existing account.

### Problem 6

A file is denied for insufficient documented income; the credit report also showed two collections that
contributed. The notice says only "does not meet our credit standards." The applicant asks why, and the
originator tells him it was the appraisal. Separately, a firm's manager codes several declined
applications as "withdrawn" because "the borrowers weren't going to proceed anyway."

**Identify every failure.**

> **Solution.** **Four failures across three frameworks.**
>
> **The generic reason fails ECOA.** The notice must contain **either the actual, specific reasons or a
> disclosure of the right to obtain them.** "Does not meet our credit standards" tells the applicant
> nothing usable.
>
> **The FCRA content is missing.** Where adverse action is based in whole or in part on a consumer
> report, the notice must additionally include **the agency's name, address, and telephone number**, a
> statement that **the agency did not make the decision** and cannot supply the reasons, and notice of
> the rights to a **free copy** and to **dispute**. That content is separate from and additional to the
> ECOA reasons — **a notice missing it is deficient even where the reasons are correct.**
>
> **Telling him it was the appraisal is a misstatement he will act on** — he may pay for a second
> appraisal for no reason — and the notice will say otherwise.
>
> **The miscoding is the most serious item.** Coding a denial as a withdrawal **makes the denial
> disappear from the HMDA data.** It may be clerical; it is also **exactly what a firm suppressing an
> adverse pattern would do**, which is why examiners test it. The file must show **which disposition
> actually occurred, when, and on what communication** — and "the borrower wasn't going to proceed" is
> not a disposition.

### Problem 7

An originator pulls a credit report on a prospect who has not applied, "to see if it's worth pursuing."
He later re-pulls it after the file dies, to check on the borrower. He emails the full loan file to his
personal account to work at home, and advises the borrower to dispute two accurate late payments and
close his oldest card to raise his score.

**Assess each.**

> **Solution.** **Five separate problems, and the first two are among the few places in this course where
> a single casual act is itself the violation.**
>
> **Pulling a report on a non-applicant lacks a permissible purpose.** A consumer report may be obtained
> only for one, and in this context it means **the consumer's credit transaction, initiated by the
> consumer.** Prospecting is not one.
>
> **Re-pulling after the file dies is the same violation.** So are curiosity, a favor, or checking a
> relative.
>
> **Emailing the file to a personal account is a safeguarding failure.** The obligations require **access
> control**, **protected transmission**, and secure disposal — and unencrypted transmission to an
> uncontrolled account fails the second whatever the intention.
>
> **Advising a dispute of accurate items is advising him to file a false dispute.** What a licensee may
> do is explain that the right to obtain and dispute report information exists, tell him what the report
> actually shows, and direct him to the agency.
>
> **Advising him to close an account is credit strategy advice**, and promising it will raise a score
> predicts a model. **Credit repair is a distinct service**, and this is where originators most often
> exceed the line — because the advice feels helpful and the borrower asked for it.

### Problem 8

A salesperson at a DRE brokerage has been taking residential applications and quoting terms for eleven
weeks. Her salesperson license is current; her MLO endorsement lapsed and she did not notice because
the license renews next year. Her advertising carries the brokerage name but no identifiers. She tells
a colleague at a bank that "you don't need any of this — you're federally registered."

**Assess each point.**

> **Solution.** **She has been originating without authorization for eleven weeks.**
>
> **The endorsement renews on its own cycle**, separate from the underlying license. **A current
> salesperson license with a lapsed endorsement does not authorize origination** — and taking a
> residential mortgage loan application or offering or negotiating terms **for compensation or its
> expectation** is exactly what the endorsement covers. Every application taken during the lapse was
> taken without authorization, and the matter goes to the broker and compliance immediately.
>
> **The advertising is deficient.** Each MLO receives a **unique identifier** that **follows the
> individual** rather than the employer, and it must appear as required on communications and
> advertising **alongside the company's identifier** — which is why a compliant mortgage advertisement
> carries more identification than a real estate sales advertisement.
>
> **Her statement to the colleague is half right and misleading.** A **federally registered** MLO at a
> depository or its regulated subsidiary is **registered** rather than state-licensed and generally does
> not face the testing, pre-licensing education, and annual continuing education a **state-licensed** MLO
> faces. But he still has a unique identifier and is still within the framework — **which exists so that
> an individual's history follows them and an action in one state is visible everywhere.**

## Chapter summary

**Federal law applies on its own terms**, and a state licensing premise answers no federal question.

**TILA promotes informed use of credit through disclosure**, applies to consumer-purpose credit, and is
delivered through the **Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure** with timing rules that carry legal
consequences.

**The APR is a comparison tool, not the note rate, not every cost, and not a conclusion.**

**Rescission attaches to certain principal-dwelling transactions and not to purchase-money loans**, each
entitled consumer must receive the notice, the period runs from the later of defined events, and funds
are not disbursed until it expires.

**RESPA prohibits payments for referrals and unearned splits, and disclosure does not cure it.** "Thing
of value" is broad, an understanding need not be written, and the test is whether the payment is for
something actually done at a value reasonably related to it.

**Affiliated business arrangements are permitted on conditions** — disclosure with an estimate, no
required use, and no thing of value beyond a return on ownership.

**ECOA reaches any aspect of a credit transaction**, on bases including **marital status** and **receipt
of public assistance income**. **The monitoring information must be requested even though it may not be
used in the decision.** Adverse action requires the actual specific reasons or the right to obtain them.

**HMDA is a data statute supporting pattern detection**, which is the answer to "nobody has complained."
Coding a denial as a withdrawal makes the denial disappear, which is why examiners test it.

**A consumer report may be pulled only for a permissible purpose**, adverse action based on a report
carries additional required content, risk-based pricing attaches to approvals, and **credit repair
advice is outside the license.**

**Safeguard, transmit securely, and dispose so data cannot be reconstructed** — and escalate red flags
rather than resolving them.

**The SAFE Act makes an individual's history follow them.** State-licensed and federally registered
differ in requirements, the unique identifier belongs to the person, and **an endorsement lapse leaves a
current license unauthorized to originate.**

**Route every question to the framework it belongs to** — and settle a consumer-versus-business purpose
question **before the file is built around an answer.**
