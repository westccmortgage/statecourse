## 1. Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure orientation

### 1.1 What the forms do

For most closed-end consumer mortgage transactions, TILA and RESPA disclosure requirements are delivered through two integrated forms.

| Form | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Loan Estimate** | States **estimated** terms and costs early, so offers can be compared |
| **Closing Disclosure** | States the **actual** terms and costs before consummation |

**Both are designed for comparison**, which is why their layout is prescribed: a consumer holding two Loan Estimates should be able to compare them line by line.

### 1.2 Loan Estimate timing

The Loan Estimate must generally be delivered or placed in the mail **within a defined short period after application**, and **a defined period before consummation.**

**Two points a licensee must hold:**

**The clock starts at application, and an application exists when the six items are in hand** — name, income, Social Security number, property address, an estimate of value, and the loan amount sought. **It does not wait for a signature or a form.**

**Structuring to avoid the trigger is a compliance problem, not a technique.** Deliberately withholding a request for one item to delay the clock is the conduct the definition was written against.

### 1.3 What the Loan Estimate contains

In outline: the loan terms — amount, rate, monthly principal and interest, and whether any can increase; projected payments over the life of the loan; costs at closing; a detailed loan cost breakdown separating **origination charges**, services the consumer **cannot** shop for, and services the consumer **can** shop for; other costs including taxes, prepaids, and initial escrow; calculating cash to close; and comparison figures.

**The "services you can shop for" section matters to a consumer and is routinely ignored**, because nobody explains that it is an invitation rather than a list.

### 1.4 Closing Disclosure timing

The Closing Disclosure must generally be **received by the consumer a defined period before consummation.**

> **The waiting period is a timing rule with legal consequences, not a scheduling courtesy.**

**Certain changes restart it.** In outline, a change in the APR beyond defined tolerances, a change in the loan product, or the addition of a prepayment penalty generally requires a new form and a new waiting period.

**Other changes are corrected without restarting**, and the distinction is a compliance determination.

### 1.5 What this means operationally

**Do not promise a closing date that a late change makes impossible.**

The changes most likely to move a closing:

| Change | Effect |
|---|---|
| Consumer changes loan program or term | Product change; new form and new waiting period |
| A fee increases beyond its tolerance | Revised disclosure, and possibly a cure |
| Rate lock expires and is re-locked | May change disclosed figures |
| Consumer changes lenders mid-process | Restarts substantial parts of the process |
| Appraisal issue changes the loan amount | Terms change; disclosure follows |
| A prepayment penalty is added | New waiting period |

**A consumer proposing any of these should be told plainly what it does to the closing date, before he decides.**

### 1.6 Delivery and evidence

**Delivery is evidenced**: what was delivered, to whom, when, by what method, and with what proof.

**Electronic delivery carries its own consent requirements**, and a disclosure sent to an address the consumer never consented to receive documents at may not count as delivered.

> **A file that cannot show when a disclosure reached the consumer has a gap exactly where an examiner looks — and exactly where the consequence attaches.**

### 1.7 The California layer

The **mortgage loan disclosure statement** of Lesson 08 is a **separate California requirement with its own timing.**

> **Satisfying a federal disclosure obligation does not satisfy it**, and treating the Loan Estimate as covering it is one of the most common findings in a DRE examination of a mortgage brokerage.

### 1.8 Checkpoint

You should be able to state each form's purpose and timing, name the changes that restart the waiting period, and explain why the California statement is separate.

## 2. Broker compensation disclosure

### 2.1 Why compensation is regulated

Because a compensation structure that rewards an originator for placing a consumer in a worse loan produces exactly that outcome — and the consumer cannot detect it.

**The framework's core prohibition follows directly from that concern.**

### 2.2 The prohibition on term-based compensation

> **A loan originator's compensation may not be based on a term of a transaction**, or on a proxy for a term.

**What that reaches:** the interest rate, points, the presence of a prepayment penalty, and other terms — along with **proxies**, meaning factors that consistently vary with a term and that the originator can influence.

**What compensation may be based on:** the loan amount, a fixed amount per transaction, volume over a period, the quality of the files, long-term performance, and similar bases the framework permits.

### 2.3 The dual compensation restriction

> **Where a loan originator receives compensation directly from the consumer, the originator generally may not also receive compensation from any other person in connection with that transaction.**

**This is the "borrower-paid or lender-paid, not both" structure**, and it is the point at which a broker's business model becomes a compliance question rather than a preference.

**Two consequences:**

**The choice is made and disclosed before it matters**, not adjusted at closing.

**A payment from another party that arrives on a borrower-paid transaction is a problem**, regardless of what it is called or who initiated it.

### 2.4 Steering

The framework prohibits **steering a consumer to a transaction to increase the originator's compensation** where the consumer is not better off.

**The related concept a licensee should understand:** the framework contemplates that a consumer be presented with genuine options rather than only the transaction paying the originator most — and a firm's policy will define how that is documented.

### 2.5 What must be disclosed

**Federally:** compensation appears on the Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure in the prescribed places, and the amounts must reconcile to what is actually received.

**In California:** the **mortgage loan disclosure statement** requires the broker's compensation, **from every source.**

> **A compensation figure disclosed in one place and a different amount received is among the findings with the widest consequences** — reaching the California disclosure, the federal framework, and potentially the points-and-fees calculation of Section 2.7.

### 2.6 The reconciliation nobody performs

**Disclosed against received, on every file.**

It is a simple control and it is skipped constantly, because the disclosure is prepared by one function and the payment is received by another. **A firm that does not reconcile them will not know it has a problem until an examiner does the arithmetic.**

### 2.7 Compensation and the points-and-fees calculation

**Loan originator compensation can count toward the points-and-fees calculation** used in the qualified mortgage and high-cost frameworks.

**Two consequences:**

**A compensation arrangement can move a loan into a category** with requirements the firm may not be prepared to meet — or out of qualified mortgage status entirely.

**The calculation is performed by rule, not by intuition**, and it belongs to compliance.

**An originator who negotiates a compensation figure with a borrower without knowing whether it affects the calculation has made a decision he was not equipped to make.**

### 2.8 What a licensee must never do with compensation

- **Vary compensation based on the rate or another term.**
- **Accept compensation from another party on a borrower-paid transaction.**
- **Disclose one figure and receive another.**
- **Accept anything of value from a settlement service provider for referrals** — Section 3.
- **Accept compensation for licensed activity from anyone other than the responsible broker**, in the case of a salesperson.
- **Structure a payment to avoid a limit**, which is a compliance and counsel question and a pattern examiners recognize.

### 2.9 Checkpoint

You should be able to state the term-based prohibition and what a proxy is, state the dual compensation restriction, explain why compensation affects points and fees, and name the reconciliation firms skip.

## 3. Affiliated business and referral risk

### 3.1 The prohibition, restated

> **No person may give or accept any fee, kickback, or thing of value pursuant to an agreement or understanding that settlement business be referred**, and no person may accept a **split of a charge other than for services actually performed.**

> **Disclosure does not cure it.**

Unlike an ordinary conflict of interest, which disclosure manages, **a payment for referrals is prohibited whether or not it is disclosed** — which is the single most important sentence in this section.

### 3.2 "Thing of value" is broad

It reaches money, and also: gifts, meals beyond ordinary courtesy, event tickets, trips, free or discounted services, below-market rent or desk space, marketing or advertising paid on someone's behalf, leads provided at less than value, staffing provided to a referral source, and reciprocal arrangements.

**And an agreement or understanding need not be written.** A pattern of conduct can establish one.

### 3.3 The test

> **Is this payment for something actually done, at a value reasonably related to it — or is it for producing business?**

If the second, escalate. **Assessing whether a structure works belongs to compliance and counsel**, not to the people inside it.

### 3.4 The arrangements that look like ordinary business

| Arrangement | Analysis |
|---|---|
| Escrow or title paying per file | Payment for referrals |
| A lender funding a broker's marketing in exchange for volume | Thing of value for referrals |
| A "marketing services agreement" paying above fair market value | The excess is payment for referrals |
| Desk rental to a referral source below market | Thing of value |
| Co-marketing where one party pays more than its proportional benefit | The excess is a thing of value |
| Free processing or services provided to a referral source | Thing of value |
| Paying an unlicensed person per funded loan | **Both** unlicensed activity **and** a referral payment |
| Lead purchases priced by closed loans rather than by leads | Payment for produced business |

**Marketing services agreements deserve particular attention**, because they are the structure most often used to dress a referral payment as something else. **The agreement is not the problem; paying above fair market value for services actually performed is.**

### 3.5 Affiliated business arrangements

Where a person in a position to refer settlement business has an **ownership or beneficial interest** in a provider it refers to, that is an **affiliated business arrangement** — permitted subject to conditions:

1. **Disclosure of the relationship and an estimate of charges**, at or before referral.
2. **No required use** of the affiliate, except as permitted.
3. **No thing of value beyond a return on the ownership interest.**

> **The third condition is the one firms fail.** An ownership interest legitimizes the **return on ownership** and nothing else — a per-file payment on top of distributions converts a permissible structure into a prohibited one.

**And "we use our own escrow company on all our files" states a required use** where the conditions require the choice be the consumer's.

### 3.6 California adds a layer

**A licensee may not accept anything of value for directing settlement business** — which parallels the federal prohibition and carries its own licensing consequences.

**And a salesperson may accept compensation for licensed activity only from the responsible broker**, which independently defeats most direct-payment arrangements.

> **An arrangement can be prohibited federally, prohibited by California licensing law, and an unlicensed activity problem all at once.** Paying an unlicensed person per funded loan is the common example.

### 3.7 What to do when offered one

**Decline, and escalate.** Do not evaluate the structure yourself, do not accept pending review, and do not assume the offering party's compliance function has cleared it for your firm.

**The report belongs on the report-promptly list**, because the offer is information the firm needs even if this licensee declines it.

### 3.8 Checkpoint

You should be able to state the prohibition and that disclosure does not cure it, apply the services-actually-performed test, state the three affiliated business conditions and which fails most often, and describe the correct response to an offer.

## 4. Fee tolerance and changed circumstances

### 4.1 Why tolerances exist

Because an estimate that could be revised at will would not be an estimate. **The tolerance framework makes the Loan Estimate binding to defined degrees**, so a consumer can rely on it to compare offers.

### 4.2 The three categories

| Category | Rule, in outline |
|---|---|
| **Zero tolerance** | The charge generally **may not increase** at closing |
| **Ten percent tolerance** | Certain charges may increase, but **the category total may not exceed the estimate by more than ten percent** |
| **No tolerance limit** | Charges that may vary, provided the estimate was made in **good faith** based on the best information reasonably available |

**In outline, the categories run roughly as follows.** Zero tolerance generally covers **the originator's own charges**, fees paid to a provider the consumer **could not shop for**, and transfer taxes. The ten percent category generally covers recording fees and charges for **services the consumer could shop for where the consumer used a provider from the written list.** The unlimited category generally covers prepaid interest, property insurance premiums, escrow deposits, and charges for services the consumer shopped for and selected **off** the list.

**Two points a licensee must hold:**

**The consumer's shopping choice moves a charge between categories.** That is why the written list of providers matters and why "services you can shop for" is an invitation rather than a list.

**"Good faith" is a real standard even in the unlimited category.** An estimate deliberately low is not a good faith estimate, and the practice of quoting low to win a file and correcting at closing is exactly what the framework addresses.

### 4.3 Cures

Where a tolerance is exceeded without a valid changed circumstance, the creditor generally must **cure** — refunding the excess to the consumer within a defined period, with a corrected disclosure.

**A cure is a remedy, not a business decision.** A firm treating tolerance violations as a cost of doing business has a systemic problem an examination will find, because the cures themselves are the evidence.

### 4.4 Changed circumstances

A **revised Loan Estimate** may be provided, and the tolerance baseline reset, only where a valid reason exists. In outline:

| Valid reason | Example |
|---|---|
| **Changed circumstance affecting settlement charges** | An extraordinary event, or information the creditor relied on that turns out inaccurate |
| **Changed circumstance affecting eligibility** | Information about the consumer's creditworthiness or the transaction changes |
| **Consumer-requested changes** | The consumer asks for a different product, term, or feature |
| **Rate lock** | The rate is locked after the estimate was issued |
| **Expiration** | The consumer does not indicate intent to proceed within the defined period |
| **Delayed settlement on new construction** | Where the framework permits |

### 4.5 What is not a changed circumstance

- **Discovering that the original estimate was wrong** because nobody checked.
- **A vendor's price the firm could have known.**
- **A fee the firm forgot to include.**
- **Correcting a low quote given to win the file.**

> **A changed circumstance is a change, not a discovery of an error.** That distinction is the whole framework, and it is the one originators most often blur.

### 4.6 The documentation requirement

**A revised estimate must be justified by documentation of the reason and the date it was known**, and the revision must generally be provided within a defined period after the reason is known.

**Two disciplines:**

**Document the reason and the date contemporaneously.** A justification assembled later, when the tolerance question arises, is worth much less and looks worse.

**Do not issue a revised estimate without a valid reason.** A revision without one does not reset the baseline, and the file now contains a document suggesting the firm believed it did.

### 4.7 What an originator contributes

**Accurate initial estimates**, built from actual vendor pricing rather than habit.

**Prompt identification of a changed circumstance**, with the reason and date recorded when it occurs.

**Honest communication with the consumer** when costs change — including what changed, why, and what it means for the closing date.

**Never a quote given low to win a file**, which is the practice the entire framework was built to stop.

### 4.8 Checkpoint

You should be able to name the three tolerance categories, explain how a consumer's shopping choice moves a charge, distinguish a changed circumstance from a discovered error, and state what a cure is.

## 5. Prepayment, balloon, and adjustable-rate disclosure

### 5.1 The principle

> **Any feature that changes the payment or the balance in a way the initial figure does not show must be presented in plain terms, with the same prominence as the initial figure.**

The disclosure forms carry this in their prescribed content — the loan terms section states whether the rate, the payment, or the balance can increase, and whether there is a prepayment penalty or a balloon payment.

**But a form the consumer did not understand is not a disclosure in any useful sense**, which is why the licensee's plain-language explanation matters alongside it.

### 5.2 Prepayment penalties

**What must be conveyed:** that a penalty exists, **what triggers it** — sale, refinance, or paying more than a threshold — **how long it lasts**, and **what it costs.**

**Two points:**

**Whether a prepayment penalty is permitted at all depends on the loan's category and the framework**, and adding one can change a loan's status and restart a waiting period.

**A borrower who plans to sell or refinance within the penalty period is being placed in a product that penalizes his actual plan** — which is why the holding-period question from Lesson 04 belongs in the first conversation.

### 5.3 Balloon payments

**What must be conveyed:** the **amount due at maturity**, when it is due, and that the paths are to refinance, sell, or pay it.

> **All three paths depend on conditions at maturity** — the borrower's credit, the property's value, and whether credit is available. **"I'll just refinance" assumes a market**, and naming that assumption is the disclosure that matters.

### 5.4 Adjustable-rate disclosure

**What must be conveyed**, beyond the form:

| Element | Plain-language version |
|---|---|
| **Index and margin** | "Your rate is this published index plus a fixed 2.75%, and the margin never changes" |
| **Fully indexed rate** | "Using today's index, your rate would be 7.25% — your start rate is below that" |
| **Adjustment frequency** | "It can change every six months after the first five years" |
| **Periodic cap** | "It can move at most 1% at any single adjustment" |
| **Lifetime cap** | "It can never exceed 11%" |
| **Worst case** | "At 11%, your payment would be $4,672 instead of $2,998" |

> **The fully indexed rate is the fact most often omitted**, and it is the one that tells a borrower whether his start rate is a discount that will expire even if markets never move.

**A licensee who cannot state a borrower's own index, margin, and caps should not be presenting the product.**

### 5.5 Interest-only and negative amortization

**Interest-only:** principal is not retired during the period, and at the end the payment recalculates to amortize the **full original balance** over the **remaining, shorter** term. The increase is larger than borrowers expect.

**Negative amortization:** the payment does not cover accrued interest, so **the balance grows while payments are made** — and a recast can produce a large sudden increase.

> **Negative amortization must be explained in the plainest available terms**, because it contradicts every intuition a borrower has about what a payment does.

### 5.6 Temporary buydowns and escrow

**A temporary buydown** reduces the payment for an initial period. **The payment after it ends is disclosed with equal prominence** — a borrower who budgeted to the first-year payment and never registered the third-year payment has been sold rather than advised.

**Escrow (impound) accounts** mean the payment includes taxes and insurance and **can change when they do.** An escrow shortage after a tax or insurance increase produces a payment increase on a loan the borrower believed was fixed, and **in California a purchase reassessment makes this near-certain** where the estimate used the seller's tax figure.

### 5.7 The presentation that satisfies the obligation

For each feature: **state that it exists, state what it does, state the number it produces, and state when.**

> "This loan has a balloon. In seven years the remaining balance — about $412,000 — is due in a single payment. You would need to refinance, sell, or pay it, and all three depend on conditions then."

That is four sentences and it discharges more than a form does.

### 5.8 Checkpoint

You should be able to present each feature in plain terms with its number, state what the fully indexed rate tells a borrower, and explain negative amortization to someone who has never heard of it.

## 6. Consumer comparison and acknowledgment

### 6.1 The comparison standard

Products are presented **on a common basis**, and **a comparison that omits a material difference is misleading even when every number in it is accurate.**

The elements:

1. **The same loan amount**, or the difference stated.
2. **The same term and structure**, or the difference stated.
3. **The note rate, the payment, the points, and the lender charges**, each separately.
4. **The break-even** where points differ.
5. **The APR**, identified as a comparison tool with its assumptions.
6. **What is a lender charge and what is a third-party or prepaid item.**
7. **The worst-case path** for any adjustable or non-amortizing feature.
8. **PITI**, not principal and interest alone.

### 6.2 The comparisons that mislead

| Presentation | Why it misleads |
|---|---|
| Lower payment shown, points cost omitted | The saving was purchased |
| Note rate compared where fee structures differ | Incomplete |
| Initial payment on a hybrid against a fixed payment | Different products |
| P&I compared against a competitor's PITI | Different measures |
| APR presented as "the real rate" | It is not the note rate and assumes full term |
| Cash to close compared where prepaids differ | Prepaids are owed anyway |
| A worst case shown for one product and not the other | Selective |

**The last one is the most common and the least noticed.**

### 6.3 What the consumer actually needs

Before presenting anything, the originator should know:

- **How long does the borrower expect to hold the property and the loan?** This decides points, buydowns, and hybrid terms.
- **What payment is he comfortable with**, as distinct from what he can qualify for?
- **What is his tolerance for payment change?**
- **What cash does he have**, beyond the down payment?
- **What does he expect to happen** — income change, a sale, a move?

**The answers are recorded**, because they are what makes a later presentation defensible.

### 6.4 Acknowledgment is not understanding

**A signature proves delivery. It does not prove comprehension**, and treating an acknowledgment as evidence that a feature was understood is the error behind most disclosure complaints.

**What actually evidences that a feature was explained:**

- **A record of the explanation** — what was said, when, and by whom.
- **A written summary sent to the consumer**, so the explanation exists in his hands.
- **The consumer's questions and the answers**, recorded.
- **A worst-case illustration provided in writing**, not only spoken.

> **"He signed it" is an answer to a different question than "did he understand it."**

### 6.5 What must never appear in a comparison

- **A predicted rate** or a statement that rates will fall.
- **A predicted value** or a statement that the property will appreciate.
- **An assurance of future refinancing.**
- **A statement that a fee is "standard."**
- **A qualifying threshold stated as a rule.**
- **A statement that the consumer is approved** before he is.

> **"You can just refinance before the balloon" is the single most damaging sentence in mortgage sales**, because it converts a real risk into a plan and the consumer stops evaluating it.

### 6.6 What the file must show

- The consumer's stated objectives and constraints, with dates.
- The products presented, with the figures **as presented**.
- The assumptions underlying each figure.
- Each feature requiring plain-language explanation, and that it was given.
- The comparison as presented, on a common basis.
- Every disclosure delivered — **what, to whom, when, by what method, with evidence.**
- Any revised disclosure, its reason, and the date the reason was known.
- **Compensation disclosed, reconciled to compensation received.**
- The consumer's decision, and any instruction departing from the recommendation.

### 6.7 The test

> **Reconstructability: could a person who was not there determine what the consumer was shown, on what assumptions, what he was told about each feature, and what he decided?**

**If a comparison in the file cannot be reproduced from the figures it contains, it is not a record of advice. It is a marketing document that happens to be in a loan file.**

### 6.8 Checkpoint

You should be able to present two products on a common basis, name the comparisons that mislead, explain why acknowledgment is not understanding, and state what the file must show.

## Practice problems

### Problem 1

An originator has the consumer's name, income, Social Security number, property address, an estimated
value, and the requested amount — gathered across two calls. No form has been signed. His manager says
the Loan Estimate clock has not started, and suggests not asking for property addresses early "so we
have more room."

**Assess both.**

> **Solution.** **The clock has likely started.** An application exists when the **six items** are in
> hand — name, income, Social Security number, property address, an estimate of value, and the loan
> amount sought — and **it does not wait for a signature or a form.** The Loan Estimate must be delivered
> or mailed within a defined short period after application, and a defined period before consummation.
>
> **The manager's suggestion is a compliance problem, not a technique.** Deliberately withholding a
> request for one of the six items in order to delay the clock is **exactly the conduct the definition
> was written against**, and a pattern of it across files is visible to an examiner comparing intake
> records to disclosure dates.
>
> **And the California layer is separate.** The **mortgage loan disclosure statement** has its own
> timing, and **satisfying the federal obligation does not satisfy it** — treating the Loan Estimate as
> covering it is one of the most common findings in a DRE examination of a brokerage.

### Problem 2

A broker's compensation plan pays originators an extra 25 basis points on loans closed above a 6.5%
note rate, and pays a flat $1,500 on loans with prepayment penalties. On one transaction the borrower
is paying the broker directly, and the lender also pays the broker a rebate. The Loan Estimate showed
$4,200 of broker compensation; the broker received $5,650.

**Identify each violation.**

> **Solution.** **Four violations, and the plan itself is the source of two.**
>
> **Compensation tied to the note rate is term-based compensation**, prohibited outright. So is the
> $1,500 tied to the presence of a **prepayment penalty** — another term. The framework permits
> compensation based on the loan amount, a fixed amount per transaction, volume, file quality, long-term
> performance, and similar bases; **it does not permit varying with a term or with a proxy for one.**
>
> **Receiving compensation from the lender on a transaction where the consumer is paying the originator
> directly violates the dual compensation restriction.** This is the "borrower-paid or lender-paid, not
> both" structure, and the choice is **made and disclosed before it matters**, not adjusted at closing.
>
> **Disclosing $4,200 and receiving $5,650 is the finding with the widest consequences** — it reaches the
> California mortgage loan disclosure statement, the federal disclosures, and potentially the
> **points-and-fees calculation**, since originator compensation can count toward it and can move a loan
> out of qualified mortgage status.
>
> **The control that would have caught it is the one firms skip: reconcile disclosed against received, on
> every file.** The disclosure is prepared by one function and the payment received by another, and a
> firm that never compares them will not know until an examiner does the arithmetic.

### Problem 3

Four offers arrive in one week: a title company offering $400 per file; a lender offering to pay for the
brokerage's mailer in exchange for volume; a real estate brokerage proposing a marketing services
agreement at $6,000 a month for services worth about $1,500; and an escrow company offering desk space
at half market rent. The broker says all four are fine if disclosed on the Loan Estimate.

**Assess.**

> **Solution.** **All four are prohibited, and the disclosure plan does nothing.**
>
> **No person may give or accept any fee, kickback, or thing of value pursuant to an agreement or
> understanding that settlement business be referred**, and no person may accept a split other than for
> **services actually performed.**
>
> > **Disclosure does not cure it.** Unlike an ordinary conflict, which disclosure manages, a payment for
> > referrals is prohibited **whether or not it is disclosed.**
>
> **"Thing of value" is broad** — the mailer and the below-market desk space are things of value as
> squarely as the cash, and **an agreement or understanding need not be written**; a pattern of conduct
> establishes one.
>
> **The marketing services agreement is not inherently improper**, and that is what makes it the
> structure most often used to dress a referral payment as something else. **Paying $6,000 for $1,500 of
> services means the $4,500 excess is payment for referrals.**
>
> **The test:** is this payment for something actually done, at a value reasonably related to it — or for
> producing business? All four are the second.
>
> **The correct response: decline and escalate.** Do not evaluate the structure yourself, do not accept
> pending review, and do not assume the offering party's compliance function cleared it for your firm.
> The offer belongs on the report-promptly list because the firm needs to know it was made.

### Problem 4

A brokerage owns 35% of a title company. Its originators tell consumers "we use our title company,"
provide no estimate of charges, and the brokerage receives a $250 per-file payment in addition to
ownership distributions.

**Assess against the affiliated business conditions.**

> **Solution.** **This is an affiliated business arrangement, permitted subject to three conditions — and
> the firm has failed all three.**
>
> **Disclosure of the relationship with an estimate of charges, at or before referral**, was not given.
>
> **"We use our title company" states a required use**, where the conditions require the choice be the
> consumer's except as permitted. **A licensee must not tell a consumer he must use an affiliate.**
>
> **The $250 per-file payment is a thing of value beyond a return on the ownership interest** — and this
> is the condition firms fail most often, because they believe the ownership interest legitimizes the
> whole arrangement. **It legitimizes the return on ownership and nothing else.** The per-file payment is
> payment for referrals, and it converts a permissible structure into a prohibited one.
>
> **California adds a layer:** a licensee may not accept anything of value for directing settlement
> business, with its own licensing consequences — so this arrangement is exposed federally and under
> California licensing law simultaneously.

### Problem 5

At closing, the appraisal fee is $200 higher than estimated, the title fee from a provider on the firm's
written list is $340 higher, and prepaid interest is $600 higher because closing moved four days. The
originator issues a revised Loan Estimate the day before closing citing "changed circumstances," having
realized the week before that he had used last year's vendor pricing.

**Analyze each charge and the revision.**

> **Solution.** **Three charges in three different categories, and the revision is invalid.**
>
> **The appraisal fee is generally zero tolerance** — a service the consumer could not shop for. It
> **may not increase** absent a valid changed circumstance, and a $200 increase requires a **cure**:
> refunding the excess within a defined period with a corrected disclosure.
>
> **The title fee sits in the ten percent category** because the consumer used a provider from the
> **written list** — the category total may not exceed the estimate by more than ten percent, so whether
> this is a violation depends on the category total, not this line alone. **The consumer's shopping
> choice is what moved it there**, which is why the written list matters.
>
> **Prepaid interest has no tolerance limit** and moves with the closing date — **subject to a real good
> faith standard** in the original estimate.
>
> **The revision is invalid, and that is the central failure.** Using last year's vendor pricing is
> **discovering the estimate was wrong**, not a changed circumstance. **A changed circumstance is a
> change, not a discovery of an error** — and a forgotten fee, a vendor price the firm could have known,
> or correcting a low quote are all outside it.
>
> A revision without a valid reason **does not reset the baseline**, and the file now contains a document
> suggesting the firm believed it did. **A cure is a remedy, not a business decision.**

### Problem 6

A 5/6 hybrid: 6.0% start, 2.75% margin, index at 4.50%, caps 2/1/5, $500,000 balance, with an
interest-only period, a temporary buydown for two years, and a prepayment penalty for three years. The
borrower plans to sell in about two and a half years. The originator's presentation shows the year-one
payment and says the rate "is fixed for five years so there's nothing to worry about."

**State what must be disclosed in plain terms and identify the mismatch.**

> **Solution.** **Five features, and the product actively penalizes the borrower's stated plan.**
>
> **The fully indexed rate is the omitted fact that matters most:** 4.50 + 2.75 = **7.25%**, above the
> 6.0% start. **Even if markets never move, the rate rises at the first adjustment** — his start rate is
> a discount that expires.
>
> **Worst case:** first adjustment capped at 2% → **8.0%**; 1% per adjustment after; lifetime ceiling
> **11.0%**. Payments on $500,000: roughly **$2,998** at 6.0%, **$3,619** at 8.0%, **$4,672** at 11.0%.
>
> **The temporary buydown's post-buydown payment is disclosed with equal prominence.** A borrower who
> budgeted to the first-year payment and never registered the third-year payment has been sold rather
> than advised.
>
> **The interest-only period retires no principal**, and at its end the payment recalculates to amortize
> the **full original balance** over a **shorter remaining term.**
>
> **The prepayment penalty is the mismatch.** He plans to sell at about two and a half years, inside a
> three-year penalty period — **he is being placed in a product that penalizes his actual plan.** What
> must be conveyed: that it exists, **what triggers it**, how long it lasts, and what it costs. Adding
> one can also change the loan's status and restart a waiting period.
>
> **"Nothing to worry about" is the failure.** The obligation for each feature is to **state that it
> exists, what it does, the number it produces, and when.**

### Problem 7

An originator presents two options. Option A: 6.25%, no points, $1,200 lender fees, payment $2,398.
Option B: 5.875%, 2 points, $1,200 lender fees, payment $2,258 — presented as "$140 a month cheaper."
He shows PITI for A and P&I for B, shows a worst-case table for A's hybrid alternative but not for B's,
and compares cash to close without noting that B's prepaids are lower because of the closing date.

**Identify every defect and give the correct presentation.**

> **Solution.** **Four misleading comparisons, and every individual number may be accurate.**
>
> **The points cost is omitted.** 2 points on $400,000 = **$8,000.** The $140 saving was **purchased**,
> and the break-even is 8,000 ÷ 140 = **57 months**, about 4 years 9 months. A borrower holding longer
> comes out ahead; one selling sooner does not — which is why the **holding-period question** belongs
> first.
>
> **PITI against P&I compares different measures**, and the difference is large.
>
> **A worst case for one product and not the other is selective** — the most common and least noticed
> misleading comparison.
>
> **Cash to close where prepaids differ misleads**, because prepaids are amounts owed anyway rather than
> a cost of the loan.
>
> **The correct presentation:** same amount, term, and structure or the difference stated; note rate,
> payment, points, and lender charges **each separately**; the break-even; the APR **identified as a
> comparison tool with its assumptions**; what is a lender charge versus a third-party or prepaid item;
> the worst-case path for **any** adjustable or non-amortizing feature; and **PITI for both.**

### Problem 8

A consumer complains eighteen months after closing that nobody told him his payment could reach $4,672
or that his loan had a balloon. The file contains signed disclosures, an acknowledgment page, and a note
reading "reviewed all terms with borrower." The originator recalls telling him "you can just refinance
before the balloon."

**Assess the file and the statement.**

> **Solution.** **The file proves delivery and proves nothing about understanding.**
>
> **A signature proves delivery. It does not prove comprehension**, and treating an acknowledgment as
> evidence that a feature was explained is the error behind most disclosure complaints. **"He signed it"
> answers a different question than "did he understand it."** And "reviewed all terms with borrower" is a
> characterization, not a record — it contains no content anyone can test.
>
> **What actually evidences an explanation:** a record of what was said, when, and by whom; **a written
> summary sent to the consumer** so the explanation exists in his hands; his questions and the answers,
> recorded; and **a worst-case illustration provided in writing**, not only spoken.
>
> **The refinance statement is the most damaging sentence available in mortgage sales.** It converts a
> real risk into a plan, and **the consumer stops evaluating it.** All three balloon paths — refinance,
> sell, or pay — **depend on conditions at maturity**: his credit, the property's value, and whether
> credit is available. Naming that assumption **is** the disclosure that matters.
>
> **The four-sentence discharge that was owed:** *"This loan has a balloon. In seven years the remaining
> balance — about $412,000 — is due in a single payment. You would need to refinance, sell, or pay it.
> All three depend on conditions then."*
>
> **The governing test:** could a person who was not there determine what he was shown, on what
> assumptions, what he was told about each feature, and what he decided? **This file cannot.**

## Chapter summary

**The Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure exist for comparison**, and their timing rules carry legal
consequences. The clock starts at application — **the six items, not a signature** — and structuring to
avoid it is a compliance problem.

**Certain changes restart the waiting period**, so never promise a closing date a late change makes
impossible.

**Delivery is evidenced**, electronic delivery carries consent requirements, and **the California
mortgage loan disclosure statement is separate — federal compliance does not satisfy it.**

**Originator compensation may not be based on a term or a proxy for one**, and **borrower-paid and
lender-paid are not combined on the same transaction.**

**Reconcile compensation disclosed against compensation received on every file** — the control firms
skip and examiners perform.

**Compensation can count toward points and fees**, so an arrangement can change a loan's category.

**Payments for referrals are prohibited and disclosure does not cure them.** "Thing of value" is broad,
an understanding need not be written, and the test is services actually performed at a related value.
**Affiliated business arrangements fail most often on the no-thing-of-value-beyond-ownership condition.**

**Three tolerance categories**, and **the consumer's shopping choice moves charges between them.** Good
faith is a real standard, and a **cure is a remedy, not a business decision.**

**A changed circumstance is a change, not a discovery of an error** — documented contemporaneously with
its reason and date.

**Every feature that changes the payment or balance is presented in plain terms with its number**: what
triggers a prepayment penalty and what it costs; the balloon amount and that all three paths depend on
conditions then; the **fully indexed rate**, caps, and the worst-case payment; the recast on
interest-only and negative amortization; the post-buydown payment; and that escrow makes a "fixed"
payment change.

**Present on a common basis, and never selectively** — a worst case shown for one product and not the
other is the least noticed misleading comparison.

**Acknowledgment is not understanding**, and **"you can just refinance before the balloon" converts a
risk into a plan.**

**The file must let someone who was not there reconstruct what was shown, on what assumptions, what was
explained, and what was decided.**
