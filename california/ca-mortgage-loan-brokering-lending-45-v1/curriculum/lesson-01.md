## 1. DRE real estate broker mortgage authority

### 1.1 Concept and purpose

DRE real estate broker mortgage authority is studied as an operating concept, not as a vocabulary label. The learner identifies the legal or transactional purpose of the concept, the people whose rights may be affected, and the point in a California real estate matter when the concept becomes important. In California mortgage brokerage and regulatory structure, the correct professional response begins by separating verified facts from assumptions. A licensee should avoid promising a legal result, use the approved or authorized process, and preserve enough evidence for a broker, instructor, regulator, or later reviewer to reconstruct the decision.

### 1.2 Essential elements

The essential elements of DRE real estate broker mortgage authority are the parties, property or obligation involved, the source of authority, required disclosures or documents, relevant timing, and the consequence of noncompliance. Students should ask what must exist, who must act, when action must occur, and what record proves completion. A label alone is insufficient. The same phrase can produce different results when ownership, occupancy, agency, loan purpose, or written consent changes. The lesson therefore uses a structured issue-fact-rule-application-conclusion method and requires the learner to identify missing facts before choosing a response.

### 1.3 Roles and duties

Responsibility for DRE real estate broker mortgage authority may be divided among the principal, broker, salesperson, lender, escrow holder, title company, appraiser, property manager, attorney, public agency, or other specialist. A real estate professional must know the boundary of the license and the limits of delegated authority. The professional explains process accurately, makes required disclosures, follows supervision, and refers questions requiring legal, tax, engineering, appraisal, or other licensed judgment. Students practice naming the responsible party and documenting the referral instead of silently assuming another participant will perform the task.

### 1.4 Workflow

A defensible workflow for DRE real estate broker mortgage authority starts with intake and identity, continues through fact collection and document review, and ends with confirmation, communication, and retention. The learner creates a chronology: when the issue was discovered, what source was consulted, what was disclosed, who received it, what decision followed, and how that decision was confirmed. Checklists support consistency but do not replace judgment. If facts conflict, the workflow pauses at the conflict; the student is trained to obtain clarification and broker or professional guidance before an irreversible deadline passes.

### 1.5 Documents and evidence

Documents connected with DRE real estate broker mortgage authority must be read for content, not merely collected. Names, dates, property descriptions, signatures, amounts, attachments, delivery evidence, and version identifiers should reconcile across the file. Students distinguish a draft, an executed instrument, a disclosure acknowledgment, a receipt, and independent evidence. Electronic records require the same integrity as paper records. The course model preserves the submitted version, timestamp, actor, and any later amendment so that correction does not erase history. Missing or inconsistent evidence becomes a visible exception requiring resolution.

### 1.6 Quantitative and factual analysis

Where DRE real estate broker mortgage authority involves money, time, area, ratios, or comparisons, the student states the known values, selects the correct unit, shows the calculation, and tests whether the result is reasonable. Where the issue is qualitative, the student creates the same discipline by distinguishing direct evidence, reported information, professional opinion, and inference. A correct number built on an unsupported assumption is not a compliant conclusion. The applied exercise requires a written explanation that another person could reproduce and a note identifying any fact that still requires verification.

### 1.7 California law and professional boundary

California law controls the licensed activity associated with DRE real estate broker mortgage authority; however, course discussion is education rather than legal advice for a particular transaction. Statutes, Commissioner's Regulations, DRE forms and publications, contract language, and broker policy perform different functions. Students are trained to identify which source is controlling and to check the current version before action. When an answer depends on facts or authority outside the licensee's competence, the compliant response is disclosure, supervision, and referral - not improvisation or concealment.

### 1.8 Consumer protection and fair access

Consumer protection requires that DRE real estate broker mortgage authority be handled consistently, honestly, and without discrimination. The professional avoids steering, selective service, unsupported urgency, hidden conflicts, and explanations that obscure material costs or risks. Equal service does not mean ignoring disability-related accommodation or other legally relevant needs. Students compare treatment across similarly situated consumers, identify where discretion can introduce bias, and build objective criteria and written records. A consumer should be able to understand the material choice and the source of the professional's recommendation.

## Knowledge check and remediation

Complete the five-item lesson quiz in the secure LMS. An incorrect response opens targeted remediation: an alternate explanation, a parallel example, and a new item on the same concept. The 80% lesson mastery threshold is a StateCourse instructional policy proposed for DRE review; it is not represented as a DRE-mandated score.

## Assignment completion

Completion requires all instructional segments, the applied memorandum, the quiz submission, and any triggered remediation. Assignment completion does not waive the separate final examination, minimum elapsed-time, identity, proctor, or approval requirements.
