## 1. DRE real estate broker mortgage authority

### 1.1 What this course is about

Mortgage brokering is licensed activity, and in California it can be conducted under more than one licensing framework. This course addresses the activity as conducted by a **real estate broker licensed by the Department of Real Estate**, and by salespersons acting under such a broker.

That framing matters immediately, because the same functional activity — arranging a loan secured by real property — is conducted in California by firms licensed under different statutes, supervised by different regulators, subject to overlapping federal requirements.

**A licensee who does not know which framework they operate under cannot answer a compliance question correctly**, because the answer frequently depends on it.

### 1.2 The broker's mortgage authority

A California real estate broker license authorizes, among other activities, **soliciting borrowers or lenders for, negotiating, arranging, and performing services in connection with loans secured by real property.**

Two features of that authority are worth stating precisely at the start.

**It is activity-based.** The license authorizes the acts. It does not create a separate "mortgage license" — the same license that permits listing a house permits arranging a loan secured by one, subject to the additional requirements this lesson develops.

**It carries the same supervisory structure.** A salesperson performing mortgage activity does so **for and under the responsible broker**, exactly as in a sales transaction, and the brokerage is the party to the engagement.

### 1.3 What the authority does not include

| Not included | Where it belongs |
|---|---|
| Making loans as a lender under a lending statute | A lending license, where the activity requires one |
| Acting as an escrow holder outside an applicable exemption | Escrow licensure or an exemption |
| Appraising | Appraiser licensure or certification |
| Giving legal advice | Counsel |
| Giving tax advice | A tax professional |
| Servicing loans where servicing requires its own authority | The applicable framework |

**The recurring error is the last several.** A mortgage broker sits at the center of a transaction where legal, tax, appraisal, and title questions all arrive, and the temptation to answer them is constant. Section 3 develops the routing.

### 1.4 Broker and salesperson, restated for this context

The relationship from the Principles course applies without modification:

- A **broker** may conduct mortgage activity independently and may employ salespersons.
- A **salesperson** may conduct it **only for and under a responsible broker**, and may accept compensation for licensed activity **only from that broker.**
- **The brokerage is the party** to the engagement with the borrower.
- **A salesperson may not contract in her own name** for licensed mortgage activity.

### 1.5 Why the DRE framework exists for mortgage activity

The consumer-protection logic is the same as in a sales transaction and is worth being explicit about, because it explains the rules that follow:

- Consumers transact infrequently, in a market with imperfect information, on the largest obligation most of them will incur.
- **The broker is accountable for the salesperson**, because the consumer deals with an individual and the accountable party must be one with the resources and duty to supervise.
- **Trust fund rules are strict**, because the money is not the licensee's.
- **Advertising is regulated**, because a consumer cannot evaluate a claim about terms they have not received.
- **Records are retained**, because a complaint about a loan can arrive long after it funded.

### 1.6 Checkpoint

You should be able to state what a DRE broker license authorizes in mortgage activity and name four things it does not cover.

## 2. MLO endorsement orientation

### 2.1 What the endorsement is

A licensee who acts as a **mortgage loan originator** must hold an **MLO license endorsement** on their DRE license, and must be registered through the **Nationwide Multistate Licensing System and Registry**.

This is an **endorsement on the existing license**, not a separate license. The underlying broker or salesperson license remains the license; the endorsement records that the licensee is authorized to act as an MLO.

### 2.2 What triggers the requirement

In outline, a person acts as a mortgage loan originator when they, for compensation or gain or in the expectation of it:

- **Take a residential mortgage loan application**, or
- **Offer or negotiate terms** of a residential mortgage loan.

**Two elements do the work**, and both must be present: the activity, and the compensation or expectation of it.

**"Residential mortgage loan" is the scope limiter.** The endorsement requirement is tied to consumer residential lending, which is why business-purpose and certain commercial transactions are analyzed differently — a distinction Lesson 12 develops in detail and that licensees regularly get wrong in both directions.

### 2.3 What the endorsement requires

The endorsement framework, which implements the federal SAFE Act, generally involves:

| Requirement | In outline |
|---|---|
| **Pre-licensing education** | Approved coursework |
| **Examination** | A national test with content on federal and state law |
| **Background and credit review** | Fingerprints and a review of financial responsibility and character |
| **NMLS registration** | A unique identifier assigned to the individual |
| **Continuing education** | Annual, to maintain the endorsement |
| **Annual renewal** | Separate from the underlying license renewal |

**Two points licensees miss.** The **unique identifier follows the individual**, not the employer, and it must appear on communications and advertising as required. And **the endorsement renews on its own cycle** — a licensee whose broker license is current but whose endorsement lapsed is not authorized to originate.

### 2.4 The individual and the company

Both levels exist. **The individual** holds the endorsement and the unique identifier. **The company** — here, the brokerage — has its own registration and identifier.

Advertising and communications carry both identifiers as required, which is why a compliant mortgage advertisement carries more identification than a compliant sales advertisement.

### 2.5 What the endorsement does not do

- **It does not expand what the underlying license authorizes.** A salesperson with an endorsement is still a salesperson acting under a broker.
- **It does not substitute for the underlying license.** Both must be current.
- **It does not make the licensee a lender.**
- **It does not authorize legal or tax advice.**

### 2.6 Checkpoint

You should be able to state what triggers the MLO requirement, name what the endorsement involves, and explain the relationship between the endorsement and the underlying license.

## 3. DRE and other regulator boundaries

### 3.1 The regulators a mortgage transaction touches

A single residential mortgage transaction can involve the following, and a licensee should be able to say which answers which question.

| Body | What it addresses |
|---|---|
| **Department of Real Estate** | The real estate broker license, its mortgage activity, MLO endorsements on DRE licenses, trust funds, advertising, supervision, discipline |
| **Department of Financial Protection and Innovation** | Firms licensed under California's lending and financing statutes, and certain other financial services |
| **NMLS** | The registry through which MLO licensing and registration is administered across states |
| **Consumer Financial Protection Bureau** | Federal consumer financial law — disclosure, servicing, and related rules |
| **HUD** | Federal housing programs and fair housing enforcement |
| **Federal banking regulators** | Depository institutions and their subsidiaries |
| **California Civil Rights Department** | State housing discrimination enforcement |
| **Bureau of Real Estate Appraisers** | Appraiser licensing and certification |
| **Local jurisdictions** | Land use, permits, and local ordinances |

### 3.2 The California licensing frameworks, in outline

The point most often confused: **the same functional activity is conducted in California under more than one licensing framework.**

A firm arranging residential mortgage loans may operate under a **DRE real estate broker license**, or under a license issued by the **DFPI** pursuant to California's residential mortgage lending or finance lending statutes.

**The frameworks differ** in what they authorize, what they require, how compensation and fees are treated, and which regulator examines the firm.

> **For a licensee, the practical rule is narrow and important: know which license your firm holds, and do not import a rule you learned from a different framework.** A requirement that applies to a DFPI-licensed lender may not apply to a DRE broker, and vice versa — and a licensee who answers a compliance question from the wrong framework has answered confidently and wrongly.

### 3.3 Federal law applies across frameworks

The federal consumer mortgage statutes taken up in Lesson 07 — Truth in Lending, RESPA, ECOA, HMDA, the Fair Credit Reporting Act, and the SAFE Act framework — apply according to their own terms rather than by reference to the state license held.

**This is why the federal material is a separate lesson.** A licensee cannot reason from "we're a DRE broker" to a conclusion about a federal disclosure obligation.

### 3.4 The routing discipline

The single most useful skill in this section is knowing where a question goes.

| Question | Goes to |
|---|---|
| "Is this activity licensed?" | The licensing framework, and where genuinely unclear, counsel |
| "What must this disclosure say and when?" | The federal rule and the firm's compliance function |
| "Can we charge this fee?" | The applicable framework and compliance — **not** industry custom |
| "Is this property worth enough?" | The appraiser, through the proper channel |
| "Does the borrower have good title?" | Title, through escrow |
| "Can the borrower cancel?" | Counsel |
| "What will this do to the borrower's taxes?" | A tax professional |
| "Should the borrower take this program?" | A suitability discussion the licensee may hold — and never a legal or tax recommendation |

### 3.5 The standard a licensee actually applies

> **Would answering require a legal conclusion, a tax conclusion, a valuation, or an underwriting decision I am not authorized to make?** If yes, route it.

Experience does not extend the scope of a license. A licensee with twenty years of origination experience is no more entitled to tell a borrower whether they may rescind than one with two weeks.

### 3.6 Checkpoint

You should be able to name the regulators a mortgage transaction touches, explain why "we're a DRE broker" does not answer a federal question, and route a set of questions correctly.

## 4. Licensed and exempt activity

### 4.1 The analysis

Whether a person needs a license turns on **what they do** and **whether compensation is involved or expected** — not on the title they use.

**Activity that is licensed** in the mortgage context includes soliciting borrowers or lenders, negotiating or offering terms, taking a residential mortgage loan application, arranging loans secured by real property, and performing services in connection with such loans, where done for another for compensation or its expectation.

### 4.2 The title is not the test

A person who performs licensed activity does not escape the requirement by being called a **loan coordinator, transaction assistant, marketing associate, or consultant.**

This matters because mortgage operations are staffed with people whose roles sit close to the line:

| Role | Generally administrative | Crosses into licensed activity |
|---|---|---|
| **Processor** | Collecting documents, ordering third-party reports, tracking conditions | Discussing terms with the borrower, negotiating, advising on program choice |
| **Assistant** | Scheduling, data entry, sending forms | Taking the application, quoting rates, soliciting |
| **Marketing staff** | Producing materials under approval | Soliciting borrowers, discussing terms in response |
| **Referral source** | Mentioning the firm | Being **paid** for producing borrowers |

**The last row is the recurring compliance failure**, and it appears in Lesson 09 again from the federal side: paying an unlicensed person for producing loans is both an unlicensed activity problem and, where a settlement service referral is involved, a separate federal prohibition that **disclosure does not cure.**

### 4.3 Exemptions

Exemptions exist and are narrow. They can turn on the nature of the entity, the nature of the transaction, the number of transactions, or the purpose of the loan.

**Three disciplines about exemptions:**

1. **An exemption is a legal conclusion.** Whether one applies to a particular arrangement is a question for counsel and the firm's compliance function, not for an originator in a conversation with a prospect.
2. **A federal exemption is not a state exemption**, and vice versa. The analyses are separate.
3. **"We're exempt" is a reason to escalate, not a reason to proceed.**

### 4.4 The business-purpose distinction

The distinction generating the most misapplied exemptions in mortgage practice, and Lesson 12 develops it fully.

Consumer residential mortgage requirements — including the MLO endorsement requirement and much of the federal disclosure regime — attach to **consumer-purpose loans secured by a dwelling.** Loans made **primarily for a business purpose** are treated differently.

**Two errors, running in opposite directions:**

- **Treating a consumer loan as business-purpose** because the borrower signed a statement saying so, or because the property is a rental. **The determination is based on the actual purpose and the facts**, not on a certification the borrower was handed.
- **Treating every investment property loan as exempt from everything**, which sweeps in requirements that still apply.

> **A borrower's signature on a business-purpose certification does not make a loan business-purpose.** The certification records the borrower's statement; it does not establish the fact, and a file assembled around a certification that the surrounding facts contradict is a file with a problem in it.

### 4.5 Compensation and the expectation of it

Compensation is what converts assistance into licensed activity, and it is defined broadly — money, but also things of value, referral arrangements, and reciprocal benefits.

**An arrangement structured to avoid the word "compensation" while delivering value for producing loans is a compensation arrangement.** That analysis belongs to compliance and counsel, and an originator's role is to recognize the pattern and escalate rather than to assess whether the structure works.

### 4.6 Checkpoint

You should be able to determine whether an activity is licensed, explain why a title does not resolve it, and state what a business-purpose certification does and does not establish.

## 5. Broker supervision

### 5.1 The duty

The responsible broker must exercise **reasonable supervision** over the licensed activities conducted under the license. In mortgage activity, this reaches origination, disclosure delivery, advertising, fee handling, trust funds, and records.

**Supervision is an affirmative duty discharged by a system, not by a manual.**

### 5.2 What a real system contains

| Element | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Written policies** | State what the firm requires, in terms a licensee can follow |
| **File review** | Close enough in time to catch a problem before the loan closes |
| **Disclosure tracking** | Confirms what was delivered, to whom, when, and by what method |
| **Advertising review** | Before publication, including identifiers and rate claims |
| **Fee and compensation controls** | So charges match what was disclosed and what is permitted |
| **Trust fund controls** | Including reconciliation, where the firm handles trust funds |
| **Training** | Particularly for new licensees and on rule changes |
| **A usable escalation path** | So a problem raised is a problem addressed |
| **Vendor oversight** | For third parties acting on the firm's behalf |
| **Complaint handling** | A defined route, and a record |

**A policy nobody follows is not supervision**, and it is not a defense for anyone — not for the broker who wrote it and not for the licensee who departed from it.

### 5.3 Why mortgage supervision is harder than sales supervision

Three structural features:

**The timelines are compressed and the deadlines are legal.** Disclosure timing rules attach consequences to dates, and a missed delivery is not a service failure but a compliance failure.

**Much of the work is invisible to the client.** A borrower cannot evaluate whether an application was accurately completed or whether a fee was properly disclosed.

**The volume is transactional.** A defect in a process repeats on every file until someone notices — which is the property management lesson from the Principles course in a different setting.

### 5.4 Supervision from the receiving end

Licensees encounter supervision failures from below, and the responses matter:

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| A manager tells you to omit or alter something on an application | **Refuse. Escalate to the responsible broker.** The instruction does not protect you |
| Office practice deviates from the written policy | Raise it; a policy nobody follows protects no one |
| You are asked to originate a product you do not understand | Say so in writing and ask for training or reassignment |
| A file is submitted with information you know to be inaccurate | Escalate. Do not sign or transmit it |

> **Following an improper instruction and documenting that it came from someone else does not transfer responsibility for the act. It documents that you knew.**

### 5.5 Independent contractor status changes nothing

Many originators are compensated as independent contractors. That characterization addresses the tax and compensation relationship. **It does not reduce the broker's supervisory duty or the licensee's obligation to act under the broker.**

### 5.6 The records are the brokerage's

Loan files, disclosures, communications, and trust fund records belong to the **brokerage**, and the responsible broker answers for them whoever created them.

A licensee leaving a firm may not unilaterally take files. What may be taken is governed by firm policy and by client direction — a question that should be settled in writing in the policy long before anyone departs.

### 5.7 Checkpoint

You should be able to describe a real supervision system, explain why mortgage supervision is structurally harder, and state the correct response to an improper instruction.

## 6. Consumer complaint and enforcement context

### 6.1 How a matter reaches a regulator

Complaints arrive from borrowers, from other participants in a transaction, from other licensees, and from a regulator's own examination or data review.

**A licensee should understand that the routes are plural.** A file with a defect is not safe because the borrower is satisfied — an examination, a data submission, or an unrelated complaint can surface it.

### 6.2 What produces enforcement in mortgage practice

| Category | Typical conduct |
|---|---|
| **Misrepresentation to a borrower** | Terms, rates, costs, or what a disclosure means |
| **Misrepresentation to a lender** | Income, occupancy, assets, or the source of funds |
| **Unlicensed activity** | Originating without an endorsement, or paying an unlicensed person |
| **Trust fund violations** | Commingling, conversion, shortages, inadequate records |
| **Advance fee violations** | Collecting a fee in advance without meeting the applicable requirements |
| **Advertising violations** | Missing identifiers, unsupported rate or terms claims, misleading comparisons |
| **Disclosure failures** | Late, missing, or inaccurate required disclosures |
| **Compensation violations** | Prohibited arrangements, undisclosed compensation, referral payments |
| **Fair lending violations** | Discrimination in any aspect of a credit transaction |
| **Failure to supervise** | A broker who did not review, enforce, or notice |
| **Falsification** | Backdating, altering, or creating records |

**Most of these do not require anyone to have set out to defraud.** Late disclosures, inaccurate applications assembled carelessly, and inadequate records are the ordinary cases.

### 6.3 The one that ends careers

Everything on that list is survivable — errors, complaints, findings, even discipline — **provided the licensee's conduct in response was honest.**

> **Falsifying the record is not.** Creating a document after the fact and dating it to match, altering an executed document, deleting records that reflect badly, or removing surrounding documents so an inconsistency does not show converts a compliance problem into a question about integrity.
>
> **A documented gap is survivable. A manufactured document ends a career.**

Corrections are made **visibly**, with the original preserved and the change attributable. A record that can be silently altered is not a record.

### 6.4 When a regulator makes contact

**Notify the broker immediately. Preserve records. Do not respond independently or informally.**

Cooperation is expected. **Improvised** cooperation without review is where licensees make statements they cannot support — and in a mortgage file, a statement about what was disclosed and when can be checked against the file within minutes.

### 6.5 Responding to a borrower complaint

The first response frequently determines whether the matter resolves or escalates:

1. **Acknowledge promptly.** Silence reads as evasion.
2. **Notify the broker immediately.**
3. **Preserve everything** — the file, the disclosures as delivered, communications, drafts, texts, and the advertising as it ran.
4. **Respond factually**, without admitting or denying liability before the file has been reviewed.
5. **Do not offer a fee reduction or credit** on your own initiative.
6. **Report to the carrier** where the matter could become a claim.

**Deleting a record that reflects poorly is far worse than the record.** It is generally discoverable anyway, and it changes the subject from professional judgment to integrity.

### 6.6 What to report to the broker, promptly

Any error affecting a borrower's decision or a loan's terms; any borrower dissatisfaction that could become a complaint; any allegation of misrepresentation or discrimination; any contact from counsel or a regulator; any suspected fraud; any wire fraud incident **or near-miss**; any request to alter, backdate, or omit anything; and any instruction you declined to follow.

**The bias should be toward reporting.** A report that turns out unnecessary costs a conversation. An unreported matter that becomes a claim costs the coverage and the credibility.

### 6.7 The record is the defense

In a mortgage file, nearly every question a regulator or a borrower asks is answerable from documents: what was disclosed, to whom, when, by what method; what the borrower was told; what the file contained when the decision was made; what was verified and how.

> **The test is reconstructability: could a person who was not there determine what happened, when, and on what basis?**

That test governs the rest of this course.

### 6.8 Checkpoint

You should be able to name the enforcement categories, state the correct response to regulator contact and to a borrower complaint, and explain the line between a documented gap and a falsified record.

## Practice problems

### Problem 1

A salesperson at a DRE-licensed brokerage takes residential mortgage applications and quotes terms. Her
real estate salesperson license is current. Her MLO endorsement lapsed eleven weeks ago and she did not
notice because her license renewal is not due until next year. She also signs the engagement letters
with borrowers in her own name.

**Identify both problems.**

> **Solution.** **She is originating without authorization.** The MLO endorsement is an endorsement on
> the existing license, **not a separate license — but it renews on its own cycle**, separate from the
> underlying license renewal. A licensee whose broker or salesperson license is current but whose
> endorsement has lapsed is not authorized to act as an MLO, and taking applications and offering or
> negotiating terms for compensation is exactly what the endorsement covers.
>
> **The engagement letters are a second, independent problem.** **The brokerage is the party** to the
> engagement with the borrower. A salesperson may not contract in her own name for licensed activity,
> and she acts **for and under the responsible broker** — including for compensation, which she may
> accept only from that broker.
>
> Both should be escalated to the broker immediately, and the files opened during the lapse examined by
> compliance rather than by her.

### Problem 2

A borrower asks a mortgage originator four questions in one conversation: whether the appraisal came in
high enough, whether he can rescind if he changes his mind, what the loan will do to his taxes, and
whether the fee the firm charges is "standard."

**Route each and state the standard.**

> **Solution.** **The appraisal is a valuation question** for the appraiser, reached only through the
> proper channel — and the originator may supply **information**, never a target.
>
> **Rescission is a legal conclusion.** It goes to counsel, and the originator may explain that a right
> exists in defined circumstances without telling this borrower whether it applies to him.
>
> **The tax question goes to a tax professional**, and it should be flagged **early**, because most tax
> considerations in a financing decision close when the transaction does.
>
> **"Is the fee standard?" is the one that sounds harmless and is not.** Fees are governed by the
> applicable framework, by what was disclosed, and by the firm's compliance function — **not by industry
> custom**, and describing a charge as standard is both unsupported and the kind of statement that later
> gets quoted.
>
> **The standard:** would answering require a legal conclusion, a tax conclusion, a valuation, or an
> underwriting decision I am not authorized to make? If yes, route it. **Experience does not extend the
> scope of a license.**

### Problem 3

A brokerage's compliance manager tells a new originator that a particular fee cap "doesn't apply to us"
because she read it in a bulletin about California mortgage lenders. The brokerage is licensed as a DRE
real estate broker. The originator also assumes a federal disclosure obligation does not apply for the
same reason.

**Assess both conclusions.**

> **Solution.** **The first is the classic framework error.** The same functional activity — arranging
> residential mortgage loans — is conducted in California under more than one licensing framework: a DRE
> real estate broker license, or a license issued by the **DFPI** under the state's residential mortgage
> lending or finance lending statutes. **The frameworks differ** in what they authorize, what they
> require, how fees and compensation are treated, and which regulator examines the firm.
>
> **Importing a rule from a different framework produces confidently wrong answers in both directions** —
> a requirement that binds a DFPI-licensed lender may not bind a DRE broker, and one that binds the
> broker may not appear in lender guidance at all. Know which license your firm holds.
>
> **The second reasoning is worse, because it is a category error.** **Federal consumer mortgage law
> applies according to its own terms**, not by reference to the state license held. A conclusion about a
> federal disclosure obligation cannot be reasoned from "we're a DRE broker" at all.
>
> Both questions go to the firm's compliance function and, where genuinely unclear, to counsel.

### Problem 4

A brokerage employs a "loan coordinator" who collects documents and orders reports, and a "marketing
associate" who calls prospects from a purchased list, quotes current rates, and is paid $400 for each
prospect who ends up funding. Neither holds a license. The broker says the titles make clear they are
support staff.

**Analyze.**

> **Solution.** **The title is not the test. The activity is.**
>
> **The coordinator's described work is generally administrative** — collecting documents, ordering
> third-party reports, tracking conditions. That does not require a license, and it stays that way only
> as long as the work stays there.
>
> **The marketing associate is performing licensed activity.** Soliciting borrowers and quoting terms is
> exactly what the license covers, and **the compensation element is present and explicit** — $400 per
> funded loan is compensation whatever it is called. **Compensation is defined broadly**: money, things
> of value, referral arrangements, and reciprocal benefits.
>
> **This is two problems at once.** It is unlicensed activity, and paying an unlicensed person for
> producing loans is separately a compensation problem — and where a settlement service referral is
> involved, a federal prohibition that **disclosure does not cure.**
>
> **An arrangement structured to avoid the word "compensation" while delivering value for producing
> loans is a compensation arrangement**, and assessing whether a structure works belongs to compliance
> and counsel, not to the people inside it.

### Problem 5

An originator is preparing a file on a four-unit property. The borrower will live in one unit. The
originator's manager instructs him to process it as business-purpose, "since it's an income property,"
and to have the borrower sign the business-purpose certification.

**Assess.**

> **Solution.** **The instruction inverts the analysis, and the certification does not fix it.**
>
> Consumer residential mortgage requirements — including the MLO endorsement requirement and much of the
> federal disclosure regime — attach to **consumer-purpose loans secured by a dwelling.** A borrower
> occupying one unit of a fourplex is a fact pointing hard toward consumer purpose, and **"it's an income
> property" is not the test.**
>
> **The determination rests on the actual purpose and the surrounding facts**, not on a certification the
> borrower was handed. **A borrower's signature on a business-purpose certification records his
> statement; it does not establish the fact** — and a file assembled around a certification the
> surrounding facts contradict is a file with a problem in it, visible to any examiner who reads the
> occupancy line.
>
> **Both errors run in this area**, and this is the more common one: treating a consumer loan as
> business-purpose. The mirror error is treating every investment property loan as exempt from
> everything, which sweeps in requirements that still apply.
>
> **What the originator does:** decline the instruction, escalate to the responsible broker, and route
> the purpose determination to compliance. Following it while noting that the manager directed it
> **documents that he knew.**

### Problem 6

A branch manager tells an originator to "clean up" a borrower's application before submission — moving
a recent job change to an earlier date so the employment history reads better, and omitting a small
consumer debt that "won't matter to the ratios anyway."

**What does the originator do, and what does not protect him?**

> **Solution.** **Refuse, do not transmit the file, and escalate to the responsible broker immediately.**
>
> This is misrepresentation to a lender — one of the enforcement categories — and the fact that the
> omitted debt is small does not change what the file now asserts. An application is a set of
> representations the lender relies on in pricing risk, and altering an employment date is not tidying;
> it is changing a fact.
>
> **What does not protect him:** doing it and documenting that the manager directed it. **Following an
> improper instruction and documenting that it came from someone else does not transfer responsibility
> for the act. It documents that you knew.**
>
> Nor does the reasoning that the ratios would work anyway. If that were true, the alteration was
> unnecessary — and it was still made.
>
> **The escalation goes above the person who gave the instruction**, and the matter belongs on the
> report-promptly list along with any request to alter, backdate, or omit anything.

### Problem 7

A firm's compliance manual is thorough and current. In practice, disclosures are tracked on a
spreadsheet nobody updates, advertising goes out without review because the approval turnaround is four
days, and file review happens quarterly — after most loans in the quarter have closed. No borrower has
complained.

**Assess the supervision.**

> **Solution.** **A policy nobody follows is not supervision**, and it is not a defense for anyone — not
> for the broker who wrote it and not for the licensees who departed from it.
>
> **Each failure defeats the control's purpose.** File review must be **close enough in time to catch a
> problem before the loan closes**; quarterly review after closing documents history rather than
> preventing harm. **Advertising review must precede publication**, and a four-day turnaround is the
> cause of the non-compliance, not an excuse for it — most routing around an approval process is caused
> by the process being slow. **Disclosure tracking must show what was delivered, to whom, when, and by
> what method**, because disclosure timing rules attach consequences to dates.
>
> **Mortgage supervision is structurally harder than sales supervision** for three reasons visible here:
> the timelines are compressed and the deadlines are **legal** rather than service commitments; much of
> the work is **invisible to the borrower**, who cannot evaluate whether a disclosure was timely; and the
> volume is transactional, so **a defect in a process repeats on every file until someone notices.**
>
> **The absence of complaints is not evidence of compliance.** Complaints are only one of several routes
> — an examination, a data submission, or an unrelated complaint can surface the same defect.

### Problem 8

A borrower emails alleging he was misled about his rate lock. The originator replies within the hour
with a detailed defense, apologizes for "the confusion I caused," offers to credit $750 at closing, and
deletes a text thread with the borrower that reads badly. He plans to tell his broker only if it
escalates. Two days later the DRE calls him directly and he answers the questions on the spot.

**Identify every error and state the correct sequence.**

> **Solution.** **Six errors, and two of them are categorically worse than the rest.**
>
> **The immediate defense** commits a position before anyone has reviewed the file — and in a mortgage
> file, what was disclosed and when can be checked within minutes. **The apology accepting fault** may
> be treated as an admission and can compromise coverage. **The $750 credit** commits the brokerage,
> may read as an admission, and rarely ends the matter. **Waiting to tell the broker** compounds the
> original problem with a supervision failure.
>
> **Deleting the thread is falsification territory** — the one category that is not survivable. It is
> generally discoverable anyway, and it changes the subject from professional judgment to integrity.
> **A documented gap is survivable; a manufactured or destroyed record ends a career.**
>
> **Answering the regulator on the spot is the second serious error.** On regulator contact: **notify the
> broker immediately, preserve records, and do not respond independently or informally.** Cooperation is
> expected; **improvised** cooperation without review is where licensees make statements they cannot
> support.
>
> **The correct sequence:** acknowledge promptly, because silence reads as evasion; notify the broker
> immediately; **preserve everything** — the file, the disclosures as delivered, communications, drafts,
> texts, and the advertising as it ran; respond factually without admitting or denying liability before
> the file is reviewed; offer nothing on your own initiative; and report to the carrier where the matter
> could become a claim.

## Chapter summary

**A California real estate broker license authorizes mortgage activity** — soliciting, negotiating,
arranging, and performing services in connection with loans secured by real property — under the same
supervisory structure as sales activity, with the brokerage as the party.

**It does not authorize lending, escrow, appraisal, legal advice, or tax advice.**

**An MLO endorsement is required to act as a mortgage loan originator** — taking a residential mortgage
loan application, or offering or negotiating terms, for compensation or its expectation. It is an
endorsement rather than a separate license, it carries a unique identifier that follows the individual,
and **it renews on its own cycle.**

**The same activity is conducted in California under more than one licensing framework.** Know which one
your firm holds, and never import a rule from another. **Federal law applies on its own terms**, not by
reference to the state license.

**Route by the standard:** would answering require a legal conclusion, a tax conclusion, a valuation, or
an underwriting decision you are not authorized to make?

**Licensing turns on the activity and on compensation, not on a title.** Paying an unlicensed person for
producing loans is both an unlicensed activity problem and a compensation problem.

**Exemptions are narrow legal conclusions**, separate under state and federal law. "We're exempt" is a
reason to escalate.

**A business-purpose certification records a statement; it does not establish a fact.** The actual
purpose governs, and the error runs in both directions.

**Supervision is a system, not a manual**, and mortgage supervision is structurally harder because the
deadlines are legal, the work is invisible to the borrower, and process defects repeat on every file.
Independent contractor status changes none of it.

**Following an improper instruction and documenting its source does not transfer responsibility. It
documents that you knew.**

**Enforcement reaches mortgage practice through many routes**, and most categories require no intent to
defraud. On regulator contact: notify the broker, preserve, and do not respond informally. On a borrower
complaint: acknowledge, notify, preserve, respond factually, commit nothing, notify the carrier.

**Falsifying or destroying a record is the one thing that is not survivable**, and the governing test of
this course is **reconstructability**.
