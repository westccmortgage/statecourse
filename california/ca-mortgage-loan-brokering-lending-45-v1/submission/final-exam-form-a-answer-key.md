## Items, with the material reference for each answer

### 1. What triggers the mortgage loan originator endorsement requirement for an individual?

A. Receiving compensation from a lender rather than from a borrower.
B. Handling more than a threshold number of loans in a calendar year.
C. Holding a real estate broker license and arranging any loan.
**D. Taking a residential mortgage loan application or offering or negotiating terms of a residential mortgage loan for compensation or gain.**

> **Correct answer: D.** The trigger is the activity performed for compensation or the expectation of it, not the license held, the source of payment, or a volume threshold.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (California mortgage brokerage and regulatory structure), section 2.2

### 2. What does the endorsement NOT do?

**A. It does not enlarge the authority the underlying license confers, and it does not substitute for a license under a different framework.**
B. It does not require a unique identifier.
C. It does not require annual renewal.
D. It does not permit an individual to take applications.

> **Correct answer: A.** The endorsement authorizes the originator activity within the license already held. Application-taking is exactly what it permits, and renewal and the identifier are part of it.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (California mortgage brokerage and regulatory structure), section 2.5

### 3. Whether an activity requires licensure is determined by what?

A. How the person describes his role to the borrower.
**B. What was actually done and whether it was for compensation — a job title is not the test.**
C. Whether the loan ultimately funded.
D. Whether the person's name appears on any transaction document.

> **Correct answer: B.** Calling a role 'assistant', 'processor', or 'coordinator' does not determine whether the activity performed was licensed activity.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (California mortgage brokerage and regulatory structure), section 4.2

### 4. Why does the business-purpose distinction matter to a mortgage originator?

**A. Much of the consumer protection framework applies to loans for personal, family, or household purposes, so the characterization changes which rules apply.**
B. Business-purpose loans require a different real estate license.
C. Business-purpose loans may not be secured by residential property.
D. Business-purpose loans are exempt from all California regulation.

> **Correct answer: A.** The distinction changes the applicable framework rather than the license required, and it is not an exemption from regulation generally.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (California mortgage brokerage and regulatory structure), section 4.4

### 5. Which conduct is described as the one that ends careers?

A. Missing a rate lock expiration.
B. Failing to deliver a disclosure within the required period.
C. Quoting a payment that omits taxes and insurance.
**D. Falsifying a record — altering or creating a document so it says something it did not.**

> **Correct answer: D.** The others are compliance failures a brokerage can survive with findings and corrections. Falsification converts a defensible examination into a question about integrity.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (California mortgage brokerage and regulatory structure), section 6.3

### 6. A borrower asks whether a proposed arrangement is permitted under a framework the originator does not work in. What is the correct response?

A. Tell the borrower the arrangement is permitted unless he is told otherwise.
B. Decline to discuss anything outside the originator's own license.
**C. Route it — name the authority that would answer it and take it to the broker rather than reasoning by analogy to a familiar framework.**
D. Apply the rule from the framework the originator knows best.

> **Correct answer: C.** Reasoning by analogy across frameworks is how a brokerage answers at least one question wrong. Routing is the discipline; refusing to engage at all is not required.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (California mortgage brokerage and regulatory structure), section 3.4

### 7. A borrower owes $412,000 at 6.25% annual simple interest. What is one month's interest?

A. $2,575.00
B. $1,716.67
**C. $2,145.83**
D. $25,750.00

> **Correct answer: C.** $412,000 × 0.0625 = $25,750 per year; ÷ 12 = $2,145.83. The distractors use a wrong divisor, the annual figure, and a 5% rate.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Money, interest, notes, and loan mathematics), section 1.2

### 8. A payoff of $412,000 at 6.25% is computed on a 365-day basis. What is the per diem interest?

**A. $70.55**
B. $2,145.83
C. $71.53
D. $25,750.00

> **Correct answer: A.** $412,000 × 0.0625 ÷ 365 = $70.55. A 360-day basis produces the second figure, which is why a payoff demand states its basis and its good-through date.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Money, interest, notes, and loan mathematics), section 1.4

### 9. How does an amortized payment split early in the loan term?

A. Evenly between interest and principal for the first half of the term.
B. Entirely interest until the midpoint of the term.
C. Mostly principal, since the balance is highest at the start.
**D. Mostly interest, with a small portion reducing principal, and the proportion shifts toward principal over time.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Interest is computed on the outstanding balance, which is highest at the start. This is why a borrower who sells early has retired very little principal.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Money, interest, notes, and loan mathematics), section 2.2

### 10. What is negative amortization?

A. The lender reduces the balance as part of a modification.
**B. The loan balance increases because the payment does not cover the interest accruing.**
C. The borrower pays extra principal, reducing the balance faster than scheduled.
D. The interest rate decreases while the payment stays the same.

> **Correct answer: B.** Unpaid interest is added to the balance. It is a feature requiring a plain-language explanation with its numbers, not a benefit.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Money, interest, notes, and loan mathematics), section 2.4

### 11. What distinguishes an acceleration clause from an alienation clause?

**A. Acceleration makes the balance due on a stated event such as default; alienation makes it due on transfer of the property.**
B. Acceleration applies to adjustable loans and alienation to fixed loans.
C. Acceleration is exercised by the trustee and alienation by the beneficiary.
D. They are two names for the same provision.

> **Correct answer: A.** Different triggers. Confusing them is why borrowers are surprised that selling or transferring can call the loan due.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Money, interest, notes, and loan mathematics), section 3.4

### 12. Why can two loans with the same note rate carry different annual percentage rates?

A. The APR includes property taxes and insurance and the note rate does not.
B. The APR is set by the lender's margin and the note rate by the index.
C. The APR applies to adjustable loans only.
**D. The APR reflects the cost of credit including certain fees, so differing finance charges produce differing APRs at the same note rate.**

> **Correct answer: D.** APR is a cost measure and the note rate is the accrual rate. Taxes and insurance are not finance charges.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Money, interest, notes, and loan mathematics), section 4.4

### 13. Why does a deed of trust involve a third party that a mortgage does not?

A. The trustee services the loan and collects the payments.
B. The trustee holds the borrower's title until the loan is repaid, which a mortgagee cannot.
**C. The trustee holds the power of sale, which enables the nonjudicial process California practice relies on.**
D. The trustee insures the lender against loss.

> **Correct answer: C.** The trustee's power of sale is the operative difference. Servicing and insurance are separate functions performed by other parties.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Security instruments and lien priority), section 2.2

### 14. What is the general rule of lien priority?

A. The largest lien takes priority regardless of recording order.
**B. First in time of recording is first in right, subject to defined exceptions.**
C. Purchase money liens always rank last.
D. Liens rank by the date the underlying obligation was incurred.

> **Correct answer: B.** Recording order governs, subject to exceptions such as property tax liens and the relation-back feature of mechanic's liens.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Security instruments and lien priority), section 4.3

### 15. Why is a mechanic's lien a particular problem for a lender?

**A. Its priority may relate back to a point earlier than its recording, so a lien recorded later can outrank an intervening deed of trust.**
B. It survives a foreclosure by a senior lienholder.
C. It attaches to the contractor's property rather than the owner's.
D. It cannot be released without a court order.

> **Correct answer: A.** The relation-back feature is the danger, which is why title work resolves whether work has commenced before a loan records.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Security instruments and lien priority), section 4.5

### 16. What does a subordination agreement accomplish?

A. It releases the property from the subordinated lien.
B. It transfers the subordinated lien to a new beneficiary.
C. It reduces the balance of the subordinated lien.
**D. It changes the priority the recording order would otherwise produce, moving a senior lien behind a junior one.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Subordination reorders priority without reducing, releasing, or transferring the obligation.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Security instruments and lien priority), section 5.1

### 17. What distinguishes taking title 'subject to' an existing loan from assuming it?

**A. An assumption makes the new owner personally liable on the obligation; taking subject to does not, though the lien remains against the property.**
B. An assumption removes the lien from the property and taking subject to leaves it.
C. Taking subject to requires lender approval and an assumption does not.
D. They are alternative names for the same arrangement.

> **Correct answer: A.** The difference is personal liability. In both cases the lien remains against the property and an alienation clause may be triggered.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Security instruments and lien priority), section 5.6

### 18. In the nonjudicial sequence, what distinguishes reinstatement from redemption?

A. Reinstatement is available only to junior lienholders.
**B. Reinstatement cures the arrearage and allowable costs within the period allowed; redemption requires the full accelerated balance.**
C. They are alternative names for the same right.
D. Redemption is available before the notice of default and reinstatement after it.

> **Correct answer: B.** The amounts differ, and the distinction is the most consequential thing a borrower in default does not know.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 03 (Security instruments and lien priority), section 6.3

### 19. What is the secondary market for?

**A. Buying closed loans from originators, which returns capital so they can lend again and connects mortgage rates to investor demand.**
B. Servicing loans after the originating lender sells them.
C. Insuring lenders against borrower default.
D. Making loans directly to borrowers who cannot qualify in the primary market.

> **Correct answer: A.** It supplies liquidity to originators. Servicing and insurance are distinct functions, and the secondary market does not originate.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Mortgage markets, sources, and loan products), section 1.2

### 20. What distinguishes a mortgage banker from a mortgage broker?

**A. A banker funds loans with its own or warehouse funds and is the lender of record; a broker arranges loans funded by others.**
B. A banker charges points and a broker charges only a commission.
C. A banker services loans and a broker does not.
D. A banker may only make government-backed loans and a broker only conventional loans.

> **Correct answer: A.** The distinction is who funds and who is the lender of record, which changes capacity, disclosure, and how compensation is structured.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Mortgage markets, sources, and loan products), section 2.2

### 21. What is the common structure of government-backed loan programs?

A. The agency lends directly to the borrower at a rate it sets.
**B. The agency insures or guarantees the loan against loss and an approved lender makes it.**
C. The agency appraises the property and issues the title policy.
D. The agency purchases the loan from the lender at closing.

> **Correct answer: B.** Insuring or guaranteeing is not lending, and the rate is set in the market between lender and borrower.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Mortgage markets, sources, and loan products), section 3.2

### 22. Which set of terms describes how an adjustable rate actually moves?

**A. Index, margin, adjustment period, and the caps on each adjustment and over the life of the loan.**
B. Loan-to-value, debt-to-income, and reserves.
C. Note rate, annual percentage rate, and discount points.
D. Term, amortization schedule, and payment factor.

> **Correct answer: A.** The index plus the margin sets the rate at each adjustment, and the caps bound how far it can move — which is what produces the worst-case payment.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Mortgage markets, sources, and loan products), section 4.2

### 23. What may a licensee never do with rates?

A. Tell a borrower the rate is not locked until the lock is confirmed in writing.
B. Direct a borrower to the lender for a current quote.
C. Explain what an index and a margin are.
**D. Predict where rates are going, or tell a borrower he can simply refinance later if the payment rises.**

> **Correct answer: D.** A prediction stated as a plan is the single most damaging sentence available in a financing conversation. The other three are accurate and within scope.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Mortgage markets, sources, and loan products), section 4.4

### 24. What does presenting products on a common basis require?

**A. The same loan amount, closing date, point assumption, and time horizon for every option, with the worst-case payment shown wherever the payment can change.**
B. Presenting the option with the lowest initial payment first.
C. Presenting each product with the assumptions the lender supplied for it.
D. Presenting only the products for which the borrower is already approved.

> **Correct answer: A.** A borrower can only compare options presented the same way, and the worst case is the figure the presentation exists to convey.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 04 (Mortgage markets, sources, and loan products), section 6.2

### 25. When does an application exist for disclosure purposes?

A. When the borrower pays an application fee.
**B. When the defined items are in the originator's hands — name, income, Social Security number, property address, an estimate of value, and the loan amount sought.**
C. When the borrower signs the uniform application form.
D. When the originator submits the file to a lender.

> **Correct answer: B.** It does not wait for a signature, a form, a submission, or a fee — and it does not require the borrower to supply the sixth item deliberately.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Application, borrower information, and qualification), section 1.3

### 26. A borrower stops responding to requests for documents. How is the file coded?

A. Approved but not accepted, since no decision was made.
B. Withdrawn by applicant, since the borrower discontinued participation.
**C. Incomplete or denied depending on what preceded it — 'he stopped answering' is not a withdrawal.**
D. Left open indefinitely until the borrower responds.

> **Correct answer: C.** Coding a denial or an incomplete file as a withdrawal removes the applicant from the denial data and from the notice he was owed.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Application, borrower information, and qualification), section 1.6

### 27. A large deposit appears in the borrower's account. What is the originator's role?

A. Draft the letter of explanation for the borrower's signature so the file moves.
B. Advise the borrower to wait until the deposit has seasoned.
C. Omit the account from the file and use a different one.
**D. Request the source documentation and the borrower's own explanation, and transmit them — never draft the explanation or advise how the funds should be characterized.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Drafting the borrower's account of his own facts is authorship, advising on seasoning is advising concealment, and substituting an account is worse than both.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Application, borrower information, and qualification), section 2.5

### 28. What must exist before a consumer report is obtained?

**A. A permissible purpose, and the authorization the framework requires.**
B. A completed appraisal.
C. A lender's conditional approval.
D. A signed purchase agreement for the subject property.

> **Correct answer: A.** A report pulled without permissible purpose is a violation, and the pull date is frequently what exposes an application date recorded later than the facts support.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Application, borrower information, and qualification), section 3.3

### 29. A borrower's monthly income is $9,400. Housing payment is $2,538 and other monthly obligations total $1,222. What are the housing and total debt ratios?

A. 24% and 36%
**B. 27% and 40%**
C. 27% and 13%
D. 40% and 27%

> **Correct answer: B.** $2,538 ÷ $9,400 = 27%; ($2,538 + $1,222) ÷ $9,400 = 40%. The second option omits the housing payment from the total ratio, which is the most common error.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Application, borrower information, and qualification), section 5.2

### 30. What must never happen to an application?

A. A supplement added when the borrower produces additional documentation.
B. A resubmission to a second lender after a denial.
C. A correction made after the borrower identifies an error.
**D. A figure altered, a document created inside the brokerage in the borrower's voice, or a verification represented that did not occur.**

> **Correct answer: D.** Corrections, supplements, and resubmission are ordinary. Altering, authoring, and misrepresenting a verification are not.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 05 (Application, borrower information, and qualification), section 6.2

### 31. What does the ability-to-repay requirement obligate a creditor to do?

C. Guarantee that the consumer will not default during the loan term.

### 32. Which payment must the ability-to-repay determination use where the payment can change?
