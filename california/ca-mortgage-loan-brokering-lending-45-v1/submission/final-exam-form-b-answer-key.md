## Items, with the material reference for each answer

### 1. What does a California real estate broker's mortgage authority NOT include?

**A. Activity that another licensing framework governs, which the broker license does not reach.**
B. Negotiating loan terms on a borrower's behalf.
C. Advertising the availability of mortgage brokerage services.
D. Arranging loans secured by residential property for compensation.

> **Correct answer: A.** Naming the authority that permits a new activity, before it begins, is the operating rule — reasoning by analogy from a familiar framework is how a brokerage gets one wrong.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (California mortgage brokerage and regulatory structure), section 1.3

### 2. How do the individual and company requirements relate under the endorsement framework?

A. The company's registration covers every individual it employs.
B. The individual's endorsement covers the company's activity.
C. Only the entity is required to register where all originators are employees.
**D. Both exist — the individual performing the activity and the entity through which it is performed each carry their own obligations.**

> **Correct answer: D.** The obligations are separate and neither absorbs the other, which is why a licensee confirms both before performing the activity.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (California mortgage brokerage and regulatory structure), section 2.4

### 3. How should an exemption from a licensing requirement be treated?

A. As permanent once relied on in a prior transaction.
B. As broad, since exemptions exist to keep ordinary business out of regulation.
**C. As narrow and specific — the burden of establishing that it applies rests on the person relying on it.**
D. As applying to every framework once it applies to one.

> **Correct answer: C.** An exemption from one requirement is not a general release, which is the error that produces unlicensed activity by people who believed they had checked.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (California mortgage brokerage and regulatory structure), section 4.3

### 4. Why is mortgage supervision harder than sales supervision?

A. Mortgage transactions involve more parties than sales transactions.
**B. The obligations run on clocks started by events rather than on visible transaction milestones, so a failure leaves nothing to notice.**
C. Mortgage files are larger and take longer to review.
D. Mortgage licensees work remotely more often than sales licensees.

> **Correct answer: B.** A disclosure not delivered on time looks exactly like one delivered on time until someone checks the dates, which is why review points are defined rather than triggered by problems.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (California mortgage brokerage and regulatory structure), section 5.3

### 5. A borrower sends a complaint alleging he was misled about his payment. What is the correct first step?

**A. Notify the broker, preserve everything, and reconstruct what the file shows before responding.**
B. Wait to see whether the borrower escalates it further.
C. Forward the complaint to the lender for a response.

> **Correct answer: A.** The first response sets the trajectory, and a reply written from memory is how a service problem becomes a credibility problem.

> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (California mortgage brokerage and regulatory structure), section 6.5

### 6. Why is the record described as the defense?

A. Because regulators accept a brokerage's own summary of its files.
B. Because a record shifts responsibility to the party who created each document.
C. Because a complete record prevents complaints from being filed.
**D. The process is largely documentary, and recollection, sincerity, and explanation carry very little against a record that says something else — and almost none against a record that says nothing.**

> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 01 (California mortgage brokerage and regulatory structure), section 6.7

### 7. What does it mean that residential mortgage interest is generally paid in arrears?

B. Interest is added to principal each month.

> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Money, interest, notes, and loan mathematics), section 1.5

### 8. How does a shorter term affect an amortized loan?

A. The payment falls and total interest rises.
**B. The payment rises and the total interest paid over the loan falls.**
C. The payment rises and total interest rises.

> **Correct answer: B.** Principal is retired faster, so less interest accrues in total even though each payment is larger.

### 9. Which note provision most directly affects what a borrower can do with the property later?

### 10. A borrower pays 1.5 points to reduce his payment by $92 a month. Approximately how long is the break-even on a $380,000 loan?

**A. About 62 months.**
B. About 24 months.
C. About 41 months.
D. About 96 months.

> **Correct answer: A.** $380,000 × 0.015 = $5,700; $5,700 ÷ $92 ≈ 62 months. The break-even is what makes points a question about the borrower's holding period.
>
> **Found in the course material at:** Assignment 02 (Money, interest, notes, and loan mathematics), section 4.2

### 11. What does PITI comprise?

C. Principal, interest, term, and index.
