# RE 306 Items 2 and 10 — Fictitious business name determination

**Applicant:** StateCourse LLC
**Prepared:** August 20, 2026
**Status:** Submission draft. Not filed. **This determination requires a decision from the owner before RE 306 can be signed** — see "The decision" below.

## The question

The registered legal name is **StateCourse LLC**. The public-facing brand is **StateCourse**. Does dropping only the entity suffix create a California fictitious business name requirement?

## The answer, from the statute

**Yes — if the business is transacted under the shortened name.** The text is not ambiguous.

**Business and Professions Code § 17900(b)(5):**

> "As used in this chapter, 'fictitious business name' means: … **(5) In the case of a limited liability company, any name other than the name stated in its articles of organization** and in the case of a foreign limited liability company that has filed an application for registration with the California Secretary of State pursuant to Section 17708.02 of the Corporations Code, any name other than the name of the limited liability company as on file with the California Secretary of State, in accordance with subdivision (b) of Section 17910.5."

The test for an LLC is **"any name other than the name stated in its articles of organization."** It is a literal identity test. It contains no exception for a name that differs only by the entity designator, no materiality threshold, and no "confusingly similar" allowance.

StateCourse LLC's Articles of Organization, filed with the California Secretary of State on 18 August 2026 as file number **B20260376786**, state the name as **"StateCourse LLC"**. The string **"StateCourse"** is not that name. It is therefore, on the face of § 17900(b)(5), a fictitious business name.

**Note the contrast with individuals and partnerships.** For an individual, § 17900(b)(1) makes a name fictitious only if it "does not include the surname" or "suggests the existence of additional owners." For a partnership, § 17900(b)(2) is similar. Those tests are functional and forgiving. **The LLC and corporation tests in (b)(3) and (b)(5) are not** — they are exact-match tests against the filed name. The Legislature drew the distinction deliberately, and it is the reason the intuition "it's basically the same name" does not survive contact with the statute.

## The filing duty, if the name is used

**Business and Professions Code § 17910:**

> "Every person who **regularly transacts business in this state for profit under a fictitious business name** shall do all of the following: (a) File a fictitious business name statement in accordance with this chapter **not later than 40 days from the time the registrant commences to transact such business**. (b) File a new statement after any change in the facts… (c) File a new statement when refiling…"

Two conditions must both be met before the duty attaches: the name must be fictitious (it is), **and** the registrant must **regularly transact business for profit under it**. The second is a question of fact about conduct, not branding. Displaying a wordmark is not obviously transacting business under a name; contracting, invoicing, enrolling students, and taking payment under "StateCourse" plainly is.

**As things stand today, StateCourse LLC transacts no business at all** — enrollment is closed, no course is approved, and nothing has been sold. The duty has not yet been triggered. It would be triggered by the first enrollment taken under the shortened name, and § 17910(a) then allows 40 days.

## The decision

There are two clean positions. **Both are lawful. Neither may be left unresolved, because RE 306 item 10 is a yes/no question under a signature.**

### Option A — Use the full legal name publicly. No FBNS.

Present the school as **StateCourse LLC** in every place that matters to DRE and to students: the RE 313 General Information Page, enrollment and registration screens, the enrollment agreement, receipts, the RE 311 completion certificate, and the school-identifying line in advertising.

- RE 306 item 2 (other names used): *none*
- RE 306 item 10: **NO**
- No FBNS filing, no county publication, no attachment, no renewal every five years.
- The `statecourse.com` domain and the visual wordmark are unaffected. **A domain name and a logo are not the name a business transacts under**; the operative question is the name on the agreement and the receipt.

Note that **RE 307 Regulation 3004(a)** independently requires advertising to "clearly and conspicuously identify the entity offering an equivalent course of study as a private vocational school or the sponsor … and shall include the name of the entity." Using the full legal name in advertising satisfies that regulation at the same time, which makes Option A do double duty.

### Option B — Trade as "StateCourse". File the FBNS first.

- File a Fictitious Business Name Statement for "StateCourse" with the **Los Angeles County Clerk** (the county of the principal place of business, Burbank).
- Publish once a week for four successive weeks in an adjudicated newspaper of general circulation in that county, and file the affidavit of publication.
- RE 306 item 2: **StateCourse**
- RE 306 item 10: **YES**, print "StateCourse", and **attach a copy of the filed FBNS** — the form requires the copy.
- Renew every five years.
- **The application cannot be filed until the FBNS is filed**, because item 10 requires the copy as an attachment. Add the county's processing time and the four-week publication run to the schedule.

### Recommendation

**Option A.** It costs nothing, delays nothing, removes an attachment from the envelope, satisfies Regulation 3004(a) at the same stroke, and eliminates a recurring five-year obligation. The brand is unaffected in every place a brand actually lives. Option B buys the ability to put "StateCourse" on an enrollment agreement, and that is the only thing it buys.

**If Option A is chosen, one consistency rule follows and the audit enforces it:** the *school name* on RE 313, on the enrollment flow, and on the completion certificate reads "StateCourse LLC". Marketing prose elsewhere may say "StateCourse" the way any company refers to itself informally.

## What is asserted here, and what is not

**Asserted:** under B&P § 17900(b)(5) the string "StateCourse" is a fictitious business name of StateCourse LLC, because it is not the name stated in the Articles of Organization.
**Asserted:** the § 17910 filing duty attaches only on regularly transacting business for profit under that name, which has not yet occurred.
**Not asserted:** that an FBNS has been filed. None has.
**Not a request for counsel.** The statutory text resolves the classification question. What remains is a business decision about which name to trade under, and that decision belongs to the owner rather than to a lawyer.
