## How to organise the envelope

**Do not staple anything.** DRE scans and re-sorts; staples get torn out and pages get lost.

- **Binder clip** each application's forms and documents together, in the numbered order above.
- **Rubber-band or clip** each bound course text to its application, or place it directly behind.
- **Manila folder or divider** between the four sections, labelled on the tab:
  1. `ADMINISTRATIVE`
  2. `01 — REAL ESTATE PRINCIPLES`
  3. `02 — REAL ESTATE PRACTICE`
  4. `03 — MORTGAGE LOAN BROKERING AND LENDING`
- **Cover letter on top**, one page: who you are, that three applications are enclosed, the three course titles, the total fee and how it is paid, and your phone and email. Keep it short — a reviewer wants the contents list, not an argument.
- Everything into one box or envelope, unless the course texts make it too heavy, in which case two boxes clearly labelled **"1 of 2"** and **"2 of 2"** with the administrative section in box 1.

## What to expect from DRE next

- **Review takes 60 days** for pre-license/statutory submissions. That is DRE's own published figure. Do not chase before it elapses.
- The **fee is non-refundable** and must be received before processing starts, so the clock effectively begins when the check clears.
- **A deficiency letter is normal, not a failure.** First-time applications commonly get one. Answer it promptly and completely.
- **On approval**, DRE issues a certificate of course approval and assigns your **sponsor/statutory ID number** and a **course approval number for each course**. Approved courses are then listed publicly on the DRE website with your provider name, statutory ID, address, telephone, website, course title, and format.
- **Nothing goes on sale before that.** Once the approval numbers exist, they go into the platform configuration, the certificate gate opens, and only then can a course be marked approved and enrollment enabled. Those are three separate deliberate steps and none of them happens automatically.

## If DRE calls

The two questions most likely to come up, and the short answers:

- **"Why are both boxes checked on 15B?"** The examination is delivered over the internet **and** supervised in person by a school-designated proctor, as Regulation 3000(a)(2)(B) requires of a correspondence course. Checking only one would misdescribe it. There is a continuation sheet behind the form saying exactly that.
- **"What is your BPPE status?"** Exempt under Education Code § 94874(d)(2)(A) — license examination preparation approved by a government agency, other than the Bureau, that licenses the profession. Continuation sheet A4 sets out the analysis.
