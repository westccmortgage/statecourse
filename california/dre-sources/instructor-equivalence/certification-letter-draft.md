# Certification letter — draft for the reviewing instructor to adapt

**Send this only after the instructor has reviewed the material and indicated they are willing to
certify.** Sending it earlier presumes the conclusion.

---

## What RE 303 requires the certification to be

> a certification from a **college instructor in the field of study** for **each specific course**,
> confirming the course content is **equivalent to course material used in a college or university
> course**. This certification must be **on official school letterhead** and **signed by the
> instructor**.

Four elements, all mandatory:

| Element | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| **Who** | A college instructor in the field of study |
| **Scope** | One certification per course, naming that course |
| **Substance** | Confirms equivalence to college or university course material |
| **Form** | Official institutional letterhead, signed by the instructor |

## An important point about this draft

The paragraphs below are a starting point, not a script. **The certification has value precisely
because it is the instructor's own professional judgment**, and a letter that reads as though it was
written by the applicant and signed by someone else is worth less — to DRE and to anyone reading it
later — than a shorter letter in the instructor's own words.

Offer this as a structure. Invite them to change anything, including qualifying their conclusion or
narrowing what they are attesting to. A qualified certification honestly given is far stronger than
an unqualified one that overstates what the reviewer believes.

## Notes for the applicant — do not send

**Do not edit the instructor's substantive conclusions.** If they qualify the certification, the
qualification goes to DRE as written. Removing or softening a reviewer's qualification would
misrepresent their opinion to the Department, in a submission certified under penalty of perjury.

**A qualification is not a failure.** A letter saying the curriculum is equivalent in coverage and
objectives while recommending expansion in two areas is a credible document, and it tells DRE
exactly what an honest reviewer would tell them. It is more persuasive than uniform praise.

**Three separate letters are required**, one per course, each naming that course. One letter covering
all three does not satisfy "for each specific course."

**Ask for the letter in original signed form** on letterhead, and ask whether the institution will
provide a scanned copy as well.

**Keep the reviewer's comments**, including anything not in the letter. They are the input to the
curriculum work, and being able to show DRE that reviewer comments were acted on is worth having.

**Check the mailing address against the current RE 306** before using the address block above; it is
taken from RE 306 (Rev. 1/26) and DRE addresses do change.
