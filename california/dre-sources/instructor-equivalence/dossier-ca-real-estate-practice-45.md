## Why this document exists

California DRE form RE 303 sets the approval standard for a statutory pre-license course:

> To obtain approval, curriculum must be equivalent to real estate courses offered by colleges
> or universities accredited by the Western Association of Schools and Colleges as recognized by
> the United States Department of Education.
>
> **If you write your own course content, it will be necessary to obtain a certification from a
> college instructor in the field of study for each specific course, confirming the course content
> is equivalent to course material used in a college or university course. This certification must
> be on official school letterhead and signed by the instructor.**

This dossier is the material a reviewing college instructor needs in order to make that judgment.

## Structure and why it takes this form

The 15-assignment structure is not a design preference. Commissioner's Regulation 3000(a)(1)
provides that *"a correspondence course shall consist of not less than 15 separate lesson
assignments,"* and the course is built to that requirement. Each assignment is scoped to 180
instructional minutes, giving 45 hours across the course.

Each assignment covers six topics, carries its own learning objectives, an applied written
exercise, and a five-item scored check against an 80 percent mastery threshold, with a separate
five-item remediation set covering the same concepts a second way for students who do not meet it.

## Lesson detail

### 1. California practice standards and the regulated professional

**Topics covered:**

1. DRE authority and license categories
2. Broker supervision
3. Professional competence and scope
4. Office policies and delegation
5. Consumer-first communication
6. Transaction file accountability

**Learning objectives — on completion the student can:**

- Explain what the Department of Real Estate regulates, which activities require a license, and how the broker and salesperson licenses differ in authority.
- Describe the responsible broker's supervisory obligations and distinguish delegating a task from transferring responsibility.
- Apply the competence standard to unfamiliar work and identify the boundary between explaining real estate practice and practicing law.
- Evaluate whether an office's written policies actually control the conduct they are meant to govern.
- Distinguish verified fact, unverified report, and professional opinion in communications with consumers.
- Assemble a transaction file that shows what was done, when, by whom, and on what basis.

## Assessment design

| Component | Per lesson | Across the course |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Applied written exercise | 1 | 15 |
| Scored engagement items | 5 | 75 |
| Remediation items (alternate set) | 5 | 75 |
| **Total scored items** | **11** | **165** |

Every item has its own prompt, its own answer choices, and its own rationale; no item shares an
answer set with any other item in any of the three courses. Correct answers are distributed across
positions rather than sitting in a fixed place. A student who does not reach the 80 percent
threshold receives an alternate explanation and a different item set covering the same concepts,
rather than being re-shown the questions they missed.

The final examination is separate from these lesson checks and is governed by the requirements in
RE 303: not fewer than 100 multiple-choice or fill-in questions, a passing score of 60 percent or
greater, comprehensive coverage of the entire course, proctored administration, and one permitted
retake using different questions.
