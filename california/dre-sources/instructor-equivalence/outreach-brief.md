# Outreach brief — request for curriculum review

*One page. Send this first, with nothing attached but the course dossier. Its purpose is to obtain a
yes to "would you look at this", not a yes to signing anything.*

---

**Subject:** Request for curriculum review — California DRE pre-license course

Dear Professor [Name],

I am developing a California Department of Real Estate statutory pre-license course in
**[Real Estate Practice / Real Estate Principles / Mortgage Loan Brokering and Lending]** and I am
looking for a college instructor in the field who would be willing to review the curriculum.

I am writing to you because you teach this subject at [Institution], which is exactly the standard
the review is measured against.

## What the review is for

DRE approves a pre-license course only if the curriculum is equivalent to the same course as taught
at an accredited college or university. Where a provider writes its own content — as I have — RE 303
requires a certification from a college instructor in the field of study, on institutional
letterhead, confirming that equivalence.

## What I am asking

That you read the course material and give me your professional assessment of it against the college
course you teach:

- Does the coverage correspond to what your course covers?
- Is the depth comparable?
- Are the objectives appropriate, and does the content actually deliver them?
- Is the assessment appropriate to the objectives?
- **What is missing or thin?**

That last question is the one I most want answered. The course is still being written, and a list of
gaps now is worth considerably more to me than an endorsement later.

If, having read it, you conclude it is equivalent, I would ask you to say so in a signed letter on
your institution's letterhead. If you conclude it is not, that is a useful answer and I would rather
have it than not.

## What I am not asking

- Not to teach the course, or to be its instructor of record.
- Not to endorse the school, the business, or anything beyond the curriculum itself.
- Not to take on any responsibility for the program's regulatory compliance.
- Not to sign anything before you have read the material and formed your own view.

## Practical points

- **Compensation.** I would like to pay an honorarium for your review time. It is for the review,
  not for a particular conclusion.
- **Your institution's policies.** If outside professional work or use of letterhead requires
  disclosure or approval where you teach, I would rather we sort that out at the start.
- **Timing.** There is no deadline pressure from my side. The application has not been filed and the
  course is not approved; I am building this properly rather than quickly.

## What is enclosed

A course dossier: the timed outline, the learning objectives for each of the 15 lesson assignments,
the time allocation, and the assessment design. It also states plainly where the material currently
stands against DRE's own page benchmark, which is roughly [N] pages against the ~450 pages RE 303
describes as equivalent to a college textbook. The course is real instruction at less than that
depth, and I am not going to present it as more than it is.

If you are willing to look further, I will send complete lesson files rather than summaries — I
would rather you judge the actual material.

Thank you for considering it. I am happy to talk by phone if that is easier.

## Notes for the sender — delete before sending

- **Fill in [N]** from the dossier's page count for that course. Do not round it upward.
- **One course per letter.** Three courses need three certifications, and an instructor qualified in
  practice may not be the right reviewer for mortgage lending.
- **Do not attach the certification draft.** It reads as though the conclusion is already decided.
- **Do not say the course is approved or that an application is pending** unless it actually is.
- **If they ask about liability:** they are certifying their own professional judgment on curriculum
  content. They are not certifying the school's compliance, the accuracy of every statement of law,
  or anything about the business.
- **If they decline:** ask whether they would suggest a colleague. Referrals within a department are
  the most productive source.
