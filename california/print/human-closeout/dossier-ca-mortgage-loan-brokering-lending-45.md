provides that *"a correspondence course shall consist of not less than 15 separate lesson
assignments,"* and the course is built to that requirement. Each assignment is scoped to 180
instructional minutes, giving 45 hours across the course.

Each assignment covers six topics, carries its own learning objectives, an applied written
exercise, and a five-item scored check against an 80 percent mastery threshold, with a separate
five-item remediation set covering the same concepts a second way for students who do not meet it.

### 1. California mortgage brokerage and regulatory structure

**Topics covered:**

1. DRE real estate broker mortgage authority
2. MLO endorsement orientation
3. DRE and other regulator boundaries
4. Licensed and exempt activity
5. Broker supervision
6. Consumer complaint and enforcement context

**Learning objectives — on completion the student can:**

- Explain the mortgage authority a California real estate broker license carries and what it does not cover.
- Describe the mortgage loan originator endorsement, what triggers the requirement, and what it adds to a license.
- Distinguish the DRE from the other regulators a mortgage transaction touches, and identify which answers which question.
- Determine whether a given activity is licensed, exempt, or unlicensed, and identify what compensation does to the analysis.
- Describe the broker's supervisory duty over mortgage activity and what a real supervision system contains.
- Identify how consumer complaints and enforcement reach a mortgage broker and what a licensee does when contacted.

### 15. Integrated California mortgage brokerage file

**Topics covered:**

1. Consumer inquiry and scope
2. Application and identity
3. Product comparison and disclosures
4. Underwriting and conditions
5. Closing and compensation
6. Post-closing audit and the regulator-ready file

**Learning objectives — on completion the student can:**

- Trace a California mortgage transaction from first inquiry through funding as a single chronology in which each step depends on the one before it.
- Identify the moment an application arises and every obligation that begins running from it.
- Build and document a product comparison a later reviewer could reconstruct without the originator present.
- Clear underwriting conditions without becoming the author of the borrower's evidence.
- Reconcile disclosed compensation against compensation actually received at closing.
- Audit a closed mortgage file for reconstructability, rank its defects, and state the line between a documented gap and a manufactured document.

Every item has its own prompt, its own answer choices, and its own rationale; no item shares an
answer set with any other item in any of the three courses. Correct answers are distributed across
positions rather than sitting in a fixed place. A student who does not reach the 80 percent
threshold receives an alternate explanation and a different item set covering the same concepts,
rather than being re-shown the questions they missed.

The final examination is separate from these lesson checks and is governed by the requirements in
RE 303: not fewer than 100 multiple-choice or fill-in questions, a passing score of 60 percent or
greater, comprehensive coverage of the entire course, proctored administration, and one permitted
retake using different questions.
