# Maine Sales Agent Course — Module 02

## Hours 8–14 · Agency relationships

**Commission topic covered:** Agency Relationships (13%, 390 instructional minutes)
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Not approved. No Maine sales agent credit is available for this course.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module the student will be able to:

- State the § 13282 presumption and explain what it takes to displace it.
- Distinguish a transaction broker from a seller agent, buyer agent, subagent and disclosed dual
  agent, and say what each owes.
- Recite the seven duties in § 13272 and the specific obligations in §§ 13273 and 13274.
- Explain why knowledge is not imputed within a Maine agency, and where the statute says so.
- Complete the relationship analysis for a multi-party transaction and identify the documents each
  step requires.

## Hour 08, second half — The presumption, and what displaces it

### 8.4 § 13282, read exactly

> Except as otherwise provided in this subchapter, a real estate brokerage agency providing real
> estate brokerage services is **presumed to be acting as a transaction broker** unless the real
> estate brokerage agency has agreed, **in a written brokerage agreement**, to represent one or more
> parties to the real estate transaction as the real estate brokerage agency's clients.
> **Client representation may not be created orally or by implication** or be assumed by a real
> estate brokerage agency or any party to a real estate transaction.

Four consequences, each of which a sales agent will meet in the first month.

**Nobody is a client until there is a written brokerage agreement.** Not after a long conversation.
Not after three showings. Not because the consumer thinks so.

**Nothing a licensee does creates representation by implication.** Helpfulness does not create
agency in Maine. This protects the licensee as much as the consumer, and it is the reason the
relationship disclosure form exists.

**The default has real content.** A transaction broker is not nothing: § 13283 2 imposes six
duties. But it is not agency, and the seven duties in § 13272 do not attach.

**The consumer must be told which they have.** § 13279 requires the agency to provide buyers and
sellers of residential real property a meaningful, written relationship disclosure form in a timely
manner, as the Commission's rules define and mandate.

### 8.5 The five relationships

**Transaction broker — § 13283.** Represents nobody as a client, is not bound by § 13272, and owes
the six duties in § 13283 2.

**Seller agent — § 13273.** Represents the seller as a client.

**Buyer agent — § 13274.** Represents the buyer as a client.

**Subagent.** § 13272 names the subagent as one of the forms an agency may take. A subagent works
through another agency's client relationship.

**Disclosed dual agent — § 13275.** Represents both clients in the same transaction, only with the
informed written consent of all parties.

To which add the **appointed agent** under § 13278 — not a fifth relationship but a way of
allocating an agency's client relationships among its licensees.

## Hour 09 — The seven duties, and what is not imputed

### 9.1 § 13272, verbatim

> A real estate brokerage agency that provides services through a brokerage agreement for a client
> is bound by the duties of **loyalty, obedience, disclosure, confidentiality, reasonable care,
> diligence and accounting** as set forth in this chapter.

Seven duties, and unlike Virginia — where § 54.1-2144 abrogates the common law of agency to the
extent inconsistent — Maine keeps them. A student who has read national agency material will find it
useful here, provided they check every proposition against §§ 13273 and 13274, which are where the
duties acquire their Maine content.

**Loyalty.** The client's interests come before the licensee's and before anyone else's.
**Obedience.** Lawful instructions are followed. Unlawful ones are not, and § 13273 1 E makes
compliance with fair housing and civil rights law a duty in its own right, so a client instruction
cannot authorise a violation.
**Disclosure.** § 13273 1 B (3) — material facts of actual knowledge **or which the agent, acting
reasonably, should have known**.
**Confidentiality.** § 13273 1 F — information provided during the relationship that might have a
negative impact on the client's real estate activity, subject to four exceptions.
**Reasonable care.** § 13273 1 C — reasonable skill and care.
**Diligence.** Pursuing the client's objective actively, not passively.
**Accounting.** § 13273 1 B (5) — timely accounting for all money and property in which the client
has or may have an interest.

### 9.2 The seller agent's specific obligations — § 13273 1

**A.** Perform the terms of the brokerage agreement.

**B.** Promote the seller's interests by:
 **(1)** seeking a sale at the price and terms stated in the agreement or acceptable to the seller —
 with the express carve-out that **the seller agent is not obligated to seek additional offers while
 the property is subject to a contract of sale unless the brokerage agreement so provides**;
 **(2) presenting in a timely manner all offers to and from the seller, even when the property is
 subject to a contract of sale**;
 **(3) disclosing material facts of actual knowledge or which the agent, acting in a reasonable
 manner, should have known**, except as directed in § 13280;
 **(4) advising the seller to obtain expert advice on material matters beyond the seller agent's
 expertise**; and
 **(5)** accounting in a timely manner for all money and property received in which the seller has
 or may have an interest.

**C.** Exercise reasonable skill and care.
**D.** Comply with the brokerage licence laws and the Commission's rules.
**E.** Comply with applicable federal, state and local law, **including fair housing and civil rights
law**.
**F.** Preserve confidential information provided by the seller that might have a negative impact on
the seller's real estate activity, unless **(1)** the seller consents, **(2)** disclosure is required
by law, **(3)** the information is or becomes public otherwise than through the agent, or **(4)**
disclosure is necessary to defend the agent against an accusation of wrongful conduct before a
court, the Commission, or a professional committee.

**§ 13274** carries the same architecture for a buyer agent.

Two of these deserve emphasis because they are broader than the equivalents in several other states.

**B (3) is a constructive-knowledge standard.** "Should have known" means a Maine seller agent
cannot rely on not having asked. Where a reasonable agent in the circumstances would have known a
material fact, the duty attaches.

**B (4) is an affirmative duty to send the client elsewhere.** The statute requires the agent to
advise the client to get expert advice on material matters beyond the agent's expertise. That is not
a defensive habit; it is a statutory obligation, and it is the answer to almost every question a
sales agent is not qualified to answer.

### 9.3 Vicarious liability, and the two places Maine says knowledge is not imputed

A licensee's acts within the scope of the agency relationship can expose the agency; the designated
broker's supervision duty under § 13179 is the mechanism that manages that exposure. But Maine
limits the doctrine in a specific and examinable way.

**§ 13275 3** — in a disclosed dual agency, each client, the agency and its affiliated licensees are
considered to possess **only actual knowledge and information**, and **there is no imputation of
knowledge or information by operation of law** among or between them.

**§ 13278 3** — the same rule where agents have been appointed.

So in a Maine agency operating with appointed agents, what the seller's appointed agent knows is not
attributed to the buyer's appointed agent, nor to the buyer, nor to the agency at large. A course
teaching that knowledge is imputed within a firm is teaching the opposite of the statute — and it is
what makes appointed agency workable at all.

## Hour 10 — Written client agreements

### 10.1 § 13177-A, applied

Module 01 stated the five required components. This hour uses them.

**A. The signature of the client to be charged.** The party who will owe the fee signs. Where a
property is jointly owned, everyone whose signature is needed to convey should be on the agreement.

**B. The terms and conditions of the brokerage services to be provided.** What the agency will
actually do. A client who later says "I did not know you would not do that" is describing a defect
in this component.

**C. The method or amount of compensation to be paid.** Stated, not implied, and negotiable.

**D. The date upon which the agreement will expire.** Maine requires an end date. There is no
open-ended Maine listing, and no default period fills a gap — the statute requires the date to be
in the agreement.

**E. A statement that the agreement creates an agency-client relationship.** This is the component
that connects § 13177-A to § 13282. Without a written brokerage agreement the presumption is
transaction brokerage; element E is the agreement saying, in terms, that representation exists.

### 10.2 The two protections

> A brokerage agreement may not be enforced against any client who **in good faith subsequently
> engages the services of another real estate brokerage agency following the expiration date** of
> the first brokerage agreement.

> Any brokerage agreement provision extending a real estate brokerage agency's right to a fee
> following expiration **may not extend that right beyond 6 months.**

A protection period, holdover clause, or fee tail is permitted — capped at six months from
expiration — and it cannot be used against a client who has in good faith engaged another agency
after the expiration date.

### 10.3 Before the agreement: policy and consent

**§ 13278 4** requires the Commission's rules to include a requirement that clients be **informed of
the agency's appointed agent policy and give written consent to that policy in advance of entering
into a brokerage agreement.** § 13277 requires the agency to have a **written policy**.

So the order in a Maine agency is: the relationship disclosure form under § 13279 in a timely
manner; the appointed agent policy explained and consented to in writing; and then the brokerage
agreement satisfying § 13177-A. Doing them out of order is how an agency ends up with a signed
agreement and an uninformed client.

### 10.4 What the agreement does not do

It does not make the licensee a lawyer. It does not permit the licensee to draft bespoke legal
terms. And it does not convert the client's obligations into the licensee's: the client still signs
their own contracts, gives their own instructions, and makes their own decisions.

## Hour 11 — Seller agency, buyer agency, subagency, and disclosed dual agency

### 11.1 Seller agency

The agency has a written brokerage agreement with the seller. § 13273 governs. The seller is the
client; the buyer, unless separately represented, is a **customer**.

To the buyer as customer the seller agent owes honesty, no knowingly false information, and — under
the same principle that runs through the chapter — disclosure of material defects in the physical
condition of the property that the agent knows about. The agent does **not** owe the buyer loyalty,
confidentiality, or advocacy, and saying so plainly at the outset prevents most complaints.

### 11.2 Buyer agency

**§ 13274** carries the § 13273 architecture to the buyer side: perform the agreement; promote the
buyer's interests; disclose material facts; exercise reasonable skill and care; comply with the
brokerage laws, the Commission's rules, and fair housing law; and preserve confidential information
on the same four exceptions.

The seller is then the customer, and the same boundary applies in reverse.

### 11.3 Subagency

**§ 13272** names the subagent. A subagent is an agency that works through another agency's client
relationship, owing the client's duties without a direct agreement with the client. Subagency has
largely fallen out of use in practice, and where it is offered it must be accepted or declined
deliberately rather than by default.

### 11.4 Disclosed dual agency — § 13275

Permitted **only with the informed written consent of all parties**, on the five statements Module
01 listed, and subject to the four categories of information a disclosed dual agent may **never**
disclose:

1. the willingness or ability of the seller to accept less than the asking price;
2. the willingness or ability of the buyer to pay more than has been offered;
3. confidential negotiating strategy not disclosed in the sales offer as terms of the sale;
4. the motivation of the seller for selling and the motivation of the buyer for buying.

**§ 13275 4** provides that the dual agent's duty to the selling client is the same as § 13273's and
to the buying client the same as § 13274's, subject to the section's exceptions.

The practical consequence, and it must be explained to clients before consent rather than after: a
disclosed dual agent **cannot advocate on price or terms for either side**, because advocating for
one is acting against the other. What the dual agent can do is facilitate — carry offers, explain
process, keep the transaction moving — and the four protected categories are what stops the
arrangement from being a trap for whichever client speaks first.

### 11.5 Choosing

For a consumer the choice is real, and § 13275 1 D requires the consent agreement to say so: the
client **may choose to consent or not consent**. A client who declines dual agency in an agency that
has both sides may be offered appointed agents under § 13278, or may take their business elsewhere.
A licensee who presents consent as a formality has defeated the section.

## Hour 12 — Appointed agents, company policy, and supervision

### 12.1 § 13278, worked

**Subsection 1.** The agency, **through the designated broker**, may **appoint in writing to the
client** those affiliated licensees who will act as **appointed agents** of that client, **to the
exclusion of all other affiliated licensees** in the agency.

**Subsection 2.** The agency and the designated broker are **not dual agents solely because of an
appointment** — **except** that any affiliated licensee who **personally represents both the seller
and the buyer as clients** in a particular transaction **is** a dual agent and must comply with
§ 13275.

**Subsection 3.** No imputation of knowledge or information among the clients, the agency, and the
appointed agents. Each possesses only actual knowledge and information.

**Subsection 4.** The Commission's rules define the methods of appointment and the roles of the
agency and designated broker, and must require that clients be **informed of the appointed agent
policy and give written consent to it in advance of entering into a brokerage agreement.**

### 12.2 Why subsection 2 matters

In several states a designated or appointed arrangement makes the supervising broker a dual agent —
Virginia's § 54.1-2139.1 A says so expressly. **Maine says the opposite.** The agency and the
designated broker are not dual agents merely because appointments were made.

That is a different architecture, and a licensee moving between the two states must not carry one
into the other. The exception is narrow and personal: an individual licensee who represents both
clients in the same transaction is a dual agent no matter what the agency's structure is.

### 12.3 Company policy — § 13277

The agency must have a **written policy**. For a sales agent, the policy answers the questions the
statute leaves to the agency: whether the agency practises appointed agency at all; whether and when
it will act as a disclosed dual agent; how relationship disclosure forms are delivered and recorded;
how deposits are handled; what advertising must contain; and what must be escalated to the designated
broker.

A sales agent who has not read their agency's written policy cannot answer a client's first question
about how the agency works.

### 12.4 Supervision — § 13179

Supervision is the designated broker's duty. What it means for a sales agent in practice: the
designated broker reviews agreements and documents; the designated broker is responsible for the
agency's records under § 13184 and its trust accounts under § 13178; and the designated broker is
the escalation point for anything unusual.

The instinct to solve a problem quietly before anyone notices is the instinct that turns a small
error into a disciplinary matter.

## Hour 13 — The relationship disclosure form and documentation

### 13.1 § 13279

> A real estate brokerage agency shall provide **in a timely manner** to buyers and sellers of
> residential real property **a meaningful, written real estate brokerage relationship disclosure
> form as defined and mandated by rules adopted by the commission.** For purposes of this section,
> "residential real property" means real estate consisting of **not less than one nor more than 4
> residential dwelling units.**

Four things to hold.

**It is mandatory** for residential property of one to four dwelling units.

**Its content and its timing are set by the Commission's rules** under § 13280 — so it is used as
published, and this course states no timing rule of its own.

**It is a disclosure, not an agreement.** Giving it creates nothing. Under § 13282 representation
requires a written brokerage agreement, and cannot arise by implication.

**Its purpose is to prevent the misunderstanding in Module 01's engagement check** — the consumer who
believes, reasonably but wrongly, that the helpful licensee is their agent.

### 13.2 Documentation as a practice

What belongs in the file, and when:

**At first substantive contact:** the relationship disclosure form, delivered and recorded.
**Before the brokerage agreement:** the appointed agent policy, explained and consented to in
writing where the agency practises appointed agency.
**At engagement:** the written brokerage agreement satisfying § 13177-A, signed by the client to be
charged, with the expiration date and the statement that it creates an agency-client relationship.
**Where the agency will be on both sides:** the § 13275 disclosed dual agency consent, or the
§ 13278 written appointments — never neither.
**Throughout:** every offer and counteroffer with date and time and evidence of presentation, since
§ 13273 1 B (2) requires all offers to be presented in a timely manner **even when the property is
under contract**; the property disclosure statement under 33 M.R.S. § 173; and a note of every
material conversation.
**On termination:** the record of how and when it ended, because § 13281 measures the surviving
duties from that point.

### 13.3 Why the record wins

Every complaint in brokerage is a dispute about what was said, when, and to whom. A file that
answers those three questions contemporaneously answers the complaint. A file assembled afterwards
answers nothing and suggests that nothing was kept.

## Hour 14 — Termination, surviving duties, conflicts, and integration

### 14.1 § 13281 — duration

**Subsection 1.** The relationships commence on the **effective date of the brokerage agreement**
and continue until **performance, completion, termination or expiration** of that agreement.

**Subsection 2.** After termination, expiration, completion or performance, the agency and the
affiliated licensee **owe no further duty or obligation except**:

**A.** **Accounting** in a timely manner for all money and property related to, and received during,
the relationship; and

**B.** For seller agents, buyer agents, subagents and disclosed dual agents, **treating as
confidential** information provided by the client during the relationship that **could have a
negative impact on the client's real estate activity** — unless **(1)** the client grants **written**
consent, **(2)** disclosure is required by law, **(3)** the information is or becomes public
otherwise than through the agency or licensee, or **(4)** disclosure is necessary to defend against
an action of wrongful conduct before a court, the Commission, or a professional committee.

Two features worth noticing. The surviving confidentiality duty requires **written** consent to
release, where the in-relationship duty under § 13273 1 F (1) says only "grants consent". And the
list is exhaustive: **no other duty survives**, which is a protection for the licensee as much as an
obligation.

### 14.2 Conflicts

**§ 13251-A** — a broker or associate broker may not knowingly provide or offer an **appraisal or
opinion of market value** on real estate in a transaction where the broker, or **any other licensee
licensed with the agency**, is to receive a fee on that transaction.

**Self-interest.** A licensee buying or selling for their own account, for family, or through an
entity they hold an interest in, must disclose the position.

**Two clients whose interests conflict.** § 13275 if the same licensee represents both; § 13278 if
the agency allocates them to different appointed agents; and a decision, made before the conflict
arises rather than during it, about which the agency will do.

**A former client.** § 13281 2 B is the section, and the duty does not expire with the agreement.

### 14.3 Integrated scenario

*A Maine agency has a written brokerage agreement with a seller, signed 3 March, expiring 3
September, with a 90-day protection period. On 12 August a buyer who has been working with the same
agency for six weeks — no written agreement — decides to offer. The agency practises appointed
agency and has a written policy. During the negotiation the buyer tells their licensee they would go
$15,000 higher. The seller has told the listing licensee they will take $20,000 less.*

Work it through:

**The buyer is not a client.** § 13282 — no written brokerage agreement, and representation cannot
arise by implication from six weeks of showings. The agency is a **transaction broker** as to the
buyer, owing the § 13283 2 duties and bound by the § 13283 3 prohibitions.

**The seller is a client**, under a § 13177-A agreement that is still within its term.

**So this is not dual agency**, and appointed agency is not needed for the buyer's side, because the
buyer has no client relationship to allocate. The agency should have provided the buyer the § 13279
relationship disclosure form long before now, and should offer the buyer the choice of a written
brokerage agreement or of proceeding as a customer — clearly, and before the offer.

**The buyer's statement that they would go higher.** As a transaction broker the agency owes the
buyer no confidentiality — § 13283 1 says a transaction broker is not bound by the § 13272 duties.
But the agency **does** owe the seller, as its client, disclosure of material facts under
§ 13273 1 B (3). That is exactly why the buyer must be told, before they speak, that the agency is
not representing them — and why the relationship disclosure form is mandatory.

**The seller's statement that they will take less.** Confidential under § 13273 1 F, and it stays
confidential after the agreement ends under § 13281 2 B.

**The protection period.** Permitted, and capped at six months from expiration by § 13177-A, and
unenforceable against a client who in good faith engages another agency after the expiration date.

### Module 02 engagement check

**Question.** An agency has written brokerage agreements with both the seller and the buyer in the
same transaction. The designated broker appoints Licensee A to the seller and Licensee B to the
buyer, in writing, and both clients consented in writing to the agency's appointed agent policy
before signing their agreements. The buyer asks Licensee B whether the seller would take less;
Licensee A knows the seller would. What is each person's status, and what may be said?

**Expected answer.** This is **appointed agency under § 13278**. Under **§ 13278 2** the agency and
the **designated broker are not dual agents solely because of the appointment** — which is the
opposite of the rule in some other states — and neither A nor B is a dual agent, because neither
personally represents both clients. Under **§ 13278 3** each client, the agency and the appointed
agents possess **only actual knowledge and information**, with **no imputation of knowledge by
operation of law**, so what A knows is not attributed to B or to B's client.

Licensee A owes the seller **confidentiality** under § 13273 1 F as to information that might have a
negative impact on the seller's real estate activity, and the seller's willingness to take less is
squarely that. So **A may not tell B**, and B — who genuinely does not know — tells the buyer that
they do not know and cannot obtain it. Had the same licensee represented both, § 13278 2's exception
would make them a **disclosed dual agent** under § 13275, and the willingness of the seller to accept
less than the asking price is the **first** of the four categories a disclosed dual agent may never
disclose.

## Extended treatment — agency worked through

### E2.1 The four relationships, side by side

| | Transaction broker | Seller agent | Buyer agent | Disclosed dual agent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Source | § 13283 (default under § 13282) | § 13273 | § 13274 | § 13275 |
| Requires | Nothing; it is the presumption | Written brokerage agreement | Written brokerage agreement | Written agreement plus **informed written consent of all parties** |
| Bound by the § 13272 duties | **No** — § 13283 1 | Yes | Yes | Yes, as limited by § 13275 |
| Disclosure standard | **Actual notice or knowledge** of material defects in physical condition, to a buyer | **Actual knowledge or should have known**, of material facts, to the client | Same architecture as § 13273 | Both, as limited |
| Must advise client to get expert advice | No such duty | **Yes** — § 13273 1 B (4) | Yes | Yes |
| May investigate for a party | **No** — § 13283 3 A | Yes, within competence | Yes, within competence | Yes, as limited |
| May verify others' statements | **No** — § 13283 3 B | — | — | — |
| May promote a party's interests | **No** — § 13283 3 C | Yes, for the client | Yes, for the client | **Neither** — the duties are limited |
| Four protected categories | n/a | n/a | n/a | **§ 13275 1 C** |

The table is the module in one page, and the column that surprises people is the first. A transaction
broker is the **default**, owes real duties, and is **prohibited** from doing three things a consumer
will assume they are doing.

### E2.2 Duties illustrated, one at a time

**Loyalty.** A listing agent learns the buyer's agent is a friend and would like to help the deal
close. Loyalty means the seller's interest governs, and any personal interest is disclosed.

**Obedience.** A seller instructs the agent not to present offers below a figure. That instruction
**cannot be followed**: § 13273 1 B (2) requires all offers to be presented in a timely manner even
when the property is under contract. Obedience is to **lawful** instructions.

**Disclosure.** The agent learns from a neighbour that the road association has voted a $9,000 per lot
assessment. Actual knowledge of a material fact concerning the transaction; § 13273 1 B (3) requires
it be disclosed to the client — and the "should have known" limb means the agent cannot avoid the
duty by declining to ask an obvious question.

**Confidentiality.** The seller says they are relocating for a job in six weeks. That could have a
negative impact on the seller's real estate activity, so § 13273 1 F protects it — and § 13281 2 B
keeps protecting it after the agreement ends.

**Reasonable care.** The agent computes a net-proceeds estimate and omits the mortgage prepayment
penalty. § 13273 1 C is engaged, and the fix is to state assumptions and to say what has not been
verified.

**Diligence.** A listing sits unmarketed for five weeks. § 13272 lists diligence as a duty, and
§ 13273 1 A requires performance of the agreement's terms.

**Accounting.** A deposit is held by the agency for four months and never reconciled against the
client's file. § 13273 1 B (5) and § 13178.

### E2.3 What is owed to a customer

A **customer** is a party to the transaction whom the agency does not represent. What is owed:

**Honesty**, and **no knowingly false information** — § 13283 2 E for a transaction broker, and the
same principle throughout the chapter.

**Disclosure of material defects in the physical condition** of the property that are actually known.

**Fair housing compliance**, which runs to everyone.

**Ministerial acts** as agreed — opening a door, providing a form, transmitting a document.

What is **not** owed: loyalty, confidentiality, advocacy, or advice. And saying so plainly, early, in
writing, and with the § 13279 form, prevents most complaints in Maine brokerage.

### E2.4 The conversation that prevents the complaint

A Maine licensee meeting a consumer for the first time has one job before anything else: make the
relationship visible.

*"Before we go any further — I want to be clear about who I work for. Right now I'm not representing
you. In Maine that's the default, and it's called transaction brokerage. Here's the Commission's
disclosure form that explains it. What it means practically is that I'll be honest with you, I'll
tell you about any material defect in the property's condition that I actually know about, and I'll
handle the paperwork — but I won't be advising you on price or strategy, I can't investigate the
property for you, and I can't verify what the seller tells you. If you'd like me to represent you,
that takes a written agreement, and I'd be glad to explain what that would change."*

That paragraph is the whole of §§ 13279, 13282, 13283 2 and 13283 3 in language a consumer can act
on. A licensee who says it in the first ten minutes will rarely have the argument later.

### E2.5 Termination scenarios

**Expiration.** The agreement's stated date arrives — and § 13177-A requires every Maine agreement to
have one. The relationship ends; § 13281 2's two duties survive; and a protection period, if any,
runs but cannot extend beyond **six months** and cannot be enforced against a client who in good
faith engaged another agency after expiration.

**Mutual release.** Both sides agree in writing. Record who released what, and whether any protection
period survives.

**Client termination.** A client may end the relationship; whether they owe a fee is a contract
question. A licensee who argues about it rather than referring it to the designated broker has made a
service problem into a dispute.

**Agency withdrawal.** An agency may withdraw, and must where continuing would require a violation —
a client instructing a fair housing breach being the clearest case.

**Completion.** Performance completes it, and § 13281 1 says so.

**Death or incapacity of the client**, which raises questions of authority and of the estate that go
to counsel.

In every case: the record of **how and when it ended** is what the surviving duties are measured
from, and it belongs in the file.

## Instructor source note for Module 02

Every Maine statement in this module rests on text read directly from `legislature.maine.gov` on
2026-08-20 and recorded in `maine/regulatory/primary-text-readings.md`. Quoted verbatim or in
substance: **§ 13177-A**, **§ 13251-A**, **§ 13272**, **§ 13273 1 A–F**, **§ 13274**, **§ 13275 1–4**,
**§ 13277**, **§ 13278 1–4**, **§ 13279**, **§ 13281 1–2**, **§ 13282**, **§ 13283 1–3**, and
**§ 13179** and **§ 13280** by title.

**Two contrasts are drawn deliberately and are the point of the module.** Maine did **not** abrogate
the agency duties — § 13272 keeps loyalty, obedience, disclosure, confidentiality, reasonable care,
diligence and accounting — where Virginia's § 54.1-2144 abrogates the common law of agency to the
extent inconsistent. And **§ 13278 2 provides that the agency and designated broker are not dual
agents solely because of an appointment**, where Virginia's § 54.1-2139.1 A makes the supervising
broker a dual agent. A licensee moving between the two frameworks must not carry one into the other,
and Hour 12.2 says so.

**Deliberately not stated, for want of a primary reading:** the **content and timing rules for the
relationship disclosure form**, which § 13280 delegates to the Commission's rules; the requirements
of **§ 13277 (written policy)**, **§ 13178 (trust accounts)** and **§ 13179 (supervision)**; and the
**methods of appointment** § 13278 4 delegates to the Commission. Each is cited by title, and the
