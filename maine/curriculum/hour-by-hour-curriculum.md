# Hour-by-hour curriculum and learning objectives

This manuscript is the first-pass student-text backbone. Each unit is paired in the LMS with definitions, an official-source link, an example, an interaction and an instructor-question channel. Statutory/form citations are revalidated before filing and at every controlled content release.

## Hour 1 — Orientation and the Maine real estate industry

**Objectives:** Describe the course gates; identify the major participants and the two sides of a transaction.

- **Lesson:** The Commission regulates licensing, education and minimum practice standards. Agencies and affiliated licensees provide brokerage; appraisers estimate value within their authority; lenders underwrite credit; title professionals address title and settlement; inspectors report condition.
- **Key terms:** qualifying education, listing side, selling side, designated broker, customer, client.
- **Practice:** Place ten transaction events in buyer-side and seller-side order. A licensee's role is determined by law, agreement and disclosed relationship—not by job title alone.

## Hour 2 — Commission mission, authority and discipline

**Objectives:** Distinguish licensing, rulemaking/oversight, investigation and disciplinary functions.

- **Lesson:** Study Commission composition, public protection mission, complaint intake, investigation, hearings and sanctions. A complaint is not itself a finding; due process separates allegation, evidence and final action.
- **Maine law note:** Commission rules, statute, office policy, REALTOR® ethics and MLS rules are different authorities and must not be treated as interchangeable.
- **Practice:** Classify six directives by source and identify which source controls licensure.

## Hour 3 — Licensed activity and license structure

**Objectives:** Recognize brokerage activity and Maine license categories; explain affiliation and supervision.

- **Lesson:** Brokerage includes activities defined by Title 32 and must be performed under the appropriate license and agency structure. A sales agent works through an affiliated agency and designated broker; the course does not authorize independent practice.
- **Key terms:** sales agent, associate broker, broker/designated broker, affiliated licensee, agency umbrella.
- **Practice:** Decide whether each of eight fact patterns presents brokerage, an exempt activity, or an issue to escalate.

## Hour 4 — Brokerage agreements, supervision, records and trust funds

**Objectives:** Identify written-agreement, supervision, record and earnest-money controls.

- **Lesson:** Client relationships require a written brokerage agreement. Designated-broker systems control document review, retention and handling of money. A sales agent follows the agency's lawful procedures and promptly routes funds/documents; the student does not invent a deposit deadline from another state.
- **Regulatory note:** Current Chapter 400 text and the agency policy must be consulted for exact operational deadlines.
- **Practice:** Build an audit trail from receipt of an offer and earnest money through designated-broker review.

## Hour 5 — Minimum standards of practice I

**Objectives:** Apply advertising, self-interest, market-value, net-listing and document-delivery concepts.

- **Lesson:** Advertising must truthfully identify the brokerage context. A licensee discloses self-interest and avoids misleading representations. A sales agent may provide permitted market information but must not misrepresent an appraisal. Net listings are prohibited; parties receive documents as required.
- **Example:** A licensee buying an aunt's property must analyze both the relationship and the licensee's own interest; silence is not a disclosure strategy.
- **Practice:** Repair four deficient advertisements and document the reason for each revision.

## Hour 6 — Minimum standards of practice II

**Objectives:** Explain compensation disclosure, dual/appointed agency procedures, solicitation and relationship disclosure.

- **Lesson:** Compensation does not by itself create agency. Disclosed dual agency and appointed agency depend on informed procedures and written documentation. Solicitation must respect existing relationships and law. Relationship forms explain options; they do not replace the brokerage agreement.
- **Practice:** Select the correct disclosure moment for five consumer encounters.

## Hour 7 — Maine property disclosure

**Objectives:** Distinguish facts, defects and source documents; organize a defensible disclosure inquiry.

- **Lesson:** The licensee helps obtain and communicate required property information without concealing material defects or converting uncertainty into fact. Topics include water, waste disposal, heating, hazardous materials, access and flood risk as reflected in current Maine forms/rules.
- **Source discipline:** Registry, municipality, DEP/LUPC/DOT, service professionals and the owner answer different questions. A tax card is not a boundary survey; a mortgage inspection is not necessarily a boundary survey.
- **Practice:** Complete a source map for a rural property with private well, septic, shared road and flood-zone question.

## Hour 8 — Disclosure lab; agency formation

**Objectives:** Complete a disclosure-quality-control pass; distinguish agency from transaction brokerage.

- **Lesson (minutes 0–30):** Check every disclosure entry for source, date, qualification and unresolved follow-up. “Unknown” is not a license to ignore a red flag.
- **Lesson (minutes 30–60):** Maine presumes transaction brokerage unless an agency relationship is created as required. Agency carries fiduciary duties; transaction brokerage is a non-agency relationship with defined duties.
- **Practice:** Redline a disclosure and label a consumer relationship before and after execution of a client agreement.

## Hour 9 — Fiduciary duties and liability

**Objectives:** Explain loyalty, obedience to lawful instruction, disclosure, confidentiality, accounting and care; recognize vicarious-liability and imputed-knowledge risk.

- **Lesson:** Fiduciary duties shape advice and handling of information. Confidentiality can survive termination. Vicarious liability and imputed knowledge make communication and supervision systems essential.
- **Example:** A buyer's willingness to pay more is confidential unless disclosure is authorized or required by law.
- **Practice:** For eight statements, identify disclose, protect, verify or escalate.

## Hour 10 — Written client agreements

**Objectives:** Identify the parties, services, duration, compensation, consent and termination terms of a brokerage agreement.

- **Lesson:** A written agreement memorializes the client relationship and scope. The licensee explains approved language but does not provide legal advice or rewrite legal forms beyond authority.
- **Practice:** Audit a sample agreement for blank fields, inconsistent dates, missing signatures and ambiguous service scope.

## Hour 11 — Seller, buyer and dual-agency relationships

**Objectives:** Compare seller and buyer representation; recognize when dual-agency procedures are implicated.

- **Lesson:** Seller and buyer agents advocate for different clients within lawful instructions. When the agency represents both sides, disclosed dual-agency safeguards and consent become critical; a licensee cannot solve the conflict by pretending it does not exist.
- **Practice:** Diagram information flows in single-agency and dual-agency files.

## Hour 12 — Appointed agency and company policy

**Objectives:** Explain appointed-agent structure and the role of the agency's written policy.

- **Lesson:** Appointment can designate separate affiliated licensees while the agency manages conflicts under its policy and Maine rules. Appointment is a documented process, not a casual reassignment.
- **Practice:** Identify required escalation points when confidential information is held by different team members.

## Hour 13 — Brokerage Relationships Form

**Objectives:** Explain the form's consumer purpose, timing and relationship to other agreements.

- **Lesson:** The form is an educational disclosure about brokerage choices. It does not, standing alone, substitute for all other agreements. Delivery, discussion and acknowledgment must be documented.
- **Practice:** Role-play a neutral explanation; avoid steering the consumer toward the option most profitable to the licensee.

## Hour 14 — Termination and integrated agency scenarios

**Objectives:** Identify termination events and duties that survive; apply relationship rules to multi-step scenarios.

- **Lesson:** Agency may end by agreement, expiration, completion or other legal event, but confidentiality/accounting obligations can continue. File notes must show when roles changed.
- **Practice:** Resolve three scenarios involving expiration, a new offer and previously learned confidential information.

## Hour 15 — Property classification; Quiz 1

**Objectives:** Distinguish real from personal property and apply fixture analysis; demonstrate MREC/agency learning.

- **Lesson (15 minutes):** Property becomes a fixture through attachment, adaptation and intent considered in context. The contract should resolve items likely to cause dispute.
- **Assessment (45 minutes):** Quiz 1. Missed objectives trigger explanations and assigned review before another progression attempt.

## Hour 16 — Descriptions and surveys

**Objectives:** Recognize metes-and-bounds, lot/plan and rectangular-survey descriptions; distinguish inspection from boundary survey.

- **Lesson:** Legal descriptions identify land differently from street addresses. Bearings, distances, monuments and plan references can reveal inconsistencies that require a survey/title professional.
- **Practice:** Match description fragments to the appropriate system and flag ambiguous calls.

## Hour 17 — Freehold estates and future interests

**Objectives:** Compare fee simple absolute, defeasible estates, life estates, remainder and reversion.

- **Lesson:** The estate defines duration and conditions of ownership. A life tenant's interest and the future interest holder's rights differ; marketing must not promise more title than the owner can convey.
- **Practice:** Draw ownership timelines for three deed clauses.

## Hour 18 — Concurrent and common-interest ownership

**Objectives:** Compare tenancy in common, joint-tenancy concepts, tenancy by the entirety, condominium and cooperative structures.

- **Lesson:** Concurrent owners hold undivided interests under different survivorship/transfer rules. Condominium ownership combines a unit interest with common elements; cooperative occupancy rests on entity ownership and proprietary rights.
- **Practice:** Identify documents a buyer should review for each ownership form.

## Hour 19 — Leasehold estates and lease types

**Objectives:** Distinguish estate for years, periodic tenancy, tenancy at will/sufferance and common lease structures.

- **Lesson:** The leasehold's duration and termination rules affect possession. Gross, net, percentage, ground, index and graduated leases allocate cost and risk differently.
- **Practice:** Translate six clauses into plain-language economic consequences.

## Hour 20 — Maine landlord–tenant foundations

**Objectives:** Recognize habitability, access, security-deposit, late-fee, eviction and abandoned-property issues.

- **Lesson:** Landlord and tenant rights arise from statute and agreement. Self-help eviction and casual handling of deposits create risk. A property manager follows current Maine law and the agency's trust/accounting controls.
- **Practice:** Route five disputes to communicate, document, statutory procedure or legal counsel.

## Hour 21 — Private controls

**Objectives:** Explain deed restrictions, covenants, conditions and association rules.

- **Lesson:** Private controls can burden title even when zoning permits a use. Recorded declarations, bylaws and rules must be evaluated together; the licensee identifies issues and avoids unauthorized legal conclusions.
- **Practice:** Compare a zoning-permitted rental with a covenant-restricted rental.

## Hour 22 — Public controls

**Objectives:** Explain zoning, permits, variances, nonconforming uses, building codes and enforcement.

- **Lesson:** Police power regulates land use. A variance is not the same as a zoning change; a nonconforming use may be constrained by discontinuance or expansion rules.
- **Practice:** Build a municipal due-diligence checklist for a proposed home business.

## Hour 23 — Maine land-use framework

**Objectives:** Recognize subdivision, shoreland zoning/transfer, site-location, natural-resource, minimum-lot-size and LUPC issues.

- **Lesson:** Maine properties may be affected by local and state layers. Unorganized territories involve LUPC. Shoreland and protected-resource questions require current maps, ordinances and agency sources.
- **Practice:** Identify the correct public source for seven land-use questions.

## Hour 24 — Environmental conditions

**Objectives:** Recognize lead paint, arsenic, asbestos, radon, mold, PFAS, wetlands, flood plains, endangered species and water-quality issues.

- **Lesson:** The licensee identifies known facts, required disclosures and need for qualified testing; the licensee does not diagnose. Federal lead rules apply to covered housing; Maine-specific water/wood/flood/access materials must be kept current.
- **Practice:** Draft a neutral recommendation for professional evaluation without minimizing or exaggerating risk.

## Hour 25 — Taxes and transfers

**Objectives:** Recognize property-tax/exemption concepts and voluntary/involuntary alienation.

- **Lesson:** Assessment and market value differ. Homestead, tree-growth and other programs can change tax treatment and may carry conditions. Transfer can occur by deed/will or through foreclosure, eminent domain, adverse possession, escheat or partition.
- **Practice:** Classify ten transfers and identify the professional/source needed to verify title impact.

## Hour 26 — Recording, deeds and title defects

**Objectives:** Explain recording priority concepts; distinguish deed forms/covenants; recognize common title defects.

- **Lesson:** Recording provides public notice and establishes priority under Maine law. Deed labels and covenants allocate assurances differently. Missing heirs, liens, encroachments, boundary disputes and capacity/fraud questions require title/legal resolution.
- **Practice:** Read a deed abstract and list five unanswered title questions.

## Hour 27 — Property-information sources

**Objectives:** Use Registry, municipal, DEP/LUPC/DOT and service-provider sources appropriately.

- **Lesson:** Every source has scope and date limits. Registry data addresses recorded interests; municipal records address tax/zoning/code; environmental agencies address regulated resources; experts address condition.
- **Practice:** Create a source/evidence/date/caveat table for a sample listing.

## Hour 28 — Construction and styles

**Objectives:** Recognize common Maine house styles and basic components; describe observed condition without diagnosing.

- **Lesson:** Ranch, Cape, split, Garrison, manufactured, modular, contemporary and Gambrel labels organize form but do not prove condition. Foundation, sill, joist, stud, rafter, sheathing and flashing perform different functions.
- **Practice:** Label a wall/roof cross-section and rewrite ten overconfident condition statements.

## Hour 29 — Case Study 1; valuation introduction

**Objectives:** Integrate rural/waterfront due diligence; distinguish value types.

- **Case (45 minutes):** The Alder Cove property presents shared access, private water/wastewater, shoreland, flood and title-source issues. Submit the structured source and disclosure plan in `case-studies.md`.
- **Lesson (15 minutes):** Market, assessed, insurance, condemnation and liquidation values answer different questions. A sales agent's market analysis is not automatically an appraisal.

## Hour 30 — Principles of value

**Objectives:** Apply location, condition, contribution, supply/demand, substitution, competition and highest/best-use concepts.

- **Lesson:** Value reflects market behavior. Contribution is measured by market response, not necessarily cost. Highest and best use must be legally permissible, physically possible, financially feasible and maximally productive.
- **Practice:** Explain why a costly improvement may contribute less than it costs.

## Hour 31 — Market-data approach

**Objectives:** Select comparables and reason through adjustments.

- **Lesson:** Compare like with like, verify transaction facts and adjust the comparable—not the subject—to model market differences. Recency, location, condition, rights conveyed and financing terms matter.
- **Practice:** Reconcile three adjusted indications and explain the weight assigned to each.

## Hour 32 — Income approach

**Objectives:** Calculate and interpret capitalization rate, GRM and GIM.

- **Lesson:** Direct capitalization relates net operating income to value; gross multipliers use gross income/rent and require market-derived factors. Mixing gross and net inputs produces a false result.
- **Practice:** Solve five calculations and identify the input that requires verification.

## Hour 33 — Cost approach and depreciation

**Objectives:** Outline cost approach and distinguish physical, functional and external depreciation.

- **Lesson:** Cost approach combines land value with depreciated improvement cost. Physical wear, functional inadequacy/obsolescence and external influences require different evidence.
- **Practice:** Classify ten conditions and explain whether cure cost alone measures market effect.

## Hour 34 — Valuation integration; Quiz 2

**Objectives:** Reconcile approaches and respect appraisal-law boundaries; demonstrate product/valuation mastery.

- **Lesson (15 minutes):** No approach is automatically superior; assignment and data determine relevance. Disclose the nature and limits of any broker price opinion/CMA.
- **Assessment (45 minutes):** Quiz 2 followed by objective remediation.

## Hour 35 — Finance markets and sources

**Objectives:** Distinguish primary/secondary markets and identify common funding sources.

- **Lesson:** Primary lenders originate loans; secondary-market activity supports liquidity and standardization. Brokers, banks, credit unions, mortgage lenders and public programs have different roles.
- **Practice:** Trace a loan from application through sale/servicing without promising a program outcome.

## Hour 36 — Instruments and clauses

**Objectives:** Distinguish note, mortgage, contract for deed and deed of trust; explain acceleration and defeasance concepts.

- **Lesson:** The note evidences debt; the mortgage secures it with real property. Alternative instruments allocate title, possession and default risk differently and require legal advice.
- **Practice:** Match document to function and flag legal-counsel moments.

## Hour 37 — Financing programs

**Objectives:** Compare FHA, VA, conventional, MaineHousing, Rural Development, construction and seller financing at a high level.

- **Lesson:** Programs differ in eligibility, insurance/guarantee, property standards, underwriting and costs. Product terms change; a licensee verifies with lender/current program source and avoids steering based on protected status.
- **Practice:** Prepare neutral questions a buyer can ask a licensed lender.

## Hour 38 — Repayment and qualification

**Objectives:** Compare amortizing, balloon, term, ARM and biweekly structures; calculate points and debt ratios.

- **Lesson:** Amortization changes principal over time; balloon loans leave a balance; ARM rates/payment can change by contract. Qualification ratios are screening concepts, not approval promises.
- **Practice:** Complete point and ratio calculations and label all assumptions.

## Hour 39 — Lender requirements; contract classification

**Objectives:** Organize underwriting/closing requirements and recognize fraud; classify contracts.

- **Lesson (30 minutes):** Insurance, escrow, title, survey/inspection, appraisal, credit, income, collateral and cash-to-close evidence serve different risk decisions. Never assist undocumented side agreements or false application facts.
- **Lesson (30 minutes):** Valid, void, voidable, enforceable/unenforceable, bilateral/unilateral and executed/executory describe different legal characteristics.

## Hour 40 — Contract elements

**Objectives:** Identify competent parties, legal purpose, consideration and mutual assent.

- **Lesson:** Contract formation requires agreement by capable parties for lawful purpose with consideration. Duress, undue influence and incapacity can affect enforceability. Licensees identify concern and refer legal questions.
- **Practice:** Diagnose which element is disputed in eight scenarios.

## Hour 41 — Offer and acceptance

**Objectives:** Trace offer, counteroffer, rejection, revocation, expiration and communicated acceptance.

- **Lesson:** A counteroffer generally changes the proposal and affects the prior offer. Timing and communication are evidence issues. The effective date must be derived from the executed agreement and applicable law—not guessed.
- **Practice:** Build a timestamped offer tree from six communications.

## Hour 42 — Statute of Frauds and termination

**Objectives:** Explain writing requirements at an entry level and identify performance, breach, expiration and unmet-contingency outcomes.

- **Lesson:** Real-estate agreements commonly require writing. A licensee tracks every deadline and notice method. Breach/termination consequences require the agreement and legal advice.
- **Practice:** Convert a contract into a deadline/control calendar.

## Hour 43 — Brokerage and transaction-broker agreements

**Objectives:** Compare seller, buyer and transaction-broker agreements; verify required content.

- **Lesson:** Agreements define role, services, duration, compensation and termination. Client agreements create agency only when statutory/rule requirements are met. Transaction brokerage must not be mislabeled as advocacy.
- **Practice:** Select the appropriate agreement category for six relationship facts.

## Hour 44 — Other real-estate agreements

**Objectives:** Distinguish purchase/sale, lease, option, right of first refusal and installment-sale arrangements.

- **Lesson:** Each agreement allocates rights differently. An option grants a choice within its terms; a right of first refusal depends on a triggering offer/event; installment sale can create substantial financing/title risk.
- **Practice:** Identify which instrument best describes each scenario without drafting legal language.

## Hour 45 — Form completion and unauthorized practice

**Objectives:** Complete factual fields accurately; manage contingencies/addenda; avoid unauthorized legal drafting.

- **Lesson:** Use current approved forms, preserve party language and document advice/referrals. A licensee can fill authorized forms but must not create custom legal solutions beyond competence/authority.
- **Practice:** Quality-control a purchase agreement for dates, property, price, deposits, financing, inspection, title, closing and signatures.

## Hour 46 — Case Study 2; Quiz 3

**Objectives:** Resolve an offer/agency/disclosure file; demonstrate finance/contracts learning.

- **Case (15 minutes):** Submit the Harbor Street decision sequence from `case-studies.md` (prework completed in Hour 45).
- **Assessment (45 minutes):** Quiz 3 with remediation.

## Hour 47 — Case debrief; closing sequence

**Objectives:** Correct the contract file and map responsibilities from contract to recording.

- **Lesson (15 minutes):** Compare the case response to the key; write a corrective file note.
- **Lesson (45 minutes):** Buyer, seller, lenders, title/closing professionals, agency and public registry each perform distinct tasks. The licensee coordinates without practicing law.

## Hour 48 — Costs, proceeds and prorations

**Objectives:** Categorize costs and compute basic prorations.

- **Lesson:** Debits/credits depend on contract and closing convention. Taxes, rents and fuel may be allocated to a date. The student must state the day-count assumption and who owns the day of closing.
- **Practice:** Complete three prorations, then compare to settlement disclosure entries.

## Hour 49 — Title, deeds, affidavits and recording

**Objectives:** Recognize closing/title documents and recording sequence.

- **Lesson:** Deed conveys the stated interest; title insurance protects under policy terms; affidavits support specific facts; transfer tax/recording complete public record steps. Legal/title professionals resolve defects.
- **Practice:** Match twelve documents to purpose and custodian.

## Hour 50 — RESPA/TILA integrated disclosures

**Objectives:** Recognize Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure and compare transaction figures.

- **Lesson:** Federal integrated disclosures provide loan/cost information on covered transactions. The sales agent confirms transaction facts and escalates lender/title questions, but does not reinterpret lender legal obligations.
- **Practice:** Locate loan terms, projected payments, cash to close and seller-paid items in a sample disclosure.

## Hour 51 — Closing readiness; fair housing

**Objectives:** Prepare parties for closing; apply fair-housing principles to sales, rental and advertising.

- **Lesson (15 minutes):** Confirm walk-through, possession, insurance, funds, utilities, keys and settlement review without promising title/funding.
- **Lesson (45 minutes):** Federal and Maine protections prohibit discriminatory practices. Neutral service means consistent criteria, equal information and inclusive advertising; steering and selective availability are prohibited.
- **Practice:** Rewrite four advertisements and compare service scripts for consistency.

## Hour 52 — ADA, antitrust, credit/advertising and marketing controls

**Objectives:** Recognize ADA scope, antitrust conduct, TILA triggering terms, ECOA concerns and contact restrictions.

- **Lesson:** Avoid price fixing, group boycotts, market allocation and tying. Finance advertising can trigger disclosures. Equal-credit rules and fair-housing duties overlap but are distinct. Do-not-call, fax and email rules require current consent/suppression systems.
- **Practice:** Triage eight brokerage-meeting and marketing scenarios.

## Hour 53 — Tax/FIRPTA orientation; final examination begins

**Objectives:** Recognize tax/FIRPTA issues requiring professional handling; synthesize the course.

- **Lesson (15 minutes):** Residential tax exclusions, deductions and FIRPTA withholding are fact-specific and change; licensees flag the issue and refer tax/legal professionals rather than advise.
- **Assessment (45 minutes):** Final Questions 1–22. Navigation is locked to the secure exam environment.

## Hour 54 — Final examination continuation

**Objective:** Demonstrate integrated competency across Product Knowledge through Financing.

- **Assessment:** Final Questions 23–51. The server records item version, answer, elapsed time and integrity events; no feedback reveals secure answers during the attempt.
