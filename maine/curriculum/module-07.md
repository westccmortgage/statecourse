By the end of this module the student will be able to:

  executory or executed.
- Identify the four requirements of a valid contract and apply the statute of frauds to a land

- Trace an offer through revocation, rejection, counteroffer and acceptance and say whether and when
  a contract formed.
- State what § 13177-A requires of a Maine brokerage agreement and distinguish a client agreement
  from a transaction-broker engagement.
- Identify the standard components of a purchase and sale agreement and manage its deadlines.
- Say precisely where completing a form ends and unauthorised practice of law begins.

## Hour 39 (30–60) — Classifications and vocabulary

### 39.5 What a contract is

A **contract** is a legally enforceable agreement. The vocabulary below is examined heavily and is
worth learning precisely rather than approximately.

**Valid** — all requirements met; binding on both parties.
**Void** — no legal effect at all, and never had any. An agreement with an illegal objective.
**Voidable** — valid and operative **unless** the party with the power to avoid it disaffirms. A
minor's contract; a contract induced by fraud.
**Unenforceable** — valid on its face but not enforceable in court. An oral land contract; a claim
barred by limitation. The parties may perform it voluntarily; no court will make them.

**Bilateral** — a promise for a promise. A purchase and sale agreement is bilateral.
**Unilateral** — a promise for an act; the offeree accepts by performing. An open listing and a
reward offer are the classic examples.

**Executory** — something remains to be done. A ratified purchase and sale agreement before closing
is executory.
**Executed** — **fully performed**. Note the trap: the industry says "executed" to mean signed, and
the legal meaning is performed. Examinations use the legal meaning.

**Express** — stated in words. **Implied** — inferred from conduct. Note that in Maine
**§ 13282 forecloses implied client representation entirely**: representation cannot be created by
implication, so an implied agency-client relationship is not available here.

## Hour 40 — The four requirements

### 40.1 Mutual assent

An **offer** and an **acceptance** producing agreement on the essential terms. Assent must be
genuine, and it is undermined by **fraud**, **misrepresentation**, **mutual mistake**, **duress** and
**undue influence** — each of which makes the contract voidable at the injured party's option.

**Fraud** is a knowing misstatement of material fact relied on to the other party's detriment.
**Innocent misrepresentation** is the same statement made without knowledge of its falsity, and it
can still support rescission.

For a licensee, this is where the disclosure duties of Module 02 meet contract law: a seller agent
who passes on a material fact they should have known to be wrong under § 13273 1 B (3), or a
transaction broker who knowingly gives false information contrary to § 13283 2 E, has supplied the
element that makes the contract voidable.

### 40.2 Competent parties

Legal age and legal capacity. A **minor's** contract is generally **voidable by the minor**. A person
adjudicated incompetent cannot contract, and a person lacking capacity at the moment of contracting
produces a voidable contract.

Entities act through authorised signatories; trusts through the trustee; estates through the personal
representative. Whether the signer has authority is a legal question, evidenced by organisational
documents, trust certificates, and letters of appointment, and verified by the closing attorney.

A **power of attorney** must be in the correct form and must be recorded where the transaction
requires it — again a matter for the attorney, and one that must be raised **early**, because a power
of attorney discovered at the closing table is a delayed closing.

### 40.3 Lawful purpose and consideration

**Lawful purpose** — a contract to do something illegal is void and no court will enforce it.

**Consideration** — something of legal value exchanged by each side. Courts examine whether it exists,
not whether it was adequate. In a purchase and sale agreement the mutual promises are the
consideration; the earnest money deposit is evidence of good faith and of ability to perform, not the
consideration itself.

## Hour 41 — Offer, acceptance, and formation

### 41.1 The offer

An **offer** shows present intent to be bound, is communicated to the offeree, and states the
essential terms definitely enough that a court could enforce them — parties, property, price, and the
material terms.

An advertisement is generally **not** an offer; it is an invitation to make one. A listing is not an
offer to sell to whoever appears.

### 41.2 How an offer ends

**Acceptance** — a contract forms.

**Rejection** — the offer is dead and cannot afterwards be accepted.

**Counteroffer** — in law a **rejection plus a new offer**. The moment a seller counteroffers the
buyer's original offer no longer exists, and if the buyer walks away the seller cannot then accept
the original. This is the mechanic students most often get wrong and the one with the most practical
consequence.

**Revocation by the offeror**, effective when communicated, at any time before acceptance — unless
the offeree has paid for an **option**, which is a separate contract holding the offer open.

**Lapse of time**, on the offer's own expiry or after a reasonable time if none is stated.

**Death or incapacity** of either party before acceptance.

**Destruction of the property** before acceptance.

### 41.3 Acceptance and the effective date

Acceptance must be **unqualified** — the mirror image rule — and **communicated** in the manner the
offer requires or permits. Any change of terms is a counteroffer.

**The effective date matters more than anything else in this hour**, because every deadline in the
contract runs from it. Maine residential forms define the effective date expressly, generally by
reference to delivery of the fully signed agreement, and the licensee's job is to read that
definition rather than assume the common-law **mailbox rule** that treats a mailed acceptance as
effective on dispatch. **Read the contract; do not assume.**

### 41.4 Multiple offers

A seller may accept one offer, counter one, counter several, or reject all. **Countering several
simultaneously creates a real risk of two contracts on one property**, and language making acceptance
subject to the seller's further written approval is the answer — which is drafting, and therefore a
legal question.

The licensee's own duty is statutory and unqualified: **§ 13273 1 B (2)** requires a seller agent to
**present in a timely manner all offers to and from the seller, even when the property is subject to
a contract of sale.** Every written offer goes to the client, including one the licensee thinks is
insulting, and an existing ratified contract does not stop the obligation.

Whether the existence or terms of competing offers may be disclosed is a different question,
controlled by the client's instructions and by the **confidentiality** duty in § 13273 1 F. And in a
**disclosed dual agency**, **§ 13275 1 C** forbids disclosure of the seller's willingness to accept
less, the buyer's willingness to pay more, confidential negotiating strategy, and either party's
motivation — which covers most of what a party would want to know in a multiple-offer situation.

## Hour 42 — Statute of frauds, performance, breach, and contingencies

### 42.1 The statute of frauds

A contract for the sale of an interest in real property must be **in writing and signed by the party
to be charged**. An oral agreement to sell land is unenforceable however clear the evidence of it.

The same principle runs through Maine brokerage: **§ 13177-A** requires a brokerage agreement to be
**in writing**, and **§ 13282** provides that client representation may not be created orally.

### 42.2 Contingencies

A **contingency** makes a duty conditional on a stated event. Three things can happen to it:

**Satisfaction** — the event occurs and the duty becomes absolute.
**Waiver** — the party it protects gives it up, in writing.
**Failure with a stated consequence** — the event does not occur within the period and the contract
says what follows, usually a right to terminate on written notice within a stated time.

And the dangerous fourth: **the period expires and nobody acts.** Under many forms a contingency not
exercised in time is deemed satisfied or waived, and the protection is simply gone. This is why
deadline management is a professional skill rather than an administrative chore.

The recurring contingencies in a Maine transaction: **financing**; **appraisal**; **inspection**,
which in Maine routinely includes **water quality on a private well**, **radon in air and water**,
**septic inspection**, **wood-destroying insects**, and on older housing a **lead** assessment;
**title**; **sale of the buyer's current property**; and **association documents** where the property
is in a condominium.

### 42.3 Performance, breach, and remedies

A contract is discharged by **performance**, by **mutual rescission**, by **novation** — substituting
a new contract or party with everyone's consent — by **operation of law**, or by **breach**.

A **material breach** defeats the purpose of the contract; a trivial failure is not one, and calling
it a breach is itself a breach.

Remedies: **damages**; **liquidated damages**, commonly the earnest money deposit; **rescission**;
and **specific performance**, available in land contracts because every parcel is unique and money is
treated as an inadequate substitute.

**Time is of the essence** clauses make deadlines strict. Where the clause is present, a day late is
a breach.

**Whether a party is in default, and what remedy follows, are legal conclusions.** A licensee who
says "they've breached, keep the deposit" has given legal advice, and if wrong has caused a loss. The
answer is: this needs your attorney, today, and here is the deadline we are working against.

### 42.4 The deposit

Earnest money held by an agency is **trust money** under § 13178 and the designated broker is
responsible for it. A **disputed deposit is never resolved by the licensee choosing a side**; it goes
to the designated broker and, where necessary, to counsel, and it is released only as the contract
and the law permit.

## Hour 43 — Brokerage agreements and transaction-broker engagements

### 43.1 The client agreement — § 13177-A

Written, and containing at a minimum: **(A)** the signature of the client to be charged; **(B)** the
terms and conditions of the services to be provided; **(C)** the method or amount of compensation;
**(D)** **the date upon which the agreement will expire**; and **(E)** **a statement that the
agreement creates an agency-client relationship**.

Plus the two protections: unenforceable against a client who **in good faith engages another agency
after the expiration date**, and **no fee tail beyond six months** after expiration.

Element **E** is the hinge. Under **§ 13282** the agency is presumed to be a transaction broker;
element E is the written agreement saying, in terms, that representation exists.

### 43.2 Seller and buyer agreements

A **seller brokerage agreement** — a listing — engages the agency to market and to seek a sale on the
seller's terms, and triggers the § 13273 duties. It should record the property's inclusions and
exclusions, the price and terms, the marketing authorised, whether the property is occupied by a
tenant, and the seller's authority to sell.

A **buyer brokerage agreement** engages the agency to seek property and triggers the § 13274 duties.
Its recurring negotiating points are the geographic and property scope, the term, the compensation
and how it is sourced, and whether it is exclusive.

Both must satisfy § 13177-A, and in an agency practising **appointed agency** the client must have
been **informed of the appointed agent policy and consented to it in writing in advance** —
§ 13278 4.

### 43.3 The transaction-broker engagement

Where no written brokerage agreement exists, **§ 13282** makes the agency a **transaction broker**,
and § 13283 supplies the content: account for money and property; **disclose to a buyer all material
defects in the physical condition of which the broker has actual notice or knowledge**; comply with
the brokerage laws and Commission rules; comply with fair housing law; **treat all parties honestly
and not knowingly give false information**; and perform agreed ministerial acts.

And the three prohibitions: **no inspection, investigation or analysis for a party's benefit**; **no
verification of the accuracy or completeness of anyone's statements**; and **no promotion of either
party's interests**.

A transaction broker may still agree the terms of their engagement in writing, and should — because
the § 13283 3 prohibitions mean a consumer who expects advocacy will be disappointed, and the time to
say so is at the start.

### 43.4 The disclosure that precedes everything

**§ 13279** requires the agency to provide buyers and sellers of residential real property — **one to
four dwelling units** — a **meaningful, written relationship disclosure form**, in a timely manner,
as the Commission's rules define and mandate. It is a disclosure, not an agreement, and it creates
nothing.

## Hour 44 — Purchase and sale agreements and related contracts

### 44.1 The components of a Maine purchase and sale agreement

**Parties**, in their full legal capacity. **Property**, by address and reference to the deed, with
**inclusions and exclusions** — where the fixture work of Hour 15 lands, and where leased propane
tanks and rented water-treatment equipment must be identified.

**Price and deposit** — the amount, who holds it, when it is delivered, and what happens to it in
every way the contract can end.

**Financing** — cash or financed, the type and amount, and the **financing contingency** period.

**Closing and possession** — the closing date, who conducts closing, and when possession transfers.
The two are separate, and any gap needs its own written agreement.

**Title** — the quality of title the seller must convey, the buyer's right to examine and object, the
objection period, and the seller's right and obligation to cure.

**Inspections** — which investigations, in what period, at whose cost, and what remedies follow.

**Risk of loss** between agreement and closing.

**Default and remedies**, and how the deposit is treated.

**Notices** — how notice is given and when effective. This clause decides disputes about whether a
deadline was met.

**Time provisions** — whether time is of the essence and how days are counted.

**Disclosures** — the **33 M.R.S. § 173 property disclosure statement** and the federal lead-based
paint package for pre-1978 target housing.

**Addenda**, each referenced and attached.

### 44.2 Leases, options, and rights of first refusal

A **lease** is a conveyance and a contract, covered in Module 03.

An **option** is a contract in which the optionor holds an offer open for a stated period in exchange
for consideration. It is unilateral until exercised: the optionee may buy but is not obliged to.
Consideration must actually pass, and the option should be recorded to bind successors.

A **right of first refusal** is different and is frequently confused with an option. It gives the
holder the right to match a **bona fide offer** the owner receives, and it is triggered by that
offer rather than exercisable at will. Rights of first refusal appear in Maine condominium documents,
in family land arrangements, and in shorefront covenants, and a listing licensee who discovers one
must route it to counsel immediately — because marketing a property subject to an unexercised right
of first refusal without addressing it produces a contract that may not be performable.

A **lease with option to purchase** combines the two; **lease-purchase** obliges the tenant to buy.
The difference is whether the tenant may or must, and it changes everything about the arrangement.

### 44.3 Installment sale and contract for deed

An **installment land contract** or **bond for a deed** leaves legal title with the seller until
payment is complete while the buyer takes possession and equitable title. Maine regulates these, and
this course states no requirement from that regime because it has not been read from primary text for
this project. A licensee who meets one routes it to counsel before anything is signed.

## Hour 45 — Form completion, and the line

### 45.1 What a licensee may do with a form

A Maine sales agent may **complete a standard form** — filling in the parties, property, price,
dates, contingencies and agreed terms as the parties have instructed — and may explain what the form
provides in ordinary language.

That is what §§ 13273 and 13274 contemplate a licensee doing, and refusing to help a client complete
a standard form would be a failure of the diligence duty in § 13272.

### 45.2 What a licensee may not do

**Draft bespoke legal language** to solve a legal problem. **Advise on the legal effect** of a term.
**Advise whether a party is in default** or what remedy is available. **Prepare a deed.** **Give
advice on title, tax, or the structure of ownership.**

The distinction is between **filling in a form the parties have agreed** and **deciding what the law
does**. A licensee who writes a new clause into a contract to solve a problem has crossed it, however
sensible the clause.

Under **§ 13273 1 B (4)** the seller agent is required to **advise the client to obtain expert advice
on material matters beyond the seller agent's expertise** — so the statute itself supplies the exit.
The professional sentence is: *this is beyond what I can advise on; here is who can, and here is the
deadline we are working against.*

### 45.3 Addenda, amendments, and version control

An **addendum** is attached at the time of contracting and forms part of the original agreement. An
**amendment** changes an existing contract and needs the assent of all parties. A **contingency** is
neither; it is a condition.

Every change is initialled and dated by everyone. Every addendum referenced in the agreement is
actually attached. And where several signed counterparts circulate by email, **version control** is a
real risk: the question of which document is the contract must never be genuinely open.

### 45.4 Electronic signature

Electronic records and signatures are effective for real estate contracts under federal **E-SIGN**
and state adoption of the **Uniform Electronic Transactions Act**, provided the parties consented to
transact electronically. The professional issues are practical: recorded consent, identity
verification by the platform, preservation of the audit trail, version control, and — the risk that
actually causes losses — **wire fraud**, where an intercepted email carries changed closing
instructions. Module 08 covers it.

## Hour 46 (0–15) — Case Study 2

### 46.1 The facts

*A Maine agency holds a written brokerage agreement with a seller, signed 2 May, expiring 2 November,
with a 90-day protection period. The property is a 1971 house with a private well and septic system.
On 14 August, Buyer A — who has no written agreement with anyone — offers $412,000 with a financing
contingency and an inspection period. The seller counters at $427,000. While the counteroffer is
outstanding, Buyer B, represented by another agency, offers $424,000 with no inspection contingency.
Buyer A telephones to say they accept the counteroffer but has not delivered a signed copy. The
seller now prefers Buyer B.*

### 46.2 The analysis the case requires

**Buyer A is not a client of the listing agency.** § 13282 — no written brokerage agreement, and
representation cannot arise orally or by implication. The agency is a **transaction broker** as to
Buyer A, owing the § 13283 2 duties and bound by the § 13283 3 prohibitions, and the § 13279
relationship disclosure form should have been given long before the offer.

**Whether a contract with Buyer A has formed** turns on the contract's own definition of the
effective date — most Maine residential forms require delivery of the fully signed agreement, so an
oral acceptance probably has not formed a contract. That is close enough to a legal conclusion that
the correct step is **counsel**, not a licensee's ruling.

**Buyer B's written offer must be presented to the seller promptly.** § 13273 1 B (2) requires all
offers to be presented in a timely manner **even when the property is subject to a contract of sale**
— so the obligation stands whether or not a contract with Buyer A exists.

**The 1971 construction** makes this **target housing**: the federal lead-based paint disclosure,
records, pamphlet, contract acknowledgments, and the **ten-day** assessment opportunity, plus
§ 173(4)(B) in the Maine statement.

**The private well and septic** require the § 173(1) and § 173(3) disclosures, and Buyer B's waiver
of the inspection contingency is a decision with real consequences on a property with both — which
the seller's licensee must not characterise for Buyer B, because § 13283 3 B bars a transaction
broker from verifying anyone's statements and § 13283 3 C bars promoting either party's interests.

**Confidentiality.** Anything the seller has told the listing agency about their willingness to take
less is confidential under § 13273 1 F and stays confidential after the agreement ends under
§ 13281 2 B.

## Hour 47 (0–15) — Debrief and contract-file quality control

### 47.1 The file audit

At ratification, a Maine transaction file should contain and a licensee should be able to check
without prompting:

The **§ 13279 relationship disclosure form**, delivered and recorded, for every buyer and seller of
one-to-four-unit residential property.
The **appointed agent policy** consent, in writing and in advance, where the agency practises
appointed agency.
The **§ 13177-A brokerage agreement**, signed by the client to be charged, with the expiration date
and the statement that it creates an agency-client relationship.
The **§ 13275 disclosed dual agency consent**, or the **§ 13278 written appointments**, where the
agency is on both sides.
The **33 M.R.S. § 173 property disclosure statement**.
The **federal lead-based paint package** for pre-1978 target housing, with the signed acknowledgment.
**Every written offer and counteroffer**, with date and time and evidence of presentation.
The **ratified agreement** with every addendum referenced and attached, initialled where changed.
The **deposit** receipt and evidence of delivery to the holder the agreement names.
The **deadline map**, from the effective date.

### 47.2 The deadline map

Built from the **effective date as the contract defines it**, after reading the day-counting rule and
the notice provision. Every deadline in date order, each with **who acts, what document is required,
and what happens if it passes**: deposit delivery; loan application; inspection period and the notice
that must follow it; water, radon and septic testing; lead assessment period; appraisal; title
examination and objection; seller cure; loan commitment; Closing Disclosure delivery; walk-through;
closing.

Internal reminders sit several days ahead of each. **A deadline discovered on the day it falls is a
deadline already partly lost.**

### Module 07 engagement check

**Question.** A buyer client asks you to add a clause to their offer that "protects us if the septic
turns out to be too small for four bedrooms". You know what they mean and you could write a sentence
that says it. What do you do?

**Expected answer.** I do not write it.

Completing a standard form as the parties have agreed is licensed activity; **drafting bespoke
language to solve a legal problem is not**, however sensible the sentence would be. A clause
allocating risk on septic capacity is exactly that — it has to define what "too small" means, what
evidence establishes it, what the remedy is, who bears the cost, and how notice is given, and every
one of those is a legal question with a legal consequence.

What I do instead: identify the **standard contingency** the form already provides that covers the
investigation — the inspection contingency, which in Maine routinely extends to a **septic
inspection** — and make sure the period, the notice requirement and the remedy are understood and
diarised. Establish what the buyer actually needs to know, which is the **septic design on file with
the local plumbing inspector** showing how many bedrooms the system is permitted for, and get that
requested immediately. And, because this is a material matter beyond my expertise, **§ 13273 1 B (4)**
requires me to **advise the client to obtain expert advice** — so the drafting question goes to their
attorney, in writing, with the deadline stated.

## Extended treatment — contracts developed

### E7.1 A purchase and sale agreement, read clause by clause

What each clause allocates, and what a licensee checks in it:

**Parties.** Full legal names and the capacity in which each signs. Check against the deed and the
property card. A trust, an estate, an LLC, or a party acting under a power of attorney each need
authority evidenced, and the time to discover that is now.

**Property.** Address, deed reference, map and lot, and the **inclusions and exclusions**. Check that
leased propane tanks, rented water treatment, and anything the seller intends to remove are named.

**Price and deposit.** Amount, who holds it, when it is delivered, and its treatment in every way the
contract can end. Check the delivery deadline has not already passed by the time the agreement is
signed — a recurring error in a multiple-offer week.

**Financing.** Type, amount, and the contingency period. Check that the period ends **before**
closing and that the loan type matches what the buyer's lender is actually processing.

**Closing and possession.** Two separate events. Check that any gap between them is addressed by a
written occupancy agreement.

**Title.** The quality of title required, the buyer's examination and objection period, and the
seller's cure right and obligation. Check that the objection period allows time for the commitment to
arrive.

**Inspections.** Which investigations, in what period, at whose cost, and what remedy follows. In
Maine check that the period is long enough for **water testing, radon in air and water, and a septic
inspection** — laboratory turnaround alone can consume a short period.

**Risk of loss** between agreement and closing, and who insures.

**Default and remedies**, and the deposit's treatment.

**Notices.** How notice is given and when effective. This clause decides deadline disputes, and
"email to the agent" and "written notice delivered to the party" are not the same requirement.

**Time provisions.** Whether time is of the essence, how days are counted, and what happens when a
deadline falls on a weekend or a holiday.

**Disclosures.** The § 173 statement and the federal lead-based paint package.

**Addenda**, referenced and actually attached.

### E7.2 Issue-spotting without giving legal advice

A licensee reviewing an agreement is looking for **incompleteness and inconsistency**, not legal
meaning. What that means concretely:

**Blanks not filled.** **Dates that contradict one another** — a financing contingency expiring after
closing, an inspection period ending before the agreement was signed. **Parties named inconsistently**
between the first page and the signature block. **Addenda referenced but not attached.** **Initials
missing** where a change was made. **A deposit deadline already past.** **A closing date that does
not allow for the Closing Disclosure delivery period.** **An inspection period that cannot
accommodate laboratory turnaround.**

Every one of those is within a licensee's competence and is what the reasonable-skill-and-care duty
in § 13273 1 C requires. **What a clause means, and what a party should do about a breach, are not.**

### E7.3 The deadline map, built

From the **effective date as the contract defines it**, after reading the day-counting rule and the
notice provision:

| # | Deadline | Who acts | Document required | If it passes |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Deposit delivery | Buyer | Cleared funds to the named holder | Seller may have a remedy |
| 2 | Loan application | Buyer | Application; Loan Estimate follows | Financing contingency at risk |
| 3 | Inspection period ends | Buyer | Report; **written notice** if acting | Contingency may be deemed satisfied or waived |
| 4 | Water and radon results | Laboratory | Test reports | May outrun the inspection period |
| 5 | Septic inspection | Buyer's inspector | Report; permit from the plumbing inspector | Same |
| 6 | Lead assessment period | Buyer | Federal ten-day opportunity | Waived if unused |
| 7 | Appraisal | Lender | Appraisal report | Reconsideration window closes |
| 8 | Title objection | Buyer | Written objection | Objection right may be lost |
| 9 | Seller cure period | Seller | Curative instruments | Buyer's remedy arises |
| 10 | Loan commitment | Lender | Written commitment | Financing contingency terminates |
| 11 | Closing Disclosure delivery | Lender | Received by borrower | Closing date moves |
| 12 | Walk-through | Buyer | Completed checklist | Condition claims weaken |
| 13 | Closing | All | Signed and funded | Default analysis begins |

Internal reminders sit several days ahead of each, and the map is reviewed weekly rather than
consulted when something goes wrong.

### E7.4 What happens when a contingency is not exercised

The single most expensive habit in transaction management is treating a deadline as a date on which
something *may* be done rather than a date after which a right is *gone*.

*A buyer's inspection reveals a failing septic system on day 9 of a 10-day inspection period. The
buyer says they want to "think about it over the weekend".*

The correct professional action is to state, in writing, exactly what the contingency permits, exactly
when it expires, and exactly what notice the contract requires — and to make clear that if the period
passes without the required notice, the protection may be gone under the contract's own terms. The
licensee does **not** advise whether to terminate, whether the seller is in default, or whether the
deposit is returnable; those are legal conclusions and § 13273 1 B (4) requires the client to be
advised to obtain expert advice on a material matter beyond the agent's expertise.

*An extension* is a written **amendment** requiring the assent of all parties. It is not created by
the other agent saying "that's fine".

### E7.5 Case Study 2 debrief — the file at ratification

The case is graded on the file. At ratification it must show:

The **§ 13279 relationship disclosure form** to each of Buyer A and Buyer B, delivered and recorded.
Evidence that **every written offer and counteroffer was presented promptly**, with date and time —
§ 13273 1 B (2).
The **§ 13177-A brokerage agreement** with the seller, in date and satisfying all five components.
The **federal lead-based paint package** for the 1971 house, with the signed acknowledgment.
The **§ 173 property disclosure statement**, with its blanks identified.
The **deposit** receipt and evidence of delivery.
A record showing that the **effective-date question was referred to counsel** rather than decided by
the licensee.
And **no record of the licensee characterising** the consequences of Buyer B's waiver of the
inspection contingency to Buyer B — because § 13283 3 B and 3 C prohibit exactly that.

### E7.6 Contract file quality control, as a checklist

Run before the file is closed:

Every page present and every referenced addendum attached. Every change initialled and dated by every
party. The version in the file matching the version everyone signed. Every deadline in the map, with
its outcome recorded. Every notice given, with the method and the date. Every deposit accounted for.
Every disclosure delivered, with evidence. The relationship documented from first contact to
termination. And the correspondence — because in a dispute the correspondence is the file.
