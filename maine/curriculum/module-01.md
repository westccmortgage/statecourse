## Hour 01 — Part A: how this course works

### 1.1 What the course is, and what it is not

This is the **Sales Agent Course** required of an applicant for a Maine real estate sales agent
licence. It is 55 clock hours: **3,000 minutes of instruction** and **300 minutes of assessment**,
which is 9.09 percent of the course and therefore within the Commission's ceiling on assessment
time.

Two things it is not.

**It is not a licence.** Completing the course qualifies a person to sit the licensing examination.
The licence comes from the Commission on application, after examination, and on the Commission's
own terms.

**It is not, at the moment you are reading this, an approved course.** This version is a submission
draft. Chapter 360 of the Commission's rules bars a provider from advertising a course as approved,
enrolling students for licensing credit, or conducting classes for credit before written approval
is issued. Every page of this package says so, and no completion of this version carries credit.

### 1.2 Grading and completion

The course carries **three quizzes of twenty objective questions**, delivered after Hours 15, 34 and
46, and a **final examination of eighty questions** delivered across Hours 53, 54 and 55 after all
instruction is complete.

**The passing grade is 75 percent, and that figure is the Commission's, not this school's.** It
comes from the Commission's rules and its published Sales Agent Course guideline. Where you meet a
figure in this course, the course will tell you whose figure it is — because a school that presents
its own standard as the regulator's has misled the student about what the law requires.

Completion requires the instructional minutes, the case studies, the quizzes, and the final. The
LMS records assessment minutes separately from instructional minutes; **no assessment minute counts
toward a topic allocation**, which is why the arithmetic at the head of this module separates them.

### 1.3 Academic integrity, and why it protects you

The work must be the enrolled student's own. Identity verification exists to protect every honest
student's certificate: a certificate that anyone could obtain by proxy is worth nothing to the
people who earned theirs.

Assistance during an assessment, sharing items, and substitution of another person invalidate the
attempt and are reportable to the Commission.

### 1.4 How to use the primary sources

One habit is worth more than any mnemonic in this course: **read the section, not a summary of it.**

Maine publishes the Revised Statutes free and current at `legislature.maine.gov`, and the
Commission publishes its rules and its course guideline at the Department of Professional and
Financial Regulation. Title 32, chapter 114 is the Real Estate Brokerage License Act. Subchapter 7,
§§ 13271 through 13283, is brokerage relationships and is the part of the Act a sales agent uses
most.

This course cites the section behind every Maine rule it states. Where a rule has not been read
from primary text, the course says so and tells you where to look rather than giving you a number
to memorise that may be wrong.

## Hour 01 — Part B: how the industry is organised

### 1.5 The participants

A Maine transaction typically involves:

**The seller and the buyer**, who are the principals.

**A real estate brokerage agency**, which is the licensed business entity. Maine licenses the
agency itself under subchapter 3 of chapter 114.

**A designated broker**, who is the individual broker responsible for the agency — for its records
under § 13184, for its trust accounts under § 13178, and for supervision of employees under
§ 13179.

**Affiliated licensees** — brokers, associate brokers and sales agents — who conduct brokerage
activity on the agency's behalf.

**A lender**, and often a mortgage broker or loan originator, separately regulated.

**An attorney**, who in Maine practice frequently conducts the closing and who prepares the deed.

**A title company or closing agent**, and a **title insurer**.

**An appraiser**, licensed under a separate chapter — Title 32, chapter 124 — and note § 13251-A,
which restricts a broker's own opinion of market value where the agency is to be paid a fee on the
transaction.

**Inspectors and specialists** — home inspectors, well and septic evaluators, radon and air-quality
testers, surveyors, and in Maine very often a shoreland or code enforcement officer.

**Municipal officers**, including the code enforcement officer, the assessor, and the plumbing
inspector, whose records answer a large share of Maine due-diligence questions.

### 1.6 The listing side and the selling side

Brokerage traditionally divides into a **listing side**, working with the seller, and a **selling
side**, working with the buyer. In Maine both sides need care with the vocabulary, because
"working with" is not "representing".

**§ 13282 makes transaction brokerage the presumption.** A Maine agency providing brokerage
services is presumed to be acting as a **transaction broker** unless it has agreed, **in a written
brokerage agreement**, to represent one or more parties as clients — and **client representation
may not be created orally or by implication**, nor assumed by the agency or by any party.

That single sentence is the most important structural fact in Maine brokerage, and Module 02 is
built on it. Carry it from Hour 1: **in Maine, nobody is a client until it is written down.**

### 1.7 What a sales agent may do, and under whom

A **real estate sales agent** — § 13200 — is licensed to conduct brokerage activity **on behalf of
and under the supervision of** an agency and its designated broker. A sales agent does not hold an
independent licence, does not operate an agency, and does not supervise.

An **associate broker** — § 13199 — holds a broker-level licence but works under a designated
broker. A **real estate broker** — § 13198 — may be a designated broker and may operate an agency.

Two boundaries a sales agent must know on day one. First, **the licence is a licence to broker real
estate**, not to give legal, tax, engineering, appraisal, or lending advice. Second, the sales
agent's authority is **derived** — from the agency's licence and the designated broker's
supervision — and never original.

## Hour 02 — The Commission: policy, composition, powers, and discipline

### 2.1 Why the Commission exists

**§ 13061** is the declaration of policy: the chapter exists to protect the public. That framing
decides how ambiguous questions are resolved throughout the Act, and it is why the disciplinary
provisions reach conduct that harms consumers rather than conduct that merely embarrasses the
profession.

### 2.2 Organisation and powers

**§ 13062** establishes the Real Estate Commission and its organisation. **§ 13065** gives it
rulemaking power — the source of Chapter 360, of the Commission's brokerage relationship form
requirements under § 13280, and of the appointed-agent rules § 13278 4 requires.

**§ 13069** provides for the Director, who administers the Commission's work, whose authorised
representative may inspect an agency's records under § 13184, and to whom complaints are made.

**Sections that have been repealed** and must not be cited: § 13063 compensation, § 13064 seal and
records, § 13066 hearings, § 13067 causes for disciplinary action. Older Maine course material
frequently still cites § 13067; the operative section is **§ 13067-A**.

### 2.3 Discipline — § 13067-A

**§ 13067-A** governs denial or refusal to renew a licence and disciplinary action. The Commission
acts under the Maine Administrative Procedure Act, and **§ 13068** governs decisions.

**§ 13005** provides penalties and **§ 13004** provides for civil actions; **§ 13003** is the
licence requirement itself, and unlicensed practice is what it reaches.

**§ 13006** deals with confidentiality of the Commission's own information.

This course states no specific ground, sanction, or penalty amount from § 13067-A, because the
section has not been read line by line for this project. What a student needs to carry is the
shape: complaints go to the Director; the Commission investigates; the licensee is given notice and
an opportunity to respond; the matter is resolved administratively or after hearing; and the
sanctions run from a warning through suspension to revocation, with civil penalties available.

**What a licensee does when a complaint arrives** is the same everywhere: tell the designated
broker immediately; preserve every record and change nothing; do not contact the complainant to
argue; respond within the time given; and obtain counsel.

### 2.4 Rule, ethics, office policy, and MLS — four different things

Students conflate these constantly, and Hour 8 returns to it as a laboratory. The distinction is
worth stating early:

**A statute or Commission rule** is law. Violating it is a disciplinary matter and may be a legal
one.

**A code of ethics** belongs to a private trade association. It binds members of that association
and it is enforced by that association. It is not Maine law, and the Commission does not enforce
it.

**Office policy** is the agency's own rule. § 13277 requires an agency to have a **written policy**,
and the designated broker sets it. Breaking it is an employment matter unless it also breaks a rule.

**MLS rules** belong to a multiple listing service, which is a private cooperative. They bind
participants and are enforced by the MLS.

All four can apply to the same act. Only the first is the law of Maine.

## Hour 03 — Licensed activity, licence categories, and supervision

### 3.1 What requires a licence

**§ 13003** requires a licence to conduct real estate brokerage activity. **§ 13001** defines the
terms, and **§ 13002** sets out the exceptions to brokerage — the categories of person who may do
things that would otherwise require a licence.

The everyday consequence for a sales agent is that **unlicensed assistants may not do licensed
work**. An unlicensed person may place a sign, book an appointment, prepare marketing material
under supervision, and handle administration. An unlicensed person may not negotiate terms, give
information that amounts to advice about a transaction, or conduct a showing in a way that
substitutes for the licensee.

### 3.2 The agency licence

Maine licenses the **business**, not only the people. Subchapter 3 governs: **§ 13171** the agency
itself, **§ 13172** the original application, **§ 13173** qualifications, **§ 13174** denial,
**§ 13175** agency changes, **§ 13176** trade names, **§ 13181** contents and display of the
licence, **§ 13182** renewal, and **§ 13183** the acts the agency is authorised to perform.

**§ 13180** governs termination of employment — the mechanics of a licensee leaving an agency, which
matters because a sales agent's authority is derived from the agency's.

### 3.3 The individual licences

**Sales agent — § 13200.** Works on behalf of and under the supervision of the agency.

**Associate broker — § 13199.** Broker-level qualification, working under a designated broker.

**Broker — § 13198.** May serve as designated broker and operate an agency.

**§ 13191** sets the general qualifications; **§ 13193** deals with nonresidents and **§ 13193-A**
with licensure by endorsement; **§ 13194** renewal; **§ 13195** changes; **§ 13196** inactive
licences; **§ 13197** continuing education.

**This course states no education hour count, fee, or renewal deadline.** Those sit in § 13007, in
§ 13197, and in the Commission's rules and fee schedule; they change, and a student's own
obligations must be read from the current publication rather than from a course.

### 3.4 Supervision — § 13179

**§ 13179** requires supervision of employees, and it is the designated broker's obligation.
Supervision is not a formality: it is the mechanism by which the agency's licence stands behind
everything an affiliated licensee does.

For a sales agent the practical content is: know who your designated broker is; know what the
agency's **written policy** under § 13277 says, including its appointed agent policy; escalate
anything you are unsure of before you act rather than after; and never sign, promise, or agree to
something the policy does not permit you to.

## Hour 04 — Brokerage agreements, records, and trust accounts

### 4.1 Brokerage agreements — § 13177-A

**§ 13177** is repealed. The operative section is **§ 13177-A**, and it is short enough to know
completely.

A brokerage agreement between an agency and a client **must be in writing** and must at a minimum
include:

**A.** The **signature of the client to be charged**.
**B.** The **terms and conditions** of the brokerage services to be provided.
**C.** The **method or amount of compensation** to be paid.
**D.** The **date upon which the agreement will expire**.
**E.** **A statement that the agreement creates an agency-client relationship.**

Then two limits that protect the consumer:

> A brokerage agreement may not be enforced against any client who **in good faith subsequently
> engages the services of another real estate brokerage agency following the expiration date of the
> first brokerage agreement.**

> Any brokerage agreement provision extending a real estate brokerage agency's right to a fee
> following expiration of the brokerage agreement **may not extend that right beyond 6 months.**

Three things follow that a sales agent uses constantly. **Maine requires an expiration date in
every agreement** — there is no open-ended listing. **A fee tail is capped at six months.** And
**element E is the link back to § 13282**: the written statement that the agreement creates an
agency-client relationship is what converts the transaction-broker presumption into representation.

**§ 13177-B** separately addresses unfair agreements to list residential real estate.

### 4.2 Records — § 13184

> A designated broker shall maintain **complete and adequate records** of all real estate brokerage
> activity conducted on behalf of the broker's agency. The commission shall specify by rule the
> records required to establish complete and adequate records, **including retention schedules**.
> The records must be **open for inspection by the director or the director's authorized
> representative at the agency's place of business during generally recognized business hours**.

Note what the statute does **not** do: it does not state a retention period. The Commission
specifies it by rule, so **this course states no number of years** and directs the licensee to the
Commission's current rules. A course that gives a retention figure it has not read is exactly the
failure this project's source rule exists to prevent.

What a sales agent contributes to the agency's records is the file: the written brokerage agreement,
the relationship disclosure, every offer and counteroffer with its date, the property disclosure
statement, the communications, and the notes of what was said and when.

### 4.3 Trust accounts — § 13178

**§ 13178** governs trust accounts, and the designated broker is responsible for them.

The concepts a sales agent must hold, whatever the rule's detail:

**Client and customer money is not the agency's money.** Earnest money, deposits, and rents held by
the agency are held in trust.

**Commingling** — mixing trust money with the agency's operating funds — and **conversion** — using
trust money for any other purpose — are the two failures the whole scheme exists to prevent.

**Deposits must be delivered to the person who holds them, in the time the agreement and the rules
require.** A sales agent who carries a cheque in a bag over a weekend has created a problem.

**Disputed deposits are never resolved by the licensee choosing a side.** They go to the designated
broker and, where necessary, to counsel.

The specific requirements — where the account is held, what it is called, how quickly funds must be
deposited, and how disbursement is authorised — are in § 13178 and in the Commission's rules, and
must be read there.

## Hour 05 — Minimum standards of practice

### 5.1 Advertising

Advertising must be truthful and must not mislead. Maine's rules require advertising to identify the
agency, and the designated broker is responsible for what the agency's licensees publish.

The recurring failures are the same in every state: a listing left live after it has gone under
contract; a price no longer available; a photograph that no longer shows the property's condition;
a licensee's personal branding that obscures the agency; and language that expresses a preference
about who should live somewhere, which is a fair housing violation before it is an advertising one.

### 5.2 Self-interest and disclosure of the licensee's own position

A licensee buying or selling for their own account, or for a family member, or for an entity they
have an interest in, must disclose that position. The rule exists because a consumer dealing with a
licensee is entitled to know when the licensee is on the other side of the table rather than beside
them.

### 5.3 Documents

A licensee must give the parties copies of what they sign, when they sign it. A file in which the
client has never seen the signed version of their own agreement is a file that will not survive a
complaint.

### 5.4 Opinions of market value — § 13251-A

This is the Maine rule most often missed, and it is short:

> A real estate broker or associate broker **may not knowingly provide or offer an appraisal or
> opinion of market value**, as set forth in section 14004, on real estate in a transaction
> **where the broker or associate broker, or any other licensee licensed with the agency, is to
> receive a fee on that transaction.**

Read it carefully. The prohibition reaches an **appraisal or opinion of market value**; it applies
to a transaction in which **the broker or any other licensee in the agency** is to receive a fee;
and it therefore bars an agency from selling an opinion of value into its own deal.

What a licensee may still do is present **market evidence** to a client for the purpose of setting
a listing price or an offer price, which is ordinary brokerage. The line between market evidence
presented as brokerage and an opinion of market value offered as a product is the line § 13251-A
draws, and Hour 34 returns to it with the valuation methods in hand. When in doubt, the question
goes to the designated broker, and a licensed appraiser does appraisal work.

### 5.5 Net listings

A **net listing** is an arrangement under which the seller receives a fixed amount and the agency
keeps everything above it. It creates a direct conflict between the agency's interest and the
client's, and it is prohibited or restricted in most jurisdictions. The agency's **written policy**
under § 13277 should address it, and the answer for a sales agent is that a net listing is never
taken without the designated broker.

## Hour 06 — Compensation, dual and appointed agency procedure, solicitation

### 6.1 Compensation

Compensation is a matter of the **brokerage agreement**, and § 13177-A C requires the **method or
amount of compensation** to be stated in it.

Three points a sales agent must hold:

**Compensation is negotiable** between the client and the agency, and no schedule is fixed by law.
Any suggestion that fees are set at a customary level, or any agreement among competing agencies
about fees, raises an antitrust problem — Hour 52 covers it.

**A sales agent is paid by their agency**, not by the client or the other agency. Money flows to the
agency licensed under subchapter 3 and is distributed under the agency's agreement with the
licensee.

**Who pays does not decide who is represented.** In Maine that is doubly true, because § 13282 makes
**a written brokerage agreement** the only route to client representation.

### 6.2 Disclosed dual agency procedure — § 13275

A Maine agency may act as a **disclosed dual agent only with the informed written consent of all
parties**. Consent is presumed informed if the party signs an agreement containing five statements:

**A.** A description of the transactions in which the agency will serve as a disclosed dual agent.
**B.** A statement that in serving as a disclosed dual agent the agency **represents two clients
whose interests are adverse and that the agency duties are limited**.
**C.** A statement that the dual agent **may disclose any information to one party gained from the
other if relevant to the transaction, except** four categories.
**D.** A statement that the client **may choose to consent or not consent**.
**E.** A statement that consent was given **voluntarily** and that the agreement has been read and
understood.

The four categories a disclosed dual agent may **never** disclose:

1. **The willingness or ability of the seller to accept less than the asking price.**
2. **The willingness or ability of the buyer to pay more than has been offered.**
3. **Confidential negotiating strategy** not disclosed in the sales offer as terms of the sale.
4. **The motivation of the seller for selling and the motivation of the buyer for buying.**

Two further rules from the section. **§ 13275 2**: no cause of action arises against a disclosed
dual agent for making disclosures the subchapter permits or requires, and making them does not
terminate any client relationship. **§ 13275 3**: in a disclosed dual agency each client, the agency
and its licensees are considered to possess **only actual knowledge and information** — there is
**no imputation of knowledge by operation of law**.

### 6.3 Appointed agents — § 13278

An agency entering a brokerage agreement may, **through the designated broker, appoint in writing to
the client** those affiliated licensees who will act as **appointed agents** of that client, to the
exclusion of all other affiliated licensees in the agency.

Then the rule that distinguishes Maine sharply from several other states:

> A real estate brokerage agency and the designated broker are **not considered to be dual agents
> solely because of an appointment** under the provisions of this section, except that any
> affiliated licensee who **personally represents both the seller and the buyer**, as clients, in a
> particular transaction **is** considered to be a dual agent.

**§ 13278 3** repeats the no-imputation rule: each client, the agency and its appointed licensees
possess only actual knowledge and information.

**§ 13278 4** requires the Commission's rules to define methods of appointment and the roles of the
agency and the designated broker, and requires that **clients be informed of the agency's appointed
agent policy and give written consent to that policy in advance of entering into a brokerage
agreement.**

So the sequence in a Maine agency is: the agency has a **written policy** under § 13277; the client
is **informed of the appointed agent policy and consents to it in writing before** the brokerage
agreement; the designated broker **appoints in writing**; and only a licensee who personally
represents both sides becomes a dual agent under § 13275.

### 6.4 Solicitation and the relationships form

**§ 13279** requires an agency to provide **buyers and sellers of residential real property** a
**meaningful, written real estate brokerage relationship disclosure form** as defined and mandated
by the Commission's rules, **in a timely manner**. "Residential real property" for that section
means real estate of **not less than one nor more than four residential dwelling units**.

Two things students get wrong. The form is a **disclosure**, not an agreement: giving it does not
create a relationship, and § 13282 says representation cannot arise by implication. And the form's
**content and timing are set by the Commission's rules under § 13280**, so it is used as the
Commission publishes it and not as a licensee would prefer to draft it.

## Hour 07 — Property disclosure, material defects, and information sources

### 7.1 Maine requires a seller disclosure statement — 33 M.R.S. § 173

Unless the transaction is exempt under **33 M.R.S. § 172**, **the seller of residential real
property shall provide the purchaser a property disclosure statement.** § 173 sets out what it must
contain, and the level of detail is the point:

**Water supply system** — the type of system; and for a **private** supply, the type, the location,
any malfunctions, **the date of the most recent water test**, and whether the seller has experienced
a problem such as an unsatisfactory test or a test with notations.

**Heating system or source** — the type; **the age**; the company that services it; **the date of
the most recent service call**; **the annual fuel consumption per system or source**; any
malfunctions **within the past two years**; and **the date of the most recent inspection of the
chimneys and vents**.

**Waste disposal system** — the type; and for a **private** system, the type, **the size and type of
the tank**, its location, any malfunctions, **the date of installation**, **the location of the
leach field**, any leach field malfunctions, its installation date, **the date of the most recent
servicing**, and the servicing contractor — and, **for systems within shoreland zones**, the septic
disclosures required by **30-A M.R.S. § 4216**.

**Hazardous materials** — the presence or prior removal of hazardous materials or elements,
including **asbestos**, **lead-based paint for pre-1978 homes in accordance with federal
regulations**, **radon**, and **underground oil storage tanks as required under 38 M.R.S.
§ 563(6)**.

**Maine is not a buyer-beware state.** A licensee who has learned another state's framework — where
the seller's statutory statement disclaims representations and tells the buyer to investigate — must
not carry it into Maine. Here the seller answers specific questions about specific systems.

### 7.2 The licensee's own duties are separate

The Act imposes duties on the **licensee** that do not depend on the seller's statement.

**A seller agent** — § 13273 1 B (3) — must disclose to the seller **material facts of which the
seller agent has actual knowledge or, if acting in a reasonable manner, should have known**
concerning the transaction. That is a **constructive-knowledge** standard, and it is broader than an
actual-knowledge duty.

**A seller agent** — § 13273 1 B (4) — must **advise the seller to obtain expert advice on material
matters beyond the seller agent's expertise**. This is an affirmative duty to send the client
somewhere else, and it is the statutory answer to almost every question a sales agent is not
qualified to answer.

**A transaction broker** — § 13283 2 B — must **disclose in a timely manner to a buyer all material
defects pertaining to the physical condition of the property of which the transaction broker has
actual notice or knowledge**, and is **not obligated to discover latent defects**.

Two different standards in the same chapter, attached to two different roles. Know which role you
are in before you decide what you owe.

### 7.3 Flood risk, access, and the Maine questions

Several questions arise in Maine transactions more than elsewhere and belong in a licensee's
standard investigation prompt list:

**Water and septic**, because private systems are common. **Shoreland zoning**, which restricts what
may be done near water and is administered locally under 30-A M.R.S. **Flood risk**, both mapped and
historical. **Access** — whether a property is reached by a public way, a private road with a
maintenance agreement, or a right of way that may or may not be recorded, and whether the road is
maintained in winter. **Heating fuel and tanks**, including buried tanks. **Radon and arsenic** in
water and air. **Right-to-farm and forestry practices** adjacent to residential property. And **tree
growth, farmland and open space** current-use tax classifications, which can carry a substantial
penalty on withdrawal.

None of these is answered by the licensee. Each is a question to be raised, routed, and documented.

### 7.4 Where the answers actually live

The professional skill is knowing the source:

**The Registry of Deeds** for recorded instruments, easements, and the chain of title.
**The municipal office** — the code enforcement officer for zoning, permits, shoreland and septic
records; the assessor for tax status, classification, and mapping; the plumbing inspector for septic
permits.
**The Department of Environmental Protection** and, in the unorganised territories, the **Land Use
Planning Commission**.
**The Department of Transportation** for entrance permits and road status.
**Service providers** — well drillers, septic contractors, fuel dealers — for system history.

A licensee who tells a buyer what the zoning permits, from memory, has done the one thing this hour
exists to prevent.

## Hour 08 — Disclosure laboratory, and four kinds of rule

### 8.1 The laboratory

Working from a completed § 173 property disclosure statement, the student identifies, for each
section: what the seller has stated; what the statement does **not** answer; which of the questions
in 7.3 the property raises; and, for each, **who** answers it and **what document** would record the
answer.

The output is an investigation prompt list a buyer could actually use, with a named source against
every line.

### 8.2 What the exercise is designed to teach

Three things.

**A completed disclosure is a starting point, not an answer.** "No known malfunctions" is a
statement about knowledge, not about condition.

**A blank is information.** A heating age left blank, or a water test date left blank, tells a buyer
what to ask next.

**The licensee's role is to notice and route.** Under § 13273 1 B (4) a seller agent must **advise
the client to obtain expert advice** on material matters beyond the agent's expertise; under
§ 13283 3 a transaction broker **may not conduct an inspection, investigation or analysis for the
benefit of any party**, nor **verify the accuracy or completeness of statements** made by the
seller, buyer or a third party. In neither role is the licensee the person who determines the
condition of the property.

### 8.3 Rule, ethics, office policy and MLS — worked

For each of the following, say which of the four applies, and whether more than one does:

1. A licensee fails to give the buyer the relationship disclosure form until the offer is written.
2. A licensee posts a listing photograph belonging to another agency.
3. A licensee takes a listing at a fee below the agency's stated minimum.
4. A licensee tells a buyer the septic system "should be fine".
5. A licensee fails to submit a sold price to the multiple listing service within its deadline.

**1** is the Act and the Commission's rules — § 13279 requires the form in a timely manner, and
§ 13280 sets its content and timing. **2** is copyright, likely the MLS rules, and possibly the
agency's policy; it is not the brokerage Act. **3** is **office policy**, unless the agreement or
the conduct breaches something else. **4** is the Act — an unqualified statement about a material
matter, against § 13273 1 B (4)'s duty to advise the client to obtain expert advice and, if it
misleads a buyer, against the honesty duty in § 13283 2 E for a transaction broker. **5** is
**MLS rules**, a private cooperative's requirement.

Only the first and the fourth are Maine law. Being able to say which is which is what stops a
licensee from treating a trade association's standard as a legal one — or, far more dangerous, from
treating a legal requirement as optional because "the office does it differently".

### Module 01 engagement check

**Question.** A Maine licensee meets a prospective buyer at an open house, spends an hour with them,
answers questions about the heating system, and drives them to two other properties. Nothing is
signed. The buyer later says the licensee was "their agent" and should have told them about a known
septic problem at one of the properties. Analyse.

**Expected answer.** Under **§ 13282** the agency is **presumed to be acting as a transaction
broker**, because client representation may not be created orally or by implication and no written
brokerage agreement exists. So the buyer was not a client, whatever they believed, and none of the
§ 13272 agency duties — loyalty, obedience, disclosure, confidentiality, reasonable care, diligence,
accounting — were owed to them.

What **was** owed is the transaction broker's duties under **§ 13283 2**: to treat all parties
honestly and not knowingly give false information, and — critically here — **to disclose in a timely
manner to a buyer all material defects pertaining to the physical condition of the property of which
the transaction broker has actual notice or knowledge.** If the licensee actually knew of the septic
problem, the duty was breached. If they did not, § 13283 2 also provides that a transaction broker
**is not obligated to discover latent defects**, and § 13283 3 A bars them from conducting an
investigation or analysis for a party's benefit.

Separately, **§ 13279** required the agency to provide the buyer the Commission's **written
relationship disclosure form in a timely manner** for residential property of one to four dwelling
units — and the whole misunderstanding is what that form exists to prevent. Answering questions
about the heating system risks straying into § 13283 3 B, which bars a transaction broker from
verifying the accuracy or completeness of statements made by the seller.
