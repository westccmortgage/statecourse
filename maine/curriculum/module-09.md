# Maine Sales Agent Course — Module 09

## Hours 51–53 · Miscellaneous federal and state laws

**Commission topic covered:** Miscellaneous Federal/State Laws (4%, 120 instructional minutes) —
beginning at Hour 51 minutes 15–60 and running to Hour 53 minutes 0–15, which concludes instruction.
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Not approved. No Maine sales agent credit is available for this course.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module the student will be able to:

- State the federal and Maine protected classes and name the classes Maine protects that federal law
  does not.
- Distinguish a reasonable accommodation from a reasonable modification and say who pays for each.
- Identify steering, blockbusting, redlining and discriminatory advertising in a fact pattern.
- Explain the antitrust prohibitions that reach a real estate licensee.
- Identify a FIRPTA or Maine non-resident withholding question at listing rather than at closing.

## Hour 51 (15–60) — Fair housing

### 51.4 The federal floor

The **federal Fair Housing Act** — Title VIII of the Civil Rights Act of 1968, amended in 1974 and
1988 — prohibits discrimination in the sale, rental and financing of dwellings on the basis of
**race, colour, religion, national origin, sex, familial status, and disability**. Seven classes.

The **Civil Rights Act of 1866** is older, narrower and in one respect stronger: it prohibits
**racial** discrimination in the sale or lease of all property and, as the Supreme Court held in
*Jones v. Alfred H. Mayer Co.* (1968), **carries no exemptions**. A transaction exempt from the Fair
Housing Act is not exempt from the 1866 Act where the discrimination is racial.

**Prohibited practices**: refusing to sell or rent; discriminating in terms or conditions;
discriminatory advertising; representing that a dwelling is unavailable when it is not;
**blockbusting** — inducing sales by representations about the entry of protected-class persons into
a neighbourhood; **steering** — directing prospects toward or away from areas on a protected basis;
and **redlining** — denying loans or insurance based on the composition of an area rather than the
applicant or the property.

### 51.5 The Maine Human Rights Act goes further

Maine's fair housing law sits in the **Maine Human Rights Act, 5 M.R.S. ch. 337**, and it protects
**more classes than federal law**.

**This course does not print the Maine list**, because 5 M.R.S. ch. 337 has not been read from
primary text for this project and a protected-class list is exactly the thing a course must not
approximate. What a licensee must carry from this hour is: **Maine's list is broader than the federal
seven**, the Maine Human Rights Commission enforces it, and **the current list must be read from
5 M.R.S. ch. 337 directly** before any licensee relies on a remembered version of it.

That instruction is not a hedge. Protected-class lists are amended by legislatures more often than
almost any other provision a licensee deals with, and a licensee working from a three-year-old
memory of one is working from a list that may have changed.

### 51.6 The duty is statutory, not merely legal

**§ 13273 1 E** makes compliance with **applicable federal, state or local laws, rules, regulations
or ordinances related to real estate brokerage including fair housing and civil rights laws** a duty
a seller agent owes their client; **§ 13274** carries it to the buyer side; and **§ 13283 2 D**
imposes it on a **transaction broker** as well.

So a fair housing violation in Maine is **simultaneously** a matter for the Human Rights Commission
and a breach of a duty under the Real Estate Brokerage License Act. And a client's instruction cannot
authorise it: obedience under § 13272 is obedience to **lawful** instructions.

### 51.7 Disability: accommodation and modification

**A reasonable accommodation** is a change to a **rule, practice, policy or service** necessary to
afford a person with a disability equal opportunity to use and enjoy a dwelling. Waiving a no-pets
policy for an **assistance animal**; assigning a parking space nearer the door; permitting a payment
date that matches a benefit date. **The housing provider bears the ordinary cost of making it.**

**A reasonable modification** is a **physical change** to the premises necessary to afford full
enjoyment. A ramp; grab bars; a widened doorway. **It is at the disabled person's expense**, and in a
rental the landlord may, where reasonable, condition permission on the tenant agreeing to restore the
interior.

Two rules that follow: **a no-pets policy is not an answer to an assistance-animal request**, because
an assistance animal is not a pet, and **pet fees and pet deposits may not be charged for one** —
though the tenant remains responsible for damage the animal causes and for its behaviour.

**Service animals under the Americans with Disabilities Act are a different question.** The ADA
governs places of public accommodation and commercial facilities; **housing is governed by fair
housing law**. A leasing office open to the public may be a place of public accommodation, and the
two regimes can apply to different parts of the same property. Do not import ADA service-animal rules
into a housing accommodation analysis.

### 51.8 Practice rules

**Describe the property, never the occupants or the desired occupants.** "Four bedrooms, fenced yard,
walk to the elementary school" describes the property. "Perfect for a young family" expresses a
familial-status preference. "Quiet building, ideal for mature professionals" expresses preferences on
age and family status.

**Answer neighbourhood questions with objective sources**, not demographics: school data, crime
statistics published by the municipality, census data, tax rates, commute times.

**Show every buyer everything that meets their stated criteria**, and let the buyer narrow. **Record
what the client asked for in their own words.**

**Use written, objective, uniform screening criteria** in leasing and apply them identically to every
applicant. Deviating for one applicant is the fact pattern that produces liability, whatever the
reason.

**Do not honour a discriminatory covenant.** Historic racial covenants remain in Maine chains of
title. They are void and unenforceable, and repeating them as though they operated is itself a
problem.

## Hour 52 — ADA, antitrust, advertising, and credit

### 52.1 The Americans with Disabilities Act

The **ADA** governs **places of public accommodation** and commercial facilities. A real estate
office open to the public is one; so is a leasing office. Employment provisions apply to employers of
the covered size.

**Housing itself is governed by fair housing law rather than the ADA**, and the two must not be
conflated. Where a licensee's own office or website is concerned, the ADA question is about access —
physical and, increasingly, digital.

### 52.2 Antitrust

The **Sherman Act** prohibits agreements in restraint of trade, and four patterns reach real estate
licensees directly:

**Price fixing.** Any agreement or understanding among competing firms about the fees they will
charge. **Commission rates are negotiable between a client and an agency, and no schedule is set by
law.** A conversation among competing licensees about "what everyone charges" is the conversation
that produces the case.

**Market allocation.** Agreements among competitors to divide territories, property types, or
customers.

**Group boycott.** Agreements among competitors to refuse to deal with a particular firm — most often
a discount or non-traditional broker.

**Tying.** Conditioning the sale of one service on the purchase of another.

Antitrust exposure is created by **conversation**, and the safest professional habits are simple:
never discuss your firm's fees with a competing firm; state your own fee as your own agency's,
negotiable; never agree, even informally, to avoid a competitor; and leave any conversation that
starts down one of these roads, visibly.

### 52.3 TILA advertising and the trigger terms

**TILA and Regulation Z** control the advertising of consumer credit. If an advertisement states one
of the **trigger terms** — the amount or percentage of a down payment, the number of payments or the
period of repayment, the amount of any payment, or the amount of any finance charge — the
advertisement must also state the additional required disclosures, including the **annual percentage
rate**.

The practical rule for a licensee: **"$1,850 a month!" in a property advertisement is a credit
advertisement**, and it triggers obligations most licensees do not know exist. Say what the property
is; leave financing terms to the lender.

### 52.4 ECOA and credit

**ECOA and Regulation B** prohibit discrimination in credit transactions on the basis of race,
colour, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age where the applicant has capacity to
contract, receipt of public assistance income, and the good faith exercise of rights under consumer
credit protection law. An applicant denied credit is entitled to an **adverse action notice**.

For a licensee the connection is that steering a buyer toward or away from a lender, or handling a
buyer differently because of the credit product they intend to use, can implicate both ECOA and fair
housing.

### 52.5 Marketing controls, and the record

**Do Not Call** and the **CAN-SPAM Act** control telephone and email solicitation, and the **TCPA**
controls automated calls and texts. Consent, record-keeping, and honouring opt-outs are the
substance, and the agency's **written policy** under § 13277 should address them.

**Copyright** — photographs, floor plans, drone footage and video are copyrighted works, usually
owned by the photographer unless the agreement says otherwise. Reusing another agency's listing
photographs after a listing changes hands is infringement, not economy.

**Advertising truthfulness** and the requirement that advertising be in the agency's name, under the
designated broker's supervision, remain the Commission's rules — Module 01, Hour 5.

## Hour 53 (0–15) — Tax and FIRPTA orientation, and the conclusion

### 53.1 The rule that governs this section

Every tax question in a real estate transaction depends on facts the licensee does not have and on
law that changes. **A licensee identifies that a question exists and refers it.** Under
**§ 13273 1 B (4)** a seller agent is required to **advise the client to obtain expert advice on
material matters beyond the seller agent's expertise**, and tax is the clearest case of it.

### 53.2 FIRPTA

The **Foreign Investment in Real Property Tax Act** requires a buyer to **withhold** a percentage of
the amount realised on the purchase of a US real property interest **from a foreign person**, and to
remit it. The buyer — not the seller — is the withholding agent, which is why the buyer's side must
know about it.

Exemptions and reduced rates exist, and a **non-foreign affidavit** from the seller is the ordinary
route where the seller is not a foreign person.

**This course states no rate and no threshold.** What a licensee must do is **identify the question
at listing**: is the seller a US person? If the answer is not plainly yes, the question goes to the
closing agent and to the seller's tax adviser **immediately**, because a FIRPTA question discovered
at the closing table is a delayed closing at best.

### 53.3 Maine withholding on sales by non-residents

Maine imposes its own **real estate withholding** on certain sales by sellers who are not Maine
residents, collected through the transfer documentation, with an exemption process. **This course
states no rate and no threshold**, because that provision of 36 M.R.S. has not been read from primary
text for this project.

The licensee's action is identical to FIRPTA's: **establish the seller's residency at listing**, and
route it.

### 53.4 The other tax questions to recognise

**Exclusion of gain on a principal residence**, subject to ownership and use requirements and dollar
limits set by federal law.
**Basis** — broadly purchase price plus capital improvements, less depreciation taken — and **gain**
on sale.
**Depreciation** on income-producing property, on which **land is not depreciable**.
**Section 1031 deferred exchanges**, with their identification and completion periods and the
requirement for a qualified intermediary.
**Maine's current-use programmes** — Tree Growth, Farmland, Open Space — and the **withdrawal
penalty**, which Module 04 covered and which is a **contract term** rather than an afterthought.
**Municipal property tax exemptions** and relief programmes, administered by the assessor.

Each is named so the licensee recognises it. None is answered here.

### 53.5 The conclusion of instruction

Three things to carry out of 3,000 minutes.

**In Maine, nobody is a client until it is written down.** § 13282 is the structural fact this whole
course rests on, and § 13279's relationship disclosure form exists to make sure the consumer knows
which they have.

**Your duties depend on which role you are in.** A seller agent owes the seven duties in § 13272 and
the specific obligations in § 13273, on an **actual knowledge or should have known** standard, with
an affirmative duty under § 13273 1 B (4) to advise the client to get expert advice. A transaction
broker owes the six duties in § 13283 2 on an **actual notice or knowledge** standard, and is barred
by § 13283 3 from investigating, verifying, or promoting either side. Know which you are before you
decide what you owe.

**Read the section, not a summary of it.** Maine publishes the statutes free and current at
`legislature.maine.gov`, and the Commission publishes its rules. Every figure in this course that was
not read from primary text was left out and the source named instead — deliberately, because a
remembered number is the thing that goes wrong.

### 53.6 About the examination and this course's status

The **final examination** is eighty questions, delivered across Hours 53, 54 and 55, after all
instruction and both case studies are complete. **The passing grade is 75 percent, and that figure is
the Commission's.** Assessment minutes are recorded separately and count toward no topic allocation.

And the status, stated one last time: **this course is a submission draft. It is not approved, and it
carries no Maine sales agent credit.** Approval is the Commission's decision, and until it is issued
in writing no completion of this course qualifies anyone for anything. Completing an approved version
of it qualifies a person to **sit the licensing examination** — the licence itself comes from the
Commission on application.

### Module 09 engagement check

**Question.** At a listing appointment the seller mentions that they moved to New Brunswick two years
ago and now rent the Maine house to a tenant. They also ask you to "screen out anyone with kids,
because the stairs are steep". What questions have just been raised, and what do you say?

**Expected answer.** Three things, and one of them I refuse outright.

**"Screen out anyone with kids" is discrimination on the basis of familial status**, prohibited by
the federal Fair Housing Act and by the Maine Human Rights Act, and I decline it **in writing**.
Under **§ 13273 1 E** compliance with fair housing and civil rights law is a duty I owe the client,
and obedience under § 13272 is obedience to **lawful** instructions — a client's instruction cannot
authorise a violation. The steepness of the stairs is a property condition to be disclosed, not a
basis for excluding families.

**The seller is not a Maine resident**, which raises **Maine's real estate withholding on sales by
non-residents** and, depending on their status, **FIRPTA**. Both are for the closing agent and the
seller's tax adviser, and both must be raised **now, at listing**, rather than at closing — the whole
point of this hour.

**The property is tenant-occupied**, so at intake I need every lease, the deposits held and where,
rent paid through what date, and whether any tenancy is oral. **A sale does not end a lease**, and
the deposits and prepaid rent must be transferred and accounted for — which is a term of the purchase
contract and, where my agency holds any of it, trust money under **§ 13178**.

and the TCPA — are federal law and are stated at the level of the well-settled requirement.

**The distinction between an accommodation and a modification, and the ADA/fair-housing boundary, are
taught as boundaries** rather than as summaries of either regime.
