# Maine Sales Agent Course — Module 08

## Hours 47–51 · Closings

**Commission topic covered:** Closings (8%, 240 instructional minutes) — beginning at Hour 47
minutes 15–60 and running to Hour 51 minutes 0–15.
**Course version:** 1.0 submission draft
**Regulatory status:** Not approved. No Maine sales agent credit is available for this course.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module the student will be able to:

- Name the participants in a Maine closing and say what each is responsible for.
- Sequence a closing from title order to recording and identify the stop conditions.
- Prorate taxes, rent, fuel and association charges and place each entry on the correct side.
- Read a Loan Estimate and a Closing Disclosure well enough to spot an inconsistency.
- Execute a wire-instruction verification protocol.

## Hour 47 (15–60) — Roles and sequence

### 47.3 Who does what in a Maine closing

**The closing agent.** In Maine this is very often an **attorney**, and the attorney typically
prepares the deed. Maine practice is more attorney-centred than in many states, and a licensee should
know which attorney is acting for whom — because an attorney acting for the lender is not acting for
the buyer.

**The title abstractor and the title insurer.** The abstractor searches the Registry; the insurer
issues the commitment and the policy.

**The lender**, its processor, underwriter and closing department.

**The municipality** — the tax collector for the tax status, and sometimes the code enforcement
officer where a certificate is outstanding.

**The licensees** on each side, whose job is coordination, deadline management, and documentation —
not the closing itself.

**The parties**, who sign.

### 47.4 The sequence

1. **Effective date** established, and the deadline map built from it.
2. **Deposit** delivered to the holder the agreement names, into the agency's trust account under
   § 13178 where the agency holds it.
3. **Title ordered** — early, because Schedule B-I requirements take time.
4. **Loan application**; **Loan Estimate** issued.
5. **Inspections and testing** within the inspection period, with the required notice.
6. **Appraisal**.
7. **Title commitment** received and read: Schedule A facts, **B-I requirements**, **B-II
   exceptions**.
8. **Objections and cure** within the contract's periods.
9. **Loan conditions** cleared; **clear to close**.
10. **Closing Disclosure** delivered and received for the required period.
11. **Walk-through**.
12. **Closing** — signing, funding.
13. **Recording** at the Registry of Deeds.
14. **Possession**, as the contract provides.

### 47.5 The stop conditions

The recurring ones: a **title requirement discovered late**; a **payoff figure** that arrives short
or expires; an undisclosed **lien or judgment** against a seller with a common name; a **septic or
well** result that changes the deal; an **unpermitted structure** the code enforcement officer will
not sign off; a **loan condition** raised at the last moment; a **Closing Disclosure change** that
re-starts the waiting period; a **failed walk-through**; and a **wire that has not arrived**.

Every one of them is met the same way: **know the deadline, know who owns the item, put the request
in writing, escalate early.** A closing date is met by work done three weeks earlier.

## Hour 48 — Costs, proceeds, and prorations

### 48.1 Debit and credit

A **debit** is a charge against a party; a **credit** is an amount in their favour. Each side's
statement balances, and any prorated amount appears on **both** statements in opposite directions and
to the penny.

### 48.2 The direction rule

**Paid in arrears** — the bill has not been paid and the buyer will pay it for a period including the
seller's ownership. **Debit seller, credit buyer.**

**Paid in advance** — the seller has paid through a period extending past closing. **Debit buyer,
credit seller.**

**Rent** — collected in advance, so the seller holds rent covering the buyer's days: **debit seller,
credit buyer** for the unearned portion.

**Security deposits** — never prorated. The **whole** deposit transfers: debit seller, credit buyer.

**Heating fuel in the tank** — a Maine item that appears in almost every winter closing. The oil or
propane remaining is measured or estimated at closing and the buyer buys it: **debit buyer, credit
seller**. And where the **tank is leased** from the dealer, it is not conveyed at all and the lease
must be assigned or terminated.

### 48.3 The conventions come from the contract

Before any arithmetic, three things must be settled and **all three come from the contract**: the
**day count** — a 365-day year with actual days, or a 360-day banker's year; **whether the closing
day is charged to the buyer or the seller**; and **the period** — Maine municipalities set their own
fiscal years and tax due dates, and a municipal fiscal year is frequently **not** the calendar year.

That last point is a Maine trap. A licensee who prorates municipal property tax on a calendar-year
assumption in a town with a 1 July fiscal year has produced a wrong number.

### 48.4 Worked prorations

**Property tax, paid in arrears, calendar-year period.** Annual tax $4,015; closing 17 March;
365-day year; closing day to the buyer.
Daily: 4,015 ÷ 365 = **$11.00**. Seller's days 1 January–16 March = 31 + 28 + 16 = **75**.
75 × 11.00 = **$825.00 — debit seller, credit buyer.**

**Property tax, fiscal year beginning 1 July, paid in advance.** Annual tax $4,015 covering 1 July to
30 June, paid by the seller; closing 17 March; 365-day year; closing day to the buyer.
Daily: **$11.00**. Buyer's days 17 March–30 June = 15 + 30 + 31 + 30 = **106**.
106 × 11.00 = **$1,166.00 — debit buyer, credit seller.**

**Same tax, same closing date, opposite sides**, because the fiscal year and the payment timing
changed. This is why the convention is read rather than assumed.

**Rent.** Monthly rent $1,400, paid 1 March; closing 17 March; 31 days in March; closing day to the
buyer.
Daily: 1,400 ÷ 31 = **$45.1613**. Buyer's days 17–31 March = **15**.
15 × 45.1613 = **$677.42 — debit seller, credit buyer.**
**Security deposit $1,400 — debit seller, credit buyer, in full.**

**Fuel oil.** 187 gallons in the tank at $3.42 per gallon = **$639.54 — debit buyer, credit seller.**

### 48.5 Assembling the two sides

**Buyer's cash to close** = price + closing costs + prepaids and reserves + prorated items charged to
the buyer − loan amount − earnest money − seller and lender credits − prorated items credited to the
buyer.

**Seller's net proceeds** = price − mortgage payoffs and accrued interest − the brokerage fee −
seller-allocated charges including the **transfer tax** share − prorated items charged to the seller
+ prorated items credited to the seller.

*Worked seller's side:* price $412,000; mortgage payoff $238,940 including accrued interest;
brokerage fee 5% = $20,600; transfer tax share and seller-allocated charges $1,320; tax proration
debit $825.00; rent and deposit debits $2,077.42; fuel credit $639.54.
412,000 − 238,940 − 20,600 − 1,320 − 825.00 − 2,077.42 + 639.54 = **$148,877.12**.

### 48.6 What a licensee's estimate is

**An estimate.** The controlling figures are the lender's **Closing Disclosure** and the closing
agent's statement. Under § 13273 1 C the duty is reasonable skill and care, and an estimate presented
as a final figure is not that. Every estimate should state its assumptions — the payoff used and its
good-through date, the tax convention and fiscal year, the fee, and what has been excluded.

## Hour 49 — Deeds, affidavits, title insurance, transfer tax, and recording

### 49.1 The deed at closing

The **deed** is prepared by the attorney, executed by the seller, acknowledged before a notary, and
delivered at closing. **Title passes on delivery and acceptance**; recording gives notice.

Maine forms, as Module 04 set out: the **warranty deed**; the **quitclaim deed with covenant**, which
functions as a limited warranty deed and is a Maine usage that misleads people from other states; the
**release deed**, which warrants nothing; and **fiduciary deeds** carrying the authority under which
the signer acts.

Everyone whose signature is needed to convey must sign — co-owners, a life tenant and the
remaindermen, a trustee, a personal representative, and a spouse whose interest must be released.
Establishing that at **listing**, not at closing, is the licensee's contribution.

### 49.2 Affidavits and closing documents

**Affidavits** at a Maine closing commonly include a **title affidavit** as to liens, parties in
possession and recent improvements; a **non-foreign affidavit** for **FIRPTA**, the federal
withholding regime on a sale by a foreign person; and, where required, an affidavit as to the septic
system or the property's use.

Maine also imposes its own **real estate withholding** on certain sales by non-residents, collected
through the transfer documentation. **This course states no rate and no exemption threshold**, because
that provision of 36 M.R.S. has not been read from primary text for this project. What a licensee
must do is **identify at listing whether the seller is a Maine resident**, because if they are not,
withholding and the exemption process are questions for the closing agent and the seller's tax
adviser well before closing.

### 49.3 Title insurance

**Backward-looking indemnity** against defects existing at the policy date and not excepted. One
premium, paid at closing.

An **owner's policy** insures the buyer for the purchase price; a **lender's policy** insures the
lender for the loan amount and declines as it is repaid. **The lender's policy does not protect the
buyer.**

**Schedule B-I requirements** are actions; **Schedule B-II exceptions** are risks the insurer
declines, commonly including rights of parties in possession and **matters an accurate survey would
show**. **Exclusions** are printed categories including zoning and eminent domain. **No physical
condition is covered.**

### 49.4 Transfer tax and recording

Maine imposes a **real estate transfer tax** on conveyances, collected at the Registry of Deeds on a
**declaration of value**, and conventionally shared between the parties. **This course states no
rate**; the closing agent computes it, and any exemption is claimed on the declaration.

Recording at the **county Registry of Deeds** gives constructive notice. The deed and the mortgage
are recorded, and the discharge of the seller's paid-off mortgage should follow — which is the
seller's problem but a foreseeable one, and a licensee who confirms it has served their client.

## Hour 50 — Loan Estimate, Closing Disclosure, and the federal frame

### 50.1 The two disclosures

**TRID** — the TILA-RESPA Integrated Disclosure rule — merged two regimes into two forms.

The **Loan Estimate** is delivered shortly after application and sets out the loan terms, projected
payments, costs at closing, and cash to close, with the sections a consumer can compare between
lenders.

The **Closing Disclosure** must be **received by the borrower a defined period before consummation**,
and it states the final terms and costs. Certain changes — including a change in loan product — **re-
start** that period.

**No period is stated in this course.** The lender and the closing agent state it, and a licensee who
promises a date the lender has not confirmed has made a promise the process may not keep.

### 50.2 Reading them against the contract

A licensee is not the person who verifies the loan's arithmetic. A licensee **is** the person who
should notice when the documents and the contract do not describe the same transaction:

The **sale price** on the disclosure against the ratified agreement and every amendment.
The **seller credit** actually appearing, in the agreed amount.
**Prorations** matching the convention the contract specified, including the municipal fiscal year.
The **deposit** credited.
**Repairs** agreed by amendment reflected where the parties agreed they would be.
The **loan type and amount** matching the financing contingency the buyer's protection rests on.
The **payoff** and its good-through date on the seller's side.

Where they do not match, the licensee raises it **in writing, immediately**, with the closing agent
and the lender — and does not attempt to resolve it.

### 50.3 RESPA, applied to a licensee personally

**Section 8(a)** — no fee, kickback or thing of value pursuant to an agreement or understanding for
the **referral** of settlement service business. **Section 8(b)** — no **unearned fees**.

The label does not decide it. A "marketing services agreement" that is in substance payment for
referrals is a violation, and the fact that referrals are expected to rise as a result of the payment
is exactly the evidence enforcement looks for. Any such proposal goes to the **designated broker** and
to counsel before money changes hands.

**Affiliated business arrangements** are permitted where the relationship is disclosed in the
prescribed form, the referral is not required, and the only thing of value received is a return on
ownership interest.

### 50.4 Wire fraud

The pattern is consistent. An attacker compromises an email account in the transaction — a licensee,
a closing agent, a lender, or a party — reads quietly, learns the closing date and the amount, and
sends the buyer a message that appears to come from a trusted party carrying **changed wire
instructions**. The buyer wires. The money is gone within hours.

The rule, taught as an absolute:

> **Any change to wire instructions is fraudulent until verified. Verify by telephoning a number you
> obtained independently — never a number in the email, its signature block, or an attachment.**

Supporting practice: give the buyer a written wire-fraud warning at ratification **and again before
closing**; establish the closing agent's verification protocol in advance and tell the buyer what it
is; and instruct the buyer to call their bank **immediately** on any suspicion, because a recall
attempted within hours occasionally succeeds and one attempted the next day almost never does.

Under **§ 13273 1 C** the duty is reasonable skill and care, and a licensee who knows this pattern
exists and never warns the client has a problem under that standard.

## Hour 51 (0–15) — Walk-through, possession, and the post-closing file

### 51.1 The walk-through

Not an inspection. Its purpose is narrow and specific: confirm that the property is in the condition
the contract requires; that **agreed repairs were completed**, with documentation; that **items
included are present and items excluded are gone**; that systems operate; that the property is
**vacant** if it was to be; and that no new damage has occurred since the inspection.

Maine additions: **the fuel level is read** for the proration; **the water runs and the well pump
works**, which on a vacant seasonal property may mean confirming the system was not drained; **the
heat works**; and, on a property with a leased propane tank or rented water treatment, that the
arrangement is as the contract provided.

### 51.2 Utilities, keys, possession

Utility transfer arranged for the closing date. Keys, garage remotes, mailbox keys, alarm codes,
association fobs, and — on a Maine property — **well and septic records, appliance manuals, and any
service contracts** handed over. Possession transfers as the contract provides, and any gap between
closing and possession is a separate written agreement with its own insurance and liability
consequences.

### 51.3 The post-closing file

The buyer receives their complete file. The agency retains its records under **§ 13184**, whose
retention schedule is set by the **Commission's rules** — this course states no period, and a
licensee must read the current rule.

A last item worth doing: confirm the seller's paid-off mortgage is **discharged of record**. It is
not the licensee's obligation and it is a foreseeable problem, and confirming it is the difference
between a transaction that is finished and one that surfaces in five years.

### Module 08 engagement check

**Question.** Two days before a 17 March closing your buyer forwards an email that appears to come
from the closing attorney's paralegal — correct display name, firm logo — with the subject "Updated
wire instructions". Separately, you notice the Closing Disclosure prorates property tax on a
calendar-year basis, but the town's fiscal year begins 1 July and the seller has already paid through
30 June. Handle both.

**Expected answer.** **The email is fraudulent until verified.** I tell the buyer **in writing, at
once, not to send anything.** I telephone the closing attorney on a number obtained **independently**
— from the ratified agreement, my own file, or the firm's published listing, and **never** a number
in the email or its attachments — and confirm with a named person whether anything changed. I notify
my **designated broker** and the lender. If money has already moved, the buyer calls their bank
**immediately** to request a recall and reports it, because hours matter. I preserve the email with
its full headers and change nothing.

**The proration is on the wrong basis.** The town's fiscal year runs from 1 July and the seller has
**paid in advance** through 30 June, so the buyer owes the seller for the buyer's days — **debit
buyer, credit seller** — and a calendar-year arrears calculation would put it on the wrong side
entirely. I raise it **in writing, immediately**, with the closing agent and the lender, and I do
**not** attempt to recompute or resolve it: the closing agent's statement and the lender's Closing
Disclosure are the controlling documents, and my job is to notice, report, and make sure it is fixed
before the parties sign.

### E8.3 Reading a Closing Disclosure against the file

A licensee is not verifying the lender's arithmetic. A licensee is checking that the documents and
the transaction describe the same thing:

**Loan terms** against the financing contingency the buyer's protection rests on — type, amount,
rate, and whether there is a prepayment penalty or a balloon.
**Projected payments** including taxes, insurance and any mortgage insurance.
**Cash to close** against the estimate the buyer has been planning around.
**Seller credits** actually appearing, in the agreed amount and in the agreed place.
**Prorations** on the convention the contract specified, and on the correct fiscal year.
**Deposit** credited.
**Repairs** agreed by amendment reflected where the parties agreed.
**Payoff** and its good-through date on the seller's side.
**Fees** to the licensee's own agency stated correctly.

Where they do not match, raise it in writing immediately with the closing agent and the lender — and
do not attempt to resolve it.

### E8.4 The Maine winter closing

A Maine closing between November and April carries items a national text does not:

**Fuel in the tank** — measured or estimated, priced, and prorated, with the tank's ownership
confirmed.
**Water systems on a seasonal property** — whether the system was drained, whether it can be
recommissioned in winter, and who does it before the walk-through.
**Access** — whether the road is plowed and by whom, and whether the buyer can reach the property on
the closing date.
**Frozen ground** — which can make a septic inspection, a survey, or a well yield test impossible, so
those must be scheduled around the season or the contract must address it.
**Heat maintained** — a vacant property with the heat off is a burst-pipe claim waiting to happen,
and who is responsible for maintaining heat between agreement and closing belongs in the contract.

## Instructor source note for Module 08

Maine statements rest on text read directly from `legislature.maine.gov` on 2026-08-20 and recorded
in `maine/regulatory/primary-text-readings.md`: **32 M.R.S. §§ 13178, 13184 and 13273 1 C**.

**Deliberately not stated, for want of a primary reading:** the **records retention schedule**, which
§ 13184 delegates to the Commission's rules; the **real estate transfer tax rate** and Maine's
**real estate withholding on sales by non-residents**, both in 36 M.R.S.; and the **TRID waiting
period** in days, which is federal and which the lender states.

**That Maine closings are frequently conducted by an attorney who prepares the deed** is stated as
Maine practice. **That municipal fiscal years frequently differ from the calendar year** is stated as
a Maine fact that changes the proration, and Hour 48.4 works the same tax on both bases to show the
entry landing on opposite sides — which is the point of the exercise and the substance of the
engagement check.

**The wire-fraud rule is a practice standard**, not a Maine rule, and Hour 50.4 grounds the
professional obligation in the reasonable-skill-and-care duty at § 13273 1 C rather than claiming a
statutory requirement that does not exist.

All arithmetic is worked in full. Prices, rates, fuel prices and tax figures are illustrative.
