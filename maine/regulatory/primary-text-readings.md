## The five Maine rules a course most often states wrongly

### 1. Transaction brokerage is the default — § 13282, verbatim

> Except as otherwise provided in this subchapter, a real estate brokerage agency providing real
> estate brokerage services is **presumed to be acting as a transaction broker** unless the real
> estate brokerage agency has agreed, **in a written brokerage agreement**, to represent one or
> more parties to the real estate transaction as the real estate brokerage agency's clients.
> **Client representation may not be created orally or by implication** or be assumed by a real
> estate brokerage agency or any party to a real estate transaction.

Maine's starting point is the opposite of most states'. Nobody is a client until a written
brokerage agreement says so, and no course may teach that agency arises from conduct.

### 2. The seven agency duties survive — § 13272, verbatim

> A real estate brokerage agency that provides services through a brokerage agreement for a client
> is bound by the duties of **loyalty, obedience, disclosure, confidentiality, reasonable care,
> diligence and accounting** as set forth in this chapter. Such a real estate brokerage agency may
> be a seller agent, a buyer agent, a subagent or a disclosed dual agent.

Maine did **not** abrogate agency duties the way Virginia's § 54.1-2144 did. Both frameworks exist
in this project and they are not interchangeable.

### 3. The disclosure standard is actual knowledge **or should have known** — § 13273 1 B

A seller agent shall promote the seller's interests by, among other things:

> (2) **Presenting in a timely manner all offers to and from the seller, even when the property is
> subject to a contract of sale**;
> (3) **Disclosing to the seller material facts of which the seller agent has actual knowledge or
> if acting in a reasonable manner should have known** concerning the transaction, except as
> directed in section 13280;
> (4) **Advising the seller to obtain expert advice on material matters that are beyond the
> expertise of the seller agent**; and
> (5) Accounting in a timely manner for all money and property received in which the seller has or
> may have an interest.

Subsection C adds the duty to **exercise reasonable skill and care**; D compliance with the
brokerage licence laws and Commission rules; E compliance with fair housing and civil rights law;
and F the obligation to preserve confidential information that might have a negative impact on the
seller's real estate activity, with four stated exceptions — client consent, disclosure required by
law, information already public or made public by the client or another source, and disclosure
necessary to defend against an accusation of wrongful conduct.

**"Or if acting in a reasonable manner should have known" is a constructive-knowledge standard.**
It is broader than Virginia's actual-knowledge duty, and § 13273 1 B (4) adds an affirmative duty
to tell the client to get expert advice — a duty Virginia does not impose in those terms.

### 4. Appointed agents do not make the designated broker a dual agent — § 13278 2

> A real estate brokerage agency and the designated broker are **not considered to be dual agents
> solely because of an appointment** under the provisions of this section, except that any
> affiliated licensee who personally represents both the seller and the buyer, as clients, in a
> particular transaction is considered to be a dual agent.

**This is the opposite of Virginia**, where § 54.1-2139.1 A makes the supervising broker a dual
agent in a designated arrangement. § 13278 1 requires the appointment to be made **in writing, by
the designated broker, to the client**; § 13278 4 requires the Commission's rules to include a
requirement that clients be **informed of the agency's appointed agent policy and give written
consent to it in advance of entering into a brokerage agreement**.

**§§ 13275 3 and 13278 3 both provide that each client, the agency and its licensees are
considered to possess only actual knowledge and information, with no imputation of knowledge by
operation of law.** A course teaching that knowledge is imputed within a Maine firm is teaching the
opposite of the statute.

### 5. Maine requires a seller property disclosure statement — 33 M.R.S. § 173

Unless the transaction is exempt under § 172, **the seller of residential real property shall
provide the purchaser a property disclosure statement** containing, among other things:

- **Water supply system** — the type; and for a private supply, the type, the location, any
  malfunctions, the date of the most recent water test, and whether the seller has experienced an
  unsatisfactory test or a test with notations.
- **Heating system or source** — the type; the age; the servicing company; the date of the most
  recent service call; the annual fuel consumption per system or source; any malfunctions within
  the past two years; and the date of the most recent inspection of the chimneys and vents.
- **Waste disposal system** — the type; and for a private system, the type, the size and type of
  tank, its location, any malfunctions, the installation date, the leach field location, any leach
  field malfunctions, its installation date, the date of most recent servicing, and the servicing
  contractor — plus, **for systems in shoreland zones, the septic disclosures required by 30-A
  M.R.S. § 4216**.
- **Hazardous materials** — the presence or prior removal of hazardous materials or elements,
  including asbestos, **lead-based paint for pre-1978 homes in accordance with federal
  regulations**, radon, and **underground oil storage tanks as required under 38 M.R.S. § 563(6)**.

**Maine is not a buyer-beware disclosure state.** The contrast with Virginia's § 55.1-703, where
the owner's statutory statement says the owner makes no representations, is total, and a licensee
who has learned one framework must not carry it into the other.

## Two more sections that decide practice

### § 13177-A — what a brokerage agreement must contain

A brokerage agreement between an agency and a client **must be in writing** and must at a minimum
include: **(A)** the signature of the client to be charged; **(B)** the terms and conditions of the
brokerage services to be provided; **(C)** the method or amount of compensation to be paid;
**(D) the date upon which the agreement will expire**; and **(E) a statement that the agreement
creates an agency-client relationship.**

> A brokerage agreement may not be enforced against any client who in good faith subsequently
> engages the services of another real estate brokerage agency following the expiration date of the
> first brokerage agreement. Any brokerage agreement provision extending a real estate brokerage
> agency's right to a fee following expiration of the brokerage agreement **may not extend that
> right beyond 6 months.**

Maine requires an **expiration date** in every agreement and caps any post-expiration fee tail at
**six months**. Virginia instead supplies a 90-day default where no date is stated. Neither rule
substitutes for the other.

### § 13251-A — the conflict of interest that governs opinions of value, verbatim

> A real estate broker or associate broker **may not knowingly provide or offer an appraisal or
> opinion of market value**, as set forth in section 14004, on real estate in a transaction
> **where the broker or associate broker, or any other licensee licensed with the agency, is to
> receive a fee on that transaction.**

This is a real constraint on comparative market analysis work in Maine and it reaches the **whole
agency**, not just the individual licensee. Any Maine valuation hour must teach it.

### § 13281 — duration, and the two duties that survive

The relationship commences on the effective date of the brokerage agreement and continues until
performance, completion, termination or expiration. After that the agency and the affiliated
licensee owe **no further duty except (A) accounting in a timely manner for all money and property
related to and received during the relationship, and (B) treating as confidential information
provided by the client during the relationship that could have a negative impact on the client's
real estate activity** — subject to the same four exceptions, with client consent required to be
**written**.

### § 13283 — what a transaction broker does, and may not do

**Shall:** account in a timely manner for all money and property received; **disclose in a timely
manner to a buyer all material defects pertaining to the physical condition of the property of
which the transaction broker has actual notice or knowledge**; comply with the brokerage licence
laws and Commission rules; comply with applicable fair housing and civil rights law; **treat all
parties honestly and not knowingly give false information**; and perform such ministerial acts as
may be agreed.

A transaction broker **is not liable for providing false information** that was supplied to them
and that they did not know was false, **is not obligated to discover latent defects**, and no cause
of action arises against them for revealing information the subchapter permits or requires.

**May not:** conduct an inspection, investigation or analysis of a property for the benefit of any
party; verify the accuracy or completeness of oral or written statements made by the seller, buyer
or any third party; or promote the interests of either party to the transaction.

Note the standard shift: a transaction broker's disclosure duty is **actual notice or knowledge**,
where a seller agent's under § 13273 1 B (3) is **actual knowledge or should have known**. Two
different standards in the same chapter, attached to two different roles.

### § 13275 — disclosed dual agency

Permitted **only with the informed written consent of all parties**, and consent is presumed
informed if the signed agreement contains five statements: a description of the transactions; that
the agency represents two clients whose interests are adverse and that the agency duties are
limited; **that the dual agent may disclose any information gained from one party to the other if
relevant to the transaction, except four categories**; that the client may choose not to consent;
and that consent was given voluntarily after reading and understanding the agreement.

The four categories a disclosed dual agent may **not** disclose:

1. **The willingness or ability of the seller to accept less than the asking price;**
2. **The willingness or ability of the buyer to pay more than has been offered;**
3. **Confidential negotiating strategy not disclosed in the sales offer as terms of the sale;** and
4. **The motivation of the seller for selling and the motivation of the buyer for buying.**

### § 13184 — records

The designated broker must maintain complete and adequate records of all brokerage activity
conducted on behalf of the agency, **open for inspection by the director at the agency's place of
business during generally recognized business hours**. **The Commission specifies by rule what
records are required and the retention schedule** — so no retention period is stated in this course
from the statute. It must be read from the Commission's current rules.
