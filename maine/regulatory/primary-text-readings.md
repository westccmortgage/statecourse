## The five Maine rules a course most often states wrongly

### 1. Transaction brokerage is the default — § 13282, verbatim

> Except as otherwise provided in this subchapter, a real estate brokerage agency providing real
> estate brokerage services is **presumed to be acting as a transaction broker** unless the real
> estate brokerage agency has agreed, **in a written brokerage agreement**, to represent one or
> more parties to the real estate transaction as the real estate brokerage agency's clients.
> **Client representation may not be created orally or by implication** or be assumed by a real
> estate brokerage agency or any party to a real estate transaction.

Maine's starting point is the opposite of most states'. Nobody is a client until a written
brokerage agreement says so, and no course may teach that agency arises from conduct.

### 2. The seven agency duties survive — § 13272, verbatim

> A real estate brokerage agency that provides services through a brokerage agreement for a client
> is bound by the duties of **loyalty, obedience, disclosure, confidentiality, reasonable care,
> diligence and accounting** as set forth in this chapter. Such a real estate brokerage agency may
> be a seller agent, a buyer agent, a subagent or a disclosed dual agent.

Maine did **not** abrogate agency duties the way Virginia's § 54.1-2144 did. Both frameworks exist
in this project and they are not interchangeable.

### 3. The disclosure standard is actual knowledge **or should have known** — § 13273 1 B

A seller agent shall promote the seller's interests by, among other things:

> (2) **Presenting in a timely manner all offers to and from the seller, even when the property is
> subject to a contract of sale**;
> (3) **Disclosing to the seller material facts of which the seller agent has actual knowledge or
> if acting in a reasonable manner should have known** concerning the transaction, except as
> directed in section 13280;
> (4) **Advising the seller to obtain expert advice on material matters that are beyond the
> expertise of the seller agent**; and
> (5) Accounting in a timely manner for all money and property received in which the seller has or
> may have an interest.

Subsection C adds the duty to **exercise reasonable skill and care**; D compliance with the
brokerage licence laws and Commission rules; E compliance with fair housing and civil rights law;
and F the obligation to preserve confidential information that might have a negative impact on the
seller's real estate activity, with four stated exceptions — client consent, disclosure required by
law, information already public or made public by the client or another source, and disclosure
necessary to defend against an accusation of wrongful conduct.

**"Or if acting in a reasonable manner should have known" is a constructive-knowledge standard.**
It is broader than Virginia's actual-knowledge duty, and § 13273 1 B (4) adds an affirmative duty
to tell the client to get expert advice — a duty Virginia does not impose in those terms.

### 4. Appointed agents do not make the designated broker a dual agent — § 13278 2

> A real estate brokerage agency and the designated broker are **not considered to be dual agents
> solely because of an appointment** under the provisions of this section, except that any
> affiliated licensee who personally represents both the seller and the buyer, as clients, in a
> particular transaction is considered to be a dual agent.

**This is the opposite of Virginia**, where § 54.1-2139.1 A makes the supervising broker a dual
agent in a designated arrangement. § 13278 1 requires the appointment to be made **in writing, by
the designated broker, to the client**; § 13278 4 requires the Commission's rules to include a
requirement that clients be **informed of the agency's appointed agent policy and give written
consent to it in advance of entering into a brokerage agreement**.

**§§ 13275 3 and 13278 3 both provide that each client, the agency and its licensees are
considered to possess only actual knowledge and information, with no imputation of knowledge by
operation of law.** A course teaching that knowledge is imputed within a Maine firm is teaching the
opposite of the statute.

### 5. Maine requires a seller property disclosure statement — 33 M.R.S. § 173

Unless the transaction is exempt under § 172, **the seller of residential real property shall
provide the purchaser a property disclosure statement** containing, among other things:

- **Water supply system** — the type; and for a private supply, the type, the location, any
  malfunctions, the date of the most recent water test, and whether the seller has experienced an
  unsatisfactory test or a test with notations.
- **Heating system or source** — the type; the age; the servicing company; the date of the most
  recent service call; the annual fuel consumption per system or source; any malfunctions within
  the past two years; and the date of the most recent inspection of the chimneys and vents.
- **Waste disposal system** — the type; and for a private system, the type, the size and type of
  tank, its location, any malfunctions, the installation date, the leach field location, any leach
  field malfunctions, its installation date, the date of most recent servicing, and the servicing
  contractor — plus, **for systems in shoreland zones, the septic disclosures required by 30-A
  M.R.S. § 4216**.
- **Hazardous materials** — the presence or prior removal of hazardous materials or elements,
  including asbestos, **lead-based paint for pre-1978 homes in accordance with federal
  regulations**, radon, and **underground oil storage tanks as required under 38 M.R.S. § 563(6)**.

**Maine is not a buyer-beware disclosure state.** The contrast with Virginia's § 55.1-703, where
the owner's statutory statement says the owner makes no representations, is total, and a licensee
who has learned one framework must not carry it into the other.

## Two more sections that decide practice

### § 13177-A — what a brokerage agreement must contain

A brokerage agreement between an agency and a client **must be in writing** and must at a minimum
include: **(A)** the signature of the client to be charged; **(B)** the terms and conditions of the
brokerage services to be provided; **(C)** the method or amount of compensation to be paid;
**(D) the date upon which the agreement will expire**; and **(E) a statement that the agreement
creates an agency-client relationship.**

> A brokerage agreement may not be enforced against any client who in good faith subsequently
> engages the services of another real estate brokerage agency following the expiration date of the
> first brokerage agreement. Any brokerage agreement provision extending a real estate brokerage
> agency's right to a fee following expiration of the brokerage agreement **may not extend that
> right beyond 6 months.**

Maine requires an **expiration date** in every agreement and caps any post-expiration fee tail at
**six months**. Virginia instead supplies a 90-day default where no date is stated. Neither rule
substitutes for the other.

### § 13251-A — the conflict of interest that governs opinions of value, verbatim

> A real estate broker or associate broker **may not knowingly provide or offer an appraisal or
> opinion of market value**, as set forth in section 14004, on real estate in a transaction
> **where the broker or associate broker, or any other licensee licensed with the agency, is to
> receive a fee on that transaction.**

This is a real constraint on comparative market analysis work in Maine and it reaches the **whole
agency**, not just the individual licensee. Any Maine valuation hour must teach it.

### § 13281 — duration, and the two duties that survive

The relationship commences on the effective date of the brokerage agreement and continues until
performance, completion, termination or expiration. After that the agency and the affiliated
licensee owe **no further duty except (A) accounting in a timely manner for all money and property
related to and received during the relationship, and (B) treating as confidential information
provided by the client during the relationship that could have a negative impact on the client's
real estate activity** — subject to the same four exceptions, with client consent required to be
**written**.

### § 13283 — what a transaction broker does, and may not do

**Shall:** account in a timely manner for all money and property received; **disclose in a timely
manner to a buyer all material defects pertaining to the physical condition of the property of
which the transaction broker has actual notice or knowledge**; comply with the brokerage licence
laws and Commission rules; comply with applicable fair housing and civil rights law; **treat all
parties honestly and not knowingly give false information**; and perform such ministerial acts as
may be agreed.

A transaction broker **is not liable for providing false information** that was supplied to them
and that they did not know was false, **is not obligated to discover latent defects**, and no cause
of action arises against them for revealing information the subchapter permits or requires.

**May not:** conduct an inspection, investigation or analysis of a property for the benefit of any
party; verify the accuracy or completeness of oral or written statements made by the seller, buyer
or any third party; or promote the interests of either party to the transaction.

Note the standard shift: a transaction broker's disclosure duty is **actual notice or knowledge**,
where a seller agent's under § 13273 1 B (3) is **actual knowledge or should have known**. Two
different standards in the same chapter, attached to two different roles.

### § 13275 — disclosed dual agency

Permitted **only with the informed written consent of all parties**, and consent is presumed
informed if the signed agreement contains five statements: a description of the transactions; that
the agency represents two clients whose interests are adverse and that the agency duties are
limited; **that the dual agent may disclose any information gained from one party to the other if
relevant to the transaction, except four categories**; that the client may choose not to consent;
and that consent was given voluntarily after reading and understanding the agreement.

The four categories a disclosed dual agent may **not** disclose:

1. **The willingness or ability of the seller to accept less than the asking price;**
2. **The willingness or ability of the buyer to pay more than has been offered;**
3. **Confidential negotiating strategy not disclosed in the sales offer as terms of the sale;** and
4. **The motivation of the seller for selling and the motivation of the buyer for buying.**

### § 13184 — records

The designated broker must maintain complete and adequate records of all brokerage activity
conducted on behalf of the agency, **open for inspection by the director at the agency's place of
business during generally recognized business hours**. **The Commission specifies by rule what
records are required and the retention schedule** — so no retention period is stated in this course
from the statute. It must be read from the Commission's current rules.

### § 13271 — definitions, and the two that decide who owes what

> **1. Affiliated licensee** — *"a licensee who is authorized to engage in brokerage activity **by
> and on behalf of a real estate brokerage agency**."*
> **2. Appointed agent** — *"that affiliated licensee who is appointed by the designated broker ...
> **to act solely for a client of that real estate brokerage agency to the exclusion of other
> affiliated licensees of that real estate brokerage agency**."*
> **3. Brokerage agreement** — *"a contract that establishes the relationships between the parties
> and the brokerage services to be performed."*
> **4. Buyer agent** — *"**a real estate brokerage agency** that has entered into a **written**
> brokerage agreement with the buyer in a real estate transaction to represent the buyer as its
> client."*
> **5. Client** — *"a person who has entered into a **written** brokerage agreement with a real
> estate brokerage agency that has agreed to represent that person and **be bound by the duties set
> forth in section 13272** on behalf of that person."*

**Two definitions carry most of the weight.** In Maine the **agent is the AGENCY**, not the
individual — § 13271 4 defines "buyer agent" as *"a real estate brokerage agency"*, and the same
construction runs through the subchapter. And a **client** exists only where there is a **written**
brokerage agreement; without one there is no client, and § 13282's transaction brokerage default
applies instead. A course that speaks of "the agent" meaning the salesperson standing in the room
has already misstated Maine.

### § 13274 — buyer agent, verbatim in substance

The section carries the § 13273 architecture to the buyer side, and one clause in it is the reason
a Maine course must not teach the buyer agent as a mirror image of the seller agent.

> **1. Duty to buyer.** A buyer agent:
> **A.** Shall **perform the terms of the brokerage agreement** made with the buyer;
> **B.** Shall **promote the interests of the buyer** by exercising agency duties as set forth in
> **section 13272**, including: **(1)** *"Seeking a property at a price and terms specified by the
> buyer **except that the buyer agent is not obligated to seek other properties for the buyer while
> the buyer is a party to a contract to purchase that property** unless it is provided by the
> brokerage agreement"*; **(2)** *"Presenting **in a timely manner** all offers to and from the
> buyer"*; **(3)** *"Disclosing to the buyer material facts of which the buyer agent has **actual
> knowledge or, if acting in a reasonable manner, should have known** concerning the transaction,
> **except as directed in section 13280**. **Nothing in this subchapter limits any obligation of a
> buyer to inspect the physical condition of the property**"*; **(4)** *"Advising the buyer to
> obtain expert advice on material matters that are beyond the expertise of the buyer agent"*; and
> **(5)** *"Accounting **in a timely manner** for all money and property received in which the
> buyer has or may have an interest"*;
> **C.** Shall **exercise reasonable skill and care**, *"**except that a buyer agent is not
> obligated to discover latent defects in the property**"*;
> **D.** Shall comply with the laws governing real estate brokerage licences and the Commission's
> rules;
> **E.** Shall comply with applicable federal, state or local law **including fair housing and
> civil rights** laws; and
> **F.** Has an obligation to **preserve confidential information** provided by the buyer that
> might have a negative impact on the buyer's real estate activity, unless **(1)** the buyer
> consents, **(2)** disclosure is required by law, **(3)** the information is or becomes public
> other than through the buyer agent, or **(4)** disclosure is necessary to defend the buyer agent
> against an action of wrongful conduct before a court, the commission, or a professional committee.

**The disclosure standard on the buyer side is the same as § 13273 1 B (3): actual knowledge OR
should have known.** The two exculpatory clauses are what a course most often loses — **no
obligation to seek other properties once the buyer is under contract**, and **no obligation to
discover latent defects.**

### § 13178 — trust accounts, verbatim in substance

> *"Every agency shall maintain a **federally insured account or accounts** in a financial
> institution authorized to do business in this State ... **for the sole purpose of depositing all
> earnest money deposits and all other money held by it as an agency in which its clients or other
> persons with whom it is dealing have an interest.** The trust account and withdrawal orders,
> including all checks drawn on the account, **must name the subject agency and be identified as a
> real estate trust account.** Real estate trust accounts must be **free from trustee process,
> except by those persons for whom the brokerage agency has made the deposits and then only to the
> extent of the interest.** The designated broker, except for an amount necessary to maintain the
> accounts not to exceed an amount prescribed by commission rule, **shall withdraw from the
> accounts all fees due within 30 days after but not until consummation or termination of the
> transaction when the designated broker makes or causes to be made a full accounting to the
> broker's principal.** The designated broker shall **maintain trust accounts and supporting
> records in a manner prescribed by commission rule.** These accounts and records **must be open
> for inspection by the director** ... **Upon order of the director, the designated broker shall
> authorize the director in writing to confirm the balance of funds held in all agency trust
> accounts.*"

**Three figures and one prohibition, all from the statute itself.** The fee withdrawal window is
**within 30 days after** — but **not until** — consummation or termination, **and it is conditioned
on a full accounting to the principal.** The cushion the broker may leave in the account is
**prescribed by commission rule, not by the statute**, so **no cushion figure is stated in this
course.** The rest of the account mechanics — what records, how long — are **also delegated to
commission rule**, and this course states none of them from the statute.

### § 13179 — supervision of employees, verbatim

> *"The designated broker shall exercise a **reasonable level of supervision commensurate with the
> level of qualification and experience of agency employees and independent contractors
> supervised**, in order to **protect and promote the interests of its clients with absolute
> fidelity.** The designated broker **shall not permit or authorize any person to engage in any
> activity for which they are not properly licensed.**"*

**Two things follow.** Supervision in Maine is **calibrated to the individual supervised**, not a
fixed procedure applied uniformly. And **"absolute fidelity"** appears here in the supervision
section, where a reader who has just learned that the transaction brokerage default is not an
agency relationship might not expect it.

### § 13277 — written policy, verbatim

> *"Every real estate brokerage agency shall adopt a **written company policy that identifies and
> describes the types of real estate brokerage relationships in which the designated broker and
> affiliated licensees may engage.**"*

One sentence, and it is a **mandatory agency document.** The statute prescribes **no form, no
filing and no approval** — only that the policy exist, be written, and identify and describe the
relationship types the agency offers.

### § 13279 — relationship disclosure required, verbatim

> *"A real estate brokerage agency shall provide **in a timely manner** to **buyers and sellers of
> residential real property** a **meaningful, written real estate brokerage relationship disclosure
> form as defined and mandated by rules adopted by the commission.** For purposes of this section,
> **"residential real property" means real estate consisting of not less than one nor more than 4
> residential dwelling units.**"*

**The form is the Commission's, not the agency's** — its content is *"defined and mandated by rules
adopted by the commission"*, and **this project has not read that rule**, so no field of the form is
described anywhere in the course. The **one-to-four-unit** definition is the statute's own and is
stated as such.

### § 13280 — Commission rules, verbatim in substance

> *"The commission **shall** adopt rules setting forth criteria necessary to the implementation of
> this subchapter. The rules **must include, but are not limited to**, the following:
> **1. Disclosure.** Those aspects of the services of a real estate brokerage agency and its
> affiliated licensees **that must be disclosed to a client**; and
> **2. Handling of information.** Procedures to be followed ... **to prevent the mishandling of
> information and undisclosed dual agency** in the representation of clients. In adopting these
> rules, the commission shall consider the **formal and informal sharing of information within a
> real estate brokerage agency**, the **arrangement of real estate brokerage agency office space**,
> the relationships of affiliated licensees **who are representing clients with adverse interests**
> and means of avoiding client representation by an undisclosed dual agent. The commission shall
> **review the professional responsibility rules and practices of the legal profession** with regard
> to conflict of interest in considering the adoption of rules under this subsection."*

**This is the section § 13274 1 B (3) carves out of the disclosure duty** — *"except as directed in
section 13280"* — so the buyer agent's duty to disclose material facts is expressly subject to the
Commission's information-handling rules. **Those rules have not been read**, and the course
therefore states the carve-out and sends the reader to the rule rather than describing what it
requires. The statute's own reasons for the rule are worth teaching on their own: **office layout
and informal conversation** are named by the Legislature as ways undisclosed dual agency happens.

### § 13067-A — grounds for denial, refusal to renew, and discipline

The section operates **in addition to** the grounds in **10 M.R.S. § 8003(5-A)(A)**, and authorises
the sanctions in **10 M.R.S. § 8003(5-A)**. Its own grounds include **lack of trustworthiness and
competence**; **any act or conduct that constitutes or demonstrates bad faith, incompetency,
untrustworthiness or dishonest, fraudulent or improper dealings**; performing an act for which a
licence could lawfully be denied; **substantial misrepresentation by omission or commission, but
expressly not innocent misrepresentation**; **failing to act in a reasonably prudent manner to
protect and promote the interests of the principal with absolute fidelity**; **failing to act in a
reasonably prudent manner to avoid error, exaggeration or concealment of pertinent information**;
and **liability of the agency and designated broker** for a violation by a licensed or unlicensed
person acting on the agency's behalf where the designated broker **had prior knowledge and did not
take reasonable action to prevent it.**

**"But not including innocent misrepresentation" is the clause to teach.** **10 M.R.S. § 8003 was
not read**, so **no sanction, fine amount or procedural deadline is stated anywhere in this
course** — only the grounds, which are in this section.

### § 13197 — continuing education

> *"As a prerequisite to renewal of a license, applicants must complete **21 clock hours of
> continuing education within 2 years prior to the date of application** in programs or courses
> approved by the commission. **This requirement does not apply to agency and company licenses.**"*

The commission **may** establish a **core requirement of not more than 6 clock hours** per licence
type, with the remainder from approved electives. **Whether a core requirement is currently in
force, and what it contains, is in the Commission's rules, which have not been read** — so this
course states the **21 hours in 2 years** and the **6-hour ceiling on any core**, and states no
current core content. The **fee** under § 13007 is not stated.
