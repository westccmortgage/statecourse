# Required Sales Agent case studies with answer keys

Both cases are required course content. Production presents the facts first, captures a student response, then releases the scored key/remediation. Each case is worth 10% of the proposed course grade; weighting is pending regulator confirmation.

## Case Study 1 — Alder Cove rural/waterfront listing

### Student facts

Mae lists a 1974 seasonal dwelling in a Maine town near a lake. The seller says the gravel road is “shared by everybody” but has no agreement at hand. The deed references an older plan and a right-of-way. A tax card lists two bedrooms; the property now has three sleeping rooms. Water is from a private well and waste is handled by a septic system. The seller remembers a high-water event but says the house itself “never flooded.” A corner of the parcel appears wet in spring. A neighbor mentions PFAS testing in the region. The buyer asks whether the lot can support year-round conversion and an addition. Mae has a mortgage loan inspection sketch, but no boundary survey.

### Student deliverable

Prepare a table with: issue; known fact; unknown fact; authoritative source/professional; disclosure/action; prohibited overstatement. Address at least: title/access, boundary, use/permits, shoreland/flood, wetland/protected resource, water/PFAS, septic/bedroom count, seasonal conversion and current Maine property disclosure.

### Answer key / rubric (100 points)

| Issue | Expected analysis | Points |
|---|---|---:|
| Access/title | Obtain current deed, referenced plan/right-of-way and title review; do not promise legal access from custom/usage | 15 |
| Boundary | Explain mortgage inspection does not establish boundary; recommend qualified survey/title analysis | 10 |
| Zoning/permits | Verify municipal zoning, code, permits, bedroom/use history and expansion rules | 10 |
| Shoreland/flood | Check current municipal/state shoreland rules, maps, disclosure/form requirements and documented flood history | 15 |
| Wetland/resources | Use DEP/LUPC/local sources and qualified delineation where needed; do not diagnose from seasonal wetness | 10 |
| Well/PFAS | Disclose known information, obtain current water records/testing through qualified sources; do not guarantee safety | 10 |
| Septic | Verify system design/records/capacity and current conversion/addition requirements | 10 |
| Seasonal conversion | Verify 30-A M.R.S. §4215 and current DHHS/local requirements; no promise of approval | 10 |
| Disclosure quality | Source/date/qualification for each representation; document seller responses and unresolved issues | 10 |

**Required remediation:** Any score below 75 reopens Hours 7, 16 and 21–29, requires a corrected source map and an instructor-reviewed resubmission. No additional clock minutes are awarded for duplicate time.

## Case Study 2 — Harbor Street offer and relationship file

### Student facts

StateCourse Realty has a written seller-client agreement for 18 Harbor Street. Sales agent Priya is the listing agent. Another affiliated agent, Jonah, has worked with buyer Lena as a transaction broker but Lena now asks Jonah to “be my buyer agent” immediately before writing an offer. The agency has an appointed-agency policy. The seller tells Priya privately that $410,000 is the lowest acceptable price. The listing is $435,000. Lena offers $405,000 with inspection, financing and condominium-document contingencies, expiring at 5:00 p.m. The seller signs at 4:40 p.m. after changing price to $420,000 and shortening inspection. At 4:50 p.m. Lena texts Jonah “Looks fine.” At 5:10 p.m. she signs the changed document. A prior disclosure mentions roof repair but not a new active leak Priya observed at a showing. The seller asks Priya not to mention it until after acceptance.

### Student deliverable

Create a chronological decision log covering relationship/disclosures, company approval/appointment, confidentiality, material-defect handling, offer/counteroffer/acceptance, deadlines, documentation, unauthorized-practice boundaries and next steps.

### Answer key / rubric (100 points)

| Issue | Expected analysis | Points |
|---|---|---:|
| Relationship | Do not treat request as instant agency; execute required written agreement and follow policy/relationship disclosure | 15 |
| Appointment/conflict | Escalate to designated broker; use Maine appointed/dual-agency procedures and informed documentation | 15 |
| Seller price | Preserve seller confidential floor; do not reveal without authority/legal requirement | 10 |
| Active leak | Do not obey instruction to conceal a material defect; document and disclose/resolve under Maine duties | 15 |
| Counteroffer | Seller's changes create a counteroffer rather than acceptance of $405,000 terms | 10 |
| Text response | “Looks fine” is not safely treated as executed acceptance; apply approved form/signature/communication requirements | 10 |
| Timing | Analyze whether counteroffer remained open at 5:10 under its written terms; do not invent extension | 10 |
| Documentation | Preserve all versions, timestamps, delivery evidence, consents and file notes | 10 |
| UPL boundary | Explain facts/options; refer legal enforceability/custom language questions to counsel | 5 |

**Required remediation:** Below 75 reopens Hours 8–14 and 39–47. Student submits a corrected offer tree and agency-information flow diagram for instructor review.
